# Islamic terrorism in Europe

This article is about Islamist terrorist attacks and plots in Europe. For other terrorist incidents in Europe, see [Terrorism in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Europe).

a wreath of flowers that highlight many other gifts of flowers and candles outside a short metal fence around the area of investigation

Memorial to the people killed in the [January 2015 Île-de-France attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2015_%C3%8Ele-de-France_attacks)

[Islamic terrorism](566821.md) (also known as "Islamist terrorism" or "Jihadist terrorism") has been carried out in Europe by the [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) groups [Islamic State](9087364.md) (ISIL) or [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) as well as Islamist [lone wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_wolf_\(terrorism\)) since the late 20th century. [Europol](525329.md), which releases the annual EU Terrorism Situation and Trend report (TE-SAT), used the term "Islamist terrorism" in reports for the years 2006–2010, "religiously inspired terrorism" for the years 2011–2014, and has used "jihadist terrorism" since then.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Europol20-3] Europol defines jihadism as "a violent ideology exploiting traditional Islamic concepts".[^cite_note-Europol20-3]

In the 2000s, the deadliest attacks of this period were the [2004 Madrid train bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Madrid_train_bombings), which killed 193 civilians (the deadliest Islamist attack in Europe), and the [7 July 2005 London bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_July_2005_London_bombings), which killed 52.

After 2014, there was a rise in Islamic terrorist incidents in Europe.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] The years 2014–16 saw more people killed by Islamic terrorist attacks in Europe than all previous years combined, and the highest rate of attack plots per year.[^cite_note-IS_effect-7] Most of this terrorist activity was inspired by ISIL,[^cite_note-IS_effect-7][^cite_note-alliesunderattack-8] and many European states have had some involvement in the [military intervention against it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_military_intervention_against_ISIL). A number of plots involved people who entered or re-entered Europe as asylum seekers during the [European migrant crisis](46415102.md),[^cite_note-alliesunderattack-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] and some attackers had returned to Europe after fighting in the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md).[^cite_note-alliesunderattack-8] The [Jewish Museum of Belgium shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Museum_of_Belgium_shooting) in May 2014 was the first attack in Europe by a returnee from the Syrian war.[^cite_note-Europol15-11]

While most earlier Islamic terrorist attacks in Europe were carried out by groups and involved bombs, most attacks since 2014 have been carried out by individuals using guns, knives and vehicles.[^cite_note-IS_effect-7] The deadliest attacks of this period have been the [November 2015 Paris attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2015_Paris_attacks) (130 killed). These attacks and threats have led to major security operations and plans such as [Opération Sentinelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Sentinelle) in France, [Operation Vigilant Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vigilant_Guardian) and the [Brussels lockdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_lockdown) in Belgium, and [Operation Temperer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Temperer) in the United Kingdom.

The 2020 TE-SAT by [Europol](525329.md) describes jihadism as "a violent ideology exploiting traditional Islamic concepts".[^cite_note-Europol20-3] Jihadists do this by exploiting the concept of *[jihad](16203.md)*, which means 'striving' or 'exertion' but can also refer to religiously sanctioned warfare and aim to create an Islamic state governed exclusively by their interpretation of [Islamic law](28840.md). The report describes jihadism as a violent subcurrent of [Salafism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafism), while noting that other subcurrents of Salafism are quietist. The two major representatives of jihadism are [al-Qaeda](1921.md) and [ISIL](9087364.md).[^cite_note-Europol20-3]

The first incidents of jihadist terrorism occurred in France in 1995 when a network with ties to Algeria carried out [a string of bombings in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_France_bombings) in retaliation for French involvement in the [Algerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Civil_War).[^cite_note-LorenzoMaroneEntenmann2017-15]

In the early 2000s, much of this terrorist activity was linked to Al-Qaeda and the plots tended to involve groups carrying out co-ordinated bombings. The deadliest attacks of this period were the [2004 Madrid train bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Madrid_train_bombings), which killed 193 civilians (the deadliest Islamist attack in Europe), and the [7 July 2005 London bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_July_2005_London_bombings), which killed 52.

Although militants in Syria had started to organize attacks in Europe by sending terrorist operatives to carry out attacks as early as 2012, security services in the European countries they sought to attack did not see the arrested individuals as part of a network with a cohesive strategy. Instead the general consensus saw them as radicalized individuals. Many of these operatives were arrested, while others carried out unsophisticated attacks which caused little damage but still served to overload security services.[^cite_note-LorenzoMaroneEntenmann2017-15]

Since 2014, more than 20 fatal attacks have been carried out in Europe. France saw eight attacks between January 2015 and July 2016;[^cite_note-guardian30072016-16] this included the [January 2015 Île-de-France attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2015_%C3%8Ele-de-France_attacks), the [November 2015 Paris attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2015_Paris_attacks), and the July 2016 [Nice truck attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Nice_truck_attack). The United Kingdom saw three major attacks carried out in a span of four months in early 2017 ([Westminster attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Westminster_attack), [Manchester Arena bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Arena_bombing), and [London Bridge attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_London_Bridge_attack)). Other targets in Europe have included [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Brussels_bombings), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Berlin_truck_attack), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Saint_Petersburg_Metro_bombing), and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Barcelona_attacks). The transcontinental city of [Istanbul](3391396.md) also saw both bombings and shootings, including in [January 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2016_Istanbul_bombing), [June 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Atat%C3%BCrk_Airport_attack) and [January 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_nightclub_shooting).

In 2015, the [Islamic State](9087364.md), which in 2014 had claimed that all Muslims were under a religious obligation to join it, declared that the only excuse for Muslims to not join the group in territories under its control was to perpetrate terrorist attacks in their current place of residence. According to Europol's annual report released in 2017, the Islamic State exploited the flow of refugees and migrants to commit acts of terrorism, which was a feature of the 2015 Paris attacks. In 2016 attack planning against Western countries took place in Syria and Iraq. Groups such as al-Qaeda and ISIL had the intent and capabilities to mount mass casualty attacks with volunteers.[^cite_note-Europol17-17]

The [Counter Extremism Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_Extremism_Project) states police investigations have found links between [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) radicalization and terrorist attacks.[^cite_note-online-18] In 2019, [Julian King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_King_\(diplomat\)), the [European Commissioner for the Security Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner_for_the_Security_Union), stated that terrorist content on the internet "had a role to play in every single attack on European soil in the last few years".[^cite_note-online-18] However, Swedish news agency [Tidningarnas Telegrambyrå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidningarnas_Telegrambyr%C3%A5) reviewed attacks in Western Europe between 2014 and 2017 and stated that most attackers radicalize as a result of personal contact rather than online.[^cite_note-19]

In 2017, the EU Counter-terrorism Coordinator [Gilles de Kerchove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilles_de_Kerchove) stated in an interview that there were more than 50,000 radicals and jihadists in Europe.[^cite_note-20] In 2016, French authorities stated that 15,000 of the 20,000 individuals on the [list of security threats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiche_%22S%22) belong to [Islamist movements](15012.md).[^cite_note-21] After the Manchester Arena bombing in May 2017, British authorities and [MI5](100076.md) estimated they had 500 ongoing investigations into 3,000 jihadist extremists as potential terrorist attackers, with a further 20,000 having been "subjects of interest" in the past, including the Manchester and Westminster attackers.[^cite_note-22]

According to [Lorenzo G. Vidino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_G._Vidino), jihadi terrorists in Europe mobilized by ISIL have tended to be second-generation immigrant Muslims.[^cite_note-PBS20170817-23] Consequently, countries such as Italy and Spain with a smaller demographic in this category have experienced fewer attacks than countries in Central and Northern Europe such as France, the United Kingdom, Germany and Belgium.[^cite_note-PBS20170817-23]

British think tank[^cite_note-24] [ICSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_International_Centre_for_the_Study_of_Radicalisation_and_Political_Violence) argues for a connection between terrorism and crime: up to 40% of terrorist plots in Europe are part-financed through [petty crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petty_crime) such as drug-dealing, theft, robberies, loan fraud and burglaries, and most jihadists have been imprisoned for petty or violent crime prior to radicalisation (some of whom radicalise while in prison). Jihadists use ordinary crime as a way to finance their activity and have also argued this to be the "ideologically correct" way to wage 'jihad 'in ['lands of war'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_al-Harb).[^cite_note-25]

According to German anthropologist [Susanne Schröter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanne_Schr%C3%B6ter), attacks in European countries in 2017 showed that the military defeat of the Islamic State did not mean the end of Islamist violence. Schröter also compared the events in Europe to a jihadist strategy formulated in 2005 by [Abu Musab al-Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Musab_al_Suri), where an intensification of terror would destabilise societies and encourage Muslim youth to revolt. The expected civil war never materialised in Europe, but did occur in other regions such as Libya, Syria, Iraq and the Philippines ([Battle of Marawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marawi)).[^cite_note-26]

### 2017

In 2017, a total of 62 people were killed in ten completed jihadist attacks in the European Union, according to Europol figures. The number of attempted jihadist attacks reached 33 in 2017, double that of the previous year. Most of the deaths were in the UK (35), Spain (16), Sweden (5) and France (3). In addition to those killed, a total of 819 people were injured in 14 attacks. The pattern of jihadist attacks in 2017 led Europol to conclude that terrorists preferred to attack ordinary people rather than causing property damage or loss of capital.[^cite_note-Europol18-85]

According to [Europol](525329.md)'s annual report on terrorism in the [European Union](9317.md), the jihadist attacks in 2017 had three patterns: indiscriminate killings (London attacks in [March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Westminster_attack) and [June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_London_Bridge_attack) and [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Barcelona_attacks) attacks), attacks on Western lifestyle (the [Manchester bombing in May 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Arena_bombing), [2017 Istanbul nightclub shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Istanbul_nightclub_shooting)), and attacks on symbols of authority (Paris attacks in February, June and August). The agency's report also noted that jihadist attacks had caused more deaths and casualties than any other type of terrorist attack, that such attacks had become more frequent, and that there had been a decrease in the sophistication and preparation of the attacks.[^cite_note-Europol18-85]

In 2017, a total of 705 individuals were arrested in 18 EU Member states, 373 of those in France. Most arrests were on suspicion of membership in a terrorist organisation (354), suspicion of planning (120), or of preparing (112) a terrorist attack.[^cite_note-Europol18-85]

### 2018

In 2018, a total of 13 people were killed and 46 were injured in seven completed jihadist attacks in the European Union, according to Europol figures. The number of attempted jihadist attacks was 24, down from 33 the previous year. All attacks were carried out by perpetrators acting alone. Europol noted in its 2019 report that generally, individuals who act alone seldom do so in total isolation as attackers often maintain relations in small or loosely defined networks and may receive moral or material support from individuals sharing their ideas. A number of the stopped attacks involved groups of perpetrators. The year saw equal numbers of EU citizens and non-EU citizens carrying out attacks. All attackers were male and their average age was 26.[^cite_note-Europol19-127]

Arrests for suspicion of jihadist-related terrorist offences\
in the European Union 2006–2020



&#x20;  Europol annual number of arrests. TE SAT reports 2008,[^cite_note-TESAT2008-33] 2010,[^cite_note-TESAT2010-34] 2014,[^cite_note-249] 2016,[^cite_note-Europol16-52] 2017,[^cite_note-Europol17-17] 2018,[^cite_note-Europol18-85] 2019,[^cite_note-Europol19-127] 2020,[^cite_note-Europol20-3] 2022.[^cite_note-Europol22-156]

According to Europol, the number of people arrested on suspicion of jihadist-related terrorist offences in the European Union increased from 395 in 2014 to 687 in 2015.[^cite_note-Europol16-52]

In 2015, most arrests were made in France (377), followed by Spain (75) and Belgium (60); statistics for the United Kingdom were not available.[^cite_note-Europol16-52] During 2015, jihadist terrorism related verdicts were 198 out of a total of 527 terrorism related verdicts.[^cite_note-Europol16-52] The average sentence for jihadist terrorism increased from 4 years in 2014 to 6 years.[^cite_note-Europol16-52] In Austria, Belgium, Denmark and Sweden, all terrorism verdicts concerned jihadist terrorism.[^cite_note-Europol16-52]

In 2016, a total of 718 people were arrested on suspicion of jihadist-related terrorist offences in the European Union.[^cite_note-Europol17-17] During 2016, 358 verdicts on jihadi terrorism were delivered by courts in the EU, the vast majority of all terrorism verdicts. Belgium had the highest number of such verdicts at 138. All terrorism verdicts in Austria, Belgium, Estonia, Finland, France, Italy, Portugal and Sweden related to jihadist terrorism. Of those convicted for jihadist terrorist offences, 22 were women, such offences were punished with an average sentence of 5 years in prison.[^cite_note-Europol17-17]

After the [vehicle-ramming attack](44318569.md), European countries began equipping [pedestrian areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian_zone) with barriers.[^cite_note-PBS20170817-23]

In 2017, the total number of arrests was 705.[^cite_note-Europol18-85] During 2017, 352 verdicts on jihadi terrorism were delivered by courts in the EU, this was the vast majority of all terrorist convictions (569). The average sentence remained at 5 years in prison. The country with the highest number of jihadist convictions was France with 114.[^cite_note-Europol18_p58-250]

In 2017, according to [Gilles de Kerchove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilles_de_Kerchove), the European Union's Counter-terrorism Coordinator, the United Kingdom had the highest number of known Islamist radicals of any European country at around 20 to 25 thousand. de Kerchove said that three thousand of those were considered a direct threat by [MI5](100076.md) and 500 were under constant surveillance.[^cite_note-251]

A number of European countries—Austria, Belgium, Denmark, France, the Netherlands, and the United Kingdom—made legal changes which enable deprivation of citizenship of individuals engaged in terrorism if they have dual citizenship.[^cite_note-252]
