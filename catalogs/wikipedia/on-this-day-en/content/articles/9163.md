**Des Moines**[^cite_note-6] is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) and [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Iowa) in the U.S. state of [Iowa](26810748.md). It is named after the [Des Moines River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_River), likely derived from the French *Rivière des Moines* meaning 'River of the Monks'. The city was incorporated in 1851 as Fort Des Moines and shortened to Des Moines in 1857. Its population was 214,133 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census). The six-county [Des Moines metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_metropolitan_area) has an estimated 750,000 residents, the [largest metropolitan area located entirely in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_statistical_areas). It is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Polk County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polk_County,_Iowa), with parts south of County Line Road extending into [Warren County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_County,_Iowa).

Des Moines is a major center of the United States insurance industry and has a sizable financial services and publishing business base. The city is the headquarters for the [Principal Financial Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Financial_Group) and [Wellmark Blue Cross Blue Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellmark_Blue_Cross_Blue_Shield). Other major corporations such as [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo), [Cognizant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognizant), [Voya Financial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voya_Financial), [Nationwide Mutual Insurance Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationwide_Mutual_Insurance_Company), [ACE Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubb_Limited), [Bayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer), and [Corteva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corteva) have large operations in or near the metropolitan area. In recent years, [Microsoft](19001.md), [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard), and [Facebook](7529378.md)[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] have built data-processing and logistical facilities in the Des Moines area.

Des Moines is an important city in U.S. presidential politics; as the state's capital, it is the site of the first [caucuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_caucuses) of the [presidential primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_primary) cycle. Many presidential candidates set up campaign headquarters in Des Moines. A 2007 article in *[The New York Times](30680.md)* said, "If you have any desire to witness presidential candidates in the most close-up and intimate of settings, there is arguably no better place to go than Des Moines."[^cite_note-9]

## History

### Etymology

Des Moines takes its name from Fort Des Moines (1843–46), which was named for the [Des Moines River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_River). This was adopted from the name given by [French colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonization_of_the_Americas). *Des Moines* (pronounced [\[de mwan\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/71/DesMoines1.ogg/DesMoines1.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/71/DesMoines1.ogg/DesMoines1.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; formerly [\[de mwɛn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)) translates literally to either "from the monks" or "of the monks" from French.[^cite_note-10]

Moines is an abbreviation used by the French for *Moingouena*, an Algonquian subgroup. The Native American term is *mooyiinkweena*, and it was a derogatory name applied to the Moingouenas by the Peorias, another subgroup, meaning shit-face.[^cite_note-11]

### Prehistoric inhabitants of early Des Moines

Based on archaeological evidence, the junction of the [Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_River) and [Raccoon Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon_River) has attracted humans for at least 7,000 years. Several prehistoric occupation areas have been identified by archaeologists in [downtown Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Des_Moines). Discovered in December 2010, the "[Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_Site)" is an expansive 7,000-year-old site found during excavations before construction of the new wastewater treatment plant in southeast Des Moines. It contains well-preserved house deposits and numerous graves. More than 6,000 artifacts were found at this site. State of Iowa archaeologist John Doershuk was assisted by [University of Iowa](241119.md) archaeologists at this dig.[^cite_note-12]

At least three villages, dating from about AD 1300 to 1700, stood in or near what developed later as downtown Des Moines. In addition, 15 to 18 prehistoric [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) mounds were observed in the area by early settlers. All have been destroyed during development of the city.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-DMIndians-14]

### Origin of Fort Des Moines

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Des Moines, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Des_Moines,_Iowa).



Map of Fort Des Moines sites in downtown Des Moines

[^cite_note-15]

Des Moines traces its origins to May 1843, when Captain [James Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Allen_\(Army_engineer\)) supervised the construction of a fort on the site where the Des Moines and Raccoon Rivers merge. Allen wanted to use the name Fort Raccoon; however, the [U.S. War Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._War_Department) preferred Fort Des Moines. The fort was built to control the [Sauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauk_people) and [Meskwaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskwaki) peoples, whom the government had moved to the area from their traditional lands in eastern Iowa. The fort was abandoned in 1846 after the Sauk and Meskwaki were removed from the state and shifted to the [Indian Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Territory).[^cite_note-Forts-16]

The Sauk and Meskwaki did not fare well in Des Moines. The illegal whiskey trade, combined with the destruction of traditional lifeways, led to severe problems for their society. One newspaper reported:

> "It is a fact that the location of Fort Des Moines among the Sac and Fox Indians (under its present commander) for the last two years, had corrupted them more and lowered them deeper in the scale of vice and degradation, than all their intercourse with the whites for the ten years previous".[^cite_note-Forts-16]

After official [removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Removal), the Meskwaki continued to return to Des Moines until around 1857.[^cite_note-DMIndians-14]

Archaeological excavations have shown that many fort-related features survived under what is now [Martin Luther King Jr. Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr._Parkway_\(Des_Moines\)) and First Street.[^cite_note-Forts-16][^cite_note-17] Soldiers stationed at Fort Des Moines opened the first coal mines in the area, mining coal from the riverbank for the fort's blacksmith.[^cite_note-18]

### Early settlement



Flood of Des Moines, 1851

Settlers occupied the abandoned fort and nearby areas. On May 25, 1846, the state legislature designated Fort Des Moines as the seat of Polk County. Arozina Perkins, a school teacher who spent the winter of 1850–1851 in the town of Fort Des Moines, was not favorably impressed:

> This is one of the strangest looking "cities" I ever saw\... This town is at the juncture of the Des Moines and Raccoon Rivers. It is mostly a level prairie with a few swells or hills around it. We have a court house of "brick" and one church, a plain, framed building belonging to the Methodists. There are two taverns here, one of which has a most important little bell that rings together some fifty boarders. I cannot tell you how many dwellings there are, for I have not counted them; some are of logs, some of brick, some framed, and some are the remains of the old [dragoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragoon) houses... The people support two papers and there are several dry goods shops. I have been into but four of them... Society is as varied as the buildings are. There are people from nearly every state, and Dutch, Swedes, etc.[^cite_note-19]

In May 1851, much of the town was destroyed during the [Flood of 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_of_1851). "The Des Moines and Raccoon Rivers rose to an unprecedented height, inundating the entire country east of the Des Moines River. Crops were utterly destroyed, houses and fences swept away."[^cite_note-20] The city started to rebuild from scratch.

### Era of growth



The Barney Sakulin cabin, moved from Washington County, memorializes Fort Des Moines.

[^cite_note-21]

On September 22, 1851, Des Moines was incorporated as a city; the charter was approved by voters on October 18. In 1857, the name "Fort Des Moines" was shortened to "Des Moines", and it was designated as the second state capital, previously at [Iowa City](113933.md). Growth was slow during the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) period, but the city exploded in size and importance after a railroad link was completed in 1866.[^cite_note-22]

In 1864, the Des Moines Coal Company was organized to begin the first systematic mining in the region. Its first mine, north of town on the river's west side, was exhausted by 1873. The Black Diamond mine, near the south end of the West Seventh Street Bridge, sank a 150-foot (46 m) [mine shaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaft_mining) to reach a 5-foot-thick (1.5 m) coal bed. By 1876, this mine employed 150 men and shipped 20 carloads of coal per day. By 1885, numerous mine shafts were within the city limits, and mining began to spread into the surrounding countryside. By 1893, 23 mines were in the region.[^cite_note-lees_coal-23] By 1908, Des Moines' coal resources were largely exhausted.[^cite_note-hinds-24] In 1912, Des Moines still had eight locals of the [United Mine Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Mine_Workers) union, representing 1,410 miners.[^cite_note-25] This was about 1.7% of the city's population in 1910.

By 1880, Des Moines had a population of 22,408, making it Iowa's largest city. It displaced the three Mississippi River ports: Burlington, Dubuque, and Davenport, which had alternated holding the position since the territorial period. Des Moines has remained Iowa's most populous city. In 1910, the Census Bureau reported Des Moines' population as 97.3% white and 2.7% black, reflecting its early settlement pattern primarily by ethnic Europeans.[^cite_note-census-26]

### "City Beautiful" project, decline and rebirth

Iowa State Capitol building in 1917

Iowa State Capitol building, 1917



[Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) in Des Moines on June 30, 1966, near 5th Avenue and the (now-demolished) Hotel Franklin

At the turn of the 20th century, encouraged by the Civic Committee of the [Des Moines Women's Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Women's_Club), Des Moines undertook a "[City Beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Beautiful)" project in which large [Beaux Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaux_Arts_\(architecture\)) public buildings and fountains were constructed along the Des Moines River. The former [Des Moines Public Library building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Downtown_Des_Moines_Library) (now the home of the [World Food Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Food_Prize)); the United States [central Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Post_Office_\(Des_Moines,_Iowa\)), built by the federal government (now the Polk County Administrative Building, with a newer addition); and the [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_City_Hall) are surviving examples of the 1900–1910 buildings. They form the [Civic Center Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Center_Historic_District_\(Des_Moines,_Iowa\)).

The ornate riverfront balustrades that line the Des Moines and Raccoon Rivers were built by the federal [Civilian Conservation Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_Conservation_Corps) in the mid-1930s, during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) under Democratic President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), as a project to provide local employment and improve infrastructure. The ornamental fountains that stood along the riverbank were buried in the 1950s when the city began a postindustrial decline that lasted until the late 1980s.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The city has since rebounded, transforming from a blue-collar industrial city to a white-collar professional city.



An aerial view of floodwaters, July 19, 1993

In 1907, the city adopted a [city commission government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_commission_government) known as the Des Moines Plan, comprising an elected mayor and four commissioners, all elected [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large), who were responsible for public works, public property, public safety, and finance. Considered progressive at the time, it diluted the votes of ethnic and national minorities, who generally could not command a majority to elect a candidate of their choice.

That form of government was scrapped in 1950 in favor of a [council-manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council-manager) government, with the council members elected at-large. In 1967, the city changed its government to elect four of the seven city council members from [single-member districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_district) or wards, rather than at-large. This enabled a broader representation of voters. As with many major urban areas, the city core began losing population to the suburbs in the 1960s (the peak population of 208,982 was recorded in 1960), as highway construction led to new residential construction outside the city. The population was 198,682 in 2000 and grew slightly to 200,538 in 2009.[^cite_note-citypop-29] The growth of the outlying suburbs has continued, and the overall metropolitan-area population is over 700,000 today.

During the [Great Flood of 1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Flood_of_1993), heavy rains throughout June and early July caused the Des Moines and Raccoon Rivers to rise above flood stage levels. The Des Moines Water Works was submerged by floodwaters during the early morning hours of July 11, 1993, leaving an estimated 250,000 people without running water for 12 days and without drinking water for 20 days. Des Moines suffered major flooding again in June 2008 with a major [levee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levee) breach.[^cite_note-30] The Des Moines River is controlled upstream by [Saylorville Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saylorville_Lake). In both 1993 and 2008, the flooding river overtopped the reservoir spillway.

Today, Des Moines is a member of [ICLEI Local Governments for Sustainability USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICLEI_Local_Governments_for_Sustainability_USA). Through ICLEI, Des Moines has implemented "The Tomorrow Plan", a regional plan focused on developing central Iowa in a sustainable fashion, centrally planned growth, and resource consumption to manage the local population.[^cite_note-31]

## Geography



Aerial view of Des Moines

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has an area of 90.65 square miles (234.78 km<sup>2</sup>),[^cite_note-32] of which 88.93 square miles (230.33 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 1.73 square miles (4.48 km<sup>2</sup>) is covered by water.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_files-33] It is 850 feet (260 m) above sea level at the confluence of the Raccoon and Des Moines Rivers.

In November 2005, Des Moines voters approved a measure that allowed the city to annex parcels of land in the northeast, southeast, and southern corners of Des Moines without agreement by local residents, particularly areas bordering the [Iowa Highway 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Highway_5)/[U.S. 65](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_65_in_Iowa) bypass. The annexations became official on June 26, 2009, as 5,174 acres (20.94 km<sup>2</sup>) and around 868 new residents were added to the city of Des Moines.[^cite_note-annex-34] An additional 759 acres (3.07 km<sup>2</sup>) were voluntarily annexed to the city over that same period.[^cite_note-annex-34]

### Metropolitan area

Main article: [Des Moines metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_metropolitan_area)

The Des Moines metropolitan area, officially the Des Moines–West Des Moines, IA Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA), serves six counties in central Iowa: [Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polk_County,_Iowa), [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_County,_Iowa), [Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_County,_Iowa), [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_County,_Iowa), [Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guthrie_County,_Iowa), and [Jasper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasper_County,_Iowa).[^cite_note-35] Des Moines is the principal city, with other major cities being West Des Moines and Ankeny. As of 2024, the population is 779,048, being the 78th highest metropolitan area based on population in the United States.[^cite_note-36]

### Cityscape

See also: [List of tallest buildings in Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Iowa)

The skyline of Des Moines changed in the 1970s and the 1980s, when several new skyscrapers were built. Additional skyscrapers were built in the 1990s, including Iowa's tallest. Before then, the 19-story [Equitable Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equitable_Building_\(Des_Moines\)), from 1924, was the tallest building in the city and the tallest building in Iowa. The 25-story [Financial Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Center) was completed in 1973 and the 36-story [Ruan Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruan_Center) was completed in 1974. They were later joined by the 33-story [Des Moines Marriott Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Marriott_Hotel) (1981), the 25-story [HUB Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HUB_Tower) and 25-story [Plaza Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Building_\(Des_Moines,_Iowa\)) (1985). Iowa's tallest building, [Principal Financial Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Financial_Group)'s 45-story tower at [801 Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/801_Grand) was built in 1991, and the 19-story [EMC Insurance Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMC_Insurance_Building) was erected in 1997.

During this time period, the [Civic Center of Greater Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Center_of_Greater_Des_Moines) (1979) was developed; it hosts Broadway shows and special events. Also constructed were the [Greater Des Moines Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Des_Moines_Botanical_Garden) (1979), a large city botanical garden/greenhouse on the east side of the river; the [Polk County Convention Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Events_Center#Polk_County_Convention_Complex) (1985), and the State of Iowa Historical Museum (1987). The Des Moines [skywalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyway) also began to take shape during the 1980s. The skywalk system is 4 miles (6.4 km) long and connects many downtown buildings.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

In the early 21st century, the city has had more major construction in the downtown area. The new [Science Center of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Center_of_Iowa) and Blank IMAX Dome Theater and the [Iowa Events Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Events_Center) opened in 2005. The new central branch of the Des Moines Public Library, designed by renowned architect [David Chipperfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Chipperfield) of London, opened on April 8, 2006.

The [World Food Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Food_Prize) Foundation, which is based in Des Moines, completed adaptation and restoration of the [former Des Moines Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Library_of_Des_Moines) building in October 2011. The former library now serves as the home and headquarters of the [Norman Borlaug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Borlaug)/World Food Prize Hall of Laureates.

### Climate

At the center of North America and far removed from large bodies of water, the Des Moines area has a hot summer type [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfa*), with warm to hot, humid summers and cold, dry winters. Summer temperatures can often climb into the 90 °F (32 °C) range, occasionally reaching 100 °F (38 °C). Humidity can be high in spring and summer, with frequent afternoon [thunderstorms](70807.md). Fall brings pleasant temperatures and colorful [fall foliage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_foliage). Winters vary from moderately cold to bitterly cold, with low temperatures venturing below 0 °F (−18 °C) quite often. Snowfall averages 36.5 inches (93 cm) per season, and annual precipitation averages 36.55 inches (928 mm), with a peak in the warmer months. Winters are slightly colder than [Chicago](6886.md), but still warmer than [Minneapolis](6097240.md), with summer temperatures being very similar between the [Upper Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest) metropolitan areas.

Many insurance companies are headquartered in Des Moines, including the [Principal Financial Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Financial_Group), [Fidelity & Guaranty Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidelity_&_Guaranty_Life), [Allied Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Insurance), [GuideOne Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GuideOne_Insurance), [Wellmark Blue Cross Blue Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellmark_Blue_Cross_Blue_Shield) of Iowa and [FBL Financial Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBL_Financial_Group). Iowa has one of the lowest insurance premium taxes in the nation at 1%, and does not charge any premium taxes on qualified life insurance plans, making the state attractive to insurance business.[^cite_note-70] Des Moines has been referred to as the "Hartford of the West" and "Insurance Capital" because of this.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] Principal is one of two [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies with headquarters in Iowa (the other being [Casey's General Stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey's)), ranking 201st on the magazine's list in 2020.[^cite_note-73]

As a center of financial and insurance services, other major corporations headquartered outside of Iowa have a presence in the Des Moines Metro area, including [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo), [Voya Financial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voya_Financial), and [Electronic Data Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Data_Systems) (EDS). The [Meredith Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meredith_Corporation), a leading publishing and marketing company, was also based in Des Moines prior to its acquisition by [IAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAC_\(company\)) and merger with [Dotdash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotdash) in 2021. Meredith published *[Better Homes and Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Homes_and_Gardens_\(magazine\))*, one of the most widely circulated publications in the United States. Des Moines was also the headquarters of *[Golf Digest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_Digest)* magazine.

## Arts and culture

### Arts and theater



The Civic Center of Greater Des Moines

The City of Des Moines is a cultural center for Iowa and home to several art and history museums and [performing arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts) groups. The Des Moines Performing Arts routinely hosts touring [Broadway shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_shows) and other live professional theater. The Temple for Performing Arts and Des Moines Playhouse are other venues for live theater, comedy, and performance arts.

The [Des Moines Metro Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Metro_Opera) has been a cultural resource in Des Moines since 1973. The Opera offers educational and outreach programs and is one of the largest performing arts organizations in the state.&#x20;

[Ballet Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet_Des_Moines) has seen many iterations since its founding in 1965. A professional resident company with more than two dozen professional dancers, an Apprentice and Trainee program, and a newly launched Academy in 2026, BDM has won numerous awards for its education and outreach programming.

The [Des Moines Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Symphony) performs frequently at different venues. In addition to performing seven pairs of classical concerts each season, the Symphony also entertains with New Year's Eve Pops and its annual Yankee Doodle Pops concerts.

*Jazz in July*[^cite_note-77] is an annual event founded in 1969 that performs free jazz shows daily at venues throughout the city during July.



Casey's Center

[Casey's Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey's_Center) is the Des Moines area's primary venue for sporting events and concerts since its opening in 2005. Named for title sponsor [Casey's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey's), Casey's Center holds 16,980 and books large, national touring acts for [arena concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_concert) performances, while several smaller venues host local, regional, and national bands. It is the home of the [Iowa Wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Wolves) of the [NBA G League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_G_League), the [Iowa Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Wild) of the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League), and the [Iowa Barnstormers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Barnstormers) of the [Indoor Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_Football_League).

The [Simon Estes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Estes) Riverfront Amphitheater is an outdoor concert venue on the east bank of the [Des Moines River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_River) which hosts music events such as the Alive Concert Series.

The [Des Moines Art Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Art_Center), with wings designed by architects [I.M. Pei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.M._Pei) and [Richard Meier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Meier), presents art exhibitions and educational programs as well as studio art classes. The Center houses a collection of artwork from the 19th century to the present. An extension of the art center is downtown in an urban museum space, featuring three or four exhibitions each year.



The Pappajohn Sculpture Park in the Downtown's [Western Gateway Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Gateway_Park) plays host to the [Des Moines Arts Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Arts_Festival).

The [Pappajohn Sculpture Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pappajohn_Sculpture_Park) was established in 2009. It showcases a collection of 24 sculptures donated by Des Moines philanthropists John and Mary Pappajohn. Nearby is the Temple for Performing Arts, a cultural center for the city. Next to the Temple is the 117,000-square-foot (10,900 m<sup>2</sup>) Central Library, designed by renowned English architect [David Chipperfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Chipperfield).

[Salisbury House and Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury_House_\(Des_Moines,_Iowa\)) is a 42-room historic house museum on 10 acres (4 ha) of woodlands in the South of Grand neighborhood of Des Moines. It is named after—and loosely inspired by—King's House in [Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury,_England), England. Built in the 1920s by cosmetics magnate Carl Weeks and his wife, Edith, the Salisbury House contains authentic 16th-century English oak and rafters dating to [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare)'s days, numerous other architectural features re-purposed from other historic English homes, and an internationally significant collection of original fine art, tapestries, decorative art, furniture, musical instruments, and rare books and documents. The Salisbury House is listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places), and has been featured on A\&E's *[America's Castles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Castles)* and PBS's *[Antiques Roadshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiques_Roadshow_\(American_TV_program\))*. Prominent artists in the Salisbury House collection include [Joseph Stella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stella), [Lillian Genth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillian_Genth), [Anthony van Dyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_van_Dyck) and [Lawrence Alma-Tadema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Alma-Tadema).

Built in 1877 by prominent pioneer businessman [Hoyt Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoyt_Sherman), [Hoyt Sherman Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoyt_Sherman_Place) mansion was Des Moines' first public art gallery and houses a distinctive collection of 19th and 20th century artwork. Its restored 1,250-seat theater features an intricate [rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo) plaster ceiling and excellent acoustics and is used for a variety of cultural performances and entertainment.

### Attractions



The [Iowa State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_Capitol), completed in 1886, is one of two state capitols to feature five domes, a central golden dome surrounded by four smaller domes. The other is the [Rhode Island State House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_State_House).

Arising in the east and facing westward toward downtown, the [Iowa State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_Capitol) building with its 275-foot (84 m), 23-karat gold leafed dome towering above the city is a favorite of sightseers. Four smaller domes flank the main dome. The Capitol houses the governor's offices, legislature, and the old Supreme Court Chambers. The ornate interior also features a grand staircase, mural "Westward", five-story law library, scale model of the [USS *Iowa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Iowa_\(BB-61\)), and collection of first lady dolls. Guided tours are available.

The Capitol grounds include a World War II memorial with sculpture and Wall of Memories, the 1894 Soldiers and Sailors Monument of the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) and memorials honoring those who served in the [Spanish–American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War), [Korean](16772.md), and [Vietnam Wars](32611.md). The West Capitol Terrace provides the entrance from the west to the state's grandest building, the [State Capitol Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Capitol_Building). The 10-acre (4 ha) "people's park" at the foot of the Capitol complex includes a promenade and landscaped gardens, in addition to providing public space for rallies and special events. A granite map of Iowa depicting all 99 counties rests at the base of the terrace and has become an attraction for in-state visitors, many of whom walk over the map to find their home county.



The State of Iowa Historical Museum is near the state capitol in Des Moines' East Village.

Iowa's history lives on in the [State of Iowa Historical Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Historical_Society_of_Iowa). This modern granite and glass structure at the foot of the State Capitol Building houses permanent and temporary exhibits exploring the people, places, events, and issues of Iowa's past. The showcase includes native wildlife, [American Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) and pioneer artifacts, and political and military items. The museum features a genealogy and Iowa history library, museum gift shop, and cafe.

[Terrace Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_Hill), a National Historic Landmark and Iowa Governor's Residence, is among the best examples of American Victorian Second Empire architecture. This opulent 1869 home was built by Iowa's first millionaire, Benjamin F. Allen, and restored to the late 19th century period. It overlooks downtown Des Moines and is situated on 8 acres (3.2 ha) with a re-created Victorian formal garden. Tours are conducted Tuesdays through Saturdays from March through December.

The 110,000-square-foot (10,000 m<sup>2</sup>) Science Center of Iowa and Blank IMAX Dome Theater offers seven interactive learning areas, live programs, and hands-on activities encouraging learning and fun for all ages. Among its three theaters include the 216-seat Blank IMAX Dome Theater, 175-seat [John Deere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Deere) Adventure Theater featuring live performances, and a 50-foot (15 m) domed Star Theater.

The [Greater Des Moines Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Des_Moines_Botanical_Garden), an indoor conservatory of over 15,000 exotic plants, is one of the largest collections of tropical, subtropical, and desert-growing plants in the Midwest. The Center blooms with thousands of flowers year-round. Nearby are the Robert D. Ray Asian Gardens and Pavilion, named in honor of the former governor whose influence helped relocate thousands of Vietnamese refugees to Iowa homes in the 1970s and 1980s. Developed by the city's Asian community, the Gardens include a three-story Chinese pavilion, bonsai landscaping, and granite sculptures to highlight the importance of diversity and recognize Asian American contributions in Iowa.

[Blank Park Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blank_Park_Zoo) is a landscaped 22-acre (8.9 ha) zoological park on the south side. Among the exhibits include a tropical rain forest, Australian Outback, and Africa. The Zoo offers education classes, tours, and rental facilities.

The [Iowa Primate Learning Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Primate_Learning_Sanctuary) was established as a scientific research facility with a 230-acre (93 ha) campus housing bonobos and orangutans for the noninvasive interdisciplinary study of their cognitive and communicative capabilities.



Locust Street looking east from 4th Street toward the Iowa State Capitol in East Village

The [East Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Village,_Des_Moines), on the east side of the [Des Moines River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_River), begins at the river and extends about five blocks east to the State Capitol Building, offering an eclectic blend of historic buildings, hip eateries, boutiques, art galleries, and a wide variety of other retail establishments mixed with residences.

[Adventureland Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventureland_Park) is an amusement park in neighboring [Altoona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altoona,_Iowa), just northeast of Des Moines. The park boasts more than 100 rides, shows, and attractions, including six rollercoasters. A hotel and campground is just outside the park. Also in Altoona is [Prairie Meadows Racetrack and Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Meadows_Racetrack), an entertainment venue for gambling and horse racing. Open 24 hours a day, year-round, the racetrack and casino features live racing, plus over 1,750 slot machines, table games, and concert and show entertainment. The racetrack hosts two [Grade III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_stakes_race) races annually, the Iowa Oaks and the Cornhusker Handicap.

[Living History Farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_History_Farms) in suburban [Urbandale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbandale,_Iowa) tells the story of Midwestern agriculture and rural life in a 500-acre (2.0 km<sup>2</sup>) open-air museum with interpreters dressed in period costume who recreate the daily routines of early Iowans. Open daily from May through October, the Living History Farms include a 1700 Ioway Indian village, 1850 pioneer farm, 1875 frontier town, 1900 horse-powered farm, and a modern crop center.

[Wallace House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wallace_House) was the home of the first Henry Wallace, a national leader in agriculture and conservation and the first editor of *[Wallaces' Farmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallaces'_Farmer)* farm journal. This restored 1883 Italianate Victorian houses exhibits, artifacts, and information covering four generations of Henry Wallaces and other family members.

Historic [Jordan House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_House_\(West_Des_Moines,_Iowa\)) in [West Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Des_Moines,_Iowa) is a stately Victorian home built in 1850 and added to in 1870 by the first white settler in West Des Moines, [James C. Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_C._Jordan?action=edit\&redlink=1). Completely refurbished, this mansion was part of the [Underground Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_Railroad) and today houses 16 period rooms, a railroad museum, West Des Moines community history, and a museum dedicated to the Underground Railroad in Iowa. In 1893 Jordan's daughter Eda was sliding down the banister when she fell off and broke her neck. She died two days later, and her ghost is reputed to haunt the house.[^cite_note-78]

The *[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune)* wrote that Iowa's capital city has "walker-friendly downtown streets and enough outdoor sculpture, sleek buildings, storefronts and cafes to delight the most jaded stroller".[^cite_note-Chicago_Tribune-79]

## Parks and recreation



Kruidenier Trail bridge across Gray's Lake

Des Moines has 76 city parks and three golf courses, as well as three family aquatic centers, five community centers and three swimming pools. The city has 45 miles (72 km) of trails. The first major park was Greenwood Park. The park commissioners purchased the land on April 21, 1894.

The [Principal Riverwalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Riverwalk) is a riverwalk park district being constructed along the banks of the Des Moines River in the downtown. Primarily funded by the [Principal Financial Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Financial_Group), the Riverwalk is a multi-year jointly funded project also funded by the city and state. Upon completion, it will feature a 1.2-mile (1.9 km) recreational trail connecting the east and west sides of downtown via two pedestrian bridges. A landscaped promenade along the street level is planned. The Riverwalk includes the downtown Brenton Skating Plaza, open from November through March.

Gray's Lake, part of the 167 acres (68 ha) of [Gray's Lake Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray's_Lake_Park), features a boat rental facility, fishing pier, floating boardwalks, and a park resource center. Located just south of the downtown, the centerpiece of the park is a lighted 1.9-mile (3.1 km) Kruidenier Trail, encircling it entirely.

From [downtown Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Des_Moines) primarily along the east bank of the Des Moines River, the Neil Smith and John Pat Dorrian Trails are 28.2-mile (45.4 km) paved recreational trails that connect Gray's Lake northward to the east shore of [Saylorville Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saylorville_Lake), [Big Creek State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Creek_Lake_\(Iowa\)), and the recreational trails of [Ankeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankeny,_Iowa) including the [High Trestle Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Trestle_Trail).[^cite_note-NorthSouthTrails-85] These trails are near several recreational facilities including the Pete Crivaro Park, [Principal Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Park), the Principal Riverwalk, the [Greater Des Moines Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Des_Moines_Botanical_Garden), Union Park and its Heritage Carousel of Des Moines, Birdland Park and the Birdland Marina/Boatramp on the [Des Moines River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_River), Riverview Park, McHenry Park, and River Drive Park.[^cite_note-Parks-86] Although outside of Des Moines, Jester Park has 1,834 acres (742 ha) of land along the western shore of Saylorville Lake and can be reached from the Neil Smith Trail over the Saylorville Dam.

Just west of Gray's Lake are the 1,500 acres (607 ha) of the Des Moines [Water Works Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Works_Park_\(Des_Moines\)). The Water Works Park is along the banks of the [Raccoon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raccoon_River) immediately upstream from where the Raccoon River empties into the Des Moines River. The Des Moines Water Works Facility, which obtains the city's drinking water from the Raccoon River, is entirely within the Water Works Park. A bridge in the park crosses the Raccoon River. The Water Works Park recreational trails link to downtown Des Moines by travelling past Gray's Lake and back across the Raccoon River via either along the Meredith Trail near Principal Park, or along the Martin Luther King Jr. Parkway. The Water Works Park trails connect westward to [Valley Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Des_Moines,_Iowa) and the [recreational trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Greenbelt_Trail#Connections_to_other_trails) of the western suburbs: [Windsor Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsor_Heights,_Iowa), [Urbandale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbandale,_Iowa), [Clive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive,_Iowa), and [Waukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waukee,_Iowa). Also originating from Water Works Park, the [Great Western Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Western_Trail_\(Iowa\)) is an 18-mile (29 km) journey southward from Des Moines to [Martensdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martensdale,_Iowa) through the Willow Creek Golf Course, Orilla, and [Cumming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumming,_Iowa). Often, the location for summer music festivals and concerts, Water Works Park was the overnight campground for thousands of bicyclists on Tuesday, July 23, 2013, during [RAGBRAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAGBRAI) XLI.[^cite_note-WaterWorks-87]

The council consists of a mayor who is elected in citywide vote, two at-large members, and four members representing each of the city's four wards. A plan to merge the governments of Des Moines and Polk County was rejected by voters during the November 2, 2004, election. The [consolidated city-county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_city-county) government would have had a full-time mayor and a 15-member council that would have been divided among the city and its suburbs. Each suburb would still have retained its individual government but with the option to join the consolidated government at any time. Although a full merger was soundly rejected, several city and county departments and programs have been consolidated.

## Education



Old Main on the campus\
of [Drake University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_University)

The [Des Moines Independent Community School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Independent_Community_School_District) is the largest community school district in Iowa with 32,062 enrolled students as of the 2012–2013 school year. The district consists of 63 schools: 38 elementary schools, eleven [middle schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school), five high schools ([East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_High_School_\(Des_Moines,_Iowa\)), [Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover_High_School_\(Des_Moines\)), [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln_High_School_\(Des_Moines\)), [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_High_School_\(Des_Moines\)), and [Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt_High_School_\(Des_Moines\))), and ten special schools and programs.[^cite_note-93] Small parts of the city are instead served by [Carlisle Community Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle_Community_Schools),[^cite_note-Ourlocation-94] [Johnston Community School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnston_High_School),[^cite_note-95] the [Southeast Polk Community School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Polk_Community_School_District)[^cite_note-96] and the [Saydel Community School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saydel_Community_School_District)[^cite_note-97] [Grand View Christian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_View_Christian_School) is the only private school in the city, although [Des Moines Christian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Christian_School) (in Des Moines from 1947 to 2006) in Urbandale, [Dowling Catholic High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowling_Catholic_High_School) in West Des Moines, and Ankeny Christian Academy on the north side of the metro area serve some city residents.

Des Moines is also home to the main campuses of three four-year private colleges: [Drake University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_University), [Grand View University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_View_University), and [Mercy College of Health Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy_College_of_Health_Sciences). The [University of Iowa](241119.md) has a satellite facility in the city's [Western Gateway Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Gateway_Park), while [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) hosts [Master of Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) classes downtown. [Des Moines Area Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Area_Community_College) is the area's [community college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_college) with campuses in Ankeny, Des Moines, and West Des Moines. The city is also home to [Des Moines University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_University), an [osteopathic medical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteopathic_medicine_in_the_United_States).

### Print

*[The Des Moines Register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Des_Moines_Register)* is the city's primary daily newspaper. ### Music

Des Moines is the birthplace of many famously known bands and artists today. [Slipknot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipknot_\(band\)), a popular American heavy metal band, was founded in 1995 by percussionist [Shawn Crahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Crahan), former vocalist [Anders Colsefni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Colsefni) and bassist [Paul Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Gray_\(American_musician\)); the band would be also founded by drummer [Joey Jordison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Jordison). The band was signed to [Roadrunner Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadrunner_Records) and has become one of the biggest bands in the metal world.

[Stone Sour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Sour), an American rock band, was founded in 1992 by [Corey Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Taylor) and former drummer [Joel Ekman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Sour). Taylor would later go on to become the lead singer for [Slipknot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipknot_\(band\)). The band has since been on an indefinite hiatus since 2020.

[Vended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vended), an American heavy metal band, was founded in 2018[^cite_note-\:0-111] by [Griffin Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Taylor) and [Simon Crahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Crahan), who are the sons of well-known musicians [Corey Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Taylor) and [Shawn "Clown" Crahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Crahan) from [Slipknot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipknot_\(band\)). They are currently an independent band that has released one studio album in 2024[^cite_note-\:0-111] called *Vended*, several singles and one EP. ## Infrastructure

### Transportation



The [Edna M. Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edna_Griffin) Memorial Pedestrian Bridge over [Interstate 235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_235_\(Iowa\))



Skywalks connecting buildings over 8th Street in downtown Des Moines

Des Moines has an extensive [skywalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skywalk) system within its downtown core. With over four miles of enclosed walkway, it is one of the largest of such systems in the United States. The [Des Moines Skywalk System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Skywalk_System?action=edit\&redlink=1) has been criticized for hurting street-level business, though a recent initiative has been made to make street-level Skywalk entrances more visible.

[Interstate 235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_235_\(Iowa\)) (I-235) cuts through the city, and [I-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_35_in_Iowa) and [I-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80_in_Iowa) both pass through the Des Moines metropolitan area, as well as the city of Des Moines. On the northern side of the city of Des Moines and passing through the cities of Altoona, Clive, Johnston, Urbandale and West Des Moines, I-35 and I-80 converge into a long concurrency while I-235 takes a direct route through Des Moines, Windsor Heights, and West Des Moines before meeting up with I-35 and I-80 on the western edge of the metro. The [Des Moines Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Bypass) passes south and east of the city.[^cite_note-112] Other routes in and around the city include [US 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_6_in_Iowa), [US 69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_69_in_Iowa), [Iowa 28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Highway_28), [Iowa 141](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Highway_141), [Iowa 163](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Highway_163), [Iowa 330](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Highway_330), [Iowa 415](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Highway_415), and [Iowa 160](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Highway_160).

Des Moines's public transit system, operated by DART ([Des Moines Area Regional Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Area_Regional_Transit)), which was the Des Moines Metropolitan Transit Authority until October 2006, consists entirely of buses, including regular in-city routes and express and commuter buses to outlying suburban areas.

Characteristics of household ownership of cars in Des Moines are similar to national averages. In 2015, 8.5 percent of Des Moines households lacked a car, and that number increased to 9.6 percent in 2016. The national average was 8.7 percent in 2016. Des Moines averaged 1.71 cars per household in 2016, compared to a national average of 1.8.[^cite_note-113]

[Jefferson Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Lines) and [Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) run long-distance, intercity bus routes through Des Moines. The [bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Bus_Station) is located north of downtown.&#x20;



The Des Moines Rock Island station

Although Des Moines was historically a train hub, it does not have direct passenger train service. For east–west traffic it was served at the [Rock Island Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Rock_Island_&_Pacific_Railroad_Depot_\(Des_Moines,_Iowa\)) by the *Corn Belt Rocket* express from [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha) to the west, to [Chicago](6886.md) in the east. The Rock Island also offered the *[Rocky Mountain Rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountain_Rocket)* from [Colorado Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Springs) in the west, to Chicago, and the *[Twin Star Rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Star_Rocket)* to Minneapolis to the north and Dallas and Houston to the south. The last train was an unnamed service ending at [Council Bluffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Bluffs), and it was discontinued on May 31, 1970.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] Today, this line constitutes the mainline of the [Iowa Interstate Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Interstate_Railroad).

Other railroads used the [Des Moines Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_Union_Station). Northward and northwest bound, there were [Chicago and North Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_and_North_Western) trains to destinations including [Minneapolis](6097240.md). The [Wabash Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_Railroad) ran service to the southeast to [St. Louis](27687.md). These lines remain in use but are now operated by Union Pacific and BNSF.

The nearest [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) station is in [Osceola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osceola,_Iowa), about 40 miles (64 km) south of Des Moines. The [Osceola station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osceola_\(Amtrak_station\)) is served by the Chicago–San Francisco *[California Zephyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Zephyr)*; there is no Osceola–Des Moines [Amtrak Thruway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Thruway) connecting service.[^cite_note-116] There have been proposals to extend Amtrak's planned Chicago–Moline *[Quad City Rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quad_Cities_\(train\))* to Des Moines via the [Iowa Interstate Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Interstate_Railroad).[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

The [Des Moines International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines_International_Airport) (DSM), on Fleur Drive in the southern part of Des Moines, offers nonstop service to destinations within the United States. The only international service has been cargo service, but there have been discussions about adding an international terminal.
