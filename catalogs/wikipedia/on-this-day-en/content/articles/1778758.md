The **Royal Thai Air Force (RTAF)** ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): กองทัพอากาศไทย; [RTGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_General_System_of_Transcription): *Kong Thap Akat Thai*) is the [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) of [Thailand](30128.md). Since its establishment in 1913 as one of the earliest air forces of Asia, the Royal Thai Air Force has engaged in numerous major and minor conflicts. During the [Vietnam War](32611.md) era, the RTAF was supplied with [USAF](32090.md)-aid equipment.

## History

Main article: [Military history of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Thailand)

Belgian pilot Charles Van Den Born performed a flying demonstration over [Bangkok](56615.md) in January 1911, greatly impressing Prince [Chakrabongse Bhuvanath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakrabongse_Bhuvanath), brother of King [Vajiravudh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajiravudh), and he even accepted an invitation for a flight. Chakrabongse sent three army officers to France, who began flight training at [Vélizy-Villacoublay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A9lizy-Villacoublay) in July 1912. The officers became qualified aviators a year later. Soon after, [Sra Pathum airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sra_Pathum_Airfield) was established along with a temporary hangar to house planes and also host flight training. In late 1913, the three new aviators returned home after arranging for the purchase of four [Nieuport monoplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuport_IV) and a [Bréguet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%A9guet_Aviation) biplane. The aviation section put on a demonstration in January 1914, gaining the support of the King and a permanent aviation group was established and an air base at [Don Muang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Mueang_district) was assigned, as the **Royal Aeronautical Service**, under Army control.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-JF1-3][^cite_note-4]

Siam entered [World War I](4764461.md) with the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) in July 1917, and a [Siamese Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siamese_Expeditionary_Forces?action=edit\&redlink=1) of around 1,200 men was sent to France, arriving in June 1918. Among them were 370 pilots and [groundcrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundcrew), including more than 100 officers who were sent to flight school first at Istres and Avord, and then at [Istres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istres), [Le Crotoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Crotoy), [La Chapelle-la-Reine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Chapelle-la-Reine), [Biscarosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscarosse) and Piox. Eventually, The 95 pilots who qualified as military aviators flew a few operational [sorties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sortie) in the closing weeks of the war but suffered no casualties, nor scored any kills. Their training did mean that Siam entered the post-World War I period with one of the best equipped and trained air forces in Asia.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-Mar2-6]

In the 1930s the Royal Aeronautical Service began to replace French aircraft with American designs, purchasing more than 95 aircraft, including the [Boeing P-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_P-12)E, [Curtiss Hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Curtiss_Hawk_aircraft), and [Vought Corsairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_O2U_Corsair). The air force was formally separated into its own branch, the **Royal Siamese Air Force**, in April 1937 and five operational wings were established. In 1939, when Siam became Thailand, the service was renamed the **Royal Thai Air Force**.[^cite_note-JF1-3] At the end of 1940, the RTAF once again saw combat, this time in the [Franco-Thai War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Thai_War), a border conflict against [French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina). The RTAF operated in the [Mekong Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_Delta), attacking ground forces and [gunboats](274471.md) and defending against French bombing raids, until a ceasefire was arranged in January 1941. Later that year, on 7 December, Thailand was invaded by Japan. The RTAF took an active role in the resistance. Combat Wings 1 and 5 engaged significantly more advanced Japanese aircraft over Thailand's eastern border, but suffered heavy losses, including almost 30 percent of Wing 5, before a cease-fire took effect the following day.[^cite_note-7]

## Structure

See also: [List of commanders-in-chief of the Royal Thai Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_commanders-in-chief_of_the_Royal_Thai_Air_Force)

The Air Force is commanded by the **Commander of the Royal Thai Air Force** (ผู้บัญชาการทหารอากาศไทย). The Royal Thai Air Force Headquarters is located in Don Muang Airbase, [Bangkok](56615.md), [Thailand](30128.md).

The RTAF consists of headquarters and five groups: command, combat, support, education and training, and special services.[^cite_note-org-8]

### Directorate of Medical Services

First set up in 1913 in the same year as the Air Force, providing nursing services only, and over the years has gradually expanded. It operates [Bhumibol Adulyadej Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumibol_Adulyadej_Hospital) and [Royal Thai Air Force Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_Air_Force_Hospital?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Bangkok, as well as smaller hospitals at each wing. The directorate has made a teaching agreement with the [Faculty of Medicine, Chulalongkorn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Medicine,_Chulalongkorn_University) to train students at Bhumibol Adulyadej Hospital, accepting about 30 students per academic year.

### RTAF Software Center

The Chief of the Air Force has envisioned and identified weaknesses in [procurement software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurement_software), which include limitations regarding copyright usage, maintenance, envisioning obstacles, and expanding usage to cover and cater to the needs of the Air Force. Consequently, there has been an initiation to ethically [software development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_development), independently, for the first time. This encompasses care from [artificial intelligence](1164.md) systems, [Big Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Data), [Avionics software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avionics_software), [strategic planning software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_planning_software), and support technology for the future, leading to the [ISO 29110 software development standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_29110_software_development_standard?action=edit\&redlink=1). This initiative has also propelled the consideration to establish a comprehensive software-focused unit within the Air Force.

### Security Force Command

The [RTAF Security Force Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTAF_Security_Force_Command) (Thai: หน่วยบัญชาการอากาศโยธิน) is a Division size unit in the Royal Thai Air Force. It has been in existence since 1937. They are based near Don Mueang International Airport. The RTAF Security Force Command is the main air force ground forces and special forces which providing light infantry for anti-hijacking capabilities, protecting air bases and high value assets, protecting international airport in insurgent areas. It also serves as the [Royal Thai Air Force Special Operations Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Operations_Regiment_\(Thailand\)) (RTAF SOR) which consists of various units such as Combat Control Team (CCT), Pararescue Jumpers (PJs), Tactical Air Control Party (TACP).[^cite_note-16] Royal Thai Air Force Security Force Command consist of 3 main regiments and multiple support units. Additionally, one separated air base protection battalions and one separated anti-aircraft battalions are station in each air bases.

## Royal Thai Air Force bases

Main article: [Royal Thai Air Force Bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_Air_Force_Bases)

The Royal Thai Air Force maintains a number of modern bases which were constructed between 1954 and 1968, have permanent buildings and ground support equipment.

All but one were built and used by United States forces until their withdrawal from Thailand in 1976 when the RTAF took over the installations at [Takhli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhli_Royal_Thai_Air_Force_Base) and [Nakhon Ratchasima (Korat)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korat_Royal_Thai_Air_Force_Base). In the late 1980s, these bases and [Don Muang Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Muang_Royal_Thai_Air_Force_Base) outside [Bangkok](56615.md), which the air force shares with civil aviation, remain the primary operational installations.

Maintenance of base facilities abandoned by the United States (Ubon, Udorn) proved costly and exceeded Thai needs; they were turned over to the Department of Civil Aviation for civil use. Nonetheless, all runways were still available for training and emergency use.

By 2004 the Royal Thai Air Force had its main base at Don Muang airport, adjacent to [Don Mueang International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Mueang_International_Airport). The RTAF also had large air fields and facilities at Nakon Ratchasima [Ubon Ratchathani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubon_Royal_Thai_Air_Force_Base), and [Takhli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhli_Royal_Thai_Air_Force_Base).
