**President Donald J. Trump International Airport** ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **DJT**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **KDJT**, [FAA](11186.md) [LID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_identifier): **DJT**),[^cite_note-e350-3] formerly known as **Palm Beach International Airport**,[^cite_note-4] is a public airport in [Palm Beach County, Florida](73647.md), United States. It is located just west of the city of [West Palm Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Palm_Beach), for which it serves as the primary airport. It is also the primary airport for most of Palm Beach County, serving the suburbs and cities of [Boca Raton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Raton), [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington,_Florida), [Boynton Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boynton_Beach,_Florida), [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter,_Florida), and [Palm Beach Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_Gardens). It is the third-busiest airport in the [Miami metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_metropolitan_area), after [Miami International Airport](128968.md) and [Fort Lauderdale–Hollywood International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale%E2%80%93Hollywood_International_Airport).

The airport is operated by the Palm Beach County Department of Airports. Road access to the airport is direct from [I-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95_in_Florida), [Southern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Boulevard_\(Palm_Beach_County,_Florida\)), and [Congress Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Avenue_\(Florida\)). The airport is bordered on the west by [Military Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Trail_\(Florida\)).

In February 2026, the [Florida Legislature](429346.md) approved a bill to rename the airport to the "President Donald J. Trump International Airport" after the 45th and 47th president [Donald Trump](4848272.md). The bill was signed into law by Florida governor [Ron DeSantis](36729527.md) the following month. The renaming took effect on July 9, 2026.[^cite_note-Guilfoil_NBC-5] The [IATA code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_code) was officially changed from PBI to DJT on August 18, 2026.[^cite_note-6] It is the first time a United States airport was named after a sitting president.[^cite_note-7]

## History

### Early history, 1936–1959

#### Original construction and World War II

The airport began operations in 1936 as **Morrison Field**. It was named in honor of [Grace Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Morrison), a key participant in the planning and organization of the airfield. The first flight departing the field was a New York-bound [DC-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-2) operated by [Eastern Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Air_Lines) in 1936. The airport was officially dedicated on December 19, 1936.\[^cite_note-[www.pbia.org-8](http://www.pbia.org-8/)]

In 1937, the airport expanded beyond an airstrip and an administration building when the Palm Beach Aero Corporation obtained a lease, built hangars and the first terminal on the south side of the airport. The new terminal was known as the Eastern Air Lines Terminal.\[^cite_note-[www.pbia.org-8](http://www.pbia.org-8/)]

The field was used by the [U.S. Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Air_Forces) during [World War II](32927.md). Following the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor), Morrison Field was used for training and later as a staging base for the Allied invasion of France, with numerous aircraft departing Morrison en route to the United Kingdom to take part in the [D-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Day) invasion of Normandy.\[^cite_note-[www.pbia.org-8](http://www.pbia.org-8/)] Morrison Field was a stopover for flights to and from India, via Brazil and West Africa.[^cite_note-pbcho-9]

#### Post-war era

In 1947, the newly established [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force) returned ownership of Morrison Field to Palm Beach County. The name was then changed to **Palm Beach International Airport** one year later, in 1948.[^cite_note-pbcho-9]

The airport was again used by the [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force) in 1951 and renamed **Palm Beach Air Force Base** under the control of the [Military Air Transport Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Air_Transport_Service) (MATS). USAF operations occupied the north half of the airfield while civil operations and the airline terminal used the south half. MATS used the base for training with the host unit being the [1707th Air Transport Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1707th_Air_Transport_Wing) (Heavy), and its 1740th Heavy Transport Training Unit. The 1707 ATW was known as the "University of MATS", becoming the primary USAF training unit for all Air Force personnel supporting and flying heavy transport aircraft. These included [C-124 Globemaster II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-124_Globemaster_II), [C-118 Liftmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-118_Liftmaster), [C-97 Stratofreighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-97_Stratofreighter), and [C-54 Skymaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-54_Skymaster) maintenance training along with aircrew and transition pilot training. Nearly 23,000 airmen trained at Palm Beach AFB during the [Korean War](16772.md).\[^cite_note-[www.pbia.org-8](http://www.pbia.org-8/)]

The [Air Weather Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Weather_Service) used Palm Beach AFB as headquarters for hurricane research, flying the first [WB-50D Superfortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WB-50D_Superfortress) "Hurricane Hunter" aircraft from the base in 1956.[^cite_note-10]

After several years of Palm Beach County fighting the Air Force presence in West Palm Beach, the Air Force started to close down operations there. The 1707 ATW was inactivated on June 30, 1959, and reassigned to [Tinker AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinker_AFB), Oklahoma. With the wing's departure, Palm Beach County took over airfield operations. The Air Force retained a small presence at the base with the [9th Weather Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Weather_Group) becoming the main operational unit at Palm Beach AFB, performing hurricane and weather research for the [Air Weather Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Weather_Service). The [Air Photographic and Charting Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Photographic_and_Charting_Service?action=edit\&redlink=1) (APCS) moved its [1370th Photo-Mapping Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1370th_Photo-Mapping_Wing?action=edit\&redlink=1) to the base, performing geodetic survey flights. The Air Force finally closed Palm Beach AFB in 1962, and all property was conveyed to Palm Beach International Airport the same year.

### Commercial service era, 1959–present

[Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines) began scheduled flights in 1959 and [Capital Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Airlines_\(United_States\)) in 1960. The first turbine-powered flights were Eastern Airlines [Lockheed L-188 Electras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-188_Electra) in 1959, and Eastern DC-8 nonstops to Idlewild started in December 1960.

[Air Force One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_One) was a frequent visitor to PBI during [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy)'s presidency in the early 1960s. Local voters defeated a proposal to relocate the airport around this time, instead choosing to expand the existing facilities. In October 1966, an eight-gate Main Terminal opened on the northeast side of the airport; in 1974 [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines) moved into its own six-gate terminal with the airport's first jetways.\[^cite_note-[www.pbia.org-8](http://www.pbia.org-8/)] The [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) built a new [air traffic control](48563.md) tower on the south side of the airport during this period.

By the mid-1970s, the airport's dominant carriers were [Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines), [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Air_Lines) and [National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Airlines_\(1934%E2%80%931980\)). Eastern operated the airport's only widebody service at the time, daily L-1011s to [New York JFK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_JFK) and [Newark](248492.md).[^cite_note-11] By 1979, National operated daily [DC-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-10) service to JFK, [LaGuardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaGuardia) and [Miami](128968.md), while Eastern operated L-1011s to [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartsfield%E2%80%93Jackson_Atlanta_International_Airport) and Delta operated L-1011s to [Tampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_International_Airport).[^cite_note-12] By 1985, eight widebodies a day flew between PBI and the three New York airports.[^cite_note-13]

The 25-gate [David McCampbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_McCampbell) Terminal, named for a World War II naval flying ace, was dedicated in 1988.[^cite_note-14] In 2003, a new landscaped [I-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95_in_Florida) interchange was built to decrease traffic on Southern Boulevard ([US 98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_98_in_Florida)) extending Turnage Boulevard (the road around the perimeter of the concourse).

Competition from rapidly expanding [Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale-Hollywood_International_Airport) cut growth at the airport in the 1990s.&#x20;

##### 21st century



Palm Beach International Airport logo before 2026

The 2001 recession and the September 11 terrorist attacks further inhibited growth, but development in South Florida since 2002 has finally led to a surge of passenger traffic at the airport.[^cite_note-assets.simpleviewinc.com-15] In addition, discount carriers such as [JetBlue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetBlue) and [Southwest Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Airlines) began service to PBI. In 2006, the county embarked on an interim expansion program by breaking ground on a 7-story parking garage and the addition of 3 gates in Concourse C. Long range expansions include gates at Concourse B and the eventual construction of a new 14 gate Concourse D to be extended east from the present terminal.[^cite_note-assets.simpleviewinc.com-15]

[Donald Trump](4848272.md) sued to block the expansion of one of the runways at PBI in 2010.[^cite_note-16] In 2015, he initiated a \$100 million lawsuit over the flight path that passes over his [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md) estate.[^cite_note-17] After his win in the [2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election) ensured some type of [no fly zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_fly_zone) over his property, he dropped the lawsuit.[^cite_note-18] During [Trump's first presidential term](52231341.md), Air Force One again became a frequent visitor to PBI, typically parking on the south side of the airport near Southern Boulevard.[^cite_note-19] Until 2017, a line of [school buses](633072.md) was used as a temporary barrier between the aircraft and onlookers.[^cite_note-20] Temporary security fences began being used requiring assembly and disassembling.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

#### Renaming after Donald Trump, 2026



Entrance sign for President Donald J. Trump International Airport

In February 2026, lawmakers in the [Florida legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_legislature) approved a bill to rename the airport to "President Donald J. Trump International Airport" by a vote of 25 to 11 in the Florida Senate and 81 to 30 in the Florida House of Representatives.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Florida governor Ron DeSantis signed the bill into law in March 2026,[^cite_note-desantis-signature-25] though the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) needed to update its charts and databases for the change of name to take full effect.[^cite_note-26] [The Trump Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trump_Organization) filed to [trademark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trademark) the airport's new name.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] This marked the first time a United States airport was named after an incumbent president.[^cite_note-29] The renaming took effect on July 9, 2026. [Trump Force One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_Force_One), a Boeing 757 owned by the Trump Organization, was the first plane to land after the renaming.[^cite_note-Guilfoil_NBC-5]

## Facilities



2006 panorama of Palm Beach International Airport from what was the 391st Bomb Group Restaurant off of [Southern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Boulevard_\(Palm_Beach_County,_Florida\))

### Runways and taxiways

The airport covers 2,120 acres (858 ha) and has three [runways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway):[^cite_note-FAA-1][^cite_note-30]

- 10L/28R: 10,001 x 150 ft. (3,048 x 46 m), asphalt

- 10R/28L: 3,214 x 75 ft. (980 x 23 m), asphalt

- 14/32: 6,931 x 150 ft. (2,113 x 46 m), asphalt

The airport's runway designations were changed by the Federal Aviation Administration to their current configuration on December 17, 2009. Previously, they had been 09L/27R, 09R/27L, and 13/31.[^cite_note-31]

### Control tower

A 240-foot (73 m) Airport Traffic Control tower is active on the north side of the airport – west of concourse A, off Belvedere Road – along with a single-story, 9,000-square-foot (840 m<sup>2</sup>) ATBM Base Building.\[^cite_note-[www.pcl.com-36](http://www.pcl.com-36/)] The current tower replaced the previous one, which was located on the southern side of the airport.[^cite_note-assets.simpleviewinc.com-15] The old tower was eventually demolished after the new tower came into service.\[^cite_note-[www.pcl.com-36](http://www.pcl.com-36/)]

### Fire protection and emergency medical services



One of the airport's [crash response trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_crash_tender) in 2008

The [Palm Beach County Fire Rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_County_Fire_Rescue) Aviation Battalion is located between the runways at PBI. The fire station, which is located near the center of the airport grounds, is home to 13 pieces of specialized fire fighting equipment.[^cite_note-37]

These apparatus include:

- An [air stair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_support_equipment#Passenger_boarding_steps/stairs) for emergency deplanings

  [^cite_note-38]

- Five [airport crash tenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_crash_tender), with call sign *Dragon* (Dragon 1, Dragon 2, etc.)

  [^cite_note-39]

- A foam unit that carries [Purple-K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple-K) concentrate to assist with extinguishing a fire

  [^cite_note-40]

- A [heavy rescue vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_rescue_vehicle) that carries additional tools for [plane crashes](164536.md) and other [mass-casualty incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass-casualty_incident)

  [^cite_note-41]

### Trauma Hawk

Main article: [Trauma Hawk Aero-Medical Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trauma_Hawk_Aero-Medical_Program)



Trauma Hawk 1 at its hangar at Palm Beach International Airport in 2006

The Trauma Hawk Station, which is located at the south west corner of the airport, [Palm Beach County Fire Rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_County_Fire_Rescue) has two [Sikorsky S-76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_S-76)C helicopters.[^cite_note-station-th-42] The department partners with the Palm Beach County Health Care District to operate the [Trauma Hawk Aero-Medical Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trauma_Hawk_Aero-Medical_Program).[^cite_note-traumahawk-43] The Trauma Hawk program, which was established in November 1990, replaced the use of [Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_County_Sheriff's_Office) helicopters to [medevac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medevac) critically injured patients to area hospitals.[^cite_note-traumahawk-43] [Air ambulances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_ambulance) are identically equipped and can carry two patients each and up to four medical attendants if needed.[^cite_note-station-th-42] Each helicopter is staffed with a pilot, a [registered nurse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registered_nurse) (RN) and a [paramedic](75006.md). The nurses and paramedics are Palm Beach County Fire Rescue employees while the pilots are Health Care District employees.[^cite_note-traumahawk-43]

## Ground transportation

### Car

[Interstate 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95_in_Florida), [Southern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Boulevard_\(Palm_Beach_County,_Florida\)), and [Congress Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Road_807) all serve – and provide direct vehicular access to – the airport.\[^cite_note-[www.pbia.org-2-99](http://www.pbia.org-2-99/)][^cite_note-100]

### Public transportation

#### Bus

[Palm Tran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Tran) bus routes 2 and 44 serve the airport.\[^cite_note-[www.pbia.org-2-99](http://www.pbia.org-2-99/)] Both provide connections to the [Amtrak/Tri-Rail West Palm Beach train station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Palm_Beach_station); the station is also served by [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) buses.\[^cite_note-[www.pbia.org-2-99\]\[^cite_note-www.pbia.org-3-101](http://www.pbia.org-2-99]\[^cite_note-www.pbia.org-3-101)]

#### Rail

The airport is near the [West Palm Beach Brightline Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Palm_Beach_station_\(Brightline\)) (served by [Brightline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brightline)) and the [West Palm Beach Amtrak/Tri-Rail station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Palm_Beach_station) (served by [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) intercity trains and [Tri-Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-Rail) commuter trains). A Palm Tran shuttle bus service connects the latter station with the airport.[^cite_note-www\.pbia.org-2-99][^cite_note-www\.pbia.org-3-101]

## Accidents and incidents

- On August 21, 1956, a USAF [Douglas C-124C Globemaster II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-124C_Globemaster_II) crashed during its initial climb at then [Palm Beach Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_Air_Force_Base) when a prop cuff came off an engine and went through the fuselage, cutting control cables, the aircraft banked to the right and crashed into a tree nursery; three out of the six occupants were killed.

  [^cite_note-102]

- On November 11, 2010, a [Piper PA-44 Seminole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_PA-44_Seminole) flying from Palm Beach International Airport to [Melbourne Orlando International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Orlando_International_Airport) crashed on a taxiway after an engine failed during takeoff. The plane was operated by [Florida Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Institute_of_Technology)'s College of Aeronautics and all four aboard—two FIT flight students, a flight instructor, and a passenger—were killed.

  [^cite_note-103]
