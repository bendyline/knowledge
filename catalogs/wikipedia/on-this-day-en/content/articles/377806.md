**Diyala Governorate** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): محافظة ديالى *_Muḥāfaẓat Diyālā_*) or **Diyala Province** is a [governorate](222800.md) in northeastern [Iraq](7515928.md).

## Provincial Government

- Governor: Adnan al-Shimmari

  [^cite_note-3]

- Deputy Governor: Mustafa al-Luhaybi

- Provincial Council Chairman: Omar al-Kirwi

## Geography



[Diyala river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyala_River)

Diyala Governorate extends to the northeast of [Baghdad](4492.md) as far as the [Iranian border](10282111.md). Its [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) is [Baqubah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baqubah). It covers an area of 17,685 square kilometres (6,828 sq mi).

A large portion of the province is drained by the [Diyala River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyala_River), a major tributary of the [Tigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris). Because of its proximity to two major sources of water, Diyala's main industry is agriculture, primarily [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_dactylifera) grown in large groves. The province also contains one of the largest [olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) groves in the Middle East.[^cite_note-4] It is also recognized as the [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)) capital of the Middle East. The [Hamrin Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamrin_Mountains) pass through the governorate.

## Population

The governorate is home to a diverse population of [Arabs](2185.md), [Kurds](17068.md) and [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Turkmen), though the clear majority are [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [Arabs](2185.md), constituting around 60% of the provinces population.[^cite_note-5] According to the latest statistics, the number of inhabitants is approximately 1.6 million.[^cite_note-6]

## Culture

### Sports

Diyala is home to [Diyala SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyala_SC) that plays in [Iraq Stars League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Stars_League), the highest division of the Iraqi football league system, following promotion from [Iraqi Premier Division League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Premier_Division_League) in 2024. The club is based in [Baqubah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baqubah) and [Ba'quba Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'quba_Stadium) is its home stadium.

## Infrastructure

The Diyala Province boasts the Diyala Media Center which has one of the Middle East's tallest radio and television antennas at 349 metres (1,047 ft). The Diyala Media Center was built under contract by a Japanese architectural firm in 1989. It is one of Iraq's few independent radio and television stations that offer local television and radio news coverage as well as rebroadcasting state-run television.

## Civil unrest/Iraq War

There is evidence that [Al-Qaeda in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_Iraq) moved its base of operations from [Anbar](467036.md) province to Diyala in 2006 and during late 2006, Baqubah and majority of the Diyala province were reported to have come under Sunni insurgent control.[^cite_note-Engel-9] This insurgent control is reported to have continued through 2007 and into early 2008.[^cite_note-Engel-2007-01-17-10]

On May 11, 2007, Army Maj. Gen. [Benjamin Mixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Mixon), commander of the [Multi-National Division North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War_order_of_battle#Multinational_Force_Iraq:_complete_current_order_of_battle) said he needed more troops in order to contain the current level of violence in the Diyala province, this coming in the recent wake of a [troop "surge"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War_troop_surge_of_2007), [involuntary recalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Ready_Reserve#Activations_in_the_Global_War_on_Terror) by the U.S. military, and the [public debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Study_Group) about the [level of commitment from the U.S. government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Force_Iraq#Multi_National_Force_Iraq).[^cite_note-DoD-transcript-11] By mid-2007 the [Islamic State of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_of_Iraq), already holding Baqubah and most of the province under its control, declared its capital to be [Baqubah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baqubah). The Sunni insurgent group [Jamaat Ansar al-Sunna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat_Ansar_al-Sunna) was also active in the region around this time.

In June 2007, US forces launched [Operation Arrowhead Ripper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrowhead_ripper) with night air assaults in [Baqubah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baqubah). By August 19, Baqubah was largely secured, although some insurgent presence remained in the city and surrounding areas. Fighting continued in the [Diyala River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyala_River) valley but by the beginning of October, US and Iraqi forces held most of the province while the insurgents were in retreat to the north and west. On October 27 the Islamic State of Iraq attacked a police base in Baqubah, killing 28 Iraqi policemen and police recruits, showing that insurgent cells still remain in the province.

In January 2008 [Operation Phantom Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Phantom_Phoenix) was launched in an attempt to eradicate the remaining insurgents following the [Diyala campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyala_campaign) between 2006 and 2007.

Mid-2008 saw many changes in Diyala province with an increased effort by U.S. Forces and a substantial Iraqi Army presence, and in the Baqubah region, [Islamic State of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_of_Iraq)'s activity was dramatically hampered, and the [Sons of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_Iraq) program served only to further weaken Islamic State in Iraq.

### Declaration of autonomy

In December 2011, the governing council in Diyala province declared itself a semi-autonomous region within Iraq.[^cite_note-12] This comes two months after [Saladin Governorate](200537.md) made a similar declaration. The council in Diyala, using [Article 119 of the Iraqi Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Iraq#Regions) as justification, made the declaration because of suspicion of the [Shi'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'a)-dominated government of [Prime Minister](208879.md) [Nouri al-Maliki](4723094.md). Unlike [Salah ad Din province](200537.md) however, Diyala province is more ethnically and religiously mixed, and such an announcement led to the outbreak of protests in the province.[^cite_note-13]
