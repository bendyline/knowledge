**Galle** ([Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language): ගාල්ල, romanized: *Gālla*;[^cite_note-1] [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language): காலி, romanized: *Kāli* [^cite_note-2])[^cite_note-4] is a major city on the southwestern tip of [Sri Lanka](26750.md), 119 km (74 mi) south of [Colombo](56636.md). Galle is the provincial capital and largest city of [Southern Province, Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Province,_Sri_Lanka) and is the capital of [Galle District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_District).

Galle was known as *Gimhathiththa*[^cite_note-5] before the arrival of the [Portuguese](23033.md) in the 16th century, when it was the main port on the island. [Ibn Batuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Batuta), a [Moroccan](19291.md) [Berber Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_Muslims) traveller in the 14th century, referred to it as *Qali*.[^cite_note-6] Galle reached the height of its development in the 18th century, during the [Dutch colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_period_in_Ceylon). Galle is the best example of a fortified city built by the [Portuguese](23033.md) in South and Southeast [Asia](689.md), showing the interaction between [Portuguese](23033.md) [architectural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural) styles and native traditions. The city was extensively fortified by the Dutch during the 17th century from 1649 onwards. The [Galle fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_fort) is a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) and is the largest remaining [fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress) in Asia built by European occupiers.

Other prominent [landmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmark) in Galle include the [city's natural harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Harbour), the [National Maritime Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Maritime_Museum_\(Galle\)), [St. Mary's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary's_Cathedral,_Galle) founded by [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) [priests](23707.md), one of the main [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) temples on the island, and [Amangalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amangalla), the historic luxury hotel. On 26 December 2004, the city was devastated by the massive tsunami caused by the [2004 Indian Ocean earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake), which occurred off the coast of [Indonesia](14579.md) a thousand miles away. Thousands were killed in the city alone. Galle is home to the [Galle International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_International_Stadium), which is considered to be one of the most picturesque [cricket](25675557.md) grounds in the world.[^cite_note-7] The ground, which was severely damaged by the tsunami, was rebuilt and [test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_Cricket) matches resumed there on 18 December 2007.

Important natural geographical features in Galle include Rumassala in [Unawatuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unawatuna), a large mound-like hill that forms the eastern protective barrier to [Galle Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Harbour). Local tradition associates this hill with some events of [Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana), one of the great Hindu epics. The major river in the area is the [Gin Ganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gin_Ganga), which begins from Gongala Kanda, passes villages such as Neluwa, Nagoda, [Baddegama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baddegama), [Thelikada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thelikada) and Wakwella, and reaches the sea at [Gintota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gintota). The river is bridged at Wakwella by the [Wakwella Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakwella_Bridge).

## Etymology

Galle was known as *Gimhathitha* in ancient times. The term is believed to be derived from the classical [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language) term meaning "port near the River Gin". It is believed that the town got its name as *Gaalla* in the native tongue as a result of the large number of bullock carts that took shelter in the area, following the long slow journeys from remote areas of the island. *Gaala* in Sinhala means the place where cattle are herded together; hence the Sinhalese name for Galle, ගාල්ල, is a development from *Gaala*.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Another theory is that the word *Galle* is derived from the Latin word *gallus* or the Portuguese word *galo*, which mean '[rooster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooster)'. The Dutch used the rooster as a symbol of Galle.

## History

According to [James Emerson Tennent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Emerson_Tennent) (Colonial Secretary of Ceylon) Galle was the ancient seaport of [Tarshish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarshish),[^cite_note-tarshish-10] from which [King Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Solomon) drew [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory), [peacocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock) and other valuables. [Cinnamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon) was exported from Sri Lanka as early as 1400 BC, and as the root of the word itself is [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language), Galle may have been a main [entrepôt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrep%C3%B4t) for the spice.[^cite_note-11]



Wesleyan Methodist Chapel, 1868



The port of Galle in 1754

Ancient Greek and Roman geographers may have known about Galle, which they might have called the Cape of Birds. Ptolemy might also have known about the port which he referred to as Odoka. Moroccan traveller Ibn Battuta visited Galle (or Qali as he called it) in 1342. During the 12th and 13th centuries, Sinhalese refugees fleeing Tamil armies from the north began to settle in Galle and other nearby areas.[^cite_note-12]

Galle had been a prominent seaport long before western rule in the country: [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people), [Arabs](2185.md), [Greeks](42056.md), [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)), [Indians](14533.md), and [Chinese](5405.md) were doing business through Galle's port. In 1411, the [Galle Trilingual Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Trilingual_Inscription), a stone tablet inscription in three languages, [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language), was erected in Galle to commemorate [the second visit to Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_treasure_voyages) by the Chinese admiral [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He). In 1502, a small fleet of [Portuguese](23033.md) ships, under the command of [Lourenço de Almeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louren%C3%A7o_de_Almeida) on their way to the [Maldives](19117.md), were blown off course by a storm. Realising that the [King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kotte#History) resided in [Kotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Jayawardenepura_Kotte) close to [Colombo](56636.md), Lourenço proceeded there after a brief stop in Galle.

In 1640, the [Portuguese were forced to surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Galle_\(1640\)) to the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company). The Dutch built the present [fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Fort) in 1663. They built a fortified solid granite wall and three bastions, known as "Sun", "Moon" and "Star".

After the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Ceylon) took over the country from the Dutch in 1796, they preserved the fort unchanged and used it as the administrative centre of the district.

## Climate

Galle features a [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate). The city has no true [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season), though it is noticeably drier in the months of January and February. As is commonplace with many cities with this type of climate, temperatures show little variation throughout the course of the year, with average temperatures hovering at around 27 °C (81 °F) throughout.

## Governance

The [Galle Municipal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Municipal_Council) governs the City of Galle, established under the Municipalities Ordinance of 1865. It was at the time, only the third municipal council in the country. The first mayor of the city, [Wijeyananda Dahanayake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wijeyananda_Dahanayake), was appointed in 1939; he later became the fifth [Prime Minister of Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Ceylon).

The last appointed mayor was Methsiri De Silva, who served from 2009 to 2016. The mayoral system has been dissolved the Galle administration, with the city presently administered by a commissioner. The main vision of the city is "Building of moderate city through the supply of relatively increased utility services to the citizens who pay taxes to the Galle Municipal Council". The other vision is to brand Galle as "Green City-Green Galle" to create and promote Galle as one of Sri Lanka's cool and healthy coastal cities with a clean green canopy.[^cite_note-16]

## Demographics

Galle is a sizeable city by Sri Lankan standards, and has a population of 101,749, the majority of whom are of [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people) ethnicity, with a large population of [Sri Lankan Moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Moor) (Muslims), particularly in the fort area, who descend from [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) merchants that settled in the ancient port of Galle and married [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people) women. Galle is also notable for its foreign population, both residents and owners of holiday homes. Religious composition in Galle DS division according to 2024 census is as follows Buddhist-67502-62.31%, Islam-39021 -36.02%, Roman Catholic-774-0.71%, Hindu-661-0.61%, Other Christian-355-0.31%, Others-20-0.02%.[^cite_note-17]

### Universities



Faculty of Engineering, Galle

Three main faculties of the [University of Ruhuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ruhuna) are located in Galle. The [Faculty of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_Of_Engineering_Ruhuna) is located at Hapugala, about 6 km from the city center. The Faculty of Medicine is located at Karapitiya near the Karapitiya Teaching Hospital. The Faculty of Allied Health Sciences of the University of Ruhuna is located in Uluwitike, in the city limits. A study centre of the [Open University of Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University_of_Sri_Lanka) is also located in Galle, at Labuduwa junction.

### Higher education institutes



Advanced Technological Institute, Labuduwa



NIBM, Galle

The Advanced Technological Institute in [Labuduwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labuduwa), Galle was started in 2000, and it planned to offer Higher National Diploma in Information Technology and Higher National Diploma in Agriculture Technology – HNDT (Agri). Since then, Labuduwa ATI has been a pioneer in technological education in Sri Lanka. The [National Institute of Business Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Business_Management_\(Sri_Lanka\)) in Galle was established in 2010 to provide higher education opportunities in the fields of information technology and business management. The Ruhunu National College of Education, operated under the auspices of the Ministry of Education, trains teachers training for government schools in Kurunduwatta, Galle. Additionally, the Amarasuriya Teachers' Training College for government school teachers is in Unawatuna, Galle.

## Transport



Southern Expressway



[Galle Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Railway_Station)

Galle is served by [Sri Lanka Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Railways)' [Coastal Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Line_\(Sri_Lanka\)) and is connected by rail to Colombo and [Matara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matara,_Sri_Lanka). [Galle Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galle_Railway_Station) is a major station on the line and serves as the meeting point of the west- and south-coast segments of the line.[^cite_note-18] The [A2 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2_highway_\(Sri_Lanka\)), which is commonly known as the Galle Road, runs through the city and connects Galle to Colombo by the west-coast portion, and to [Hambanthota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hambanthota) by the south-coast portion. The [Southern Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Expressway_\(Sri_Lanka\)), Sri Lanka's first E Class highway, links the Sri Lankan capital Colombo with Galle and currently reduces the time spent for travel to one hour from the three hours taken by the regular A2 highway.
