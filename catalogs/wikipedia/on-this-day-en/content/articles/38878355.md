[Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_resources_and_consumption) in [Serbia](29265.md) is dominated by [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel), despite the public preference for [renewable energy](25784.md).[^cite_note-1]

In 2021 Serbia's total [energy supply](5260053.md) was almost 700 [PJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petajoule), with the energy mix comprising coal (45%), oil (24%), gas (15%), and renewables (16%). Bioenergy and hydroelectric power were the leading contributors within the renewable energy category, accounting for 67% and 29% of the renewable supply, respectively.

## History

On 6 October 1893, the first Serbian power plant, located in the [Dorćol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dor%C4%87ol) urban neighborhood of Belgrade, began production of electricity.[^cite_note-2]

In 1900, the first [alternating current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_current) hydroelectric power plant *Pod gradom* in [Užice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%C5%BEice) on the river Đetinja went online. The first alternating current transmission line from hydroelectric power plant *Vučje* to [Leskovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leskovac), with the length of 17 kilometres (11 mi), went online three years later.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] In 1909, hydroelectric plants *Gamzigrad* in [Zaječar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaje%C4%8Dar) and *Sveta Petka* in [Niš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C5%A1) began to build.[^cite_note-5] Two years later, the hydroelectric power station on the river Moravica in [Ivanjica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivanjica) was put in the operation.[^cite_note-6]

In Belgrade, the power plant *Snaga i Svetlost* was built in 1933, being one of the largest in the Balkans at that time.

The establishment of the *Električno preduzeće Srbije* followed in 1945. Between 1947 and 1950, the hydroelectric power plant *Sokolovica* and coal power plants *Mali Kostolac* and *Veliki Kostolac*, the first power stations to be built in Serbia after the Second World War.[^cite_note-7] In 1952, the underground mining of the coal field *Kolubara* had started. Four years later, coal power plant [RB Kolubara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RB_Kolubara) went in operation. A year earlier, the hydroelectric power plants *Vlasina* and *Zvornik* have been connected to the power grid. In the period from 1960 to 1967, hydroelectric power plants *Bistrica*, *Kokin Brod* and *Potpeć* were under construction.

In the period from 1942 to 1943, Serbia conducted the first exploration potential of oil field. The first drill hole was made in 1947 in [Banat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat) (Velika Greda), and in 1949 founded the company *Naftagas*.



[Đerdap II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_II_Hydroelectric_Power_Station) (Serbia)

In 1965, *Združeno elektroprivredno preduzeće Srbije* was founded. The coal-fired power plant *Bajina Bašta* began with the production of electricity a year later. The two largest power plants in Serbia, the hydroelectric power plant [HPP Đerdap I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_I_Hydroelectric_Power_Station) at the Danube river and the coal power plant [TENT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TPP_Nikola_Tesla), went into operation in 1970. Twelve years later, the pumped storage plant *Bajina Bašta* was built, and in 1990 the hydroelectric power station *Pirot* was put into operation.

In 2010 the framework agreement for Kostolac B3 was signed with the contractor, [China Machinery Engineering Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Machinery_Engineering_Corporation) (CMEC). Construction started in 2017.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

In late 2020, the [Balkan Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Stream), a pipeline running from Russia to Serbia through Bulgaria and Turkey (considered an extension of [TurkStream](45206475.md)), completed construction to deliver natural gas.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] However, the Balkan Stream came with political troubles for Bulgaria.[^cite_note-11] In order to diversify its gas supplies, another [interconnector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interconnector) and pipeline through Bulgaria was constructed in December 2023, transporting natural gas from Azerbaijan to Serbia.[^cite_note-12]

In December 2024 Thermal power plant B3 in Kostolac was officially handed over to EPS.[^cite_note-13] Kostolac B3 has 350 MW capacity with a net efficiency level of 37.3%, and it's the newest thermal power plant in Serbia with the last one Kostolac B2 being built in 1991.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

## Electricity

### Generation



Serbia electricity production by source

The main producer and supplier of electricity in Serbia is state-owned [Elektroprivreda Srbije](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektroprivreda_Srbije) (EPS). The company has an installed capacity of 7,662 [MW](21347693.md) and generates 38.9 [TWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWh) of [electricity](9550.md) per year. Its installed capacity in [lignite-fired thermal power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_power_station) is 4,390 MW, gas-fired and liquid fuel-fired [combined heat and power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_heat_and_power_plant) is 336 MW, and [hydropower plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydropower_plant) is 2,936 MW.[^cite_note-14]

EPS is also the largest producer of [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite) in [Serbia](29265.md) operating in the [Kolubara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolubara) and [Kostolac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostolac) basins, producing around 37 million tonnes per year.[^cite_note-15] Also, 85 MW is generated from [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), mainly through facilities of [MK Fintel Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MK_Fintel_Wind).

Shifting from coal to renewable sources, and therefore the need for new hydro, wind and solar generation plants, is a recognized goal in the Serbian politics. In July 2021, the country's energy and mining minister stated in an interview that the new energy strategy was under preparation, under it up to 50% electricity would be generated from renewable sources by 2040;[^cite_note-21] later in November 2021 she said in another interview that "by 2040 or 2050 (Serbia) should have zero lignite (consumption)" and that such shift would require minimum 17 billion euro investment.[^cite_note-22]

### Transmission and distribution

While Elektroprivreda Srbije (EPS) is responsible for mining and [electricity generation](9540.md), [Elektromreža Srbije](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektromre%C5%BEa_Srbije) (EMS) is responsible for the [electric power transmission](38824.md), and [Elektrodistribucija Srbije](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektrodistribucija_Srbije) (ED) for the [electric power distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_distribution).

### Laws

In order to increase the efficiency of the sector through the action of market mechanisms in the production and supply of electricity, the Government of Serbia has introduced competition in the electricity sector by adopting the Law on Energy in 2004. All electricity consumers are tariff buyers which are, according to the law, provided by electricity retailer responsible to supply tariff customers within [Elektroprivreda Srbije](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektroprivreda_Srbije) at regulated prices. At the same time, buyers who meet the criteria according to the Act are given the opportunity to become a qualified buyers, and thus get the opportunity to purchase electricity on the open market. In the first phase, the electricity market has been open to all potential customers with an annual electricity consumption was above 25 GWh. From 1 January 2007, the Council of the Energy Agency of the Republic of Serbia made decision that the right to acquire the status of an eligible customer is available to all electricity customers with an annual consumption of more than 3 GWh.

## Oil and natural gas



[NIS](7562798.md) refinery in [Pančevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan%C4%8Devo)

[Naftna Industrija Srbije](7562798.md) (NIS) is the only company in Serbia which deals with exploration and production of crude oil and gas, as well as with production of geothermal energy.[^cite_note-23] The company disposes with all necessary equipment for the performance of a whole range of complex activities such as geophysical exploration, control of production of crude oil, gas and geothermal energy. The majority of NIS oil fields are located on the territory of Serbia, in [Banat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat) region, but [upstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstream_\(petroleum_industry\)) has business operations both in Serbia and abroad. In 2011 NIS started to expand business in Southeast Europe: in [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), [Romania](25445.md) and [Hungary](13275.md).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

The company owns and operates oil refineries in [Pančevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan%C4%8Devo) (annual capacity 4.8 million tons of crude oil) and Novi Sad (annual capacity 2.6 million tons of crude oil), and natural gas refinery in [Elemir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elemir). NIS refining complex produces a whole range of petroleum products – from motor [gasoline](23639.md) and [diesel fuel](81761.md) to mechanical [lube oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lube_oil) and feedstock for the [petrochemical industry](2293201.md), heavy fuel oil, road and industrial [bitumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitumen), etc.

[Srbijagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srbijagas), public gas company, operates the natural gas transportation system which comprise 3,177 kilometers of trunk and regional natural gas pipelines and a 450 million cubic meter underground gas storage facility at [Banatski Dvor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banatski_Dvor).[^cite_note-27]

## Renewable energy

See also: [Wind power in Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_Serbia)

Bioenergy and hydroelectric power were the leading contributors within the renewable energy category, accounting for 67% and 29% of the renewable supply, respectively.[^cite_note-31] Installed capacity of [hydropower](381399.md) is 2,835 MW and as of December 2019 [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) capacity is 500 MW.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-\:0-33] Serbia also makes use of geothermal and solar energy, currently 27% of Serbia's electricity comes from hydro while 4% comes from other renewables.[^cite_note-Serbia-28] Additional 600 MW of wind capacity is planned by 2030.[^cite_note-\:0-33]

As of 20 November 2024, Serbia's installed capacity of electricity from solar sources was estimated to be 166 MW.[^cite_note-34]
