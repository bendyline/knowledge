On the morning of 26 August 2026, a series of [flash floods](244391.md) and [debris flows](4415356.md) destroyed the [Gyirong Port](79134779.md) on the [China–Nepal border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Nepal_border) and struck dozens of settlements along a 72 km (45 mi)[^cite_note-10] stretch of the [Trishuli River](27394714.md)[^cite_note-Bhotekoshi-11] in [Nepal](171166.md). These events are also known as the **2026 Nepal-China floods**,[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] **Bhotekoshi flood**,[^cite_note-\:25-14][^cite_note-15] or **Rasuwa flood**.[^cite_note-\:26-16][^cite_note-\:27-17]

At least 1,454 deaths and 4,718 missing persons in Nepal, and 43 deaths and 519 missing in the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) of China were reported.[^cite_note-\:43-3][^cite_note-\:24-4] The flood carried the bodies of some victims 240 km (150 mi) away into neighbouring [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-\:35-18]

The precipitating geological event, initially reported as a [glacier collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier_collapse) in the [Langtang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang) region of the [Himalayas](37966.md), caused a chain reaction whose tremors were detected by [seismometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismometer) worldwide, with magnitudes reaching [M<sub>s</sub>](6207163.md#Ms) 5.2.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] According to experts at the [National Society for Earthquake Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Society_for_Earthquake_Technology)–Nepal (NSET), the [University of Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Reading) and [Newcastle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_University), the flood was triggered by a large [rock-ice avalanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock-ice_avalanche) originating from the [Lirung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang_Lirung) massif, involving glacier ice and rock, which transformed into a highly mobile, long-runout [debris flow](4415356.md) and generated destructive downstream flooding.[^cite_note-NSET-21][^cite_note-Reading-22][^cite_note-Newcastle-23]

## Background

### Geography and history

The [Trishuli River](27394714.md)[^cite_note-Bhotekoshi-11] is joined by the Lhende Khola (also known as Lende River or *Donglin Tsangpo* in [Lhasa Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhasa_Tibetan)), which originates in [Gyirong County](11219645.md), [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) before entering Nepal. It then flows past [Dhunche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhunche), downstream from which it forms part of the Trishuli River system.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

The [flash floods](244391.md) and [mudslides](2431323.md) occurred around the [China–Nepal border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Nepal_border) crossing at [Gyirong Port](79134779.md), a strategic border crossing connecting Nepal's Rasuwa District and Gyirong County, located at the confluence of the Trishuli and Lhende Khola rivers. The crossing saw large amounts of daily traffic related to both commerce and tourism, especially the [pilgrimage](23763.md) tour to [Mount Kailash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kailash), a holy site in [Bon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon), [Buddhism](3267529.md), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) and [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism).[^cite_note-26] The Gyirong crossing accounted for 30% of all Nepal-China trade before the disaster, and more than 100,000 people had used the crossing in the first half of 2026 according to Chinese media.[^cite_note-\:15-27]

Since 1900, dozens of floods have occurred near the border between Nepal and China.[^cite_note-Joselow-2026-28] [Langtang Lirung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang_Lirung), opposite the site of the 2026 landslide, experienced a major [glacier collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier_collapse) and debris avalanche triggered by the [April](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2015_Nepal_earthquake) and [May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2015_Nepal_earthquake) 2015 Nepal earthquakes. The landslide in the [Langtang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang) valley destroyed and buried several villages, killing over 350 people.[^cite_note-Lacroix16-29] A [July 2025 flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Nepal_floods#Rasuwagadhi_flood) caused by a glacial lake outburst washed away the bridge at the border crossing.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-\:25-14]

Analysis of satellite imagery found that from 8 January 2026 to 18 August 2026, the glacier north of Langtang Lirung moved around 10 millimetres per month, but the rate accelerated weeks prior to the glacier's collapse.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

### Climate change

Further information: [Effects of climate change on the water cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_the_water_cycle)

See also: [Retreat of glaciers since 1850](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retreat_of_glaciers_since_1850)

[Climate change](5042951.md) contributes to the melting of permafrost, which in turn increases the likelihood of glacier collapses. Alton C. Byers, a senior research associate at the [Institute of Arctic and Alpine Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Arctic_and_Alpine_Research), [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder), said, "Permafrost has been the cryospheric glue that's held the rock and the ice and the glaciers together for millennia, and now we're getting increasing evidence that it's being weakened by warming trends."[^cite_note-Joselow-2026-28]

Several researchers quoted by *[The New York Times](30680.md)* pointed to more proximate causes and stopped short of attributing the disaster directly to climate change.[^cite_note-Joselow-2026-28] Researchers quoted by the BBC, CNN, and NPR said climate change probably increased the likelihood of the event.[^cite_note-15km-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-\:4-36] Nepal foreign minister [Shisir Khanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shisir_Khanal) called for increased global response to climate change and stated that Nepal was bearing a disproportionate share of its consequences.[^cite_note-\:2-37]

### Early warning systems

Chinese and Nepali scientists have maintained a [flash flood early warning system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_warning) for years, which the *[South China Morning Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Morning_Post)* credited with consistently preventing casualties.[^cite_note-38] The system did not detect the August 2026 flood in time as the water level monitors were configured for detecting [monsoon](57630.md) and perennial floods.[^cite_note-Sands-2026-39] Upstream monitoring stations in Nepal were swept away by the flooding before alerts could be sent.[^cite_note-DibyeshConversation-40] A senior fellow at the [Stimson Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimson_Center) remarked that the affected region was a monitoring "blind spot" and that geologists paid little attention to it.[^cite_note-Sands-2026-39]

After the 2025 floods, Chinese authorities in [Gyirong County](11219645.md) took a number of measures in preparation for a new [glacial lake outburst flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_lake_outburst_flood). The local government updated emergency plans, set up a sentry system monitoring water level changes, constructed [flood prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_prevention) structures such as a new flood wall, dikes and channels, trained local officials in "glacial lake outburst defense", and simulated an emergency evacuation of villagers. The August 2026 flood, which was caused by a different event than the system was built to anticipate, overwhelmed these defenses within minutes.[^cite_note-41]

The incident began at around 08\:37 [NPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_Standard_Time). At 09\:00, the Nepalese Department of Hydrology and Meteorology was informed of the flood by the District Administrative Officer in Rasuwa, by which time several communications towers had been destroyed and door-to-door warnings were being sent.[^cite_note-42] The first text alerts were sent out by 09\:15, with 679,295 warning text messages sent within one minute.[^cite_note-\:12-43] Authorities issued flood warnings for communities along rivers in the [Nepali districts](2918695.md) of [Rasuwa](2919378.md), [Nuwakot](2919311.md), [Dhading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhading_District), [Gorkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_District), and [Chitwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitwan_District).[^cite_note-44]

## Cause

Further information: [Timeline of the 2026 Nepal–Tibet floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_2026_Nepal%E2%80%93Tibet_floods)



A glacier collapse on the north face of [Langtang Lirung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang_Lirung), a mountain in [Langtang National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang_National_Park), Nepal, is widely thought to be the [proximate cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proximate_cause) of the floods (view from south-east).

No rain was reported on the morning of 26 August at the Rasuwa district headquarters.[^cite_note-45]

A seismic event measuring [M<sub>s</sub>](6207163.md#Ms) 5.2 occurred at 02\:52 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time) (08\:37 [NPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_Standard_Time); 10\:52 [BJT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_China)) and was centred just north of [Langtang Lirung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang_Lirung), a mountain in [Langtang National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang_National_Park).[^cite_note-46] The seismic event was initially believed to be an earthquake; later analysis by the [United States Geological Survey](23814944.md) (USGS) indicated that the seismic event did not precede the collapse but was rather generated by the collapse itself.[^cite_note-168_dead-47][^cite_note-\:38-48] The USGS' analysis found that the long-period [seismic waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismic_wave) were consistent with a [glacier collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier_collapse).[^cite_note-49] The [GFZ Helmholtz Centre for Geosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GFZ_Helmholtz_Centre_for_Geosciences) (GFZ), in [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam), Germany, recorded an event measuring [M<sub>w</sub>](6207163.md#Mw) 5.7 that was consistent with a landslide with a timestamp identical to the USGS.[^cite_note-gfz1-1] Other early analyses suspected a [glacial lake outburst flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_lake_outburst_flood) (GLOF).[^cite_note-kathmandupost-50][^cite_note-51]

A second landslide event was identified by USGS three hours after the first by analyzing low-frequency seismic waves. The second landslide was recorded as a M<sub>s</sub> 4.2 seismic event.[^cite_note-52]

There were indications of heavy water flow almost immediately after the seismic waves were detected. The combination of the two near-simultaneous signals, and review of the first [satellite imagery](1184557.md), led researchers to conclude that a glacier located around 64 kilometres (40 mi) north of the capital city [Kathmandu](17168.md) and some 15 km (9.3 mi) east of where the flood was first noticed, measuring 600 metres (2,000 ft) in width may have fallen 1,200 metres (4,000 ft),[^cite_note-15km-33][^cite_note-60] in what is known as a glacier collapse (also called an [ice avalanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_avalanche)).[^cite_note-61] According to Daniel Shugar, a geologist at the [University of Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calgary) in Canada who reviewed satellite imagery after the event, a 0.2 km<sup>2</sup> (49-acre) piece of ice and rock broke off at about 5.2 km (3.2 mi) above sea level and fell vertically about 1.2 km (0.75 mi) to the valley below.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

Satellite images and [seismology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismology) confirm that a glacier collapsed near [Langtang Lirung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang_Lirung),[^cite_note-\:16-64] which itself is a likely result of destabilisation by tectonic uplift and climate warming.[^cite_note-65] Compared to GLOFs, glacier collapse floods are less frequent in the region, but more destructive.[^cite_note-66] The collapsed glacial material was believed to have originated from an altitude of 5,200–5,400 m (17,100–17,700 ft)[^cite_note-67] and had a size of around 197×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup>–492×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup> (7.0×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>9</sup>–17.4×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>9</sup> ft<sup>3</sup>).[^cite_note-\:16-64] It then moved north-westwards before shifting west.[^cite_note-15km-33]

The debris flowed up to some 100 km (62 mi) downstream.[^cite_note-15km-33]

### Risk of further flooding

Two [barrier lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_dam) formed following the glacier collapse.[^cite_note-68] On 27 August, Chinese authorities warned that one of the lakes with a volume of 2×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup> (71×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> ft<sup>3</sup>) had formed near the confluence of the Chhochen Khola and Purepu Tsangpo rivers, adding that the possibility of a breach was high due to an expected water inflow of 3×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup> (110×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> ft<sup>3</sup>) until 30 August.[^cite_note-69] The lake overflowed slightly on 28 August, prompting Chinese authorities to briefly pause rescue operations and warn about the potential for additional flooding.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] The barrier lake slowly drained out and was largely empty by 30 August.[^cite_note-\:2-37] The other lake was situated at a high elevation, near where the glacier collapsed.[^cite_note-72] Flood warning notices were sent on 30 August after Chinese authorities reported increased water flow upstream.[^cite_note-\:2-37]

## Casualties

[Surveillance](87179.md) footage showing floodwater destroying the backside of [Gyirong Port](79134779.md), looking upstream along the Trishuli River.

As of 26 September, at least 1,454 people died,[^cite_note-\:43-3] 9,287 were injured,[^cite_note-\:36-6] and 4,718 were missing in Nepal.[^cite_note-\:43-3] Among the missing were 636 foreign nationals from 35 countries.[^cite_note-\:42-73] According to the Ministry of Health on 1 September, just 4% of the roughly 900 bodies found so far had been identified.[^cite_note-74] The [National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority](84091405.md) (NDRRMA) stated on 2 September that nearly 1.6 million people had been affected by the floods.[^cite_note-1.6m-75]

As of 16 September, 43 people died and 519 were missing in Tibet.[^cite_note-\:24-4] Among the missing in Tibet include 261 foreign nationals from 23 countries.[^cite_note-\:17-76] This includes 104 Nepali nationals.[^cite_note-77]

Among the missing in Nepal include 189 Indians, 188 Nepali tourists, 65 Americans, 55 Malaysians, 54 Ukrainians, 37 Chinese, 33 Australians, 33 Latvians, 32 British, and 30 Canadians.[^cite_note-\:34-78][^cite_note-\:33-79] The [Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs](7453497.md) reported nearly 100 Chinese citizens in Nepal missing.[^cite_note-168_dead-47][^cite_note-80] Australia's foreign minister said on 27 August that at least 34 of the missing were [pilgrims](23763.md).[^cite_note-168_dead-47][^cite_note-81] A group of 77 [Isha Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isha_Foundation) pilgrims from 19 different nationalities traveling to Tibet were at Gyirong Port during the flash flood.[^cite_note-82] The [South Korean Foreign Ministry](11533941.md) reported 9 citizens working at a hydropower plant construction site went missing.[^cite_note-83]

At least 147 government officials, including eight local elected representatives, 32 local government employees, 11 teachers,[^cite_note-84] 25 police officers, 13 [Armed Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Police_Force) personnel, 45 [Nepali Army](12593188.md) personnel,[^cite_note-85] 15 customs office employees, and 4 immigration office employees were reported missing.[^cite_note-The_Himalayan_Times-2026a-86] Around 622 students were also unaccounted for.[^cite_note-87] An estimated 300 trade and transportation workers[^cite_note-\:6-88] and 200 workers in the tourism industry were reported missing.[^cite_note-\:31-89] Over 900 people involved in 12 hydropower projects were reported missing, with 500 believed to be trapped in tunnels.[^cite_note-Dervisevic-90][^cite_note-\:8-91] At the [Upper Trishuli 1 Hydroelectric Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Trishuli_1_Hydroelectric_Project), at least 576 workers were reported missing, with around 300 trapped inside the project's tunnel.[^cite_note-\:0-92] On September 7, the Nepal Army estimated that 121 people were trapped inside hydropower tunnels.[^cite_note-93]

[UNICEF](19698110.md) estimated that 32,021 children were impacted by the floods.[^cite_note-\:32-94] UN spokesperson [Stéphane Dujarric](10302459.md) stated that an estimated 2,600 children younger than 5 required treatment for severe [wasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasting) and 10,500 pregnant and breastfeeding women required nutrition assistance.[^cite_note-\:0-92] Around 540 children were separated from a parent and 102 children were orphaned by the floods.[^cite_note-\:32-94][^cite_note-95] [UN Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Women) estimated that 157,050 women and girls were impacted by the floods and 43,000 women and girls were in need of humanitarian aid.[^cite_note-96]

The flood carried the bodies of some victims 240 km (150 mi) away,[^cite_note-\:35-18] with bodies recovered as far downstream as [Nawalparasi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawalparasi_District) in Nepal[^cite_note-\:3-97] and [Kushinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushinagar_district) and [Maharajganj districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharajganj_district) in [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md), India.[^cite_note-BBCLive-98]

## Damage

Satellite imagery before and after the flooding. [Trishuli](27394714.md) (Bhotekoshi) is left, Lhende Khola at the top, Chhochen Khola top right, probable glacier scarp centre right, [Langtang Lirung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang_Lirung) bottom right.

It was estimated that the resulting debris traveled at a speed of around 52 m/s (190 km/h; 120 mph),[^cite_note-\:38-48] while analysis of satellite images suggests that discolorations caused by the flood reached an elevation of 800 m (2,600 ft) in a valley some 7 km (4.3 mi) from the origin.[^cite_note-108] Following the flood, more than 1.5 m (4 ft 11 in) of silt was deposited in the main disaster zone.[^cite_note-109] The [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme) estimated that the flooding generated 2.2 million tonnes of debris, including 556,200 tonnes of building debris.[^cite_note-110]

Floodwaters reached settlements as high as 80 metres (270 ft) above the river level.[^cite_note-Prasai-2026-111]

### Nepal

In Nepal, residential areas and infrastructure, including roads, sustained extensive damage.[^cite_note-RU26a-112][^cite_note-Ratopati-113] The districts of Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha, and Chitwan were impacted by the flooding.[^cite_note-114] Around 7,570 houses were destroyed,[^cite_note-\:19-115] along with seven health facilities and 30 cultural sites.[^cite_note-kpost1287-116][^cite_note-kpostdamage-117] In Nuwakot, floodwater entered areas including [Timure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timure), [Dhunche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhunche), [Mailung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mailung_Khola_Hydropower_Station), [Syafrubesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syaphru), [Betrawati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betrawati_Bazaar), [Trishuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishuli_Bazar), and [Devighat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devighat).[^cite_note-Prasai-2026-111][^cite_note-Ratopati-113][^cite_note-118] An estimate by the Nepal government reported that the floods caused over [रू](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_rupee) 277.79 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)1.8 billion) in damages and रू 136.27 billion (US\$883 million) in economic losses.[^cite_note-\:19-115] A Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment report estimated that rebuilding costs would be around रू 723.67 billion (US\$4.78 billion).[^cite_note-\:19-115] A report estimated that immediate relief and short-term reconstruction costs would be रू 8.74 billion and long-term reconstruction would cost रू 714.58 billion.[^cite_note-\:28-119]

The [Roads Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Roads_\(Nepal\)) estimated रू 30 billion in road and bridge infrastructure had been damaged,[^cite_note-\:20-120] impacting 68 [suspension bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_bridge),[^cite_note-kpost1287-116] 41 driveable bridges,[^cite_note-121] and 69 km (43 mi) of road.[^cite_note-kpostdamage-117] The government deemed 33 suspension bridges damaged beyond repair.[^cite_note-122] A road connecting Syafrubesi and [Rasuwagadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasuwa_Fort), part of the [Trishuli Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishuli_Corridor) (NH42), which was under reconstruction after damage from [flooding in 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Nepal_floods), was further damaged.[^cite_note-BBC_News_नेपाली-2026a-123] A bridge connected to [Trishuli Bazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishuli_Bazar) was swept away.[^cite_note-Dahal-2026-124] A section of the [Prithvi Highway](8203073.md) from [Baireni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baireni) to [Mugling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugling) was blocked until 28 August,[^cite_note-125] while another section of the highway between Mugling and Kathmandu was closed due to erosion from the Trishuli River.[^cite_note-126] Multiple villages lost road access due to the flooding, hampering relief distribution efforts by requiring air transport.[^cite_note-127] Helicopters were unable to reach the village of Tiru in Rasuwa district until 9 September, 15 days after the floods began.[^cite_note-128]

Around 1,806 ha (4,460 acres) of agricultural lands were damaged in the floods,[^cite_note-\:40-129] while a large number of [livestock](25160767.md) were killed.[^cite_note-130] The NDRRMA estimated that food production would fall by 7,686 tonnes.[^cite_note-\:40-129] Drinking water infrastructure was heavily damaged; testing of 15 water sources in [Bidur](16194374.md) found five were contaminated with [E.coli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.coli).[^cite_note-131] A 4.5 km (2.8 mi) section of a pipeline providing drinking water to the area was washed away, affecting 4,000 households, while 465 households in other areas were also affected by damage to water utilities.[^cite_note-kpostdamage-117] Four flood monitoring stations were destroyed.[^cite_note-\:12-43][^cite_note-132] Around 3,761 people across Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading were displaced.[^cite_note-kpost1386-133] 48 government buildings were damaged,[^cite_note-kpostdamage-117] including the rural municipality offices in [Gosaikunda Rural Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosaikunda_Rural_Municipality) and [Uttargaya Rural Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttargaya_Rural_Municipality) which were washed away.[^cite_note-134]

Eleven schools were destroyed and seven were damaged by the flooding, impacting over 9,000 students.[^cite_note-135] 17 bank branches and around 4,800 commercial establishments were also damaged,[^cite_note-kpostdamage-117] including 142 hotels and 60 restaurants.[^cite_note-\:31-89] The Rasuwagadhi customs office was destroyed,[^cite_note-Prasai-2026-111] with more than 300 vehicles queued up at the site swept away,[^cite_note-136] along with around 1,000 electric vehicles and containers with spare parts being exported into Nepal.[^cite_note-137] Around 900 delivery vehicles were damaged.[^cite_note-138] Over 400 [shipping containers](22849124.md), of which over 100 were loaded, were swept away in the flooding.[^cite_note-139] [Nepal Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_Telecom) said 120 of its towers had been affected, while [Ncell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ncell) said 78 towers had been affected[^cite_note-kpost1287-116] with losses of more than रू 90 million.[^cite_note-140] The [Ministry of Information and Communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_and_Communication_\(Nepal\)) estimated रू 88.51 billion in damages to the telecommunications sector.[^cite_note-141] The [Nepal Tourism Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_Tourism_Board) estimated रू 52.67 billion in losses in the tourism sector.[^cite_note-\:41-142]

The [Asian Development Bank](2512.md) reduced Nepal's annual fiscal growth forecast from 4.5% to 4.1% growth due to the floods.[^cite_note-\:41-142] The [International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization) estimated that the floods disrupted the employment of 21,400 people within 4 km (2.5 mi) of the flood footprint.[^cite_note-\:19-115]

#### Power infrastructure

Damage to electricity generation, transmission, and distribution infrastructure disrupted supplies in affected areas. 13 hydropower projects were damaged, causing an estimated रू 100 billion in damage.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-\:21-144] These included Rasuwagadhi, [Chilime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilime_Hydropower_Plant), Trishuli 3A, the Trishuli 3B Hub 220 [kV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilovolt) substation, [Trishuli Hydropower Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishuli_Hydropower_Station), and [Devighat Hydropower Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devighat_Hydropower_Station).[^cite_note-145] The NEA reported that damage to energy infrastructure had removed an estimated 431 megawatts of electricity off of the grid and that projects under construction with a total capacity of 470 megawatts were damaged.[^cite_note-Dev_Bhattarai-2026-146] Five [solar power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power_plants) with a capacity of 24 megawatts were damaged.[^cite_note-\:28-119] The [Ministry of Energy, Water Resources and Irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Energy,_Water_Resources_and_Irrigation) estimated रू 390.62 billion in rebuilding costs for the energy sector.[^cite_note-147]

Australian tunnel rescue expert [Arnold Dix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Dix), who was asked by Nepalese authorities to assist with rescue efforts at [Upper Trishuli 3A Hydropower Station](65895251.md), where 42 workers are reported missing, said that 27 power stations were "wiped out", with 12 "destroyed, \[with] nothing left at the surface".[^cite_note-Dervisevic-90]

### China

In China, road access, communications, and electricity supply to the [Gyirong Port](79134779.md) area were cut off by the flood,[^cite_note-Xinhua1-148][^cite_note-Sharma-2026-149] affecting a section of [China National Highway 216](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_216).[^cite_note-\:7-150] Chinese rescue workers surveilled the area on 28 August, with one describing the scene as "nothing but ruins".[^cite_note-151] Twenty-seven buildings were destroyed in the area.[^cite_note-152] Around 80 trucks and 170 containers were stranded in [the town of Gyirong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyirong_Town) after flooding destroyed about 5 km (3.1 mi) of road running from the town to Rasuwagadhi.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-\:6-88] Insurance firms estimated that the flood caused around [CN¥](66958.md) 450 million in damage.[^cite_note-Straits20260902-9]

Further disruptions occurred on 31 August, when Chinese authorities closed the [Tatopani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatopani,_Sindhupalchok) border crossing due to risks of landslides, leaving the [Korala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korala) border crossing in [Mustang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustang_District) as the only open thoroughfare across the [Nepal-China border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal-China_border).[^cite_note-154]

## Response

### Nepal

The [Government of Nepal](19233409.md) mobilised the [Nepali Army](12593188.md), [Armed Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Police_Force), and [Nepal Police](14833748.md), supported by helicopters and medical teams, to conduct search, rescue, and relief operations.[^cite_note-Onlinekhabar-155][^cite_note-156] More than 21,465 security personnel were deployed to assist rescue efforts, 9,287 personnel from the army, 7,912 from the police, and 4,203 from the APF.[^cite_note-kpost1355-157] [Bidur](16194374.md) hosted the primary [staging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staging_ground) [ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staging_ground) for the army's search and rescue operations.[^cite_note-158] The [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_Nepal) declared the area a disaster-hit zone for three months under the Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act, 2074 (2017).[^cite_note-The_Himalayan_Times-2026b-159] The government also offered free medical treatment for those injured and financial assistance to families who lost members.[^cite_note-\:27-17][^cite_note-The_Himalayan_Times-2026b-159] On 27 August, Prime Minister [Balen Shah](70833999.md) arrived in Nuwakot District to monitor the situation.[^cite_note-160] The Prime Minister's Natural Disaster Relief Fund was launched by the government, raising over [रू](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_rupee) 9.68 billion and [US\$](18717338.md)23.28 million in multiple accounts for a total of [रू](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_rupee) 13.23 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)86.69 million) by 11 September.[^cite_note-161] Shah and his cabinet also pledged to donate one month's salary to the fund.[^cite_note-162] [Tribhuvan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_University) donated रू 92.5 million and Kathmandu University donated रू 8.5 million towards rescue and relief efforts.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] The government also released रू 675 million to 15 local units in five affected districts from its Disaster Management Fund.[^cite_note-165] It also announced plans to establish a Scholarship Bank to provide free schooling up to the secondary level for children displaced or orphaned by the floods.[^cite_note-scholarship-166] The government announced that it would send रू 15,000 per month to families whose homes had been damaged.[^cite_note-167] The Nepal Electricity Administration pledged रू 50,000 and a subsistence allowance to the families of missing employees.[^cite_note-168]

Nepali officials reported that over 13,784 people had been rescued by 24 September.[^cite_note-\:19-115] Initial rescue efforts were hampered by heavy rain[^cite_note-169] and the absence of aviation assets by the Nepal Police.[^cite_note-170] By 9 September, 1,348 flights had been conducted as part of rescue and relief efforts.[^cite_note-\:23-171] As hospitals and morgues in affected areas reached full capacity, injured survivors and cadavers were transferred to other locations including [Bhairahawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairahawa), [Bharatpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatpur,_Nepal), [Butwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butwal), [Gorkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_Municipality), [Kathmandu](17168.md), [Nawalparasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawalparasi_District), [Palpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palpa_District), and [Pokhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhara).[^cite_note-\:3-97] Due to a lack of cold storage, many unidentified bodies were temporarily buried until they could be identified,[^cite_note-\:26-16] with [DNA testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_testing) and photographs used to identify bodies.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-\:9-173] Photos of bodies were uploaded into a public online database to allow people to search for the missing.[^cite_note-174] As of 16 September, 105 bodies had been identified.[^cite_note-\:24-4] By 26 September, 1,044 bodies were buried,[^cite_note-175] 1,334 bodies had DNA sampled[^cite_note-\:43-3] and 2,060 people had DNA sampled.[^cite_note-KathmanduPostMonth-176] Some family members of the victims criticised the practice, saying that it disregarded [Hindu funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_funeral) rites.[^cite_note-\:9-173] Other families who had not found their deceased relatives resorted to using effigies made with [kusha grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusha_grass) in their funeral rituals.[^cite_note-kusha-177]

Thirty-seven holding centers were established in Nuwakot, Rasuwa, and Dhading to temporarily house impacted people, housing a peak of 4,500 people.[^cite_note-\:39-178][^cite_note-179] Residents of [Trishuli Bazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trishuli_Bazar) reported looting of houses and said that very few police officers were present.[^cite_note-180] [Rajendra Dawadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Dawadi), headteacher of Tribhuvan Trishuli secondary school in [Nuwakot District](2919311.md), evacuated about 900 children ten minutes before the flash flood reach the school. He had received four warnings from different sources, including one from the town of [Betrawati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betrawati), directly upstream of the school.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

Following the flooding, the Nepal [Roads Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Roads_\(Nepal\)) began installing [bailey bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey_bridge) to replace collapsed bridges.[^cite_note-BBC_News_नेपाली-2026a-123] [Tuins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuin_\(aerial_lift\)) were also installed to replace collapsed pedestrian bridges.[^cite_note-183] The Nepal Army delivered eight tonnes of relief supplies to Rasuwa by 27 August.[^cite_note-184] The government deployed over 150 [mental health professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health_professional) in Rasuwa and Nuwakot, and launched a 24/7 mental health [helpline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helpline).[^cite_note-\:23-171][^cite_note-185] By 3 September, over 1,239 people had received with [psychological first aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_first_aid), counseling, and mental health services.[^cite_note-\:22-186] The government issued a three-month suspension of health insurance co-payments and enforcement of identification requirements for people seeking medical treatment in Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading.[^cite_note-187] It also sent 500 portable solar systems to Rasuwa as part of efforts to provide electricity to households,[^cite_note-solar-188] and deployed a fuel tanker to Trishuli for supplying aircraft engaged in relief efforts.[^cite_note-189] By 9 September, 1,591 kg (3,508 lb) of food had been delivered to villages via [drone](58900.md).[^cite_note-\:29-190] Authorities launched a [cholera](21053458.md), [tetanus-diphtheria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPT_vaccine), and [measles vaccination](21053550.md) campaign in impacted areas, with over 59,732 people vaccinated by 18 September.[^cite_note-191]

The [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](35831443.md) initially turned down offers from several countries to send their search-and-rescue teams to support relief operations, citing mismanagement and poor coordination from the joint rescue effort following the [2015 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Nepal_Earthquake) as the main reason for the decision.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-NPRhesitance-193] Later, the Nepalese government allowed for the entry of experts from India, China, Australia, and South Korea for help in tunnel rescues, DNA testing and forensic examinations.[^cite_note-194]

In the early hours of 31 August, a controlled explosion was carried out in a tunnel at the [Upper Trishuli 3A Hydropower Station](65895251.md), in an effort to locate 933 missing workers. The Nepali Army then dug a hole about 10 m (33 ft) deep, using two excavators, and began to clear water, mud and debris from the tunnel.[^cite_note-195] Two survivors were rescued from the tunnel on 4 September,[^cite_note-196] while a Chinese national was rescued from the tunnel the next day.[^cite_note-\:30-197] Rescuers deployed drones to search inside hydropower tunnels.[^cite_note-\:29-190] Staff and students at [Pulchowk Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulchowk_Campus) developed an automated machine to remove debris from tunnels.[^cite_note-198]

Separately, a woman was rescued from a buried house in Betrawati on 5 September.[^cite_note-\:30-197] By 4 September, [Nepal Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_Telecom) reported that 116 of its 120 impacted [cellular towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_towers) had been restored and [Ncell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ncell) reported that 75 of its 78 impacted cellular towers had been restored.[^cite_note-kpost1287-116] By 9 September, 83% of electricity services in Rasuwa and 95% in Nuwakot were restored.[^cite_note-199]

A national day of mourning for the victims of the disaster was held in Nepal on 7 September.[^cite_note-200] The [Ministry of Energy, Water Resources and Irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Energy,_Water_Resources_and_Irrigation) created a seven-member panel of experts in hydrology, glaciology, geology, remote sensing, hydropower infrastructure and disaster risk reduction to undertake a comprehensive and scientific investigation into the cause of the floods.[^cite_note-201]

Trade between China and Nepal shifted to [Korala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korala) after the closure of [Rasuwa](79134779.md) and [Tatopani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Nepal_Friendship_Bridge).[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203]

### China

[CCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) general secretary [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) called for continued efforts to locate missing people and improvements to disaster monitoring and warning systems.[^cite_note-Xinhua-204][^cite_note-205] The government of the Tibet Autonomous Region mobilised nearly 800 emergency workers and 150 vehicles as well as sniffer dogs and speed boats to help the rescue efforts.[^cite_note-206] Units of the [People's Armed Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Armed_Police),[^cite_note-207] the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) and rescue teams from neighbouring [Sichuan province](65185.md) were deployed to assist in rescue operations.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-Ormiston-2026-209] The [National Fire and Rescue Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Fire_and_Rescue_Administration) also sent 681 personal and 13 rescue dogs, while the [Ministry of Emergency Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Emergency_Management) sent 60 personnel.[^cite_note-210] Two people were rescued at Gyirong Port. Access to the site was hampered by the accumulation of debris about 3 km (1.9 mi) upstream.[^cite_note-211] Chinese [state media](20803507.md) reported that 499 residents and 555 tourists had been evacuated from the area in Tibet.[^cite_note-Ormiston-2026-209]

Premier [Li Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Qiang) arrived in Gyirong on 26 August.[^cite_note-Xinhua2-212][^cite_note-168_dead-47] The [China Meteorological Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Meteorological_Administration) set up portable automatic weather stations in Gyirong Port.[^cite_note-213] A [4G](486547.md)/[5G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G) base station was installed in Gyirong County to remediate disrupted communication infrastructure.[^cite_note-214] The [China National Space Administration](344618.md) stated that it coordinated and mobilised satellite resources to perform emergency imaging of the impacted area.[^cite_note-215] Rescue operator China Anneng deployed 158 personnel and 33 [heavy equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_equipment) to repair roads.[^cite_note-\:7-150] Road repair efforts were hampered by river currents flowing up ​to six times faster than normal river speeds.[^cite_note-\:7-150] A makeshift road connecting to Gyirong Port was completed by 2 September.[^cite_note-\:11-216] Chinese authorities arranged for the repatriation of least 114 Nepalis who were stranded in China through the Tatopani border crossing,[^cite_note-217] while 576 others were repatriated through the Kodari crossing.[^cite_note-218]

On the morning of 30 August, the Government of China delivered 50 tonnes of emergency relief supplies to Kathmandu via two [Xi'an Y-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an_Y-20) transport aircraft. The supplies were handed over by Chinese ambassador Zhang Maoming to Nepalese science minister [Mahabir Pun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabir_Pun).[^cite_note-219] Later that day, ambassador Zhang Maoming delivered US\$2 million in emergency flood-relief assistance from the Government of China to Nepalese Finance Minister [Swarnim Wagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarnim_Wagle). He also handed over an additional US\$200,000 contributed by the [Red Cross Society of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Cross_Society_of_China) to Nepal's Prime Minister Disaster Relief Fund.[^cite_note-\:5-220] On 1 September, a second batch of relief supplies was delivered to Nepal from China, including [drones](58900.md), DNA testing equipment, and water purification equipment.[^cite_note-221] The government also deployed a team of tunnel experts to aid rescue efforts at hydropower station tunnels and a team of forensics experts to Nepal,[^cite_note-\:5-220][^cite_note-\:13-222] as well as two [bailey bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey_bridge).[^cite_note-\:20-120] The [National Climate Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Climate_Center?action=edit\&redlink=1) also provided rolling climate monitoring and forecast products to its Nepalese counterparts to support rescue and relief efforts.[^cite_note-223] The government donated four [DJI FlyCart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI_FlyCart) 100 drones to Nepal to conduct aid delivery and retrieve bodies.[^cite_note-\:29-190] On 21 September, China donated रू 11 million to the Nepal [Ministry of Youth and Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Youth_and_Sports_\(Nepal\)).[^cite_note-224]

#### Censorship and state media coverage

Further information: [Censorship in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_in_China) and [Public opinion guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_opinion_guidance)

Coverage of the disaster within China ranged from outright [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_in_China) to reporting in line with the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md)'s "[public opinion guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_opinion_guidance)."[^cite_note-225] Videos of the disaster taken by locals in Tibet and eyewitness accounts were [censored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_China) on [Chinese social media platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_social_media_platforms).[^cite_note-NYTcensor-226][^cite_note-NYTcensor2-227][^cite_note-BBCcensor-228] *The New York Times* reported that foreign journalists may enter the region only on government-supervised trips, and that Tibetans risk punishment for speaking to them.[^cite_note-NYTcensor-226][^cite_note-GuardianCensor-229] Smaller Chinese state-media outlets initially published interviews with witnesses, but the articles were removed within hours.[^cite_note-ABCcensor-230] The [Ministry of Public Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Security_\(China\)) said at least 10 people were penalised for sharing misleading or disruptive posts about the disaster.[^cite_note-VincentGuardian-231][^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234] According to *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, China restricts the flow of information in Tibet, which it [annexed in 1951](14615536.md) and suppresses [independence protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_independence_movement). Chinese state media carried several broadcasts about the disaster and sent journalists to the affected area, although reports focused mainly on rescue efforts rather than the extent of the devastation.[^cite_note-GuardianCensor-229] The journalists as well as social media influencers were warned against reporting casualties different from officially announced figures.[^cite_note-NYTcensor2-227] A [BBC](19344654.md) headline describing coverage of the disaster as fully censored in China was changed to "heavily censored" after criticism from Chinese social media users.[^cite_note-\:15-27][^cite_note-BBCcensor-228] Several foreign journalists said that Chinese authorities denied their requests to visit the affected areas citing difficult terrain and the risk of more floods.[^cite_note-ABCcensor-230]

Restrictions on independent reporting made the extent of the damage on the Tibetan side difficult to verify.[^cite_note-GuardianCensor-229][^cite_note-DibyeshConversation-40] The [International Campaign for Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Campaign_for_Tibet) said that the casualties in Tibet exceeded the figures China reported, and that up to 300 homes were destroyed.[^cite_note-VincentGuardian-231] The deputy Asia director of [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) said that Chinese state media journalists were told not to interview survivors or members of their family.[^cite_note-NYTcensor2-227] In response to accusations of censorship and underreporting casualties, [Guo Jiakun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guo_Jiakun), spokesperson of [China's foreign ministry](7453497.md), said the disaster should not be used for "smear campaigns and hype".[^cite_note-VincentGuardian-231] On 6 September, Chinese authorities hosted a group of foreign journalists on a visit to the rescue area which had been overrun by mud. Recovered bodies were still in the process of being identified.[^cite_note-\:10-235]

## Misinformation

The [flash flood](244391.md) was initially attributed in some reports and social media posts to Chinese infrastructure projects, dam construction, or hydrological activity in Tibet.[^cite_note-scmp_bela-296][^cite_note-298] Subsequent satellite imagery and scientific analyses identified a glacier collapse on the Nepalese side of the border near Langtang Lirung as the immediate source of the flood. Hydrologists noted that detecting and providing advance warning for such high-altitude glacier-related failures is difficult. The flood reportedly reached Gyirong Port within minutes of the collapse, leaving little time for conventional flood-warning and evacuation measures.[^cite_note-scmp_bela-296]

[Press Council Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Council_Nepal) launched a portal to report flood misinformation and ordered at least 72 users to remove content, including [AI-generated](73291755.md) footage and graphic images.[^cite_note-bbclive2-299][^cite_note-300] The Nepal Police's Cyber Bureau formed a team to investigate social media users accused of posting abusive and hateful comments against flood victims.[^cite_note-301] In Dhunche, residents confronted television news crews from India over accusations of sensationalism and other inappropriate behavior while covering the disaster.[^cite_note-302]
