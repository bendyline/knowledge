The **International Federation of Journalists** (**IFJ**) is the largest [global union federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_union_federation) of [journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist)' [trade unions](17626.md) in the world. It represents more than 600,000 media workers from 187 organisations in 146 countries.[^cite_note-\:1-1]

The IFJ is an associate member of [UNESCO](21786641.md)[^cite_note-\:0-2] and has represented journalists at the [United Nations](31769.md) since 1953 (UN/[ILO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization)). It works with the [International Trade Union Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Trade_Union_Confederation) (ITUC) and the [Trade Union Advisory Committee to the OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_Union_Advisory_Committee_to_the_OECD).

The IFJ President is the French journalist and trade unionist Dominique Pradalié. She succeeded [Younes Mjahed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Younes_Mjahed?action=edit\&redlink=1). [Anthony Bellanger](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony%20Bellanger), a French journalist and trade unionist, is the organisation's General Secretary.[^cite_note-3]

Upon request, the IFJ delivers the International Press Card to members of its affiliated organisations, the only press pass endorsed by national journalists' organisations in more than 130 countries.[^cite_note-4]

The Federation's headquarters are located in [Brussels](3708.md), [Belgium](3343.md) (155, rue de la Loi).

## History

The International Federation of Journalists was founded in 1926 in [Paris](22989.md), on the initiative of the French [Syndicat national des journalistes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicat_national_des_journalistes) (SNJ). At the time, it represented about 25 000 media workers from about twenty countries and had its headquarters in Paris. Its first President was the French reporter [Georges Bourdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Bourdon?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:3-5]

With the occupation of France by Nazi Germany, the [Second World War](32927.md) put an end to the IFJ's activities in Paris. Several unions then decided to form in [London](17867.md) the International Federation of Journalists of the Allies or Free Countries (IFJAFC), whose first Congress took place in 1941. IFJAFC dissolved in 1946, once the war was over, becoming the [International Organisation of Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Organisation_of_Journalists) (IOJ).[^cite_note-\:3-5]

As a result of the [Cold War](325329.md), the International Federation of Journalists was relaunched in 1952 at a World Congress in Brussels attended by 49 delegates. It competed with the IOJ, based in [Prague](23844.md) and composed mainly of national journalists' unions from Central and Eastern Europe and developing countries, until the collapse of the IOJ in the 1990's with the end of the Cold War.

The IFJ was officially recognised by the [United Nations Economic and Social Council](31958.md)[^cite_note-6] (Ecosoc) and [UNESCO](21786641.md).[^cite_note-\:0-2]

At IFJ Second World Congress in [Bordeaux](4097.md) in 1954, delegates representing more than 43 000 media workers from 21 unions in 18 countries adopted the IFJ Declaration of Principles on the Conduct of Journalists,[^cite_note-7] the first widely recognised text on journalistic ethics.[^cite_note-\:3-5]

The gradual disappearance of the IOJ in the 1990s gave a new impetus to the IFJ, with the membership of national trade unions from non-aligned states or from the former Soviet bloc. At the same time, IFJ encouraged the regionalisation of its activities and regional offices appeared in Africa, Asia-Pacific, Latin America, Europe and the Middle East.[^cite_note-\:3-5]

In 2019, IFJ 30th World Congress took place for the first time in Africa and in an Arab Country, in [Tunis](57659.md), [Tunisia](30188.md).[^cite_note-8] The Congress proved historical also because the 300 delegates from more than 100 countries adopted the Global Charter of Ethics for Journalists,[^cite_note-9] which reinforced the ethical standards laid down by the 1954 IFJ Declaration of Principles on the Conduct of Journalists.[^cite_note-10]

In February 2023, during the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), the [Russian Union of Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Union_of_Journalists) was suspended from IFJ after having established activities in occupied parts of Ukraine.[^cite_note-11]

In 2024, the IFJ condemned "Israel’s strategy of slandering Gaza’s journalists with unproven allegations" and called for an investigation into "Israel’s systematic targeting and [killing of journalists" in the Gaza war](75146557.md).[^cite_note-12] The IFJ marked 26 February 2024 as the International Day for Palestinian Journalists.[^cite_note-13]

## Priorities and campaigns

The International Federation of Journalists supports and organises union of journalists’ activities in defending their rights and press freedom worldwide.[^cite_note-\:1-1]

It fights for the improvement of journalists' working conditions and rights: [freedom of expression](21401843.md), fair remuneration, stable employment and decent pay, [gender equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_equality) and the fight against all forms of discrimination, [copyright protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright), [freedom of association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_association), safety. ... In this regard, it campaigns for the adoption of standards aimed at protecting the journalists’ rights and strengthening collective agreements.[^cite_note-\:1-1]

Freelance journalists' rights are a priority concern for the IFJ, which encourages its affiliates to integrate them in their structures.[^cite_note-14]

The IFJ deals with various threats to press freedom: [media concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_of_media_ownership), repressive laws, censorship, intimidations, and impunity for crimes against media workers.[^cite_note-15]

### Gender equality

The Federation promotes equality between women and men journalists in editorial offices and the workplace, in trade unions and in the content of information.[^cite_note-\:2-16]

IFJ Gender Council was founded in 2001 and is composed of representatives of organisations affiliated to the IFJ. It is an advisory body that sets the organisation's priorities in terms of gender equality and LGBTI representation.[^cite_note-17]

The IFJ's objectives for achieving equality include: equal pay, women's access to positions of responsibility in both the media and trade unions, conciliation of private and professional life, combating all forms of harassment, discrimination and violence against women journalists, balanced and unbiased representation of women and men in information content and at all levels of the media industry.[^cite_note-\:2-16]

### Safety

Since 1990, the IFJ has published an annual report (the "Killed list") which documents cases of journalists and media staff killed during the course of each year. It uses the information to campaign for greater safety for journalists, particularly local and freelance reporters and support staff who lack the resources to protect themselves in conflict zones. The annual reports are archived on the website.[^cite_note-18]

Since 1992, the Federation has also had a Safety Fund to support journalists (and their families) when faced with persecution. It has become internationally recognised as an important and crucial source of support for journalists under threat. It is the only international assistance fund for journalists established by journalists.[^cite_note-19]

The Safety Fund is an integral part of the IFJ Safety Programme which includes casework, protests, campaigns, provision of information and production of various publications.

Besides, the IFJ organises safety trainings for journalists working in dangerous areas.

## Organization

The IFJ Head Office is located in [Brussels](3708.md), [Belgium](3343.md).[^cite_note-20]

The Federation has regional organisations in Africa (Federation of African Journalists – FAJ), Europe ([European Federation of Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Federation_of_Journalists)) and Latin America (Federación de Periodistas de América Latina y el Caribe -FEPALC).

Regional offices are located in [Sydney](27862.md) ([Australia](4689264.md)), [Dakar](44251.md) ([Senegal](27278.md)), [Brussels](3708.md) and [Buenos Aires](255919.md) ([Argentina](18951905.md)).[^cite_note-21]

The Congress is the supreme organ of the Federation. Every three years, it brings together delegates from all its member unions.[^cite_note-22]

French journalist and trade unionist [Dominique Pradalié](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Pradali%C3%A9?action=edit\&redlink=1) was elected IFJ President during IFJ Congress in Muscat (Oman) in 2022. She succeeded the Moroccan journalist [Younes Mjahed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Younes_Mjahed?action=edit\&redlink=1), elected at the Tunis Congress in 2019.[^cite_note-23]

French journalist and trade unionist [Anthony Bellanger](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony%20Bellanger) has been the IFJ General Secretary since 2015. He was Deputy General Secretary from 2014 to 2015 and First General Secretary of the French [Syndicat National des Journalistes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicat_National_des_Journalistes?action=edit\&redlink=1) (SNJ) from 2011 to 2014.
