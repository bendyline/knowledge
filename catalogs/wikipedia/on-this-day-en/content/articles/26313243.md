The **Community of Latin American and Caribbean States** (**CELAC**)[^cite_note-2] is a bloc of [Latin American](18524.md) and [Caribbean](18956035.md) states, consisting of 33 countries, and has five official working languages.[^cite_note-UIA-3][^cite_note-4] It is seen as an alternative to the [Organization of American States](22788.md) (OAS) and includes all OAS member states (except the United States and Canada) plus includes the nations of [Nicaragua](21362.md) and [Cuba](5042481.md).[^cite_note-5] Initially proposed on February 23, 2010, at the [Rio Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Group)–[Caribbean Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Community) [Unity Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_and_Caribbean_Unity_Summit),[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] CELAC is seen as the successor of the [Rio Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Group) and the Latin American and Caribbean Summit on Integration and Development (CALC).[^cite_note-9] CELAC was created to deepen [Latin American integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_integration) and to reduce [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony) within the politics and economics of the region. The date of creation was on December 3, 2011, in [Caracas, Venezuela](55289.md), with the signing of the Declaration of Caracas.[^cite_note-10] As of 2013, CELAC possesses a population of roughly 600 million.[^cite_note-ICWA2013-1]

## History

### 2008–2010: Brazil and Mexico initiatives

See also: [Rio Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Group), [I Latin American and Caribbean Summit on Integration and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Latin_American_and_Caribbean_Summit_on_Integration_and_Development), and [Latin American and Caribbean Unity Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_and_Caribbean_Unity_Summit)

The immediate predecessor of the CELAC is the [Rio Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Group). Formed in 1986, it gathered 24 Latin American and Caribbean countries around summits to cooperate regional policy issue independently of the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERojas_AravenaAltmann_BorbónBeirute_Brealey201225–27-11]

On 16–17 December 2008, the [I Latin American and Caribbean Summit on Integration and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Latin_American_and_Caribbean_Summit_on_Integration_and_Development) (CALC) took place in Costa do Sauipe, [Bahia](199070.md), [Brazil](3383.md). It was organized at the initiative of the [Lula](99328.md) administration with the goal of building cooperation mechanism with greater autonomy from the United States and Canada. Most heads of state from Latin America and the Caribbean states attended, with the exception of [President of Colombia](251302.md) [Álvaro Uribe](243680.md) and [President of Peru](239109.md) [Alan García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Garc%C3%ADa).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERojas_AravenaAltmann_BorbónBeirute_Brealey201227-12] The summit finished with the signing of the Bahia Declaration, a common agenda establishing the following priorities: cooperation between mechanism of regional and subregional integration, the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), energy, infrastructures, social development and eradication of hunger and poverty, [food security](216361.md), [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development), [natural disasters](58921.md), [human rights](13831.md) promotion, [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_migration), [South–South cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South%E2%80%93South_cooperation) and Latin America and Caribbean projection.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERojas_AravenaAltmann_BorbónBeirute_Brealey201228-13][^cite_note-14]

In 2008, the [Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Calder%C3%B3n_Hinojosa) administration of [Mexico](3966054.md) proposed the creation of the Latin American and the Caribbean Union (Spanish: *Unión Latinoamericana y del Caribe*, ULC). The proposal was formalized on 27 March 2009 at [Rio Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Group) meeting. At the initiative of Mexico, the XXI Rio Summit and the II CALC summit were held together on 22–23 February 2010 in [Playa del Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_del_Carmen), [Mexico](3966054.md). The joint summit was named the [Latin American and Caribbean Unity Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_and_Caribbean_Unity_Summit) and the 33 attending states decided to create the Community of Latin American and Caribbean States (CELAC), which would be formally established in 2011.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERojas_AravenaAltmann_BorbónBeirute_Brealey201229–30-15]

[Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez), [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md) and [Rafael Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Correa) were among the other prominent leaders who praised the creation of CELAC.[^cite_note-estadao-16] In July 2010, CELAC selected [President of Venezuela](3329692.md) [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez) and [President of Chile](11885300.md) [Sebastián Piñera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Pi%C3%B1era), as co-chairs of the forum to draft statutes for the organization.[^cite_note-17]

The announcement prompted debate and discussion across Latin America and the Caribbean about whether it was more beneficial to have close ties with the U.S. and Canada or to work independently.[^cite_note-18]

An editorial in Brazil's *[Estadão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Estado_de_S._Paulo)* newspaper said, "CELAC reflects the disorientation of the region's governments in relation to its problematic environment and its lack of foreign policy direction, locked as it is into the illusion that snubbing the United States will do for Latin American integration what 200 years of history failed to do."[^cite_note-estadao-16]

### 2011: Founding

Main article: [2011 CELAC summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_CELAC_summit)

CELAC's inaugural summit was due to be held in mid-2011, but was postponed because of the ill-health of [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez), president of the host nation, Venezuela. The summit was instead held on December 2 and 3, 2011, in [Caracas](55289.md).[^cite_note-19] It primarily focused on the global economic crisis and its effects on the region. Several leaders, including presidents [Cristina Fernández de Kirchner](1644780.md), [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff) and [Juan Manuel Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Santos), encouraged an increase in regional trade, economic development, and further economic cooperation among members in order to defend their growing economies.[^cite_note-rueda-20]

Chávez, and other leaders such as [Rafael Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Correa) and [Daniel Ortega](8778.md), expressed hope that the bloc would work to further [Latin American integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_integration), end U.S. hegemony and consolidate control over regional affairs.[^cite_note-rueda-20] Chávez, citing the [Monroe Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_Doctrine) as the original confirmation of U.S. interference in the region, openly called for CELAC to replace the OAS: "As the years go by, CELAC is going to leave behind the old and worn-out OAS." Correa called for a new human rights commission to replace the [Inter-American Commission on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-American_Commission_on_Human_Rights). Other leaders argued that the organisation should be used as a tool to resolve regional disagreements and uphold democratic values, but not as a replacement of the OAS.[^cite_note-rueda-20] Santos stated that he would like to see dialogue within the group over whether existing counter-drug regulations should be revised.[^cite_note-rueda-20] The president of the [Latin American Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_Parliament) (Parlatino) said he expects that Parlatino will become the main legislative institution of CELAC.[^cite_note-21] Amongst the key issues on the agenda were the creation of a "new financial architecture," sanction for maintaining the legal status of [coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca) in Bolivia and the rejection of the [Cuban embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_embargo) by the U.S.[^cite_note-22]

United States President Barack Obama's senior adviser on Latin America, Daniel Restrepo, informed reporters from Miami that the U.S. government would "watch and see what direction CELAC takes".[^cite_note-23]

### Ongoing

Brazil decided to suspend its participation in the Community of Latin American and Caribbean States in January 2020 under the administration of [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md).[^cite_note-24] Following the [2022 Brazilian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Brazilian_general_election), newly elected president [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md) signalled his intention to rejoin the Community of Latin American and Caribbean States,[^cite_note-govplan-25] and effectively did so in the first days of his administration.[^cite_note-26]

In June 2023, CELAC recognized the Latin American and Caribbean character of the island of [Puerto Rico](23041.md) and “calls on the UN General Assembly to examine the question of Puerto Rico in its entirety and in all its aspects, and rule on this matter as soon as possible”.[^cite_note-27]

The bloc also produced the *Declaration of Buenos Aires (CELAC): Glaring omissions in relation to indigenous peoples* of 2023 to consider a regional perspective for [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) of the hemisphere.[^cite_note-28]

## Organization

See also: [Pro tempore presidency of the Community of Latin American and Caribbean States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_tempore_presidency_of_the_Community_of_Latin_American_and_Caribbean_States)

The CELAC has six organs:[^cite_note-bernal-29]

- The summit of [Heads of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [Government](37570.md).

- The meeting of [Ministers of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs).

- The meeting of National Coordinators.

- The *pro tempore* presidency.

- The Troika.

The [*pro tempore* presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_tempore_presidency_of_the_Community_of_Latin_American_and_Caribbean_States) is the main representative of the CELAC. The troika is composed by the current *pro tempore* presidency, its predecessor, its successor and the presidency of the [CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM).[^cite_note-bernal-29]

### Forums

The regional body has joint forums that work with external global entities, including China[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU%E2%80%93LAC_Foundation), Japan[^cite_note-32] and India.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

#### 2014 Summit – Cuba

Main article: [2014 CELAC summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_CELAC_summit)

During the summit, the region was declared a "peace zone". After three days and with the approval of participating representatives, a document with 83 focus points was created. It emphasized that, despite cultural and regional differences, unity between the participating countries is necessary in order to create progress. "Unity and the integration of our region must be gradually constructed, with flexibility, with respect to differences, diversity, and the sovereign right of each of our countries to choose our own forms of political and economic organization" stated the document. It also states which countries have been developing the best and how they are doing it in order for them to be a model for other countries.

The issue of poverty was widely discussed. Cuba's [Raúl Castro](221749.md) pointed out that throughout Latin America and the Caribbean, people wanted a fairer distribution of wealth, access to affordable education, employment, better salaries, and the eradication of illiteracy. He argued that CELAC countries can work together, support each other, to create new plans and solutions for these problems.[^cite_note-41]

## Currencies

See also: [Currency of Spanish America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_of_Spanish_America), [Single South American currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_South_American_currency), and [List of currencies in the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_currencies_in_the_Americas)

Member states within the bloc use numerous currencies for internal usage or for trade. Including: the [Argentine peso](407877.md), [Bahamian dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamian_dollar), [Barbadian dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbadian_dollar), [Belize dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize_dollar), [Bolivian boliviano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_boliviano), [Brazilian real](34201323.md), [Chilean peso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_peso), [Colombian peso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_peso), [Costa Rican colón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rican_col%C3%B3n), [Cuban peso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_peso), [Dominican peso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_peso), [Ecuadorian centavo coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_centavo_coins), [Guatemalan quetzal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemalan_quetzal), [Guyanese dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyanese_dollar), [Haitian gourde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_gourde), [Honduran lempira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_lempira), [Jamaican dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_dollar), [Mexican peso](147253.md), [Nicaraguan córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_c%C3%B3rdoba), [Panamanian balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamanian_balboa), [Paraguayan guaraní](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_guaran%C3%AD), [Peruvian sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_sol), [Surinamese dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinamese_dollar), [Trinidad and Tobago dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_dollar), [Uruguayan peso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguayan_peso), and the [Venezuelan bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_bol%C3%ADvar).[^cite_note-45]

Some initiatives have been made towards shared currencies or [currency unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_union),[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] including the current existing: [Caribbean guilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_guilder), [Eastern Caribbean dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Caribbean_dollar), and the [SUCRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUCRE). Additionally Argentina and Brazil have discussed the formation of a currency called the 'Sur' ("south") for bilateral trade, but this has not moved beyond planning stage.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Some CELAC member nations formally use the United States dollar including: (Ecuador and El Salvador). Other nations have made moves to offer their own [central bank digital currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank_digital_currency).
