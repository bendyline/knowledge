The **Iraq – Kuwait border** is 254 km (158 mi) in length and runs from the [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) with [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) in the west to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) coast in the east.[^cite_note-1]

## Description

The border starts in the west at the Saudi tripoint on the [Wadi al-Batin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_al-Batin), and then follows this [wadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi) as it flows north-eastwards. The border then turns east, following a straight line for 32 km (20 mi), before another straight line veers to the south-east for 26 km (16 mi), terminating at the coast by the junction of the [Khawr Abd Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khawr_Abd_Allah) and Khor as Subiyah opposite [Hajjam Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajjam_Island?action=edit\&redlink=1).

## History

Historically there was no clearly defined boundary in this part of the Middle East; Kuwait [de jure](43594.md) fell under [Basra Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basra_vilayet) administration from 1875 until the end of [World War I](4764461.md). At the start of the 20th century the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) controlled what is now Iraq and the United Kingdom [de facto](43594.md) controlled Kuwait as a [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Kuwaiti_Agreement_of_1899).[^cite_note-IBS103-2] The United Kingdom and the Ottoman Empire theoretically divided their realms of influence via the so-called "[Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Ottoman_Convention_of_1913)" and "[Violet lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violet_Line_\(1914\))" in the [Anglo-Ottoman Convention of 1913](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Ottoman_Convention_of_1913), by which the Ottomans recognised British claims on Kuwait, divided from Ottoman Mesopotamia along the Wadi al-Batin (the so-called 'green line', *see map right*). The convention was never ratified therefore remained not binding. Finally, the Ottomans and British emerged as enemies within months of the convention, as the outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md) diminished any hope left for ratification.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-Brown-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]



Map with red circle and green circle boundaries according to the Anglo-Ottoman Convention of 1913

During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) an [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt), supported by the United Kingdom, succeeded in removing the Ottomans from most of the Middle East. As a result of the secret 1916 Anglo-French [Sykes-Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes-Picot_Agreement) the United Kingdom gained control of the Ottoman Vilayets of [Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosul_Vilayet), [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad_Vilayet) and [Basra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basra_Vilayet). After a [revolt broke out in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Revolt) that demanded independence, the three Vilayets became [mandatory Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Iraq) in 1921, following a previous policy of centralization by [Mamluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_dynasty_\(Iraq\)) and [Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Iraq).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] In 1932, the year that Iraq gained [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iraq), the United Kingdom confirmed that the border between Iraq and Kuwait would run along the Wadi al-Batin, as well as confirming that [Bubiyan](1550688.md) and [Warbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warbah_Island) islands were Kuwaiti territory, though the precise positioning of the northern straight line segments near Safwan remained imprecise.[^cite_note-Brown-5]

Kuwait gained independence in 1961, though Iraq refused to recognise the country claiming it as part of Iraq, which resulted in a [show of force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vantage) by the United Kingdom and the [Arab League](52625.md) in support of Kuwait.[^cite_note-rrgp-12][^cite_note-cia-13][^cite_note-14] Following an [Iraqi coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_1963_Iraqi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) in 1963, a treaty of friendship was signed the same year by which Iraq recognised the 1932 border.[^cite_note-Brown-5] Despite this, the treaty was never ratified thus remained unbinding, and was later rejected by the [revolutionary command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Command_Council_\(Iraq\)).[^cite_note-15] Over the ensuing decade Iraq often raised the issue of sea access and the traditional claim to Kuwait, most notably in 1973 with the [1973 Samita border skirmish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Samita_border_skirmish).

In 1990 Iraq [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Kuwait) and [annexed Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_Governorate). The invasion instigated the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), which restored Kuwait's sovereignty.[^cite_note-FrontlineCron-16][^cite_note-17] In July 1992 the matter of [border demarcation](13541266.md) was referred to the [United Nations](31769.md), which accurately mapped the boundary and then demarcated it on the ground, following the 1932 line with some minor adjustments.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-Brown-5] The border initially was accepted by Kuwait but not Iraq.[^cite_note-Brown-5] Iraq accepted the border in November 1994.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] The [United Nations Iraq–Kuwait Observation Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Iraq%E2%80%93Kuwait_Observation_Mission) monitored the border during the period 1991–2003. Relations between the two states have improved since the [fall of Saddam Hussein](5043324.md) in 2003.

### Barrier

The **Iraq–Kuwait barrier** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): حدود العراق-الكويت *Hudud al-'Irāq-al-Kuwayt*) is a 120-mile (190 km) [border fence](40601687.md) extending six miles (9.7 km) into [Iraq](7515928.md), three miles (4.8 km) into [Kuwait](7515890.md), and across the full length of their mutual border from [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). Constructed by authorisation of the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) [resolution 689](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_689), its stated purpose was to stop a re-invasion of Kuwait by Iraq.

The [border barrier](40601687.md), made of electrified fencing and [concertina wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concertina_wire), is braced by a 15-foot-wide (4.6 m) and 15-foot-deep (4.6 m) trench, complete with a 10-foot-high (3.0 m) dirt berm and guarded by hundreds of soldiers, several patrol boats, and helicopters. Construction of the barrier began in 1991.

In January 2004, Kuwait decided to install a new 135-mile (217 km) iron barrier along the border. The barrier was estimated to have cost \$28 million and the entire length of the border; asphalted roads were also constructed to facilitate border security movement.[^cite_note-21]

### Maritime border

The 1992 UN demarcation of the Iraq–Kuwait border applied only to the land border, not the [maritime border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_border), which was deliberately left out.[^cite_note-Reuters-02-18-2023-22] The dispute surrounds the region of the [Khor Abdullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khor_Abdullah) waterway. Although [an agreement was reached in 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Iraq%E2%80%93Kuwait_agreement_on_the_Khor_Abdullah_waterway?action=edit\&redlink=1), the dispute was not settled. In 2014 Kuwait provided maps to the United Nations, which Iraq said were not the agreed maps. In 2019 Iraq filed a formal compaint with the UN after Kuwait built a port facility on the [Fasht Al A'aij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasht_Al_A'aij) [shoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal), and in 2023 an Iraqi court voided the 2012 agreement because the agreement did not receive proper approval from the [Iraqi parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_parliament). In February 2026 Iraq submitted a proposal which put the [Fasht al-Qaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasht_al-Qaid?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Fasht al-Aij shoals in Iraqi [territorial waters](496983.md). Kuwait rejected Iraq's proposal, with Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Oman, and Saudi Arabia taking Kuwait's side of the dispute.[^cite_note-AP-02-23-26-23]
