**Hendrik Johannes Nicasius** "**Nyck**" **de Vries** (Dutch pronunciation: [\[ˈnɪɡ də ˈvris\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch); born 6 February 1995) is a Dutch [racing driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_driver) who competes in the [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) for [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Gazoo_Racing) and in [Formula E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_E) for [Mahindra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahindra_Racing). In [formula racing](1618495.md), De Vries competed in [Formula One](10854.md) at 11 [Grands Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One_Grands_Prix) from [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Formula_One_World_Championship) to [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Formula_One_World_Championship), and won the [2020–21 Formula E World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Formula_E_World_Championship) with [Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-EQ_Formula_E_Team). In [endurance racing](699018.md), De Vries won the [24 Hours of Le Mans](1401596.md) in [2026](77197114.md) with Toyota.

Born and raised in [Uitwellingerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uitwellingerga), De Vries began competitive [kart racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kart_racing) aged nine. After a successful karting career—culminating in back-to-back victories at the [senior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KF1) [World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karting_World_Championship) in 2010 and 2011—De Vries graduated to [junior formulae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_formulae). Signed to the [McLaren Young Driver Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLaren_Young_Driver_Programme) from 2010 to 2019, de Vries won his first championship at the [Formula Renault Eurocup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Renault_Eurocup) in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Eurocup_Formula_Renault_2.0) with [Koiranen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koiranen_GP). He finished third in the [2015 Formula Renault 3.5 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Formula_Renault_3.5_Series) before winning multiple races in the [2016 GP3 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_GP3_Series). De Vries progressed to [FIA Formula 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_Formula_2) for its inaugural [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Formula_2_Championship) season, winning the title in his [third season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Formula_2_Championship), driving for [ART](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ART_Grand_Prix). De Vries graduated to [sportscar racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportscar_racing) that year, competing in the [LMP2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMP2) class of the [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) with [Nederland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Team_Nederland). He took his first class win the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship) at [Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_6_Hours_of_Fuji), before joining [Formula E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_E) with the recently established [Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-EQ_Formula_E_Team) team in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Formula_E_Championship). In his [second season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Formula_E_World_Championship), de Vries took multiple victories as he won his maiden [World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_E_champions). Alongside his successes in Formula E, De Vries also became a race-winner in the [European Le Mans Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Le_Mans_Series) with [G-Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-Drive_Racing) in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_European_Le_Mans_Series) and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_European_Le_Mans_Series).

A test and reserve driver for [Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Racing), [Mercedes](25098794.md), [McLaren](20994.md) and [Aston Martin](63473867.md), de Vries made his Formula One debut with the former at the [2022 Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Italian_Grand_Prix), as a substitute for [Alexander Albon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Albon). After scoring a points finish on his debut, he joined [AlphaTauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_AlphaTauri) for [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Formula_One_World_Championship) but was replaced by [Daniel Ricciardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Ricciardo) after ten rounds. De Vries moved back to Formula E with [Mahindra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahindra_Racing) for his [2023–24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Formula_E_World_Championship) campaign, and signed for [Toyota](3796361.md) in the [Hypercar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mans_Hypercar) class of WEC in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship), winning the [6 Hours of Imola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_6_Hours_of_Imola). He has served as a test driver and simulator driver for McLaren in Formula One since 2025.

## Early and personal life

Hendrik Johannes Nicasius de Vries was born on 6 February 1995 in [Uitwellingerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uitwellingerga), [Friesland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friesland), Netherlands.[^cite_note-Nyck_de_Vries_Age,_Height,_Wiki,_Bio,_Sister,_Parents_and_Salary-3] De Vries has partial Indonesian ancestry, with his grandfather hailing from [Malang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malang), [East Java](320443.md).[^cite_note-4]

In 2008, de Vries won the WSK World Series for the [KF3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KF3) category, as well as the [German Junior Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Karting_Championship). In 2009, he retained both his German Junior and WSK World Series titles, as well as winning the European KF3 Championship. In September, he won the 2010 [Karting World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karting_World_Championship). He also won the World Championship in 2011.[^cite_note-5]

### Formula Renault Eurocup

#### 2012



De Vries in 2012

In 2012, de Vries switched to single-seaters, being given a cockpit in the [Eurocup Formula Renault 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Eurocup_Formula_Renault_2.0) at [R-ace GP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-ace_GP). De Vries achieved his first podium finish at his debut race in [Alcañiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alca%C3%B1iz) with a second place, and he managed to repeat this result at the [Hungaroring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungaroring). De Vries finished the season in fifth place, beating all of his teammates. In addition, de Vries took part in several races of the [Formula Renault 2.0 NEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Formula_Renault_2.0_NEC_season), in which he managed a victory at his home track in [Assen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TT_Circuit_Assen). He was on the podium four times in eleven starts in the series and ended up tenth in the drivers' standings.

#### 2013

The following year, de Vries switched to [Koiranen GP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koiranen_GP) for his second season in the [series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Eurocup_Formula_Renault_2.0). He won one race each at the [Hungaroring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungaroring) and the [Circuit de Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Catalunya) and again ended the season in fifth place in the championship.

#### 2014

In 2014, de Vries stayed with Koiranen and competed in his third season of the [Eurocup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Eurocup_Formula_Renault_2.0). He won six races and finished on the podium in eleven of fourteen races. With 254 points to 124, he convincingly beat the runner-up [Dennis Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Olsen_\(racing_driver\)) in the championship. In addition, he took part in the entire Alpine Formula Renault season. He won ten out of fourteen races and only failed to make the podium twice.

### Formula Renault 3.5 Series

In 2015, de Vries made the switch to the [Formula Renault 3.5 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Formula_Renault_3.5_Series), racing for [DAMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAMS). He was on the podium five times before winning the final race of the season at the [Circuito de Jerez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuito_de_Jerez), putting him third in the final standings with 160 points, only placing behind [Oliver Rowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Rowland) and [Matthieu Vaxivière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthieu_Vaxivi%C3%A8re).

### GP3 Series

De Vries took part in the [GP3 Series in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_GP3_Series) in with [ART Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ART_Grand_Prix). He scored his first podium at the [Red Bull Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Red_Bull_Ring_GP3_Series_round) with a third place, then obtained his first pole in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Hungaroring_GP3_Series_round). It wasn't until the second race at [Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Monza_GP3_Series_round) that he took his first win, a result he replicated at the [Yas Marina Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Yas_Marina_GP3_Series_round) at the end of the season. He finished sixth in the championship, behind his teammates [Charles Leclerc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Leclerc) and [Alexander Albon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Albon), who became champion and vice-champion respectively.

### FIA Formula 2 Championship

#### 2017

In 2017, de Vries switched to the [FIA Formula 2 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIA_Formula_2_Championship) with [Rapax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapax_Team).[^cite_note-6] He achieved his first win in the [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Monte_Carlo_Formula_2_round) sprint race and scored a further three podium finishes before the summer break.[^cite_note-7] Before the round at [Spa-Francorchamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Spa-Francorchamps_Formula_2_round), de Vries switched to [Racing Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Engineering), with whom he scored a second place at that very round. He ended his season seventh in the championship, placing second-highest of all rookies.

#### 2018



De Vries at the [Red Bull Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Ring) during the [2018 FIA Formula 2 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIA_Formula_2_Championship)

For the [2018 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Formula_2_Championship), de Vries moved to partner [Sean Gelael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Gelael) at [Prema Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prema_Racing).[^cite_note-8] His season started slowly with just two podiums from the opening third of the season putting him a fair way behind his title rivals. De Vries's first victory of the campaign in the sprint race at [Le Castellet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Le_Castellet_Formula_2_round) was followed by a point-less round in [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Spielberg_Formula_2_round) and two finishes outside the top five in [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Silverstone_Formula_2_round).[^cite_note-9] Following that however, de Vries's form improved, winning the feature races in [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Budapest_Formula_2_round) and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Spa-Francorchamps_Formula_2_round) respectively.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] However, even with his sixth podium of the season in the [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Sochi_Formula_2_round) feature race, de Vries was unable to finish in the top-three in the standings, being beaten by [Alex Albon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Albon), [Lando Norris](48978813.md) and [George Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Russell_\(racing_driver\)) by ten, seventeen and 85 points respectively.

#### 2019



De Vries at the [2019 Spielberg Formula 2 round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Spielberg_Formula_2_round) with [ART Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ART_Grand_Prix)

In 2019, de Vries remained in [Formula 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Formula_2_Championship), returning to his former team [ART Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ART_Grand_Prix) alongside [2018 GP3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_GP3_Series) vice-champion [Nikita Mazepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Mazepin).[^cite_note-12] His season started in strong fashion with a podium in [Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Baku_Formula_2_round) and a sprint race victory in [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Barcelona_Formula_2_round).[^cite_note-13] He followed that up with a win from pole position in [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Monte_Carlo_Formula_2_round) and took the championship lead by winning the main race at [Le Castellet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Le_Castellet_Formula_2_round).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] A pair of third places at the [Red Bull Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Spielberg_Formula_2_round) and a podium each in [Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Silverstone_Formula_2_round) and [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Budapest_Formula_2_round) extended de Vries's advantage,[^cite_note-16] and following another pair of thirds in [Monza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Monza_Formula_2_round) he had put one hand onto the trophy. After his fourth and final victory of the season, which came at the penultimate round in [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Sochi_Formula_2_round), de Vries was mathematically crowned Formula 2 champion.[^cite_note-17]

De Vries at the [2018 6 Hours of Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_6_Hours_of_Silverstone)

On 15 March 2018, de Vries signed for [Racing Team Nederland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Team_Nederland) to compete in the LMP2 class of the [FIA World Endurance Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship).[^cite_note-18] He later remained with the team for the [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_FIA_World_Endurance_Championship), partnering [Giedo van der Garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giedo_van_der_Garde) and bronze-ranked [Frits van Eerd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frits_van_Eerd).[^cite_note-TDSYellowDutchman-19] De Vries and his teammates won the [6 Hours of Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_6_Hours_of_Fuji), thus taking the team's first win in the series.[^cite_note-20] This result helped Racing Team Nederland to end up fourth in the LMP2 standings.

On 6 July 2020, de Vries was announced as the [Toyota Gazoo Racing Europe GmbH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Gazoo_Racing_Europe_GmbH) test and reserve driver. His roles include testing the [Toyota TS050 Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_TS050_Hybrid) and Toyota's hypercar.[^cite_note-21] The same year saw him competing in three rounds of the [European Le Mans Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_European_Le_Mans_Series) with [G-Drive Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-Drive_Racing), scoring a podium and fastest lap combo at [Le Castellet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_4_Hours_of_Le_Castellet) and winning the season finale at the [Algarve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algarve_International_Circuit).[^cite_note-22]



De Vries driving at the [2022 24 Hours of Le Mans](68512218.md)

During the test week prior to the [2022 24 Hours of Le Mans](68512218.md) the ACO officials prohibited [TDS Racing x Vaillante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TDS_Racing) driver [Philippe Cimadomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Cimadomo) from starting. Due to his reserve role for the Toyota Hypercar team, de Vries was already at the circuit and was asked to step in.[^cite_note-23] De Vries ended up finishing fourth in class alongside [Mathias Beche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathias_Beche) and [Tijmen van der Helm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijmen_van_der_Helm), setting the fastest top 20 average laptime.[^cite_note-24]



De Vries driving the [GR010 Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_GR010_Hybrid) at the [2024 6 Hours of Spa-Francorchamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_6_Hours_of_Spa-Francorchamps)

On 20 November 2023, de Vries was announced as one of the primary drivers for the No. 7 [Toyota Gazoo Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Gazoo_Racing) Hypercar, replacing the outgoing [José María López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_L%C3%B3pez).[^cite_note-25] Having qualified second for the season-opening race in [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Qatar_1812_km), de Vries managed to take his first overall WEC victory at [Imola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_6_Hours_of_Imola), profiting from an impressive stint by veteran teammate [Kamui Kobayashi](9537340.md).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] de Vries would finish second overall at [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) taking his first podium at Le Mans.

## Formula E career

### Mercedes-EQ (2019–2022)

#### 2019–20 season

Near the end of 2019, de Vries was announced as a driver for the new [Mercedes-Benz EQ Formula E Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_EQ_Formula_E_Team) for the [2019–20 Formula E season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Formula_E_Championship) alongside [Stoffel Vandoorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoffel_Vandoorne), having failed to find a seat in [F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Formula_One_World_Championship).[^cite_note-28] De Vries scored a single podium, finishing second behind his teammate at the final [Berlin ePrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Berlin_ePrix) race, to end up 11th overall with 60 points.[^cite_note-\:02-29]

#### 2020–21 season

For the [2020–21 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Formula_E_World_Championship), de Vries continued at the renamed [Mercedes-EQ Formula E Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-EQ_Formula_E_Team) with Stoffel Vandoorne.[^cite_note-30] He qualified on pole for the first race of the season-opening [Diriyah ePrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diriyah_ePrix) double-header, proceeding to lead every lap en route to his first-ever victory in the series. Before the second race, de Vries did not take part in qualifying as Mercedes and [Venturi Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venturi_Racing), who were both using Mercedes powertrains, were suspended from the session following the crash of [Edoardo Mortara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edoardo_Mortara) during a practice start procedure. In the race, he finished ninth after penalties were issued to multiple cars. At the round in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Rome_ePrix), de Vries failed to score points in both races, having collided with his teammate Vandoorne and [Sam Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Bird) respectively in the two races. De Vries achieved his second victory of the season in [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Valencia_ePrix), where he was one of the only drivers to not run out of usable energy before the end of the race.[^cite_note-31]



Nyck de Vries during the [2021 London ePrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_London_ePrix)

Following two rounds in which he only amassed a total of two points, de Vries fought for victory in both races of the [London ePrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_London_ePrix) and finished in second place in both races, thus taking the lead in the championship before the final round.[^cite_note-32] In the first race in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Berlin_ePrix), he did not score any points but managed to maintain his lead in the standings. Having qualified 13th for the final race of the season, de Vries was given an early advantage when title rivals [Mitch Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_Evans) and [Edoardo Mortara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edoardo_Mortara) collided at the start, and fellow contender [Jake Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Dennis) was involved in a crash shortly after the restart.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] De Vries finished the race in eighth place, thus winning his first ever World Championship in Formula E.[^cite_note-35] He ended the campaign with a total of two wins, four podiums and 99 points, seven ahead of vice-champion Mortara.

#### 2021–22 season

De Vries and Vandoorne remained with [Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-EQ_Formula_E_Team) for their swansong season in Formula E.[^cite_note-36] De Vries won the first race of season 8 in Diriyah, and got pole the next day, but finished in tenth, and ended the championship in ninth with 106 points.[^cite_note-\:02-29] De Vries left at the end of the season, securing a contract to compete in Formula One.

## Formula One career

De Vries was signed to the [McLaren Young Driver Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLaren_Young_Driver_Programme) in 2010,[^cite_note-formula1.com-41] and the [Audi Sport Racing Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_Sport_GmbH) in 2016. He left McLaren before the 2019 season to focus on his duties at Audi, where he stayed until September 2019.[^cite_note-42]

### Test and reserve driver roles (2020–2022)



De Vries driving for [Mercedes](25098794.md) during free practice for the [2022 French Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Grand_Prix)

In December 2020, de Vries and fellow [Mercedes-EQ Formula E Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-EQ_Formula_E_Team) driver Stoffel Vandoorne performed their first [Formula One](10854.md) test for the [Mercedes](25098794.md) team at the season-ending Young Driver Test.[^cite_note-43] The following year he became one of the two reserve drivers for the team, again partnered with his Mercedes-EQ teammate Stoffel Vandoorne.[^cite_note-44]

#### 2022: Race debut on loan to Williams

De Vries made his Formula 1 practice debut at the [2022 Spanish Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Spanish_Grand_Prix), where he took [Alex Albon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Albon)'s place at [Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Grand_Prix_Engineering) for the hour-long session.[^cite_note-45] For the [French Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Grand_Prix), de Vries ran in another practice session at [Mercedes](25098794.md), taking seven-time World Champion [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton)'s place.[^cite_note-46] De Vries then again drove a Mercedes in free practice for the [Mexico City Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mexico_City_Grand_Prix) in place of [George Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Russell_\(racing_driver\)).[^cite_note-47]

In September, de Vries tested the [Alpine A521](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_A521) at the [Hungaroring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungaroring), alongside [Antonio Giovinazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Giovinazzi) and [Jack Doohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Doohan_\(racing_driver\)).[^cite_note-48]

For the [2022 São Paulo Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_S%C3%A3o_Paulo_Grand_Prix), de Vries was appointed as [Lando Norris](48978813.md)'s replacement at [McLaren](20994.md) in case Norris was unable to race due to food poisoning; he recovered the next day and was able to compete.[^cite_note-49]

##### 2022 Italian Grand Prix

At the [Italian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Italian_Grand_Prix), de Vries took part in first practice in place of [Sebastian Vettel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Vettel) at [Aston Martin](63473867.md).[^cite_note-50] De Vries then replaced [Alex Albon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Albon) at [Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Grand_Prix_Engineering) for each of third practice, qualifying, and the race, after Albon suffered [appendicitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appendicitis) and was ruled out on Saturday morning.[^cite_note-51] He managed to advance to Q2 and qualify thirteenth, ahead of his Williams teammate [Nicholas Latifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Latifi). He started eighth after penalties were applied to multiple drivers.[^cite_note-52] He finished the race in ninth position, scoring points on his race debut and was rewarded with Driver of the Day by fans.[^cite_note-53]

### AlphaTauri (2023)



De Vries at the [2023 Austrian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Austrian_Grand_Prix)

De Vries was signed by [Scuderia AlphaTauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_AlphaTauri) for the [2023 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Formula_One_World_Championship), replacing [Pierre Gasly](40843850.md), who left for [Alpine](65240620.md).[^cite_note-54]

De Vries qualified 19th and finished 14th for the season-opening [Bahrain Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Bahrain_Grand_Prix)[^cite_note-55] and followed that result by qualifying 18th and finishing 14th at the [Saudi Arabian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Saudi_Arabian_Grand_Prix).

De Vries qualified 15th making Q2 for the first time at the [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Grand_Prix) but he retired from a chaotic race after [Logan Sargeant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Sargeant) crashed into the back of him taking both cars out and he was classified in 15th. De Vries crashed in qualifying for the [Azerbaijan Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Azerbaijan_Grand_Prix). He qualified last for the sprint and finished the shorter race 14th. He crashed out of the main race on lap 10 after clipping the wall at turn 5. In both the races that he retired from, teammate [Yuki Tsunoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Tsunoda) had scored points putting pressure on de Vries for his results to improve.

De Vries qualified 15th in the [Miami Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Grand_Prix), out-qualifying teammate Tsunoda for the first time that year. He drove into the back of [Lando Norris](48978813.md)' [McLaren](20994.md) at the start of the race, ending his chance of a good result. He finished the race 18th and 24 seconds behind Tsunoda.

De Vries qualified 12th in [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Monaco_Grand_Prix) and finished the race where he started in 12th but he beat Tsunoda for the first time that year in a race. De Vries qualified 14th in [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Spanish_Grand_Prix), out qualifying Tsunoda for the second time that year but he finished the race in 14th behind Tsunoda. De Vries qualified and finished the race 18th in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Canadian_Grand_Prix) whilst coming under criticism for forcing [Kevin Magnussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Magnussen) off the road on lap 35.

De Vries qualified last for the [Austrian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Austrian_Grand_Prix) but qualified 14th for the sprint and finished the shorter race 17th. He finished 17th in the main race after receiving a 15-second time penalty for numerous track limit infringements and another 5-second penalty for forcing Magnussen off track for the second race in a row. After the race, Magnussen said De Vries was driving like a desperate man. De Vries qualified 18th and finished 17th and last at the [British Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_British_Grand_Prix). With ten races of the season completed, de Vries was 20th and last in the drivers championship having scored no points whilst teammate Tsunoda had scored two points.

Two days after the Silverstone race, de Vries was released by AlphaTauri with [Daniel Ricciardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Ricciardo) replacing him for the rest of the season.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Prior to his dismissal, de Vries was criticised for his performance, most notably by Red Bull motorsport advisor [Helmut Marko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Marko).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] The decision to dismiss de Vries halfway through his rookie season was criticised by several Dutch and Formula E racing drivers.[^cite_note-60]
