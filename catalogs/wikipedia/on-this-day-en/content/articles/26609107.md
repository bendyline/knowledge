**Epping** is a [market town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town) and [civil parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_parish) in the [Epping Forest District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest_District) of [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex), England. It lies 17 miles (27 km) north-east of [Charing Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charing_Cross) in central London. It is surrounded by the northern end of [Epping Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest), and stands on a ridge of land between the [River Roding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Roding) and [River Lea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Lea) valleys.

Epping forms part of the [London commuter belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_commuter_belt); it is the eastern terminus of the [London Underground](17839.md)'s [Central line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_line_\(London_Underground\)), and the town lies just outside the [M25 London Orbital Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M25_motorway). The town has a number of historic Grade I and II\* and Grade II [listed buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_buildings). The weekly market, which dates to 1253, is held each Monday.[^cite_note-3]

As well as the town itself, the parish also includes [Coopersale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coopersale) and [Fiddlers Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddlers_Hamlet). At the [2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%932022_United_Kingdom_censuses) the parish had a population of 12,547 and the built up area had a population of 10,695.

Epping became [twinned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_towns_and_sister_cities) with the German town of [Eppingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eppingen) in north-west [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md) in 1981.[^cite_note-4]

## History



High Street, Epping, in *Leaves from a Hunting Diary in Essex* (1900). St John's Church is at the left, and shows it before a new and present tower was constructed in 1909.

The name Epping derives from the [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) *yppeingas* meaning 'the people of the raised place'.[^cite_note-5]

"Epinga", a small community of a few scattered farms and a chapel on the edge of the forest, is mentioned in the *[Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book)* of 1086. However, the settlement referred to is known today as [Epping Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Upland). It is not known for certain when the present-day Epping was first settled. By the mid-12th century a settlement known as Epping Heath (later named Epping Street), had developed south of Epping Upland as a result of the clearing of forest for cultivation. In 1253 [King Henry III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III_of_England) conveyed the right to hold a weekly market in Epping Street which helped to establish the town as a centre of trade and has continued to the present day (the sale of cattle in the High Street continued until 1961).[^cite_note-6]

The [linear village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_village) of Epping Heath developed into a small main-road town and by the early 19th century development had taken place along what is now High Street and Hemnall Street. Hemnall Street was until 1894 in the parish of [Theydon Garnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theydon_Garnon), as was the railway station.[^cite_note-\:0-7] Up to 25 [stagecoaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach) and [mailcoaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mail_coach) a day passed through the town from London en route to [Norwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) and [Bury St. Edmunds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_St._Edmunds). In the early 19th century, 26 [coaching inns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaching_inn) lined the High Street.[^cite_note-8] Two survive today as [public houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_house): The George and Dragon and The Black Lion. The advent of the railways ended coach traffic and the town declined, but it revived after the extension of a railway branch line from [Loughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughton) in 1865 and the advent of the motor car.

A number of listed buildings, most dating from the 18th century, line both sides of the High Street although many were substantially altered internally during the 19th century. Some of the oldest buildings in the town are at each end of the Conservation Area, such as Beulah Lodge in Lindsey Street (17th century), and a group of 17th- and early 18th-century cottages numbered 98–110 on High Street.[^cite_note-9]

The original parish church, first mentioned in 1177, was All Saints' in Epping Upland, the nave and chancel of which date from the 13th century.[^cite_note-10] In 1833, the 14th-century chapel of St [John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist) in the High Road was rebuilt in the [Gothic Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival_architecture) style. It became the parish church of Epping by the **Epping Church Act 1888** ([51 & 52 Vict.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51_&_52_Vict.) c. clii) and was again rebuilt. A large tower was added in 1909.[^cite_note-11]

The town was historically known in the 18th and 19th centuries for the Epping sausage and Epping butter, which is no longer produced.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

## Governance



Epping Hall: Town Council headquarters

There are three tiers of local government covering Epping, at [parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_parish) (town), [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_district) and [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county) level: Epping Town Council, [Epping Forest District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest_District_Council) and [Essex County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_County_Council). The town council is based at Epping Hall on St John's Road.[^cite_note-14] The district council is also based in the town, at the Civic Offices on High Street.[^cite_note-15] The [Old Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Hall,_Epping) was built in 1863.[^cite_note-16]

The town sits in the Epping and Theydon Bois division of Essex County Council. The town is divided into two district council wards. Epping Hemnall encompasses most of the town south-east of Epping High Street (B1393) including Ivy Chimneys, Fiddlers Hamlet, [Coopersale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coopersale) and Coopersale Street. The rest of Epping lies in Epping Lindsey and Thornwood ward, as does Thornwood in the adjacent parish of [North Weald Bassett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Weald_Bassett). Both wards elect three [councillors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Councillors) each.[^cite_note-17]

### Administrative history

Epping was an [ancient parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_parish) in the [Waltham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundreds_of_Essex) [hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_\(county_division\)) of Essex. When elected parish and district councils were created in 1894, Epping was initially given a parish council and included in the [Epping Rural District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Rural_District).[^cite_note-18] Shortly afterwards it was decided that the town should become a separate [urban district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_district_\(England_and_Wales\)), but the more rural north-western part of the parish, including the original village of Epping, was not considered appropriate for inclusion in an urban district. Therefore the parish was split on 1 April 1896 into a rural parish called [Epping Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Upland), which remained in the Epping Rural District, and an urban district called Epping (which also gained territory from the neighbouring parishes of [Theydon Garnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theydon_Garnon) and [Theydon Bois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theydon_Bois) at the same time).[^cite_note-19]

Epping Urban District was abolished in 1974 when the modern Epping Forest District was created. A [successor parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successor_parish) was created covering the area of the former urban district, with its parish council taking the name Epping Town Council.[^cite_note-20]

### Constituencies

Epping is part of the [Epping Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) parliamentary constituency, represented by Conservative MP [Neil Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Hudson_\(politician\)) and previously represented by former Deputy Speaker [Eleanor Laing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Laing) before the [2024 General Election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_General_Election). From 1924 to 1945, the old [Epping division of Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) (which included [Woodford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodford,_London), [Chingford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chingford), [Harlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlow) and [Loughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughton) as well as Epping) was represented by [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill).

## Geography

Epping is 17 miles (30 km) north-east of the centre of London, and towards the northern end of [Epping Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest) on a ridge of land between the [River Roding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Roding) and [River Lea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Lea) valleys. It is 3 miles (5 km) north-east of [Loughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughton), 10 miles (16 km) north of [Ilford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilford), 5 miles (8 km) south of [Harlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlow) and 11 miles (20 km) north-west of [Brentwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentwood,_Essex). Epping is north of the village of [Theydon Bois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theydon_Bois).

The Town lies north-east of junction 26 ([Waltham Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltham_Abbey_\(town\)), [Loughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughton) [A121](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A121_road_\(England\))) of the [M25 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M25_motorway) and south-west of junction 7 ([Harlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlow)) of the [M11 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M11_motorway).

## Community



Sign showing twin towns of Epping

Most of the population live in the built up area centred on and around the High Street (B1393) and Station Road. About a thousand people live in the village of [Coopersale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coopersale) which, while physically separated from Epping by forest land, is still part of the civil parish. A few dozen households make up the hamlets of [Coopersale Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coopersale_Street) and [Fiddlers Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddlers_Hamlet). Much of the eastern part of the present parish was until 1896 in the parish of Theydon Garnon.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

The centre of Epping on and around the High Street is a designated conservation area.[^cite_note-21]

The [2025 British anti-immigration protests](80681466.md) started in Epping following the arrest of an Ethiopian asylum seeker in regards to three sexual assaults; he had arrived in the UK eight days prior and was being housed in the local Bell Hotel.[^cite_note-22] A temporary injunction[^cite_note-23] granted to Epping Forest district council was overturned by the court of appeal, allowing more than 130 asylum seekers to remain in the Bell Hotel.&#x20;

## Transport



A route 541 bus at Epping Tube Station



Railway track of the [Epping Ongar Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Ongar_Railway) close to Epping tube station (Epping Forest Halt). Passengers cannot alight here due to the absence of a platform.

### Rail

[Epping tube station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_tube_station) is a [London Underground](17839.md) terminus, on the [Central line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_line_\(London_Underground\)).[^cite_note-\:1-24]

The station is in [London fare zone 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_fare_zone_6).[^cite_note-\:1-24] There is a car park at the station.[^cite_note-25] There is no [Night Tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Tube), as Central line services overnight on Fridays and Saturdays terminate at [Loughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughton_tube_station).[^cite_note-26]

The Central line links Epping directly with [east London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London), [Stratford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford_station), [The City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London), [Oxford Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Street), and destinations in [west London](60445975.md).[^cite_note-\:1-24]

Until 1994, the Central line extended north from Epping to [North Weald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Weald_railway_station), [Blake Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Hall_tube_station) (until 1981), and [Ongar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ongar_railway_station).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Much of the line is now served by a heritage railway - the [Epping Ongar Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Ongar_Railway). The heritage railway does not serve Epping tube station, but the museum runs a heritage [London Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Bus) to the Central line station on some open days.[^cite_note-29]

### Road

Epping High Street is numbered [B1393](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B_roads_in_Zone_1_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme). The route runs north-south through the town.

The B181 runs east-west through Epping, between Roydon and North Weald.

The B182 runs along the south-western perimeter of the town, between Epping's [Bell Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Common) and [Epping Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Upland).

These roads are maintained by [Essex Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_County_Council).[^cite_note-30]

### Walking

Much of Epping Forest has unlimited walking access. The [City of London Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London_Corporation), which looks after Epping Forest, has produced several waymarked walking routes for leisure.[^cite_note-31]

## Education

- [Epping St John's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_St_John's_School), a [Church of England](5955.md) school, is the only mainstream secondary school in Epping.

There are also a number of primary schools including Ivy Chimneys Primary School, Epping Primary School, and Coopersale Hall School.

## Media

Because of its close proximity to London, television signals are received from the [Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Palace_transmitting_station) TV transmitter placing Epping in the [BBC London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_London) and [ITV London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_London) areas.[^cite_note-32] However, the town can also receive the [Sandy Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Heath_transmitting_station) TV transmitter which broadcast [BBC East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_East) and [ITV Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Anglia).[^cite_note-33]

Local radio stations are [BBC Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Essex) on 95.3 FM, [Heart East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_East) (formerly [Ten-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten-17)) on 101.7 FM, and Forest Gold Radio, a community based radio station the broadcast from near the [St Margaret's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Margaret's_Hospital,_Epping) in the town on 99.3 FM.[^cite_note-34]

The [Epping Forest Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epping_Forest_Guardian) is town's local newspaper.[^cite_note-35]
