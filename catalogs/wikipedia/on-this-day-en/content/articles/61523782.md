Notable spaceflight events during the year 2026.

## Overview

### Astronomy and astrophysics

On 11 January, [NASA](18426568.md) launched the [Pandora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandora_\(spacecraft\)) small space telescope to study [exoplanet](9763.md) atmospheres.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] The [same Falcon 9 flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_spaceflight_launches_in_January%E2%80%93March_2026#SpXTwilight) also carried two [CubeSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat)-type space telescopes by NASA: [SPARCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPARCS) and [BlackCAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackCAT), alongside other payloads.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] Pandora completed its commissioning phase and started scientific observations in August 2026.[^cite_note-7]

In February, [ESA](10363.md)'s experimental [heliophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliophysics) mission [PROBA-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PROBA-3) suffered an anomaly which led to the loss of contact with one of the two spacecraft.[^cite_note-8] Contact was re-established with PROBA-3 in March[^cite_note-9] and the spacecraft resumed observations in July.[^cite_note-10]

On 19 May, [ESA](10363.md) and [CAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Academy_of_Sciences) launched their joint [heliophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliophysics) mission [SMILE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMILE_\(spacecraft\)) to study the dynamic interaction between solar wind and Earth's magnetosphere.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The spacecraft reached its final science orbit on 20 June[^cite_note-14] and started science observations on 23 September.[^cite_note-15]

On 3 July, Katalyst Space Technologies launched the [Swift boost mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swift_boost_mission) under a contract from NASA to save the [Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Gehrels_Swift_Observatory) by boosting it to a higher orbit.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] They used the LINK spacecraft launched on a [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman) [Pegasus XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegasus_XL) aircraft-borne rocket. This was the final launch of Pegasus XL and means there are no remaining active orbital [air launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-launch-to-orbit) systems anywhere in the world.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Once in orbit, LINK suffered a series of technical issues, and on 19 August, NASA cancelled the rescue efforts.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

On 30 August, NASA launched the [Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope](28371067.md), an infrared space telescope for [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology) and search for [exoplanets](9763.md), to Sun-Earth [Lagrange Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_point) 2.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

In 2026, [NASA](18426568.md) plans to launch the [SunRISE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Radio_Interferometer_Space_Experiment) mission consisting of six [CubeSats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat) for studying [solar activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_phenomena).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

[Taiwan](25734.md) plans to launch its first space telescope, the [Gamma-ray Transients Monitor (GTM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gamma-ray_Transients_Monitor), as a secondary payload on the [Formosat-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formosat-8)B satellite, in December.[^cite_note-30]

### Solar system exploration

On 15 May, [NASA](18426568.md)'s [Psyche spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyche_\(spacecraft\)) performed a [gravity assist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_assist) maneuver at [Mars](14640471.md), flying 4,500 km from the planet's surface on its way to the asteroid [Psyche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16_Psyche).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In July 2026, humanity got the first close-up views of two new Solar system objects.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] [CNSA](344618.md)'s [Tianwen-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-2) arrived at the [near-Earth asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_asteroid) [Kamoʻoalewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/469219_Kamo%CA%BBoalewa), a [quasi-satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-satellite) of Earth, for sample collection[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] and [JAXA](716184.md)'s [Hayabusa2](10915042.md) flew by the near-Earth asteroid [Torifune](46319186.md).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

The joint [ESA](10363.md)-[JAXA](716184.md) mission [BepiColombo](551228.md) is expected to enter orbit around [Mercury](19694.md) in late 2026.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The arrival operations began on 3 September 2026 with the separation of the propulsion module from the remaining spacecraft.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

On 20 October,[^cite_note-48] [JAXA](716184.md) plans to launch the [Martian Moons eXploration (MMX)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martian_Moons_eXploration) mission to the moons of [Mars](14640471.md) with the aim of bringing back the first samples from [Phobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phobos_\(moon\)).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] The mission also carries the European Phobos rover [Idefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idefix_\(rover\)).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

[ESA](10363.md)'s [Hera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hera_\(space_mission\)) spacecraft is expected to arrive at the double asteroid [Didymos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65803_Didymos) in November.[^cite_note-53]

On 15 November, [Voyager 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_1) will be one [light-day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-day) from Earth.[^cite_note-54]

In November, [NASA](18426568.md)'s twin [ESCAPADE](73006261.md) spacecraft are expected to perform a [gravity assist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_assist) maneuver at Earth that will send them towards Mars.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

On 24 December, [ESA](10363.md)'s [Solar Orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Orbiter) is expected to perform its 5th [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus) flyby, which will tilt its heliocentric orbit from 17° to 24°.[^cite_note-57]

### Lunar exploration

No earlier than November,[^cite_note-58] [NASA](18426568.md) and [Astrobotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrobotic_Technology) plan to launch [Griffin Mission One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin_Mission_One) aiming to land near the Moon's south pole.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

### Human spaceflight

#### International Space Station

On 8 January, [Mike Fincke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fincke),[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] one of [Crew-11](78935408.md) astronauts on the [ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS), suffered a serious medical issue, which included a loss of ability to speak for approximately 20 minutes.[^cite_note-63] In response, [NASA](18426568.md) called off a spacewalk that was under preparation at that time and later decided to return the entire Crew-11 mission back to Earth earlier than planned in a "controlled medical evacuation". This marks the first time in U.S. history, as well as in the ISS program, that a space mission has been cut short due to a medical issue,[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] although such cases happened before during Soviet space station missions.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] The Crew Dragon [Endeavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Endeavour) with all 4 crew members of Crew-11 safely [splashed down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splashdown) off California coast on 15 January.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

On 13 February, NASA launched the first crewed spaceflight of the year, the [Crew-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Crew-12) mission carrying four NASA, ESA, and Roscosmos astronauts to ISS,[^cite_note-72] returning the station to its normal crew complement of seven in a rare indirect handover caused by the early return of [Crew-11](78935408.md).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] The second crewed flight of the year to ISS was [Soyuz MS-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_MS-29) launched on 14 July[^cite_note-76] and the third was [Crew-13](82571911.md) launched on 1 October.[^cite_note-77]

On 22 March, [Roscosmos](406573.md) launched [Progress MS-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_MS-33) to ISS, the first space station uncrewed cargo flight of the year and the first launch from the [Baikonur Cosmodrome Site 31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur_Cosmodrome_Site_31) after substantial repairs necessitated by the damage caused by the November 2025 launch of [Soyuz MS-28](76567874.md).[^cite_note-78] Further resupply missions to ISS were: [Cygnus NG-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_NG-24) on 11 April,[^cite_note-79] [Progress MS-34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_MS-34) on 25 April,[^cite_note-80] [SpaceX CRS-34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_CRS-34) on 15 May,[^cite_note-81] and [Progress MS-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_MS-35) on 16 September.[^cite_note-82]

No earlier than December 2026,[^cite_note-83] [NASA](18426568.md) and [Boeing](18933266.md) plan to launch the uncrewed [ISS](15043.md) cargo mission [Starliner-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Starliner-1), the fourth orbital flight of the [Starliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Starliner) spacecraft.[^cite_note-84]

[NASA](18426568.md) and [Sierra Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Space) plan to launch [Dream Chaser Demo-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSC_Demo-1), the first free flyer orbital demonstration flight of the uncrewed cargo [spaceplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceplane) [Dream Chaser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Chaser).[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

#### Artemis program

On 30 January, [Blue Origin](887418.md) announced that it will pause the [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard) suborbital [space tourism](67896.md) program for "at least two years" in order to focus on crewed lunar exploration.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] On 9 February, [SpaceX](832774.md) likewise announced a delay in its [Mars ambitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Mars_colonization_program) for "about five to seven years" in order to focus on lunar missions.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

On 27 February, [NASA](18426568.md) announced a number of changes to the [Artemis program](60758751.md) with the stated purpose of increasing its cadence of missions,[^cite_note-93] including changing the [Artemis III](50493439.md) mission to a [LEO](47568.md) demonstration flight, moving the first Artemis Moon landing to [Artemis IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_IV),[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] and replacing the [Exploration Upper Stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_Upper_Stage) with another stage,[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] later identified as [Centaur V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centaur_V).[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

On 1 April 2026, [NASA](18426568.md) launched the [Artemis II](41391511.md) mission on the [Space Launch System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_System), sending astronauts around the Moon on a ten-day lunar flyby.[^cite_note-101] On 6 April, Artemis II became the farthest human spaceflight in history when it surpassed the previous distance record of [Apollo 13](1770.md).[^cite_note-102] The mission's re-entry capsule *Integrity* safely [splashed down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splashdown) in the Pacific Ocean southwest of [San Diego](28504.md) on 11 April 2026.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

#### Chinese lunar program

On 11 February, China performed a successful in-flight abort test of the new [Mengzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengzhou_\(spacecraft\)) spacecraft, which verified the spacecraft's [launch escape system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Launch_escape_system) performance, as well as the [Long March 10A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10) first stage ability of soft landing on water.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] Later in 2026 or possibly in 2027,[^cite_note-109] China plans to launch [Mengzhou 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengzhou_1), the first uncrewed orbital flight of the spacecraft and the complete Long March 10A rocket, both intended for the country's crewed lunar program.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

#### Tiangong space station

On 30 March, China launched a prototype of the new [cargo spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_spacecraft) [Qingzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingzhou_\(spacecraft\)) on the first flight of the [Kinetica 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetica_2) rocket.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] Once in orbit, the spacecraft conducted a series of rendezvous and proximity operations tests involving another satellite.[^cite_note-116]

On 11 May, China launched its first [Tiangong space station](2481401.md) resupply mission of the year, [Tianzhou 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianzhou_10),[^cite_note-117] followed by the first Chinese crewed spaceflight of the year, [Shenzhou 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_23), on 24 May.[^cite_note-118]

#### Indian human spaceflight program

[ISRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISRO) aims to launch [Gaganyaan-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaganyaan-1), [India](14533.md)'s first uncrewed orbital test flight to support human exploration missions.[^cite_note-119]

### Rocket innovation

On 12 February, [Arianespace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianespace) launched 32 [Amazon Leo](60415184.md) satellites on the first flight of [Ariane 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariane_6#Ariane_64), the 4-booster variant of [Ariane 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariane_6), marking the first successful orbital [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight) of the year.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] Other successful first flights to orbit were achieved by the Chinese [Kinetica 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetica_2), [Long March 12B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_12B), [Long March 10B](82301965.md), and [Pallas-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallas-1), the Russian [Soyuz-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-5_\(rocket\)), the Japanese [H3-30S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H3_\(rocket\)#Variants), and the Indian [Vikram-1](70739748.md).

[SpaceX](832774.md) continued testing the [Starship](59118602.md) system, with a partially successful suborbital [Flight 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_12) in May,[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] a mostly successful suborbital [Flight 13](78384679.md) in July,[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] and a mostly successful orbital [Flight 14](78384681.md) in September.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

On 10 July, China became the second nation to successfully recover a booster after orbital flight, when a [Long March 10B](82301965.md)'s first stage landed on a net-like structure on a ship at sea during its maiden flight.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] The second Chinese launcher to achieve first-stage recovery was [Zhuque-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuque-3), after a successful orbital launch on 18 August.[^cite_note-132]

On 11 July, [JAXA](716184.md) conducted the first [VTVL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTVL) flight test of its RV-X small [reusable rocket](336014.md) demonstrator. The vehicle serves as a technology pathfinder for [CALLISTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CALLISTO), a larger European-Japanese demonstrator rocket under development in collaboration between JAXA, [CNES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNES), and [DLR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Aerospace_Center).[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] [ESA](10363.md) plans first test flights of the [Themis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Themis_programme) reusable rocket demonstrator in 2026.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

On 18 July, Skyroot Aerospace launched [Vikram 1](70739748.md), India's first commercially built rocket, with a successful flight carrying multiple payloads to Low Earth Orbit.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

On 5 September, the [German](11867.md) company [Isar Aerospace](65261460.md), supported by [ESA](10363.md), successfully launched its [Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_\(rocket\)) rocket from [Andøya](30864935.md) Spaceport in [Norway](21241.md), marking the first orbital launch from West European soil.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

### Satellite technology

In January, [Hisdesat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisdesat) announced that the [Spainsat NG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spainsat_NG) II communications satellite launched in October 2025 for Spanish government and military use was struck by a “space particle" while on its way to [Geostationary orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geostationary_orbit) and suffered non-recoverable damage.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]

On 12 January, the Spanish [space capsule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_capsule) company Orbital Paradigm, supported by [ESA](10363.md)'s [FLPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Launchers_Preparatory_Programme), launched its first technology demonstration mission. The [KID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KID_\(spacecraft\)) capsule was expected to [re-enter Earth atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_entry) after reaching orbit on the [PSLV-C62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSLV-C62) flight. The Indian [PSLV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_Satellite_Launch_Vehicle) rocket failed in flight but KID separated from the falling launch vehicle and managed to transmit flight data during its non-nominal suborbital flight, thus becoming the lone survivor of 2026's first launch failure.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

On 27 January, the [EU](9317.md) announced that the [EUSPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Agency_for_the_Space_Programme)'s [GOVSATCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS%C2%B2#GOVSATCOM) program for encrypted communications is now operational, integrating eight satellites from five EU member states.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] The program serves as an operational bridge to the planned [IRIS<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS%C2%B2) constellation.[^cite_note-\:0-151] In March, [Cyprus](5593.md) became the first country to use GOVSATCOM operationally, possibly in connection with the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md) or the country's [Presidency of the Council of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_the_Council_of_the_European_Union).[^cite_note-\:0-151] The [IRIS<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS%C2%B2) project entered its implementation phase in August.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]

In March 2026, the Chinese company Sustain Space demonstrated on-orbit operations of a flexible robotic arm for satellite servicing and refuelling on its Xiyuan-0 satellite launched on a [Kuaizhou-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuaizhou) rocket on 16 March 2026.[^cite_note-154]

On 28 March, [ESA](10363.md) launched the first two satellites of the [GNSS augmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNSS_augmentation) constellation [Celeste (LEO-PNT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_\(LEO-PNT\)) on a [Rocket Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Lab)'s [Electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Lab_Electron) rocket.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] The mission delivered its first navigation signals on 8 April 2026.[^cite_note-158]

On 3 May, the German [ICARUS Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICARUS_Initiative) launched its first operational satellite of the [ICARUS 2.0 constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICARUS_Initiative#ICARUS_2.0_satellites), built by the Bulgarian company [EnduroSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EnduroSat) and named RAVEN, for [tracking animal migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_migration_tracking).[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160]

On 27 August, [Ariane 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariane_6) performed its first [geostationary orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geostationary_orbit) mission, launching the European weather satellite [MTG-I2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTG-I2) (Meteosat-14), which completed the first trio of [MTG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteosat#Third_Generation_\(%22MTG%22\)) satellites.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163] In a further expansion of its [Earth observation satellite](46597.md) fleet, Europe launched ESA's [FLEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FLEX_\(satellite\)) for monitoring terrestrial vegetation by measuring [chlorophyll fluorescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll_fluorescence) together with the [oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanography) satellite [Sentinel-3C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel-3C) for the [EU](9317.md)'s [Copernicus Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus_Programme) on a single flight of the [Vega C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega_C) rocket on 15 September.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] Then in October, Ariane 6 will launch the second [MetOp-SG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetOp-SG) weather satellite, [MetOp-SG B1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetOp-SG_B1) carrying [microwave imagers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_imaging) and [radars](25676.md), to low Earth orbit.[^cite_note-166]

On 31 August and 1 September, [ESA](10363.md) sent a small airplane to observe the destructive [reentries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_entry) of the last two satellites of the magnetospheric mission [Cluster II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_II_\(spacecraft\)).[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] To better coordinate the aerial observation campaign, ESA commanded the satellites in late January 2026 to slightly alter their orbits so they would reenter closer to each other. This initiative was part of ESA [Space Safety Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Safety_Programme)'s efforts to reduce the risks of [space debris](266344.md) and served as precursor to the *[Draco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draco_\(space_mission\))* mission planned for 2027.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171]

On 3 September, [ISRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISRO) launched [EOS-05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EOS-05), India's first geostationary Earth observation satellite.[^cite_note-172] The satellite was launched on [PSLV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_Satellite_Launch_Vehicle)'s return-to-flight mission after the failed [PSLV-C62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSLV-C62) in January 2026.[^cite_note-173]

On 1 October 2026, [Ukraine](31750.md) launched the first satellite of [UASAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UASAT), its sovereign [Satellite internet constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_internet_constellation).[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175]

In 2026, [NASA](18426568.md) plans to launch [LOXSAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOXSAT), a [cryogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenic) fluid management demonstration satellite mission.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177]
