**Torrance** is a city in the [Los Angeles metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_metropolitan_area) in southwestern [Los Angeles County, California](79734.md), United States, founded in 1912, neighboring [Redondo Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redondo_Beach,_California), [Rolling Hills Estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Hills_Estates,_California), [Palos Verdes Estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_Verdes_Estates,_California), [Lomita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomita,_California), [Gardena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardena,_California), [Lawndale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawndale,_California), and [Alondra Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alondra_Park,_California), as well as the Los Angeles neighborhoods of [Harbor City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_City,_Los_Angeles) and [Harbor Gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Gateway,_Los_Angeles). The city is part of what is known as the [South Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bay_\(Los_Angeles_County\)) region.&#x20;

Torrance has a moderate year-round climate with average rainfall of 12 inches (300 mm) per year. Torrance has one and a half miles (2.4 km) of beachfront abutting [Santa Monica Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Bay) and 30 parks.[^cite_note-city-9] It is the birthplace of the [American Youth Soccer Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Youth_Soccer_Organization) (AYSO). The population was 147,067 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census),[^cite_note-2020_Census_(City)-6] decreasing by 5.1% to an estimated 139,576 in 2024.[^cite_note-USCensusEst2024-7]

## History

Torrance was founded in October 1912 and was incorporated on May 12, 1921.

### Pre-colonial era

For thousands of years, the area of Torrance was occupied by the [Tongva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongva_people) [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States). The land that is now part of the City of Torrance and much of the modern South Bay was part of the extensive marshlands.

### Spanish and Mexican eras

In 1784, the [Spanish Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain) deeded [Rancho San Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_San_Pedro) (including present-day Torrance), a tract of over 75,000 acres (300 km<sup>2</sup>) in the province of [Las Californias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Californias) of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), to soldier Juan José Domínguez.

[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] It was later divided in 1846, with Governor [Pío Pico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%ADo_Pico) granting [Rancho de los Palos Verdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_de_los_Palos_Verdes) to José Loreto and [Juan Capistrano Sepúlveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Sepulveda) in the [Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California) territory of independent [Mexico](3966054.md).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

### Modern Era

In the early 1900s, real estate developer [Jared Sidney Torrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Sidney_Torrance) and other investors saw the value of creating a mixed industrial–residential community south of [Los Angeles](18110.md). They purchased part of an old Spanish land grant and hired landscape architect [Frederick Law Olmsted Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Law_Olmsted_Jr.) to design a [planned community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_community).[^cite_note-14] The resulting town was founded in October 1912 and named after Torrance. The city of Torrance was formally incorporated in May 1921, the townsite initially being bounded by Western Avenue on the east, Del Amo Boulevard on the north, [Crenshaw Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crenshaw_Boulevard) on the west, and on the south both by Plaza del Amo east of where it meets Carson Street, and by Carson Street west of where it meets Plaza del Amo.[^cite_note-15]

The first residential avenue created in Torrance was Gramercy and the second avenue was Andreo. Many of the houses on these avenues reached the centennial mark in 2012. Both avenues are located in the area referred to as Old Torrance. This section of Torrance is under review to be classified as a [historical district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_district). Some of the early civic and residential buildings were designed by the renowned and innovative [Southern California](62520.md) architect [Irving Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Gill), in his distinctive combining of [Mission Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Revival_Style_architecture) and early [Modernist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture).[^cite_note-16]

#### Historic Olmsted District

Torrance was planned as a new prototype of a balanced industrial city based on the principles of the [Garden City Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_city_movement). The original tract developed by the Olmsted Brothers consists of 109 city blocks divided into three sub-districts: residential, commercial, and industrial. The plan is most notable for its axial landscaped downtown commercial neighborhood aligned to have a view of Mount San Antonio in the San Gabriel Mountains. The Olmsted Tract includes a number of buildings designed by the noted Southern California Architect Irving Gill, including the original train depot.[^cite_note-17]

The footprint of the downtown neighborhood, now called Old Torrance, was designed on a diagonal to allow the trade breezes coming from the Pacific Ocean to keep the air clean from industrial pollution for the residential and commercial neighborhoods. The industrial sections of the city were placed on the eastern side of the original tract.

Public transportation played a key role in the founding of Torrance. The [Pacific Electric Red Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Electric) connected downtown Los Angeles to the new development of downtown Torrance. Designed in 1912 by Irving Gill, the terminus depot of the Red Car line was designed in a Spanish revival style popularized during this era.[^cite_note-18] In May 1913, the [Pacific Electric Railroad Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Electric_Railroad_Bridge) was built. Often called the "El Prado Bridge", it further expanded the industrial heart of the South Bay. The concrete double-tracked arch bridge was the Pacific Electric Railway's first interurban line that connected north–south to San Pedro via the Gardena Line.[^cite_note-19] The bridge was used for transporting freight and commuting workers to Torrance factories. The Red Car line connected under the bridge as it connected to the train depot located on Cabrillo Avenue. The bridge no longer carries any rail cars, with Pacific Electric closing the Red Car line to Torrance in the 1940s. The bridge became the city of Torrance's second entry in the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) on July 13, 1989, and is used as a logo for the city's new wayfinding signage and city materials.[^cite_note-20]



The Pacific Railroad Bridge, often called the El Prado Bridge, was designed by famed architect Irving Gill. The bridge stands as an icon for the city of Torrance.

## Geography



Torrance Beach lies between the [Palos Verdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_Verdes) Peninsula and [Redondo Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redondo_Beach,_California) on the [Santa Monica Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Bay).

Torrance is a coastal community in southwestern Los Angeles County sharing the climate and geographical features common to the [Greater Los Angeles area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles_area). Its boundaries are: Redondo Beach Boulevard and the cities of [Lawndale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawndale,_California) and [Gardena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardena,_California) to the north; [Western Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Avenue_\(Los_Angeles\)) and the [Harbor Gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Gateway) neighborhood of [Los Angeles](18110.md) to the east; the [Palos Verdes Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_Verdes_Hills) with the cities of [Lomita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomita,_California), [Rolling Hills Estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Hills_Estates,_California) and [Palos Verdes Estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_Verdes_Estates,_California) on the south; and the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) and the city of [Redondo Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redondo_Beach,_California) to the west.

The western portion of Torrance is in [ZIP Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZIP_Code) 90277, which is a city of [Redondo Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redondo_Beach,_California) postal address.[^cite_note-21] It is about 20 miles (32 km) southwest of [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles).[^cite_note-FujitaAkikoToyota-22]

Torrance Beach lies between Redondo Beach and [Malaga Cove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaga_Cove) on [Santa Monica Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Bay).[^cite_note-23] The southernmost stretch of Torrance Beach, on a cove at the northern end of the [Palos Verdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_Verdes) peninsula, is known to locals as [Rat Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAT_Beach) (Right After Torrance).

An urban [wetland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland), the [Madrona Marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrona_Marsh) is a [nature preserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve) on land once set for oil production and saved from development, with [restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_ecology) projects enhancing the vital habitat for birds, wildlife, and [native plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_plant).[^cite_note-MMcity-24][^cite_note-25]

### Climate

Torrance has a [warm-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csb*), bordering with a [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) (*Bsk*). The rainy season is November through March, as shown in the adjacent table.[^cite_note-26] Summers tend to be warm but dry, despite Torrance's proximity to the coast.[^cite_note-27] The [Sunset Western Garden Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Books) places most of Torrance in Zone 22, part of a basin area in Greater Los Angeles where cold air can pool—hence the surprisingly chilly record low temperatures for each calendar month at the airport, which has risk of frost over six months. Milder microclimates are found upslope of the airport to the south (Zone 23—thermal belt) and in the western blocks bordering the beach communities and [Palos Verdes Estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_Verdes_Estates) (Zone 24—marine influence dominant).[^cite_note-28]

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), there were 147,067 people, 56,655 households, and \_ families residing in the city.[^cite_note-39] The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 7,167.71 inhabitants per square mile (2,767.5/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 58,775 housing units at an average density of 2,864.56 per square mile (1,106.0/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the city was 38.99% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 3.43% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.55% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 35.70% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.41% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(U.S._Census\)), 7.75% from some other races and 13.17% from two or more races. [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) people of any race were 19.09% of the population.[^cite_note-40]

The census reported that 99.0% of the population lived in households, 0.5% lived in non-institutionalized group quarters, and 0.5% were institutionalized.

There were 56,655 households, out of which 30.0% included children under the age of 18, 52.4% were married-couple households, 4.8% were [cohabiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohabitation) couple households, 25.8% had a female householder with no partner present, and 16.9% had a male householder with no partner present. 25.6% of households were one person, and 11.2% were one person aged 65 or older. The average household size was 2.57. There were 38,894 [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_\(U.S._Census\)) (68.7% of all households).

The age distribution was 19.5% under the age of 18, 7.4% aged 18 to 24, 25.8% aged 25 to 44, 28.7% aged 45 to 64, and 18.6% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 43.0 years. For every 100 females, there were 95.1 males.

There were 58,775 housing units at an average density of 2,864.6 units per square mile (1,106.0 units/km<sup>2</sup>), of which 56,655 (96.4%) were occupied. Of these, 54.1% were owner-occupied, and 45.9% were occupied by renters.

### 2010 census

As of the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), there were 145,438 people, 56,001 households, and \_ families residing in the city. The population density was 7,102.16 inhabitants per square mile (2,742.2/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 58,377 housing units at an average density of 2,850.72 per square mile (1,100.7/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the city was 51.11% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 2.72% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.38% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 34.54% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.36% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(U.S._Census\)), 5.37% from some other races and 5.51% from two or more races. [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) people of any race were 16.12% of the population.

The Census reported that 144,292 people (99.2% of the population) lived in households, 506 (0.3%) homeless who lived in non-institutionalized group quarters, and 640 (0.4%) were institutionalized.

There were 56,001 households, out of which 18,558 (33.1%) had children under the age of 18 living in them, 29,754 (53.1%) were [opposite-sex married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) living together, 6,148 (11.0%) had a female householder with no husband present, 2,510 (4.5%) had a male householder with no wife present. There were 2,152 (3.8%) [unmarried opposite-sex partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POSSLQ), and 309 (0.6%) [same-sex married couples or partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_partnerships). 14,472 households (25.8%) were made up of individuals, and 5,611 (10.0%) had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.58. There were 38,412 [families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_\(U.S._Census\)) (68.6% of all households); the average family size was 3.14.

There were 31,831 people (21.9%) under the age of 18, 10,875 (7.5%) aged 18 to 24, 38,296 (26.3%) aged 25 to 44, 42,710 (29.4%) aged 45 to 64, and 21,726 (14.9%) who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 41.3 years. For every 100 females, there were 94.7 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 91.8 males.

There were 58,377 housing units at an average density of 2,840.3 units per square mile (1,096.6 units/km<sup>2</sup>), of which 31,621 (56.5%) were owner-occupied, and 24,380 (43.5%) were occupied by renters. The homeowner vacancy rate was 0.8%; the rental vacancy rate was 5.3%. 85,308 people (58.7% of the population) lived in owner-occupied housing units, and 58,984 people (40.6%) lived in rental housing units.

Torrance has the second-highest percentage of residents of [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese-American) ancestry in California (8.9%), after the neighboring city of Gardena.[^cite_note-41]

### Japanese Americans

The inside of the old demolished Torrance [Mitsuwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsuwa) (relocated to the [Del Amo Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Amo_Mall))

In the pre-[World War II](32927.md) period, the South Bay region was one of the few areas that allowed non-U.S. citizens to acquire property, so a Japanese presence came. Toyota moved its operations to its Torrance campus in 1982 because of its proximity to the [Port of Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Long_Beach) and [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md), and many other Japanese companies followed suit. In 2014, Toyota announced it was moving its U.S. headquarters to [Plano, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano,_Texas).[^cite_note-FujitaAkikoToyota-22]

The [Del Amo Fashion Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Amo_Fashion_Center), at 2.5 million square feet (230,000 m<sup>2</sup>), is one of the [ten largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_shopping_malls_in_the_United_States) [malls](235723.md) in the United States by gross leasable area. The current mall was created when Del Amo Center, built in 1958, merged with Del Amo Fashion Square, built in 1972. Once located on opposite sides of Carson Street, an expansion of the mall spanning Carson Street joined the two centers by 1982, making it the largest mall in the world at the time. In 2005, the east end of the original mall north of Carson Street was demolished to make way for a new open-air shopping center, opened in mid-September 2006. This was followed in 2015 by the opening of an expanded northern Fashion Wing, with [Nordstrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordstrom) as the mall anchor and supplemented by luxury retailers such as [Kate Spade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Spade_&_Company), [Hugo Boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Boss), [Uniqlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniqlo), [Michael Kors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kors_\(brand\)), and [Ben Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Bridge_Jeweler).[^cite_note-47] The [Old Towne Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Towne_Mall) was an entertainment-themed mall operating in the 1970s.

As a major oil-producing region, Torrance was once dotted with thousands of [oil wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_wells) and [oil derricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_derrick). Though the oil wells are not as common as they once were, the Torrance oil refinery owned by [PBF Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBF_Energy) in the north end of the city is responsible for much of Southern California's gasoline supply.[^cite_note-48] Torrance was an important hub and shop site of the [Pacific Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Electric_Railway).[^cite_note-Walker2007-49]

Torrance has a [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) airport, originally named simply "Torrance Airport" and since renamed [Zamperini Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamperini_Field) after local track star, World War II hero and Torrance High graduate [Louis Zamperini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Zamperini). The airport handles approximately 175,000 annual take-offs and landings (473 per day),[^cite_note-50] down from the 1974 record of 428,000 operations. Airport noise abatement is a major local issue.

Torrance is also home to the main bakery facility for [King's Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Hawaiian), the dominant brand of [Hawaiian bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_bread) in North America.[^cite_note-NihipaliPelayo2012-51] Younger Optics, Torrance's 10th-largest employer, created the first seamless or "invisible" bifocal.[^cite_note-52]

The headquarters of [Mitsuwa Marketplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsuwa_Marketplace)[^cite_note-53] and [Nijiya Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijiya_Market)[^cite_note-bloombergJinon-54] are both located in Torrance.

## Government

### Local government

The City of Torrance is a [charter city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_city). The original city charter was voted on and ratified by the qualified electors at an election held August 20, 1946, and filed with the Secretary of State January 7, 1947. The elective officers of the city are the mayor, six members of the City Council, five members of the Board of Education, the City Clerk and the City Treasurer.[^cite_note-74]

Using the council-manager form of government, the City Council, as the elected body, adopts legislation, sets policy, adjudicates issues, and establishes the budget of the city. The City Council appoints the City Manager and the City Attorney. The city has 13 appointed boards and commissions which advise the council on matters of concern to local residents, such as the city airport, arts, parks, and libraries.[^cite_note-75]

### State and federal representation

In the [California State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Senate), Torrance is split between [the 24th senatorial district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California's_24th_senatorial_district), represented by [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Democratic_Party) [Ben Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Allen_\(California_politician\)), and [the 35th senatorial district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California's_35th_senatorial_district), represented by [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Democratic_Party) [Laura Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Richardson).[^cite_note-76] In the [California State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Assembly), it is in [the 66th Assembly district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California's_66th_State_Assembly_district), represented by [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Democratic_Party) [Al Muratsuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Muratsuchi).[^cite_note-77]

In the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md), Torrance is split between [California's 36th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California's_36th_congressional_district), represented by [Democrat](5043544.md) [Ted Lieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Lieu), and [California's 43rd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California's_43rd_congressional_district), represented by [Democrat](5043544.md) [Maxine Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxine_Waters).[^cite_note-78]

### Fire

- Torrance Fire Department staffs seven Engine Companies, five Paramedic Rescue Squads, and two Truck Companies. The department operates out of six Fire Stations providing Fire and EMS coverage for the City and Mutual Aid to the surrounding communities. Torrance Memorial Medical Center, Little Company of Mary Hospital, Harbor-UCLA Medical Center, Kaiser Hospital-South Bay, and Memorial Hospital of Gardena are receiving hospitals for residents in Torrance who call 911 for medical assistance. The department is a Class 1 rated Fire Department, the Fire Chief is Martin Serna. Ambulance transportation is provided through McCormick Ambulance.

  [^cite_note-87]

### Police

- [Torrance Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrance_Police_Department) provides 24-hour law enforcement coverage to the city. The department is broken down into four major divisions, each with its own subdivisions. The department has one main station located at the Civic Center near City Hall. It houses the administrative offices, the city jail, and the public safety dispatch center. The department works closely with other local law enforcement agencies for training and SWAT operations. The police chief is Bob Dunn.

  [^cite_note-88]

- Torrance operates its own 911 dispatch center located at the police station, and is responsible for all 911 calls originating in Torrance. The communications center answers emergency and non-emergency calls and requests for assistance in addition to dispatching for both the Fire and Police Departments.

### Public library

The City of Torrance operates a main library facility (named after former mayor [Katy Geissert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Geissert?action=edit\&redlink=1)) in the city Civic Center, as well as the North Torrance, Southeast, Walteria, El Retiro, and Isabel Henderson branch libraries.[^cite_note-89]

## Transportation

[Zamperini Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamperini_Field) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association): TOA [ICAO](14985.md): KTOA) is a general aviation airport. ### Rail

Union Pacific currently operates what is left of the [Pacific Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Electric)'s [San Pedro via Gardena Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_via_Gardena_Line) and [Torrance Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrance_Industrial_Lead) Line both built in 1911 (passenger service was provided until 1940, afterwards only the Torrance shop train was operated for employees). The [Pacific Electric Torrance Shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Electric_Torrance_Shops?action=edit\&redlink=1) were completed in 1918 and closed in 1955 two years after all passenger service was taken over by [Los Angeles Metropolitan Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metropolitan_Transit_Authority). Freight operations were taken over by PE's parent company, [Southern Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Pacific_Transportation_Company), in 1965. SP was merged into UP in 1996.

The [Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Metropolitan_Transportation_Authority) (LA Metro) plans to complete the [K Line Extension to Torrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Line_Extension_to_Torrance) of their [Metro Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_Rail) system from [Redondo Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redondo_Beach,_California) sometime between 2030 and 2033, though there are plans to accelerate the project as part of the [Twenty-eight by '28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-eight_by_'28) initiative so it can be done by the [2028 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Olympics).

Freight to Torrance is served by [BNSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSF_Railway) and [Union Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad). BNSF operates on the former [Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atchison,_Topeka_and_Santa_Fe_Railway) [Harbor Subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Subdivision) line originally built in the 1920s. AT\&SF was merged with [Burlington Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_Northern_Railroad) in 1996 to form BNSF.

### Proposed metro expansion

Main article: [K Line Extension to Torrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Line_Extension_to_Torrance)

There have been proposals to expand the [LA Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Metropolitan_Transportation_Authority) to Torrance, but these proposals have faced opposition by Torrance politicians. In 2023, members of the Torrance City Council, Aurelio Mattucci and Jon Kaji, sought to block the expansion, arguing that the Metro would bring crime and homelessness to Torrance.[^cite_note-91] Metro approved an extension of the K line from El Segundo to the [Torrance Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrance_Transit_Center) via the K line in January 2026.

## Education

### Primary and secondary schools

#### Public schools

[Torrance Unified School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrance_Unified_School_District) (TUSD) was established in 1947 and unified in 1948. The district comprises the City of Torrance, bordered by the Palos Verdes Peninsula on the south, the cities of Redondo Beach and Gardena on the north, the City of Los Angeles (Harbor Gateway) on the east and the Pacific Ocean on the west. The district's jurisdiction covers approximately 21 square miles (54 km<sup>2</sup>), and it operates 17 elementary schools, eight middle schools, five high schools (one of which is a continuation school), three adult education centers, and a child development center.



[Fern Elementary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern_Elementary_School_\(California\))

#### Private schools

Torrance also has several private schools. Catholic schools under the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Los_Angeles) include [Bishop Montgomery High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_Montgomery_High_School), Nativity Catholic School, St James Catholic School and St Catherine Laboure Catholic School. Protestant private schools include Ascension Lutheran School and First Lutheran School.[^cite_note-93]

In 1980 the [Lycée Français de Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Fran%C3%A7ais_de_Los_Angeles) bought the 6.2-acre (2.5 ha) former Parkway School property, located in the Hollywood Riviera section of Torrance, from TUSD.[^cite_note-94] This property became the Lycee's Torrance campus, and as of February 1990 the campus had 100 students. In November 1989 the Lycee sold the property for \$2.65 million to Manhattan Holding Co. and scheduled to transfer the students to its West Los Angeles campuses. As of February 1990 neighbors of the campus site were asking the City of Torrance to not modify the zoning of this property. The Lycee stated that the campus closed due to low enrollment.[^cite_note-95]

At one time, Coast Christian Schools (now [Valor Christian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valor_Christian_Academy)) maintained a high school campus in Torrance.[^cite_note-Campuses1999-96]

### Colleges and universities

Torrance is in the [El Camino Community College District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Camino_Community_College_District). The campus [El Camino College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Camino_College) is mostly outside the city limits in unincorporated [Alondra Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alondra_Park,_California), while a portion is in the Torrance City limits.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] El Camino College was founded in 1947, and the campus covers 126 acres (0.51 km<sup>2</sup>). As of 2011, the college enrolls over 25,000 students each semester.[^cite_note-99]

## Media

The *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* is the metropolitan area's newspaper.

The *[Daily Breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Breeze)*, a 70,000-circulation daily newspaper, is published in Torrance. It serves the [South Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bay,_Los_Angeles) cities of [Los Angeles County](79734.md). Its slogan is "LAX to LA Harbor". Herald Publications, media group started the *Torrance Tribune*, a community newspaper, which was started November 2010, it has a distribution of 15,000 newspapers to single-family homes and businesses in the City of Torrance.

Torrance CitiCABLE, shown on KNET 25.2, Spectrum 3, Frontier FiOS 31 is the government access channel. Programming includes news, sports, entertainment, information, public affairs, and city council meetings.

## Sister cities

In 1973, Torrance established a sister-city relationship with [Kashiwa, Chiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashiwa,_Chiba), [Japan](15573.md), as part of the [Sister Cities International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_Cities_International) program.[^cite_note-135] Since then, citizens of Torrance have regularly engaged in cultural exchange with Kashiwa through the guidance of the Torrance Sister City Association, which facilitates a Japanese cultural festival, a yearly student exchange program, and contact between officials of the two cities. [North High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_High_School_\(Torrance\)) is the official sister high school of Kashiwa Municipal High. Torrance has also had a sister-city relationship with [Konya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konya), [Turkey](11125639.md) since 1958.
