# Mass media in Japan

"Japanese media" redirects here. For popular entertainment media, see [Japanese popular culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_popular_culture).

The **mass media in Japan** include numerous [television](29831.md) and [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio) networks as well as [newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper) and [magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magazine) in [Japan](15573.md). For the most part, television networks were established based on capital investments by existing radio networks. [Variety shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_variety_show), [serial dramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_television_drama), and news constitute a large percentage of Japanese [evening shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late-night_talk_show).

Western movies are also shown, many with a [subchannel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subchannel) for English. There are all-English television channels on cable and satellite (with Japanese subtitles).

## Television networks

Main article: [Television in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_Japan)

There are 6 nationwide television networks, as follows:

- [NHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK) is a [public service broadcaster](178808.md). The company is financed through "viewer fees," similar to the [licence fee](48551.md) system used in the [UK](31717.md) to fund the [BBC](19344654.md). NHK deliberately maintains neutral reporting as a public broadcast station, even refusing to mention commodity brand names.

  [^cite_note-1]

  &#x20;NHK has 2 terrestrial TV channels, unlike the other TV networks (in the Tokyo region—channel 1 ([NHK General TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK_General_TV)) and channel 3 ([NHK Educational TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK_Educational_TV))).

- [Nippon Television Network System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Television_Network_System) (NNS)/[Nippon News Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_News_Network) (NNN) headed by [Nippon Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Television) (NTV). In the Tokyo region, channel 4. Affiliated with the *[Yomiuri Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomiuri_Shimbun)* newspaper.

- The [Tokyo Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Broadcasting_System) holding company owns the [Tokyo Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Broadcasting_System) (TBS) station (which is broadcast nationally) and the [Japan News Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_News_Network) (JNN) which supplies news programming to TBS and other affiliates. In the Tokyo region, channel 6. Affiliated with the *[Mainichi Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainichi_Shimbun)* newspaper. [Chubu-Nippon Broadcasting Co., Ltd.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubu-Nippon_Broadcasting_Co.,_Ltd.), a quasi-key station in Nagoya, is related to the *Chunichi Shimbun* newspaper.

- [Fuji Network System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_Network_System) (FNS) and the [Fuji News Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_News_Network) (FNN) share the flagship station [Fuji Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_Television). In the Tokyo region, channel 8. Part of the [Fujisankei Communications Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujisankei_Communications_Group), a [keiretsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keiretsu). [Tokai TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokai_TV), a quasi-key station in Nagoya, is related to the *Chunichi Shimbun* newspaper.

- [TV Asahi Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Asahi)/[All-Nippon News Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Nippon_News_Network) (ANN) headed by [TV Asahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Asahi). Affiliated with the *[Asahi Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahi_Shimbun)* newspaper, which owns nearly 25% of the station. In the Tokyo region, channel 5.

- [TV Tokyo Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Tokyo_Network) (TXN) headed by [TV Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Tokyo). Owned by [Nikkei, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikkei,_Inc.) In the Tokyo region, channel 7.

In addition, there is the [Japanese Association of Independent Television Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Association_of_Independent_Television_Stations) (JAITS), which consists of independent stations in the three major metropolitan areas (excluding Ibaraki, Shizuoka, and Osaka), and includes TV stations affiliated with the *[Chunichi Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chunichi_Shimbun)* newspaper such as [Tokyo MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_MX) and [TV Kanagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Kanagawa).

## Social media

[Facebook](7529378.md), [Twitter](9988187.md), [Instagram](31591547.md), and [Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_\(software\)), are the leading used media platforms in the Japanese industry.[^cite_note-\:0-2] [Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_\(software\)) is an app used for instant communication on electronic devices. Statistics show that Facebook use in Japan is at 47.75%, X (formerly [Twitter](9988187.md)) use is at 19.33%, [YouTube](3524766.md) use is at 13.9%, [Pinterest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinterest) use is at 10.69%, Instagram use is at 4.93%, and [Tumblr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumblr) use is at 2.29%.[^cite_note-3] In Japan, as of 2017, nearly 100% of residents are online, smartphone use is reaching 80%, and some form of social media is being used by over half of the population.[^cite_note-4]

### Specialty papers

Among niche newspapers are publications like the widely circulated [Nikkan Kogyo Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikkan_Kogyo_Shimbun) (The Business and Technology Daily News), the Buddhist organization [Sōka Gakkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%8Dka_Gakkai)'s daily [Seikyo Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seikyo_Shimbun) (聖教新聞), and [Shimbun Akahata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimbun_Akahata), the daily organ of the [Japanese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Communist_Party). Other niches include papers devoted entirely to predicting the results of horse races. One of the best-known papers in the genre is Keiba Book (競馬ブック). Shūkan Go (週刊碁) is a weekly newspaper that covers the results of professional [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_\(game\)) tournaments and contains hints on Go strategy.

As in other countries, surveys tend to show that the number of newspaper subscribers is declining, a trend which is expected to continue.

### Claims of media bias

Claims of media bias in Japanese newspapers and the mainstream media in general are often seen on blogs and [right-leaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netouyo) Internet forums, where the "mass media" (*masu-komi* (abbreviation of [mass communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_communication)) in Japanese) are often referred to as "mass garbage" (*masu-gomi*). Signs with this epithet were carried by demonstrators in Tokyo on 24 October 2010, at what was reportedly the first demonstration in Japan to be organized on [Twitter](9988187.md).[^cite_note-nico2010-7] Among the general public, the credibility of the press suffered after the [Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant](11986472.md) crisis, when reporters failed to press government and industry sources for more information, and official reports turned out to be inaccurate or simply wrong.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-ejc.net-9] [Kazuo Hizumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuo_Hizumi?action=edit\&redlink=1), a journalist turned lawyer, details structural problems in his book, 「マスコミはなぜマスゴミと呼ばれるのか？」, *"Masukomi wa naze masugomi to yobareru no ka?"*, (*"Why is mass media called mass garbage?"*), which argues that a complex network of institutions, such as elite bureaucrats, judiciary, education system, law enforcement, and large corporations, all of whom stand to gain from maintaining the status quo, shapes the mass media and communication in a way that controls Japanese politics and discourages critical thinking.[^cite_note-10]

## Key stations: television and radio

See also: [Radio in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_in_Japan)

In Japan, there are five [broadcasting stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting) which take the lead in the network of [commercial broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_broadcasting). The five stations are [Nippon Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Television), [Tokyo Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Broadcasting_System), [Fuji Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_Television), [TV Asahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Asahi), and [TV Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Tokyo). Their head offices are in [Tokyo](30057.md), and they are called **zaikyō kī kyoku** (在京キー局, Key stations in Tokyo) or **kī kyoku** (キー局, Key stations).

The key stations make [news shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_show) and entertainment programs, and wholesale them to local broadcasting stations through the networks. Although local broadcasting stations also manufacture programs, the usage of the key stations is very large, and 55.7% of the TV program total sales in the 2002 fiscal year (April 2002 to March 2003) were sold by the key stations. Furthermore, the networks are strongly connected with newspaper publishing companies, and they influence the media very strongly. For this reason, they are often criticized.[^cite_note-11]

In addition, there is [CS broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_television) and [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) distribution by the subsidiaries of the key stations. The definition of key station has changed a little in recent years.

### Outline

In Japan, every broadcasting company (except [NHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK) and [Radio Nikkei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Nikkei)) which performs [terrestrial television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_television) broadcasts has an appointed broadcast region. In Article 2 of the Japanese Broadcasting Law ([放送法](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%94%BE%E9%80%81%E6%B3%95)), the [Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Internal_Affairs_and_Communications) defines the fixed zone where the broadcast of the same program for every classification of broadcast is simultaneously receivable. So, the broadcasting company constructs a network with other regions, and with this network establishes the exchange of [news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News) or programs. The broadcasting companies which send out many programs to these networks are called key stations.

Presently the broadcasting stations located in Tokyo send out the programs for the whole country. However, although [Tokyo MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_MX) is in the Tokyo region, it is only a [Tokyo](30057.md) [region](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%9C%8C%E5%9F%9F%E6%94%BE%E9%80%81) [UHF independent station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Association_of_Independent_Television_Stations).

Broadcasting stations in [Nagoya](61720.md) and other areas are older than those in Tokyo. However, in order to meet the large costs of making programs key stations were established in Tokyo to sell programs nationwide. Some local stations have a higher profit ratio since they can merely buy programs from the networks.

#### Sub-key stations

Since the broadcasting stations which assign the head offices in [Kansai region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_region) (especially in [Osaka](52230.md)) have a program supply frame at [prime time](24973.md) etc. and sent out many programs subsequently to kī kyoku, they are called **jun kī kyoku** (準キー局, sub-key stations).
