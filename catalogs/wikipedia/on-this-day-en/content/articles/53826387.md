**LHS 1140** is a [red dwarf](56099.md) star in the [constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation) of [Cetus](6362.md). Based on [stellar parallax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_parallax) measurement, it is 48.8 [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year) (15.0 [parsecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsec)) away from the [Sun](26751.md).[^cite_note-GaiaDR3-2] 'LHS' refers to the [Luyten Half-Second Catalogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luyten_Half-Second_Catalogue) of stars with [proper motions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_motion) exceeding [half a second of arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minute_and_second_of_arc) annually.[^cite_note-7] The star is over 5 billion years old and has only about 18% the mass of the Sun and 21% of its radius.[^cite_note-Pineda2021-8] LHS 1140's rotational period is 130 days. No flares have been observed.[^cite_note-ST-20170419-9]

## Planetary system

As of October 2023, LHS 1140 is known to have two planets orbiting it. The inner planet is LHS 1140 c, a hot rocky planet; the outer planet, which was the first to be discovered, is LHS 1140 b, a water-rich super-Earth in the habitable zone.[^cite_note-Cadieux2023-5]

### LHS 1140 c

The existence of LHS 1140 c was first proposed by Feng et al. in July 2018[^cite_note-Feng-10] and confirmed by Ment et al. in August 2018, using the [transit method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_method) of detection. It has a mass about 1.9 times Earth's and a radius 1.3 times as large, giving it a density of about 5 g/cm<sup>3</sup>,[^cite_note-Ment2018-4] consistent with a rocky composition.[^cite_note-Cadieux2023-5] From eclipse observations, its dayside temperature has been measured at 561±44 [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin), consistent with a low-albedo planet with no atmosphere. The observations rule out pure [CO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) atmospheres with a pressure ≥10 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) and pure [H<sub>2</sub>O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_vapor) atmospheres ≥1 [bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)). This result is similar to other hot rocky planets around red dwarfs, such as [LHS 3844 b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LHS_3844_b) and [TRAPPIST-1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRAPPIST-1b).[^cite_note-Fortune2025-11]

### LHS 1140 b

Main article: [LHS 1140 b](53915700.md)

[LHS 1140 b](53915700.md) was discovered by the [MEarth Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEarth_Project) in 2017 using the transit method.[^cite_note-NAT-20170419-3] Follow-up radial velocities were measured by the [High Accuracy Radial Velocity Planet Searcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Accuracy_Radial_Velocity_Planet_Searcher) instrument to confirm the planet and measure its mass.[^cite_note-ST-20170419-9] The planet LHS 1140 b is a [super-Earth](10883868.md) in the [habitable zone](1072751.md) and transits the star every 24.7 days. This allows its atmosphere to be studied: the combination of the transiting super-Earth and the relatively small and nearby host star make this system one of the most promising known for atmosphere studies, along with the [TRAPPIST-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRAPPIST-1) system.[^cite_note-NAT-20170419-3][^cite_note-NYT-20170419-12] Observations by the [Hubble Space Telescope](40203.md) in 2020 found signs of [water vapor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_vapor) in the planet's atmosphere, but this has not been confirmed.[^cite_note-Edwards2021-13] Later observations with the [James Webb Space Telescope](434221.md) (JWST) suggest the presence of a [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen)-rich atmosphere.[^cite_note-Damiano2024-14][^cite_note-Cadieux2024-15]

[LHS 1140 b](53915700.md) was initially estimated to be about 7 times Earth's mass and about 1.4 times its radius, suggesting a dense rocky planet.[^cite_note-NAT-20170419-3] Later studies in 2018 and 2020 revised the radius upwards to about 1.7 times Earth's, giving it a density of about 7.5 [g/cm<sup>3</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_per_cubic_centimetre), still consistent with a rocky composition.[^cite_note-Ment2018-4][^cite_note-Lillo-Box2020-16] However, a 2023 study measuring the planet's mass and radius with greater precision found a lower mass of about 5.6 times Earth's, and a correspondingly lower density, no longer consistent with a rocky planet given the planet's size. LHS 1140 b is likely an [ocean planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_world) with 9-19% of its mass composed of water;[^cite_note-Cadieux2023-5] JWST observations rule out a hydrogen atmosphere, so it is not a [mini-Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini-Neptune).[^cite_note-Damiano2024-14][^cite_note-Cadieux2024-15]

### Search for additional planets

In July 2018, Feng et al. published a reanalysis of the [radial velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial_velocity_method) data for LHS 1140, and proposed the likely existence of two additional planets: an inner Earth-mass planet orbiting every 3.8 days (later confirmed as planet c) and an outer Neptune-mass planet orbiting every 90 days.[^cite_note-Feng-10] The orbital period of the outer planet candidate, LHS 1140 d, was refined to 78 days in 2020,[^cite_note-Lillo-Box2020-16] but this radial velocity signal was found to originate from stellar activity rather than a planet in 2023.[^cite_note-Cadieux2023-5]
