# List of FIFA World Cup top goalscorers

refer to caption

[Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md) holds the record at 22 goals.

Players have scored more than 3,000 goals[^cite_note-1] in the 23 men's [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) tournaments, the goal record includes [own goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) scored, but not counting [penalty shoot-outs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_penalty_shoot-outs).[^cite_note-2] Since the first goal, by [French](250197.md) player [Lucien Laurent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucien_Laurent) in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup),[^cite_note-milestones-3] nearly 1,300  footballers have scored goals at the World Cup tournaments,[^cite_note-worldfootball-4] of whom 111 have scored five or more.

The top goalscorer of the inaugural competition was [Argentina](454699.md)'s [Guillermo Stábile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_St%C3%A1bile), with eight goals. Since then, only 28 players have scored more World Cup goals in total than Stábile did during the 1930 tournament. The first to do so was [Brazil](149286.md)'s [Ademir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ademir_de_Menezes) in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), with nine goals. This was followed by [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team)'s [Sándor Kocsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1ndor_Kocsis) scoring eleven in [1954](60523.md). At the [1958 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup), France's [Just Fontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Fontaine) further improved on this record, recording thirteen goals in just six matches. [Gerd Müller](172313.md) then scored ten goals for [West Germany](250204.md) in [1970](157227.md), before breaking the overall record when he scored his fourteenth World Cup goal during West Germany's win in the [1974 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_final). Müller's record stood for more than three decades, until [Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_\(Brazilian_footballer\)) recorded fifteen goals between [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) for Brazil. Germany's [Miroslav Klose](201840.md) broke Ronaldo's record with his 16th goal in [2014](656933.md); his record stood until Argentina's [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) scored his 17th through 21st goals in [2026](36581929.md). At the same World Cup, France's [Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md) became the current record holder after scoring his 22nd goal.

Of all the players who have played at the World Cup, only six have achieved an average of two goals or more per match played: Kocsis, Fontaine, Stábile, [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team)'s [Oleg Salenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Salenko), [Switzerland](681862.md)'s [Josef Hügi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_H%C3%BCgi), and [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team)'s [Ernst Wilimowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Wilimowski) – the latter of whom scored four in his [only ever World Cup match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_v_Poland_\(1938_FIFA_World_Cup\)), played in 1938.[^cite_note-goalscorers-6] The top 111 goalscorers have represented 31 nations, with 15 players scoring for both Brazil and Germany or West Germany. In total, 75 footballers came from [UEFA](32332.md) (Europe), 31 from [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) (South America), and only five from elsewhere: [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team), [Ghana](1029620.md) and [Senegal](1017436.md) from [CAF](715265.md) (Africa), [Australia](4792249.md) from [AFC](715249.md) (Asia) (formerly from [OFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation) of Oceania), and the [United States](582488.md) from [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) (North/Central America).

Fontaine's thirteen goals in 1958 remains the record for the most scored in a single World Cup tournament. The players that came closest to this tally were Kocsis in 1954 (eleven goals), Müller in 1970 (ten goals), Mbappé in 2026 (ten goals), Brazil's Ademir in 1950 (nine goals) and [Portugal](576328.md)'s [Eusébio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bio) in [1966](61629.md) (nine goals). The top scorers with the fewest goals were from the [1962 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup), when six players finished joint-top with four goals each. Across the 23 tournaments of the World Cup, 31 footballers have been credited as the tournament top scorer; Mbappé is the only player to achieve this feat twice, in [2022](17742072.md) and 2026. Ten of these players scored at least seven goals in a tournament, while Brazil's [Jairzinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jairzinho) in 1970, Argentina's Lionel Messi in 2022 and 2026, and [England](9904.md)'s [Jude Bellingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_Bellingham) and [Norway](203277.md)'s [Erling Haaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erling_Haaland) in 2026 were the only footballers to record at least seven goals but still not finish as the tournament's top scorer. These 31 top goalscorers played for 19 nations, with the most (five) coming from Brazil. Another five came from other South American countries, with the remaining 21 coming from Europe.

Brazil's [Leônidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B4nidas_\(footballer,_born_1913\)) became the first player to score eight goals in knockout matches across his [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup) World Cup appearances, a feat that was surpassed by Mbappé in 2026 with a total of twelve (including eight from the previous [2018](13327177.md) and 2022 tournaments).[^cite_note-MostKnockoutGoals-7] Mbappé became the first player to score four goals in [World Cup final matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_finals): he netted one in the [2018 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_final) followed by a [hat-trick](411541.md#Association_football) in the [2022 final](70432805.md). England's [Geoff Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Hurst) is the only other player to record a hat-trick in a World Cup final, doing so in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_final).

## Top goalscorers in knockout matches

The format has varied considerably between different tournaments, resulting in different numbers of knockout matches per tournament. The 1934 and 1938 World Cups were exclusively knockout competitions. The 1950 edition featured no knockout games, as the champion was decided by a final group stage. Four others had very few: 1930 (three), 1974 (two), 1978 (two) and 1982 (four):[^cite_note-MostKnockoutGoals-7]

- The 1930 tournament featured only three knockout matches after the group stage, consisting of the two semi-finals and the final.

- In 1974 and 1978, a second group phase replaced the traditional quarter-finals and semi-finals used from 1954 through 1970, leaving only the final and third-place match as knockout fixtures.

- The 1982 tournament featured four knockout matches, consisting of the reintroduced semi-final stage alongside the third-place match and final. It also marked the last time a second group phase was used instead of direct knockout rounds after the group stage.
