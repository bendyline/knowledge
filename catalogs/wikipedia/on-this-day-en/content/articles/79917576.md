**Paramount Skydance Corporation** ([doing business as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_name) **Paramount**; formerly **Paramount, a Skydance Corporation**)[^cite_note-7] is an American [multinational](214491.md) [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media) and [entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment) [conglomerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\(company\)). The company's primary corporate headquarters was located at the [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures) lot in the [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles) neighborhood of [Los Angeles](18110.md), California,[^cite_note-HQ-5] and Paramount's secondary and operational headquarters, alongside many of Paramount's divisions and subsidiaries, as well as one of Paramount's major offices and meeting places is headquartered at the previous Paramount Global headquarters at [One Astor Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Astor_Plaza) in [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-8] [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS), a major Paramount asset, is also headquartered in New York City, but at the [CBS Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Building) and the [CBS Broadcast Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Broadcast_Center) both in [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan). Paramount Skydance also houses major offices and operations at the previous Skydance Media headquarters in [Santa Monica, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California). [Larry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Ellison) and [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison) control the company, owning 77.5% of the voting shares and 47.2% of all shares.[^cite_note-Quarterly_Report-1] [RedBird Capital Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RedBird_Capital_Partners) own 22.5% of the voting shares and 13% of all shares.[^cite_note-Form_10-K/A-2][^cite_note-Form_S-4-3]

Paramount Skydance was formed on August 7, 2025, by David Ellison, through the [merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Skydance_Media_and_Paramount_Global) of [Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media), [Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global), and [National Amusements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Amusements).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] The company trades under the ticker symbol "PSKY" on the [Nasdaq](21559.md).[^cite_note-13]

Shortly after its formation, Paramount Skydance began pursuing an unsolicited bid to [acquire Warner Bros. Discovery](81768101.md) and subsequently won a bidding war for the company in February 2026. Paramount Skydance's purchase of WBD was approved by the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md) on June 12, 2026.[^cite_note-14] A subsequent anti-trust lawsuit by 12 state attorneys general (led by California) initially led Paramount to delay the closure of the acquisition until June 1, 2027,[^cite_note-2027Merger-15] and a federal judge ruled in favor of holding a trial for the lawsuit in March 2027.[^cite_note-2027AntitrustTrial-16] Ultimately, on September 21, 2026, Paramount Skydance reached a settlement with the states, which included provisions for independent editorial boards at CNN and CBS News and a commitment by Paramount to release 30 films annually, with a \$30 million penalty for each film below that target.[^cite_note-17] On October 2, 2026, David Ellison announced that Paramount Skydance will be restructured and renamed to [Skydance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Corporation), and its stock will be traded on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker symbol "SKYD" upon the merger's forthcoming completion, which is scheduled to close on October 6, 2026.[^cite_note-18]

Through an assortment of corporate mergers, splits, and partnerships, [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures), [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS), [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_\(1952%E2%80%932005\)), and [Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media) shared a long history prior to the 2025 merger joining the entities as Paramount Skydance.[^cite_note-19]

### 1912–1986

Further information: [Paramount Pictures § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures#History), [Gulf and Western Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_and_Western_Industries), and [Viacom (1952–2005)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_\(1952%E2%80%932005\))

Paramount Pictures was founded in 1912 as the [Famous Players Film Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famous_Players_Film_Company).[^cite_note-20] CBS was founded in 1927, which Paramount Pictures held a 49% ownership stake in from 1929 to 1932.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

In 1952, CBS formed CBS Television Film Sales, a division which handled [broadcast syndication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_syndication) rights for CBS's library of network-owned television series.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] This division was renamed [CBS Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Films) in 1958[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] and again renamed CBS Enterprises Inc. on December 1, 1967.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] It was renamed [Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_\(1952%E2%80%932006\)) (an acronym of Video and Audio Communications) in 1970.[^cite_note-30] In 1971, this syndication division was [spun off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off) amid new [FCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) rules forbidding television networks from [owning syndication companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Interest_and_Syndication_Rules). These rules were eventually abolished completely in 1993.[^cite_note-31]

Meanwhile, Paramount Pictures was acquired by [Gulf and Western Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_and_Western_Industries) in 1966, which then re-branded itself as [Paramount Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Communications) in 1989.[^cite_note-32]

### 1986–2005

In 1986, Viacom purchased [MTV Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks) and [Showtime/The Movie Channel Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_Networks) from [Warner Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Communications) and [American Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Express).[^cite_note-33] In 1987, a majority stake in Viacom was acquired by theater operator company [National Amusements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Amusements).[^cite_note-34]

Viacom then purchased a 50.1% stake in Paramount Communications in February 1994 for \$9.75 billion, following a five-month battle with shopping channel [QVC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QVC). The merger was completed in July.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] In 1999, Viacom made its biggest acquisition to date by announcing plans to merge with its former parent [CBS Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Corporation_\(1997%E2%80%932000\)) (the renamed [Westinghouse Electric Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Electric_Corporation), which had merged with CBS in 1995). The merger was completed in 2000, resulting in CBS reuniting with its former syndication division.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-Wilkerson-39]

### 2005–2022

Further information: [Split of CBS Corporation and Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_of_CBS_Corporation_and_Viacom), [Viacom (2005–2019)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_\(2005%E2%80%932019\)), [CBS Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Corporation), [Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media), and [Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global)

On December 31, 2005, Viacom was split into two companies: [the second incarnation of CBS Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Corporation), the former's corporate successor, and [the second incarnation of Viacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viacom_\(2005%E2%80%932019\)), which was formed as a spin-off.[^cite_note-Wilkerson-39][^cite_note-40] The two companies re-merged in 2019, forming ViacomCBS, renamed to [Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global) in 2022.

[Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media) was founded in 2006 by [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison).[^cite_note-LA_Times-41] In 2009, Skydance and Paramount Pictures signed a five-year co-financing, production and distribution agreement, with Paramount holding an additional option of distribution.[^cite_note-LA_Times-41] That deal would later be extended twice in 2013 and 2017.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

## History

### Formation

Further information: [Merger of Skydance Media and Paramount Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Skydance_Media_and_Paramount_Global)

In 2023, after struggling with debt and striving to remain competitive in the entertainment industry, Paramount's parent company, [National Amusements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Amusements), explored potential merger and acquisition opportunities for Paramount Global. Numerous prominent companies, such as [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures), [Warner Bros. Discovery](67824355.md), [Apollo Global Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Global_Management), [Warner Music Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Music_Group), [Allen Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Media_Group), and [Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Media), had indicated their interest in exploring potential business partnerships or purchasing the company.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

After first coming to a merger deal with Skydance, Paramount and Skydance canceled the proposed merger on June 11 due to unsatisfactory conversations. Following a break in the talks, Skydance was able to reach a preliminary agreement on July 2, 2024, to perform a 3-way merger between it, National Amusements, and Paramount to establish what was then known as "New Paramount".[^cite_note-47] After the merger closed, Skydance Media [CEO](52234.md) [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison) became the chairman and CEO of the combined company and [Jeff Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Shell) became the president.[^cite_note-Mullin-48]

Paramount said in February 2025, and May 2025, that it expected the transaction to close within the first half of the year, but it did not happen.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] With the deal not yet approved, the first automatic extension to July 7, 2025, went into effect on April 8, 2025,[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] after which the second automatic extension to October 4, 2025, went into effect on July 7, 2025.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The [SEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission) and the [European Commission](9974.md) (EC) approved the transaction in February 2025.[^cite_note-55] On July 24, 2025, the FCC approved the merger between Paramount Global and Skydance Media.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

In September 2025, *The Wall Street Journal* and others reported that Paramount Skydance was weighing a takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery, a deal that, unlike the Skydance–Paramount tie-up, was expected to sidestep [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) review because WBD holds no broadcast licenses.[^cite_note-deadline-goldsmith-wbd-116] The idea drew immediate political scrutiny, with Senator [Elizabeth Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Warren) urging regulators to stop it,[^cite_note-awful-lerner-senatorwarren-117] while price talk swung widely: early chatter reached about \$70 billion,[^cite_note-bleedingfool-braly-70billion-118] mid-month commentary pointed to the mid-\$50 billion range,[^cite_note-Ponsi-55billion-119] and by September 19 reporting centered on scenarios "north of" \$45 billion alongside a potential \$22–\$24 per-share offer structure that could be 70–80% cash, with support from [Larry Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Ellison). By September 29, CNBC's [David Faber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Faber_\(journalist\)) suggested a friendly agreement looked unlikely and that Paramount might need to take any bid directly to WBD shareholders.[^cite_note-gurufocus-khan-stockfall-120][^cite_note-cnbc-salinas-update-121][^cite_note-fastco-taylor-45billion-122]

Industry voices spent the month debating what such a combination would mean. UTA chief David Kramer called it a mixed prospect for Hollywood,[^cite_note-deadline-hayes-kramer-123] and [FX Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_Networks) chairman [John Landgraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Landgraf) predicted that large-scale consolidation among legacy studios was essentially unavoidable.[^cite_note-deadline-kanter-landgraf-124] Strategically, observers noted the sheer scale on offer: Paramount Skydance's roughly 4,500-film (including the entire of [Miramax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miramax), pre-2005 [Dimension Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_Films), pre-February 1952 [United Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Artists) and pre-2011 [DreamWorks Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DreamWorks_Pictures) libraries), 200,000-episode library (including the CBS/[Paramount Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Television), [Spelling Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelling_Television), [Big Ticket Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ticket_Television) and libraries from the cable networks alongside the pre-1973 programming libraries of [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) and [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)) alongside Warner Bros.' catalog of about 10,000 films (including the pre-May 1986 [MGM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer) and pre-May 1982 [Orion Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_Pictures) libraries) and 120,000 episodes (including the [Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Broadcasting_System), [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO), [Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery,_Inc.) and [Scripps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Networks_Interactive) legacy libraries).[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] Much industry speculation centred around how this could change competition, and the line-up of streaming services such as [Pluto TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto_TV).[^cite_note-deadline-hayes-kramer-123][^cite_note-130] Financially, late-month research cautioned that even post-deal, most earnings power would still come from traditional TV: [Bernstein Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstein_Research) estimated roughly 70% of EBITDA would be tied to linear networks, even as [KPMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPMG) highlighted US content spend surpassing \$200 billion and continuing to rise.[^cite_note-friedman-mediapost-bernstein-131]

On December 5, Netflix [announced](81768101.md) that they would be buying the Warner Bros. streaming and studio company for \$72 billion after the split closes in the third quarter, valuing WBD at \$82.7 billion. Although Netflix ultimately won the bidding process, the transaction was still subject to [regulatory approval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_approval) in multiple jurisdictions.[^cite_note-132] Amid the ongoing antitrust review, reports indicated that Paramount Skydance might consider launching a [hostile takeover bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostile_takeover_\(disambiguation\)) should the approval process create an opening.[^cite_note-133] Three days later, Paramount launched a hostile takeover bid of WBD for an enterprise value of \$108.4 billion.[^cite_note-134] Paramount argued its \$30 per share all-cash offer was superior to the \$27.75 per share offer from Netflix, and that a combination with Paramount faced lower regulatory hurdles.[^cite_note-135] On December 11, Paramount Skydance was considering raising its takeover offer for Warner Bros. Discovery by as much as 10% as it plots its next move to break up a merger agreement with Netflix. The new offer of Warner Bros. for Paramount would now be \$33 per share for \$90 billion, while the new offer of Warner Bros. for Netflix would now be \$30.75 per share for \$86 billion.[^cite_note-136] On December 17, WBD told its shareholders to reject Paramount's bid, instead proceeding with the Netflix acquisition.[^cite_note-137]

On January 7, 2026, it was reported by *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* that Warner Bros. Discovery's board of directors had rejected the amended offer by Paramount Skydance to acquire the entire company, with the board writing in a [SEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Securities_and_Exchange_Commission) filing that they considered the Paramount Skydance offer to be of "insufficient value", and that the offer was "inadequate", as they believed the Netflix offer was of better value to their shareholders, with the board sticking with the Netflix offer for Warner Bros. Discovery's Streaming and Studios division.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

On February 15, 2026, *Bloomberg* reported that Warner Bros. Discovery was considering reopening sale talks with Paramount Skydance.[^cite_note-140] On February 20, 2026, the company announced its bid had passed the second request review process undertaken by the Department of Justice, with the company saying "there was no statutory proposal to close down the acquisition".[^cite_note-141]

On February 26, 2026, Warner Bros. Discovery announced that they found Paramount's offer of \$31 per share was superior to the deal Netflix made.[^cite_note-142] Shortly after, Netflix announced they were not going to raise their bid and pulled out, with Paramount emerging as the winner of the bidding war.[^cite_note-143] On February 27, the studios revealed that the deal is worth \$110 billion.[^cite_note-144] According to David Zaslav, the acquisition is expected to close within 6–12 months, pending regulatory and shareholder approval.[^cite_note-145] If the deal closes, it would be the largest all-cash transaction in corporate history, ahead of [Microsoft](19001.md)'s \$67 billion acquisition of [Activision Blizzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activision_Blizzard) in 2023, and would mark the second [horizontal integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_integration) of two legacy media conglomerates and film studios after the [2019 Disney-Fox merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_21st_Century_Fox_by_Disney).[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147][^cite_note-Disney_Old-148]

On April 8, 2026, Jeff Shell departed Paramount Skydance as president.[^cite_note-ShellDepartureDeadline-149][^cite_note-ShellDepartureTHR-150] The departure came a month after Robert James "R.J." Cipriani filed a \$150 million lawsuit against Shell and his wife for breach of contract and fraud complaint, in which it also lists Paramount's CEO [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison), his father [Larry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Ellison), and several other advisors. Cipriant accused Shell of "gossiping about deals surrounding [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC), [WBD](67824355.md), and [Donald Trump](4848272.md)";[^cite_note-ShellDepartureDeadline-149][^cite_note-ShellDepartureTHR-150] Shell has since denied the allegations, calling it an "utterly false tale created in a shakedown attempt".[^cite_note-ShellLawsuitVar-151] Paramount Skydance later came out saying that Shell exited the company with a severance package of \$5 million; the contract set an annual base salary of \$3.5 million, as well as a \$1.5 million bonus.[^cite_note-ParaSkyResponse-152]

On April 23, 2026, shareholders of Warner Bros. Discovery approved the sale to Paramount Skydance.[^cite_note-153]

On June 12, 2026, the Paramount-WBD deal was approved by the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md)'s [Antitrust Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_Antitrust_Division).[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

A coalition of 12 U.S. states: Arizona, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Nevada, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Oregon, and Washington all announced that they filed a lawsuit, and they were all seeking to block the proposed Paramount Skydance and Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition.[^cite_note-156]

On July 20, 2026, it was announced that a federal judge ordered Paramount Skydance and Warner Bros. Discovery to halt their \$81B merger for at least two weeks, allowing states that were challenging the deal more time to see their case through in court. The states' top prosecutors called on Warner and Paramount to not close the transaction until after a court had time to "fully evaluate" their claims. And when the companies refused, they filed for a temporary restraining order - which was what District Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín granted. A Warner-Paramount tie-up would bring together two of the five last legacy studios in Hollywood - as well as host of TV networks, titles filling streaming libraries and news operations. A hearing in the court would be on August 3, 2026, for a hearing on the states' preliminary injunction motion, although that schedule could also be pushed back.[^cite_note-157]

On September 21, 2026, Paramount and the state attorneys general have settled the blockbuster antitrust lawsuit over the \$110.9 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, clearing the way to close the Paramount-WBD deal.[^cite_note-158] The deal included \$52 billion financing that included varying degrees of debt and loans. Initial demand was more than 3x for the \$30 billion investment grade tranche.[^cite_note-159]

On September 30, 2026, it was reported that Paramount intends to complete the Warner Bros. acquisition on October 6, 2026. It is also reported that the new name and logo for the newly-merged entity will be announced before this date.[^cite_note-160]

On October 2, 2026, it was announced that co-chairs and CEOs of Warner Bros. Pictures [Pamela Abdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Abdy) and [Michael De Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_De_Luca) would be exiting after the close of the Paramount and Warner Bros. merger.[^cite_note-161] It was also announced that, post-acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, Paramount Skydance would be restructured and rebranded to [Skydance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Corporation).[^cite_note-162]

Paramount Skydance comprises 3 major business segments:

- **Paramount Skydance Studios** consists of the company's film and television production studios.&#x20;

  - **Paramount Motion Picture Group** focuses on theatrical [film production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmmaking) and [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_distribution), including film releases under [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures), the company's namesake division and flagship label as well as the [Paramount Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Animation), [Paramount Players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Players), Paramount Primal and [Nickelodeon Movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Movies) labels. Other assets include [Skydance Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Animation) (and its animation studio division, [Skydance Animation Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Animation_Madrid)), [Republic Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Pictures#As_a_Paramount_Global/Paramount_Skydance_Corporation_subsidiary) and a 49% stake in [Miramax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miramax).

    [^cite_note-163]

    &#x20;Paramount Pictures also consists of Paramount Studio Group (physical studio and post-production), production facilities and lot, and archives for restoration/preservation for [Paramount Home Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Home_Entertainment) and [Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Music).

  - **Paramount Television Group** which consists of [Paramount Television Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Television_Studios) and [CBS Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Studios) (including BET Studios and [Nickelodeon Animation Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_Animation_Studio) (including [Avatar Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar_Studios))), both focusing on television production.

  - **Paramount Experiences** focuses on the retailing and licensing of merchandising for [Paramount Consumer Products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Consumer_Products), and also consists of some theme parks, and gaming at [Paramount Games Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Consumer_Products#Paramount_Games_Studio).

    [^cite_note-164]

  - **Paramount Sports Entertainment** focuses on scripted and unscripted sports programming, as well as interactive sports games and experiences. The division also oversees [Skydance Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skydance_Sports), a joint venture with the [NFL](21211.md).

- **Paramount Skydance Direct-to-Consumer** focuses on the global [over-the-top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-top_media_service) streaming services, and encompasses [Paramount+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount+), [Pluto TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto_TV), [SkyShowtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyShowtime) (50% with [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast) through [Sky Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Group)), [CBS News 24/7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_24/7), [CBS Sports HQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports_HQ), and an undisclosed stake in [FuboTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FuboTV), which it acquired in 2020.

  [^cite_note-165]

- **Paramount Skydance TV Media** encompasses the company's domestic and international linear television channels. The division also oversees See It Now Studios and a 50% stake in South Park Digital Studios.&#x20;

  - **[CBS Entertainment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Entertainment_Group)** oversees [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS), [CBS News and Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_and_Stations), [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports) and [CBS Media Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Media_Ventures) (including [Big Ticket Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ticket_Television)).

  - **[BET Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET_Media_Group)** which owns channels with the [BET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BET) name.

  - **[Paramount Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Media_Networks)** encompasses the pay television channels owned by Paramount in the United States such as [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV), [Nickelodeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon) (including [Nick Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Jr._Channel)), [Comedy Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_Central), [TV Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Land), [Paramount Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Network), [Logo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo_TV), [CMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMT_\(American_TV_channel\)), [Pop TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_\(American_TV_channel\)), [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_\(TV_network\)), [The Movie Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Movie_Channel), [Flix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flix_\(TV_network\)), [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1) and the [Smithsonian Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Channel).

  - **[Paramount International Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_International_Networks)** encompasses certain international versions of their cable channels split into three regional hubs: [United Kingdom and Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Networks_UK_&_Australia), [Europe, Middle East, Africa and Asia (EMEAA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Networks_EMEAA), and [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Networks_Americas), as well as region-specific free-to-air networks (including [Channel 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_5_\(British_TV_channel\)) in the United Kingdom and [Network 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_10) in Australia). It also co-owns channels that were previously under the CBS name across Europe with [AMC Networks International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Networks_International).

  - **[Paramount Global Content Distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Global_Content_Distribution)** serves as the international television distribution arm for Paramount's television networks and streaming services.

The former subsidiaries of [National Amusements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Amusements) such as [Showcase Cinemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showcase_Cinemas) have been sold as of summer 2026.[^cite_note-166]
