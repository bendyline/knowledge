The **National Shiite Movement** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): التيار الوطني الشيعي), also known as the **Sadrist Movement** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): التيار الصدري, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-Tayyār al-Sadrī*), is an [Iraqi](7515928.md) [Shia](26961.md) [Islamic](6037917.md) national movement and political party, led by [Muqtada al-Sadr](351058.md).

The Sadrist Movement ended as largest political party in the October [2021 Iraqi parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Iraqi_parliamentary_election), with 73 seats in Parliament, but in June 2022, during the [2021–2022 Iraqi political crisis](71007490.md), Muqtada al-Sadr’s bloc resigned from parliament.[^cite_note-indep-3]

## 2009 governorate elections

In the [2009 governorate elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Iraqi_governorate_elections), the Sadrist Movement took part as the **Independent Free Movement List**, receiving 9.8% of the vote and winning 43 out of 440 seats, in third place behind the [State of Law Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Law_Coalition) and the [Islamic Supreme Council of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Supreme_Council_of_Iraq).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## 2010 parliamentary election

In the [2010 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Iraqi_parliamentary_election), the Sadrist Movement ran as part of the [National Iraqi Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iraqi_Alliance). At a press conference on 6 March, ahead of the election]], Muqtada al-Sadr called on all Iraqis to participate in the election and support those seeking the [withdrawal of U.S. troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_U.S._troops_from_Iraq_\(2007%E2%80%932011\)) from the country. Al-Sadr warned that any interference by the United States would be unacceptable. Al-Sadr, who had thousands of staunch followers across Iraq, had been consistently opposing the presence of foreign forces and repeatedly called for an immediate end to the [Iraq War](5043324.md).[^cite_note-HA-7]

## Involvement in the Syrian civil war

In October 2012, various Iraqi religious sects joined the conflict in Syria on both sides. Shiites from Iraq, in [Babil Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babil_Governorate) and [Diyala Governorate](377806.md), have traveled to [Damascus](8914.md) from [Baghdad](4492.md), or from the Shiite holy city of [Najaf](200512.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), claiming to protect [Sayyida Zeinab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyida_Zeinab), an important Shiite shrine in Damascus.[^cite_note-Iraqi_Sects_Join_Battle_in_Syria_On_Both_Sides_October_28,_2012-10] Abu Mohamed, with the Sadrist trend, said he recently received an invitation from the Sadrists' leadership to discuss the shrine in Damascus.[^cite_note-Iraqi_Sects_Join_Battle_in_Syria_On_Both_Sides_October_28,_2012-10] A senior Sadrist official and former member of Parliament, said that convoys of buses from Najaf, under the cover story of pilgrims, were carrying weapons and fighters to [Damascus](8914.md).[^cite_note-Iraqi_Sects_Join_Battle_in_Syria_On_Both_Sides_October_28,_2012-10] Some of the pilgrims were members of [Iran](14653.md)'s [Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps](1285472.md)' Elite [Quds Force](4028355.md).[^cite_note-Iraqi_Sects_Join_Battle_in_Syria_On_Both_Sides_October_28,_2012-10]

However, later in 2017 following the [Khan Shaykhun chemical attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Shaykhun_chemical_attack) in Syria, Muqtada al-Sadr called for Syria's president [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md) to step down from power.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## 2014 parliamentary elections

In the [2014 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Iraqi_parliamentary_election), the Sadrists formed the **Al-Ahrar Bloc** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): كتلة الأحرار, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Kotlat Al-Ahrar* or *freeones Bloc*), headed by [Dia al-Asadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dia_al-Asadi), which strongly opposed a third term for al-Maliki and his [State of Law Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Law_Coalition). Al-Ahrar won 7% of the vote and 34 seats, making it the second largest group in the Iraqi parliament, after the [State of Law Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Law_Coalition), which won 24% and 92 seats. ## 2018 parliamentary elections

In preparation for the [2018 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Iraqi_parliamentary_election), Sadr withdrew the Al-Ahrar Bloc from parliament and urged its MPs not to stand in the May poll, in order to make way for a new list known as [Alliance Towards Reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Towards_Reforms), mainly composed of the Sadrist Movement and the [Iraqi Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Communist_Party). This list finished as the largest party in the election on 12 May, with 14.38% of the votes and 54 seats in Parliament.

## 2021 parliamentary elections

The Sadrist Movement ended as the largest political party in the 10 October [2021 Iraqi parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Iraqi_parliamentary_election), with 10% of the votes and 73 seats in Parliament. On 13 June 2022, during the [2021–2022 Iraqi political crisis](71007490.md), 74 MPs from Muqtada al-Sadr's bloc resigned from parliament.[^cite_note-indep-3]
