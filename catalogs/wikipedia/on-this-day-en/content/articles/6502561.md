**Mian Muhammad Shehbaz Sharif**[^cite_note-3] (born 23 September 1951) is a Pakistani politician and businessman who has served as the [prime minister of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Pakistan) since 2024, being the only person to hold it for two consecutive terms, having previously been in the role between 2022 and 2023. He has also served as the [chief minister of Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_minister_of_Punjab_\(Pakistan\)) three times, spanning over 12 years, making him the longest-serving person in the role and the [president of the Pakistan Muslim League (N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(N\)#Leaders).

Sharif was elected to the [Punjab Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_the_Punjab) in 1988 and later to the [National Assembly of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan) in 1990, when his brother became head of the conservative [Islami Jamhuri Ittihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islami_Jamhuri_Ittihad) (IJI). He was re-elected to the Punjab Assembly in 1993 and appointed [leader of the opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_of_Punjab_\(Pakistan\)). He was elected as chief minister of Pakistan's most populous province, [Punjab](24751.md), for the first time on 20 February 1997. After the [1999 Pakistani coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Pakistani_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), Sharif and his brother were arrested and exiled the next year to [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) at the request of the Saudis, returning to Pakistan in 2007. Sharif was elected chief minister for a second term after the PML(N)'s victory in Punjab in the [2008 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Pakistani_general_election). He was elected as chief minister of Punjab for the third time after [provincial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Punjab_provincial_election) in 2013. Sharif was nominated as the president of the PML(N) after his brother, [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif), was disqualified from holding office in the wake of the [Panama Papers case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers_case).[^cite_note-4]

Sharif was nominated as the [leader of the opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Pakistan\)) after the [2018 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Pakistani_general_election) and elected as prime minister in April 2022 after the [no-confidence motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-confidence_motion_against_Imran_Khan) against [Imran Khan](413184.md) was passed. After the PML(N) won a plurality in the controversial [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Pakistani_general_election), which was widely marred by [allegations of electoral rigging and irregularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegations_of_rigging_in_the_2024_Pakistani_general_election) in his party's favour, Sharif became prime minister with a coalition government.&#x20;

During his [tenure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehbaz_Sharif_government), Pakistan has faced [high inflation and economic difficulties](71036397.md), leading to greater reliance on petroleum levies and indirect taxation. His government has been criticized for increasing privileges for [parliamentarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Pakistan), introducing [constitutional amendments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan) affecting the [judiciary and military command structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-seventh_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan), and its treatment of the political opposition. He has also faced domestic and international criticism over restriction on free expression, [violence against journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_against_journalists_in_Pakistan), human rights violations, misuse of cyber laws, and digital censorship. Critics have accused his government of weakening [fundamemntal rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_rights_in_Pakistan) and institutions and of seeking greater influence over judicial and military appointments. He has also been accused by opposition groups and other critics of benefiting from irregularities and alleged rigging in the 2024 general elections, allegations that have been disputed by the government.&#x20;

On foreign policy, he received international praise for Pakistan's diplomatic role in efforts to [mediate](82923583.md) between [Iran and the United States](82737344.md) and for strengthening defence cooperation with other countries through the [Mecca defence pact](83903032.md).

## Early life and education

Sharif was born on 23 September 1951[^cite_note-pakistantoday/30july2017-5][^cite_note-hindustantimes/29july2017-6][^cite_note-7] in [Lahore](125315.md), [West Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Punjab).[^cite_note-dawn/13jan2012-8] His [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_family) is [Punjabi with Kashmiri ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiris_in_Punjab). Sharif's father, [Mian Muhammad Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mian_Muhammad_Sharif), was an upper-middle-class businessman and industrialist whose family had emigrated from [Anantnag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anantnag_district) in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) for business, and eventually settled in the village of [Jati Umra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jati_Umra_\(Amritsar\)) in [Amritsar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar_district), Punjab, at the beginning of the twentieth century. His mother's family came from [Pulwama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulwama).[^cite_note-9] Following the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) and Pakistan's independence in 1947, his parents migrated from Amritsar to Lahore.[^cite_note-10] He has two brothers: [Abbas Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Sharif) and [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif). Nawaz is a three-time elected prime minister of Pakistan, serving the longest non-consecutive tenure in the role.[^cite_note-11] Nawaz's wife, Begum [Kulsoom Nawaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulsoom_Nawaz), was the first lady of Pakistan for three non-consecutive terms and his daughter, [Maryam Nawaz](34181325.md) is the current [chief minister of Punjab](9228428.md).[^cite_note-12]

Sharif attended [St. Anthony High School, Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anthony_High_School,_Lahore), and received a Bachelor of Arts degree from the [Government College University, Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_College_University,_Lahore).[^cite_note-pap/profile-13] After his graduation, he joined his family-owned [Ittefaq Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ittefaq_Group). He was elected president of [Lahore Chamber of Commerce & Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Chamber_of_Commerce_&_Industry) in 1985.[^cite_note-dawn/13jan2012-8][^cite_note-dawn/25april2013-14]

## Early political career

Sharif began his political career after getting elected to the [Punjab Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_Punjab) from Constituency PP-122 (Lahore-VII) as a candidate of the [Islami Jamhoori Ittehad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islami_Jamhoori_Ittehad) (IJI) in the [1988 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Pakistani_general_election).[^cite_note-dawn/25april2013-14][^cite_note-ecp/pa88-97-15][^cite_note-thenews/1june2013-16] He had 22,372 votes and defeated a candidate of the [Pakistan People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_People's_Party) (PPP).[^cite_note-ecp/pa88-97-15] However, his term prematurely ended in 1990 when the assemblies were dissolved.[^cite_note-pap/profile-13][^cite_note-dawn/2aug2008-17]

He was re-elected to the Provincial Assembly of Punjab from Constituency PP-124 (Lahore-IX) as an IJI candidate in the [1990 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Pakistani_general_election). He received 26,408 votes and defeated a candidate of the [Pakistan Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Democratic_Alliance) (PDA).[^cite_note-ecp/pa88-97-15] In the same election, he was elected to the [National Assembly of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan) from Constituency NA-96 (Lahore-V) as a candidate of IJI. He secured 54,506 votes and defeated [Jehangir Bader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehangir_Bader).[^cite_note-dawn/25april2013-14][^cite_note-ecp/na-88-97-18] He vacated the Punjab Assembly seat to retain his National Assembly seat.[^cite_note-thenews/1june2013-16] His term prematurely ended in 1993[^cite_note-pap/profile-13] when the assemblies were dissolved.[^cite_note-dawn/2aug2008-17]

In April 1993, the widow of Chief of Army Staff General [Asif Nawaz Janjua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asif_Nawaz_Janjua), alleged that Brigadier [Imtiaz Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imtiaz_Ahmed_\(brigadier\)), head of the [Intelligence Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Bureau_\(Pakistan\)), [Nawaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawaz_Sharif), Shahbaz Sharif, and [Chaudhary Nisar Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhary_Nisar_Ali_Khan) had planned to eliminate her husband.[^cite_note-auto2-19][^cite_note-auto-20]

He was re-elected to the Punjab Assembly from Constituency PP-125 (Lahore-X) as a candidate of the [Pakistan Muslim League (N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(N\)) (PML(N)), a new party founded by his brother, Nawaz, in the [1993 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Pakistani_general_election).[^cite_note-thenews/1june2013-16] He received 28,068 votes and defeated a candidate of PPP.[^cite_note-ecp/pa88-97-15] In the same election, he was re-elected to the National Assembly from Constituency NA-96 (Lahore-V) as a candidate of the PML(N). He secured 55,867 votes and defeated [Yousuf Salahuddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousuf_Salahuddin).[^cite_note-ecp/na-88-97-18] He vacated the National Assembly seat and retained his Provincial Punjab Assembly seat.[^cite_note-dawn/25april2013-14] Shortly after the election, he was elected Leader of the Opposition in the Punjab Assembly.[^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21] During his tenure as Leader of the Opposition, he travelled to the [United Kingdom](31717.md) for medical treatment. In his absence, [Chaudhry Pervaiz Elahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhry_Pervaiz_Elahi) was made Acting Leader of the Opposition in the Punjab Assembly.[^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21][^cite_note-dawn/29nov2002-22] His term as a member of the Punjab Assembly and Leader of the Opposition prematurely ended in November 1996[^cite_note-pap/profile-13] when the assemblies were dissolved.[^cite_note-dawn/25april2013-14][^cite_note-dawn/2aug2008-17]

## Chief Minister of Punjab (1997–1999) and exile

Main articles: [Chief ministership of Shehbaz Sharif § First term (1997–99)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_ministership_of_Shehbaz_Sharif#First_term_\(1997%E2%80%9399\)), [First Shehbaz Sharif provincial government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Shehbaz_Sharif_provincial_government), and [1999 Pakistani coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Pakistani_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)

Sharif was re-elected to the Punjab Assembly from Constituency PP-125 (Lahore-X) as a candidate of the PML(N) in the [1997 Punjab provincial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Punjab_provincial_election).[^cite_note-thenews/1june2013-16] He received 25,013 votes and defeated a candidate of PPP.[^cite_note-ecp/pa88-97-15] In the same election, he was re-elected to the National Assembly from Constituency NA-96 (Lahore-V) as a candidate of PML(N). He secured 47,614 votes and defeated [Hanif Ramay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanif_Ramay).[^cite_note-ecp/na-88-97-18] He was elected as the [Chief Minister of Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chief_Ministers_of_Punjab_\(Pakistan\)) for the first time and was sworn in as 13th Chief Minister of Punjab on 20 February 1997.[^cite_note-pakistantoday/30july2017-5][^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21]

Sharif focused on healthcare, education, agriculture and the promotion of industry in [Punjab](24751.md).[^cite_note-dawn/13jan2012-8][^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21] He undertook several development projects in Lahore and launched a crackdown on criminals across the province to maintain law and order.[^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21] Sharif introduced a series of educational reforms such as reformed exams in a variety of subjects, as well as self-finance schemes for students.[^cite_note-dawn/13jan2012-8]

He held his office until 12 October 1999, when he was removed from the post of Chief Minister in the [1999 Pakistani coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Pakistani_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-dawn/13jan2012-8][^cite_note-pap/profile-13][^cite_note-dawn/25april2013-14][^cite_note-dawn/2aug2008-17]

In 1999, a complainant, Saeeduddin, lodged a [first information report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_information_report) and accused Sharif of allowing the [Punjab Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Police_\(Pakistan\)), as chief minister, to kill his son along with other men in a fake encounter.[^cite_note-23] In the encounter, his two sons and three other people were killed by the police.[^cite_note-dawn/2march2008-24][^cite_note-25] Following the coup Sharif was imprisoned.[^cite_note-pap/profile-13] In December 2000, he, along with his immediate family members, struck a deal with [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf) negotiated by the Saudi royal family and went into exile in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21][^cite_note-thenews/5nov2006-27][^cite_note-dawn/13jan2012-8]

While in exile in Saudi Arabia, Sharif was elected as the PML(N) President in August 2002 and moved to the United Kingdom in 2003 for medical treatment.[^cite_note-thenews/5nov2006-27][^cite_note-dawn/25april2013-14][^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21]

In 2003, an anti-terrorism court summoned Sharif and five others accused in alleged extrajudicial killings in 1998. He was in the exile at that time and failed to show-up to the court. Later, the court issued an arrest warrant for Sharif.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-bbc/7sept2007-29] In 2004, Sharif attempted to return to Pakistan to appear before the court, but was forcibly deported back to Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21][^cite_note-bbc/7sept2007-29]

In August 2007, the [Supreme Court of Pakistan](843857.md) gave its verdict which allowed the Sharif and Nawaz Sharif to return to Pakistan.[^cite_note-30] In September 2007, an anti-terrorism court in Pakistan ordered police to arrest Sharif based on a 2003 arrest warrant.[^cite_note-bbc/7sept2007-29][^cite_note-31] He later received bail from an anti-terrorism court.[^cite_note-32] Sharif denied ordering the alleged killings and said the charges against him were politically motivated.[^cite_note-bbc/7sept2007-29] He further added that in 2004, he had landed at [Lahore Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Airport), wanting to appear before the court, but was sent back to Saudi Arabia by the government in violation of the orders of the Supreme Court.[^cite_note-33] In 2008, he was acquitted by the Anti-Terrorism Court in the [Sabzazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabzazar) case.[^cite_note-dawn/2march2008-24]

Sharif was re-elected as PML(N) President for a second term in August 2006[^cite_note-dawn/25april2013-14][^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21] and returned to Pakistan along with Nawaz Sharif in November 2007.[^cite_note-thenews/25nov2007-21]

Sharif was ineligible to take part in the [2008 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Pakistani_general_election) due to the Sabzazar case, but a few months later he was acquitted by the court.[^cite_note-peoplesdaily/9june2008-34][^cite_note-dawn/2march2008-24]

On 14 July 2019, the *[Daily Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mail)* published a news with the headline: "Did the family of Pakistani politician who has become the poster boy for British overseas aid STEAL funds meant for earthquake victims?" According to the report, Shehbaz stole aid funds from the [UK](31717.md)'s [Department for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_International_Development) (DFID) following the [2005 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kashmir_earthquake). It was written by *Daily Mail* journalist [David Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rose_\(journalist\)). Investigations have shown that the donated more than £500 million to the earthquake victims in Pakistan through the DFID, a UK government organisation.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

David Rose claimed Shehbaz used the aid funds to send one million pounds through money laundering to his frontman Aftab Mehmood, a British citizen of Pakistani descent, who then gave the money to Shehbaz's family. Shehbaz and his family allegedly stole British citizens' tax relief funds, according to news reports. Salman Shehbaz, Shehbaz's son, refuted this information.[^cite_note-37]

A DFID representative referred to the reports as "baseless" and "fabricated" on the organisation's website, and in January 2020, Shehbaz filed a claim for damages against the *Daily Mail* and Rose in the [Royal Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Court_of_Justice) in [London](17867.md).[^cite_note-38]

According to court documents, the *Daily Mail* took almost three years to submit a defence of Rose's story. On 20 April 2020, Justice [Matthew Nicklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Nicklin) issued the initial order for the listing hearing window running from 21 April 2020 to 31 July 2020. On 7 May 2020, Justice Nicklin issued a second ruling that extended the due dates. On 20 October 2020, Justice Nicklin issued the first order, merging the claims of Shehbaz and his son-in-law Yousaf. On 28 January 2021, Justice Nicklin issued another order pertaining to the preliminary issue trial.[^cite_note-auto1-39] The verdict and order were delivered on 5 February 2021. Justice Nicklin ruled at the meaning hearing at the [London High Court](26569402.md) in favour of the PML(N). Justice Nicklin determined that the *[Mail on Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mail_on_Sunday)'s* article carried the highest level of defamatory meaning for both Shehbaz and Yousaf.

On 18 February 2021, Justice Nicklin issued the directive with relation to certain deadlines. On 15 March 2022, the *Daily Mail* filed its defence. On 17 March 2022, Master Thornett issued the directive to file the defence. On 23 June and 20 September 2022, he then issued the orders to extend the deadline for filing the defence. Following negotiations to resolve the dispute after March 2022 between *Daily Mail* and Shehbaz, it was made apparent to his legal counsel that the publication would issue an apology and take down the item under his conditions. On 26 September 2022, Justice Nicklin issued an order and scheduled a joint case management meeting for 9 November.[^cite_note-auto1-39] Three days prior to this hearing, Shehbaz pulled his request for a delay in favour of a full trial in a shrewd political move. According to the regulations, the court was not informed that the lawyers for *Daily Mail* had been secretly negotiating with Shehbaz's lawyers for several months, proposing to apologise. Shehbaz was persuaded by his legal staff that there was no use in submitting more paperwork as the paper had already agreed to apologise and remove the defamatory and misleading piece.[^cite_note-auto1-39]

*Daily Mail* publishers and Shehbaz's lawyers signed an agreement of settlement with Tomlin Order in the second week of December 2022, after which *Daily Mail* removed the defamatory article and apologised to the prime minister and his son-in-law. The ANL promised that it will never propagate these false allegations at any forum, it has also worked with [Google](1092923.md) to remove all articles carrying *Daily Mail's* previous allegations.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-auto1-39][^cite_note-41]

## Chief Minister of Punjab (2008–2018)

Main articles: [Chief ministership of Shehbaz Sharif § Second and third term (2008–13)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_ministership_of_Shehbaz_Sharif#Second_and_third_term_\(2008%E2%80%9313\)), [Chief ministership of Shehbaz Sharif § Third term (2013–2018)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_ministership_of_Shehbaz_Sharif#Third_term_\(2013%E2%80%932018\)), [Second Shehbaz Sharif provincial government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Shehbaz_Sharif_provincial_government), and [Third Shehbaz Sharif provincial government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Shehbaz_Sharif_provincial_government)

Sharif was re-elected to the Punjab Assembly unopposed from [Constituency PP-48 (Bhakkar-II)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency_PP-48_\(Bhakkar-II\)) as a PML(N) candidate in the by-election held in June 2008.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Subsequently, he was elected unopposed as the Chief Minister of Punjab, receiving 265 votes unopposed after securing 265 votes.[^cite_note-dawn/25april2013-14][^cite_note-thenews/1june2013-16] Later, in June 2008, while holding the Assembly seat from Constituency PP-48 (Bhakkar-II), Sharif contested for a seat in the Punjab Assembly from [Constituency PP-10 (Rawalpindi-X)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency_PP-10_\(Rawalpindi-X\)). He was elected unopposed but resigned from this seat due to a dispute regarding his eligibility to serve as chief minister.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-nation/24june2008-45]

His second term as chief minister was interrupted on 25 February 2009 when the Supreme Court of Pakistan declared him ineligible to hold public office, resulting in the vacancy of his seat in the Punjab Assembly and his removal from the office of chief minister.[^cite_note-46] On 1 April 2009, a five-member larger bench of the Supreme Court overturned the earlier decision, reinstating Sharif as chief minister.[^cite_note-thenews/1april2009-47]

In the [2013 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Pakistani_general_election), Sharif was re-elected to the Punjab Assembly from three Constituencies PP-159 (Lahore-XXIII), PP-161 (Lahore-XXV) and PP-247 (Rajanpur-I), as a PML(N) candidate. In the same election, he was re-elected to the National Assembly from [Constituency NA-129 (Lahore-XII)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency_NA-129).[^cite_note-ecp/2013-48] Sharif chose to retain his Provincial Assembly seat PP-159 (Lahore-XXIII) and was re-elected as chief minister of Punjab for the third time, receiving 300 votes in the 371-member Provincial Assembly.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

In August 2014, a dissenting note by members of a joint investigation team, composed of representatives of the [Inter-Services Intelligence](2159021.md) (ISI), [Military Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_\(Pakistan\)) (MI), [Intelligence Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Bureau_\(Pakistan\)) (IB), and other branches, stated that Chief Minister Shehbaz, Law Minister [Rana Sanaullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rana_Sanaullah), Dr Tauqeer Shah, and other senior police officers were directly or indirectly responsible for the killing of over a dozen [Pakistan Awami Tehreek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Awami_Tehreek) (PAT) workers in the [Model Town Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_Town_Massacre).[^cite_note-51] Similarly, a sessions court in [Lahore](125315.md) ordered ordered police to register murder charges against 21 individuals, including Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif and Shahbaz Sharif for the massacre.[^cite_note-52]

### Intra-party politics

See also: [Panama Papers case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers_case)

In 2016, Sharif was elected unopposed as the president of the PML(N)'s Punjab chapter in intra-party elections.[^cite_note-53]

On 29 July 2017, following the disqualification of outgoing Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif after the [Panama Papers case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers_case) decision, Sharif was named leader of the PML(N) and their candidate for the office of prime minister.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] However, he was not a member of the National Assembly, so [Petroleum Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_Division_\(Pakistan\)) [Shahid Khaqan Abbasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid_Khaqan_Abbasi) was asked to serve as the interim prime minister of Pakistan, allowing Sharif enough time to enter the legislature through a by-election.[^cite_note-57] Later, Sharif decided to complete his tenure as chief minister until the next general election.[^cite_note-indianexpress/3aug2017-58][^cite_note-59]

In December 2017, Sharif was named as the candidate of PML(N) for the office of prime minister in the 2018 general election.[^cite_note-thenews/21dec2017-60][^cite_note-61]

In February 2018, Sharif was appointed the interim president of the PML(N).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] In March 2018, he was elected unopposed as the president of PML(N).[^cite_note-64] It was reported that [the establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Establishment_\(Pakistan\)) had previously offered Sharif the position of prime minister of Pakistan on several occasions, which he did not accept.[^cite_note-indianexpress/30july2017-65]

## Leader of the Opposition (2018–2022)



Sharif at an anti-[Imran Khan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imran_Khan_government) rally in Islamabad, November 2019

Sharif was elected to the National Assembly as a candidate of PML(N) from Constituency NA-132 (Lahore-X) in the [2018 Pakistani general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Pakistani_general_election). In the same election, he was re-elected to the Provincial Assembly of Punjab as a candidate of PML(N) from Constituency PP-164 (Lahore-XXI) and PP-165 (Lahore-XXII).[^cite_note-dawn/17aug2018-66] Following his successful election, he abandoned his Punjab seats in favour of the National assembly seat.[^cite_note-67]

On 16 August 2018, he was nominated by PML(N) for the office of Prime Minister of Pakistan.[^cite_note-68] The following day, he secured 96 votes, losing to [Imran Khan](413184.md).[^cite_note-69] Later, he was nominated for the office of [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Pakistan\)) in the National Assembly by 111 members of the National Assembly.[^cite_note-70] On 20 August 2018, he was notified as Leader of the Opposition in the National Assembly. Concurrently, he served as Chairman of the Public Accounts Committee from 22 December 2018 until 28 November 2019, when he resigned and was succeeded by [Rana Tanveer Hussain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rana_Tanveer_Hussain).

In September 2020, the [National Accountability Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Accountability_Bureau) (NAB) arrested Sharif on charges of laundering [Rs](588111.md). 7,328 million (US\$41 million) through a purported scheme involving close associates and family members.[^cite_note-71] The NAB had previously frozen 23 properties owned by Sharif, his son, and other relatives, alleging these assets were acquired through unknown sources of income and that foreign funds were funnelled via fake transactions through a company owned by Sharif's son-in-law, Haroon Yousaf.[^cite_note-72]

Following his arrest, Sharif was held in custody during the investigation.[^cite_note-73] In November 2020, he was temporarily released on parole to attend his mother's funeral prayers.[^cite_note-74] On 14 April 2021, he was granted bail by the [Lahore High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_High_Court) in the money laundering case.[^cite_note-75]

## Prime Minister (2022–2023)

Main articles: [Cypher No. I-0678](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypher_No._I-0678), [First premiership of Shehbaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_premiership_of_Shehbaz_Sharif), and [First Shehbaz Sharif government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Shehbaz_Sharif_government)

On 10 April 2022, Sharif was nominated as a candidate for prime minister by opposition parties following the [no-confidence motion against Imran Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-confidence_motion_against_Imran_Khan) after the [2022 Pakistani constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pakistani_constitutional_crisis).[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] He was elected prime minister on 11 April 2022.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-Elected_PM_Dawn-79] He took the oath of office on the same day, administered by the Chairman of the Senate, [Sadiq Sanjrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiq_Sanjrani), acting for President [Arif Alvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arif_Alvi), who was on medical leave after complaining of "discomfort".[^cite_note-80]

On 13 May 2022, it was reported that the [Federal Investigation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Investigation_Agency) (FIA) would not pursue the money laundering cases against Shehbaz and his son, Chief Minister of Punjab [Hamza Shehbaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamza_Shehbaz). These reports were based on a submission from FIA Special Prosecutor Sikandar Zulqarnain Saleem, who stated that the FIA's Director General, through the investigating officer, conveyed to Saleem that he should not continue to appear in this case because the defendants were about to be elected to the positions of Prime Minister and Chief Minister of Punjab. Additionally, Saleem wrote that "the [quarters concerned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Establishment_\(Pakistan\)) are not interested in prosecuting the accused persons." Judge Ijaz Hassan Awan of the Special Court (Central-I) made this application part of the case file.[^cite_note-81]

This came days after the death of Dr. Rizwan, the FIA director who had led the money-laundering investigation against Shehbaz and his son, and had been placed on the no-fly list by Shahbaz's government.[^cite_note-82] The following month, Maqsood Chaprasi, another suspect in the case who was a peon for the Sharif family, also died from cardiac arrest in Dubai before the proceedings concluded.[^cite_note-83] Earlier investigations in 2020 by the [National Accountability Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Accountability_Bureau) (NAB) had also focused on Nisar Ahmed, who NAB described as a front man for the Sharif family, and Mushtaq Cheeni, had also revealed being involved in money laundering for the Sharif family.[^cite_note-84]

On 12 October 2022, Prime Minister Sharif and his son were acquitted on all charges of corruption and money laundering by the Special Court Central in Lahore.[^cite_note-aljazeera.com-85] After the verdict, Sharif's lawyer Amjad Pervez described the case as "totally baseless and politically motivated."[^cite_note-aljazeera.com-85] Reacting to the verdict, former Prime Minister [Imran Khan](413184.md) said that the acquittal covered up their theft worth billions and was a "slap in the nation's face by the judicial system".[^cite_note-86]

### Domestic policy

Sharif's main priority upon his ascension as prime minister in 2022 was to resolve the severe [economic crisis](71036397.md) that began that year, as rising fuel prices due to the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), excessive borrowing, poor macroeconomic decision-making, a balance of payments crisis, among other factors had accumulated into a severe crisis that strained the nation's economy. Sharif also vowed to investigate [Imran Khan's allegations of American intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypher_No._I-0678) to instigate the latter's removal from power, vowing to step down if an "iota of evidence" was found against him.[^cite_note-87] Sharif promised to enact electoral reforms to ensure that free and fair elections occurred.[^cite_note-88]

#### Economic policy

See also: [2022–23 Pakistan federal budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Pakistan_federal_budget), [2023–24 Pakistan federal budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Pakistan_federal_budget), [Pakistani economic crisis (2021–2024)](71036397.md), and [Pakistan and the International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_the_International_Monetary_Fund)

Soon after Sharif's swearing-in ceremony, the government announced a relief package that included various measures such as raising the minimum wage from Rs. 21,000 to Rs. 25,000, subsidising wheat flour, a 10% raise in pensions, and a government employee salary reduction to less than Rs. 100,000 rupees.[^cite_note-\:1-89] He also revived the [Benazir Income Support Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Income_Support_Programme) for poverty reduction, expanding it to educational aid.[^cite_note-\:1-89]

In order to resolve skyrocketing fuel prices, Sharif sought to amplify energy production from power projects. He expressed his intentions to resolve the lack of profit from Pakistan's coal of reserves, suggesting that the [Thar Engro Coal Power Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_Engro_Coal_Power_Project) could be connected to other coal-powered plants and amplify production to up to 4,000 [megawatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatts). Sharif further suggested that the Thar Project could revive the national economy and save the government up to \$6 billion as the expenditure on energy imports rose to \$24 billion.[^cite_note-90]

Under Sharif's premiership, Pakistan's [inflation rate](38286.md) skyrocketed to a multi-decade high of 27.3% in September 2022[^cite_note-91] and 37.97% in May 2023.[^cite_note-92] In January 2023, 7 million textile workers became unemployed due to decreasing exports and the government's failure to end the economic crisis.[^cite_note-93]

Despite negotiations with the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF) for a relief package for Pakistan's economy, the [Pakistani rupee](588111.md) continued to depreciate under Sharif's tenure, with increasing inflation, unemployment, and low profits damaging business in Pakistan. The government's target of \$7.7 billion for FY 2023 for foreign commercial loans also slumped, with only \$0.2 billion gained from July to October 2022.[^cite_note-94]

Sharif's government continued negotiations with the IMF for a relief package to mitigate the effects of the economic crisis, with a staff-level agreement reached in June 2023 for a \$3 billion stand-by arrangement.[^cite_note-95] In July 2023, the IMF's executive board approved the loan and begin a disbursement of roughly \$1.2 billion. To secure the loan, Sharif's government instituted austerity measures in its annual budget, raising taxes by \$750 million and hiking its interest rate to 22%, measures to curb inflation.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

#### 2022 Pakistan floods

Main article: [2022 Pakistan floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pakistan_floods)

In 2022, [Sindh](28972.md) and [Balochistan](4687.md) received more rainfall than the August average, with 784% and 500% more, respectively.[^cite_note-98] In addition to this rise in rainfall, southern Pakistan experienced [back-to-back heat waves in May and June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_heat_wave_in_India_and_Pakistan) 2022, which were record-setting and made more likely by [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Pakistan). These conditions created a strong [thermal low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_low) that brought heavier rains than usual. On 25 August 2022, Sharif's government declared a nationwide [state of emergency](298608.md) due to the floods, which killed around 1,700 people and caused over roughly \$30–40 billion in damage.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]



Map comparing Sindh from before the 2022 floods and after

In response to the floods and farmer sit-ins over electricity and fuel bills, Sharif launched the Kissan Package for farmers' relief, forming a federal committee and unveiling a Rs. 600 billion package.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-\:2-105] As a part of this package, the government would provide Rs. 50 billion subsidised loans to young entrepreneurs in the agriculture sector across the country, alongside Rs. 10 billion to small and medium enterprises in the agriculture sector. In addition to these measures, the package rolled out Rs. 5 billion to landless farmers affected by the floods, and the provision of 1.2 million bags of certified seeds to flood-affected farmers. In regards to cost divisions, Sharif stated that the federal government would provide 50% of the contributions while the four provinces would provide the other 50%.[^cite_note-\:2-105] Sharif's government also reduced duties on [knock-down kits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knock-down_kit) tractor parts from 35% to 15%, and wheat imports were enhanced to 2.6 million tonnes in order to prevent a national wheat shortage. These measures in the Kissan Package were implemented to enhance the agriculture sector and its related sectors such as the fertiliser industry, and particularly to mitigate the harmful economic effects of the floods on the country.

#### Energy policy

See also: [Global energy crisis (2021–2023)](68934947.md)

Sharif's ascension as prime minister was also marked by an [energy crisis in Pakistan](68934947.md) as a result of a failure to open completed projects and global fuel and energy prices hiking due to the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) and the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] In June 2025, Sharif's government opted out of an [LNG](832128.md) import of \$39.8 per [MMBTU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMBTU) because of its unaffordability, instead relying on nationwide [loadshedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loadshedding). As a result of this decision, Sharif warned Pakistanis to prepare for loadshedding for the following month as he stated that his government would try and make an energy duel for LNG imports possible.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] In July 2022, Sharif ordered the resumption of energy production from closed power plants such as the [Punjab Thermal Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Thermal_Power_Plant) and the [Sahiwal Coal Power Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahiwal_Coal_Power_Project). Projects such as these two plants were delayed because of a lack of ownership or the breakdown of contractual commitments.[^cite_note-111]

Under his premiership, Sharif began the transition all government buildings to [solar power](13690575.md) to reduce the government's fuel import bill. He added that this move would be an example for the country's provincial governments, urging provincial leaders to follow suit and standardise the use of solar power for government buildings.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

### Foreign affairs

See also: [List of international prime ministerial trips made by Shehbaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_prime_ministerial_trips_made_by_Shehbaz_Sharif)



Sharif at the 2022 [Shanghai Cooperation Organisation](152422.md) summit in [Samarkand](51491.md), Uzbekistan



Sharif's Foreign Minister Bilawal Bhutto Zardari meets with US Secretary of State [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) in September 2022

Sharif's government sought to improve ties with the [United States](3434750.md) after strained relations due to [Lettergate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lettergate), despite Pakistan's close ties with [China](5405.md).[^cite_note-114] Sharif met US President [Joe Biden](145422.md) in [New York City](645042.md) in September 2022 during the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) annual summit.[^cite_note-115] During the meeting, Biden pledged support to Pakistan in the aftermath of the devastating floods. Sharif and Foreign Minister [Bilawal Bhutto Zardari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilawal_Bhutto_Zardari) rejected the statement as baseless, and the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](13048437.md) summoned the US ambassador for an explanation of Biden's remarks.[^cite_note-116]

Sharif also sought closer ties with China and the amplification of the [China–Pakistan Economic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Pakistan_Economic_Corridor). However, relations grew increasingly complicated due to a rise in attacks on Chinese workers by groups such as the [Pakistani Taliban](18953699.md) and [Balochistan Liberation Army](6010135.md), as the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) is responsible for the security of CPEC workers.[^cite_note-117] Sharif visited China on a two-day official visit in November 2022, meeting with Chinese President [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) and Premier [Li Keqiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Keqiang), and visiting after the [20th National Congress of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_National_Congress_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party). Sharif pledged to revitalise CPEC and expand trade.[^cite_note-118]

Sharif's first visit as prime minister was to [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) in April 2022 on a three-day official visit, where he met with Saudi Crown Prince [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md). Sharif's government also secured an \$8 billion package from Saudi Arabia in May 2022, alongside an expansion to a \$2.4 billion oil financing facility funded by Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-119] Sharif visited the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) and [Qatar](8238258.md) to secure investments in Pakistan; he expressed his desire to facilitate a strong and healthy business environment in Pakistan for Qatari investors during a Pakistan-Qatar trade and investment roundtable meeting in [Doha](26214389.md); 2022.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

Sharif visited [Turkey](11125639.md) and met with Turkish President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) on several occasions, seeking to further deepen Pakistan-Turkey relations and enhance trade. He invited Turkey to join CPEC.[^cite_note-122]

Sharif's government also inaugurated a new power transmission line with [Iran](14653.md) during a ceremony in [Pishin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pishin_Valley) along the [Iran–Pakistan border](11715031.md) with Iranian President [Ebrahim Raisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebrahim_Raisi), facilitating the transmission of up to 100 megawatts of Iranian electricity to Pakistan. Sharif's government also remained committed to the construction of an [Iran–Pakistan gas pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Pakistan_gas_pipeline).[^cite_note-123]

Sharif visited the [United Kingdom](31717.md) during the [funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral_of_Elizabeth_II) of [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II) in September 2022 as the [head of government](37570.md) of a [Commonwealth republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_republic), offering condolences to [Charles III](125248.md) and meeting with British prime minister [Liz Truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Truss).[^cite_note-124] Sharif again visited the United Kingdom for the [coronation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Charles_III_and_Camilla) of Charles III and [Queen Camilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Camilla), attending the ceremony at [Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey) and meeting with British prime minister [Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak).[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

## Between premierships

See also: [2022–2025 Pakistan political unrest](73265939.md) and [2024 Pakistani general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Pakistani_general_election)

On 9 August 2023, Sharif suggested the dissolution of parliament to President [Arif Alvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arif_Alvi), three days before the end of its term.[^cite_note-127] On 14 August, the 76th [independence day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Pakistan\)) of Pakistan, Sharif's term ended as prime minister and [Anwaar ul Haq Kakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwaar_ul_Haq_Kakar) was appointed [caretaker prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caretaker_Prime_Minister_of_Pakistan) until a general election would be held.[^cite_note-128]



PML(N) supporters during the party's welcome rally in Lahore for Nawaz Sharif's return to Pakistan. October 2023

On 21 October 2023, Sharif welcomed the return of his brother, Nawaz Sharif, to Pakistan after four years of self-imposed exile. Nawaz returned to Pakistan after obtaining protective bail two days prior, which allowed him to return without being arrested. Nawaz's acquisition of protective bail was a result of the Shehbaz government's adoption of a law which allowed for review petitions against previous Supreme Court verdicts to be filed.[^cite_note-129] Shehbaz was a part of the PML(N) welcome rally for Nawaz in Lahore, where tens of thousands of PML(N) supporters awaited Nawaz's arrival.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

On 22 December 2023, the [Election Commission of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_Pakistan) ruled that the PTI would not be allowed to use its party symbol in the upcoming elections, citing that the party failed to hold intra-party elections up to the commission's standards. This decision was widely controversial and viewed as pre-poll rigging by eliminating PTI opposition, thus clearing the way for Nawaz Sharif to win the election and become prime minister with ease.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

A [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Pakistani_general_election) was held on 8 February 2024, with Nawaz Sharif serving as the PML(N) candidate for prime minister. However, Nawaz did not get his projected easy victory as PTI candidates, running as independents, managed to secure the most seats.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] The PML(N) won the second most seats while the PPP won the third most; however, neither the independent candidates or the two largest parties were able to secure the 169 seats needed for a majority. On 20 February, the PML(N) and PPP formed a minority coalition government, and Shehbaz was chosen as the prime ministerial candidate over Nawaz. As a part of the coalition arrangement, senior PPP leader [Asif Ali Zardari](993655.md), was chosen as the coalition's candidate for president. The general election was marred with controversy as [Pakistan's military establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Establishment_\(Pakistan\)) was accused of rigging the election to prevent the PTI from winning. International media also denounced the election while members of the international community voiced concern over its fairness.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

## Prime Minister (2024–present)

See also: [Second Shehbaz Sharif government](76333965.md)

On 3 March 2024, Sharif was re-elected as prime minister for a second term after a disputed [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Pakistani_general_election), receiving 201 votes against 92 for the [PTI](1651925.md) candidate, [Omar Ayub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Ayub), in the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan).[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] He formed a [coalition government](271434.md) with the [Pakistan People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_People's_Party), [Muttahida Qaumi Movement – Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muttahida_Qaumi_Movement_%E2%80%93_Pakistan), the [Pakistan Muslim League (Q)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Muslim_League_\(Q\)), the [Istehkam-e-Pakistan Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istehkam-e-Pakistan_Party), and the [Balochistan Awami Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_Awami_Party).[^cite_note-form_a_coalition-145][^cite_note-Shehbaz_prevails-146]

### Economy

See also: [2024–25 Pakistan federal budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Pakistan_federal_budget) and [Uraan Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uraan_Pakistan)



With Azerbaijani President [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) and Turkish President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) during the 17th summit of the [Economic Cooperation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Cooperation_Organization), July 2025

Immediately after his swearing-in, Sharif directed preparations for a national economic revival plan. He opened talks with the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF) on a new loan, ordered the [Federal Board of Revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Board_of_Revenue) o clear tax refunds worth Rs65 billion, and announced that loss-making state-owned enterprises (SOEs) would be privatised.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] He appointed [Muhammad Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Aurangzeb), a former president of [Habib Bank Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habib_Bank_Limited) and [JPMorgan](231001.md) banker, as [finance minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_minister_of_Pakistan).[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

The government negotiated a bailout agreement with the IMF in July 2024 and secured a \$7 billion programme in September.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] To meet IMF fiscal targets, it raised taxes substantially and brought private companies into the new tax scheme, raising the exporters' rate from 1% to 29% on top of a 30% [corporate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_tax), the highest in South Asia.[^cite_note-153] In August 2024, Sharif's government announced plans to renegotiate contracts with [independent power producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_power_producer) to reduce costs.[^cite_note-154]

The government later launched *[Uraan Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uraan_Pakistan),* a [five-year plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-year_plan) centred on what it termed the "five Es": Exports; E-Pakistan; Equity and Empowerment; Environment, Food and Water Security; and Energy and Infrastructure.[^cite_note-155] Its targets included achieving a 6% annual GDP growth by 2028, the creation of 1 million jobs per year, boosting exports to \$60 billion annually by 2028, and \$10 billion worth of private investment annually. The plan also envisaged Pakistan to emerge as a [trillion dollar economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trillion_dollar_club_\(macroeconomics\)) by 2035.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] Amid austerity measures, the government increased the monthly salaries of ministers and advisers by 188% to Rs519,000, drawing criticism.[^cite_note-159]

Beginning in May 2025 the government began privatising more than 50 SOEs, excluding "strategic" ones, in three phases over four years..[^cite_note-160] The IMF had recommended the move because the SOEs hold substantial assets but employ few people, and over half operate at a loss.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162] Inflation fell to 3.5% that month.[^cite_note-163]

The [2026 Iran war](82533582.md) strained the economy, as Pakistan sources nearly 90% of its energy imports from the Middle East.[^cite_note-\:6-164] In March 2026, in response to [rising global fuel prices](82562375.md) caused by the Iran war, Sharif announced emergency energy-saving measures, including a four-day work week and school closures.[^cite_note-165] Following the IMF's third programme review in May 2026, the fund cut its growth forecast for the fiscal year 2026-27 to 3.5% and raised its inflation forecast to 8.4%.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-\:6-164] On 5 June, the government presented a Rs18.77 trillion (\$67.49 billion) budget for 2026-27, with higher defence spending and targets of 4% growth and 8.2% inflation, and with reduced taxes on property purchases meant to stimulate construction. Analysts warned that meeting the IMF's revenue target would be difficult.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] On 13 September, amid continued rises in fuel prices, Sharif announced a relief scheme for users of motorcycles, [rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickshaw), and vehicles of up to 800[cc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_centimetre).[^cite_note-169]

### Social policy and environment

Main article: [Prime Minister's Youth Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister's_Youth_Programme)

&#x20;Sharif's government has promoted youth empowerment and entrepreneurship. On [International Youth Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Youth_Day) in 2024, Sharif announced an Rs.100 billion boost for over 300,000 young entrepreneurs as a part of the Prime Minister's Youth Programme, in addition to the launch of several youth empowerment schemes.[^cite_note-170] He also announced that the government would provide 1 million smartphones, tablets, and laptops to students across the country on the basis of merit, alongside personally funding the education of 1,000 graduates of [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) in China.[^cite_note-171] Sharif also created a youth employment action plan across Pakistan. As a part of this plan, a laptop scheme provided over 600,000 laptops on the basis of merit to young Pakistanis, in addition to youth training initiatives across 268 universities across the country under the Prime Minister Green Youth Program.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] Sharif also formed the [National Youth Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Youth_Council), composed of 113 youth representatives (aged 10–29), aimed at amplifying youth development and integrating young Pakistanis into policy-making.[^cite_note-174]



At the opening ceremony of [COP29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COP29) with Azerbaijani President [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) and United Nations Secretary-General [António Guterres](471806.md) in [Baku](4566.md), November 2024

At the [November 2024 United Nations Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference) in [Baku](4566.md), Sharif presented Pakistan’s priorities for climate, emphasising the need for global "climate justice."[^cite_note-\:3-175] Moreover, he revealed his government's National Climate Finance Strategy (NCFS), which sought to reduce Pakistan's greenhouse gas emissions to 50% of the national economy, leveraging 60% of clean energy resources, and shifting 30% of vehicles to [electric vehicles](279350.md) (EVs) by 2030.[^cite_note-\:3-175]

Sharif's government has enhanced climate cooperation with international partners including through the [Shanghai Cooperation Organisation](152422.md)'s Green Belt Programme and the [Mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) Sustainability Initiative, a project to protect, restore, and manage mangrove ecosystems across the globe.[^cite_note-176]

On [World Health Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Day) 2024, Sharif vowed to expand medical facilities across Pakistan to be accessible for all people, partnering with provincial governments to upgrade and establish medical facilities, launch mobile health clinics, and ensure cost-free treatment for lower-and-middle class families.[^cite_note-177] Sharif also ordered the use of advanced technology to advance digitised health reforms across the country, alongside the creation of oversight mechanisms to crack down on healthcare violations.[^cite_note-178]

### Constitutional changes

Main articles: [Twenty-sixth Amendment to the Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-sixth_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan) and [Twenty-seventh Amendment to the Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-seventh_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan)

In September 2024, Sharif's government announced a "Constitutional Package" which would create a new [Federal Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Constitutional_Court_of_Pakistan). This new court would handle petitions pertaining strictly to interpretations of constitutional clauses. The package also included raising the retirement age of judges in the proposed Constitutional Court to 68, while other judges in Pakistan are required to retire by 65. The package also included a clause that would rule individual legislators' votes against their respective parties as void.[^cite_note-179] In October 2024, Parliament passed the [Twenty-sixth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-sixth_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan), which expanded parliamentary powers, particularly allowing a parliamentary committee to select the Supreme Court's chief justice.[^cite_note-180] The new amendment was heavily criticised as attacking judicial independence, particularly amplifying judicial action against the PTI and other political opponents. According to the [New York City Bar Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Bar_Association), the amendment undermined the [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) and judicial independence in Pakistan, hindering the impartiality of the judiciary.[^cite_note-181]

In November 2025, Parliament passed the [twenty-seventh Amendment to the Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-seventh_Amendment_to_the_Constitution_of_Pakistan) proposed by Sharif's government, which asserted the authority of the Federal Constitutional Court—in which judges are selected by the executive branch, and modified Pakistan's military structure.[^cite_note-\:4-182] The amendment created the post of [Chief of Defence Forces](81566574.md)—given to Field Marshal [Asim Munir](58712552.md)—which oversees all three branches of the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md), and provides Munir with lifelong immunity from criminal prosecution.[^cite_note-\:4-182] The [Human Rights Commission of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Commission_of_Pakistan) questioned the government's urgency in passing the amendment, while [Reema Omer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reema_Omer), a legal adviser for the [International Commission of Jurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Commission_of_Jurists), warned that the new amendment only emboldens the executive and legislative branches, weakening the authority of the judiciary and degrading any balance of power within the government.[^cite_note-\:5-183] She described the amendment as a "constitutional surrender."[^cite_note-\:5-183]

### Counterterrorism and security

Main article: [Operation Azm-e-Istehkam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Azm-e-Istehkam)

In June 2024, Sharif chaired a meeting of the Apex Committee on the [National Action Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Action_Plan_\(Pakistan\)) in [Islamabad](51153.md), and announced the launch of [Operation Azm-e-Istehkam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Azm-e-Istehkam), a major [counterterrorism](83392.md) initiative that involved both military action and socioeconomic development to hinder the spread of radicalism and [terrorism](30636.md) in Pakistan.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] The launch of the operation came amidst a rise in terrorism in Pakistan in the aftermath of the [Taliban takeover of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_takeover), with militant groups such as the Pakistani Taliban taking shelter in [Afghanistan](737.md) to use it as a base for attacks on Pakistani security forces.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188]

Sharif's government has [repeatedly accused India](317645.md#India) of financially supporting the Pakistani Taliban and other militant groups that operate in [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) and [Balochistan](4687.md),[^cite_note-189] describing the Pakistani Taliban as *Fitna-al-khawarij* (lit. 'Conflict of [Kharijites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharijites)') and Baloch rebel groups as *Fitna-al-Hindustan* (lit. 'Conflict of India').[^cite_note-190]

Sharif condemned the [hijacking of the *Jaffar Express*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Jaffar_Express_hijacking) on 11 March 2025, describing it as a 'cowardly act.' He stated that the perpetrators, whom he identified as members of the [Balochistan Liberation Army](6010135.md) (BLA), were enemies of the province's development and reaffirmed the government's resolve to eliminate terrorism from the region.[^cite_note-191]

Sharif's government's continued and expanded the [deportation of undocumented Afghans from Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_of_undocumented_Afghans_from_Pakistan) that first began under the [Anwaar ul Haq Kakar caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwaar_ul_Haq_Kakar#Caretaker_Prime_Minister_\(2023-2024\)) in December 2023, citing these undocumented Afghans as a security threat.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] As of late 2025, the UNHCR confirmed that more than one million Afghans have returned from Pakistan to Afghanistan in 2025.[^cite_note-194] Following directives from Sharif's government, this campaign has expanded beyond undocumented individuals to include holders of Afghan Citizen Cards (ACC) and Proof of Registration (PoR) cards, with thousands of arrests reported monthly in early 2026.[^cite_note-195]

### Civil-military relations



With Chief of Army Staff [Asim Munir](58712552.md) at the [White House](33057.md), September 2025

During his premiership, Sharif has maintained positive relations with the [Pakistani military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_military). Sharif publicly stated that his government and the military were “on the same page” regarding all key national issues, including national security, counterterrorism, and economic development.[^cite_note-196]

Sharif developed a particularly cordial relationship with Chief of Army Staff [Asim Munir](58712552.md), describing his relationship with the army as a "role model" for the future.[^cite_note-197] The two leaders held multiple joint meetings on national security and foreign policy, including consultations on Pakistan’s response to India following the [2025 India–Pakistan conflict](79896622.md).[^cite_note-198]

In May 2025, the federal cabinet approved Munir’s promotion to the rank of [Field Marshal](63543132.md), citing his “decisive leadership” during the conflict.[^cite_note-199] The promotion ceremony, attended by both Sharif and President [Asif Ali Zardari](993655.md), was held at the [Aiwan-e-Sadr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiwan-e-Sadr) in Islamabad, where Munir was formally conferred the baton of Field Marshal.[^cite_note-200]

Sharif’s cooperative stance with the military was seen as an effort to maintain political stability and avoid confrontation with the country's powerful [military establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Establishment_\(Pakistan\)). His government’s approach was described by Defence Minister [Khawaja Asif](16345137.md) as a “hybrid model” of governance, combining civilian and military decision-making in national and economic affairs.[^cite_note-201]

With Indian Foreign Minister [S. Jaishankar](40435868.md) during the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Islamabad, October 2024

In October 2024, Sharif hosted Indian External Affairs Minister [S. Jaishankar](40435868.md) at the [Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Islamabad_SCO_summit) in Islamabad, marking the first such visit since 2015.[^cite_note-238] Relations between India and Pakistan subsequently deteriorated following an April 2025 [militant attack](79778862.md) in [Pahalgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahalgam), [Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md), which India blamed on Pakistan, a charge that Sharif's government denied.[^cite_note-239] The following crisis led to reciprocal diplomatic measures, including the suspension of the [Indus Waters Treaty](3603967.md) and [Simla Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simla_Agreement) by India and Pakistan, respectively.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241] In May 2025, Sharif oversaw Pakistan's response to Indian military strikes[^cite_note-242] through the launch of [Operation Bunyan un-Marsoos](79896622.md#10_May)[^cite_note-245] against Indian military sites, after which both sides declared a ceasefire.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247] Sharif later declared victory over India and designated 16 May as *Youm-e-Tashakur* ('Day of Gratitude'), though the conflict produced no clear winner.[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250]

Sharif met with Bangladeshi Chief Adviser [Muhammad Yunus](548105.md) in December 2024 during a [Developing-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-8_Organization_for_Economic_Cooperation) summit in [Cairo](6293.md), the first meeting between Pakistani and Bangladeshi senior leaders since the [July Revolution](78109234.md) in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-251] Sharif sought to bolster ties with Bangladesh, launching direct cargo shipments from Pakistan to [Chittagong Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Port) and offering 300 scholarships to Bangladeshi students.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253]

Sharif sought to improve relations with [Afghanistan](737.md) while urging its government to take action against the [Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan](18953699.md) (TTP), which Pakistan accuses of operating from Afghan territory.[^cite_note-254] In March 2024, he authorised [airstrikes in Eastern Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan-Pakistan_clashes_\(2024-present\)#PAF_airstrikes) targeting TTP strongholds.[^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256]

While addressing the [eightieth session of the UNGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eightieth_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly), Sharif publicly claimed that Pakistan faced problems from terrorist groups operating from "Afghan soil."[^cite_note-257] In October 2025, Sharif oversaw Pakistani [military operations](81304780.md) against both the [Afghan Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Taliban) and the TTP as the Pakistani military struck Afghan border posts and cities, including [Kabul](16826.md) and [Kandahar](17260.md).[^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259]

Fighting escalated further in 2026. After Pakistani airstrikes on alleged TTP camps on 21-22 February and Taliban attacks on border posts on 26 February, Pakistan attacked targets in Kabul, Kandahar, and elsewhere declared "open war" on 27 February.[^cite_note-260] A March ceasefire collapsed, and talks hosted by China in [Ürümqi](199155.md) did not produce a lasting settlement.[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262] After nearly three months of relative calm following Pakistani airstrikes in late June, fighting resumed in September with airstrikes and cross-border attacks.[^cite_note-263]

### Democratic backsliding



Shehbaz meeting with Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) at the [2024 Astana SCO summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Astana_SCO_summit) in Astana, Kazakhstan

Immediately after becoming prime minister for his second term, PTI supporters across Pakistan began major protests and demonstrations on 11 March, calling for Sharif's removal from office on allegations of electoral rigging in the 2024 general election. These protests also called for the restoration of the PTI's "stolen mandate" and the release of senior PTI leaders, including Imran Khan and [Shah Mahmood Qureshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Mahmood_Qureshi). In response to these protests, the [Pakistani police](9371583.md) launched a large-scale national crackdown, arresting and attacking PTI protestors.[^cite_note-\:0-264]

On 15 July 2024, Information Minister [Attaullah Tarar](74389513.md) announced that the government was considering a ban on the PTI, citing the violent [2023 Pakistani protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_protests) and the PTI allegedly "leaking classified information."[^cite_note-265] The plan to ban the PTI received large-scale backlash from both inside Pakistan and internationally, with several prominent politicians, journalists, and lawyers condemning the move.[^cite_note-266] According to the [Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution), the move against the PTI revealed the government's "weakness and political insecurity," and the move comes during a period where the [judiciary of Pakistan](4988685.md) is experiencing increasing state pressure to crack down on political opposition.[^cite_note-267]

Sharif's government continued the unofficial ban on social media platform [Twitter](9988187.md) (X) imposed by the [Kakar caretaker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakar_caretaker_government),[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269] and installed an internet [firewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firewall_\(computing\)). The firewall was created by the government in order to block content that it deemed as [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda). The firewall was widely criticised for [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_in_Pakistan). Furthermore, the internet firewall project was estimated to cost \$300 Million. The firewall negatively affected businesses in Pakistan.[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271]

## Public image

See also: [List of heads of the executive by approval rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heads_of_the_executive_by_approval_rating)

During his tenure as chief minister, Sharif developed a reputation as a diligent administrator.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273] He initiated ambitious infrastructure projects in Punjab and was noted for his efficient governance.[^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276] On 19 December 2016, Vice-Minister [Zheng Xiaosong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_Xiaosong) of the [International Liaison Department of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Department_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) coined the term "Shehbaz Speed" to acknowledge his contributions to expediting the progress of projects within the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor. Subsequently, the term has been employed to characterise the rate of advancement within the CPEC.[^cite_note-dailytimes1-277][^cite_note-thenews.com.pk-278][^cite_note-279] In official meetings, Saudi officials also declared Sharif the "Man of Action."[^cite_note-thenews.com.pk-278] In 2025, Sharif was recognised among the world's 500 most influential Muslims by *[The Muslim 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Muslim_500)*, an annual publication by the [Royal Islamic Strategic Studies Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Islamic_Strategic_Studies_Centre) in [Amman](45007.md), [Jordan](7515964.md).[^cite_note-280]
