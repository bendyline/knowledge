**Levent Hasan Ali Alpöge** (born April 1, 1992) is an American and Turkish [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician). He is known for his work in [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory) and [arithmetic statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics). He was a junior fellow in the [Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows) and later joined the artificial-intelligence company [Anthropic](6206236.md) as a member of the technical staff.[^cite_note-cv-1][^cite_note-conversation-2] In 2025, he and collaborators gave a second proof that [Hilbert's tenth problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_tenth_problem) has a negative answer over the [ring of integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_integers) of every [algebraic number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number_field), a result originally established by Koymans and Pagano in 2024.[^cite_note-inventiones-3][^cite_note-Howlett2025-4]

On July 19, 2026, Alpöge presented an explicit [counterexample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterexample) to the [Jacobian conjecture](1193823.md) in three-dimensional space, stating that it was discovered using the [Claude Fable 5](83214139.md) AI model.[^cite_note-anthtweet-5][^cite_note-6] He announced a counterexample to the [Carathéodory conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carath%C3%A9odory_conjecture) on August 19, 2026 using the model. Four days later, he announced a positive answer to [Hopf's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_manifold) concerning the existence of a complex structure on a 6-dimensional sphere using AI tools.[^cite_note-AlpogeHopf2026-7]

His AI-assisted work on [Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness](6013654.md) with Tristan Buckmaster[^cite_note-\:0-8] was cited by [OpenAI](48795986.md) in their solution to the [Millennium Prize Problem](27599234.md) on September 8, 2026.[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-10]

## Early life and education



Levent Alpöge in 2018

Alpöge was born on April 1, 1992, and grew up on [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), New York, where he attended [Half Hollow Hills High School West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Hollow_Hills_Central_School_District) in [Dix Hills, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dix_Hills,_New_York).[^cite_note-cv-1][^cite_note-maviboncuk-11] He holds dual United States and Turkish citizenship.[^cite_note-cv-1]

In 2010, he was a finalist in the [Intel Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneron_Science_Talent_Search) for a computer-science project on the algorithmic analysis of [MRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging) scans of blood vessels. His earliest publication, on a [CUDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUDA)-optimized vessel-detection method, dates from the same period.[^cite_note-maviboncuk-11][^cite_note-cv-1] He earned a [Barry Goldwater Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater_Scholarship_and_Excellence_in_Education_Foundation).[^cite_note-cv-1][^cite_note-churchill-12] The [main-belt asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid_belt) 25898 Alpöge is named after him.[^cite_note-jpl-13]

Alpöge studied at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he received a Bachelor of Arts in mathematics and a Master of Arts in physics in 2014, graduating [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude).[^cite_note-cv-1] His undergraduate thesis, *The average elliptic curve has few integral points*, was supervised by [Jacob Tsimerman](48497888.md).[^cite_note-cv-1][^cite_note-churchill-12][^cite_note-14]

After earning a graduate fellowship from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), Alpöge studied Part III of the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) at the [University of Cambridge](25978572.md) as a [Churchill Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Scholarship),[^cite_note-15] earning a [Master of Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Advanced_Studies) degree in 2015.[^cite_note-churchill-12][^cite_note-eurekalert-16] He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 2020 with a dissertation titled *Points on Curves*, supervised by [Manjul Bhargava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjul_Bhargava).[^cite_note-cv-1]

## Career

After completing his doctorate, Alpöge held an [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) postdoctoral fellowship affiliated with [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and Harvard from 2020, and was a Junior Fellow in the [Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows) from 2021 to 2025.[^cite_note-cv-1][^cite_note-quanta2022-17] He subsequently joined the artificial-intelligence company [Anthropic](6206236.md) as a member of the technical staff.[^cite_note-conversation-2]

## Research

Alpöge works in number theory and arithmetic statistics, including the study of [rational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_point) and integral points on [elliptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve) and higher-genus curves, [Selmer groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selmer_group), and the distribution of arithmetic invariants of number fields.[^cite_note-cv-1]

### Sums of two rational cubes

With [Manjul Bhargava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjul_Bhargava) and Ari Shnidman, Alpöge established the first proven bounds on the proportion of integers that can be written as a sum of two [cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_\(algebra\)) of [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_number). Their 2022 work showed that at most about five-sixths of integers are so expressible, combining [geometry of numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_of_numbers) techniques with the [circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy%E2%80%93Littlewood_circle_method).[^cite_note-quanta2022-17] The upper bound for the density of integers that are so expressible has been subsequently improved to 68.05% by Peter Koymans and Alexander Smith. [^cite_note-18]

### Hilbert's tenth problem over number fields

In a paper published in *[Inventiones Mathematicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventiones_Mathematicae)* in 2025, Alpöge, Bhargava, [Wei Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_Ho_\(mathematician\)), and Ari Shnidman proved that for any [quadratic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_extension) of number fields     K  /  F   {\displaystyle K/F}  {\displaystyle K/F} there exists an [abelian variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_variety) over     F   {\displaystyle F}  {\displaystyle F} of positive [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank_of_an_abelian_group) whose rank does not grow after [base change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_change_map) to     K   {\displaystyle K}  {\displaystyle K}. This implies that [Hilbert's tenth problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_tenth_problem) has a negative solution over the [ring of integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_integers) of every algebraic number field—that is, there is no [algorithm](775.md) to decide whether a polynomial equation has solutions in that ring.[^cite_note-inventiones-3] The result gave an alternative completion of a program relating Hilbert's tenth problem to the rank stability of elliptic curves and abelian varieties initiated by [Bjorn Poonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjorn_Poonen), [Barry Mazur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Mazur), [Karl Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Rubin), and Alexandra Shlapentokh; the Mazur--Rubin-Poonen-Shlapentokh program had been completed in 2024 by Peter Koymans and Carlo Pagano, using instead elliptic curves. [^cite_note-Howlett2025-4][^cite_note-19]

## AI assisted research

### Jacobian conjecture counterexample

In July 2026, Alpöge announced on the social-media platform [X](9988187.md) that, using Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 [large language model](73248112.md), he had found a counterexample to the [Jacobian conjecture](1193823.md), a problem in [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry) begun by [Ludwig Kraus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Kraus) in 1884 and formally posed by [Ott-Heinrich Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ott-Heinrich_Keller) in 1939.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] The example is a [polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial) map from three-dimensional [complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_number) space to itself whose [Jacobian determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_determinant) is the nonzero constant −2 but which is not injective, disproving the conjecture in dimensions three and above. The two-variable case of the conjecture remains open. The announcement was made outside of formal [peer review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review), and the arithmetic of the example was independently checked by other mathematicians in the days that followed.[^cite_note-conversation-2]

### Hadamard matrices

On August 12, 2026, Alpöge announced on X that, together with Philippe Voinov and Saul Reynolds-Haertle and using Anthropic's Claude, he had constructed [Hadamard matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamard_matrix) for all twelve orders below 2000 for which no such matrix had previously been known: 668, 716, 892, 1132, 1244, 1388, 1436, 1676, 1772, 1916, 1948 and 1964.[^cite_note-mathworld-hadamard-22][^cite_note-epoch-hadamard-23] Order 668 had been the smallest open case of the [Hadamard conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamard_matrix#Hadamard_conjecture) since 2005.[^cite_note-mathworld-hadamard-22]

### Carathéodory conjecture counterexample

On August 19, 2026, he announced an explicit      C  ∞     {\displaystyle C^{\infty }}  {\displaystyle C^{\infty }} counterexample for the [Carathéodory conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carath%C3%A9odory_conjecture) checked with John-Paul Smith and Anthropic's artificial-intelligence system Claude.[^cite_note-AlpogeCaratheodory2026-24] Once again the result was announced in a tweet[^cite_note-25], but on this occasion Alpoge offered a more comprehensive backstory, asserting that "i literally wanted to just read about it but i did see the complicated literature situation and so in hopes i could help asked him if it was ok to look at with a model" before confirming that "i did wanna have it recorded somewhere public though" and then giving the example.

### Hopf's problem

Alpöge announced an answer to [Hopf's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_manifold) on X on August 23, 2026, stating that he constructed a [complex manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_manifold) structure on a [6-dimensional sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-sphere) using one of Anthropic's models.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-AlpogeHopf2026-7] The same day Alpöge published a 100-page document containing a proof of the positive solution on his personal website.[^cite_note-27].&#x20;

Proposed formalizations by Dean Cureton[^cite_note-28] and by Boris Alexeev[^cite_note-29] were subsequently posted on [GitHub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitHub), and Philip Engel published some notes about the proof.[^cite_note-30] [Manon Bischoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manon_Bischoff?action=edit\&redlink=1) reported on the finding in *Spektrum der Wissenschaft*, the German language edition of *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*.[^cite_note-31]

### Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness

Main article: [Navier–Stokes priority controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_priority_controversy)

Alpöge worked with [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) mathematician [Tristan Buckmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_Buckmaster) on the [Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness](6013654.md) problem, one of the seven [Millennium Prize Problems](27599234.md). On August 15, 2026, the pair proved that the [Euler equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_equations_\(fluid_dynamics\)), the frictionless counterpart of Navier–Stokes, can "blow up" in finite time, extending a forcing technique developed by [Diego Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_C%C3%B3rdoba?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Luis Martínez-Zoroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Mart%C3%ADnez-Zoroa?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:0-8] After verifying the proof in [Lean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_\(proof_assistant\)), the pair published a preprint establishing finite-time blow-up with smooth forcing for the incompressible porous medium equation, the 2D Boussinesq system, and the 3D incompressible Euler equations, using Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's [Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_Codex_\(AI_agent\)) in the work.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

After word of the pair's progress reached OpenAI, a team led by OpenAI scientist [Sébastien Bubeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9bastien_Bubeck) used an internal model to find a solution for Navier–Stokes equations.[^cite_note-32] On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced a full solution to the problem that resembled the approach used by the pair.[^cite_note-\:1-9] Buckmaster suggested OpenAI researchers may have accessed private work he had stored in a Codex session before publication.[^cite_note-\:0-8][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-34]
