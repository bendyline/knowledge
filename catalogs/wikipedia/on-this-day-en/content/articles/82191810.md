The **2026 Esports World Cup** (**EWC**) was the third edition of the [Esports World Cup](76626424.md), an annual international [esports](564204.md) tournament series run by the [Esports Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esports_Foundation) (EF), a nonprofit organization funded by Saudi Arabia's [Public Investment Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Investment_Fund). It took place in [Paris](22989.md), France from 2 July to 23 August 2026 and featured 25 events in 24 esports.

The event was scheduled to take place in [Riyadh](51554.md), Saudi Arabia, as it had for the prior two editions and both editions of Gamers8, EWC's predecessor. However, it was moved to Paris amid security concerns related to the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).

This year featured one new title in the main lineup, the racing game *[Trackmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trackmania_\(2020_video_game\))* (2020), while *[Fortnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Battle_Royale)* made its return to the EWC, this time using the [Reload game mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Battle_Royale#Special_modes). On the other hand, *[Rennsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennsport)* and *[StarCraft II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II)* were not present this year. Due to their series' annual game releases, *[Call of Duty: Black Ops 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_of_Duty:_Black_Ops_7)* and *[EA Sports FC 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Sports_FC_26)* replaced *[Call of Duty: Black Ops 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_of_Duty:_Black_Ops_6)* and *[EA Sports FC 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Sports_FC_25)*, respectively, in the game lineup.

Similar to the first two EWCs, each game hosted at least one event, some were standalone events while others were part of a larger competitive circuit. *[Mobile Legends: Bang Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Legends:_Bang_Bang)* remained as the only game to host two events. A total of \$75 million was distributed across all events, an increase from \$71.5 million in 2025.

## Background

On 8 October 2025, the EWC announced the first 20 games that were included as part of the lineup. Out of the 24 games that were part of the main lineup in 2025, *[Rennsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennsport)*, *[Rocket League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_League)*, *[StarCraft II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II)*, and *[Tekken 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekken_8)* weren't listed among the initial returning games.[^cite_note-2] On 11 October, *Tekken 8* was confirmed to return as the 21st game in the lineup.[^cite_note-3]

On 17 December, the EWC announced that it entered a three-year partnership with [Epic Games](573199.md) that would see two of its games, *[Fortnite](57813623.md)* and *Rocket League*, host EWC events through 2028. The latter was featured in both of the previous EWCs while the former returns to the lineup after it was featured in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Esports_World_Cup). In the case of *Fortnite*, rather than the game being played on [the game's Battle Royale mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Battle_Royale) (during Gamers8, the predecessor of the Esports World Cup) or in custom maps made using the [Unreal Editor for Fortnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Editor_for_Fortnite) (as was the case for 2024), the event was played in Fortnite Reload, a variation of the Battle Royale mode, as part of the *Fortnite* Reload Elite Series.[^cite_note-4] *[Trackmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trackmania_\(2020_video_game\))* (2020) was announced as the 24th and final game on 19 January 2026, which also marked the game's EWC debut.[^cite_note-5]

The exclusion of *StarCraft II* from the lineup meant that the 2026 EWC would not feature any [real-time strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_strategy) games, which caused criticism from creators and fans from the *StarCraft II* and RTS communities.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] *Rennsport* was the only other game dropped from the main lineup.



[Saudi Crown Prince](40373823.md) [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md) [Al Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Saud) and [French President](24899.md) [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) at the closing ceremony of the EWC at the [Grand Palais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Palais) in Paris.

### Move to Paris

On 14 May 2026, it was reported that the Esports Foundation had communicated to stakeholders surrounding EWC that the event would move from Riyadh, Saudi Arabia to [Paris](22989.md), France. The move was cited as a response to uncertainty in the Middle East region due to the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), which has seen Iran and its allies attack Saudi infrastructure.[^cite_note-8] The war had already caused the [Saudi Arabian Grand Prix](65766701.md) in Formula One to not take place as scheduled in April.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The relocation was later confirmed by the Esports Foundation on 20 May,[^cite_note-11] with the [Paris Expo Porte de Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Expo_Porte_de_Versailles) announced as the new host venue. On July 2026, it was announced that the [Accor Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accor_Arena) would host the finals of the 2026 Esports World Cup *Counter-Strike 2* tournament.[^cite_note-12] Paris has hosted multiple major esports events, including the [2019 *League of Legends* World Championship Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_League_of_Legends_World_Championship_final), 2023's [BLAST.tv Paris Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_Paris_Major_2023) in *[Counter-Strike: Global Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Strike:_Global_Offensive)* and the [2025 *Valorant* Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Valorant_Champions), among others.

## Format

### Club Championship

The Club Championship was a cross-game competition within the Esports World Cup. The championship gave out a total of US\$30 million among the top 24 clubs (used by the EWC in place of "organization"), determined by their overall performance in various games throughout the event. To qualify for the Club Championship, a club had to finish in the top 8 in at least two competitions, and to win the championship title, the club also had to secure first place in at least one competition. Only participants who were publicly announced as part of their respective organization before 1 May 2026 and registered as a representative before 26 May are eligible to earn points for their club. ### Club Partner Program

The Esports Foundation's Club Partner Program (formerly the Club Support Program) was an initiative designed to provide substantial financial assistance to selected esports organizations. Through this program, chosen teams receive annual financial support to enhance their operations and create more opportunities for professional players. The program was kept to cover 40 organizations, the same number as in 2025.[^cite_note-14]

The 40 teams represented the five major competitive regions — Europe, North America, Asia, South America, and the Middle East — with the majority of the teams being primarily based in Europe or Asia. The initial list was revealed on 31 March 2026, accompanying the Esports World Cup Foundation's renaming to the Esports Foundation; it was leaked to Sheep Esports on 30 March.[^cite_note-Gomis-15] Several teams from the [2025 Club Partner Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Esports_World_Cup#Club_Partner_Program), including [LOUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loud_\(esports\)), [Bilibili Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibili_Gaming), [EVOS Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EVOS_Esports) and most notably [FaZe Clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FaZe_Clan), did not get accepted, while [2024 Club Support Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Esports_World_Cup#Club_Support_Program) member [NRG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRG_Esports) returns after missing out in 2025.[^cite_note-16] [HEROIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroic_\(esports\)) was later removed from the program after the removal of their *[Dota 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dota_2)* roster, leaving *[Counter-Strike 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Strike_2)* as the sole game they competed in.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] They agreed to void the slot by mutual decision. [Karmine Corp](68767537.md), who had initially missed out on the program, would be announced as their replacement on 19 May.
