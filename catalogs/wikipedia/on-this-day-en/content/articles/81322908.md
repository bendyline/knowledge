**Daraxonrasib,** sold under the brand name **Rasonque**, is an [anti-cancer medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-cancer_medication) used for the treatment of [pancreatic cancer](363559.md).[^cite_note-Rasonque_FDA_label-1] It is an inhibitor of the RAS GTPase family.[^cite_note-Rasonque_FDA_label-1] It is an orally administered small molecule pan-[RAS inhibitor](371659.md#RAS_inhibitors). It was developed by [Revolution Medicines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_Medicines?action=edit\&redlink=1).

It inhibits all three key members of the [Ras superfamily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_superfamily): [KRAS](9065739.md), [HRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HRAS), and [NRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroblastoma_RAS_viral_oncogene_homolog). The three RAS proteins are [GTPases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTPase) that become active when bound to [GTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanosine_triphosphate) but normally convert this GTP to [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanosine_diphosphate) and thereby become inactive. RAS proteins that are bound to GDP and GTP are termed respectively RAS(OFF) and RAS(ON).[^cite_note-Ma_2025-2] Daraxonrasib targets RAS in the ON state, but not in the OFF state.[^cite_note-Daraxonrasib_Revolution_Medicines-3][^cite_note-Revolution_Medicines_2026-4]

Daraxonrasib was approved for medical use in the United States in August 2026.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

## Medical uses

Daraxonrasib is [indicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indicated) for the treatment of adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who have received at least one prior systemic therapy or who are not candidates for multiagent systemic therapy.[^cite_note-Rasonque_FDA_label-1]

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action

Daraxonrasib is a [small molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_molecule) which inhibits proteins of the [RAS superfamily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_superfamily). It is a [molecular glue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_glue) which noncovalently binds RAS proteins in the ON state.[^cite_note-Daraxonrasib_RMC_6236_2026-6] To inhibit RAS, daraxonrasib first binds a chaperone molecule ([cyclophilin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptidylprolyl_isomerase_A)), which results in a complementary surface. This surface is able to bind RAS superfamily proteins, forming a tri-complex and thereby blocking RAS signaling and limiting RAS-driven cancer growth.[^cite_note-Daraxonrasib_Revolution_Medicines-3][^cite_note-Daraxonrasib_RMC_6236_2026-6]

### Safety and tolerability

In clinical study and through expanded access [case studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_study), the safety profile of daraxonrasib appears to be relatively manageable.[^cite_note-MastJ_2026-7][^cite_note-Kolata_2026-8][^cite_note-Anastacio_2026-9] The most predominant side effects observed to date include rash, diarrhea, fatigue, nausea, split fingertips,[^cite_note-Kolata_2026-8] [mucositis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucositis) and [stomatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomatitis).[^cite_note-10]

## RAS proteins

The RAS proteins are expressed on the inner surface of a cell's [plasma membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_membrane) and to lesser extents the cell's intracellular [Golgi apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus), [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum), and [endosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosome) membranes.[^cite_note-Yang_2024-11][^cite_note-Mozzarelli_2024-12] RAS proteins can develop [mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutations) that cause them to bind GTP continuously, keeping them constantly in the ON state, causing uncontrolled growth, and thereby promoting various malignancies.[^cite_note-Yang_2024-11] Patients with a cancer harboring RAS-activating mutation generally have poorer prognoses and shorter survival time than patients with the same cancer but without a RAS mutation.[^cite_note-Mozzarelli_2024-12]

Daraxonrasib reverses continuous activation of RAS by forming a tri-complex consisting of one of the three RAS proteins bound to GTP and [cyclophilin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptidylprolyl_isomerase_A), and thus inhibiting RAS. Consequently, daraxonrasib has proven effective in suppressing the cancers promoted by any one of RAS superfamily proteins.[^cite_note-Wolpin_2026-13][^cite_note-Pfaff_2026-14][^cite_note-Cregg_2025-15][^cite_note-Foth_2026-16][^cite_note-Sang_2026-17]

A mutationally active RAS protein occurs in one out of every 5 human cancers,[^cite_note-Yang_2024-11] promotes up to 30% of human cancers,[^cite_note-Jiang_2024_a-18] and in the U.S. appears responsible for 200,000 new cancer cases each year.[^cite_note-Jiang_2024_a-18][^cite_note-Prior_2020-19] One of the three RAS proteins is mutated in about 90% of [pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreatic_adenocarcinoma); 40% of [colorectal adenocarcinomas](206979.md#histopathology); 30% of [skin cutaneous melanomas](716631.md); \~30% of cases of [non-small cell lung cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-small-cell_lung_cancer); and a smaller proportion of most other solid tumor types.[^cite_note-Yang_2024-11][^cite_note-Prior_2020-19]

## Clinical trials

It is undergoing clinical studies in RAS-driven cancers, including [pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma](363559.md) (PDAC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).[^cite_note-Revolution_Medicines_2026-4] The drug received [breakthrough therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_therapy) designation from the US [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) (FDA) in 2025.[^cite_note-Sava_2025-20] It is undergoing regulatory review by both the [European Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Medical_Association) (EMA) and the FDA.[^cite_note-21] In July 2026, the FDA accepted for review the company's [New Drug Application](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Drug_Application) (NDA) under the [Commissioner's National Priority Voucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioner's_National_Priority_Voucher?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CNPV) Program, and daraxonrasib was approved by the FDA in August 2026.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

### Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is the most prevalent type of [pancreatic cancer](363559.md).[^cite_note-Rozengurt_2026-24] Mutations in a RAS protein occur in, and appear responsible for, more than 90% of PDAC cases.[^cite_note-Yang_2024-11][^cite_note-Prior_2020-19] These mutations lead to excessive RAS signaling and uncontrolled growth of their target cells, and make these cases good candidates for treatment with daraxonrasib.[^cite_note-Jiang_2024_a-18][^cite_note-Daraxonrasib_Continues_Victory_2026-25] Early-phase clinical studies found that daraxonrasib was effective and safe in treating metastatic PDAC, and lead to the candidate receiving [breakthrough therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_therapy) designation from the US [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md).[^cite_note-Daraxonrasib_Continues_Victory_2026-25]

Daraxonrasib is currently being studied in three late-phase clinical studies in PDAC, each in a different treatment setting.[^cite_note-Revolution_Medicines_2026-4]

#### Rasolute 302

A [phase III study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_clinical_research#phase_3_study) (RASolute 302) assessed the efficacy of daraxonrasib in second line (previously treated) metastatic PDAC compared to [standard treatments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_treatment) which use [chemotherapy](7172.md).[^cite_note-26] The trial, while still ongoing as of July 2026, met all primary and key secondary [endpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_endpoint), including [progression-free survival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progression-free_survival) (PFS). The study randomly assigned 248 patients to receive daraxonrasib and 252 patients to receive chemotherapy. The median [overall survival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_rate) in the overall population was 13.2 months for daraxonrasib-treated and 6.7 months for daraxonrasib-untreated patients. The [hazard ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_ratio) for death was 0.40 (a 60% reduction in risk of death; p < 0.0001). Daraxonrasib was generally well tolerated with a manageable safety profile.[^cite_note-MastJ_2026-7][^cite_note-Qu_2015-27][^cite_note-O'Reilly_2026-28] Its side effects included rash, diarrhea, fatigue, nausea and raw, split fingertips.[^cite_note-Kolata_2026-8] Subsequent to this study, the US FDA approved daraxonrasib for use in adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who have received at least one prior systemic therapy or who are not candidates for multiagent systemic therapy.[^cite_note-Rasonque_FDA_label-1][^cite_note-\:0-5]

#### Rasolute 303

Daraxonrasib is, as of July 2026, being evaluated in a phase III study (RASolute 303) in first line (not yet treated) metastatic PDAC.[^cite_note-Revolution_2026_a-29] The trial is evaluating two novel treatment regimens in comparison to standard of care chemotherapy: daraxonrasib alone (monotherapy) and daraxonrasib in combination with [gemcitabine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemcitabine) and [nab-paclitaxel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nab-paclitaxel).[^cite_note-Revolution_2026_a-29]

#### Rasolute 304

Daraxonrasib is being evaluated in a phase III study (RASolute 304) in resectable PDAC in combination with surgery in the [adjuvant setting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjuvant_therapy). Patients whose cancer was [resected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resection_\(surgery\)) and received adjuvant or [neoadjuvant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoadjuvant_therapy) chemotherapy are eligible for participation in the study. The trial is investigating daraxonrasib adjuvant to surgery versus the standard of care of patient observation via blood testing and screening. This trial positions daraxonrasib as a maintenance therapy for resectable PDAC.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

### Non-small-cell lung cancer

[Non-small-cell lung cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-small-cell_lung_cancer) (NSCLC) represents about 85% of all lung cancers. Mutations in RAS proteins are present in \~30% of NSCLC cases.[^cite_note-32] In a phase 1 study, 73 patients with NSCLC were treated with daraxonrasib. Safety and efficacy in this group supported further development.[^cite_note-Revolution_2026_b-33][^cite_note-Filis_2025-34][^cite_note-35] Currently, as of July 2026, the drug is being evaluated in NSCLC in one ongoing phase 3 clinical study, RASolve 301 and other earlier-stage studies.[^cite_note-Revolution_Medicines_2026-4][^cite_note-Revolution_2026_b-33]

#### Rasolve 301

Daraxonrasib is being studied in a phase 3 clinical study (RASolve 301) in second line (previously treated) NSCLC.[^cite_note-Revolution_Medicines_2026-4] The trial is evaluating daraxonrasib in comparison to standard of care chemotherapy ([docetaxel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docetaxel)) in this treatment setting.[^cite_note-36] Revolution Medicines is also planning a phase 3 clinical study in first line (not yet treated) NSCLC, building on early data in this indication.[^cite_note-Revolution_Medicines_2026-4]

Combination studies in NSCLC and PDAC remain ongoing, including in combination with TNG462 (a [PRMT5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_arginine_methyltransferase_5) inhibitor for [MTAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTAP) deleted cancers).[^cite_note-37]

### Melanoma

[Melanoma](716631.md) is the sixth most common cancer diagnosed in the US and the deadliest form of skin cancer.[^cite_note-Foth_2026-16] At least five patients with RAS-driven metastatic melanomas have been treated with daraxonrasib through early-stage clinical studies. These included: a 77-year-old female with mutated NRAS-driven metastatic melanoma who saw rapid clinical and symptomatic improvement with mild toxicity;[^cite_note-Anastacio_2026-9][^cite_note-Chatziioannou_2026-38] a 76-year female with mutated NRAS-driven melanoma metastatic to the brain and [lymph nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymph_node) who achieved a complete response but after 245 days relapsed;[^cite_note-Anastacio_2026-9] an 82-year-old male with mutated NRAS-driven melanoma who achieved a partial response to daraxonrasib;[^cite_note-Foth_2026-16] a 43-year-old with metastatic NRAS-driven melanoma and achieved stable disease, which progressed again after 140 days of treatment;[^cite_note-Foth_2026-16] a 55-year-old male with [acral lentiginous melanoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acral_lentiginous_melanoma), who achieved stable disease for 10 months of daraxonrasib treatment.[^cite_note-Huynh_2026-39] All patients were heavily pretreated prior to treatment.[^cite_note-Anastacio_2026-9][^cite_note-Foth_2026-16][^cite_note-Huynh_2026-39]

Daraxonrasib is also being studied in combination with BMS-986504 (a [PRMT5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_arginine_methyltransferase_5) inhibitor for [MTAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTAP) deleted cancers) in [melanoma](716631.md) in a phase 2 study, as of July 2026.[^cite_note-40]

### Other solid tumors

Daraxonrasib is being studied in other types of solid tumors as well, including in [gastrointestinal tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_cancer) and [biliary tract cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholangiocarcinoma) in combination with [anvumetostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anvumetostat) in MTAP deleted cancers.[^cite_note-41] Earlier-phase studies of daraxonrasib include patients with [colorectal cancer](206979.md).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

## Research

### Combination treatments for PDAC

Mice made to express genetically engineered PDAC-like mouse tumors or patient-derived human PDAC tumors were treated with a combination of three cancer-inhibiting agents, daraxonrasib, [afatinib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afatinib), and [SD36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD36). Mice treated with this combination therapy had far more complete and durable repressions of their PDAC tumors than mice treated with only daraxonrasib. These results suggest that treating patients with PDAC (and perhaps other cancers) with daraxonrasib in combinations with other anticancer agents prove superior to treatment with daraxonrasib alone.[^cite_note-Liaki_2026-44] Subsequent studies in mice with PDAC indicated that adding [cancer immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_immunotherapy#Types_and_categories) to daraxonrasib increased the effectiveness of treatment.[^cite_note-Mahadevan_2023-45][^cite_note-Stanger_2026-46] Other combinations in PDAC models [in vitro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro) and [in vivo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vivo) have shown that combinations of daraxonrasib with KRAS inhibitors improve treatment efficacy as compared to daraxonrasib alone.[^cite_note-47] This early combination treatment research may inform future clinical work and/or treatment.

### Melanoma

Researchers have studied daraxonrasib in NRAS-mutant melanoma mouse models and found the drug to be effective at controlling disease in this [preclinical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preclinical_development) setting.[^cite_note-Anastacio_2026-9] Though few melanoma patients have been treated with daraxonrasib to date, many cases of melanoma are driven by an [NRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroblastoma_RAS_viral_oncogene_homolog) mutation, and thus could potentially benefit from treatment with the drug based on preclinical work, according to researchers studying the drug in this context.[^cite_note-Anastacio_2026-9][^cite_note-Prior_2020-19]

### Glioblastoma

Cell lines of [glioblastoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glioblastoma) were treated with daraxonrasib, then exposed to radiation, as KRAS signaling is known to be an important mediator of [radioresistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioresistance) for this cancer type. Based on results from these cell lines, reported in a [preprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preprint), researchers concluded that daraxonrasib treatment may present a promising [radiosensitizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiosensitizer) strategy for glioblastoma.[^cite_note-48]

### Osteosarcoma

A small subset of [osteosarcoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteosarcoma) patients exhibit a KRAS mutation and thus may be candidates for treatment with daraxonrasib.[^cite_note-Prior_2020-19][^cite_note-Jung_2025-49] In an in vitro cell model of KRAS-mutant osteosarcoma, researchers found that daraxonrasib selectively inhibited the proliferation and invasiveness of this model cell line.[^cite_note-Jung_2025-49] Similar results were found in a KRAS-[wild type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_type) osteosarcoma cell line, but to a lesser extent.[^cite_note-Jung_2025-49]

### Hematologic malignancies

Work in [acute myeloid leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_myeloid_leukemia) suggests that resistance to commonly-used drugs, including [FLT3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD135) inhibitors (such as [gilteritinib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilteritinib)) and [Bcl-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bcl-2) inhibitors (such as [venetoclax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetoclax)) is driven by RAS. Researchers tested acute myeloid leukemia cell lines by treating them with daraxonrasib in combination with either a FLT3 inhibitor or Bcl-2 inhibitor. The study found that treatment with daraxonrasib overcomes RAS-mediated resistance to both drug types in vitro, and researchers suggest this could be a basis for clinical study in this indication.[^cite_note-50]
