**Siberia** ([/saɪˈbɪəriə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*sy-BEER-ee-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Сибирь, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Sibir'*, IPA: [\[sʲɪˈbʲirʲ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/79/Ru-%D0%A1%D0%B8%D0%B1%D0%B8%D1%80%D1%8C.ogg/Ru-%D0%A1%D0%B8%D0%B1%D0%B8%D1%80%D1%8C.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/79/Ru-%D0%A1%D0%B8%D0%B1%D0%B8%D1%80%D1%8C.ogg/Ru-%D0%A1%D0%B8%D0%B1%D0%B8%D1%80%D1%8C.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is an extensive geographical region comprising all of [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia), from the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains) in the west (with the [Ural River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(river\)) usually forming the southernmost portion of its western boundary) to the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) in the east.[^cite_note-Britannica-3] It has formed a part of the sovereign territory of [Russia](25391.md) since the lengthy [conquest of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Siberia), which began with the [fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Khanate_of_Sibir) of the [Khanate of Sibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Sibir) in 1582 and concluded with the annexation of [Chukotka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukchi_Peninsula) in 1778. Siberia is vast and sparsely populated, covering an area of over 13.1 million square kilometres (5,100,000 mi<sup>2</sup>) – about three-quarters of Russia's total area, but home to roughly a quarter of Russia's population. [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk), [Krasnoyarsk](72233.md), and [Omsk](62593.md) are its largest cities.[^cite_note-4]

Because Siberia is a geographic and historic concept and not a political entity, there is no single precise definition of its territorial borders. It is further defined as stretching from the territories within the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle) in the north to the northern borders of [Kazakhstan](16642.md), [Mongolia](19271.md), and [China](5405.md) in the south, although the hills of north-central Kazakhstan are also commonly included.[^cite_note-Britannica-3][^cite_note-bse-5] The Russian government divides the region into three [federal districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_districts_of_Russia) (groupings of [Russian federal subjects](701438.md)), of which only the central one is officially referred to as "[Siberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Federal_District)"; the other two are the [Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Federal_District) (which spans both Europe and western Siberia) and [Far Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Federal_District) federal districts, named for the [Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(region\)) and [Russian Far East](173135.md) regions that correspond respectively to the western and eastern thirds of Siberia in the broader sense.

Siberia is known for its long, harsh winters, with a January average of −25 °C (−13 °F).[^cite_note-6] Although it is geographically located in Asia, Russian sovereignty and colonization since the 16th century has led to perceptions of the region as culturally and ethnically European.[^cite_note-culture-7] [Over 85% of its population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Siberia) are of European descent,[^cite_note-2010Census-8][^cite_note-9] chiefly Russian (comprising the [Siberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberians) sub-ethnic group), and [Eastern Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavs) cultural influences predominate throughout the region.[^cite_note-culture-7] Nevertheless, there are sizable ethnic minorities of Asian lineage, including various [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) communities—many of which, such as the [Yakuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuts), [Tuvans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvans), [Altai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altai_people), and [Khakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khakas), are [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Siberia)—along with the [Mongolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolic_peoples) [Buryats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryats), ethnic [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryo-saram), and smaller groups of [Samoyedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoyeds) and [Tungusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungusic_peoples) peoples (several of whom are classified as [Indigenous small-numbered peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_list_of_indigenous_minority_peoples_of_the_North,_Siberia,_and_the_Far_East_of_Russia) by the Russian government), among many others.

## Etymology

The origin of the name is uncertain.[^cite_note-Britannica-3] In the Russian language, it was adopted as a [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponymy) through contact with the [Khanate of Sibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Sibir) (Сибирское ханство) since the 15th century.[^cite_note-10] The Russian name *[Yugra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugra)* was applied to the northern lands east of the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains), which had been known of since the 11th century or earlier, while the name *Siberia* is first mentioned in Russian chronicles at the start of the 15th century in connection with the death of the khan [Tokhtamysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokhtamysh) in "the Siberian land".[^cite_note-11]

Some sources say that "Siberia" originates from the [Siberian Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Tatar_language) word for 'sleeping land' (*Sib-ir*), but this discourse does not correspond to the actual Siberian Tatar language.[^cite_note-12] Mongolist [György Kara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gy%C3%B6rgy_Kara) posits that the toponym *Siberia* is derived from a [Mongolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolic_languages) word *sibir*, cognate with modern [Buryat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_language) *sheber* 'dense forest'.[^cite_note-13] A different hypothesis claims that the region was named after the [Sibe people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibe_people).[^cite_note-manchus213-14]

Polish historian Jan Chyliczkowski has proposed that the name derives from the [Proto-Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Slavic_language) word for 'north' (cf. Russian север *sever*),[^cite_note-15] as in [Severia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severia). Anatole Baikaloff has dismissed this explanation. He says that the neighboring Chinese, Turks, and Mongolians, who have similar names for the region, would not have known Russian. He suggests that the name might be a combination of two words with [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) origin, *su* 'water' and *bir* 'wild land'.[^cite_note-16]

Siberia in [Paleozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoic) times formed the continent of [Siberia/Angaraland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia_\(continent\)), which fused to [Euramerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurasia) during the Late [Carboniferous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous), as part of the formation of [Pangea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangea).[^cite_note-20] The [Siberian Traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Traps) were formed by one of the largest-known volcanic events of the last 251 million years of [Earth's geological history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_geological_history). Their activity continued for a million years, and some scientists consider it a possible cause of the "[Great Dying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian%E2%80%93Triassic_extinction_event)" about 250 million years ago,[^cite_note-21] estimated to have killed 90% of species existing at the time.[^cite_note-22]

The region has paleontological significance, as it contains bodies of prehistoric animals from the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) [Epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epoch_\(geology\)), preserved in ice or [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost). Specimens of [Goldfuss cave lion cubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthera_leo_spelaea#Specimens), [Yuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuka_\(mammoth\)) the mammoth and another woolly mammoth from [Oymyakon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oymyakon), a [woolly rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolly_rhinoceros) from the [Kolyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolyma_\(river\)), and bison and horses from [Yukagir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukagir) have been found.[^cite_note-Thesiberiantimes2015-23] Remote [Wrangel Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangel_Island) and the [Taymyr Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taymyr_Peninsula) are believed to have been the last places on Earth to support woolly mammoths as isolated populations until their extinction around 2000 BC.[^cite_note-24]

At least three species of humans lived in southern Siberia around 40,000 years ago: *[H. sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens)*, *[H. neanderthalensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal)*, and the [Denisovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denisovans).[^cite_note-Woman_X-25] In 2010, DNA evidence identified the last as a separate species.[^cite_note-26] Late Paleolithic southern Siberians appear to be related to Paleolithic Europeans and the Paleolithic [Jōmon people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_people) of Japan.[^cite_note-27] DNA analysis has revealed that the oldest fossil known to carry the derived KITLG allele, which is responsible for [blond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blond) hair in modern Europeans, is a 17,000 year old [Ancient North Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_North_Eurasian) specimen from Siberia.[^cite_note-Evans2019-28] Ancient North Eurasian populations genetically similar to [Mal'ta–Buret' culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal'ta%E2%80%93Buret'_culture) and [Afontova Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afontova_Gora) were an important genetic contributor to Native Americans, Europeans, Ancient Central Asians, South Asians, and some East Asian groups (such as the [Ainu people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainu_people)). Evidence from full genomic studies suggests that the first people in the Americas diverged from [Ancient East Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_East_Asians) about 36,000 years ago and expanded northwards into Siberia, where they encountered and interacted with Ancient North Eurasians, giving rise to both [Paleosiberian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleosiberian_peoples) and [Ancient Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Beringian), which later migrated towards the [Beringian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beringia) region, became isolated from other populations, and subsequently populated the Americas.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

### Early history



[Chukchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukchi_people), one of many [Indigenous peoples of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Siberia). Representation of a Chukchi family by [Louis Choris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Choris) (1816)

During past millennia, different groups of [nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) – such as the [Enets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enets), the [Nenets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenets_people), the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns), the [Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiongnu), the [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians), and the [Yugur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugur) – inhabited various parts of Siberia. The [Afanasievo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afanasievo_culture) and [Tashtyk cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashtyk_culture) of the [Yenisey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenisey) valley and Altay Mountains are associated with the [Indo-European migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_migrations) across Eurasia.[^cite_note-31] The proto-Mongol [Khitan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people), whose territory spanned a vast area, were also attested in the [Ob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ob) region of western Siberia in the sixteenth century.[^cite_note-32]

In the 13th century, during the period of the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire), the Mongols conquered a large part of this area.[^cite_note-33] With the breakup of the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde), the autonomous [Khanate of Sibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Sibir) was formed in the late 15th century. Turkic-speaking [Yakut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuts) migrated north from the [Lake Baikal](56865.md) region under pressure from the Mongol tribes from the 13th to 15th centuries.[^cite_note-34] Siberia remained a sparsely populated area. Historian [John F. Richards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Richards) writes: "it is doubtful that the total early modern Siberian population exceeded 300,000 persons".[^cite_note-35]

#### Early Russian exploration

Further information: [Russian conquest of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Siberia)

The first mention of Siberia in chronicles is recorded in 1032.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaumov200653-36] The city-state of [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Republic) established two trade routes to the [Ob River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ob), and laid claim to the lands the Russians called *[Yugra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugra)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaumov200653The_Russians_named_it_Yugorskaia_Zemlitsa_(Yugor_Land_or_Yugra)..._The_Novgoroders_established_two_main_routes_to_Siberia..._to_the_lower_reaches_of_the_River_Ob-37] The Russians were attracted by [its furs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) in particular.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaumov200653The_Russians_were_attracted_to_Siberia_by_its_furs-38] Novgorod launched military campaigns to extract tribute from the local population but often met resistance, such as two campaigns in 1187 and 1193 mentioned in chronicles that were defeated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaumov200653-36] After Novgorod was annexed by [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Moscow), the newly emerging centralized Russian state also laid claim to the region, with [Ivan III of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_III_of_Russia) sending [expeditionary forces to Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugra_campaigns) in 1483 and 1499–1500.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaumov200653After_Novgorod_had_been_annexed_by_the_newly_emerging_centralized_Russian_state_in_1478,_its_government,_located_in_Moscow,_tried_to_lay_claim_to_Yugor_Land_as_well..._In_1483_Prince_Ivan_III_sent_a_large_expeditionary_force_to_Siberia..._In_1499–1500_Ivan_III_sent_another_large_force-39] The Russians received tribute, but contact with the tribes ceased after they left.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENaumov200653–54-40]

The growing power of the [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia) began to undermine the Siberian Khanate in the 16th century. First, groups of traders and [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack) began to enter the area. The Russian army was directed to establish forts farther and farther east to protect new Russian settlers who migrated from Europe. Towns such as [Mangazeya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangazeya), [Tara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara,_Omsk_Oblast), [Yeniseysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeniseysk), and [Tobolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobolsk) developed, the last becoming the *de facto* capital of Siberia from 1590. At this time, *Sibir* was the name of a fortress at [Qashliq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qashliq), near Tobolsk. [Gerardus Mercator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardus_Mercator), in a map published in 1595, marks *Sibier* both as the name of a settlement and of the surrounding territory along a left tributary of the Ob.[^cite_note-41] Other sources contend that the [Sibe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibe_people), an indigenous [Tungusic people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungusic_peoples), offered fierce resistance to Russian expansion beyond the Urals. Some suggest that the term "Siberia" is a [Russified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification) version of their ethnonym.[^cite_note-manchus213-14]

### Russian Empire



Coat of arms of Siberia, which was a part of the Russian Imperial Coat of Arms until 1917



&#x20;Map of the [Siberian Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Route) in the 18th century (*green*) and the early 19th century (*red*)

By the mid-17th century, Russia had established areas of control that extended to the Pacific Ocean. Some 230,000 Russians had settled in Siberia by 1709.[^cite_note-42] Siberia became one of the destinations for sending internal exiles. Exile was the main Russian punitive practice with more than 800,000 people exiled during the 19th century.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

The first great modern change in Siberia was the [Trans-Siberian Railway](56912.md), constructed during 1891–1916. It linked Siberia more closely to the rapidly industrialising Russia of [Nicholas II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_II). Around seven million Russians moved to Siberia from Europe between 1801 and 1914.[^cite_note-45] Between 1859 and 1917, more than half a million people migrated to the Russian Far East.[^cite_note-46] Siberia has extensive natural resources: during the 20th century, large-scale exploitation of these took place, and industrial towns cropped up throughout the region.[^cite_note-47]

At 7\:15 a.m. on 30 June 1908, the [Tunguska event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunguska_event) felled millions of trees near the [Podkamennaya Tunguska River (Stony Tunguska River)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podkamennaya_Tunguska) in central Siberia. Most scientists believe this resulted from the [air burst of a meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor_air_burst) or comet. Even though no crater has ever been found, the landscape in the (sparsely inhabited) area still bears the scars of this event.[^cite_note-48]

### Soviet Union

In the early decades of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (especially in the 1930s and 1940s), the government used the [Gulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulag) state agency to administer a system of penal [labour camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_camp), replacing the previous [katorga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katorga) system.[^cite_note-49] According to semi-official Soviet estimates, which did not become public until after the [fall of the Soviet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991, from 1929 to 1953 more than 14 million people passed through these camps and prisons, many of them in Siberia. Another seven to eight million people were [internally deported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_settlements_in_the_Soviet_Union) to remote areas of the Soviet Union (including entire nationalities or ethnicities in several cases).[^cite_note-50]

Half a million (516,841) prisoners died in camps from 1941 to 1943 during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-51] At other periods, mortality was comparatively lower.[^cite_note-52] The size, scope, and scale of the Gulag slave-labour camps remain subjects of much research and debate. Many Gulag camps operated in extremely remote areas of northeastern Siberia. The best-known clusters included *[Sevvostlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevvostlag)* (*the North-East Camps*) along the [Kolyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolyma_\(river\)) and *[Norillag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norillag)* near [Norilsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norilsk), where 69,000 prisoners lived in 1952.[^cite_note-53] Major industrial cities of northern Siberia, such as Norilsk and [Magadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadan), developed from camps built by prisoners and run by former prisoners.[^cite_note-54]

After the [Soviet collapse in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), Siberia faced severe economic decline during [Russia](25391.md)'s transition to a [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy), characterized by [hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation), industrial collapse, and resource privatization. While the idea of a Siberian Republic for more regional autonomy was discussed and a Siberian Agreement was formed in the 1990s, the region remained consolidated under Russian federal authority. Economic shifts led to increased resource-based specialization and a decline in the population, particularly in the Russian Arctic, as the previous focus on industrial settlements was largely abandoned.[^cite_note-55]

On 2 December 2019, the '[Power of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_of_Siberia)' gas pipeline started functioning. The project was started in 2014 to supply [natural gas](22131.md) from Siberia to China.[^cite_note-56]

Siberia spans an area of 13.1 million square kilometres (5,100,000 mi<sup>2</sup>), covering most of Russia's total territory and almost 9% of Earth's land surface (148,940,000 km<sup>2</sup>, 57,510,000 sq mi). It geographically falls in Asia but is culturally and politically considered European, since it is a part of Russia.[^cite_note-culture-7] Major geographical zones include the [West Siberian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Siberian_Plain) and the [Central Siberian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Siberian_Plateau).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESamoylova2015135-57]

Eastern and central [Sakha](57905.md) comprises numerous north–south mountain ranges of various ages. These mountains rise to almost 3,000 metres (9,800 ft), but above a few hundred metres they are almost completely devoid of vegetation. The [Verkhoyansk Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhoyansk_Range) was extensively glaciated in the Pleistocene, but the climate was too dry for glaciation to extend to low elevations. At these low elevations are numerous valleys, many of them deep and covered with [larch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larch) forest, except in the extreme north where the [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) dominates. Soils are mainly turbels (a type of [gelisol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelisol)). The active layer tends to be less than one metre deep, except near rivers.

The highest point is the active volcano [Klyuchevskaya Sopka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klyuchevskaya_Sopka), on the [Kamchatka Peninsula](678671.md). Its peak reaches 4,750 metres (15,580 ft). The [Ukok Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukok_Plateau) is a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-58]

### Geology

The West Siberian Plain, consisting mostly of [Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic) [alluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvium) deposits, is somewhat flat. In the mid-[Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene), many deposits on this plain resulted from [ice dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proglacial_lake) which produced a large [glacial lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_lake). This mid- to late-Pleistocene lake blocked the northward flow of the Ob and [Yenisey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenisey) rivers, resulting in a redirection southwest into the [Caspian](19653787.md) and [Aral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aral_Sea) seas via the [Turgai Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turgai_Valley).[^cite_note-59] The area is very swampy, and soils are mostly peaty [histosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histosol) and, in the treeless northern part, [histels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelisol#Suborders). In the south of the plain, where [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost) is largely absent, rich grasslands that are an extension of the [Kazakh Steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_Steppe) formed the original vegetation, most of which is no longer visible.



[Belukha Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belukha_Mountain)



[Verkhoyansk Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhoyansk_Range)

The Central Siberian Plateau is an ancient [craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craton) (sometimes named *[Angaraland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia_\(continent\))*) that formed an independent continent before the [Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian). It is exceptionally rich in minerals, containing large deposits of [gold](12240.md), [diamonds](8082.md), and ores of [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese), [lead](17747.md), [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel), [cobalt](24580536.md), and [molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum). Much of the area includes the [Siberian Traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Traps)—a [large igneous province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_igneous_province). A massive eruptive period approximately coincided with the [Permian–Triassic extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian%E2%80%93Triassic_extinction_event); the volcanic event is one of the largest known volcanic eruptions in [Earth's history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Earth). Only the extreme northwest was glaciated during the [Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary) but almost all is under exceptionally deep permafrost, and the only tree that can thrive, despite the warm summers, is the deciduous [Siberian Larch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Larch) (*Larix sibirica*) with its very shallow roots. Outside the extreme northwest, the [taiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiga) is dominant, covering a significant fraction of the entirety of Siberia.[^cite_note-60] Soils here are mainly [turbels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelisol), giving way to [spodosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podzol) where the active layer becomes thicker and the ice-content lower.

The Lena-Tunguska petroleum province includes the Central Siberian platform (some authors refer to it as the "Eastern Siberian platform"), bounded on the northeast and east by the late [Carboniferous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous) through [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) Verkhoyansk [fold belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fold_and_thrust_belt), on the northwest by the [Paleozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoic) Taymr fold belt, and on the southeast, south and southwest by the Middle [Silurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silurian) to [Middle Devonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Devonian) Baykalian fold belt.[^cite_note-Meyerhof-61]<sup>: 228 </sup> A regional geologic reconnaissance study begun in 1932 and followed by surface and subsurface mapping revealed the Markova-Angara Arch ([anticline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticline)). This led to the discovery of the Markovo Oil Field in 1962 with the Markovo–1 well, which produced from the early [Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian) Osa Horizon [bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal)-[sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) at a depth of 2,156 metres (7,073 ft).[^cite_note-Meyerhof-61]<sup>: 243 </sup> The Sredne-Botuobin Gas Field was discovered in 1970, producing from the Osa and the [Proterozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proterozoic) Parfenovo Horizon.[^cite_note-Meyerhof-61]<sup>: 244 </sup> The Yaraktin Oil Field was discovered in 1971, producing from the [Vendian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ediacaran) Yaraktin Horizon at depths of up to 1,750 metres (5,740 ft), which lies below Permian to lower Jurassic [basalt traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_basalt).[^cite_note-Meyerhof-61]<sup>: 244 </sup>

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Russia)



Siberian [taiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiga)



&#x20;    [polar desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_desert)      [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra)      [alpine tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_tundra)      [taiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiga)      [montane forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montane_forest)      [temperate broadleaf forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_broadleaf_forest)      [temperate steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_steppe)      [dry steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_steppe)\
[Vegetation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome) in Siberia mostly consists of [taiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiga), with a [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) belt on the northern fringe, and a [temperate forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_broadleaf_and_mixed_forest) zone in the south.

The climate varies dramatically, as Siberia is located in the [Arctic](14534679.md), [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate) and [temperate climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate) zones.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESamoylova2015135-57] It typically has warm but short summers, and long, extremely cold winters.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESamoylova2015135-57] On the north coast, north of the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle), there is a very short (about one month long) summer.

Almost all the population lives in the south, along the route of the [Trans-Siberian Railway](56912.md). The climate in this southernmost part is [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (Köppen *Dfa/Dfb* or *Dwa/Dwb*) with cold winters but fairly warm summers lasting at least four months. The annual average temperature is about 0.5 °C (32.9 °F). January averages about −20 °C (−4 °F) and July about +19 °C (66 °F), while daytime temperatures in summer typically exceed 20 °C (68 °F).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] With a reliable growing season, an abundance of sunshine and fertile [chernozem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernozem) soils, southern Siberia is acceptable for profitable agriculture, as was demonstrated in the early 20th century.

Most of Siberia lies in a continental subarctic climate (Koppen *Dfc*, *Dwc*, or *Dsc*), with the annual average temperature about −5 °C (23 °F) and an average for January of −25 °C (−13 °F) and an average for July of +17 °C (63 °F),[^cite_note-64] although this varies considerably, with an average for January as low as −48 °C (−54 °F) in the [Sakha Republic](57905.md) to −20 °C (−4 °F) in the south of the West Siberian Plain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESamoylova2015135-57] The July average ranges from 5 °C (41 °F) in the north to 23 °C (73 °F) in the south.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESamoylova2015135-57]

*[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider)* lists [Verkhoyansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhoyansk) and [Oymyakon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oymyakon), in Sakha, as being in competition for the title of the "[Pole of Cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_of_Cold):" the coldest inhabited point in the Northern Hemisphere. Oymyakon recorded a temperature of −67.7 °C (−89.9 °F) on 6 February 1933. Verkhoyansk, further north and further inland, recorded a temperature of −69.8 °C (−93.6 °F) for three consecutive nights: 5, 6 and 7 February 1933. Each town also frequently reaches 30 °C (86 °F) in the summer, giving them, and much of the rest of Russian Siberia, the world's greatest temperature variation between summer's highs and winter's lows, often well over 94–100+ °C (169–180+ °F) between the seasons.[^cite_note-65]

Southwesterly winds bring warm air from Central Asia and the Middle East. The climate in West Siberia (Omsk, or Novosibirsk) is several degrees warmer than in the East ([Irkutsk](56872.md), or [Chita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chita,_Zabaykalsky_Krai)). But summer temperatures in other regions can reach +38 °C (100 °F). In general, Sakha is the coldest Siberian region, and the basin of the [Yana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yana_\(river\)) has the lowest temperatures of all, with permafrost reaching 1,493 metres (4,898 ft). Nevertheless, Imperial Russian plans of settlement never viewed cold as an impediment. In the winter, southern Siberia sits near the center of the semi-permanent [Siberian High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_High), so winds are usually light in the winter.

Precipitation is generally low, exceeding 500 millimetres (20 in) only in Kamchatka, where moist winds flow from the [Sea of Okhotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Okhotsk) onto high mountains – producing the region's only major glaciers, though volcanic eruptions and low summer temperatures allow only limited forests to grow. Precipitation is high in most of [Primorye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorsky_Krai) in the extreme south, where monsoonal influences can produce quite heavy summer rainfall.

#### Global warming

According to Vasily Kryuchkov, approximately 31,000 square kilometers of the Russian Arctic has been subjected to severe environmental disturbance. Researchers, including Sergei Kirpotin at [Tomsk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomsk_State_University) and Judith Marquand at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University), warn that Western Siberia has begun to thaw as a result of global warming. The frozen [peat bogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peat_bog) in this region may hold billions of tons of [methane gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane_gas) which may be released into the atmosphere. Methane is a [greenhouse gas](21350772.md) [22 times more powerful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming_potential) than [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide).[^cite_note-67] In 2008 a research expedition for the [American Geophysical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Geophysical_Union) detected levels of methane up to 100 times above normal in the atmosphere above the Siberian Arctic, likely the result of [methane clathrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane_clathrate) being released through holes in a frozen "lid" of seabed permafrost around the outfall of the [Lena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_\(river\)) and the area between the [Laptev Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laptev_Sea) and [East Siberian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Siberian_Sea).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

Since 1988, experimentation at [Pleistocene Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene_Park) has proposed to restore the grasslands of prehistoric times by conducting research on the effects of large herbivores on permafrost, suggesting that animals, rather than climate, maintained the past ecosystem. The nature reserve park also conducts climatic research on the changes expected from the reintroduction of grazing animals or large herbivores, hypothesizing that a transition from tundra to grassland would lead to a net change in energy emission to absorption ratios.[^cite_note-Zimov_2005:_Pleistocene_Park:_Return_of_the_Mammoth's_Ecosystem._Science_Mag.-70]

## Borders and administrative division



Map of the most populated area of Siberia with clickable city names (SVG)



Comparison of the nine biggest Siberian cities' growth in the 20th century

The term "Siberia" has both a long history and wide significance, and association. The understanding and association of "Siberia" have gradually changed during the ages. Historically, Siberia was defined as the whole part of Russia and North Kazakhstan to the east of Ural Mountains, including the [Russian Far East](173135.md). According to this definition, Siberia extended eastward from the Ural Mountains to the Pacific coast, and southward from the Arctic Ocean to the border of Central Asia and the national borders of both Mongolia and China.[^cite_note-86]

Soviet-era sources (*[Great Soviet Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Soviet_Encyclopedia)* and others)[^cite_note-bse-5] and modern Russian ones[^cite_note-87] usually define Siberia as a region extending eastward from the Ural Mountains to the [watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_divide) between Pacific and Arctic drainage basins, and southward from the Arctic Ocean to the hills of north-central [Kazakhstan](16642.md) and the national borders of both Mongolia and China. By this definition, Siberia includes the [federal subjects](701438.md) of the [Siberian Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Federal_District), and some of the [Ural Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Federal_District), as well as [Sakha (Yakutia) Republic](57905.md), which is a part of the [Far Eastern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Federal_District). Geographically, this definition includes subdivisions of several other subjects of Urals and Far Eastern federal districts, but they are not included administratively. This definition excludes [Sverdlovsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverdlovsk_Oblast) and [Chelyabinsk Oblast](475230.md), both of which are included in some wider definitions of Siberia.

Other sources may use either a somewhat wider definition that states the Pacific coast, not the watershed, is the eastern boundary (thus including the whole Russian Far East), as well as all Northern Kazakhstan is its subregion in the south-west[^cite_note-Britannica-3] or a somewhat narrower one that limits Siberia to the Siberian Federal District (thus excluding all subjects of other districts).[^cite_note-88] In Russian, 'Siberia' is commonly used as a substitute for the name of the federal district by those who live in the district itself, and who residing outside of it. Due to the different interpretations of Siberia, starting from [Tyumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyumen), to [Chita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chita,_Zabaykalsky_Krai), the territory generally defined as 'Siberia', some people will define themselves as 'Siberian', while others not.

A number of factors in recent years, including the fomenting of Siberian separatism, have made the definition of the territory of Siberia a potentially controversial subject.[^cite_note-89] In the eastern extent of Siberia there are territories which are not clearly defined as either Siberia or the [Far East](173135.md), making the question of "what is Siberia?" one with no clear answer, and what is a "Siberian", one of self-identification.[^cite_note-90]

### Major cities

The most populous city of Siberia, as well as the third most populous city of Russia, is [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk). Present-day Novosibirsk is an important business, science, manufacturing and cultural center of the Asian part of Russia. [Omsk](62593.md) played an important role in the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War) serving as a provisional Russian capital, as well in the [expansion into and governing of Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Central_Asia). In addition to its cultural status, it has become a major oil-refining, education, transport and agriculture hub.

Other historic cities of Siberia include [Tobolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobolsk) (the first capital and the only [kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobolsk_Kremlin) in Siberia), [Tomsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomsk) (formerly a wealthy merchant's town) and [Irkutsk](56872.md) (former seat of Eastern Siberia's governor general, near lake Baikal). Other major cities include [Barnaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnaul), [Kemerovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemerovo), [Krasnoyarsk](72233.md), [Novokuznetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novokuznetsk), [Tyumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyumen). Wider definitions of geographic Siberia also include the cities of [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk) and [Yekaterinburg](66524.md) in the Urals; [Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk) and [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok) in the Russian Far East; and even [Petropavlovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petropavl) in Kazakhstan and [Harbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin) in China.

## Economy



Russian [oil](23195.md) and [gas](22131.md) pipelines in use

Novosibirsk is the most important city for the Siberian economy; with an extra boost since 2000 when it was designated a regional center for the executive bureaucracy ([Siberian Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Federal_District)). Russia's largest oil refinery is the [Omsk Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omsk_Refinery).

Siberia is extraordinarily rich in minerals, containing ores of almost all economically valuable metals. It has some of the world's largest deposits of [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norilsk#Norilsk-Talnakh_nickel_deposits), gold, lead, coal, molybdenum, gypsum, diamonds, diopside, silver and zinc, as well as extensive unexploited resources of oil and natural gas.[^cite_note-91] Around 70% of Russia's developed oil fields are in the [Khanty-Mansiysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanty-Mansiysk) region.[^cite_note-92] Russia contains about 40% of the world's known resources of nickel at the [Norilsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norilsk) deposit in Siberia. [Norilsk Nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norilsk_Nickel) is the world's largest nickel and palladium producer.[^cite_note-93]

[Siberian agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_agriculture) is severely restricted by the short growing season of most of the region. However, in the southwest where soils consist of fertile black earths and the climate is a little more moderate, there is extensive cropping of wheat, barley, rye and potatoes, along with the grazing of large numbers of sheep and cattle. Elsewhere food production, owing to the poor fertility of the podzolic soils and the extremely short growing seasons, is restricted to the [herding of reindeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer_herding) in the tundra—which has been practiced by natives for over 10,000 years.

Siberia has the world's largest forests. Timber remains an important source of revenue, even though many forests in the east have been logged much more rapidly than they are able to recover. The [Sea of Okhotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Okhotsk) is one of the two or three richest fisheries in the world owing to its cold currents and very large tidal ranges, and thus Siberia produces over 10% of the world's annual fish catch, although fishing has declined somewhat since the collapse of the USSR in 1991.[^cite_note-94]

Reported in 2009, the development of [renewable energy in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Russia) is held back by the lack of a conducive government policy framework,[^cite_note-95] As of 2011, Siberia offers special opportunities for off-grid renewable energy developments. Remote parts of Siberia are too costly to connect to central electricity and gas grids and have therefore historically been supplied with costly diesel, sometimes flown in by helicopter. In such cases renewable energy is often cheaper.[^cite_note-96]

According to the [Russian Census of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Census_\(2010\)), the Siberian and Far Eastern Federal Districts, together have a population of about 25.6 million. [Tyumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyumen_Oblast) and [Kurgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan_Oblast) Oblasts, which are geographically in Siberia but administratively part of the [Urals Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urals_Federal_District), together have a population of about 4.3 million. Thus, the whole region of Siberia (in the broadest usage of the term) is home to approximately 30 million people.[^cite_note-104] It has a population density of about three people per square kilometre.



[Omsk](62593.md), the third largest city in Siberia



[Tomsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomsk), one of the oldest Siberian cities, founded in 1604

The largest ethnic group in Siberia is Slavic-origin [Russians](59510.md), including their sub-ethnic group [Siberians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberians), and russified [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_Siberia).[^cite_note-105] [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) and other [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans) ethnicities make up the vast majority (over 85%) of the Siberian population. There are also other groups of indigenous Siberian and non-indigenous ethnic origin. A minority of the current population are descendants of [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol) or Turkic people (mainly [Buryats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryats), [Yakuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuts), [Tuvans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvans), [Altai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altai_people) and [Khakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khakas)) or [northern indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_list_of_indigenous_minority_peoples_of_the_North,_Siberia,_and_the_Far_East_of_Russia). Slavic-origin Russians outnumber all of the indigenous peoples combined, except in the Republics of Tuva and Sakha.

According to the 2002 census there are 500,000 [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) in Siberia, but of these, 300,000 are [Volga Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Tatars) who also settled in Siberia during periods of colonization and are thus also non-indigenous Siberians, in contrast to the 200,000 [Siberian Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Tatars) which are indigenous to Siberia.[^cite_note-106] Of the indigenous Siberians, the Mongol-speaking Buryats, numbering approximately 500,000, are the most numerous group in Siberia, and they are mainly concentrated in their homeland, the [Buryat Republic](741441.md).[^cite_note-107] According to the 2010 census there were 478,085 indigenous Turkic-speaking Yakuts.[^cite_note-108] Other ethnic groups indigenous to Siberia include [Kets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ket_people), [Evenks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenks), [Chukchis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukchis), [Koryaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryaks), [Yupiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yupik_peoples), and [Yukaghirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukaghirs).

About 70% of Siberia's people live in cities, mainly in apartments.[^cite_note-109] Many people also live in rural areas, in simple, spacious, log houses.

## Religion

[Transfiguration Cathedral, Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfiguration_Cathedral,_Khabarovsk)

There are a variety of beliefs throughout Siberia, including [Orthodox Christianity](10186.md), other denominations of Christianity, [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) and [Islam](6037917.md).[^cite_note-110] The Siberian Federal District has an estimated 250,000 Muslims. An estimated 70,000 [Jews live in Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Russia),[^cite_note-111] some in the [Jewish Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Autonomous_Oblast).[^cite_note-112] The predominant religious group is the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church).

Tradition regards Siberia the archetypal home of [shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamanism), and [polytheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytheism) is popular.[^cite_note-locclass-113] These native sacred practices are considered by the tribes to be very ancient. There are records of Siberian tribal healing practices dating back to the 13th century.[^cite_note-114] The vast territory of Siberia has many different local traditions of gods. These include: [Ak Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak_Ana), [Anapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anapel), [Bugady Musun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugady_Musun), [Kara Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayra), [Khaltesh-Anki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaltesh-Anki), [Kini'je](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kini'je), [Ku'urkil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku'urkil), [Nga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nga_\(god\)), [Nu'tenut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu'tenut), [Num-Torum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Num-Torum), [Pon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukaghir_Pon), [Pugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pugu_\(deity\)), [Todote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todote), [Toko'yoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toko'yoto), [Tomam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomam), [Xaya Iccita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xaya_Iccita) and [Zonget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zonget). Places with sacred areas include [Olkhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olkhon), an island in [Lake Baikal](56865.md).

## Transport

Many cities in northern Siberia, such as [Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky), cannot be reached by road, as there are virtually none connecting from other major cities in Russia or Asia. Siberia can be reached through the [Trans-Siberian Railway](56912.md), which operates from Moscow in the west to [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok) in the east. Cities that are located far from the railway are reached by air or by the separate [Baikal–Amur Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal%E2%80%93Amur_Mainline).

## Culture

[Stroganina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroganina) is a raw fish dish of the indigenous people of northern Arctic Siberia made from raw, thin, long-sliced frozen fish.[^cite_note-115] It is a popular dish amongst native Siberians.[^cite_note-116] Siberia is known for its [pelmeni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelmeni) dumpling; in the winter, the dumplings are traditionally frozen and stored outdoors. There are various berry, nut and mushroom dishes making use of the riches of abundant nature.
