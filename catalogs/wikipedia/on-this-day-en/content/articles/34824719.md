**Jalud** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جاﻟﻭﺩ) is a [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) village in the [Nablus Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Governorate) in the northern [West Bank](33209.md). It is approximately 30 kilometers (19 mi) south of [Nablus](233369.md) and is situated just east of [Qaryut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaryut), south of [Qusra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qusra) and northeast of [Shilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilo,_Mateh_Binyamin), an [Israeli settlement](15123.md). Its land area consists of 16,517 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) (16.517 square kilometers), 98 of which constitutes its built-up area.[^cite_note-3] Jalud is encircled by four [illegal outposts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_outpost): [Esh Kodesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esh_Kodesh), [Adi Ad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adei_Ad), [Ahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahiya,_Mateh_Binyamin) and [Shvut Rachel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shvut_Rachel). Jalud residents were blocked by both [IDF](39237.md) forces and settlers from tending most of their farms from 2001 to 2007. In 2007 permission was given to farm their groves, twice a year for a few days, on condition that prior coordinating arrangements are made with the IDF.[^cite_note-Hass2-4]

## Location

Jalud is located 17 kilometers (11 mi) south of [Nablus](233369.md) (distance from the center of the village to the city center of Nablus). It is bordered by [Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duma,_Nablus) to the east, [Qusra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qusra) and [Talfit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talfit) to the north, [As Sawiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Sawiya) and [Qaryut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaryut) to the west, and [Turmus Ayya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmus_Ayya) to the south.[^cite_note-5]

## History

[Potsherds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsherd) from [Iron Age II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age#Near_East_timeline), [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate), [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states)/[Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) and [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate_\(Cairo\)) eras have been found here.[^cite_note-Finkelstein-6]

Jalud is mentioned as "Galuda of [Aqraba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqraba,_Nablus)" in a [Jewish](25955086.md) marriage certificate of "Elijah, son of Simeon", which was discovered in a cave at [Wadi Murabba'at](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Murabba'at) and is dated to 123 CE.[^cite_note-Klein2009-7][^cite_note-8] There's a possibility that merchants from this village had a court in [Lod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]



Graves, excavated by [Clermont-Ganneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Simon_Clermont-Ganneau) in the early 1870s

[Clermont-Ganneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Simon_Clermont-Ganneau) noted several rock-hewn tombs SSW of the village. One he excavated had three [arcosolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcosolia), and a fully working stone door.[^cite_note-11]

### Ottoman era

In 1596, Jalud appeared in [Ottoman](22278.md) [tax registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter) as being a village in the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* of Jabal Qubal in the *[liwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_\(Arabic\))* of [Nablus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Sanjak). It had a population of 20 households, all [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). They paid a fixed tax rate of 33.3% on agricultural products, including wheat, barley, summer crops, olive trees, goats and beehives; a total of 22,070 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e). All of the revenues went to a [waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf).[^cite_note-12] Potsherds from the early Ottoman era have also been found here.[^cite_note-Finkelstein-6]

In 1838 Jalud was counted as a Muslim village in the subdistrict of *el-Beitawi*, east of Nablus.[^cite_note-13]

In 1870 French explorer [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) visited the village, which he found to have about 300 inhabitants.[^cite_note-14] In 1882, the [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)*, (SWP), described Jalud as "a small village on low ground, with olives to the south".[^cite_note-15]

### British Mandate era

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), Jalud had a population of 145 Muslims,[^cite_note-Census1922-16] increasing in the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) to 225, still all Muslim, in 52 houses.[^cite_note-Census1931-17]

In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) the population had increased to 300 Muslims,[^cite_note-1945p18-18] while the total land area was 15,815 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam), according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-19] Of this, 457 dunams were allocated for plantations and irrigable land, 6,838 for cereals,[^cite_note-20] while 24 dunams were classified as built-up (urban) areas.[^cite_note-21]

### Jordanian era

In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and after the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), Jalud came under [Jordanian](7515964.md) rule. It was [annexed by Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) in 1950.

The Jordanian census of 1961 found 290 inhabitants.[^cite_note-22]

### Post-1967



"Let us handle them." Graffiti spray-painted in Hebrew by Israeli settlers on the wall of a home in Jalud, 2018

Since the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Jalud has been under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md).

In 2017, Jalud's population was 743 according to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS).[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1] There were 91 non-residential buildings, 94 houses and five business establishments in the village. The average household size was 5.5 persons.[^cite_note-23] According to Jalud's mayor, the village experiences high unemployment and migration due to land confiscation by [Israel](9282173.md) and sporadic violence from nearby Israeli settlements,[^cite_note-Ma'an-24] which for 10 years has impeded villagers' access to their groves.[^cite_note-Hass-25]

### Israeli settler violence

- On 9 February 2011, Israeli settlers from [Ahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahiya,_Mateh_Binyamin), a nearby settlement outpost, attacked Jalud, demanding residents evacuate their houses. Israeli police arrived following clashes between the settlers and Palestinian residents, dispersing the former using [tear gas](1202646.md) and stun grenades.

  [^cite_note-Ma'an-24]

- On 21 October 2011, while international activists and [Combatants for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combatants_for_Peace) assisted the Jalud villagers in reclaiming their harvests, a group of 4 armed and masked men from [Esh Kodesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esh_Kodesh), a [satellite outpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_outpost), confronted them yelling that they must get off what the group asserted was outpost land. They then threw a [stun grenade](662807.md) and fired in the direction of the harvesters, some of whom were then clubbed. According to the testimony of Israeli eyewitnesses, the [IDF](39237.md) and [Border Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Border_Police) present did not intervene, other than to fire more tear gas grenades at the harvesters and wounded. A formal complaint was laid. Follow-up checks by the Israeli [NGO](46539.md) [Yesh Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yesh_Din) over several months indicate that a police investigation is "ongoing".

  [^cite_note-Hass-25]

- On 9 October 2013, Israeli arsonists, apparently from the nearby outposts, intruded into the Jalud elementary school, hurled rocks and damaged 5 cars belonging to the teaching body. They then set fire to the village's olive groves, damaging some 400 trees. A settler website vindicated the attack as a [reprisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_tag_policy) for the dismantlement of another illegal outpost, Givat Geulat Zion, carried out by the IDF that morning. Three youths from Adi Ad were subsequently arrested on suspicion of involvement, but local Palestinian eyewitnesses have stated that the assailants, some 20, did not appear to be minors.

  [^cite_note-Hass2-4]

- In 2017, Fawzi Ibrahim reported waited two months for getting access to his lands, and then having only 2 days to plow and plant. Muhammad Muqbil reported that he had olive trees stolen by settlers, and needed help from [Rabbis for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbis_for_Human_Rights) to get access to his lands.

  [^cite_note-Connor-26]

  [^cite_note-27]

* In April 2022, *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* reported that [Jewish settlers](15123.md) from the nearby [illegal outposts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_outpost) that encircle the town promoted attacks with firebombs in Jalud, torching cars and house yards, in what the Israeli newspaper classified as a "*pogrom*" and "[settler](15123.md) terror".

  [^cite_note-28]

- On 9 January 2026, Israeli settlers from the [Ahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahiya) and [Esh Kodesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esh_Kodesh) outposts cut through the fence and entered Jalud’s secondary mixed school, which serves about 210 students. During the incident, settlers threw flammable materials into a classroom and spray-painted slogans on the walls.

  [^cite_note-29]

**Israeli settlers arson on Jalud March 2026**

- On 22 March 2026, the village was attacked by a group of settlers who beat and injured several residents and set fire to cars and buildings.

  [^cite_note-30]

* More than 50 settlers besieged and ultimately displaced the Tubasi family from their houses in late July 2026.

  [^cite_note-31]

- The family attempted to return in September with protection from the [Israel Border Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Border_Police), but were attacked by more than 100 settlers. Three members of the border police were injured, two settlers were arrested there and a third was arrested later.

## Olive groves

In her 2009 publication entitled *Tree Flags*, legal scholar and ethnographer [Irus Braverman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irus_Braverman) describes how Palestinians identify olive groves as "their symbol of their longtime agricultural connection to the land."[^cite_note-Bravenman_2010-34]<sup>: 1 </sup>[^cite_note-Staton_2015-35][^cite_note-tree_wars-36]
