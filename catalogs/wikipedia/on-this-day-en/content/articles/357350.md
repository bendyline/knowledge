The **Tigray Region**[^cite_note-5] (or simply **Tigray**; officially the **Tigray National Regional State**)[^cite_note-6] is the northernmost [regional state](690337.md) in [Ethiopia](187749.md). The Tigray Region is the homeland of the [Tigrayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayan), [Irob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irob_people) and [Kunama people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunama_people). Its capital and largest city is [Mekelle](1221559.md). Tigray is the fifth-largest by area, the fourth-most populous, and the fifth-most densely populated of the 14 regional states. Tigray is bordered by [Eritrea](17238590.md) to the north, the [Amhara Region](1746709.md) to the south, [Sudan](27421.md) to the west, and [Afar Region](2483633.md) to the east .[^cite_note-2009map-7]

Tigray's official language is [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language), the same language found in Eritrea. The Tigray region had an estimated pre-[Tigray war](65760352.md) population of 7,070,260.[^cite_note-8] The majority of the population (c. 80%) are farmers, contributing 46% to the regional gross domestic product (2009). The highlands have the highest population density, especially in [eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misraqawi_Zone) and [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maekelay_Zone) Tigray. The much less densely populated lowlands comprise 48% of Tigray's area. Although the percentage of Muslims in Tigray as of 2007 was approximately 4%, it has been historically Islam's doorway to the region and to Africa at large.[^cite_note-\:1-9] Approximately 95.6% of [Tigrayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayans) are [Orthodox Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches).

The government of Tigray consists of the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_branch), led by the president, [General Tadesse Werede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadesse_Werede); the [legislative branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_branch), which comprises the state council; and the [judicial branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_branch), which is led by the state supreme court. In early November 2020, a conflict between the [Tigray People's Liberation Front](1276759.md) (TPLF) and the Ethiopian federal government (with support from Eritrea)[^cite_note-10] rapidly escalated into the [Tigray War](65760352.md), destabilizing the region,[^cite_note-11] displacing millions of people.[^cite_note-12] There were around 600,000 civilian deaths[^cite_note-13] and as many as 70,000 combatants [were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casualties_of_the_Tigray_War) and up to 18,000 injured as a result of the war.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] As of&#x20;

## History

### 3rd millennium to 1st century BC

Tigray is often regarded as the cradle of Ethiopian civilization.[^cite_note-17] Its landscape has many historic monuments. Three major monotheistic religions, [Judaism](15624.md), [Christianity](5211.md), and [Islam](6037917.md) arrived in [Axum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum) through the [Red Sea](26191.md).

Given the presence of a large temple complex and fertile surroundings, the capital of the 3,000-year-old kingdom of [Dʿmt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%CA%BFmt) may have been near present-day [Yeha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeha).[^cite_note-Thurstan612-18] Dʿmt developed irrigation schemes, used the [plough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plough), grew [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), and made [iron tools and weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age). Some modern historians, including [Stuart Munro-Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Munro-Hay), [Rodolfo Fattovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Fattovich), Ayele Bekerie, [Cain Felder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cain_Felder), and [Ephraim Isaac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephraim_Isaac) consider this civilization to be indigenous, although [Sabaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaeans)-influenced due to the latter's dominance of the [Red Sea](26191.md). Others, including Joseph Michels, Henri de Contenson, Tekletsadik Mekuria, and Stanley Burstein, have viewed Dʿmt as the result of a mixture of Sabaean and indigenous peoples.[^cite_note-Munro-Hay57-19][^cite_note-Tihama-20] The most recent research, however, shows that [Ge'ez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ge'ez_language), the ancient Semitic language spoken in Tigray, Eritrea, and northern Ethiopia in ancient times, is not likely to have been derived from [Sabaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaean_language).[^cite_note-21] There is evidence of a Semitic-speaking presence in Tigray, Eritrea, and northern Ethiopia at least as early as 2000 BC.[^cite_note-Tihama-20][^cite_note-22] It is now believed that Sabaean influence was minor, limited to a few localities, and disappearing after a few decades or a century, It may have represented a trading or military colony, in some sort of symbiosis or military alliance with the civilization of Dʿmt or some other proto-[Aksumite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum) state.[^cite_note-Munro-Hay57-19][^cite_note-23]

After the fall of Dʿmt in the 5th century BC, the plateau came to be dominated by smaller, unknown successor kingdoms. This lasted until the rise of one of these polities during the first century BC, the [Aksumite Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum), which succeeded in reunifying the area[^cite_note-24] and is, in effect, the ancestor of medieval and modern states in Eritrea and Ethiopia using the name "Ethiopia" as early as the 4th century.[^cite_note-Munro-Hay57-19][^cite_note-Paul_B._Henze_2005-25]

### 1st to 10th century AD



Axum Stele in the city [Axum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum).



Aksumite gold coins.

The Kingdom of Aksum was a trading empire rooted in Tigray.[^cite_note-26] It existed from approximately 100–940 AD, growing from the proto-Aksumite [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) period c. 4th century BC to achieve prominence by the 1st century AD.

According to the *[Book of Axum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Axum)*, Axum's first capital, Mazaber, was built by Itiyopis, son of Cush.[^cite_note-27] The capital was later moved to [Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum) in northern Ethiopia.

The Empire of Aksum, at its height, at times extended across most of present-day [Eritrea](17238590.md), Ethiopia, [Djibouti](17207794.md), Sudan, Yemen and Saudi Arabia. The capital city of the empire was [Axum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum), now in northern Ethiopia. Today a smaller community, the city of Axum was once a bustling metropolis and a cultural and economic hub. Two hills and two streams lie on the east and west expanses of the city; perhaps providing the initial impetus for settling this area. Along the hills and plain outside the city, the Aksumites had cemeteries with elaborate grave stones, which are called [stelae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stelae), or [obelisks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obelisk). Other important cities included [Yeha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeha), [Hawulti-Melazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawulti-Melazo), [Matara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matara,_Eritrea), [Adulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adulis), and [Qohaito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qohaito), the last three of which are now in Eritrea. By the reign of [Endybis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endybis) in the late 3rd century, Aksum had begun minting its own currency and was named by [Mani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_\(prophet\)) as one of the four great powers of his time, along with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China) and the [Sassanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) empires. It converted to [Christianity](5211.md) in 325 or 328 under [King Ezana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezana_of_Axum) and was the first state to use the image of [the cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifix) on its coins.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

Located in [Negash, Tigray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negash), the 7th century [Al-Nejashi Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Nejashi_Mosque) is considered the oldest in Africa and serves as a symbol of early Islam's asylum in the Aksumite Kingdom. Despite sustaining damage during the 2020 Tigray War, the heritage site, which holds the tombs of the Prophet Muhammed's companions, has undergone restoration efforts.[^cite_note-30]

### 11th to 19th century AD



[Mekelle](1221559.md) palace of Emperor [Yohannes IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yohannes_IV) (emperor of the whole [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire)).

In the 11th century the Tigrinya-speaking lands (Tigray-[Mareb Melash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medri_Bahri)) were divided into two provinces, separated by the Mereb River, by the newly enthroned Agaw emperors. The governor of the northern province received the title Bahre Negash (Ruler of the sea), whereas the governor of the southern province was given the title of Tigray Mekonen (Lord of Tigray). The Portuguese Jesuit Emanuele Baradas's work titled "Do reino de Tigr", written in 1633–34, states that the "Reino de Tigr" (Kingdom of Tigray) extended from [Hamasien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamasien) to [Temben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Tembien), from the borders of [Dankel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Depression) to the [Adwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adwa) mountain. He also stated that Tigray-Mereb Melash was divided into 24 smaller political units (principalities), twelve of which were located south of the Mereb and governed by the Tigray Mekonen, based in [Enderta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enderta_Province). The other twelve were located north of the Mereb, under the authority of the [Bahre Negash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medri_Bahri), based in the district of [Serae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serae).[^cite_note-32]

The *[Book of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Aksum)*, written and compiled mainly in the period from the sixteenth to seventeenth centuries, shows a traditional schematic map of Tigray with the city of [Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksum) at its center, surrounded by the 13 principal provinces: "Tembien, Shire, Serae, Hamasien, [Bur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buri_province?action=edit\&redlink=1), Sam'a, [Agame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agame), Amba Senayt, [Garalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gar'alta?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Enderta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enderta_Province), Sahart and Abergele."[^cite_note-33]

During the Middle Ages, the position of Tigray Mekonnen ("Governor of Tigray") was established to rule over the area. Other districts included [Akele Guzay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkele_Guzay) (now part of [Eritrea](17238590.md)), and the kingdom of the [Bahr negus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahr_negus), who ruled much of what is now Eritrea and [Shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire,_Ethiopia) district and town in Western Tigray. At the time when Tigray Mekonnen existed simultaneously with that of Bahr negus, their frontier seems to have been the [Mareb River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mareb_River), which is currently constitutes the border between the Ethiopian province of Tigray and Eritrea.

After the loss of power of the Bahr negus in the aftermath of [Bahr negus Yeshaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahr_negus_Yeshaq)'s rebellions, the title of Tigray mekonnen gained power in relation to the Bahr negus and at times included ruling over parts of what is now Eritrea, especially in the 19th century.[^cite_note-34] By the unsettled [Zemene Mesafint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemene_Mesafint) period ("Era of the Princes"), both designations had declined to little more than empty titles, and the lord who succeeded them used (and received from the Emperor) the title of either [Ras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_\(title\)) or [Dejazmach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejazmach), beginning with Ras [Mikael Sehul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Sehul). Rulers of Tigray such as Ras [Wolde Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolde_Selassie) alternated with others, chiefly those of [Begemder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begemder) or [Yejju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yejju), as warlords to maintain the Ethiopian monarchy during the Zemene Mesafint.

In the mid-19th century, the lords of Tembien and Enderta managed to establish an overlordship of Tigray. One of its members, Dejazmach Kahsay Mercha, ascended the imperial throne in 1872 under the name [Yohannes IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yohannes_IV). Following his 1889 death in the [Battle of Metemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Metemma), the Ethiopian throne came under the control of the king of [Shewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shewa), and the center of power shifted south and away from Tigray.

### 20th century

In 1943, a rebellion [broke out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woyane_rebellion) all over southern and eastern Tigray under the slogan, "there is no government; let's organize and govern ourselves". Throughout Enderta Awraja, including [Mekelle](1221559.md), Didibadergiajen, [Hintalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hintalo), Saharti, [Samre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samre,_Ethiopia) and Wajirat, Raya Awraja, Kilte-Awlaelo Awraja and Tembien Awraja, local assemblies, called gerreb, were formed. The gerreb sent representatives to a central congress, called the shengo, which elected leaders and established a military command system. Although the first [Woyane rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woyane_rebellion) of 1943 had shortcomings as a prototype revolution, historians agree that it involved a fairly high level of spontaneity and peasant initiative. It demonstrated considerable popular participation and reflected widely shared grievances. The uprising was specifically directed against the central "Shoan Amhara" regime of [Haile Selassie I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Selassie) to rile support, despite Tigrayan imperial elite being collaborators and beneficiaries of the regime.

#### Ethiopian Civil War



Memorial in Mekelle to more than 60,000 TPLF fighters who died and over 100,000 fighters who were injured in the overthrow of the Marxist [Derg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derg) regime in 1991.

Following the outbreak of the [Ethiopian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Revolution) in February 1974, the first signal of any mass uprising was the actions of the soldiers of the 4th Brigade of the 4th Army Division in Nagelle in southern Ethiopia. The Coordinating Committee of the Armed Forces, Police, and Territorial Army, or the [Derg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derg) ([Ge'ez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ge'ez_language) "Committee"), was officially announced 28 June 1974 by a group of military officers. The committee elected Major [Mengistu Haile Mariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengistu_Haile_Mariam) as its chairman and Major [Atnafu Abate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atnafu_Abate) as its vice-chairman. In July 1974, the Derg obtained key concessions from the emperor, Haile Selassie, which included the power to arrest not only military officers but government officials at every level. Soon both former Prime Ministers [Tsehafi Taezaz Aklilu Habte-Wold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsehafi_Taezaz_Aklilu_Habte-Wold) and [Endalkachew Makonnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endelkachew_Makonnen), along with most of their cabinets, most regional governors, many senior military officers and officials of the Imperial court were imprisoned. In August 1974, after a proposed constitution creating a constitutional monarchy was presented to the emperor, the Derg began a program of dismantling the imperial government in order to forestall further developments in that direction. The Derg [deposed and imprisoned the emperor on 12 September 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Ethiopian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).



Nest box for *[Columba guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speckled_pigeon)* (considered a symbol of peace) in the wall of a homestead in [Zerfenti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zerfenti), a village in Tigray where hundreds were killed by Derg bombings.

In addition, the Derg in 1975 nationalized most industries and private and somewhat secure urban real-estate holdings. But mismanagement, corruption, and general hostility to the Derg's violent rule, coupled with the draining effects of constant warfare with the separatist guerrilla movements in Tigray, led to a drastic fall in general productivity of food and cash crops. In October 1978, the Derg announced the National Revolutionary Development Campaign to mobilize human and material resources to transform the economy, which led to a Ten-Year Plan (1984/85–1993/94) to expand agricultural and industrial output, forecasting a 6.5% growth in GDP and a 3.6% rise in per capita income. Instead per capita income declined 0.8% over this period. Famine scholar [Alex de Waal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Waal) observes that while the [famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%931985_famine_in_Ethiopia) that struck the country in the mid-1980s is usually ascribed to drought, "closer investigation shows that widespread drought occurred only some months after the famine was already under way". Hundreds of thousands fled economic misery, conscription, and political repression, and went to live in neighboring countries and all over the [Western world](21208200.md), creating an Ethiopian [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora).

Toward the end of January 1991, a coalition of rebel forces, the [Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (EPRDF) captured [Gondar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondar), the ancient capital city, [Bahar Dar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahar_Dar), and [Dessie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dessie).

#### Postwar

In 1998, [war erupted between Eritrea and Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean-Ethiopian_War) over a portion of territory that had been administered as part of Tigray, which included the town of [Badme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badme). A 2002 [United Nations](31769.md) decision awarded much of this land to Eritrea, but Ethiopia did not accept the ruling until 2018, when a [bilateral agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Eritrea%E2%80%93Ethiopia_summit) ended the [border conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean%E2%80%93Ethiopian_border_conflict). The text of this agreement has not been publicly availed.

### 21st century

Between April 2001 and December 2010, [Tsegay Berhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsegay_Berhe_Hadera) of the [Tigray People’s Liberation Front](1276759.md) (TPLF) served as President of the Tigray Region. A veteran of the TPLF and former vice-president (1991–2001), Berhe also held the position of vice-chairman of the party during this tenure.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-\:2-38] In 2010, Berhe was succeeded by [Abay Weldu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abay_Weldu), who served as regional president from 2010 until January 2018 and concurrently led the TPLF from September 2012 to November 2017.[^cite_note-\:2-38][^cite_note-39] Weldu came to power amid a rapidly centralized political structure under the [Ethiopian People’s Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (EPRDF), a coalition led by TPLF.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

During their administrations, Tigray’s regional government benefitted from significant fiscal autonomy. State‑linked conglomerates like EFFORT and METEC funnelled resources into regional coffers, making Tigray one of the most financially empowered regions in Ethiopia.[^cite_note-\:3-43] This economic clout helped the regional TPLF maintain political dominance, though critics argue that such financial strength also entrenched [patronage networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronage) and limited [political pluralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluralism_\(political_philosophy\)). Nationally, EPRDF—which included the TPLF as dominant member since its founding in 1989—held control over Ethiopia’s [ethnic‑federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_federalism) system.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The 2005 national parliamentary elections marked a turning point; serious opposition gains were met with strong government crackdowns, and protests later erupted in 2016–2017 demanding reforms and challenging the longstanding one‑party dominance.[^cite_note-\:3-43][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

#### 2020 administrative reorganisation

Between 2018 and 2020, as part of a reform aimed to deepen and strengthen decentralisation, woredas were reorganised, and new boundaries established. As smaller towns had been growing, they had started providing a larger range of services, such as markets and even banks, that encouraged locals to travel there rather than to their formal woreda centre. However, these locals still had to travel to their local woreda centre for most local government services – often in a different direction. In 2018 and 2019, after multiple village discussions that were often vigorous in the more remote areas, 21 independent urban administrations were added and other boundaries re-drawn, resulting in an increase from 35 to 88 woredas in January 2020.

#### Tigray War

Main article: [Tigray War](65760352.md)

See also: [Transitional Government of Tigray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Government_of_Tigray)

Following the [2020 Tigray regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Tigray_regional_election), on 4 November, after the attacks by TDF on Northern Command units in Tigray and missiles sent to Eritrea, the Ethiopian and Eritrean militaries launched counterattacks. Ethiopian forces advanced through southern Tigray, while Eritrean troops occupied northern border towns.[^cite_note-48]

Warfare, the [COVID-19 pandemic in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Ethiopia), and a [locust outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_locust_infestation) contributed to an [emergency food situation in the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famine_in_the_Tigray_War) by January [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_in_Ethiopia). Approximately two million people faced food shortages, with a critical situation in [Shire Inda Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_Inda_Selassie), hosting 100,000 refugees. The [Famine Early Warning Systems Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famine_Early_Warning_Systems_Network) indicated that parts of central and eastern Tigray were likely in emergency phase 4, a step below famine.[^cite_note-49]

#### After the Tigray War

Main article: [Tigray political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigray_political_crisis)

After the [Tigray War (2020–2022)](65760352.md), which resulted in an estimated 600,000 deaths, the Tigray region faced ongoing instability. A peace agreement in November 2022 led to the early 2023 formation of the [Interim Regional Administration of Tigray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Regional_Administration_of_Tigray), but the region struggled with implementing key provisions, such as the return of displaced people. In 2025, tensions resurfaced when Tigray’s interim president, [Getachew Reda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getachew_Reda), fled to [Addis Ababa](52593.md) after a faction led by former [TPLF](1276759.md) leader [Debretsion Gebremichael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debretsion_Gebremichael) seized control. The power struggle sparked fears of renewed conflict. In response, [Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed](56624029.md) invited [Tigrayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayans) to suggest a new leader via email and extended the interim administration’s mandate. This development followed internal struggles, including a coup attempt and clashes, as well as growing concerns over tensions with neighboring [Eritrea](17238590.md), which added to the regional instability.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

## Geography

### Location and size

Tigray is situated between 12° – 15°N and 36° 30' – 40° 30'E.

A 2006 national statistics report stated the land area as 50,079 km<sup>2</sup> (19,336 sq mi).[^cite_note-2006_stats-2] The 2011 National Statistics gave an area of 41,410 km<sup>2</sup> (15,990 sq mi), but the sum of the figures it gave for the Tigray zones was substantially different,[^cite_note-2011_stats-52] rendering the 2011 report internally inconsistent. The figure of 53,036 km<sup>2</sup> (20,477 sq mi) is supported by the [Google Maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Maps) area calculator demonstrated in [this article](https://www.aigaforum.com/amharic-article-2018/Tigray-Boundry-Before-and-After-Derg-part1.pdf).

### Geology

#### Overview

The [East African Orogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Orogeny) led to the growth of a mountain chain in the [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) (up to 800 Ma \[million years ago]), which was largely eroded afterwards.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Around 600 Ma, the [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana) break-up led to the presence of [tectonic structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonics) and a [Palaeozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeozoic) [planation surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planation_surface), that extents to the north and west of the [Dogu'a Tembien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogu'a_Tembien) massif.[^cite_note-56]

Subsequently, there was the deposition of [sedimentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary) and [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic) formations, from older (at the foot of the massif) to younger, near the summits. From Palaeozoic to [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic), Tigray was located near the South Pole. The (reactivate) Precambrian extensional faults guided the deposition of [glacial sediments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Till) ([Edaga Arbi Glacials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edaga_Arbi_Glacials) and [Enticho Sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enticho_Sandstone)). Later alluvial plain sediments were deposited ([Adigrat Sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adigrat_Sandstone)). The break-up of Gondwana ([Late Palaeozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Palaeozoic) to [Early Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Triassic)) led to an extensional [tectonic phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_phase), what caused the lowering of large parts of the [Horn of Africa](62142.md). As a consequence a [marine transgression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_transgression) occurred, leading to the deposition of [marine sediments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_sediments) ([Antalo Limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antalo_Limestone) and Agula Shale).[^cite_note-57] The region has an estimated 3.89 billion tons of mostly "excellent" quality [oil shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_shale).[^cite_note-58]



The [Antalo Limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antalo_Limestone) cliff at Mishlam in the southeastern part of Dogu'a Tembien

At the end of the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) tectonic phase, a new ([Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous)) planation took place. After that, the deposition of continental sediments ([Amba Aradam Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amba_Aradam_Formation)) indicates the presence of less shallow seas, probably caused by a regional uplift. At the beginning of the [Caenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caenozoic), there was a relative tectonic quiescence, during which the Amba Aradam Sandstones were partially eroded, which led to the formation of a new planation surface.[^cite_note-59]

In the [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene), the Afar [plume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantle_plume), a broad regional uplift, deformed the [lithosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithosphere), leading to the eruption of [flood basalts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_basalt). Three major formations may be distinguished: [lower basalts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashangi_Basalts), [interbedded lacustrine deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intra-volcanic_sedimentary_rock_in_North_Ethiopia) and [upper basalts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaji_Basalts).[^cite_note-miruts2019-60] Almost at the same time, the Mekelle [Dolerite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolerite) intruded into the Mesozoic sediments, following joints and [faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)).[^cite_note-61]

A new magma intrusion occurred in the [Early Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Miocene), which gave rise to [phonolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonolite_series_in_North_Ethiopia) plugs, mainly in the Adwa area and also in Dogu'a Tembien.[^cite_note-miruts2019-60] The present geomorphology is marked by deep valleys, eroded as a result of the regional uplift. Throughout the [Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary), deposition of [alluvium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvium) and freshwater [tufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufa) occurred in the valley bottoms.[^cite_note-62]

#### Fossils

In Tigray, there are two main fossil-bearing geological units. The [Antalo Limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antalo_Limestone) ([upper Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Jurassic)) is the largest. Its marine deposits comprise mainly [benthic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benthic) [marine invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_invertebrates). Also, the [Tertiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_period) [lacustrine deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacustrine_deposits), interbedded in the [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) formations, contain a range of [silicified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicified) [mollusc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusc) fossils.[^cite_note-lerouge\&aerts-63]

In the [Antalo Limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antalo_Limestone): large *[Paracenoceratidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracenoceratidae)* [cephalopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalopods) ([nautilus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nautilus)); *[Nerineidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerineidae)* indet.; [sea urchins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_urchins); [Rhynchonellid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhynchonellid) [brachiopod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiopod); [crustaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustaceans); [coral colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_colonies); [crinoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crinoid) stems.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-lerouge\&aerts-63]

In the Tertiary [silicified lacustrine deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intra-volcanic_sedimentary_rock_in_North_Ethiopia): *[Pila (gastropod)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pila_\(gastropod\))*; *[Lanistes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanistes)* sp.; *[Pirenella conica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirenella_conica)*; and land snails (*[Achatinidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achatinidae)* indet.).[^cite_note-lerouge\&aerts-63][^cite_note-65]

All snail shells, both fossil and recent, are called *t'uyo* in [Tigrinya language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language), which means '[helicoidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicoidal)'.

#### Traditional uses of rock

As Tigray holds a wide variety of [rock types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_types), there is expectedly a varied use of rock.

- **Natural [stone masonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_masonry)**. Preferentially, the easier shaped [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) and [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) are used to build homesteads and churches, but particularly in the upland areas, [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) is also used. Traditionally, fermented mud will be used as mortar

- **Fencing** of homesteads, generally in [dry stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_stone)

- **Church bells**, generally three elongated plates in [phonolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonolite) or [clinkstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinkstone), with different tonalities

- **Milling stone**: for this purpose plucked-bedrock pits, small [rock-cut basins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock-cut_basin) that naturally occur in rivers with [kolks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolk_\(vortex\)), are excavated from the river bed and further shaped. [Milling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_\(grinding\)) is done at home using an elongated small boulder

  [^cite_note-66]

  [^cite_note-67]

- Door and window **[lintels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lintels)**, prepared from rock types that frequently have an elongated shape ([sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone), [phonolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonolite), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone)), or that are easily shaped ([tufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufa))

- **Troughs for livestock** [watering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watering) and feeding, generally hewn from [tufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufa)

- **Footpath [paving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidewalk)**, generally done as [community work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_service). Some very ancient paved [footpaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footpaths) occur on major communication lines dating back to the period before the introduction of the automobile

- foot travellers stop, pray and put an additional stone

- Stones collected from farmlands in order to free space for the crop, and heaped in typical **rounded metres-high heaps**, called *zala*

- **[Contour bunding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_bunding)** or *gedeba*: [terrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_\(agriculture\)) walls in [dry stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_stone), typically laid out along the contour for sake of [soil conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_conservation)

- **[Check dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check_dam)** or *qetri* in [gullies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gullies) for sake of [gully erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gully_erosion) control

- **[Cobble stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobble_stone)**, used for paving secondary streets in the towns. Generally [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) is used.

### Water challenge



Regularised stream, fed by Gereb Segen dam

Overall, the region is [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid). The [wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) lasts only for a couple of months. The farmers are adapted to this, but the problem arises when rains are less than normal. Another major challenge is providing water to urban areas. Smaller towns, but particularly Mekelle, face endemic [water shortages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_shortage). [Reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoirs) have been built, but their management is sub-optimal.

#### Small rodents

The most common pest [rodents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodents) with widespread distribution in agricultural fields and storage areas are three Ethiopian endemic species: the Dembea grass rat (*Arvicanthis dembeensis*, sometimes considered a subspecies of *[Arvicanthis niloticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvicanthis_niloticus)*), Ethiopian white-footed rat (*[Stenocephalemys albipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenocephalemys_albipes)*), and [Awash multimammate mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awash_multimammate_mouse) (*Mastomys awashensis*).[^cite_note-70]

#### Bats

[Bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bats) occur in natural [caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caves), church buildings and abandoned homesteads. The large colony of bats that roosts in [Zeyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeyi) cave comprises *Hipposideros megalotis* ([Ethiopian large-eared roundleaf bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_large-eared_roundleaf_bat)), *[Hipposideros tephrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hipposideridae)*, and *Rhinolophus blasii* ([Blasius's horseshoe bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasius's_horseshoe_bat)).[^cite_note-zeyi-71]

#### Birds

With its numerous [exclosures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclosure), forest fragments and [church forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_forest), Tigray is a [birdwatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdwatcher)'s paradise. Detailed inventories[^cite_note-ALN2019-72][^cite_note-Land_rehabilitation_and_the_conserv-73] list at least 170 bird species, including numerous [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic) species. Species belonging to the [Afrotropical Highland Biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_montane_forests) occur in the dry evergreen montane forests of the highland plateau but can also occupy other habitats. [Wattled Ibis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wattled_Ibis) can be found feeding in wet grassland and open woodland. [Black-winged Lovebird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-winged_Lovebird), [Banded Barbet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banded_Barbet), Golden-mantled or [Abyssinian Woodpecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinian_Woodpecker), [Montane White-eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montane_White-eye), [Rüppell's Robin-chat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCppell's_Robin-chat), [Abyssinian Slaty Flycatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinian_Slaty_Flycatcher) and [Tacazze Sunbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacazze_Sunbird) are found in evergreen forest, mountain woodlands and areas with scattered trees including [fig trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fig_trees), *[Euphorbia abyssinica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphorbia_abyssinica)* and *[Juniperus procera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniperus_procera)*. [Erckel's spurfowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erckel's_spurfowl), [Dusky Turtle Dove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusky_Turtle_Dove), Swainson's or [Grey-headed Sparrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey-headed_Sparrow), [Baglafecht Weaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baglafecht_Weaver), [African Citril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Citril), [Brown-rumped Seedeater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown-rumped_Seedeater) and [Streaky Seedeater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaky_Seedeater) are common [Afrotropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrotropical) breeding residents of woodland edges, scrubland and forest edges. [White-billed Starling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-billed_Starling) and [Little Rock Thrush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock_Thrush) can be found on steep cliffs; [Speckled or African rock pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columba_guinea) and [White-collared Pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-collared_Pigeon) in gorges and rocky places but also in towns and villages.[^cite_note-ALN2019-72]

Species belonging to the [Somali-Masai Biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_desert_and_xeric_shrubland_ecoregions). [Hemprich's Hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemprich's_Hornbill) and [White-rumped Babbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-rumped_Babbler) are found in bushland, scrubland and dense secondary forest, often near cliffs, gorges or water. Chestnut-Winged or [Somali Starling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Starling) and [Rüppell's Weaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCppell's_Weaver) are found in bushy and shrubby areas. [Black-billed wood hoopoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-billed_wood_hoopoe) have some red at the base of the bill or an entirely red bill in this area.[^cite_note-ALN2019-72]

Species belonging to the [Sudan-Guinea Savanna Biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sudanian_savanna): [Green-backed eremomela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green-backed_eremomela) and [Chestnut-crowned Sparrow-Weaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnut-crowned_Sparrow-Weaver).[^cite_note-ALN2019-72]

Species that are neither endemic nor biome-restricted but that have restricted ranges or that can be more easily seen in Ethiopia than elsewhere in their range: [Abyssinian Roller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinian_Roller) is an Ethiopian relative of [Lilac-breasted Roller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilac-breasted_Roller), which is an intra-tropical breeding migrant of south and east Africa, and of [European Roller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Roller), an uncommon [Palearctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palearctic) passage migrant. [Black-billed Barbet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-billed_Barbet), [Yellow-breasted Barbet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow-breasted_Barbet) and [Grey-headed Batis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey-headed_Batis) are species from the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) and [Northern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Africa) but also occur in [Acacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia) woodlands in the area.[^cite_note-ALN2019-72]

The most regularly observed raptor birds in crop fields in Tigray are [Augur buzzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augur_buzzard) (*Buteo augur*), [Common Buzzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Buzzard) (*Buteo buteo*), [Steppe Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe_Eagle) (*Aquila nipalensis*), [Lanner falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanner_falcon) (*Falco biarmicus*), [Black kite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_kite) (*Milvus migrans*), [Yellow-billed kite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow-billed_kite) (*Milvus aegyptius*) and [Barn owl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_barn_owl) (*Tyto alba*).[^cite_note-74]

[Birdwatching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdwatching) can be done particularly in exclosures and forests. Eighteen bird-watching sites have been inventoried in [Enderta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enderta_\(woreda\)) and [Degua Tembien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degua_Tembien)[^cite_note-ALN2019-72] and mapped.[^cite_note-chapmap-75]

## Government and politics

### Executive branch

The executive branch is headed by the Chief Administrator of the [Interim Regional Administration of Tigray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Regional_Administration_of_Tigray). The current president is [Getachew Reda Kahsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getachew_Reda), a TPLF member, appointed in 2023. A Vice President of Tigray succeeds the president in the event of any removal from office, and performs any duties assigned by the president.[^cite_note-77] The other elected constitutional offices in the executive branch are the Regional Health Bureau (*Ato* Hagos Godefy),[^cite_note-78] Educational Bureau (*Ato* Gebre'egziabher),[^cite_note-79] Auditor General (*Ato* Alemseged Kebedew), and 12 other officials.[^cite_note-\:0-80]

### Judicial branch

There are three levels of the Tigray state [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary). The lowest level is the court of common pleas: each woreda maintains its own constitutionally mandated court of common pleas, which maintain jurisdiction over all justiciable matters.[^cite_note-81] The intermediate-level court system is the district court system. Four courts of appeals exist, each retaining jurisdiction over appeals from common pleas, municipal, and county courts in an administrative zone. A case heard in this system is decided by a three-judge panel, and each judge is elected.

The highest-ranking court, the Tigray Supreme Court, is Tigray's "court of last resort".[^cite_note-82] A seven-justice panel composes the court, which, by its own discretion, hears appeals from the courts of appeals, and retains original jurisdiction over limited matters. The chief judge is called the President of Tigray Supreme Court (*W/ro* Hirity Miheretab).

### Legislative branch

The State Council, which is the highest administrative body of the state, is made up of 152 members.[^cite_note-\:0-80]

### National politics

Tigray is represented by 38 representatives in the [Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia House of Peoples' Representatives](4501670.md). But currently after the illegitimate postponement of the national election of Ethiopia Tigray has pulled it representative from the House of House of Peoples' Representatives and has no representation in the Federal parliament[^cite_note-83].

## Demographics



Tigrayan women washing clothes in [Santarfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santarfa)



[Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady_Mary_of_Zion) in [Axum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum)

Based on the 2007 census conducted by the [Central Statistical Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Statistical_Agency_\(Ethiopia\)) of Ethiopia (CSA), the Tigray Region has a population of 4,316,988, of whom 2,126,465 are men and 2,190,523 women; urban inhabitants number 844,040 or 19.6% of the population. With an estimated area of 84,722 km<sup>2</sup>, the region had an estimated density of 51 people per km<sup>2</sup>. In the entire region 992,635 households were counted, for an average of 4.4 people per household, with urban households having on average 3.4 and rural households 4.6.[^cite_note-Census07-84]

In the previous census, conducted in 1994, the region's population was 3,136,267, of whom 1,542,165 were men and 1,594,102 women; urban inhabitants numbered 621,210, or 14% of the population.

According to the CSA, as of 2004, 54.0% of the total population had access to [safe drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_supply_and_sanitation_in_Ethiopia), of whom 42.7% were rural inhabitants and 97.3% were urban.[^cite_note-85] Values for other reported common indicators of the [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living) for Tigray as of 2005 include: 31.6% of the inhabitants fall into the lowest wealth quintile; adult literacy for men is 67.5% and for women 33.7%; and the [infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality_rate) is 67 infant deaths per 1,000 live births, less than the national average of 77; at least half of these deaths occurred in the infants' first month of life.[^cite_note-86]

The predominant religion in Tigray is Orthodox Christianity at 95.6%.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

### Ethnicity



The king of [Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum) refusing a request from pagan Meccans for Muslims [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) had sent there as refugees (1314 artwork).

With 96.6% of the local population, the region is predominantly inhabited by the [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language)-speaking [Tigrayan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrayans). The Tigrinya language belongs to the [Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages) branch of the [Afro-Asiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asiatic_languages) family of languages. Most other residents hail from other [Afro-Asiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asiatic_languages)-speaking communities, including the [Amhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amhara_people), [Irob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irob_people), [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people), [Agaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaw_people) and [Oromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oromo_people). Partly assimilated Oromo live in remoter villages in [Raya Azebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raya_Azebo) and [Alamata (woreda)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamata_\(woreda\)), whereas there are Agaw in [Abergele (woreda)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abergele_\(woreda\)). There are also [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan_languages)-speaking [Kunama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunama_people) as well.

### Terracing and dam construction



[Gestet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestet) forest

An important aspect of the agricultural work in Tigray after the end of the 1991 civil war was to minimize the problems of drought. In the past, Tigray was covered with forests and had a micro-climate that favoured the rains. Subsequently, the forests were cut down, usually to impoverish the population during the wars. Consequently, Tigray achieved a fair amount of rainfall during the rainy season, from August to September, but quickly lost these waters downstream. In the process the fertile soil of the fields eroded. After a few weeks of rain, the country again dried up.



Crop harvesting in [Khunale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khunale)

The government undertook two projects in Tigray. The first was the construction of terraces which, with the agreement and help of local communities, go up to the tops of the mountains at 2,500 metres. The goal was to prevent the rainfall flowing away immediately so that it could be conserved for the agricultural season. On the highest terraces were planted trees, mainly [eucalyptus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus), the dominant tree in Ethiopia and native to [Australia](4689264.md). These plants created a new microclimate.[^cite_note-89] The terracing method was very simple but required good organization. Long stretches of the fields were terraced by the villagers using stone walls from stones that erosion had exposed. The rains eroding the still non-terraced ground formed mudslides that were held by the topmost walls, which permitted construction of a new terrace field and another wall with uncovered stones, creating new ground terraced farmland every year.



[Addi Amharay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addi_Amharay) reservoir

Another endeavour involved the construction of small [reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir) for local irrigation. As rains last only for a couple of months per year, reservoirs of different sizes allow harvesting runoff from the rainy season for further use in the dry season. The dams needed to create these basins are typically an embankment of a few hundreds of meters, closing off one part of a valley, with a maximum height of 20 metres. Each took months of work, in which people carried earth on their back, and with assistance of donkeys. Generally 2,000–3,000 people – men, women and children – carried the earth in simple baskets.

### Vegetation and enclosures



Mountains of Lemalimo near [Inda Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inda_Selassie) in western Tigray

Tigray holds numerous [exclosures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclosure), areas that are set aside for regreening.[^cite_note-93] Logging and livestock grazing are not allowed there. Besides effects on [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity),[^cite_note-ALN2019-72][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] [water infiltration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_infiltration), protection from flooding, [sediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment) deposition,[^cite_note-96] [carbon sequestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration),[^cite_note-97] people commonly have economic benefits from these exclosures through grass harvesting, beekeeping and other [non-timber forest products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-timber_forest_product).[^cite_note-98] The local inhabitants also consider it as "land set aside for future generations".[^cite_note-99] In [Dogu'a Tembien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degua_Tembien), several exclosures are managed by the [EthioTrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EthioTrees) project. They have as an additional benefit that the villagers receive [carbon credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_credit) for the [sequestered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration) CO<sub>2</sub>,[^cite_note-chapter-100] as part of a [carbon offset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_offset) programme.[^cite_note-vivo-101] The revenues are then reinvested in the villages, according to the priorities of the communities;[^cite_note-davines-102] it may be for an additional class in the village school, a water pond, conservation in the exclosures, or a store for [incense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incense).[^cite_note-103]

Raya oxen at ploughing near Mekelle

The CSA estimated in 2005 that farmers in Tigray had a total of 2,713,750 cattle (representing 7.0% of Ethiopia's total cattle), 72,640 sheep (0.4%), 208,970 goats (1.6%), 1,200 horses (less than 0.1%), 9,190 mules (6.2%), 386,600 asses (15.4%), 32,650 camels (7.2%), 3,180,240 poultry of all species (10.3%), and 20,480 beehives (0.5%).[^cite_note-104] Cattle are an essential component in the dominant grain-plough agricultural system. ## Landmarks



Debre Damo monastery.



&#x20;Monk standing in front of the rock-hewn [Abuna Yemata Guh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abuna_Yemata_Guh)'s entrance, situated at a height of 2,580 metres (8,460 ft)

[^cite_note-110]

&#x20;that has to be climbed on foot to reach.



Rock hewn Church interior at [Abuna Yemata Guh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abuna_Yemata_Guh)

A distinctive feature of Tigray are its rock-hewn churches. Similar in design to those of [Lalibela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalibela) in the [Amhara Region](1746709.md), these churches are found in four or five clusters – [Gheralta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheralta?action=edit\&redlink=1), Teka-Tesfay, [Atsbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atsbi) and [Tembien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tembien_Province) – with Wukro sometimes included. Some of the churches are considered earlier than those of Lalibela, perhaps dating from the eighth century. Mostly [monolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolithic_architecture), with designs partly inspired by [classical architecture](52687.md), they are often located at the top of [cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff) or steep hills, for security. For example, Tigray's ancient [Debre Damo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debre_Damo) monastery is accessible only by climbing a rope 25 metres up a sheer cliff.

Looting has become a major issue in the Tigray Region, as archaeological sites have become sources for construction materials and ancient artifacts used for everyday purposes by local populations.[^cite_note-111]

The area is famous for a single rock sculptured 23 meter long obelisk in [Axum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum) as well as for other fallen obelisks. The Axum treasure site of ancient Tigrayan history is a major landmark. [Yeha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeha) is another important local landmark that is little-known outside the region.

## Transport

### Ground travel

A major north–south road corridor goes through Tigray. This is facilitated by Highway 2 which goes from Adigrat to Addis Ababa and Highway 3 which goes from Shire to Addis Ababa.

### Air travel



Alula Aba Nega Airport near to Mekelle.

Tigray has one international airport and four commercial airports. The international airport is [Alula Aba Nega Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alula_Aba_Nega_Airport) (MQX) near Mekelle. The region's four other commercial airports are [Shire Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_Airport) (SHC), [Humera Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humera_Airport) (HUE), [Dansha Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dansha_Airport), and [Emperor Yohannes IV Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum_Airport) (AXU), which serves Axum.
