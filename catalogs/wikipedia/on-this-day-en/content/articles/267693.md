**Flushing** is a neighborhood in the north-central part of the [New York City](645042.md) [borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_\(New_York_City\)) of [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens). The neighborhood is the fourth-largest [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) in New York City.[^cite_note-cleaner-3][^cite_note-4] Downtown Flushing is a major commercial and retail area, with the intersection of [Main Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Street_\(Queens\)) and [Roosevelt Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_Avenue) at its core being the third-busiest in New York City, behind [Times Square](31389.md) and [Herald Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_Square).[^cite_note-5]

Flushing was established as a settlement of [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) on October 10, 1645, on the eastern bank of [Flushing Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_River). It was named **Vlissingen**, after the Dutch city of [Vlissingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlissingen). The English took control of New Amsterdam in 1664, and when Queens County was established in 1683, the **Town of Flushing** was one of the original five towns of Queens. In 1898, Flushing was consolidated into the [City of Greater New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_New_York). Development came in the early 20th century with the construction of bridges and public transportation. A significant immigrant population, composed mostly of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people_in_New_York_City), [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_the_New_York_City_metropolitan_area), and [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_New_York_City), settled in Flushing in the late 20th century and early 21st century, leading to the neighborhood hosting the [original Queens Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatowns_in_Queens#Flushing_Chinatown) and being revered as a cultural melting pot.

Flushing contains numerous residential subsections, and its diversity is reflected by the numerous ethnicities that reside there. Flushing is served by several stations on the [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md)'s [Port Washington Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Washington_Branch), as well as by the [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway)'s [IRT Flushing Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_Flushing_Line) ([7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) and [<7>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7d_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\))​ trains), which has its terminus at [Main Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing%E2%80%93Main_Street_\(IRT_Flushing_Line\)).

Flushing is located in [Queens Community District 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Community_Board_7), and its ZIP Codes are 11354, 11355, and 11358.[^cite_note-NYCPlanning-1] It is patrolled by the [New York City Police Department](255413.md)'s 109th Precinct.

On October 10, 1645, Flushing was established on the eastern bank of [Flushing Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_Creek) under charter of the [Dutch West India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_India_Company) and was part of the [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) colony that was governed from New Amsterdam (Lower Manhattan). The settlement was named Vlissingen, after the city of [Vlissingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlissingen), which was the European base of the Dutch West India company. By 1657, the residents called the place "Vlishing". Eventually, the formal traditional English name for the Dutch town, "Flushing", would be settled upon (despite being a Dutch colony, many of the local early settlers were [British](19097669.md), who trickled down from nearby [Connecticut Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Colony)).[^cite_note-7]

Unlike all other towns in the region, the charter of Flushing allowed residents [freedom of religion](46006.md) as practiced in Holland "without the disturbance of any magistrate or ecclesiastical minister". However, in 1656, New Amsterdam Director-General [Peter Stuyvesant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Stuyvesant) issued an edict prohibiting the harboring of [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker).&#x20;

In response, on December 27, 1657, the inhabitants of Flushing approved a protest known as The [Flushing Remonstrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_Remonstrance). This petition contained religious arguments, even mentioning freedom for "Jews, Turks, and Egyptians," and ended with a forceful declaration that any infringement of the town charter would not be tolerated. Subsequently, a farmer named [John Bowne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bowne) held Quaker meetings in his home and was arrested for this and deported to Holland. Eventually he persuaded the Dutch West India Company to allow Quakers and others to worship freely.[^cite_note-8] As such, Flushing is claimed to be a birthplace of religious freedom in the New World.[^cite_note-nyt-2007-12-27-9]

Landmarks remaining from the Dutch period in Flushing include the [John Bowne House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bowne_House) (c. 1661) on Bowne Street and the [Old Quaker Meeting House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Quaker_Meeting_House_\(Flushing,_New_York\)) (1694) on [Northern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Boulevard). The Remonstrance was signed at a house on the site of the former State Armory, now a police facility, on the south side Northern Boulevard between Linden Place and Union Street.

### English colony

In 1664, the English took control of [New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam), ending Dutch control of the New Netherland colony, and renamed it the [Province of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York). When Queens County was established in 1683, the "Town of Flushing" was one of the original five [towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_\(New_York\)) which comprised the county.[^cite_note-10] Many historical references to Flushing are to this town, bounded from Newtown on the west by Flushing Creek (now [Flushing River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_River)), from [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica,_Queens) on the south by the [watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin), and from [Hempstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hempstead,_New_York) on the east by what later became the Nassau County line. The town was dissolved in 1898 when Queens became a borough of New York City, and the term "Flushing" today usually refers to a much smaller area, for example the former Village of Flushing.



Ash Street, now called Ash Avenue, in the early 20th century

Flushing was a seat of power as the [Province of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York) up to the American Revolution was led by Governor [Cadwallader Colden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadwallader_Colden), based at his Spring Hill estate.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

Flushing was the site of the first commercial [tree nurseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursery_\(horticulture\)) in North America, the most prominent being the [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnaean_Botanic_Garden), Bloodgood, and Parsons nurseries.[^cite_note-NYCPks-KissenaPark-13] A 14-acre (5.7 ha) tract of Parsons's exotic specimens was preserved on the north side of [Kissena Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kissena_Park).[^cite_note-KissenaPk-WildSide-1988-14] The nurseries are also commemorated in the names of west–east avenues that intersect Kissena Boulevard; the streets are named after plants and ordered alphabetically from Ash Avenue in the north to Rose Avenue in the south.[^cite_note-15] Flushing also supplied trees to the *Greensward Project*, now known as [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park) in Manhattan.[^cite_note-16] Well into the 20th century, Flushing contained many horticultural establishments and greenhouses.

During the American Revolution, Flushing, along with most settlements in present-day Queens County, favored the British and quartered British troops, though one battalion of [Scottish Highlanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Highlanders) is known to have been stationed at Flushing during the war. Following the [Battle of Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Long_Island), Zackary Perrine, an officer in the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army), was apprehended near Flushing Bay while on what was probably an intelligence gathering mission and was later hanged.



Flushing in 1882

The 1785 [Kingsland Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsland_Homestead), originally the residence of a wealthy [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Society_of_Friends) merchant, now serves as the home of the [Queens Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Historical_Society).[^cite_note-17]

### 19th century



Map of Flushing in 1891

During the 19th century, as New York City continued to grow in population and economic vitality, so did Flushing. Its proximity to [Manhattan](45470.md) was critical in its transformation into a fashionable [residential area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residential_area). On April 15, 1837, the [Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_\(New_York\)) of Flushing was incorporated within the Town of Flushing.[^cite_note-Flushing-Charter_1837-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-anniversary-20] The official seal was merely the words, "Village of Flushing", surrounded by nondescript flowers. No other emblem or flag is known to have been used. The Village of Flushing included the neighbourhoods of Flushing Highlands, Bowne Park, [Murray Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Hill,_Queens), Ingleside, and Flushing Park.[^cite_note-anniversary-20]

The [Flushing and North Side Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_and_North_Side_Railroad) opened its [Port Washington Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Washington_Branch) to Flushing in 1854, providing access to [Hunters Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunters_Point,_Queens) on the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River) shore.[^cite_note-21] By the mid-1860s, Queens County had 30,429 residents. The [Village of College Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Point,_Queens) was incorporated in 1867,[^cite_note-22] and the [Village of Whitestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitestone,_Queens) was incorporated in 1868. The first free public high school in what is now New York City was established in Flushing in 1875. Flushing, then a small village, established a library in 1858, the oldest in Queens County and only slightly younger than the library of the [City of Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Brooklyn) (built in 1852).

In 1898, although opposed to the proposal, the Town of Flushing (along with two other towns and other land of Queens County) was consolidated into the [City of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_New_York) to form the new [Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_New_York#Borough) of Queens. All towns, villages, and cities within the new borough were dissolved.

Local farmland continued to be subdivided and developed transforming Flushing into a densely populated neighborhood of New York City. A major factor in this was the Halleran real estate agency. From the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) to the end of the 1930s its slogan "Ask Mr. Halleran!" could be seen in ads all over Long Island, and the phrase from its maps "So This Is Flushing" became a catchphrase.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

### Early 20th century development

The continued construction of bridges over the [Flushing River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_River) and the development of other roads increased the volume of vehicular traffic into Flushing. In 1909, the [Queensboro Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensboro_Bridge) over the East River opened, connecting Queens County to [midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan).[^cite_note-25] With the opening of [Pennsylvania Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(1910%E2%80%931963\)) the next year, the Port Washington Branch, now part of the [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md), started running to midtown Manhattan.[^cite_note-26] Broadway, a main roadway through Flushing, was widened and renamed Northern Boulevard.[^cite_note-27] The Roosevelt Avenue Bridge over the Flushing River, which carries four lanes of traffic and the [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway)'s elevated [Flushing Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRT_Flushing_Line) ([7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\)) and [<7>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7d_\(New_York_City_Subway_service\))​ trains), was the largest trunnion bascule bridge in the world when it was completed in 1927.[^cite_note-Suriel_2016-28][^cite_note-Roosevelt_Ave_Bridge-29] The next year, the [Main Street terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing%E2%80%93Main_Street_station_\(IRT_Flushing_Line\)) of the Flushing subway line opened in downtown Flushing, giving the neighborhood direct subway access.[^cite_note-30]

Flushing was a forerunner of [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles), when the young American film industry was still based on the [U.S. East Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._East_Coast) and [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essanay_Studios). Decades later, the [RKO Keith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RKO_Keith's_Theater_\(Flushing,_Queens\))'s movie palace would host [vaudeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville) acts and appearances by the likes of [Mickey Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Rooney), the [Marx Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marx_Brothers) and [Bob Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hope).

### Asian immigration



[Flushing Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Flushing)

In the 1970s, immigrants from Taiwan established a foothold in Flushing, whose demographic constituency had been predominantly non-Hispanic white, interspersed with a small Japanese community. Additionally, a large South Korean population also called Flushing home. The Taiwanese immigrants were the first wave of Chinese-speaking immigrants who spoke Mandarin (Taiwanese also spoken) rather than Cantonese to arrive in New York City. Many Taiwanese immigrants were additionally [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien) and had relatives or connections to [Fujian](88585.md) province in China, which led to large influxes of [Fuzhounese Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhounese_Americans).

Over the years, many recent non-Cantonese ethnic Chinese immigrants from different regions and [provinces of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) have settled in Flushing having heard of it through word of mouth. This wave of immigrants spoke Mandarin and various regional/provincial dialects. The early 1990s and 2000s brought a wave of [Fuzhounese Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhounese_Americans) and [Wenzhounese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzhounese) immigrants, who mostly spoke Mandarin, and who settled in Flushing as well as [Elmhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmhurst,_Queens). Flushing's Chinese population became diverse over the next few decades as people from different provinces started to arrive.[^cite_note-Foner-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Due to loosened emigration restrictions in mainland China, there has been a growing [Northern Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_China) population in Flushing. The regional food cuisines have led to Flushing being considered the "food mecca" for [Chinese regional cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_regional_cuisine) outside of Asia.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-villagevoice.com-36]

### 21st century transformation

Main article: [Downtown Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Flushing)

In the 21st century, Flushing has cemented its status as an international "[melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot)", predominantly attracting immigrants from Asia, particularly from throughout the various [provinces of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China), but including newcomers from all over the world. [Flushing Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_Chinatown) is centered around [Main Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Street_\(Queens\)) and the area to its west, most prominently along [Roosevelt Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_Avenue), which have become the primary nexus of Flushing Chinatown. However, Chinatown continues to expand southeastward along [Kissena Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kissena_Boulevard) and northward beyond [Northern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Boulevard). The Flushing Chinatown houses over 30,000 individuals born in China alone, the largest Chinatown by this metric outside Asia and one of the largest and fastest-growing Chinatowns in the world.[^cite_note-FlushingChinatownWorldLargest-37] In January 2019, the *[New York Post](102227.md)* named Flushing as New York City's "most dynamic outer-borough neighborhood".[^cite_note-38] Flushing is undergoing rapid [gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification) by Chinese transnational entities.[^cite_note-39]

#### Streetscape



The busy intersection of [Main Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Street_\(Queens\)) and [Roosevelt Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_Avenue) in the [Flushing Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_Chinatown) (法拉盛華埠), [Downtown Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Flushing), the third-busiest [pedestrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian) intersection in New York City. Housing over 30,000 individuals born in [China](5405.md) alone, the largest by this metric outside [Asia](689.md), Flushing has become home to the largest and one of the fastest-growing Chinatowns in the world, known as the "Chinese [Times Square](31389.md)" or the "Chinese [Manhattan](45470.md)".

## Demographics

Based on data from the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), the population of Flushing was 72,008, an increase of 2,646 (3.8%) from the 69,362 counted in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census). Covering an area of 853.06 acres (345.22 ha), the neighborhood had a population density of 84.4 inhabitants per acre (54,000/sq mi; 20,900/km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-PLP5-2]

The racial makeup of the neighborhood was 69.2% (49,830) [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._census\)), 9.5% (6,831) [white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._census\)), 4.2% (3,016) [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._census\)), 0.1% (74) [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 0.1% (59) [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_\(U.S._census\)), 0.2% (172) from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_census\)), and 1.8% (1,303) from two or more races. [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._census\)) of any race were 14.9% (10,723) of the population.[^cite_note-PLP3A-42]

The entirety of Community Board 7, which comprises Flushing, College Point, and Whitestone, had 263,039 inhabitants as of [NYC Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Health_and_Mental_Hygiene)'s 2018 Community Health Profile, with an average life expectancy of 84.3 years.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 2, 20 </sup> This is longer than the median life expectancy of 81.2 for all New York City neighborhoods.[^cite_note-\:21-44]<sup>: 53 (PDF p. 84) </sup>[^cite_note-45] Most inhabitants are middle-aged and elderly: 22% are between the ages of between 25 and 44, 30% between 45 and 64, and 18% over 65. The ratio of youth and college-aged residents was lower, at 17% and 7%, respectively.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 2 </sup>

As of 2017, the median [household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_income) in Community Board 7 was \$51,284.[^cite_note-CB6PUMA-46] In 2018, an estimated 25% of Flushing and Whitestone residents lived in poverty, compared to 19% in all of Queens and 20% in all of New York City. One in seventeen residents (6%) were unemployed, compared to 8% in Queens and 9% in New York City. Rent burden, or the percentage of residents who have difficulty paying their rent, is 57% in Flushing and Whitestone, higher than the boroughwide and citywide rates of 53% and 51% respectively. Based on this calculation, as of 2018, Flushing and Whitestone are considered to be high-income relative to the rest of the city and not [gentrifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification).[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 7 </sup>

Flushing Chinatown, or Mandarin Town[^cite_note-47] is the world's largest and one of the fastest-growing Chinatowns, known as the "Chinese [Times Square](31389.md)" or the "Chinese [Manhattan](45470.md)".[^cite_note-FlushingPrimaryChineseDestination-48][^cite_note-Haller-40][^cite_note-DiverseCuisineFlushingChinatownTimesSquare-41] In [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese), Flushing is known as "Falasheng" ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 法拉盛; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Fǎlāshèng*).[^cite_note-49] The Chinatown of Flushing is centered around the intersection of Main Street and Roosevelt Avenue, and many of the area's Chinese businesses are located on the blocks around, or west of, Main Street.[^cite_note-FlushingChinatownWorldLargest-37] However, Chinatown continues to expand southeastward along Kissena Boulevard and northward beyond [Northern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Boulevard).

&#x20;In the 1970s, a Chinese community established a foothold in the neighborhood of Flushing, whose demographic constituency had been predominantly non-Hispanic white. [Taiwanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_people) began the surge of immigration, followed by other groups of Chinese.[^cite_note-Foner-31] A 1986 estimate by the Flushing Chinese Business Association approximated 60,000 Chinese in Flushing alone.[^cite_note-50] By 1990, Asians constituted 41% of the population of the core area of Flushing, with Chinese in turn representing 41% of the Asian population.[^cite_note-Foner-31] However, ethnic Chinese are constituting an increasingly dominant proportion of the Asian population as well as of the overall population in Flushing and its Chinatown. High rates of both legal[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] and illegal[^cite_note-53] immigration from [Mainland China](55079.md) continue to spur the ongoing rise of the ethnic Chinese population in Flushing. According to a *[Daily News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_News_\(New_York\))* article in 2011, Flushing's Chinatown ranked as New York City's second largest Chinese community with 33,526 Chinese, surpassed only by the [Brooklyn Chinatown (布鲁克林華埠)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Brooklyn), and larger than Manhattan's Chinatown.[^cite_note-54] The growth of the business activity at the core of Downtown Flushing, dominated by the Flushing Chinatown, has continued to flourish despite the Covid-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-FlushingChinatownContinuesGrowth-55]



Street vendor selling fruit under the [Flushing–Main Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing%E2%80%93Main_Street_\(LIRR_station\)) LIRR station

Flushing now rivals Manhattan's Chinatown as a center of [Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] The [Lunar New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_New_Year) Parade has become a growing annual celebration of [Chinese New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_New_Year). In addition, several [Chinese supermarkets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_supermarket) such as [Hong Kong Supermarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Supermarket) and New York Supermarket have locations in Flushing.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] The *[World Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Journal)*, one of the largest Chinese-language newspapers outside China, is headquartered in adjacent [Whitestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitestone,_Queens).[^cite_note-WJHQ-61] Numerous other Chinese- and English-language publications are available in Flushing, including [SinoVision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SinoVision), one of North America's largest Chinese language television networks.

Many styles of [Chinese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine) are accessible in Flushing,[^cite_note-62] including [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_cuisine), [Taiwanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_cuisine), [Shanghainese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_cuisine), [Hunanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_cuisine), [Sichuanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_cuisine), [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_cuisine), [Fujianese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujianese_cuisine), [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_cuisine), [Zhejiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_cuisine), and [Korean Chinese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Chinese_cuisine). Even obscure styles of cuisine indigenous to [Northeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_China), such as [Dongbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Chinese_cuisine), is available in Flushing,[^cite_note-63] as well as [Mongolian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_cuisine) and [Uyghur cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_cuisine).[^cite_note-DiverseCuisineFlushingChinatownTimesSquare-41] Varieties of Chinese spoken in Flushing include [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese),[^cite_note-64] [Fuzhou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_dialect), [Min Nan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien) ([Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien)), [Wu Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Chinese) ([Wenzhounese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzhounese), [Shanghainese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghainese), [Suzhou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou_dialect), [Hangzhou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou_dialect)), and [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese). Additionally, the [Mongolian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language) is emerging in the neighborhood. Due to its rapid growth, the Flushing Chinatown has surpassed the original New York City Chinatown in the borough of Manhattan in size and population.[^cite_note-FlushingChinatownWorldLargest-37]

In accompaniment with its rapid growth, Flushing in particular has witnessed the proliferation of highly competitive businesses touted as educational centers as well as non-profit organizations declaring the intent to educate the community. Some entities offer education in [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese), the most spoken Chinese variety in mainland China.[^cite_note-65] A diverse array of social services geared toward assisting recent as well as established [Chinese immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_immigrants) is readily available in Flushing.[^cite_note-66] As of the 2020s, about 3/4 of the Asian population in the area are of Chinese descent making them the majority of the Asian population.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

### &#x20;Korean community



Koreatown

Main article: [Koreatown, Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreatown,_Flushing)

There is a Koreatown that originated in Flushing, but has since spread eastward to [Murray Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Hill,_Queens), [Bayside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayside,_Queens), [Douglaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglaston,_Queens), and [Little Neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Neck,_Queens) in Queens, and also into [Nassau County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_County,_New_York). The Koreatown has historically been centered around Union Street, with the later growth being concentrated around [Northern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Boulevard) east of Union Street.[^cite_note-Meokjagolmok-70][^cite_note-http-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-NYTs_2003_Mar_23-74] As of the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), the Korean population of Queens was 64,107.[^cite_note-Queens_County,_New_York_QuickLinks-75]

In the 1980s, a continuous stream of [Korean immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_immigrants) emerged into Flushing, many of whom began as workers in the [medical field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine) or Korean [international students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_student) who had moved to New York City to find or initiate [professional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional) or [entrepreneurial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrepreneurial) positions.[^cite_note-http-71] They established a foothold on Union Street in Flushing between 35th and 41st Avenues,[^cite_note-http-71] featuring restaurants and [karaoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaoke) (*[noraebang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaoke)*) bars, grocery markets, education centers and [bookstores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookstores), banking institutions, offices, [consumer electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_electronics) vendors, [apparel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparel) [boutiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boutique), and other commercial enterprises.[^cite_note-Meokjagolmok-70] As the community grew in wealth and population and rose in [socioeconomic status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomic_status), Koreans expanded their presence eastward along Northern Boulevard, buying homes in more [affluent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affluence) and less dense neighborhoods in Queens and Nassau County.[^cite_note-Meokjagolmok-70][^cite_note-NYTs_2003_Mar_23-74] This expansion has led to the creation of an American *Meokjagolmok*, or Korean *Restaurant Street*, around the [Murray Hill station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Hill_station_\(LIRR\)).[^cite_note-Meokjagolmok-70] The eastward pressure to expand was also created by the inability to move westward due to the Flushing Chinatown on Main Street.[^cite_note-http-71] Per the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), the Korean population of Queens was 64,107,[^cite_note-Queens_County,_New_York_QuickLinks-75] representing the largest municipality in the United States with a density of at least 500 Korean Americans [per square mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._cities_with_significant_Korean-American_populations#Municipalities_with_density_of_at_least_500_Korean_Americans_per_square_mile_in_2010).[^cite_note-76] The [Korean American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_American) population, consisting of 218,764 individuals in the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area),[^cite_note-77] is the second largest population of ethnic Koreans outside Korea.

*[The Korea Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Korea_Times)*, a news organization based in Seoul, carries a significant presence in the Long Island Koreatown. The Long Island Koreatown features numerous restaurants that serve both traditional and/or regional Korean cuisine. [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language) is spoken frequently alongside English and Chinese varieties, and [retail signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signage) employing the [Hangul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul) alphabet are ubiquitous. A significant array of social services toward assisting recent and established Korean immigrants is available in Koreatown. There is also a significant population of Korean-Chinese or Chinese-Koreans in Flushing who can speak Mandarin, Korean, and English.[^cite_note-78]

### Other ethnic communities

The neighborhood of East Flushing, technically within Greater Flushing, also houses a substantial Chinese community along with most of [Downtown Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Flushing). However, East Flushing also substantially includes [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_American), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_American), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_American), [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_American), [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American), and [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_American) communities, as well as communities of [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_American), [Sri Lankans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_American), [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_American), [Malaysians](3607937.md), and [Hispanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans), mostly [Colombians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_American) and [Salvadorans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_American). This neighborhood tends to be more diverse visibly than Downtown Flushing because of the more even distribution of the ethnicities of East Flushing residents resulting in more ethnic businesses catering to each community rather than the dominance of Chinese and to a lesser extent Korean businesses in Downtown Flushing.



[Sri Maha Vallabha Ganapati Devasthanam Hindu Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Temple_Society_of_North_America)

The northeastern section of Flushing near Bayside continues to maintain large Italian and Greek presences that are reflected in its many Italian and Greek bakeries, grocery stores and restaurants. The northwest is a mix of [Jews](1131183.md), [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_American), and [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_American). Most of central Flushing is an ethnic mix of [Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people), [Hispanic Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans), and [Asian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_American).



[Sikh Center](27964.md) in Flushing

An area south of Franklin Avenue houses a concentration of [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_American), [Pakistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_American), [Afghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_American), and [Bangladeshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_American) markets. This concentration of Indian American and other [South Asian American](11353572.md) businesses south of Franklin Avenue has existed since the late 1970s, one of the oldest [Little India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_India_\(location\)) neighborhoods in North America. The [Sri Maha Vallabha Ganapati Devasthanam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Temple_Society_of_North_America) ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit): श्री महावल्लभ गणपति देवस्थानम्, [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language): ஸ்ரீ மகா வல்லப கணபதி தேவஸ்தானம்) at 45–57 Bowne Street in Flushing was the very first of the traditional [Hindu temple](2247213.md) organized in North America.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] However, Indians are migrating eastward into neighborhoods in northeastern Queens and into Nassau County, as with many Chinese and Korean immigrants.

## Subsections

### Broadway–Flushing

[Broadway–Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway%E2%80%93Flushing,_Queens), also known as North Flushing, is a [residential area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residential_area) with many large homes. The name refers to the area served by the ["Broadway" station of the Long Island Rail Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_station_\(LIRR\)). The Broadway station is located immediately east of the location where the LIRR's Port Washington Branch crosses Northern Boulevard, which when the station was opened in 1866 was called "Broadway". Part of this area has been designated a State and Federal historic district due to the elegant, park-like character of the neighborhood. Much of the area has been rezoned by the City of New York to preserve the low density, residential quality of the neighborhood. Broadway-Flushing is approximately bounded by 29th Avenue to the north, Northern Boulevard and Crocheron Avenue to the south, 155th Street to the west, and 172nd Streets to the east.

### Linden Hill

"Linden Hill, Queens" redirects here; not to be confused with [Linden Hill, Ridgewood, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linden_Hill,_Ridgewood,_Queens).

Linden Hill is bound by 25th Avenue to Willets Point Boulevard to the north, 154th Street to the east, [Northern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Boulevard) to the south and the [Whitestone Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitestone_Expressway) to the west.[^cite_note-81]

Linden Hill was originally a rural estate owned by the Mitchell family. Ernest Mitchell owned an adjacent area to the west known as Breezy Hill and his father owned the area now called Linden Hill.[^cite_note-queenslibrary.org-82] The two areas are sometimes referred to as the Mitchell-Linden neighborhood. A major change in the rural nature of Linden Hill occurred in the early 1950s. Neisloss Brothers with architect Benjamin Braunstein envisioned a [cooperative project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_cooperative) to be set on Linden Hill and landfill on the adjacent swamp to the west which would provide middle-income housing to veterans of [World War II](32927.md) and the [Korean War](16772.md).[^cite_note-queenslibrary.org-82] The construction was carried out under Section 213 of the Federal Housing Act of 1950 which provided mortgage insurance for non-subsidized projects.[^cite_note-83] Gerace and Castagna with architects Samuel Paul and Seymour Jarmul subsequently developed the larger Linden Towers several years after this. Paul was additionally the architect of Embassy Arms. In total, 41 six-story buildings containing 3,146 apartments comprising the Linden Hill, Mitchell Gardens, Linden Towers, and Embassy Arms cooperatives were erected.

Once a primarily European-American, largely Jewish, neighborhood, Linden Hill is now predominantly Chinese-American.

### Murray Hill

[Map](267693.md)

Map of Murray Hill (including [Broadway–Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway%E2%80%93Flushing,_Queens))

Murray Hill is bounded by 150th Street to the west and 160th Street to the east and straddles ZIP Codes 11354, 11355, and 11358.[^cite_note-NYTs_2003_Mar_23-74] Traditionally the home of families of [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American) and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_American) immigrants, many [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_American) and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_American) immigrants have moved into Murray Hill in recent years.[^cite_note-Murray_Hill_NYC.gov_2023-84] Murray Hill within Flushing is often confused with the larger [Murray Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Hill,_Manhattan) neighborhood on the East Side of [Manhattan](45470.md).[^cite_note-NYTs_2003_Mar_23-74][^cite_note-85] The [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md)'s [Murray Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Hill_station_\(LIRR\)) and [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_station_\(LIRR\)) stations serve the area.

Before the area was developed for residential housing in 1889, Murray Hill was the location of several large [nurseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_nursery) owned by the King, Murray, and Parsons families.[^cite_note-86] The [Kingsland Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsland_Homestead) has been preserved as the home of the [Queens Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Historical_Society).[^cite_note-NYTs_2003_Mar_23-74] The [Voelker Orth Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voelker_Orth_Museum), Bird Sanctuary and Victorian Garden is also located in Murray Hill.[^cite_note-87] [Comic strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_strip) artist [Richard F. Outcault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_F._Outcault), the creator of *[The Yellow Kid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Yellow_Kid)* and *[Buster Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buster_Brown)*, lived on 147th Street in Murray Hill.[^cite_note-88]

### Pomonok



Electchester Union Building – Local 3

**Pomonok** is a neighborhood in South Flushing. This large public housing development[^cite_note-89] was built in 1949 on the former site of [Pomonok Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomonok_Country_Club). The name comes from an [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages) word for [eastern Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Long_Island), and means either "land of tribute" or "land where there is travelling by water". In Pomonok, there is also **Electchester**, a [cooperative housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_housing) complex at Jewel Avenue and Parsons Boulevard in Pomonok, which was established by [Harry Van Arsdale Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Van_Arsdale_Jr.) and Local 3 of the [International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Brotherhood_of_Electrical_Workers) in 1949, when Van Arsdale worked with the Joint Industry Board of the Electrical Industry to purchase 103 acres (0.42 km<sup>2</sup>) of the former Pomonok Country Club and build apartment buildings. 5,550 people live in about 2,500 units in 38 buildings, many of which are six-story brick structures. It is served by Public School 200, which is on land donated by Electchester. The union provided the majority of the mortgage. New York state offered tax abatements. Electchester was classified as a "limited dividend nonprofit", subject to state regulations. The first families paid \$475 per room for equity shares, and carrying charges of \$26 per month per room, on apartments ranging from three-and-a-half to five-and-a-half rooms.[^cite_note-90]

Both housing complexes are patrolled by the N.Y.P.D.'s 107th Precinct. There is also an N.Y.P.D. P.S.A.-9 Housing Police Unit station located in the Pomonok Houses. Pomonok is part of [Queens Community District 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Community_Board_8).[^cite_note-91]

### Queensboro Hill

Queensboro Hill in southern Flushing is bordered to the west by College Point Boulevard, to the north by [Kissena Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kissena_Park) and Kissena Corridor Park, to the south by Reeves Avenue and the [Long Island Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Expressway), and to the east by [Kissena Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kissena_Boulevard). Queensboro Hill is a part of ZIP Codes 11355 and 11367 and contains the [NewYork–Presbyterian/Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NewYork%E2%80%93Presbyterian/Queens) hospital. One of the leading churches is the Queensboro Hill Community Church, a multi-racial congregation of the Reformed Church in America. [Turtle Playground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle_Playground_\(Queens\)) serves the residents of this section of Flushing. This area is often referred to as South Flushing.

### Waldheim



Map of Waldheim, early 20th century

The Waldheim neighborhood, an [estate subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivision_\(land\)) in Flushing constructed primarily between 1875 and 1925, is bound by Sanford and Franklin Avenues on the north, 45th Avenue on the south, Bowne Street on the west and Parsons Boulevard on the east. The area is immediately southeast of the downtown Flushing commercial core, and adjacent to Kissena Park. a small district of upscale "in-town" suburban architecture. Waldheim, German for "home in the woods", is known for its large homes of varying architectural styles and is laid out in an unusual street pattern.[^cite_note-92]

Waldheim was the home of some of Flushing's wealthiest residents until the 1960s. Notable residents include the Helmann family of condiment fame, the [Steinway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinway_&_Sons) piano-making family, as well as A. Douglas Nash, who managed a nearby [Tiffany glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_glass) plant. Starting in the 1980s, homes in Waldheim were destroyed by the Korean American Presbyterian Church of Queens, one of the area's largest land owners.[^cite_note-93] In 2008, the city rezoned the neighborhood to help preserve the low-density, residential character of the neighborhood. As with the Broadway neighborhood, preservationists have been unable to secure designation as an Historic District by the [NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYC_Landmarks_Preservation_Commission), and as of 2017, structures in Waldheim were still being torn down.[^cite_note-94]

### Houses of worship



[Free Synagogue of Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Synagogue_of_Flushing), located at 41–60 Kissena Boulevard, near Sanford Avenue



Pure Presbyterian Church, located at 142–08 32nd Avenue, near Union Street

Flushing is a religiously diverse community. Houses of worship in Flushing include the Dutch colonial epoch Quaker Meeting House, the historic Unitarian Universalist Congregation of Queens, St. Andrew Avellino Roman Catholic Church, St. George's Episcopal Church, the [Free Synagogue of Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Synagogue_of_Flushing), the [Congregation of Georgian Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_of_Georgian_Jews), St. Mel Roman Catholic Church, St. Michael's Catholic Church, St. Nicholas [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox) Shrine Church, Holy Annunciation Russian Orthodox Church, St. John's Lutheran Church, Queensboro Hill Community Church, Hindu Temple Society of North America, and the Muslim Center of New York.[^cite_note-95]

There are more than 200 houses of worship in Flushing.[^cite_note-96]

In 1657, while Flushing was still a Dutch settlement, a document known as the [Flushing Remonstrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_Remonstrance) was created by Edward Hart, the town clerk, where some thirty ordinary citizens protested a ban imposed by Peter Stuyvesant, the director general of New Amsterdam, forbidding the harboring of Quakers. The [Remonstrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remonstrants) cited the Flushing Town charter of 1645, which promised liberty of conscience.[^cite_note-nyt-2007-12-27-9]

#### Flushing Meadows-Corona Park

[Flushing Meadows–Corona Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_Meadows%E2%80%93Corona_Park), an 897-acre (3.63 km<sup>2</sup>) park, is the largest park in Queens.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] The site hosted two World's Fairs, in [1939–1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_New_York_World's_Fair) and [1964–1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_New_York_World's_Fair), and the park infrastructure reflects the construction undertaken for the Fairs.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] The northern part of the park contains [Citi Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citi_Field), home of the [New York Mets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mets) of [Major League Baseball](38776.md); the field, opened in 2009, replaced the former [Shea Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shea_Stadium).[^cite_note-119] To the south is the [USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USTA_Billie_Jean_King_National_Tennis_Center) which is the home of the [US Tennis Open](82729.md).[^cite_note-120]

Several attractions were originally developed for the World's Fairs in Flushing Meadows-Corona Park. One of the most prominent is the [Unisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unisphere), the iconic 12-story-high stainless steel globe that served as the centerpiece for the 1964 New York World's Fair, which was made a city landmark.[^cite_note-121] Additionally, there is a stone marker for the two 5,000-year [Westinghouse Time Capsules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Time_Capsules) made of special [alloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy) buried in the park, chronicling 20th-century life in the United States, dedicated both in 1938 and 1965. Also in the park are the [Queens Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Museum_of_Art) which features a scale model of the City of New York, the largest architectural model ever built; [Queens Theatre in the Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Theatre_in_the_Park); the [New York Hall of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Hall_of_Science); and the [Queens Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Zoo).[^cite_note-122] The [New York State Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Pavilion) was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2009.[^cite_note-nris-111]

## Police and crime

Flushing, College Point, and Whitestone are patrolled by the 109th Precinct of the [NYPD](255413.md), located at 37-05 Union Street.[^cite_note-133] The 109th Precinct ranked 9th safest out of 69 patrol areas for per-capita crime in 2010.[^cite_note-134] As of 2018, with a non-fatal assault rate of 17 per 100,000 people, Flushing and Whitestone's rate of [violent crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_crime) per capita is less than that of the city as a whole. The incarceration rate of 145 per 100,000 people is lower than that of the city as a whole.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 8 </sup>

The 109th Precinct has a lower crime rate than in the 1990s, with crimes across all categories having decreased by 83.7% between 1990 and 2018. The precinct reported 6 murders, 30 rapes, 202 robberies, 219 felony assaults, 324 burglaries, 970 grand larcenies, and 126 grand larcenies auto in 2018.[^cite_note-135]

## Health

As of 2018, [preterm births](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm_birth) and births to teenage mothers are less common in Flushing and Whitestone than in other places citywide. In Flushing and Whitestone, there were 63 preterm births per 1,000 live births (compared to 87 per 1,000 citywide), and 8 births to teenage mothers per 1,000 live births (compared to 19.3 per 1,000 citywide).[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 11 </sup> Flushing and Whitestone have a higher than average population of residents who are [uninsured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance_coverage_in_the_United_States). In 2018, this population of uninsured residents was estimated to be 14%, slightly higher than the citywide rate of 12%.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 14 </sup>

The concentration of [fine particulate matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulates), the deadliest type of [air pollutant](10934212.md), in Flushing and Whitestone is 0.0073 milligrams per cubic metre (7.3×10<sup>−9</sup> oz/ft<sup>3</sup>), less than the city average.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 9 </sup> Thirteen percent of Flushing and Whitestone residents are [smokers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking), which is lower than the city average of 14% of residents being smokers.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 13 </sup> In Flushing and Whitestone, 13% of residents are [obese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity), 8% are [diabetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus), and 22% have [high blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension)—compared to the citywide averages of 22%, 8%, and 23% respectively.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 16 </sup> In addition, 15% of children are obese, compared to the citywide average of 20%.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 12 </sup>

Ninety-five percent of residents eat some fruits and vegetables every day, which is higher than the city's average of 87%. In 2018, 71% of residents described their health as "good", "very good", or "excellent", lower than the city's average of 78%.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 13 </sup> For every supermarket in Flushing and Whitestone, there are 6 [bodegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convenience_store).[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 10 </sup>

The nearest major hospitals are [NewYork–Presbyterian/Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NewYork%E2%80%93Presbyterian/Queens) and [Flushing Hospital Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_Hospital_Medical_Center).[^cite_note-140] NewYork–Presbyterian/Queens serves Flushing as well as surrounding communities with comprehensive medical care services.[^cite_note-141] Numerous [tertiary medical clinics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care#Tertiary_care) also serve the residents of Flushing.

## Education

Flushing and Whitestone generally have a similar rate of college-educated residents to the rest of the city as of 2018. While 37% of residents age 25 and older have a college education or higher, 23% have less than a high school education and 40% are high school graduates or have some college education. By contrast, 39% of Queens residents and 43% of city residents have a college education or higher.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 6 </sup> The percentage of Flushing and Whitestone students excelling in math rose from 55% in 2000 to 78% in 2011, and reading achievement rose from 57% to 59% during the same time period.[^cite_note-\:17-147]

Flushing and Whitestone's rate of elementary school student absenteeism is less than the rest of New York City. In Flushing and Whitestone, 9% of elementary school students missed twenty or more days per [school year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_year), lower than the citywide average of 20%.[^cite_note-\:21-44]<sup>: 24 (PDF p. 55) </sup>[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 6 </sup> Additionally, 86% of high school students in Flushing and Whitestone graduate on time, more than the citywide average of 75%.[^cite_note-CHP2018-43]<sup>: 6 </sup>

### Roads

Major highways that serve the area include the [Van Wyck Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Wyck_Expressway) and [Whitestone Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitestone_Expressway) ([Interstate 678](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_678)), [Grand Central Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Parkway), and [Long Island Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Expressway) ([Interstate 495](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_495_\(New_York\))). [Northern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Boulevard) (part of [New York State Route 25A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Route_25A)) extends from the [Queensboro Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensboro_Bridge) in [Long Island City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_City) through Flushing into [Nassau County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_County,_New_York). The Roosevelt Avenue Bridge over Flushing Creek was the largest fixed trunnion bascule type in the world when opened in 1927. However, it was decommissioned as a moving bridge when marine navigation was eliminated in the late 1930s.[^cite_note-181]

## Political representation

The political stature of Flushing appears to be increasing significantly, with many Chinese from Flushing becoming New York City Council members. [Taiwan](25734.md)-born [John Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Liu), former [New York City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council) member representing District 20, which includes Flushing and other northern Queens neighborhoods, was elected [New York City Comptroller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Comptroller) in November 2009. In 2018, Liu defeated incumbent [Tony Avella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Avella) to become the first of two Asian Americans in the [New York State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Senate).

At the same time, [Shanghai](27643.md)-born [Peter Koo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Koo) was elected to succeed Liu to assume this council membership seat. Additionally, in 2012 Flushing resident [Grace Meng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Meng), a [State Assembly Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Assembly), was elected to [Congress](31756.md) as the first Asian-American member of the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md) from the eastern United States.
