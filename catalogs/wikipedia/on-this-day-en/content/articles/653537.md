**Sulaymaniyah** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): السليمانية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *as-Sulaymāniyyah*[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]) or **Slemani** ([Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language): سلێمانی, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Kurdish): *Silêmanî*[^cite_note-6]), is a city in the east of the [Kurdistan Region](3949386.md) of [Iraq](7515928.md) and is the capital of the [Sulaymaniyah Governorate](752619.md). It is surrounded by the Azmar, Goizha and Qaiwan Mountains in the northeast, Baranan Mountain in the south and the Tasluja Hills in the west.

The modern city of Slemani was founded in 1784[^cite_note-\:0-7] by the Ottoman-Kurdish prince Ibrahim Pasha Baban in Collaboration with Azim Beg and Haji Aziz Bey Aghal Dwanze Swarey Meriwane Jawamer Agha Rangena, who named it after his father Sulayman Pasha.[^cite_note-8] Slemani was the capital of the historic [Kurdish](17068.md) principality of [Baban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baban) from 1784 to 1850.

## History

Further information: [Timeline of Sulaymaniyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Sulaymaniyah)

It dates back to the [Old Babylonian Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Babylonian_Period). From [Qarachatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qarachatan) Village, [Slemani Governorate](752619.md), [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md). Located in the [Slemani Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaymaniyah_Museum), Iraq.

The region of Slemani was known as *[Zamwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamua)* prior to the foundation of the modern city in 1784. The capital of the [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) [Baban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baban) principality (1649–1850), before Slemani, was a territory named "Qelaçiwalan". At the time of the Babani's rule there were major conflicts between the [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) and the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). Qelaçiwalan became a battleground for the two rivals.[^cite_note-sulygov.com-9]

In 1783, [Ibrahim Baban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Baban?action=edit\&redlink=1) became ruler of the emirate and began the reconstruction of a city which once constructed by [Ottoman Sultan Sulayman](22278.md) (the name of Sulaymaniyah came from his name) new city which would become its capital. In 1784 he finished erecting a number of palaces for trade called *Qeyserîs* and bazaars, which were also used as baths, and began inviting people from the surrounding villages and emirates to move to the newly established city. Soon Melkendî, which was originally intended to be the city itself, instead became one of its quarters.[^cite_note-sulygov.com-9] The new city of Slemani was named after [Sulayman Baba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulayman_Baba?action=edit\&redlink=1), who was the first [Baban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baban) prince to gain control of the province of [Şarezûr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrizor). Sulayman Baban invaded the neighboring Kurdish vassaldom of [Ardalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardalan), defeating their forces in 1694. Ottoman Sultan [Mustafa II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_II) assigned him the district of Baban.[^cite_note-10]

Haji Aziz Bey Aghal Dwanze Swarey Meriwane Jawamer Agha Rangena is not to be confused with the [royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_family) and [aristocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy) [Jwamer Agha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jwamer_Aga), although they share family ties through marriage. He was the [billionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billionaire) [Bey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bey) of the noble and aristocratic Kurdish Noori-Aghal family, who founded the city of Slemani in collaboration with [Ibrahim Pasha Baban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Pasha_Baban) and Azim Beg. Ibrahim Pasha Baban invited Haji to help build Slemani, and together, they founded the city of Sarkarez.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-11]

Haji Aziz Bey Aghal was the first individual to establish a cemetery in Slemani, using his land, which had originally been designated for [sumac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumac) cultivation. Aghal asserted that he had no intention of generating profit, stating that his personal wealth was sufficient to support the impoverished. He further demonstrated his altruistic nature by opening the doors of his [palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace), allowing the poor to take whatever they needed. He provided them with [nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_\(food\)), [chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), and other food items that were typically consumed by the aristocracy of his era.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-11]

Additionally, he constructed the first bridge in Slemani, testing its stability by riding his [horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare) across it. He expressed indifference to his own safety, stating that if he were to perish due to the bridge's collapse, it would serve as a testament to its inadequacy and raise awareness among the citizens. He also built the first [mosque](19894.md) in Slemani, which is currently in poor condition, but has "Aghal" written on its walls.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-11]

The house was later sold and converted into a car park by the buyer, a decision that drew criticism from many older Kurds who had known him and disapproved of the change. Despite his death, Haji Aziz Bey Aghal is still highly respected as a nobleman. Many peasants offered to convert to his religion for his actions, but he refused, stating that he did not need any recognition and was content with everything he had. He was also offered royal titles, but he turned them down.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-11]

His wife, the noblewoman Princess Fatima [Khanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanum), sold all her [gold](12240.md) including her belongings to support her husband in saving hundreds of thousands of peasants with his wealth during the [First World War](4764461.md). For their acts of charity, they are buried together in a sacred private grave on the sumac farm.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-11]

According to Jewish tradition, Sulaiman Baban emphasized the importance of a town having Jewish residents for its completeness. A mission was then sent to [Qaradagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaradagh_District), a town with a thriving Jewish community, and surrounding villages. The Jews of Qaradagh responded by sending the first group of Jewish inhabitants to Sulaymaniyah.[^cite_note-\:2-12]

In the early 1800s refugees from Ardalan moved to Slemani, including [Mastura Ardalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastoureh_Ardalan), the widow of Xosraw Xanî Erdalan, the ruler of the kingdom. Erdalan wrote an account of Kurdish history in Persian and was buried in Slemani when he died in 1848.[^cite_note-13]

From 1922 to 1924, Slemani was the capital of the [Kingdom of Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kurdistan), a short-lived [unrecognized state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_historical_unrecognized_states_and_dependencies) declared by [Iraqi Kurds](35423251.md) following the collapse of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).[^cite_note-14]

## Demographics

The early 1920s [Iraqi Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Revolt) against the British rule of the [Mandate for Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Mesopotamia) led by Shaikh Mahmud triggered a wave of Jewish emigration from Sulaymaniyah.[^cite_note-\:2-12]

According to Iraqi government documents, by 1947 the number of residents had increased to 23,475.[^cite_note-\:3-15] In the same year, records documented the existence of three hundred Jewish families, comprising a total of 1,517 individuals. Notably, nearly the entire Jewish community, except for one family, immigrated to the newly established [State of Israel](9282173.md) between 1951 and 1952.[^cite_note-\:2-12]

By 1998, the local population was estimated at 548,747, which grew in 2015 to an estimated 656,100.[^cite_note-\:3-15]

[The American University of Iraq, Sulaimani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_University_of_Iraq,_Sulaimani) estimated the number of inhabitants in 2016 at 800,000.[^cite_note-16]

## Geography and climate



Panorama of Sulaymaniyah, September 2015

The city is located in Northern Iraq. Of the [main population centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Iraq) in the country, it is characterized by its cooler summer temperatures and its rainier winters. Average temperatures range from 0 to 39 °C (32–102 °F). In the winters, there can be a significant amount of snow. Snow falls every year or two.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

The [Köppen-Geiger climate classification system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen-Geiger_climate_classification_system) classifies its climate as [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) (Csa).[^cite_note-Climate-Data.org-22]

## Education

The [University of Slemani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sulaymaniyah) was opened in 1968 with instruction in Kurdish, Arabic, and English. It has faculties in engineering, agriculture, the arts, science, and medicine. It is the largest university in the Kurdistan Region.[^cite_note-27] A second university, [Sulaimani Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaimani_Polytechnic_University)[^cite_note-28] was established in 2012, also teaching in Kurdish, English and Arabic.

In 2007 [The American University of Iraq – Sulaimani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_University_of_Iraq_%E2%80%93_Sulaimani),[^cite_note-29] (AUI-S) was a new addition to the American universities in the Middle East, graduating its fifth class in 2016. Instruction at this private, not-for-profit liberal arts university is in English only, featuring a US-accredited program in English as a Second Language (ESL). There are a number of other private universities.

## Culture



[Adnan Karim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adnan_Karim) in a joint concert with the [Iraqi National Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_National_Symphony_Orchestra) conducted by the renowned Kurdish composer A.J. Sagerma performing classical [Kurdish music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_music)

Two independent newspapers *[Hawlati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawlati)*[^cite_note-30] and *[Awena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awena)*[^cite_note-31] and two independent political magazines *Lvin* and *Shock*, are published and distributed in Slemani city. Since 2016, there exists an International Film Festival in the city which is organized by the College of Fine Arts of the University of Sulaymanya.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

Slemani is the only city in South Kurdistan that regularly celebrates World Music Day or [Fête de la Musique](745073.md). In one trip to the city, a journalist working for the BBC wrote about Slemani's distinct culture:"Culture is hugely important to the Kurdish people, especially in Slemani, but there is a strong pull to the west—modernisation and consumerism—driven perhaps by the satellite televisions they have had access to since they started running their own affairs...And at the university, students mill around the campus, chattering with each other and doing some last-minute cramming for their exams. The war only stopped lectures for a few weeks. There are probably more women than men and they are happy to air their views to anyone who asks."[^cite_note-kurdishglobe.net-32]

Slemani was the home of poets such as [Nalî](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nal%C3%AE), [Mahwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahwi), and [Piramerd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piramerd).[^cite_note-Suli-200_years-33][^cite_note-34]

## Economy



Roman [amphitheater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphitheatre) in Slemani, important tourist attraction in the city



Sarchnar Park

Since 2003, Slemani has experienced a growing local economy. Its economy today relies on tourism, agriculture[^cite_note-35] and a number of small factories, most of which are involved in the building trade.

In 2004 the Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Analysis in Iraq released an in-depth survey of the Slemani Governorate in which they surveyed each city. In this survey, one can see the economic boom of 2003 mentioned earlier.[^cite_note-36]

The city is served by [Sulaimaniyah International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaimaniyah_International_Airport), with service on many commercial airlines, including [Iraqi](179397.md), [Qatar Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Airways), [Royal Jordanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Jordanian_Airlines) and [Turkish Airlines](167925.md).
