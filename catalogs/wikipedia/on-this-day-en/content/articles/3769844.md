**Bannu** ([Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): بنوں, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Urdu): *bannū̃*, pronounced [\[bənːũː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b3/Bannu_Urdu.wav/Bannu_Urdu.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b3/Bannu_Urdu.wav/Bannu_Urdu.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), also called **Bani Gul** or **Bani** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): باني, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Pashto): *bānī*, pronounced [\[ˈbɑni\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Pashto) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/53/Bannu_Soft_Pashto.wav/Bannu_Soft_Pashto.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/53/Bannu_Soft_Pashto.wav/Bannu_Soft_Pashto.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a city located on the [Kurram River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurram_River) in southern [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md), Pakistan. It is the capital of [Bannu Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannu_Division). Bannu's residents are primarily members of the [Banuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banuchi) tribe[^cite_note-5] and speak Banuchi (Baniswola),[^cite_note-IGI-6] a dialect of [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language) which is similar to the distinct [Waziristani dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waziristani_dialect). The residents regardless of their tribes are commonly called Banusi, Banuchi or Banisi.

The major industries of Bannu are cloth weaving, sugar mills[^cite_note-7] and the manufacturing of cotton fabrics, machinery and equipment.[^cite_note-8] It is famous for its weekly *Jumma* fair. The district forms a basin drained by the Kurram and [Gambila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambila_River) (or Tochi) rivers.[^cite_note-9]

## Etymology

According to the philologist [Michael Witzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Witzel), the city was originally known in [Avestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avestan) as *Varəna* (𐬬𐬀𐬭𐬆𐬥𐬀), from which its modern name derives. The ancient [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) grammarian [Pāṇini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81%E1%B9%87ini) recorded its name as *Varṇu* (वर्णु).[^cite_note-Witzel-10]



Sattagydia (𓐠𓂧𓎼𓍯𓍒𓈉, S-d-g-wꜣ-ḏꜣ), on the Egyptian Statue of Darius I.

During the 6th century BCE, the basin around Bannu was known as *[Sattagydia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sattagydia)* ([Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian): 𐎰𐎫𐎦𐎢𐏁 *Thataguš*, country of the "hundred cows").

## History



[Tomb of Xerxes I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Xerxes_I), Sattagydian soldier of the [Achaemenid army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_army), c. 480 BCE

The history of Bannu goes back to prehistoric time, due to its strategic location along the Kurram and Tochi routes which lead into the Indus Valley. [Sheri Khan Tarakai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheri_Khan_Tarakai) is an ancient settlement site located in the Bannu District with ruins of the oldest known village settlement in the Bannu region, which was occupied from the late fifth until the early third millennium BCE.[^cite_note-11] Recent archaeological excavations at [Akra, Bannu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akra,_Bannu) showed that it was a large urban site that existed throughout the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age_in_South_Asia) and had trade relations with [Central Asia](6742.md).[^cite_note-12]

The sacred texts of [Zend Avesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zend_Avesta) and [Vendidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vendidad) mentions Varəna, the Avestan predecessor of the name for Bannu, as the 14th in the list of the "16 perfect lands" created by [Ahura Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahura_Mazda).[^cite_note-Witzel-10] According to the Avesta, Varəna was the homeland and birthplace of the legendary King [Fereydun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fereydun) (known in Avestan as *Θraētaona*, and also known as *Āθβiiāni*, "of the house of [Abtin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abtin)").[^cite_note-13]<sup>: 47–50 </sup>

In the 6th century BCE, the region around Bannu was known as [Sattagydia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sattagydia) (lit. "country of 100 cows") and constituted the southern part of the greater region of [Paropamisadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paropamisadae). Under the Persian [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), Sattagydia became part of the Empire's 7th [taxation district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_districts_of_the_Achaemenid_Empire), which also included the [Gandāra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gand%C4%81ra), [Dadicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadicae), and [Aparytae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aparytae) regions. Sattagydia was mentioned in the [Behistun inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_inscription) of [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great) as one of the satrapies in revolt while the king was in [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon). The revolt was presumably suppressed in 515 BCE.

Akra (A) Mound, Bannu

The [Akra mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akra,_Bannu) in Bannu, which dates back to 6th century BC during [Achaemenid rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire)

After being conquered by [Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_III_of_Macedon) in the 4th century BCE, the region became part of the Greek [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) for a short while until the [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Empire) took control over the region around 305 BCE, as the entire region of North-West India constituting [Paropamisadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paropamisadae), [Arachosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachosia) and [Gedrosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gedrosia) was transferred to [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Empire) by the [Seleucids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire).

After the decline of [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Empire), the region fell under the administrative and political control of [Indo-Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Greeks), [Indo-Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Scythian) and [Indo-Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Parthians) successively. Kushan emperor [Kujula Kadphises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kujula_Kadphises) defeated the [Indo-Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Parthians) and incorporated the region under the [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire). In the [Tochi Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochi_Valley) of [North Waziristan](1131187.md) near Bannu, [Bactrian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactrian_language) inscriptions originally written in the 9th century have been discovered. This shows that after the collapse of the Kushan Empire, its official language continued in use for at least six more centuries.[^cite_note-14]

After the decline of the [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire), the region subsequently came under the control of [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire). The [Kidarites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidarites) began invading the North-West regions of [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) during the reign of Emperor [Kumaragupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumaragupta). The [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) ended up losing its control over the region around the early 5th century CE.

The [Kidarites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidarites) then too came under the attack from [Hephthalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hephthalites) who defeated the Kidarites and appointed sub-rulers and kings to rule over the region. Hūṇa King [Mihirakula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihirakula) was the most famous ruler from the [Alchon Hun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchon_Hun) dynasty.

[Aulikara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulikara) ruler, [Yashodharman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashodharman) defeated the Hūṇa King [Toramana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toramana) and took control over most of North-West India. The remnants of the [Hepthalite Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepthalites) continued to rule over the region until the 9th century CE when the [Hindu Shahis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Shahis) came to power and established their rule over the region for over 150 years.

In the late 10th and early 11th century CE, the [Ghaznavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids) conquered the area. [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) used the Bannu route for several of his raids deeper into Northern India.[^cite_note-15]

### British rule

The city was renamed in 1848 by [Herbert Benjamin Edwardes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Benjamin_Edwardes), a [lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant) in the [1st Bengal European Fusiliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Regiment_of_Foot_\(Royal_Bengal_Fusiliers\)) Regiment of the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company)'s private army. He ordered the construction of the fort – named Dhulipgarh (Dalipgarh) in honour of the Maharajah of Lahore – at the same time.[^cite_note-JWS-16] At the time of its founding, the town was named Dhulipnagar (Dalipnagar). Its name was later changed to Edwardesabad in 1869. In 1903, it received its current name, Bannu.[^cite_note-17]

Bannu was used as the base of operations for all punitive expeditions undertaken by detachments of the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) to the [Tochi Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochi_Valley) and the [Waziristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waziristan) frontier. A military road led from the town of Bannu toward [Dera Ismail Khan](696749.md).[^cite_note-EB1911-18] This road was built by military engineers under the supervision of a Bannu engineer, Ram N. Mullick. Mullick graduated from Banaras Engineering College[^cite_note-19] and had served in [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Lahore](125315.md) as an expert in heavy earth-moving equipment before the [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) of Pakistan in 1947.

### 1947 Bannu Jirga

Main article: [Bannu Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannu_Resolution)

On 21 June 1947 in Bannu, a [jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirga) was held by Pashtun leaders including [Bacha Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacha_Khan), his brother [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chief_Ministers_of_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa) [Dr Khan Sahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_Khan_Sahib), the [Khudai Khidmatgars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khudai_Khidmatgars), members of the Provincial Assembly, [Mirzali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirzali_Khan) (Faqir of Ipi), and other tribal chiefs, just seven weeks before the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India). The jirga declared the [Bannu Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannu_Resolution), which demanded that the Pashtuns be given a choice to have an independent state of [Pashtunistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtunistan) composing all Pashtun territories of British India, instead of being made to join either India or Pakistan. However, the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) refused to comply with the demand of this resolution, in response to which the Khudai Khidmatgars boycotted the [1947 North-West Frontier Province referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_North-West_Frontier_Province_referendum) for merging the province into Pakistan.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

### 2022 Pashtun National Jirga

Main article: [Pashtun National Jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_National_Jirga)

On 11–14 March 2022, the [Pashtun National Jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_National_Jirga) was held at [Mirakhel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirakhel) in Bannu in order to defend the rights of the [Pashtun people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_people) in the country. The critical issues which were faced by the Pashtuns were discussed during the jirga in a bid to suggest solutions to them.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-mashaal-23]

## Geography

### Climate

Bannu has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSh*) featuring sweltering summers and mild winters. The average annual temperature is 22.3 °C or 72.1 °F, with the hottest month being June with an average high of 41.5 °C or 106.7 °F and the coolest January with an average low of 2.5 °C or 36.5 °F. The average annual rainfall is 435 millimetres or 17.1 inches, with the majority falling in the monsoon season of July and August, followed by a secondary maximum due to western disturbances from December to March.

## Education
