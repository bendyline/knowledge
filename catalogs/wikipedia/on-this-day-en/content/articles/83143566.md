# 2026 Labour Party leadership election

This article is about the leadership election for the UK Labour Party. For the leadership election in Wales, see [2026 Welsh Labour leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Welsh_Labour_leadership_election). For the leadership election in Scotland, see [2026 Scottish Labour leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Scottish_Labour_leadership_election).

The **2026 Labour Party leadership election** was triggered by [Keir Starmer](18681491.md)'s 22 June 2026 announcement that, due to [the lack of confidence in his leadership](83157483.md) by his [MPs](3776360.md), he intended to resign as [Leader of the Labour Party](19043368.md) and [Prime Minister of the United Kingdom](24150.md).

[Andy Burnham](414818.md), a minister under [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) and [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown) who had been the [mayor of Greater Manchester](44287322.md) and previously ran for the Labour leadership in 2010 and 2015, had been tipped since 2025 as a potential leadership challenger to Starmer, but Labour rules dictate that only MPs can stand for the leadership. Burnham was blocked from standing in the [2026 Gorton and Denton by-election](82184590.md) (held on 26 February) by the party's [National Executive Committee](343817.md). However he stood and won the [2026 Makerfield by-election](83181171.md) on 18 June after the constituency's MP, [Josh Simons](77295092.md), resigned to allow Burnham to stand. Burnham's by-election victory intensified discussion of a leadership challenge; Starmer announced his resignation four days after the by-election, on the same day that Burnham was sworn in as an MP.

Nominations from MPs opened on 9 July and closed on 16 July. With 379 nominations, Burnham was the only eligible candidate when nominations closed, as he was nominated by over 94% of all Labour MPs, exceeding [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown)'s 88% of nominations in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Labour_Party_leadership_election_\(UK\)). He was acclaimed as [Leader of the Labour Party](19043368.md) on 17 July 2026, and became [Prime Minister](24150.md) three days later, on 20 July 2026. Burnham is the first Labour prime minister who is also a member of the [Co-operative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-operative_Party).

## Background

### Starmer leadership and premiership

See also: [Premiership of Keir Starmer](77280609.md)



Approval ratings of Keir Starmer as Prime Minister

[Keir Starmer](18681491.md) was elected Labour leader by winning the [2020 leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Labour_Party_leadership_election_\(UK\)). His party made significant gains at the [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_Kingdom_local_elections) and [2024 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_local_elections) amidst a significant drop in membership in the years prior. Starmer became the fifth Labour leader to become prime minister after a general election but with the smallest share of the electoral vote of any majority government since records began [in 1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1830_United_Kingdom_general_election).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

Starmer's [net approval rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_approval_opinion_polling_for_the_next_United_Kingdom_general_election#Leadership_approval) began slightly positive but fell over the course of his premiership to an average of −46% by November 2025.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:5-4] Starmer also presided over a period of low support for Labour, with the party facing record electoral losses during the [2025 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_Kingdom_local_elections). In January 2026, [Andy Burnham](414818.md), who had been speculated since 2025 to be planning to enter Parliament to challenge Starmer's leadership, attempted to stand to be the Labour Party candidate in the [Gorton and Denton by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorton_and_Denton_by-election) but was blocked from standing by the party's [National Executive Committee](343817.md).[^cite_note-guardian-courea-25sep25-5][^cite_note-guardian-taylor-24jan26-6][^cite_note-\:3-7][^cite_note-\:4-8] In February, [Anas Sarwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anas_Sarwar), the [Leader of the Scottish Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Scottish_Labour_Party), called for Starmer to resign as party leader amid the [Peter Mandelson appointment scandal](81477249.md).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In the May 2026 UK local elections, the Labour Party lost control of over half of the councils up for election.[^cite_note-\:0-11] Labour also lost over 1,400 councillors, which [BBC News](1139893.md) reported as the single largest loss of councillors Labour had had in a single local election.[^cite_note-\:0-11] [Reform UK](59757202.md) and the [Green Party](79787.md) made their highest gains ever.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

At the same time, Labour suffered a historic defeat in the [2026 Senedd election](68245236.md), failing to win either an outright majority or plurality in the Senedd for the first time in its history, losing to [Plaid Cymru](53343.md).[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-12] In the [2026 Scottish Parliament election](65934215.md) as well, the Labour Party failed to make expected gains, coming in joint-second with Reform UK in terms of [MSPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSPs).[^cite_note-\:0-11]

On 9 May, Labour MP [Catherine West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_West) issued an ultimatum for a leadership challenger to Starmer to step forward by 11 May or that West would put herself forward as a candidate.[^cite_note-\:2-13] West said her preferred option was that someone already in the cabinet become leader, with Starmer "given a different role".[^cite_note-\:2-13] That same day, around Labour 30 MPs had either directly called for Keir Starmer to resign, or for him to set out a timetable on his departure.[^cite_note-\:2-13] On 11 May, no candidate had been put forward, and West refused herself to stand as a challenger.[^cite_note-14]

By 11 May's evening, 70 MPs had called for Starmer to resign.[^cite_note-15] On 13 May, allies of [Health Secretary](356531.md) [Wes Streeting](17169295.md) told BBC News that they expected him to resign and launch a leadership bid as soon as tomorrow.[^cite_note-16] A spokesperson for Streeting stated that he would not do anything to "distract" from the [State Opening of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Opening_of_Parliament).[^cite_note-17] In a meeting with MPs later in the evening, Starmer vowed to fight a leadership challenge if the health secretary ran.[^cite_note-18] Cabinet ministers loyal to Starmer attempted to convince colleagues that a change in leadership would paralyse the government and cause chaos.[^cite_note-19]

On 13 May, Starmer said to *[The Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Observer)* that he wanted to be in office for ten years and would stand in a possible leadership contest.[^cite_note-20] Starmer's position rapidly deteriorated on 14 May. Former [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom) [Angela Rayner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Rayner) was cleared by the [HM Revenue and Customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Revenue_and_Customs) over her [tax scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Rayner_tax_scandal), which was viewed as paving the way for her to join a leadership race.[^cite_note-21] Streeting announced his resignation at 1\:00pm [GMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMT), stating that he had lost confidence in Starmer's government. [Al Carns](77290491.md) was reported by Sky News to be interested in a leadership challenge.[^cite_note-22]

### Return of Burnham to Parliament

On 14 May, [Josh Simons](77295092.md), the Labour member for Makerfield, resigned his seat in parliament, paving the way for Burnham to contest the by-election and so to stand for the Labour leadership,[^cite_note-23] in accordance with party rules that require any candidate for leader to be a member of the Parliamentary Labour Party.[^cite_note-24] Whilst Burnham had previously been blocked from standing in the 2026 Gorton and Denton by-election in February 2026 by the National Executive Committee of the Labour Party (NEC), they did not block his candidacy for the Makerfield by-election.

On 16 May, Wes Streeting stated he would stand in a leadership election, should there be one, but that he only supported having a "proper contest" to give Burnham time to return to Parliament and be eligible to stand, thus delaying any leadership contest until June.[^cite_note-25]

The 2026 Makerfield by-election was held on 18 June, with Burnham exceeding expectations to win the by-election with a large majority of over 9,000 votes. Burnham gained the support of over 200 Labour MPs by 20 June. *The Guardian* reported on 20 June that a Burnham leadership campaign was inevitable and that his allies were confident that Burnham would be elected uncontested, though some Labour MPs still wanted a contested leadership race.[^cite_note-26]

Starmer announced his intention to resign on 22 June 2026, four days after the by-election.[^cite_note-\:8-27]

## Candidates

Under the Labour Party's rules, for a leadership contender to be on the ballot, they must be nominated by at least 20% of the [Parliamentary Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Labour_Party), meaning 81 MPs would be required.[^cite_note-\:2-13][^cite_note-\:1-28]

Burnham was the first to announce his candidacy for the leadership contest after Starmer's resignation announcement, stating he "will put \[himself] forward as part of this process."[^cite_note-\:6-29] [Wes Streeting](17169295.md) and [Al Carns](77290491.md), who were both seen as potential candidates, endorsed Burnham.[^cite_note-\:7-30]

While not formally putting herself forward for the leadership position, [Catherine West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_West), the MP for [Hornsey and Friern Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornsey_and_Friern_Barnet), was nominated by [Neil Coyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Coyle) after nominations had opened. This occurred after it was already mathematically impossible for a candidate other than Burnham to reach the required level of 81 MPs.[^cite_note-West-31]

## Nominations

&#x20;Burnham received 322 nominations on the day nominations opened, one short of the 323 that would mean no other candidate could receive enough nominations to challenge him.[^cite_note-67] By convention, the outgoing party leader (Starmer), [NEC chair](343817.md) (Shabana Mahmood) and [party chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chair_of_the_Labour_Party_\(UK\)) ([Anna Turley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Turley)) did not nominate anyone.[^cite_note-guardian13july-68]

By 13 July he had received 349 PLP nominations, rising to 379 by 15 July.[^cite_note-guardian13july-68][^cite_note-69] After the deadline had passed, Burnham was the only remaining candidate, and secured the nominations of 23 [Labour Party affiliates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_affiliates);[^cite_note-AffilNoms-70] as there were no other eligible candidates by the affiliate deadline, Burnham was declared Leader of the Labour Party at a special party conference on 17th July.[^cite_note-71]
