**Adriana Geertruida** "**Demi**" **Vollering** (Dutch: [\[ˈdeːmi ˈvɔlərɪŋ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch); born 15 November 1996) is a Dutch professional [racing cyclist](857311.md) who rides for [UCI Women's WorldTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_Women's_Team) [FDJ United–Suez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDJ_United%E2%80%93Suez).[^cite_note-4] Considered one of the greatest riders of her generation,[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] she has achieved major successes in both [one-day classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_cycle_races) and [stage races](1755460.md#Stage_races).

Among the [monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_monument), she won the [2026 Tour of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tour_of_Flanders_\(women's_race\)) and three editions of [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes), in 2021, 2023, and 2026. This included an "[Ardennes triple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes_classics)" in 2023, when she won the [Amstel Gold Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Amstel_Gold_Race_\(women's_race\)), [La Flèche Wallonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_La_Fl%C3%A8che_Wallonne_Femmes) and [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes) in the same season, becoming the second woman to achieve this feat.[^cite_note-Marshall-Bell-2023-7] She has won the three [Grand Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour_\(cycling\)): the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France_Femmes) in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Tour_de_France_Femmes) and [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tour_de_France_Femmes), the [Vuelta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Vuelta_Femenina) in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_La_Vuelta_Femenina) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_La_Vuelta_Femenina), and the [Giro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giro_d'Italia_Women) in [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Giro_d'Italia_Women).[^cite_note-8]

In 2023 and 2025, she topped the year-end [UCI world rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_women's_road_world_rankings), winning the [Vélo d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A9lo_d'Or) in 2023.[^cite_note-uci.org-9][^cite_note-Tyson-2023-10] In 2026 she became the first rider to win the Tour de France Femmes twice.[^cite_note-BBC-TdFF26-11]

## Early life

Adriana Geertruida Vollering, known by the nickname "Demi", was born on 15 November 1996[^cite_note-12] and grew up in [Pijnacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pijnacker), Netherlands. She is the oldest of four siblings, including fellow cyclist Bodine Vollering, who made her professional debut with [VolkerWessels Cycling Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VolkerWessels_Cycling_Team) in 2025.[^cite_note-demivollering.com-13][^cite_note-14]

Following in the footsteps of her flower-growing family, Vollering initially worked as a florist and earned a qualification in floral design.[^cite_note-15]

Until 2019, Vollering also trained in [speed skating](28028.md), competing at national level in the Netherlands.[^cite_note-16] As a youngster, speed skating was her primary sport, and it was only in 2017, after a [cycling holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_touring) to the [Ardennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes) that her partner convinced her to focus on the sport full-time.[^cite_note-demivollering.com-13]

## Professional career

### 2019–2020: breakthrough

#### 2019

Having raced in 2018 as an amateur with the domestic [SwaboLadies.nl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GT_Krush_Rebellease_Pro_Cycling) team, Vollering signed her first professional contract in 2019 with Dutch team [Parkhotel Valkenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VolkerWessels_Women_Cyclingteam).[^cite_note-17]

Her first spring campaign as a professional cyclist was a successful one, with top-10 finishes at the [Amstel Gold Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Amstel_Gold_Race_\(women's_race\)), [La Flèche Wallonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_La_Fl%C3%A8che_Wallonne_Femmes), and a first Monument podium at [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes).[^cite_note-18]

In May 2019, Vollering achieved her first professional victory: a 2.7 km prologue at the [Festival Elsy Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_Elsy_Jacobs) in Luxembourg, taking the leader's jersey in the process.[^cite_note-19] She followed this up in October with a second professional win at the [Giro dell'Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giro_dell'Emilia_Internazionale_Donne_Elite), beating Elisa Longo Borghini in a two-rider sprint at the top of the [Madonna di San Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_di_San_Luca,_Bologna) climb.[^cite_note-20]

#### 2020

In the [COVID-19-affected 2020 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_UCI_Women's_World_Tour), Vollering continued to impress in WorldTour races, coming third at both [La Course by Le Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_La_Course_by_Le_Tour_de_France) and [La Flèche Wallonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_La_Fl%C3%A8che_Wallonne_Femmes), in addition to top-10 results on the cobbles at [Gent–Wevelgem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Gent%E2%80%93Wevelgem_\(women's_race\)) and the [Tour of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Tour_of_Flanders_for_Women).[^cite_note-21]

Following two successful seasons with Parkhotel Valkenburg, joined [UCI WorldTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_Women's_Team) [SD Worx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_SD_Worx%E2%80%93Protime) from the 2021 season onwards.[^cite_note-22]

### 2021–2024: SD Worx, Tour de France Femmes and stage race dominance

#### 2021

Vollering started the season strongly with top-10 finishes at [Strade Bianche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Strade_Bianche_Women) and the [Tour of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Tour_of_Flanders_\(women's_race\)).[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2021-23] She then finished as runner-up at [De Brabantse Pijl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brabantse_Pijl_\(women's_race\)), celebrating as she crossed the line only to find out she had been pipped by [Ruth Winder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Winder)'s late bike throw.[^cite_note-24]

In the [Ardennes classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes_classics), Vollering started the week with an impressive second place to [Marianne Vos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianne_Vos) at the [Amstel Gold Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Amstel_Gold_Race_\(women's_race\)), followed by a tenth-place finish at [La Flèche Wallonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_La_Fl%C3%A8che_Wallonne_Femmes).[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2021-23] Vollering then took her first career [monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_monument) victory at [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes), winning the sprint from a five-rider group containing [Annemiek van Vleuten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annemiek_van_Vleuten), [Elisa Longo Borghini](37097733.md), [Kasia Niewiadoma](40712010.md) and teammate [Anna van der Breggen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_van_der_Breggen).[^cite_note-25]

At what was to be the [final edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_La_Course_by_Le_Tour_de_France) of [La Course by Le Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Course_by_Le_Tour_de_France), Vollering took her second [WorldTour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_Women's_World_Tour) victory of the season, winning the sprint in [Landerneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landerneau) from an eight-rider group.[^cite_note-26]

In July, Vollering took her first podium in a [major tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour_\(cycling\)) at the [Giro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Giro_Rosa) in Italy, finishing in third place behind her teammates [Anna van der Breggen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_van_der_Breggen) and [Ashleigh Moolman-Pasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Moolman_Pasio). This included second-place finishes on the stage 4 [time trial](348799.md) to [Cascata del Toce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascata_del_Toce) and the stage 9 summit finish up [Monte Matajur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matajur).[^cite_note-27]

Vollering competed in the [road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_individual_road_race) at the delayed [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics) in Tokyo, placing 25th. She also achieved top-10 finishes in the [UEC European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_European_Road_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_elite_road_race) and [UCI World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race) road races.[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2021-23]

In October, Vollering won her first career stage race at [The Women's Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_The_Women's_Tour). After helping teammate [Amy Pieters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Pieters) win on stage 1, Vollering took the leader's jersey by winning the stage 3 by winning 16.6 km stage 3 [time trial](348799.md) in [Atherstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atherstone), putting more than a minute into her competitors in the process.[^cite_note-28]

#### 2022

Vollering had a strong spring classics campaign, winning [De Brabantse Pijl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brabantse_Pijl_\(women's_race\)), coming second at [Omloop het Nieuwsblad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Omloop_Het_Nieuwsblad_\(women's_race\)) and the [Amstel Gold Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Amstel_Gold_Race_\(women's_race\)), and taking the third spot on the podium at [La Flèche Wallonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_La_Fl%C3%A8che_Wallonne_Femmes) and [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes).[^cite_note-29]

In the inaugural edition of [Itzulia Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Itzulia_Women), Vollering won all three stages and the general classification.[^cite_note-30] The following week at the [Vuelta a Burgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Vuelta_a_Burgos_Feminas), Vollering took victory in the final stage to Lagunas de Neila, finishing third overall behind [Juliette Labous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliette_Labous) and [Évita Muzic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89vita_Muzic).[^cite_note-31]

Vollering's impressive results meant she went into the [first edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Tour_de_France_Femmes) of the rebooted [women's Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France_Femmes) as one of the two big favourites alongside [Annemiek van Vleuten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annemiek_van_Vleuten).[^cite_note-32] She finished the race as the runner-up to Van Vleuten after consecutive second-place finishes on stages 7 and 8 in the [Vosges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vosges) mountains. In the process, she won the iconic [polka dot jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountains_classification_in_the_Tour_de_France) as the leading rider in the mountains classification.[^cite_note-33]

In September, Vollering added her fourth stage-race podium of the season, finishing in third place overall at the [Challenge by La Vuelta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Challenge_by_La_Vuelta).[^cite_note-34]

#### 2023

The 2023 season saw Vollering complete her rise to superstardom with one of the most dominant years in cycling history.[^cite_note-35]

&#x20;In March, Vollering won her first [Strade Bianche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Strade_Bianche_Donne) on the white gravel roads of Tuscany in a [photo finish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photo_finish) ahead of teammate [Lotte Kopecky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_Kopecky). The race was noted for a dramatic incident with 16 kilometres to go, when a horse ran onto the course in front of Vollering, briefly holding her up on the narrow stretch of road.[^cite_note-36]



Vollering wearing the yellow jersey at the [2023 Tour de France Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Tour_de_France_Femmes)

Vollering added a second victory of the season in her next race at [Dwars door Vlaanderen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwars_door_Vlaanderen), before finishing as runner-up to her teammate Kopecky in the first Monument of the season at the [Tour of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Tour_of_Flanders_\(women's_race\)).[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2023-37] Another second place followed at [De Brabantse Pijl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brabantse_Pijl_\(women's_race\)), as Vollering was beaten in a sprint in [Overijse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overijse) by [Silvia Persico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvia_Persico).[^cite_note-38]

The following week, Vollering became just the fourth rider in history (after men [Davide Rebellin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Rebellin) and [Philippe Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Gilbert), and former teammate [Anna van der Breggen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_van_der_Breggen)) to win the "[Ardennes triple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardennes_classics)" of the [Amstel Gold Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstel_Gold_Race_\(women's_race\)), [La Flèche Wallonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Fl%C3%A8che_Wallonne_Femmes) and [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes) in the same season.[^cite_note-Marshall-Bell-2023-7] At the Amstel Gold Race, Vollering cemented another SD Worx one-two ahead of Lotte Kopecky, soloing to victory in the final kilometres after a late attack over the top of the [Cauberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauberg).[^cite_note-39] A few days later at La Flèche Wallonne, Vollering attacked from a reduced group at the bottom of the [Mur de Huy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mur_de_Huy) to take a dominant victory.[^cite_note-40] Vollering completed the Ardennes triple in Liège by outsprinting [Elisa Longo Borghini](37097733.md) in a two-up finish.[^cite_note-41]

Vollering's electric form did not slow as racing moved to Spain after the early-season classics. At [La Vuelta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_La_Vuelta_Femenina), she took the leader's [red jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_jersey) after winning stage 5 to the Mirador de Peñas Llanas in [Riaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riaza).[^cite_note-42] She would then lose the jersey on stage 6 in a controversial incident. Having stopped for a [nature break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urination) and not yet returned to the [peloton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloton), [Movistar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movistar_Team_\(women's_team\)) and their leader [Annemiek van Vleuten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annemiek_van_Vleuten) upped the pace in a section of [crosswinds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crosswind), splitting the peloton and leaving Vollering and her [SD Worx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_SD_Worx%E2%80%93Protime) teammates chasing for the following 70 kilometres to the finish. She was able to limit her losses on the stage to one minute and four seconds but this meant losing the general classification lead to Van Vleuten heading into the final stage.[^cite_note-43] On stage 7, Vollering distanced Van Vleuten on the final climb to [Lagos de Covadonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakes_of_Covadonga), winning the stage and securing ten bonus seconds. However, she was unable to overhaul the entirety of her deficit, ultimately finishing the race in second place, just nine seconds behind the winner Van Vleuten.[^cite_note-44]

In the following Spanish races, Vollering won the first two stages of [Itzulia Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Itzulia_Women) (making her the winner of the first five stages in the race's history), before taking second place on the final stage and finishing as runner-up to teammate [Marlen Reusser](55330743.md) in the general classification.[^cite_note-45] She then went one better at the [Vuelta a Burgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Vuelta_a_Burgos_Feminas), winning another two stages on her way to the overall race victory.[^cite_note-46]

In June, Vollering finished second to teammate Reusser in the general classification at the [Tour de Suisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Tour_de_Suisse_Women).[^cite_note-47] She then claimed her first career [Dutch National Road Race Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_National_Road_Race_Championships), launching an attack on the final lap of a hilly route around [Sittard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sittard).[^cite_note-48]

At the [Tour de France Femmes avec Zwift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Tour_de_France_Femmes), it was once again Vollering and Van Vleuten who were expected to battle it out for the [yellow jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_classification_in_the_Tour_de_France) in a renewal of their blossoming rivalry.[^cite_note-49] Going into the final two stages, it was Vollering's SD Worx teammate [Lotte Kopecky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_Kopecky) who held the race lead, with a 55-second lead over Van Vleuten and fellow contender [Kasia Niewiadoma](40712010.md), with Vollering a further 12 seconds in arrears.[^cite_note-50] Stage 7 was a [Pyrenean](24707.md) epic, and atop the [Col d'Aspin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_d'Aspin) with 30 kilometres remaining, only Vollering, Van Vleuten and Niewiadoma remained at the head of the race. Vollering and Van Vleuten marked each other profusely, allowing Niewiadoma to steal a march into the final climb up the [Col du Tourmalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_du_Tourmalet). Yet it was Vollering who dealt the killer blow, launching an attack with 5.5 km to go, and arriving at the top through the mist with an almost two-minute gap over second-placed Niewiadoma.[^cite_note-51] Wearing the yellow jersey on stage 8 in [Pau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pau,_Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Atlantiques), Vollering rode a strong [time trial](348799.md) to finish in second place on the stage and secure her first [major tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour_\(cycling\)) general classification victory.[^cite_note-52]

Following her Tour de France win, Vollering claimed the [silver medal](526647.md) behind [Lotte Kopecky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_Kopecky) at the [UCI World Championships road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race) in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) and later the [bronze medal](526648.md) at the [UCI Gravel World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UCI_Gravel_World_Championships) in [Veneto](43780.md).[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2023-37] She also won a stage and the overall classification at the [Tour de Romandie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Tour_de_Romandie_F%C3%A9minin).[^cite_note-53]

Vollering's dominant season saw her top the year-end [UCI world rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_women's_road_world_rankings) and she was awarded the prestigious [Vélo d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A9lo_d'Or).[^cite_note-uci.org-9][^cite_note-Tyson-2023-10]

#### 2024

Following on from her *[annus mirabilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annus_mirabilis)* in 2023, Vollering went through the entire spring classics campaign without raising her arms in victory. She began her season at [Omloop het Nieuwsblad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Omloop_Het_Nieuwsblad_\(women's_race\)) with a sixth-place finish, followed third place at [Strade Bianche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Strade_Bianche_Donne) and eighth place at the [Tour of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Tour_of_Flanders_\(women's_race\)), before rounding out the spring classics with back-to-back podium finishes as runner-up at [La Flèche Wallonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_La_Fl%C3%A8che_Wallonne_Femmes) and third place at [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes).[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2024-54]

Vollering quickly cleared up any doubts about her form, by dominating the Spanish stage racing block.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] She took her first win of the season at [La Vuelta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_La_Vuelta_Femenina) on the stage 5 summit finish in [Jaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaca), moving into the race leader's [red jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_jersey). She finished as runner up to [Évita Muzic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89vita_Muzic) on the following stage to Laguna Negra, before taking another stage victory and securing the general classification on stage 8 to [Valdesquí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdesqu%C3%AD).[^cite_note-57] She followed this win up by winning a stage and the overall title at [Itzulia Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Itzulia_Women), as well as two stages and the general classification at the [Vuelta a Burgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Vuelta_a_Burgos_Feminas).[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2024-54]

In June, Vollering continued her stage race dominance by winning three out of four stages and the general classification at the [Tour de Suisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Tour_de_Suisse_Women).[^cite_note-58]



Demi Vollering in stage 8 of the 2024 Tour de France Femmes

Vollering was considered the red-hot favourite heading into the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Tour_de_France_Femmes) in August.[^cite_note-59] She took a surprise win in the flat 6.3 km [individual time trial](348799.md) in [Rotterdam](26049.md) on stage 3 to move into the [yellow jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_classification_in_the_Tour_de_France) before the race had even reached French soil.[^cite_note-60] The next day, she finished second to [Puck Pieterse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puck_Pieterse) in a hilly stage that featured the same finale as [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes).[^cite_note-61] Vollering saw her luck change when the race entered France on stage 5. She came down in a mass crash with just 6 kilometres to go, causing her to lose one minute and 47 seconds in the general classification. Vollering slipped to ninth overall, one minute and 19 seconds behind the new yellow jersey wearer [Kasia Niewiadoma](40712010.md).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] Despite her injuries, Vollering remained in the race, entering the mountainous final stage with a deficit of one minute and 15 seconds to Niewiadoma. On the [Col du Glandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_du_Glandon), the first of two *[hors catégorie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hors_cat%C3%A9gorie)* climbs, Vollering attacked from the peloton with [Pauliena Rooijakkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauliena_Rooijakkers), distancing Niewadoma. Although Rooijakkers herself had moved into the virtual lead of the race, she refused to work with Vollering in the valley as the chasing group ate into the gap and they came to the foot of the final climb up [Alpe d'Huez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpe_d'Huez) just 43 seconds ahead over Niewidoma. With Vollering in visible discomfort from the injuries she had suffered a few days prior, she was nonetheless able to increase the gap to Niewiadoma. After closing down an attack by Rooijakkers with 2.5 km to go, Vollering won the sprint for the stage win, taking ten bonus seconds to Rooijakkers' six, thus moving ahead of her in the general classification. However, it proved not enough, as Niewiadoma crossed the stage finish line one minute and one second back. Vollering finished the race in second place, a mere four seconds behind the winner.[^cite_note-64]

Upon returning to her home in Switzerland after the Tour de France, medical tests revealed that Vollering had in fact been riding with a fractured [coccyx](213351.md) from the crash on stage 5.[^cite_note-65]

At the [Tour de Romandie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Tour_de_Romandie_F%C3%A9minin) in September, in what was to be her final race for [SD Worx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_SD_Worx%E2%80%93Protime), Vollering won the stage 2 summit finish at [Vercorin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vercorin) and finished second overall behind her teammate [Lotte Kopecky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_Kopecky).[^cite_note-66]

Vollering closed out her season at the [UCI Road World Championships](7171411.md) in Zürich. She won the silver medal in the [individual time trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_time_trial), 16 seconds behind [Grace Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Brown_\(cyclist\)), and finished fifth in the [road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race).[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2024-54]

### 2025–present: move to FDJ–Suez, Grand Tour wins

#### 2025

After four seasons with SD Worx, Vollering signed a two-year contract with French team [FDJ–Suez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDJ%E2%80%93Suez), bringing [Specialized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialized_Bicycle_Components) and [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) with her as new sponsors.[^cite_note-67] Vollering got off to an excellent start with her new team, winning stage 1 and the general classification of the [Setmana Ciclista Valenciana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setmana_Ciclista_Valenciana) in February.[^cite_note-68] In the spring classics, Vollering finished third at [Omloop Nieuwsblad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Omloop_Het_Nieuwsblad_\(women's_race\)) before winning [Strade Bianche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Strade_Bianche_Donne) for the second time in her career. Vollering also finished fourth in [Milano–Sanremo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Milan%E2%80%93San_Remo_Women), second in [La Flèche Wallonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_La_Fl%C3%A8che_Wallonne_Femmes) and third at [Liège–Bastogne–Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes).[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2025-69]

In the big stage races, Vollering won [La Vuelta Femenina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_La_Vuelta_Femenina) for the second time, winning two stages and finishing more than a minute ahead of [Marlen Reusser](55330743.md). At the [Tour de France Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Tour_de_France_Femmes), Vollering finished second overall behind [Pauline Ferrand-Prévot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauline_Ferrand-Pr%C3%A9vot), over three and a half minutes behind.[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2025-69] Vollering expressed her disappointment in the result, stating "I came here for stage wins and the yellow jersey but didn't get either".[^cite_note-70]

At the [UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UCI_Road_World_Championships) in Rwanda, she won the bronze medal in the [individual time trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_time_trial), 1 minute and 4 seconds behind winner Reusser, and finished seventh in the [road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_road_race).[^cite_note-FirstCycling-2025-69]

#### 2026

Vollering had a strong spring classics season, winning [Omloop Het Nieuwsblad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Omloop_Het_Nieuwsblad_\(women's_race\)), the [Tour of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tour_of_Flanders_\(women's_race\)), [La Flèche Wallonne Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_La_Fl%C3%A8che_Wallonne_Femmes) and [Liège–Bastogne–Liège Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Li%C3%A8ge%E2%80%93Bastogne%E2%80%93Li%C3%A8ge_Femmes).[^cite_note-Moultrie-2026-3]

In May 2026, Vollering extended her contract with [FDJ United–Suez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDJ_United%E2%80%93Suez) until 2028.[^cite_note-Moultrie-2026-3] Vollering skipped La Vuelta Femenina, entering the repositioned [Giro d'Italia Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Giro_d'Italia_Women) instead. During the Giro, Vollering overcame a deficit of a little less than one minute to Anna van der Breggen, taking the overall lead on the final stage to becoming the second woman to have won all three [Grand Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour_\(cycling\)) after Annemiek van Vleuten.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] She won the [2026 Tour de France Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tour_de_France_Femmes), becoming the first person to win it twice. Vollering took an eight-second lead in the penultimate stage of the race and won by over a minute.[^cite_note-73]
