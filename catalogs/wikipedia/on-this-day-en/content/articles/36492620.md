General elections were held in [Honduras](13394.md) on 24 November 2013.[^cite_note-1] Voters went to the polls to elect a new [President](23420892.md), the 128 members of the [National Congress](1604127.md), 298 Mayors and vice-mayors and their respective councilors and 20 representatives to the [Central American Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Parliament).

The closely watched presidential election saw a field of eight candidates vying to succeed outgoing President [Porfirio Lobo Sosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porfirio_Lobo_Sosa), who is not eligible to run for re-election. [Salvador Nasralla](41103646.md), a sports journalist and television personality, and [Xiomara Castro](6016595.md), the wife of the deposed president [Mel Zelaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Zelaya), both candidates from newly formed political parties (the [Anti-Corruption Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Corruption_Party) and [Libre](36492435.md), respectively) were leading in most of the early polls. However, as the election neared, the candidates of the two traditional parties – [Juan Orlando Hernández](25895048.md) of the [National Party](1641714.md) and [Mauricio Villeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Villeda) of the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Honduras) – both surged in the polls.

The elections were the first since [1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Honduran_general_election) in which a party other than the National Party and Liberal Party received over 7% of the vote and more than five seats in the legislature in a general election. It was also the first time the Liberal Party did not finish either first or second in an election since the 1920s.

## Background

This is the first election to be contested by the opposition since the controversial and polarising [2009 Honduran coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Honduran_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). The [social mobilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Popular_Resistance_Front) since then led to the founding of the main opposition party, [Libre](36492435.md).[^cite_note-2]

### Two-party system

Honduras has historically been dominated by a two-party system – the National Party and the Liberal Party. This election represents the first time in Honduran history in which other parties had a chance at winning the presidency or at least gaining a significant representation in the Congress, four of which find their genesis post-[coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Honduran_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-3]

### Human rights concerns

The elections took place amidst a deteriorating human rights situations.[^cite_note-AI-4] [Amnesty International](18947898.md) called attention to the killings of human rights defenders in the lead-up to the election, noting that Honduras had the highest homicide rate in the world yet only twenty percent of homicides were investigated.[^cite_note-AI-4] Honduran human rights organizations formed the Board of Analysis on the Human Rights Situation to monitor human rights violations surrounding the election,[^cite_note-5] pointing to the level of political violence in the country: human rights group Rights Action examined the period between May 2012 and October 2013 and documented 36 killings and 24 armed attacks against pre-candidates, candidates, their families and campaign leaders across all parties, with [Libre](36492435.md) experiencing the majority of both armed attacks and killings.[^cite_note-6] In light of this situation, 24 [U.S. Senators](24909346.md) signed a letter to the [U.S. State Department](31975.md) expressing their concerns about the upcoming elections.[^cite_note-7]

## Campaign

Key electoral issues have been citizen security, organized crime, unemployment, and corruption.[^cite_note-abanico-8] One of the main components of Hernández's campaign is his promise to put "a soldier on every corner."[^cite_note-frank-9] For her part, Castro has emphasized the need for community policing and secure borders.[^cite_note-10]

### Primaries

Primaries were held for the [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(Honduras\)), [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Honduras\)) and [Libre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertad_y_Refundaci%C3%B3n).

#### National

[Juan Orlando Hernández](25895048.md), president of the [National Congress of Honduras](1604127.md), won the presidential nomination of the National Party. The other candidates were [Ricardo Álvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_%C3%81lvarez_\(mayor\)) (the Mayor of [Tegucigalpa](25994100.md)), [Fernando Anduray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Anduray?action=edit\&redlink=1) (National Congress deputy), [Victor Hugo Barnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Hugo_Barnica?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Third Vice President of Honduras), [Eva Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Fernandez?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Loreley Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loreley_Fernandez?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Miguel Pastor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Pastor?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Secretary of State for Public Works, Transport, and Housing). The Supreme Electoral Tribunal certified Hernández's victory, but Álvarez immediately presented an appeal, accusing Hernández of fraud and asking for a recount.[^cite_note-11] The appeal was rejected by the Constitutional Chamber of the Supreme Court, four of whose five members were replaced by Hernández a month earlier in a move widely criticized as an illegal "[technical coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_coup?action=edit\&redlink=1)".[^cite_note-frank-9][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Álvarez and Pastor refused to attend the party convention in protest, claiming that they were being persecuted by their own party.[^cite_note-14]

#### Liberal

[Mauricio Villeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Villeda), won the presidential nomination of the Liberal Party. Other candidates in the fray for the presidential nomination were [Esteban Handal Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteban_Handal_Perez) and [Yani Rosenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yani_Rosenthal) (National Congress deputy and former Minister of Presidency).

#### Libre

[Xiomara Castro de Zelaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiomara_Castro_de_Zelaya), former [First Lady of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lady_of_Honduras), was the sole presidential candidate in the Libre primaries.

## Conduct

Honduran elections have historically been marred by fraud,[^cite_note-panam-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] and polls leading up to the elections found that 59% of Hondurans believe the elections would be fraudulent.[^cite_note-23] However, the [Supreme Electoral Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Electoral_Tribunal_\(Honduras\)) (TSE) has stated that these would be the most clean and fair elections in Honduras's history, and both the traditionally dominant parties – the National and Liberal parties – agree.[^cite_note-p12-24] The newly formed Libre Party and Anti-Corruption Party fear that there would be fraud, a position backed by the [Carter Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Center).[^cite_note-p12-24] Anti-Corruption Party candidate [Salvador Nasralla](41103646.md) publicly denounced attempts at vote-buying by the National Party across the country.[^cite_note-compro-25] Nasralla highlighted National Party control of key government institutions like the Public Ministry and the Supreme Court.[^cite_note-compro-25] Dana Frank, writing in *[The Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nation)*, echoed these concerns, noting National Party candidate Hernández's participation in both the illegal naming of a new attorney general in August 2013 and the illegal destitution of four Supreme Court judges in December 2012,[^cite_note-frank-9] the latter of which ultimately resulted in Hernández securing his party's nomination for the presidency.[^cite_note-panam-20]

The TSE has stated that over 700 international election observers, representing various governments and organizations, including the [United Nations](31769.md), the [Organization of American States](22788.md), the [European Union](9317.md), and the [Carter Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Center), will be present to monitor the elections.[^cite_note-26] In the days before the election, international observers in the department of [Yoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoro) and in the capital [Tegucigalpa](25994100.md) reported targeted harassment and intimidation on the part of immigration officials and unidentified armed men.[^cite_note-27] The TSE confirmed these reports and ordered the Honduran immigration authorities to stop all of these types of operations concerning election observers.[^cite_note-28]

## Aftermath

Juan Orlando Hernández was announced as the winner in a result the Supreme Electoral Tribunal's head, David Matamoros, called "irreversible",[^cite_note-irreversible-29] this followed initial claims by both leading candidates of having won. While opposition protests continued, Hernández said the result was "not negotiable with anybody" and named a transition team.[^cite_note-irreversible-29]

However, Castro and Nasralla disputed the results.[^cite_note-30] Castro called on her supporters to hold a protest on Saturday 30 November.[^cite_note-31]

According to the [North American Congress on Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Congress_on_Latin_America), the elections were "fraught with irregularities and violent intimidation, threatening to throw the embattled nation into further political disarray."[^cite_note-32] However, observers from the [Organization of American States](22788.md) and the [United Nations](31769.md) declared that the elections met international standards and were both free and fair.[^cite_note-33]
