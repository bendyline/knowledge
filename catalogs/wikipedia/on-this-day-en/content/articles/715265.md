The **Confederation of African Football**[^cite_note-2] (**CAF**) is the governing body for [association football](10568.md), [beach soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_soccer), and [futsal](188197.md) in [Africa](5334607.md). It was officially established on 8 February 1957 at the Grand Hotel in [Khartoum](17320.md), Sudan and today comprises 54 member associations. The foundation followed a decision made at the 1954 [FIFA Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Congress) in [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern), Switzerland, where Africa was formally recognised as a football confederation.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Representing the African [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_association_football) of [FIFA](11049.md), CAF organises runs and regulates [national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_team) and [club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_team) [continental competitions](#CAF_competitions) annually or biennially such as the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) and [Women's Africa Cup of Nations](47015413.md), which they control the prize money and [broadcast rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting_of_sports_events) to. CAF has been allocated 9 spots at the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) from [2026](36581929.md) with an opportunity of a 10th spots through an intercontinental play-off involving 6 teams to decide the last 2 FIFA World Cup places (46+2).

The headquarters of CAF were originally located within the offices of the Sudanese Football Association in Khartoum, before being relocated to a site near [Cairo](6293.md), Egypt, following a fire. Youssef Mohamad served as the organisation’s first general secretary, and [Abdel Aziz Abdallah Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Aziz_Abdallah_Salem) was its first [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Confederation_of_African_Football). The current president, [Patrice Motsepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Motsepe) of [South Africa](17416221.md), was first elected unopposed on 12 March 2021 in elections held in [Rabat](57400.md), Morocco.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-CAFEC-7]

## Anthem

CAF launched a competition for all African composers to create its [anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthem) without lyrics to reflect the cultural patrimony and the [music of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Africa) on 18 September 2007.[^cite_note-8]

Some African states with [limited or no international recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition) have official national teams, but none have been considered for CAF membership. Instead, they are affiliated with organisations such as [CONIFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_Independent_Football_Associations). Somaliland is not a member of CONIFA after leaving in 2010s and is a candidate to join [FIFA](11049.md) after its recognition in the coming years.

### International

Shortly after formation, CAF organised the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) (abbreviated AFCON) in 1957 and it has since become its flagship competition. Faced with undisclosed decline in popularity of local competitions and the mass exodus of homegrown footballers to Europe, Asia and the Americas in the 1990s and early 2000s, CAF launched the [African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Nations_Championship) (alternatively, though not widely used, the Championship of African Nations (CHAN)) on 11 September 2007 and began organisation two years later, to address this issue. CAF also organises qualification tournaments/competitions for the [FIFA U-20 World Cup](1168189.md) and the [FIFA U-17 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_U-17_World_Cup) for its member associations; both of which initially began on a home-and-away [two-legged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-legged_tie) basis but has since 1995 been organised in appointed host countries as respectively the [Under-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-20_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and [U-17 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-17_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).

For [women's football](191466.md) operates competitions which currently serve as qualification tournaments for the related FIFA-organised tournaments which launched at the exact same year they began formation. The flagship African women's football competition/tournament is the [Women's Africa Cup of Nations](47015413.md), which launched in 1991 as the *African Women's Championship* and was known in the mass media between 2015 and 2021 as the *Africa/African Women/Women's Cup of Nations*, which currently qualifies 4 teams to the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md). CAF also organises qualification matches for "promising future female footballers" at both the [Under-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_U-20_Women's_World_Cup_qualification) and [Under-17 levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_U-17_Women's_World_Cup_qualification), launched in 2002 and 2008 respectively, both of which crowns no champions but instead qualifies 2 teams to compete at the [FIFA U-20 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_U-20_Women's_World_Cup) and the [FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_U-17_Women's_World_Cup) respectively.

### Club

For African clubs, CAF runs the [CAF Men's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Champions_League) and [Women's Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Women's_Champions_League), the [CAF Confederation Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Confederation_Cup), the [CAF Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Super_Cup) and the African Schools Football Championship for both males and females. First held in 1964 as the *African Cup of Champions Clubs* (simply known sometimes as the **African Cup**) and rebranded in 1997 as the CAF Champions League, this football club competition currently features the champions of top-division leagues of CAF member associations and the runners-up teams of the league classifications of member associations the top 12 ranked national associations as documented by the [CAF 5-year ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_5-year_ranking) system.

A currently-former competition, the [African Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Cup_Winners'_Cup), commenced in 1975 for national cup winners of member associations and a third currently-former competition, the [CAF Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Cup), launched in 1992 for African teams who finished below the top 2 positions of the league classifications of member associations and haven't met any criteria for qualification to any CAF competition. CAF decided to merge these two competitions together to form the current second-tier CAF Confederation Cup in 2004, and it currently incorporates the participation of national cup winners from the Cup Winners' Cup, whiles maintaining the format of the participation of teams who finished 3rd in the top-division league classifications of the 12 highest-ranked member associations as documented by the CAF 5-Year Ranking system from the CAF Cup. It is also ranked below the CAF Champions League.[^cite_note-22]

The winners of the CAF Champions League play the winners of the African Cup Winners' Cup until 2004 and the CAF Confederation Cup thereafter in the [CAF Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Super_Cup) which was launched in 1993.

The [Afro-Asian Club Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asian_Club_Championship) was an annual football match jointly organised between CAF and the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC) between the winners of the CAF Champions League and the winners of the [AFC Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Champions_League) between 1987 and 1999.

The CAF Women's Champions League was announced and approved on 30 June 2020, launched on 12 September that year and began contesting the following year, i.e. 2021.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] It features women's national league and cup winners nvolving the champions of CAF's sub-confederation qualification tournaments for women's club teams.

## Sponsorship

In October 2004, South African telecommunications giant, [MTN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTN_Group), contracted a 4-year deal to sponsor CAF competitions worth US\$12.5 million, which was the biggest sponsorship deal in African sporting history at that time.[^cite_note-27]

CAF opened new sponsorship callouts when MTN's contract expired and French telecommunications giant [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_S.A.) scooped it up in July 2009, signing an 8-year comprehensive long-term undisclosed deal to sponsor CAF competitions with a value of €100 million.[^cite_note-28]

On 21 July 2016, French energy and petroleum giant, Total S.A., replaced Orange as the main sponsor with an 8-year sponsorship package from CAF for a value of €950 million[^cite_note-29] to support its competitions.[^cite_note-30] Total rebranded as [TotalEnergies](804161.md) on 28 May 2021.[^cite_note-31]

The current main CAF sponsors are:

- [1xBet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1xBet)

- [TikTok](56822861.md)

- [TotalEnergies](804161.md)

- [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_S.A.)

*Firsts*

- 1934:  [Egypt](1022953.md) first African team to qualify for the World Cup

- 1970:  [Morocco](1024865.md) first African team to draw a match in the World Cup

- 1978:  [Tunisia](1029953.md) first African team to win a match in the World Cup

- 1982:  [Algeria](1029528.md) first African team to win two matches in the World Cup

- 1986:  [Algeria](1029528.md) first African team to qualify for two consecutive World Cups

- 1986:  [Morocco](1024865.md) first African team to reach the knockout stage (round of sixteen)

- 1990:  [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) first African team to reach the knockout stage (quarter-finals)

- 1994 and 1998:  [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) first African team to top a group stage and reach the knockout stage (round of 16) in two consecutive World Cups

- 2002:  [Senegal](1017436.md) first African team to reach the knockout stage (quarter-finals) on their World Cup debut

- 2010:  [South Africa](1018598.md) first African team to host the World Cup

- 2014:  [Algeria](1029528.md) &  [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) first African teams to reach the knockout stage (round of sixteen) simultaneously in the World Cup

- 2022:  [Morocco](1024865.md) first African team to reach the knockout stage (semi-finals), taking fourth place

- 2026:  [Morocco](1024865.md) first African team to reach the quarter-finals twice
