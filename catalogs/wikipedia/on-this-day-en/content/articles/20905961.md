The **Iran and Libya Sanctions Act of 1996** (**ILSA**) was a 1996 [act of the United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Congress) that imposed [economic sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_sanction) on [firms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_entity) doing business with [Iran](14653.md) and [Libya](17633.md).[^cite_note-1] On September 20, 2004, the President signed an [executive order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order_\(United_States\)) to terminate the national emergency with respect to Libya and to end [IEEPA](2434972.md)-based economic sanctions on Libya. On September 30, 2006, the Act was renamed the **Iran Sanctions Act** (**ISA**). The Act was originally limited to five years, and has been extended several times. On September 18, 2026, the ISA was extended to 2031.[^cite_note-sec201-2]

The Act empowers the President to waive sanctions on a case-by-case basis, which is subject to renewal every six months. As of March 2008, ISA sanctions had not been enforced against any non-US company. Despite the restrictions on American investment in Iran, [FIPPA provisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment_in_Iran#FIPPA) apply to all foreign investors, and many [Iranian expatriates based in the US](2234641.md) continue to make substantial investments in Iran.[^cite_note-3]

## Background

In 1995, in response to the [Iranian nuclear program](721807.md) and Iranian [support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegations_of_Iranian_state_terrorism) for [Hezbollah](13919.md), [Hamas](13913.md), and [Palestine Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Islamic_Jihad), that are considered terrorist organizations by the US, [President](24113.md) [Bill Clinton](3356.md) had issued several [executive orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order_\(United_States\)) with respect to Iran, including [Executive Order 12957](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Executive%20Order%2012957) of March 15, 1995, banning U.S. investment in Iran's energy sector, and [Executive Order 12959](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Executive%20Order%2012959) of May 6, 1995, which banned U.S. trade with and [investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) in Iran.

## Provisions

The Act targets both U.S. and non-U.S. business making certain investments in Iran. Under ISA, unless exempted by the president, all foreign companies that provide investments over \$20 million for the development of [petroleum](23195.md) resources in Iran will be imposed two out of seven possible sanctions, by the U.S.:[^cite_note-4]

- denial of [Export-Import Bank of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export-Import_Bank_of_the_United_States) assistance

- denial of export licenses for exports to the violating company

- prohibition on loans or credits from U.S. [financial institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_institution) of over \$10 million in any 12-month period

- prohibition on designation as a primary dealer for U.S. government debt instruments

- prohibition on serving as an agent of the United States or as a repository for U.S. government funds

- denial of U.S. government [procurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurement) opportunities (consistent with [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) obligations), and

- a ban on all or some imports of the violating company.

## Renewal and expiration

ILSA included a five-year [sunset clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_clause) and was to expire on August 5, 2001. In the debate in the U.S. Congress on whether ILSA should expire, some legislators argued sanctions hindered bilateral relations, and others argued they would be seen as a concession on an effective program. ILSA was renewed by the Congress and signed by President [George W. Bush](3414021.md).[^cite_note-rep-5] ILSA was renewed for another five years,[^cite_note-6] until August 2006.

In 2005, the Iran Freedom Support bill was introduced in both [houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambers_of_parliament) to extend the provisions of ILSA indefinitely and to impose a time limit for the administration to determine whether an investment violates ILSA. The [House of Representatives](19468510.md) legislation, H.R. 282 was introduced on January 6, 2005, was reported by [committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_committee) on March 15, 2006, and passed the House on April 26, 2006, by a vote of 397–21, with 14 not voting. The companion Senate legislation, S. 333 was introduced on February 9, 2005, was referred to the [Foreign Relations Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Foreign_Relations). The bill was not reported by the committee and died.

On July 25, 2006, bill H.R. 5877, to extend ILSA until September 29, 2006, was introduced in the House, and passed the next day by [voice vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_vote). It was passed by the Senate by [unanimous consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unanimous_consent) on July 31, and was signed into law by [President](24113.md) [George W. Bush](3414021.md) on August 4, 2006.

On September 30, 2006, ILSA was renamed the Iran Sanctions Act (ISA), as it no longer applied to Libya, and extended until December 31, 2011. The [Iran Freedom and Support Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_Freedom_and_Support_Act) passed on the same day on September 30, 2006. The [Comprehensive Iran Sanctions, Accountability, and Divestment Act of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Iran_Sanctions,_Accountability,_and_Divestment_Act_of_2010) amended the Iran Sanctions Act to expand the president's ability to punish companies aiding Iran's petroleum sector.

On December 1, 2016, ISA was extended for a further ten years.[^cite_note-2016extension-7]

On September 18, 2026, the ISA was extended for another five years, to 2031.[^cite_note-sec201-2]
