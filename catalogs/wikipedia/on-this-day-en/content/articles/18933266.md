**The Boeing Company** ([/ˈboʊɪŋ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*BO-ing*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is an American [multinational corporation](214491.md) that designs and manufactures [airplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airplane), [rotorcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotorcraft), [rockets](26301.md), [satellites](27683.md), and [missiles](19594.md) worldwide.[^cite_note-6] The company also provides leasing and product support services. Boeing is among the largest global [aerospace manufacturers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_manufacturer); it is the fourth-largest [defense contractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_defense_contractors) in the world based on 2022 revenue[^cite_note-7] and is the largest exporter in the United States by dollar value.[^cite_note-8] Boeing was founded in 1916 by [William E. Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_E._Boeing) in [Seattle](11388236.md), Washington.[^cite_note-Boeing-9] The present corporation is the result of the Boeing acquisition of [McDonnell Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas) on August 1, 1997.

The Boeing Company's corporate headquarters is located in the [Crystal City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_City,_Virginia) neighborhood of [Arlington County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia), near [Washington, DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_DC).[^cite_note-10] The company is organized into three primary divisions: [Boeing Commercial Airplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Commercial_Airplanes) (BCA), [Boeing Defense, Space & Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Defense,_Space_&_Security) (BDS), and [Boeing Global Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Global_Services) (BGS). In 2021, Boeing recorded \$62.3 billion in sales.[^cite_note-11] Boeing is ranked 54th on the [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) list (2020),[^cite_note-12] and ranked 121st on the [*Fortune* Global 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500) list (2020).[^cite_note-13]

## History

Main article: [History of Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Boeing)

### Origins

The Boeing Company started in 1916, when American lumber industrialist [William E. Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_E._Boeing) founded Pacific Aero Products Company in Seattle, Washington. Shortly before doing so, he and Conrad Westervelt created the ["B\&W" seaplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Model_1).[^cite_note-14] In 1917, the organization was renamed Boeing Airplane Company, with William Boeing forming Boeing Airplane & Transport Corporation in 1928.[^cite_note-15] In 1929, the company was renamed [United Aircraft and Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Aircraft_and_Transport_Corporation), followed by the acquisition of several aircraft makers such as [Avion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avion_Corporation), Chance [Vought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought), [Sikorsky Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_Aircraft), [Stearman Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stearman_Aircraft), [Pratt & Whitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney), and [Hamilton Metalplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Metalplane_Company).[^cite_note-Britannica_Boe-3]

In 1931, the group merged its four smaller airlines into [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines). In 1934, aircraft manufacturing was required to be separate from air transportation.[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-16] Therefore, Boeing Airplane Company became one of three major groups to arise from the dissolution of United Aircraft and Transport; the other two entities were [United Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Aircraft) (later [United Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Technologies)) and United Airlines.[^cite_note-Britannica_Boe-3][^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-16]

In 1960, the company bought [Vertol Aircraft Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piasecki_Helicopter), which at the time, was the biggest independent manufacturer of [helicopters](8286923.md).[^cite_note-17] During the 1960s and 1970s, the company diversified into industries such as outer space travel, marine craft, agriculture, energy production and transit systems.[^cite_note-Britannica_Boe-3]

### Sea Launch

In 1995, Boeing partnered with Russian, Ukrainian, and Anglo-Norwegian organizations to create [Sea Launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Launch), a company providing commercial launch services sending satellites to geostationary orbit from floating platforms.[^cite_note-18] In 2000, Boeing acquired the satellite segment of [Hughes Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Electronics).[^cite_note-Britannica_Boe-3][^cite_note-19]

### Merger with McDonnell Douglas

In December 1996, Boeing announced its intention to merge with [McDonnell Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas), which, following regulatory approval, was completed on August 1, 1997.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] The delay was caused by objections from the [European Commission](9974.md), which ultimately placed three conditions on the merger: exclusivity agreements with three US airlines would be terminated, separate accounts would be maintained for the McDonnell-Douglas civil aircraft business, and some defense patents were to be made available to competitors.[^cite_note-22] In 2020, *[Quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz_\(publication\))* reported that after the merger there was a "clash of corporate cultures, where Boeing's engineers and McDonnell Douglas's bean-counters went head-to-head", which the latter won, and that this may have contributed to the events leading up to the 737 MAX crash crisis.[^cite_note-frost-23]

### Corporate headquarters moves

Boeing's corporate headquarters moved from [Seattle](11388236.md) to [Chicago](6886.md) in 2001.[^cite_note-24] In 2018, the company opened its first factory in Europe at [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield), [United Kingdom](31717.md), reinforced by a research partnership with the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield).[^cite_note-25]

In May 2020, the company cut over 12,000 jobs due to the drop in air travel during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) with plans for a total 10% cut of its workforce or approximately 16,000 positions.[^cite_note-26] In July 2020, Boeing reported a loss of \$2.4 billion as a result of the pandemic and the [Boeing 737 MAX groundings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX_groundings), and that it was in response planning to make more job and production cuts.[^cite_note-27] On August 18, 2020, CEO Dave Calhoun announced further job cuts;[^cite_note-28] on October 28, 2020, nearly 30,000 employees were laid off, as the airplane manufacturer was increasingly losing money due to the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-29]

In May 2022, Boeing announced plans to transfer its global headquarters from Chicago to [Arlington, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Virginia), a suburb of Washington, D.C. The company said that this decision was made in part to concentrate on its defense work with "proximity to our customers and stakeholders".[^cite_note-Boe_HQ_move-30][^cite_note-31] After the January 2024 [Alaska Airlines Flight 1282](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Airlines_Flight_1282) and other incidents, one shareholder proposed relocating the corporate headquarters back to the Seattle area in hopes of getting engineering and quality control teams on-site access to key decision-makers. Boeing's board soundly dismissed the attempt.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In February 2023, Boeing announced plans for laying off approximately 2,000 of its workers from finances and human resources.[^cite_note-34]

In May 2023, Boeing acquired autonomous [eVTOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EVTOL) air taxi startup [Wisk Aero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisk_Aero).[^cite_note-acq-35]

### Spirit Aerosystems acquisition

In June 2024, Boeing agreed to re-acquire [Spirit AeroSystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_AeroSystems), its longtime supplier of airplane parts, which had been established in 2005 when Boeing [spun-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off) its Wichita division to an investment firm. The deal was initially discussed in March of the same year before being closed on June 30 at \$4.7 billion.[^cite_note-36]

In December 2025, Boeing was asked by the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission) to divest Spirit assets to resolve antitrust concerns, including the businesses that supply [Airbus](26220236.md) and Malaysian aerospace company Subang. The FTC said Airbus and Subang would buy those respective businesses. EU regulators said this proposed remedy fully addressed their concerns.[^cite_note-37]

### Labor strike

See also: [2024 Boeing machinists' strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Boeing_machinists'_strike)

On September 12, 2024, a vote was held among Boeing machinist workers who are also members of the [International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Machinists_and_Aerospace_Workers) (IAM) labor union, with 94.6% of participating members rejected a contract offer that the union's bargaining committee had endorsed, with 96% voting to strike.[^cite_note-firststrikesince2008-38] At 12\:01 am on September 13, Boeing workers went on strike for the first time since 2008.[^cite_note-firststrikesince2008-38][^cite_note-39]

On October 12, 2024, the company announced plans to cut 17,000 jobs, about 10% of its global workforce, "to align with our financial reality". It would also delay the first deliveries of its 777X airliner by a year and recorded \$5 billion in losses in the third quarter of the year.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] On October 28, Boeing initiated a significant share sale, valued at nearly \$19 billion, to address cash-flow issues and avoid a potential downgrade to [junk status](1194146.md#Corporate_credit_ratings).[^cite_note-42]

On November 1, 2024, the IAM endorsed an improved contract offer which would see a 38% pay rise over four years, a \$12,000 ratification bonus, and the reinstatement of an annual bonus scheme.[^cite_note-43] On November 5, 2024, Boeing workers accepted the pay deal, ending a seven-week-long walk out.[^cite_note-44]

### Latest history

On May 20, 2026, it was announced that [China](5405.md) will purchase 200 planes from Boeing following a bilateral meeting between China and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-\:0-45][^cite_note-46] Boeing's chief executive, [Kelly Ortberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Ortberg), said: "We \[Boeing] had a very successful trip to China... this included an initial commitment for 200 aircraft."[^cite_note-\:0-45]

## Divisions



Assembly of a [737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) in the [Boeing Renton Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Renton_Factory)

The company's three divisions are Commercial Airplanes; Defense, Space & Security; and Global Services.[^cite_note-Brief-47]

- [Boeing Commercial Airplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Commercial_Airplanes) (BCA) builds commercial aircraft including the [737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737), [767](4165.md), [777](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777), and [787](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner) along with freighter and [business jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Business_Jet) variants of most. The division employs nearly 35,000 people, many working at the company's manufacturing facilities in [Everett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Everett_Factory) and [Renton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Renton_Factory), Washington (outside of [Seattle](11388236.md)), and [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_South_Carolina).

- [Boeing Defense, Space & Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Defense,_Space_&_Security) (BDS) builds military airplanes, rotorcraft, and missiles, as well as space systems for both commercial and military customers, including satellites, spacecraft, and rockets.

- [Boeing Global Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Global_Services) (BGS) provides aftermarket support, such as maintenance and upgrades, to customers who purchase equipment from BCA, BDS, or other manufacturers.

### Boeing 737 MAX crashes and groundings

Main article: [Boeing 737 MAX groundings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX_groundings)

In [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Air_Flight_610) and [2019](60187903.md), two [Boeing 737 MAX](32924090.md) narrow-body passenger airplanes crashed, leaving 346 people dead and no survivors. In response, aviation regulators and airlines around the world [grounded all 737 MAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX_groundings) airliners.[^cite_note-48] A total of 387 aircraft were grounded.[^cite_note-49] Boeing's reputation, business, and financial rating suffered after the groundings, as Boeing's strategy, governance, and focus on profits and cost efficiency were questioned.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] In 2022, [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) released an exposé, *[Downfall: The Case Against Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downfall:_The_Case_Against_Boeing)*, claiming Boeing's corporate merger with McDonnell Douglas led to the crashes through a disintegration of workplace [morale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morale).[^cite_note-Downfall-53][^cite_note-Bramesco-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

In June 2020, the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) found several 737 MAX defects that Boeing deferred to fix, in violation of [regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Regulations).[^cite_note-58] In September 2020, the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md) concluded its own investigation and cited numerous instances where Boeing dismissed employee concerns with a 737 MAX flight stabilizing feature ([MCAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maneuvering_Characteristics_Augmentation_System)) that caused the two fatal accidents, prioritized deadline and budget constraints over [safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_safety), and lacked transparency in disclosing essential information to the FAA. It further found that the assumption that [simulator training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulator_training_for_the_Boeing_MAX_737) would not be necessary had "diminished safety, minimized the value of [pilot training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_training), and inhibited technical design improvements".[^cite_note-59] On January 7, 2021, Boeing [settled](556323.md) to pay over \$2.5 billion after being charged with fraud over the company's hiding of information from the safety regulators: a [criminal monetary penalty](146699.md) of \$243.6 million, \$1.77 billion of damages to airline customers, and a \$500 million crash-victim beneficiaries fund.[^cite_note-60]

In September 2022, Boeing was ordered to pay a further \$200 million over charges of misleading investors about safety issues related to these crashes.[^cite_note-61] In March 2023, Boeing disputed in court filings that the victims of [Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302](60187903.md) (2019 crash) experienced any pain and suffering in the final six minutes as the plane was nosediving into the ground, arguing that death at an impact at the "speed of sound" would have been too quick to be painful. Boeing's claim was described as "preposterous" by *[HuffPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost)*:[^cite_note-62]

> Passengers aboard the plane, the plaintiffs argued in court, "undeniably suffered horrific emotional distress, pain and suffering, and physical impact/injury while they endured extreme G-forces, braced for impact, knew the airplane was malfunctioning, and ultimately plummeted nose-down to the ground at terrifying speed".

While the investigations into the crashes of the 737 MAX were proceeding, the [Boeing 777X](41065722.md), the company's largest capacity twin jet and the largest ever built, made its maiden flight on January 25, 2020,[^cite_note-63] but also experienced problems. Following an incident during [flight testing](41065722.md#Testing) in 2021, the estimated first delivery of the aircraft was delayed until 2024.[^cite_note-64] After [further technical problems](41065722.md#2022) were discovered in the aircraft in 2022, the release was delayed again until 2025, six years after the original date.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

### Alaska Airlines Flight 1282

Main article: [Alaska Airlines Flight 1282](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Airlines_Flight_1282)

N704AL, the Alaska Airlines aircraft involved

N704AL, the Alaska Airlines aircraft involved

On January 5, 2024, on [Alaska Airlines Flight 1282](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Airlines_Flight_1282), a door plug blowout[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] occurred on a [737 MAX 9](32924090.md#737_MAX_9) jetliner after the plane had reached just over 16,000 feet, leaving a [door-sized hole in the fuselage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX_groundings#2024) and the aircraft made an emergency landing at [Portland International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_International_Airport) successfully with several people on board injured, although all had subsequently been "medically cleared".[^cite_note-69] The FAA mandated immediate inspections of all 737 MAX 9s fitted with door plugs, [thereby grounding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Airlines_Flight_1282#Aftermath) 171 aircraft.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) found loose bolts on jets grounded by the FAA, raising questions about possible systemic problems with the Boeing 737 MAX 9.[^cite_note-73] The FAA announced on January 12 that it was expanding its scrutiny of Boeing, with a production audit of the 737 MAX 9.[^cite_note-74] On February 6, the [National Transportation Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board) released a preliminary report indicating that four bolts used to secure the panel had been removed, and appeared not to have been replaced, at Boeing's factory in [Renton, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renton,_Washington).[^cite_note-75]

In March 2024, the Justice Department opened a criminal investigation into the Alaska Airlines blowout.[^cite_note-76] In March 2024, CEO Dave Calhoun and board chairman Larry Kellner both announced they would be stepping down from their positions.[^cite_note-77]

### Potential replacement for the 737 MAX

In September 2025, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported Boeing was planning a new single-aisle airplane to succeed the 737 MAX in an effort to recover market share lost to [Airbus](26220236.md) as a result of safety and quality issues.[^cite_note-78]

## Environmental record

In 2006, the [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) Center for Environmental Risk Reduction released a study showing that Boeing's [Santa Susana Field Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Field_Laboratory), a site that was a former Rocketdyne test and development site in the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills) of eastern [Ventura County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County) in [Southern California](62520.md), had been contaminated by Rocketdyne with [toxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxic) and [radioactive waste](37257.md). Boeing agreed to a cleanup agreement with the EPA in 2017.[^cite_note-79] Clean-up studies and lawsuits are in progress.[^cite_note-80]

Boeing reported that each one of the jets it delivered in 2020 will add an average of one million tons of carbon dioxide to the Earth's atmosphere.[^cite_note-81] It was also estimated that the planes sold in 2020 will produce about 158 million tonnes of CO2.[^cite_note-Hepher_et_al._2021-82]

Climate change is impacting Boeing in the form of increased risks associated with extreme weather events, supply chain disruptions, and tough climate regulations. Boeing is forced to deal with increased operational and financial pressures associated with reducing carbon emissions and finding sustainable solutions. As such, Boeing is committed to making sustainable aviation fuel solutions suitable for its entire fleet of planes by 2030 and aims to achieve net-zero carbon emissions by 2050.[^cite_note-83]

On July 19, 2022, Boeing announced a renewed partnership with Mitsubishi to produce [carbon-neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-neutral) and sustainable solutions.[^cite_note-84]

### Jet biofuels

Main articles: [Aviation biofuel](17903761.md) and [Algae fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_fuel)



[Boeing Everett Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Everett_Factory), the assembly facility for most of the company's [wide-body aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-body_aircraft)

The airline industry is responsible for about 11% of [greenhouse gases](21350772.md) emitted by the U.S. transportation sector.[^cite_note-boe_energy-85] Aviation's share of the greenhouse gas emissions was poised to grow, as air travel increases and ground vehicles use more alternative fuels like [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel) and [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel).[^cite_note-boe_energy-85] Boeing estimates that [biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel) could reduce flight-related greenhouse-gas emissions by 60 to 80%.[^cite_note-boe_energy-85] The solution blends algae fuels with existing [jet fuel](1205435.md).[^cite_note-boe_energy-85]

Boeing executives said the company was collaborating with Brazilian biofuels maker [Tecbio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecbio), [Aquaflow Bionomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaflow_Bionomic) of New Zealand, and other fuel developers around the world. As of 2007, Boeing had tested six fuels from these companies, and expected to test 20 fuels "by the time we're done evaluating them".[^cite_note-boe_energy-85] Boeing also joined other aviation-related members in the [Algal Biomass Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algal_Biomass_Organization) (ABO) in June 2008.[^cite_note-86]

[Air New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_New_Zealand) and Boeing are researching the [jatropha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatropha) plant to see if it is a sustainable alternative to conventional fuel.[^cite_note-87] A two-hour test flight using a 50–50 mixture of the new biofuel with [Jet A-1](1205435.md) in a Rolls-Royce RB-211 engine of a 747–400 was completed on December 30, 2008.[^cite_note-Jha-2008-88] The engine was then removed to be studied to identify any differences between the Jatropha blend and regular Jet A1. No effects on performances were found.[^cite_note-Jha-2008-88]

## Political contributions, federal contracts, advocacy, criticism, sanctions



Boeing CEO [Dennis Muilenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Muilenburg) and US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) at the [787-10 Dreamliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner) rollout ceremony in 2017

In 2007 and 2008, the company benefited from over 10 billion dollars in [USD](18717338.md) of long-term loan guarantees, helping finance the purchase of their commercial aircraft in countries including [Brazil](3383.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Ireland](147575.md), and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), from the [Export-Import Bank of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export-Import_Bank_of_the_United_States), some 65% of the total loan guarantees the bank made in that period.[^cite_note-89]

In 2008 and 2009, Boeing was second on the list of [Top 100 US Federal Contractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_100_US_Federal_Contractors), with contracts totaling US\$22 billion and US\$23 billion respectively.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] Between 1995 and early 2021, the company agreed to pay US\$4.3 billion to settle 84 instances of misconduct, including US\$615 million in 2006 in relation to illegal hiring of government officials and improper use of proprietary information.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

Boeing's spent US\$16.9 million on [lobbying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbying_in_the_United_States) expenditures in 2009.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] In the [2008 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election), [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) "was by far the biggest recipient of campaign contributions from Boeing employees and executives, hauling in US\$197,000 – five times as much as [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain), and more than the top eight Republicans combined".[^cite_note-Carney-97]

Boeing has a [corporate citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_citizenship) program centered on charitable contributions in five areas: education, health, [human services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_services), [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment), [the arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_arts), culture, and [civic engagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civics).[^cite_note-98] In February 2012, Boeing Global Corporate Citizenship partnered with the [Insight Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insight_Labs) to develop a new model for [foundations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_\(non-profit\)) to more effectively lead the sectors they serve.[^cite_note-99]

The company is a member of the [U.S. Global Leadership Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Global_Leadership_Coalition), a Washington D.C.–based coalition of more than 400 major companies and NGOs that advocate a larger International Affairs Budget, which funds American diplomatic and development efforts abroad.[^cite_note-100] A series of [U.S. diplomatic cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_diplomatic_cables_release) show how U.S. diplomats and senior politicians intervene on behalf of Boeing to help boost the company's sales.[^cite_note-101] In [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s administrations, [Paolo Zampolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Zampolli) has styled himself as "Boeing’s number-two salesperson in the world, right after the president", which the company did not confirm, and claimed that, when foreign governments want access to Trump, he tells them "If you want to make the president happy, buy Boeing.”[^cite_note-102]

Boeing secured the highest-ever [tax breaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_break) at the state level in 2013.[^cite_note-103]

In March 2025, Boeing was awarded a contract to build the U.S. Air Force's most sophisticated fighter, known as [Next Generation Air Dominance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_Air_Dominance), in a contract worth more than \$20 billion.[^cite_note-104]

### Criticism

In December 2011, the non-partisan organization [Public Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Campaign#Reports) criticized Boeing for spending US\$52.29 million on [lobbying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbying) and [not paying taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_avoidance) from 2008 to 2010, instead getting US\$178 million in [tax rebates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_rebate), despite making a profit of US\$9.7 billion, laying off 14,862 workers since 2008, and increasing [executive pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_pay) by 31% to US\$41.9 million in 2010 for its top five executives.[^cite_note-105]

Boeing has been accused of unethical practices (in violation of the [Procurement Integrity Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurement_Integrity_Act)) while attempting to submit a revised bid to [NASA](18426568.md) for their lunar landing project.[^cite_note-106]

#### War profiteering



The [F-15S/SA](495724.md) jetfighters are one of the major combatants that Boeing has sold to the [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). The aircraft are heavily utilized in the [Saudi-led intervention in the Yemeni civil war](46230181.md).

The firm has been criticized for [supplying and profiting from wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_profiteering), including [the war in Yemen](46230181.md) where its missiles were found to be used for indiscriminate attacks, killing many civilians.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] In 2017 Boeing signed a deal with [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) which included Saudi Arabia buying military aircraft and guided missile systems.[^cite_note-109] Research has estimated Boeing made between \$50 billion to \$100 billion in revenue via Israeli weapons contracts from 2009 through 2022, in the years leading up to the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-110]

In 2023, it was reported that Boeing sent 1,000 small diameter "smart" bombs for the first week of [Israeli air attacks on Gaza](76139149.md), which were shipped from a [US Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Air_Force) base by [Israeli Air Force](177619.md).[^cite_note-111] During the Gaza war, Boeing's stock prices soared due to additional Israeli weapons contracts,[^cite_note-112] while mass demonstrations sought to interrupt defense supplier summits and block shipments of weapons for the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) at Boeing facilities in [St. Charles, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Charles,_Missouri),[^cite_note-113] [Tukwila, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukwila,_Washington),[^cite_note-114] and [Gresham, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham,_Oregon),[^cite_note-Foster-115] due to the mass violations of [International humanitarian law](1093604.md) committed by Israel.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] Students at [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University),[^cite_note-119] [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington),[^cite_note-120] [Saint Louis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis_University_\(United_States\)), [University of Missouri–St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri%E2%80%93St._Louis), and [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis)[^cite_note-121] called for their institutions to break partnerships with Boeing.

In 2024, students on [hunger strike](222292.md) at [Brown University](4157.md) named Boeing among the list of corporations to divest from.[^cite_note-122] Five protestors, in opposition to Boeing sales to Israel, were arrested on [felony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felony) charges after blocking entrances to a Boeing facility in [Heath, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath,_Ohio).[^cite_note-123] The [student union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students'_union) at [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis) passed a resolution calling on the university to divest from Boeing.[^cite_note-124]

Israel has used the Boeing manufactured [GBU-39 Small Diameter Bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-39_Small_Diameter_Bomb) during the Gaza war, including in bombings of a [Rafah refugee tent camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_al-Sultan_attack),[^cite_note-125] the [Al-Sardi school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Sardi_school_attack),[^cite_note-Wapo_Rafah-126] and the [Al-Tabaeen school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tabaeen_school_attack).[^cite_note-127]

The decision comes after the US announced an \$11.1 billion arms sale to [Taiwan](25734.md), the largest arms deal in history.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

Between 2010 and 2018, Boeing increased its operating [cash flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_flow) from \$3 to \$15.3 billion, sustaining its [share](867877.md) price, by negotiating [advance payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advance_payment) from customers and delaying payments to its suppliers. This strategy is sustainable only as long as orders are good and delivery rates are increasing.[^cite_note-156]

From 2013 to 2019, Boeing spent over \$60 billion on dividends and [stock buybacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_repurchase), twice as much as the development costs of the 787.[^cite_note-157]

In 2020, Boeing's second quarter revenue was \$11.8 billion as a result of the pandemic slump. Due to higher sales in other divisions and an influx in deliveries of commercial jetliners in 2021, second quarter revenue increased by 44%, reaching nearly \$17 billion.[^cite_note-158]

Revenues decreased 15 percent to \$16.9 billion in the second quarter of 2024, compared to the same time period in 2023.[^cite_note-159] The company's operating loss amounted to \$1.39 billion and its net loss to \$1.43 billion, while plane deliveries fell to 92 (from 136 in 2023).

In 2024, Boeing delivered just 348 aircraft to its customers, its lowest output since the COVID-19 pandemic. Boeing ended the year with a backlog of 5,595 unfilled orders.[^cite_note-160]

In 2025, Boeing outpaced its main rival Airbus.

## Corporate governance

In 2022, [Rory Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rory_Kennedy) made a documentary film, *[Downfall: The Case Against Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downfall:_The_Case_Against_Boeing)*, streamed by [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix).[^cite_note-Downfall-53] She said about the 21st-century history of Boeing "There were many decades when Boeing did extraordinary things by focusing on excellence and safety and ingenuity. Those three virtues were seen as the key to profit. It could work, and beautifully. And then they were taken over by a group that decided Wall Street was the end-all, be-all."[^cite_note-Bramesco-54]

On May 5, 2022, Boeing announced that it would be moving its headquarters from Chicago to Arlington, Virginia in the Washington, D.C. metropolitan area. Additionally, it plans to add a research and technology center in [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia).[^cite_note-Boe_HQ_move-30]

In July 2024, it announced a new CEO, [Kelly Ortberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Ortberg).[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] On August 8, 2024, he met with FAA Administrator Michael Whitaker to discuss the company's future direction. Ortberg has communicated his commitment to reinforcing Boeing's position as an industry leader and has outlined his vision for the company's continued success.[^cite_note-166]
