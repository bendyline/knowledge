**Naples** ([/ˈneɪpəlz/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*NAY-pəlz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Italian](14708.md): *Napoli* [\[ˈnaːpoli\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Italian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7f/It-Napoli.ogg/It-Napoli.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7f/It-Napoli.ogg/It-Napoli.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Neapolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_language): *Napule* [\[ˈnɑːpələ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Neapolitan))[^cite_note-4] is the [regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) capital of [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania), [southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy) [Italy](14532.md). With a population of 905,050 within the city's administrative limits as of 2026, it is the largest city in [southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy) and the [third-largest city in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Italy) after [Rome](25458.md) and [Milan](36511.md),[^cite_note-population-3][^cite_note-5] while [its province-level municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Naples) is the third most populous [metropolitan city in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_cities_of_Italy) with a population of 2,954,847 residents[^cite_note-population-3] – or the second largest according to [OECD](33853117.md) data.[^cite_note-6] [Its metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_metropolitan_area), the [seventh most populous in the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_the_European_Union),[^cite_note-7] has a population of approximately 4.4 million and stretches beyond the boundaries of the city proper for approximately 30 kilometres (20 miles).[^cite_note-demog-8] Naples also plays a key role in international [diplomacy](28165911.md), being home to [NATO](21133.md)'s [Allied Joint Force Command Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Joint_Force_Command_Naples)[^cite_note-9] and the [Parliamentary Assembly of the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Mediterranean).

Founded by the [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_colonisation) in the [first millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_millennium_BC) BC, Naples is one of the oldest continuously inhabited [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) in the world. In the [eighth century BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_century_BC), a colony known as Parthenope ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Παρθενόπη) was established on the [Pizzofalcone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzofalcone) hill. In the sixth century BC, it was refounded as Neápolis.[^cite_note-10] The city was an important part of [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia), played a major role in the merging of Greek and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) society, and has been a significant international cultural centre ever since with particular reference to the development of the arts.[^cite_note-11]

Naples served as the capital of the [Duchy of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Naples) (661–1139), subsequently as the capital of the [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples) (1282–1816), and finally as the capital of the [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies) — until the [unification of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy) in 1861. Naples is also considered a capital of the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque), beginning with the artist [Caravaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaggio)'s career in the 17th century and the artistic revolution he inspired.[^cite_note-12] It was also an important centre of [humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_humanism) and [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The city has long been a global point of reference for classical music and opera through the [Neapolitan School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_School).[^cite_note-15]

Between 1925 and 1936, Naples was expanded and upgraded by the [Fascist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy). During the later years of [World War II](32927.md), it sustained [severe damage from Allied bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Naples_in_World_War_II) as they invaded the peninsula. The [Four Days of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Days_of_Naples) (Italian: Quattro giornate di Napoli) was an uprising against Nazi German occupation forces from 27 September to 30 September 1943, immediately prior to the arrival of Allied forces on 1 October during World War II. The city underwent extensive reconstruction work after the war.[^cite_note-wii-16]

Since the late 20th century, Naples has had significant economic growth, helped by the construction of the [Centro direzionale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_direzionale_di_Napoli) business district and an advanced transportation network, which includes the [Alta Velocità](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treno_Alta_Velocit%C3%A0) high-speed rail link to Rome and [Salerno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salerno) and an expanded [subway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_Metro) and [suburban railway service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_metropolitan_railway_service). Naples is the third-largest urban economy in Italy by GDP, after Milan and Rome.[^cite_note-17] The [Port of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Naples) is one of the most important in Europe.

Naples's historic city centre has been designated as a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md). A wide range of culturally and historically significant sites are nearby, including the [Royal Palace of Caserta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Caserta) and the Roman ruins of [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) and [Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum). Naples is one of the world's cities with the highest density of cultural, artistic, and monumental resources, described by the BBC as "the Italian city with too much history to handle."[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

The city has long been a global point of reference for [classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music) and opera through the [Neapolitan School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_School), contributing to the development of [opera buffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_buffa)[^cite_note-20] and the modern [conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatory_of_music) system.[^cite_note-21] Naples has also fostered a distinctive theatrical tradition and a renowned musical heritage, including the [Neapolitan song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_song) (Canzone napoletana), which achieved worldwide popularity. In the visual arts, the city has given rise to original artistic movements such as the [Neapolitan Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_Renaissance)[^cite_note-22] and [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_Baroque?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-23] [Caravaggism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaggisti),[^cite_note-24] the [School of Posillipo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Posillipo),[^cite_note-25] the [School of Resina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Resina) and [Neapolitan Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_Liberty?action=edit\&redlink=1)[^cite_note-26] ([Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau)), as well as internationally significant applied arts, including [Capodimonte porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capodimonte_porcelain)[^cite_note-27] and the [Neapolitan nativity scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_nativity_scene) ("presepe").[^cite_note-28]

It is also widely recognised for its distinctive [culinary tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_cuisine),[^cite_note-29] which includes foods that have become global cultural icons, most notably [pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_pizza).[^cite_note-30] In recognition of its cultural significance, the art of Neapolitan *[pizzaiolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizzaiolo)* has been inscribed on UNESCO's [Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_elements).[^cite_note-31]

Naples is home to some of the oldest and most prestigious educational institutions in Europe. It hosts the [University of Naples Federico II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II), founded in 1224 and regarded as the oldest state-funded and secular university in the world,[^cite_note-32] as well as the [University of Naples "L'Orientale"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_%22L'Orientale%22), the oldest institution in Europe dedicated to Oriental and [Sinological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinology) studies.[^cite_note-33] The city is also home to the [Nunziatella Military School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunziatella_Military_School), one of the oldest and most prestigious military academies in the world.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

## History

Main article: [History of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Naples)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Naples history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Naples_history).

### Greek birth and Roman acquisition

See also: [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia) and [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome)



Mount Echia, the place where the polis of [Parthenope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Naples#Greek_birth,_Roman_acquisition) arose



The Columns of the [Temple of Castor and Pollux](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category\:Temple%20of%20Dioscuri%20\(Naples\)) incorporated into the facade of [San Paolo Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Paolo_Maggiore,_Naples)

Naples has been inhabited since the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) period.[^cite_note-36] In the second millennium BC, a first [Mycenaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece) settlement arose not far from the geographical position of the future city of Parthenope.[^cite_note-37]

Sailors from the Greek island of [Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes) established probably a small commercial port called [Parthenope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Naples#Greek_birth,_Roman_acquisition) (Παρθενόπη, meaning "Pure Eyes", a Siren in [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology)) on the [island of Megaride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_dell'Ovo) in the ninth century BC.[^cite_note-38] By the eighth century BC, the settlement was expanded by [Cumaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumae_\(Italy\)), as evidenced by the archaeological findings, to include Monte Echia.[^cite_note-39] In the sixth century BC the city was refounded as Neápolis (Νεάπολις), eventually becoming one of the foremost cities of [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia).[^cite_note-40]

The city grew rapidly due to the influence of the powerful Greek [city-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state) of [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily),[^cite_note-Greek_Naples-41] and became an ally of the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) against [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage). During the [Samnite Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samnite_Wars), the city, now a bustling centre of trade, was [captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Neapolis) by the [Samnites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samnium);[^cite_note-42] however, the Romans soon captured the city from them and made it a [Roman colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonies_in_antiquity).[^cite_note-rome-43] During the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars), the strong walls surrounding Neápolis repelled the invading forces of the Carthaginian general [Hannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal).[^cite_note-rome-43]

The Romans greatly respected Naples as a paragon of [Hellenistic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_civilization). During the Roman era, the people of Naples maintained their [Greek language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) and customs. At the same time, the city was expanded with elegant Roman [villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa), [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_aqueduct), and [public baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermae). Landmarks such as the [Temple of Dioscures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castor_and_Pollux) were built, and many emperors chose to holiday in the city, including [Claudius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius) and [Tiberius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberius).[^cite_note-rome-43] [Virgil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil), the author of Rome's [national epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_epic), the *[Aeneid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeneid)*, received part of his education in the city, and later resided in its environs.

It was during this period that Christianity first arrived in Naples. According to tradition, the basilica [San Pietro ad Aram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pietro_ad_Aram) stands on the site where [Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter) preached and baptized the first Neapolitan converts to Christianity. [Januarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Januarius), who would become Naples's [patron saint](68055.md), was [martyred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr) there in the fourth century AD.[^cite_note-catholi-44] The last emperor of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire), [Romulus Augustulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_Augustulus), was exiled to Naples by the Germanic king [Odoacer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoacer) in the fifth century AD.

### Duchy of Naples

Main articles: [Duchy of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Naples) and [List of Dukes of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dukes_of_Naples)



The [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_War_\(535%E2%80%93554\)) [Battle of Mons Lactarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mons_Lactarius) on [Vesuvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vesuvius), painted by [Alexander Zick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zick)

Following the decline of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire), Naples was captured by the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths), a [Germanic people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples), and incorporated into the [Ostrogothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom).[^cite_note-ostrogoths-45] However, General [Belisarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belisarius) of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) recaptured Naples after [a short siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Naples_\(536\)) in 536, after entering the city via an aqueduct.[^cite_note-46]

In 543, during the [Gothic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_War_\(535%E2%80%93554\)), [Totila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totila) briefly [took the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Naples_\(542%E2%80%93543\)) for the Ostrogoths, but the Byzantines seized control of the area following the [Battle of Mons Lactarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mons_Lactarius) on the slopes of [Vesuvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vesuvius).[^cite_note-ostrogoths-45] Naples was expected to keep in contact with the [Exarchate of Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exarchate_of_Ravenna), which was the centre of Byzantine power on the [Italian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peninsula).[^cite_note-byz-47]

After the [exarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exarch) fell, a [Duchy of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Naples) was created. Although Naples's [Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world) culture endured, it eventually switched allegiance from [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) to Rome under Duke [Stephen II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_II_of_Naples), putting it under [papal](23056.md) [suzerainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerainty) by 763.[^cite_note-byz-47]

The years between 818 and 832 saw tumultuous relations with the [Byzantine Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Byzantine_emperors), with numerous local pretenders feuding for possession of the ducal throne.[^cite_note-duchy-48] [Theoctistus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoctistus_of_Naples) was appointed without imperial approval; his appointment was later revoked and [Theodore II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dukes_of_Naples) took his place. However, the disgruntled general populace chased him from the city and elected [Stephen III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_III_of_Naples) instead, a man who minted coins with his initials rather than those of the Byzantine Emperor. Naples gained complete independence by the early ninth century.[^cite_note-duchy-48] Naples allied with the Muslim [Saracens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saracens) in 836 and asked for their support to repel the siege of [Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) troops coming from the neighbouring [Duchy of Benevento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Benevento). However, during the 850s, Muslim general [Muhammad I Abu 'l-Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_I_Abu_'l-Abbas) sacked [Miseno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miseno), but only for [Khums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khums) purposes (Islamic booty), without conquering the territories of [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania).[^cite_note-mag-49][^cite_note-50]

The duchy was under the direct control of the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) for a brief period after the capture by [Pandulf IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandulf_IV_of_Capua) of the [Principality of Capua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Capua), a long-term rival of Naples; however, this regime lasted only three years before the Greco-Roman-influenced dukes were reinstated.[^cite_note-duchy-48] By the 11th century, Naples had begun to employ [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) [mercenaries](73374.md) to battle their rivals; Duke [Sergius IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergius_IV_of_Naples) hired [Rainulf Drengot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainulf_Drengot) to wage war on Capua for him.[^cite_note-51]

By 1137, the Normans had attained great influence in Italy, controlling previously independent principalities and duchies such as [Capua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Capua), [Benevento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Benevento), [Salerno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Salerno), [Amalfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Amalfi), [Sorrento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Sorrento) and [Gaeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Gaeta); it was in this year that Naples, the last independent duchy in the southern part of the peninsula, came under Norman control. The last ruling duke of the duchy, [Sergius VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergius_VII_of_Naples), was forced to surrender to [Roger II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily), who had been proclaimed [King of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Sicily) by [Antipope Anacletus II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipope_Anacletus_II) seven years earlier. Naples thus joined the [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily), with [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) as the capital.[^cite_note-52]

### As part of the Kingdom of Sicily

Main article: [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily)



[Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor)

After a period of [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) rule, in 1189, the [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily) was in a succession dispute between [Tancred, King of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tancred,_King_of_Sicily) of an illegitimate birth and the [Hohenstaufens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenstaufen), a Germanic [royal house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasty),[^cite_note-53] as its Prince Henry had married [Princess Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_I_of_Sicily), the last legitimate heir to the Sicilian throne. In 1191, Henry invaded Sicily after being crowned as [Henry VI, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), and many cities surrendered. Still, Naples resisted him from May to August under the leadership of [Richard, Count of Acerra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard,_Count_of_Acerra), [Nicholas of Ajello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_of_Ajello), [Aligerno Cottone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aligerno_Cottone?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Margaritus of Brindisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaritus_of_Brindisi) before the Germans suffered from disease and were forced to retreat. [Conrad II, Duke of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_II,_Duke_of_Bohemia) and [Philip I, Archbishop of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_I,_Archbishop_of_Cologne) died of disease during [the siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Naples_\(1191\)). During his counterattack, [Tancred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tancred,_King_of_Sicily) captured Constance, now empress. He had the empress imprisoned at [Castel dell'Ovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_dell'Ovo) at Naples before her release on May 1192 under the pressure of [Pope Celestine III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Celestine_III). In 1194 Henry started his second campaign upon the death of Tancred, but this time Aligerno surrendered without resistance, and finally, Henry conquered Sicily, putting it under the rule of Hohenstaufens.

The [University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II), the first university in Europe dedicated to training secular administrators,[^cite_note-54] was founded by [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), making Naples the intellectual centre of the kingdom. Conflict between the Hohenstaufens and the [Papacy](23056.md) led in 1266 to [Pope Innocent IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV) crowning the [Angevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capetian_House_of_Anjou) duke [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_Naples) King of Sicily:[^cite_note-dieli-55] Charles officially moved the capital from Palermo to Naples, where he resided at the [Castel Nuovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Nuovo).[^cite_note-56] Having a great interest in architecture, Charles I imported French architects and workmen and was personally involved in several building projects in the city.[^cite_note-57] Many examples of [Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) sprang up around Naples, including the [Naples Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_Cathedral), which remains the city's main church.[^cite_note-58]

### Kingdom of Naples

Main articles: [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples), [Parthenopean Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenopean_Republic), and [Naples Lazzaroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_Lazzaroni)



The [Castel Nuovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Nuovo), a.k.a. *Maschio Angioino*, a seat of medieval kings of Naples, Aragon and Spain

In 1282, after the [Sicilian Vespers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Vespers), the Kingdom of Sicily was divided into two. The Angevin [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples) included the southern part of the Italian peninsula, while the island of [Sicily](27619.md) became the [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily).[^cite_note-dieli-55] Wars between the competing dynasties continued until the [Peace of Caltabellotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Caltabellotta) in 1302, which saw [Frederick III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_III_of_Sicily) recognised as king of Sicily, while [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_Naples) was recognised as king of Naples by [Pope Boniface VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Boniface_VIII).[^cite_note-dieli-55] Despite the split, Naples grew in importance, attracting [Pisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa) and [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) merchants,[^cite_note-59] [Tuscan](21967242.md) bankers, and some of the most prominent [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance) artists of the time, such as [Boccaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Boccaccio), [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch) and [Giotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giotto_di_Bondone).[^cite_note-60] During the 14th century, the Hungarian Angevin king [Louis the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_I_of_Hungary) captured the city several times. In 1442, [Alfonso I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_I_of_Naples) conquered Naples after his victory against the last [Angevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capetian_House_of_Anjou) king, [René](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_of_Anjou), and Naples was unified with Sicily again for a brief period.[^cite_note-61]

#### Aragonese and Spanish

Sicily and Naples were separated since 1282, but remained dependencies of [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) under [Ferdinand I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Naples).[^cite_note-62] The new dynasty enhanced Naples's commercial standing by establishing relations with the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula). Naples also became a centre of the Renaissance, with artists such as [Laurana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Laurana), [da Messina](254609.md), [Sannazzaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Sannazaro) and [Poliziano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliziano) arriving in the city.[^cite_note-63] In 1501, Naples came under direct rule from [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancien_R%C3%A9gime_in_France) under [Louis XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XII_of_France), with the Neapolitan king [Frederick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_IV_of_Naples) being taken as a prisoner to France; however, this state of affairs did not last long, as Spain won Naples from the French at the [Battle of Garigliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Garigliano_\(1503\)) in 1503.[^cite_note-spanishnaples-64]

Following the Spanish victory, Naples became part of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire), and remained so throughout the [Spanish Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Spain) period.[^cite_note-spanishnaples-64] The Spanish sent [viceroys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy) [to Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_viceroys_of_Naples) to directly deal with local issues: the most important of these viceroys was [Pedro Álvarez de Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_Toledo_\(viceroy_of_Naples\)), who was responsible for considerable social, economic and urban reforms in the city; he also tried to introduce the [Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Inquisition).[^cite_note-65] In 1544, around 7,000 people [were taken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raid_on_the_Bay_of_Naples_\(1544\)) as [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_slave_trade) by [Barbary corsairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_corsairs) and brought to the [Barbary Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_Coast) of North Africa.[^cite_note-66]



*The [Viceroy of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy_of_Naples) paying tribute to [De Ruyter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michiel_de_Ruyter)'s fleet in the port of Naples*, 1676, by [Jan van Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Essen)



*View of the [Bay of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Naples) with [Admiral Byng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Byng,_1st_Viscount_Torrington)'s Fleet at Anchor*, 1718. Painting by [Gaspar Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_Butler?action=edit\&redlink=1).

By the 17th century, Naples had become Europe's second-largest city – second only to Paris – and the largest European Mediterranean city, with around 250,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-67] The city was a major cultural centre during the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) era, being home to artists such as [Caravaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaggio), [Salvator Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvator_Rosa) and [Bernini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Lorenzo_Bernini), philosophers such as [Bernardino Telesio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardino_Telesio), [Giordano Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giordano_Bruno), [Tommaso Campanella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommaso_Campanella) and [Giambattista Vico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_Vico), and writers such as [Giambattista Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_Marino). A revolution led by the local fisherman [Masaniello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaniello) saw the creation of a brief independent [Neapolitan Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_Republic_\(1647\)) in 1647. However, this lasted only a few months before Spanish rule was reasserted.[^cite_note-spanishnaples-64] In 1656, an outbreak of [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_Plague) killed about half of Naples's 300,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-68]

In 1714, Spanish rule over Naples came to an end as a result of the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession); the Austrian [Charles VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) ruled the city from [Vienna](55866.md) through viceroys of his own.[^cite_note-69] However, the [War of the Polish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Polish_Succession) saw the Spanish regain Sicily and Naples as part of a [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union), with the 1738 [Treaty of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Vienna_\(1738\)) recognising the two polities as independent under a cadet branch of the Spanish [Bourbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bourbon).[^cite_note-70]

In 1755, the Duke of Noja commissioned an accurate topographic map of Naples, later known as the [Map of the Duke of Noja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map_of_the_Duke_of_Noja), employing rigorous surveying accuracy and becoming an essential urban planning tool for Naples.

During the time of [Ferdinand IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_the_Two_Sicilies), the effects of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) were felt in Naples: [Horatio Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horatio_Nelson,_1st_Viscount_Nelson), an ally of the Bourbons, arrived in the city in 1798 to warn against the French republicans. Ferdinand was forced to retreat and fled to [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), where he was protected by a [British fleet](26061.md).[^cite_note-parth-71] However, Naples's [lower class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class) *[lazzaroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazzaroni_\(Naples\))* were strongly pious and royalist, favouring the Bourbons; in the *mêlée* that followed, they fought the Neapolitan pro-Republican aristocracy, causing a civil war.[^cite_note-parth-71]



Naples depicted during the ephemeral [Parthenopean Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenopean_Republic)

Eventually, the Republicans conquered [Castel Sant'Elmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Sant'Elmo) and proclaimed a [Parthenopaean Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenopaean_Republic), secured by the [French Army](611740.md).[^cite_note-parth-71] A [counter-revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-revolutionary) religious army of *lazzaroni* known as the *[sanfedisti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanfedismo)* under Cardinal [Fabrizio Ruffo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_Ruffo) was raised; they met with great success, and the French were forced to surrender the Neapolitan castles, with their fleet sailing back to [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon).[^cite_note-parth-71]

Ferdinand IV was restored as king; however, after only seven years, [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I_of_France) conquered the kingdom and installed [Bonapartist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Bonaparte) kings, including installing his brother [Joseph Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bonaparte).[^cite_note-bonap-72] With the help of the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) and its allies, the Bonapartists were defeated in the [Neapolitan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_War). Ferdinand IV once again regained the throne and the kingdom.[^cite_note-bonap-72]

#### Independent Two Sicilies

Main article: [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies)

The [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) in 1815 saw the kingdoms of Naples and Sicily combine to form the [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies),[^cite_note-bonap-72] with Naples as the capital city. In 1839, Naples became the first city on the Italian Peninsula to have a railway, with the construction of the [Naples–Portici railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples%E2%80%93Portici_railway).[^cite_note-railway-73]

### Italian unification to the present day



Entrance of [Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi) into Naples on 7 September 1860

After the [Expedition of the Thousand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_of_the_Thousand) led by [Giuseppe Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi), which culminated in the controversial [siege of Gaeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Gaeta_\(1860%E2%80%931861\)), Naples became part of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) in 1861 as part of the [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy), ending the era of Bourbon rule. The economy of the area formerly known as the Two Sicilies as dependant on agriculture suffered the international pressure on prices of wheat, and together with lower sea fares prices lead to an unprecedented [wave of emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_diaspora),[^cite_note-modern-74] with an estimated 4 million people emigrating from the Naples area between 1876 and 1913.[^cite_note-75] In the forty years following unification, the population of Naples grew by only 26%, vs. 63% for Turin and 103% for Milan; however, by 1884, Naples was still the largest city in Italy with 496,499 inhabitants, or roughly 64,000 per square kilometre (more than twice the population density of Paris).[^cite_note-snowden-76]<sup>: 11–14, 18 </sup>

Public health conditions in certain areas of the city were poor, with twelve epidemics of [cholera](7591.md) and [typhoid fever](31596.md) claiming some 48,000 people between 1834 and 1884. A [death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_rate) 31.84 per thousand, high even for the time, persisted in the absence of epidemics between 1878 and 1883.[^cite_note-snowden-76] Then in [August 1884](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1884_in_Italy#August), Naples fell victim to a major cholera epidemic, caused largely by the city's poor [sewerage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewerage) infrastructure. In response to these problems, in 1885,[^cite_note-77] the government prompted a radical transformation of the city called *[risanamento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risanamento)* to improve the sewer infrastructure and replace the most clustered areas, considered the main cause of [insalubrity](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/insalubrity#English), with large and airy avenues. The project proved difficult to accomplish politically and economically due to corruption, as shown in the [Saredo Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saredo_Inquiry), land speculation and extremely long bureaucracy. This led to the project to massive delays with contrasting results. The most notable transformations made were the construction of Via Caracciolo in place of the beach along the promenade, the creation of [Galleria Umberto I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleria_Umberto_I) and [Galleria Principe](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category\:Galleria%20Principe%20di%20Napoli%20\(Naples\)) and the construction of Corso Umberto.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]



[Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) bombardment of Naples, 1943

Naples was the [most-bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Naples_in_World_War_II) Italian city during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-wii-16] Though Neapolitans did not rebel under [Italian Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Fascism), Naples was the first Italian city to [rise up against](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Days_of_Naples) [German](21212.md) [military occupation](816214.md); for the first time in Europe, the Nazis, whose leader in this case was Colonel Scholl, negotiated a surrender in the face of insurgents. The city was already completely freed by 1 October 1943,[^cite_note-80] when British and American forces entered the city.[^cite_note-81] Departing Germans [burned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_burning) the library of [the university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II), as well as the Italian Royal Society. They also destroyed the city archives. Time bombs planted throughout the city continued to explode into November.[^cite_note-82] Departing Germans also "looted all the food and fuel. They blew up the city's gas, water and sewage piping. They destroyed its port facilities ... and scuttled more than 300 ships in the harbor. They destroyed 75% of the major bridges, stole nearly 90% of the city's trucks, buses and trams, demolished railroad tracks and tunnels...."[^cite_note-83] The symbol of the rebirth of Naples was the rebuilding of the church of [Santa Chiara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Chiara,_Naples), which had been destroyed in a [United States Army Air Corps](32090.md) bombing raid.[^cite_note-wii-16]

Special funding from the Italian government's [Fund for the South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassa_per_il_Mezzogiorno) was provided from 1950 to 1984, helping the Neapolitan economy to improve somewhat, with city landmarks such as the [Piazza del Plebiscito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_del_Plebiscito) being renovated.[^cite_note-84] However, high unemployment continues to affect Naples.

Italian media attributed the city's recent [illegal waste disposal issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_waste_management_crisis) to the [Camorra](44945.md), the [organized crime](22625.md) network centered in Campania.[^cite_note-85] Due to illegal waste dumping, as exposed by [Roberto Saviano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Saviano) in his book *[Gomorrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomorrah_\(book\))*, severe environmental contamination and increased health risks remain prevalent.[^cite_note-cdm-86] In 2007, [Silvio Berlusconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Berlusconi)'s government held senior meetings in Naples to demonstrate their intention to solve these problems.[^cite_note-87] However, the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession) had a severe impact on the city, intensifying its waste-management and unemployment problems.[^cite_note-88] By August 2011, the number of unemployed in the Naples area had risen to 250,000, sparking public protests against the economic situation.[^cite_note-89] In June 2012, allegations of blackmail, extortion, and illicit contract tendering emerged concerning the city's waste management issues.[^cite_note-OneJun-90][^cite_note-OneJul-91]

Naples hosted the sixth [World Urban Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Urban_Forum) in September 2012[^cite_note-92] and the 63rd [International Astronautical Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronautical_Congress) in October 2012.[^cite_note-93] In 2013, it was the host of the [Universal Forum of Cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Forum_of_Cultures) and the host for the [2019 Summer Universiade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Summer_Universiade).

Naples's 2,800-year history has left it with a wealth of historical buildings and monuments, from medieval castles to classical ruins, and a wide range of culturally and historically significant sites nearby, including the [Palace of Caserta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Caserta) and the Roman ruins of [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) and [Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum). In 2017 the [BBC](19344654.md) defined Naples as "the Italian city with too much history to handle".[^cite_note-94]

The most prominent forms of architecture visible in present-day Naples are the [Medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_architecture), [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) and [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) styles.[^cite_note-95] Naples has a total of 448 historical churches (1000 in total[^cite_note-96]), making it one of the most Catholic cities in the world in terms of the number of places of worship.[^cite_note-churches-97] In 1995, the [historic centre of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Centre_of_Naples) was listed by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md), a United Nations programme which aims to catalogue and conserve sites of outstanding cultural or natural importance to the [common heritage of mankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_heritage_of_mankind).

> Naples is one of the most ancient cities in Europe, whose contemporary urban fabric preserves the elements of its long and eventful history. The rectangular grid layout of the ancient Greek foundation of Neapolis is still discernible. It has indeed continued to provide the layout for the present-day Historic Centre of Naples, one of the major Mediterranean port cities. From the Middle Ages to the 18th century, Naples was a focal point in terms of art and architecture, expressed in its ancient forts, the royal ensembles such as the Royal Palace of 1600, and the palaces and churches sponsored by the noble families.

— [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s Criterion

### Piazzas, palaces and castles

See also: [List of palaces in Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Palaces_in_Naples)



The [Egg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_dell'Ovo)

The main city square or *[piazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza)* of the city is the [Piazza del Plebiscito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_del_Plebiscito). Its construction was begun by the [Bonapartist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonapartism) king [Joachim Murat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Murat) and finished by the Bourbon king [Ferdinand IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_the_Two_Sicilies). The piazza is bounded on the east by the [Royal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palace_\(Naples\)) and on the west by the church of [San Francesco di Paola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francesco_di_Paola_\(Naples\)), with the colonnades extending on both sides. Nearby is the [Teatro di San Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_di_San_Carlo), which is the oldest [opera house](155040.md) in Italy. Directly across San Carlo is [Galleria Umberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleria_Umberto_I).

Naples is well known for its castles: The most ancient is [Castel dell'Ovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_dell'Ovo) ("Egg Castle"), which was built on the tiny [islet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islet) of Megarides, where the original [Cumaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumae) colonists had founded the city. In Roman times the islet became part of [Lucullus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucullus)'s villa, later hosting [Romulus Augustulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romulus_Augustulus), the exiled last western Roman emperor.[^cite_note-98] It had also been the prison for [Empress Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_I_of_Sicily) between 1191 and 1192 after her being captured by Sicilians, and [Conradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conradin) and [Giovanna I of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanna_I_of_Naples) before their executions.

[Castel Nuovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Nuovo), also known as *Maschio [Angioino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capetian_House_of_Anjou)*, is one of the city's top landmarks; it was built during the time of [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_Naples), the first [king of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Naples). Castel Nuovo has seen many notable historical events: for example, in 1294, [Pope Celestine V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Celestine_V) resigned as pope in a hall of the castle, and following this [Pope Boniface VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Boniface_VIII) was elected pope by the cardinal [collegium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_\(ancient_Rome\)), before moving to Rome.[^cite_note-99]

[Castel Capuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Capuano) was built in the 12th century by [William I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_Sicily), the son of [Roger II of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily), the first monarch of the [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples). It was expanded by [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) and became one of his royal palaces. The castle was the residence of many kings and queens throughout its history. In the 16th century, it became the Hall of Justice.[^cite_note-100]

Another Neapolitan castle is [Castel Sant'Elmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Sant'Elmo), which was completed in 1329 and is built in the shape of a [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star). Its strategic position overlooking the entire city made it a target of various invaders. During the uprising of [Masaniello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaniello) in 1647, the Spanish took refuge in Sant'Elmo to escape the revolutionaries.[^cite_note-101]

The [Carmine Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmine_Castle), built in 1392 and highly modified in the 16th century by the Spanish, was demolished in 1906 to make room for the Via Marina, although two of the castle's towers remain as a monument. The Vigliena Fort, built in 1702, was destroyed in 1799 during the royalist war against the Parthenopean Republic and is now abandoned and in ruin.[^cite_note-102]

### Museums

See also: [List of museums in Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Naples)



[National Archaeological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archaeological_Museum,_Naples)

Naples is widely known for its wealth of historical museums. The [National Archaeological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archaeological_Museum,_Naples) is one of the city's main museums, with one of the most extensive collections of [artefacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artifact_\(archaeology\)) of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in the world.[^cite_note-bestnap-103] It also houses many of the antiques unearthed at [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) and [Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum), as well as some artefacts from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia) and [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Renaissance) periods.[^cite_note-bestnap-103]

Previously a Bourbon palace, now a museum and art gallery, the [Museo di Capodimonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_di_Capodimonte) is another museum of note. The [gallery features paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_works_in_the_Galleria_Nazionale_di_Capodimonte) from the 13th to the 18th centuries, including major works by [Simone Martini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Martini), [Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael), [Titian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titian), [Caravaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaggio), [El Greco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Greco), [Jusepe de Ribera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jusepe_de_Ribera) and [Luca Giordano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Giordano). The royal apartments are furnished with antique 18th-century furniture and a collection of [porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain) and [majolica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_majolica) from the various royal residences: the famous [Capodimonte Porcelain Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capodimonte_porcelain) once stood just adjacent to the palace.

In front of the [Royal Palace of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Naples) stands the [Galleria Umberto I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleria_Umberto_I), which contains the [Coral Jewellery Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_del_Corallo). Occupying a 19th-century palazzo renovated by the Portuguese architect [Álvaro Siza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Siza), the [Museo d'Arte Contemporanea Donnaregina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_d'Arte_Contemporanea_Donnaregina) (MADRE) features an [enfilade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enfilade) procession of permanent installations by artists such as [Francesco Clemente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Clemente), [Richard Serra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Serra), and [Rebecca Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Horn).[^cite_note-104] The 16th-century palace of Roccella hosts the Palazzo delle Arti Napoli, which contains the civic collections of art belonging to the City of Naples, and features temporary exhibits of art and culture. Palazzo Como, which dates from the 15th century, hosts the [Museo Civico Filangieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Civico_Filangieri) of [plastic arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_arts), created in 1883 by [Gaetano Filangieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetano_Filangieri,_prince_of_Satriano).

### Other features

Aside from the Piazza del Plebiscito, Naples has two other major public squares: the [Piazza Dante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Dante,_Naples) and the [Piazza dei Martiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_dei_Martiri,_Naples). The latter originally had only a memorial to religious [martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr), but in 1866, after the [Italian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy), four lions were added, representing the four rebellions against the Bourbons.[^cite_note-106]

The [San Gennaro dei Poveri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gennaro_dei_Poveri) is a Renaissance-era hospital for the poor, erected by the Spanish in 1667. It was the forerunner of a much more ambitious project, the [Bourbon Hospice for the Poor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ospedale_L'Albergo_Reale_dei_Poveri,_Naples) started by [Charles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain). This was for the destitute and ill of the city; it also provided a self-sufficient community where the poor would live and work. Though a notable landmark, it is no longer a functioning hospital.[^cite_note-107]

#### Parks, gardens, villas, fountains and stairways

Main articles: [Villas in Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villas_in_Naples), [Stairways in Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stairways_in_Naples), and [List of fountains in Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fountains_in_Naples)



[Villa Comunale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Comunale)

Of the various [public parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park) in Naples, the most prominent are the [Villa Comunale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Comunale), which was built by the Bourbon king [Ferdinand IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_the_Two_Sicilies) in the 1780s;[^cite_note-108] the park was originally a "Royal Garden", reserved for members of the royal family, but open to the public on special holidays. The Bosco di [Capodimonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Capodimonte), the city's largest green space, served as a royal hunting reserve. The Park has 16 additional historical buildings, including residences, lodges, churches, fountains, statues, orchards and woods.[^cite_note-109]

Another important park is the [Parco Virgiliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_Virgiliano), which looks towards the tiny volcanic islet of [Nisida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nisida); beyond Nisida lie [Procida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procida) and [Ischia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischia).[^cite_note-parkvirg-110] [Parco Virgiliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_Virgiliano_\(Mergellina\)) was named after [Virgil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil), the classical Roman poet and Latin writer who is thought to be [entombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil's_tomb) nearby.[^cite_note-parkvirg-110] Naples is noted for its numerous stately [villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa), fountains and [stairways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stairways_in_Naples), such as the [Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) [Villa Floridiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Floridiana), the [Fountain of Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_of_Neptune,_Naples) and the [Pedamentina stairway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stairways_in_Naples).

#### Neo-Gothic, *Liberty Napoletano* and modern architecture



One of the city's various examples of *Liberty Napoletano*

Various buildings inspired by the [Gothic Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival_architecture) are extant in Naples, due to the influence that this movement had on the Scottish–Indian architect [Lamont Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamont_Young_\(Naples\)), one of the most active Neapolitan architects of the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Young left a significant footprint in the [cityscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cityscape) and designed many urban projects, such as the city's first subway ([metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_Metro)).

In the first years of the 20th century, a local version of the [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) phenomenon, known as "[Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_style) Napoletano", developed in the city, creating many buildings which still stand today. In 1935, the [Rationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalism_\(architecture\)) architect Luigi Cosenza designed a new fish market for the city. During the [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy) rule, the first structures of the city's "service center" were built, all in a Rationalist-[Functionalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functionalism_\(architecture\)) style, including the Palazzo delle Poste and the Pretura buildings. The [Centro direzionale di Napoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_direzionale_di_Napoli) is the only adjacent cluster of skyscrapers in southern Europe.

#### Subterranean Naples



[Underground Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_underground_geothermal_zone)

Main articles: [Beneath Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beneath_Naples), [Catacombs of San Gennaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs_of_San_Gennaro), [Catacombs of Saint Gaudiosus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs_of_Saint_Gaudiosus), and [San Pietro ad Aram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pietro_ad_Aram)

[Underneath Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_underground_geothermal_zone) lies a series of caves and structures created by centuries of mining, and the city rests atop a major [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_activity) zone. There are also several ancient [Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman) reservoirs dug out from the soft [tufo stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff) on which, and from which, much of the city is built. Approximately one kilometre (0.62 miles) of the many kilometres of tunnels under the city can be visited from the [Napoli Sotteranea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_underground_geothermal_zone), situated in the historic centre of the city in [Via dei Tribunali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_dei_Tribunali,_Naples). This system of tunnels and cisterns underlies most of the city and lies approximately 30 metres (98 ft) below ground level. During [World War II](32927.md), these tunnels were used as [air raid shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_raid_shelter), and there are inscriptions on the walls depicting the suffering endured by the refugees of that era.

There are large [catacombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs_of_San_Gennaro) in and around the city, and other landmarks such as the [Piscina Mirabilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piscina_Mirabilis), the main cistern serving the [Gulf of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Naples) during Roman times.

Several archaeological excavations are also present; they revealed in [San Lorenzo Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Lorenzo_Maggiore,_Naples) the [macellum of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Naples), and in [Santa Chiara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Chiara,_Naples), the biggest thermal complex of the city in Roman times.

## Geography



The [Gulf of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Naples)



Urban density in central Naples

The city is situated on the [Gulf of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Naples), on the western coast of [southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy); it rises from sea level to an elevation of 450 metres (1,480 ft). The small rivers that formerly crossed the city's centre have since been covered by construction. It lies between two notable [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic) regions, [Mount Vesuvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vesuvius) and the [Phlegraean Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phlegraean_Fields). Campi Flegrei is considered a [supervolcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervolcano).[^cite_note-111] The islands of [Procida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procida), [Capri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capri) and [Ischia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischia) can all be reached from Naples by [hydrofoils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrofoil) and ferries. [Sorrento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorrento,_Italy) and the [Amalfi Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalfi_Coast) are situated south of the city. At the same time, the Roman ruins of [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii), [Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum), [Oplontis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oplontis) and [Stabiae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabiae), which were destroyed in the [eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eruption_of_Mount_Vesuvius_in_79_AD), are also visible nearby. The port towns of [Pozzuoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozzuoli) and [Baia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiae), which were part of the Roman naval facility of [Portus Julius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portus_Julius), lie to the west of the city.

### Climate

Naples has a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*), that borders closely on a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*).[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] The climate and fertility of the [Gulf of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Naples) made the region famous during Roman times, when emperors such as [Claudius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius) and [Tiberius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberius) holidayed near the city.[^cite_note-rome-43] Maritime features mitigate the winters but occasionally cause heavy rainfall, particularly in the autumn and winter. Summers feature high temperatures and humidity.

Winters are mild, and snow is rare in the city area but frequent on [Mount Vesuvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vesuvius). November is the wettest month in Naples, while July is the driest.

### Immigration

In contrast to many northern Italian cities, there are relatively few immigrants in Naples; As of 2025, they make up 6.8% of the population. The 5 largest foreign countries of birth are [Sri Lanka](26750.md), [Ukraine](31750.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), [China](5405.md), and [Bangladesh](3454.md).[^cite_note-foreignborn-129]

Statistics show that, in the past, the vast majority of immigrants in Naples were female; this happened because male immigrants in Italy tended to head to the wealthier north.[^cite_note-demog-8][^cite_note-130]

## Education



Main building of the [University of Naples Federico II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II)

Naples is noted for its numerous higher education institutes and research centres. Naples hosts what is thought to be the oldest state university in the world, in the form of the [University of Naples Federico II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II), which was founded by [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) in 1224. The university is among the most prominent in Italy, with around 70,000 students and over 6,000 professors in 2022.[^cite_note-131] It is host to the [Botanical Garden of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_Garden_of_Naples), which was opened in 1807 by [Joseph Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bonaparte), using plans drawn up under the Bourbon king [Ferdinand IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_the_Two_Sicilies). The garden's 15 hectares feature around 25,000 samples of over 10,000 species.[^cite_note-132]

Naples is also served by the [University of Campania Luigi Vanvitelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Campania_Luigi_Vanvitelli), a modern university which opened in 1989, and which has strong links to the nearby [province of Caserta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Caserta).[^cite_note-133] Another notable centre of education is the [University of Naples "L'Orientale"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_%22L'Orientale%22), which specialises in [Eastern culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_world), and was founded by the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) missionary [Matteo Ripa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Ripa) in 1732, after he returned from the court of [Kangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor), the [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_China) of the [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people) Qing dynasty of China.[^cite_note-134]

Other prominent universities in Naples include the [Parthenope University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenope_University_of_Naples), the private [Suor Orsola Benincasa University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suor_Orsola_Benincasa_University_of_Naples), and the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) [Pontifical Theological Seminary of Southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Theological_Seminary_of_Southern_Italy).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] The [San Pietro a Maiella music conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_conservatories_of_Naples) is the city's foremost institution of musical education; the earliest Neapolitan music conservatories were founded in the 16th century under the Spanish.[^cite_note-137] The [Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_di_Belle_Arti_di_Napoli) located on the Via Santa Maria di Costantinopoli is the city's foremost art school and one of the oldest in Italy.[^cite_note-138] Naples hosts also the [Astronomical Observatory of Capodimonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_Observatory_of_Capodimonte), established in 1812 by the king [Joachim Murat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Murat) and the astronomer Federigo Zuccari,[^cite_note-139] the oldest [marine zoological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology) study station in the world, [Stazione Zoologica Anton Dohrn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stazione_Zoologica_Anton_Dohrn), created in 1872 by German scientist [Anton Dohrn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Dohrn), and the world's oldest permanent volcano observatory, the [Vesuvius Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesuvius_Observatory), founded in 1841. The Observatory lies on the slopes of Mount Vesuvius, near the city of [Ercolano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ercolano), and is now a permanent specialised institute of the [National Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Geophysics_and_Volcanology) (INGV).

### Governance

Each of the 7,894 *[comune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune)* in Italy is today represented locally by a [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council) headed by an elected mayor, known as a *sindaco* and informally called the first citizen (*primo cittadino*). This system, or one very similar to it, has been in place since the invasion of Italy by [Napoleonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) forces in 1808. When the [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies) was restored, the system was kept in place with members of the nobility filling mayoral roles. By the end of the 19th century, [party politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) had begun to emerge; during the [fascist era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy), each commune was represented by a *podestà*. Since [World War II](32927.md), the political landscape of Naples has been neither strongly right-wing nor left-wing – both [Christian democrats](328969.md) ([Christian Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democracy_\(Italy\))) and [democratic socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialism) have governed the city at different times, with roughly equal frequency. Since the early 1990s, the mayors of Naples have all belonged to left-wing or center-left political groups.

Since 2021, the mayor of Naples is [Gaetano Manfredi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetano_Manfredi), an [independent politician](2172378.md) candidated by the center-left coalition, former [minister of university and research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_University_and_Research) in the [second Conte government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Conte_government), and former rector of the [University of Naples Federico II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II).

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Naples)



The [port of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Naples)

Naples, within its administrative limits, is Italy's fourth-largest economy after [Milan](36511.md), Rome and [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), and is the world's [103rd-largest urban economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP) by [purchasing power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power), with an estimated 2024 GDP of €28.4 billion, equivalent to €30.804 per capita.[^cite_note-Brookings-140][^cite_note-PWCdata-141][^cite_note-142] Naples is a major [cargo terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Container_port), and the [port of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Naples) is one of the Mediterranean's largest and busiest. The city has experienced significant economic growth since [World War II](32927.md), but joblessness remains a major problem,[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] and the city is characterised by high levels of political corruption and [organised crime](22625.md).

Naples is a major national, and international tourist destination, one of Italy's and Europe's top tourist cities.[^cite_note-146] Tourists began visiting Naples in the 18th century during the [Grand Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour).

In the last decades, there has been a move away from a traditional agriculture-based economy in the province of Naples to one based on [service industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector). The service sector employs the majority of Neapolitans, although more than half of these are small enterprises with fewer than 20 workers; about 70 companies are said to be medium-sized with more than 200 workers, and about 15 have more than 500 workers.

### Tourism

Naples is, with [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), Rome, [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) and [Milan](36511.md), one of the main Italian tourist destinations. With 20,000,000 visitors in 2025,[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] the city has completely emerged from the strong tourist depression of past decades (due primarily to the unilateral destination of an industrial city but also due to the damage to the city's image caused by the Italian media,[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151] from the [1980 Irpinia earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Irpinia_earthquake) and the [waste crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_waste_management_crisis), in favour of the coastal centres of its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_metropolitan_area)).[^cite_note-152] To adequately assess the phenomenon, however, it must be considered that a large slice of tourists visit Naples per year, staying in the numerous localities in its surroundings,[^cite_note-153] connected to the city with both private and public direct lines.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] Daily visits to Naples are carried out by various Roman tour operators and by all the main tourist resorts of [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania): as of 2019, Naples is the tenth most visited [municipality in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune) and the first in the [south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy).[^cite_note-156]

The sector is constantly growing[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] and the prospect of reaching the [art cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_town) of its level is once again expected in a relatively short time.[^cite_note-159] Tourism is increasingly assuming a decisive weight for the city's economy, which is why, exactly as happened for example in the case of Venice or Florence, the risk of gentrification of the [historic centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Centre_of_Naples) is now high.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

### Rail

[Suburban rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail) services are provided by [Ente Autonomo Volturno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ente_Autonomo_Volturno) and [Trenitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia) through the [Naples metropolitan railway service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_metropolitan_railway_service)

The city's main railway station is [Napoli Centrale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoli_Centrale_railway_station), which is located in [Piazza Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Garibaldi,_Naples); other significant stations include the [Napoli Campi Flegrei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoli_Campi_Flegrei_railway_station)[^cite_note-167] and [Napoli Mergellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoli_Mergellina_railway_station). [Napoli Afragola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoli_Afragola_railway_station) serves [high-speed trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_trains) that do not start or finish at [Napoli Centrale railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoli_Centrale_railway_station). Naples's streets are famously narrow (it was the first city in the world to set up a pedestrian one-way street),[^cite_note-168] so the general public commonly use compact [hatchback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatchback) cars and [scooters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scooter_\(motorcycle\)) for personal transit.[^cite_note-169] Since 2007, trains running at 300 km/h (186 mph) have connected Naples with Rome with a journey time of under an hour,[^cite_note-170] and direct high speed services also operate to Florence, Bologna, Milan, Turin and Salerno. Direct sleeper 'boat train' services operate nightly to cities in Sicily.

### Water

The port of Naples runs several ferry, [hydrofoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrofoil), and [SWATH](195952.md#SWATH_and_wave-piercing_designs) [catamaran](195952.md) lines to [Capri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capri), [Ischia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischia) and [Sorrento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorrento), [Salerno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salerno), [Positano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positano) and [Amalfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalfi).[^cite_note-ferry-171] Services are also available to [Sicily](27619.md), Sardinia, [Ponza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponza) and the [Aeolian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolian_Islands).[^cite_note-ferry-171] The port serves over 6 million local passengers annually,[^cite_note-port_local_statistics-172] plus a further 1 million international [cruise ship](314855.md) passengers.[^cite_note-port_cruises_statistics-173] A regional hydrofoil transport service, the [Metrò del Mare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metr%C3%B2_del_Mare?action=edit\&redlink=1), runs annually from July to September, maintained by a consortium of shipowners and local administrations.[^cite_note-metromare-174]

### Air

The [Naples International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_International_Airport) is located in the [San Pietro a Patierno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pietro_a_Patierno) neighborhood. It is the largest airport in southern Italy and the [fourth-busiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Italy) in the whole country, with around 250 national and international flights arriving or departing daily.[^cite_note-175]

The average commute with public transit in Naples on a weekday is 77 minutes. Nineteen per cent of public transit commuters ride for more than 2 hours every day. The average time people wait at a stop or station for public transit is 27 minutes, while 56% of riders wait for over 20 minutes. The average distance people usually ride in a single trip with public transit is 7.1 km (4.4 mi), while 11% travel for over 12 km (7.5 mi) in a single direction.[^cite_note-176]

### Urban public transport

Naples has an extensive public transport network, including [trams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Naples), buses and [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus),[^cite_note-transportnap-177] most of which are operated by the municipally owned company [Azienda Napoletana Mobilità (ANM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANM_\(Naples\)). Some suburban services are operated by [AIR Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIR_Campania).

The city furthermore operates the [Naples Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_Metro) ([Italian](14708.md): *metropolitana di Napoli*), an underground [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) [railway system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) which integrates both surface railway lines and the city's [metro stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Naples_metro_stations), [many of which](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Stations_of_the_Naples_Metro) are noted for their decorative architecture and [public art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_art). In fact, the [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo_station_\(Naples\)) and [Chiaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiaia%E2%80%93Monte_di_Dio_station) stations are often in the top spots of the rankings of the most beautiful metro stations in the world.[^cite_note-transportnap-177][^cite_note-178]

There are also four [funiculars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular) in the city (operated by ANM): *[Centrale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_funicular_\(Italy\))*, [*Chiaia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiaia_Funicular), [*Montesanto*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montesanto_Funicular) and [*Mergellina*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mergellina_Funicular).[^cite_note-179] Five public [elevators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevator) are in operation in the city: within the bridge of [Chiaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiaia), in via Acton, near the Sanità Bridge,[^cite_note-180] under the [Mount Echia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Echia), and in the Ventaglieri Park, accompanied by two public [escalators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalator).[^cite_note-181]

## Culture

### Art



A [Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_art) painting by Salvatore Fergola showing the 1839 inauguration of the Naples–Portici railway line

Naples has long been a centre of art and architecture, dotted with Medieval-, Baroque- and Renaissance-era churches, castles and palaces. A critical factor in the development of the Neapolitan school of painting was Caravaggio's arrival in Naples in 1606. In the 18th century, Naples went through a period of [neoclassicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism), following the discovery of the remarkably intact Roman ruins of [Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum) and [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii).

The [Neapolitan Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_di_Belle_Arti_di_Napoli), founded by [Charles III of Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain) in 1752 as the Real Accademia di Disegno ([en](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language): *Royal Academy of Design*), was the centre of the artistic School of [Posillipo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posillipo) in the 19th century. Artists such as [Domenico Morelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Morelli), [Giacomo Di Chirico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Di_Chirico), [Francesco Saverio Altamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Saverio_Altamura) and [Gioacchino Toma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioacchino_Toma) worked in Naples during this period, and many of their works are now exhibited in the academy's art collection. The modern Academy offers courses in painting, [decorating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_design), sculpture, design, [restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation-restoration), and urban planning. Naples is also known for its theatres, which are among the oldest in Europe: the [Teatro di San Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_di_San_Carlo) [opera house](155040.md) dates back to the 18th century.

Naples is also the home of the artistic tradition of [Capodimonte porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capodimonte_porcelain). In 1743, Charles of Bourbon founded the Royal Factory of Capodimonte, many of whose artworks are now on display in the [Museum of Capodimonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Capodimonte). Several of Naples's mid-19th-century porcelain factories remain active today.

### Cuisine

Main articles: [Neapolitan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_cuisine), [Neapolitan ice cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_ice_cream), [Neapolitan pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_pizza), and [Neapolitan ragù](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_rag%C3%B9)



[Neapolitan pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_pizza). Pizza was invented in Naples.



[Sfogliatella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sfogliatella), a popular Neapolitan pastry dish

Naples is internationally famous for its [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_cuisine) and wine. It draws influences from the numerous cultures which have inhabited it throughout history, including the [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece), Spanish and French. Neapolitan cuisine emerged as a distinct form in the 18th century. The ingredients are typically rich in taste while remaining affordable to the general populace.[^cite_note-182] In May 2024, *Time Out* named Naples the best city for food.[^cite_note-183]

Naples is traditionally credited as the home of [pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizza).[^cite_note-pizza-184] This originated as a meal of the poor, but under [Ferdinand IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_the_Two_Sicilies) it became popular among the upper classes. The [Margherita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margherita_\(pizza\)) pizza was named after Queen [Margherita of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margherita_of_Savoy) after her visit.[^cite_note-pizza-184] Cooked traditionally in a wood-burning [oven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonry_oven), the ingredients have been strictly regulated by law since 2004, and must include wheat flour type "00" with the addition of flour type "0" [yeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast), natural mineral water, peeled tomatoes or fresh [cherry tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_tomato), [mozzarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozzarella), [sea salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_salt) and extra virgin [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil).[^cite_note-autogenerated2-185]

[Spaghetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaghetti) is also associated with Naples, and is commonly eaten with clams *[vongole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vongole)* or *lupini di mare*. A popular Neapolitan [folkloric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore) symbol is the comic figure [Pulcinella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulcinella) eating a plate of spaghetti.[^cite_note-186] Other popular dishes include *[Parmigiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmigiana) di melanzane*, *spaghetti alle vongole* and *[casatiello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casatiello)*.[^cite_note-187] As a coastal city, Naples is known for seafood dishes including *impepata di cozze* (peppered [mussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussel)), *purpetiello affogato* (octopus poached in broth), *alici marinate* (marinated [anchovies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchovies)), *baccalà alla napoletana* (salt [cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cod)) and *baccalà fritto* (fried cod), a dish commonly eaten during the Christmas period.

Naples is known for its sweet dishes, including colourful fruit-based local variants of Italian *[gelato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelato)*, which is similar to ice cream. Popular Neapolitan pastry dishes include *[zeppole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeppole)*, *[babà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rum_baba)*, *[sfogliatelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sfogliatelle)* and *[pastiera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastiera)*, the latter of which is prepared specially for Easter celebrations.[^cite_note-188] Another seasonal sweet is *[struffoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struffoli)*, a sweet-tasting [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey) dough decorated and eaten around Christmas.[^cite_note-189] The traditional [Neapolitan flip coffee pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_flip_coffee_pot), known as the *cuccuma* or *cuccumella*, was the basis for the invention of the [espresso machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espresso_machine), and also inspired the [Moka pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moka_pot). [Wineries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wineries) in the Vesuvius area produce wines such as the *[Lacryma Christi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacryma_Christi)* ("tears of Christ") and *Terzigno*. Naples is also the home of *[limoncello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limoncello)*, a popular [lemon liqueur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon_liqueur).[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191]

### Festivals

&#x20;The cultural significance of Naples is often represented through a series of festivals held in the city. The following is a list of several festivals that take place in Naples (note: some festivals are not held on an annual basis).



An 1813 depiction of the Piedigrotta festival

- *Festa di Piedigrotta* ("Piedigrotta Festival") – A musical event typically held in September in memory of the famous Madonna of Piedigrotta. Throughout the month, a series of musical workshops, concerts, religious events and children's events are held to entertain the citizens of Naples and surrounding areas.

  [^cite_note-192]

- *Pizzafest* – As Naples is famous for being home to pizza, the city hosts an eleven-day festival dedicated to this iconic dish. This is a key event for Neapolitans and tourists alike, as various stations are open for tasting a wide range of true Neapolitan pizza. In addition to pizza tasting, a variety of entertainment shows are displayed.

  [^cite_note-193]

- *Maggio dei Monumenti* ("May of Monuments") – A cultural event where the city hosts a variety of special events dedicated to the birth of King Charles of Bourbon. It festival features art and music of the 18th century, and many buildings which may normally be closed throughout the year are opened for visitors to view.

  [^cite_note-194]

- *Il Ritorno della festa di San Gennaro* ("The Return of the [Feast of San Gennaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_San_Gennaro)") – An annual celebration and feast of faith held over three days, commemorating Saint [Gennaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Januarius). Throughout the festival, parades, religious processions and musical entertainment are featured. An annual celebration is also held in "[Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy,_Manhattan)" in Manhattan.

### Language

Main article: [Neapolitan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_language)

The [Neapolitan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_language), considered to be a distinct language and mainly spoken in the city, is also found in the region of [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania) and has been diffused into other areas of [southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy) by Neapolitan migrants, and in many different places in the world. On 14 October 2008, a regional law was enacted by Campania which has the effect that the use of the Neapolitan language is protected.[^cite_note-197]

The term "Neapolitan language" is often used to describe the language of all of [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania) (except [Cilento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilento)), and is sometimes applied to the entire [south Italian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italian_\(disambiguation\)); *[Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue)* refers to the latter as *Napoletano–Calabrese*.[^cite_note-Ethnologue_Napoletano-Calabrese-198] This linguistic group is spoken throughout most of southern continental Italy, including the [Gaeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaeta) and [Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora,_Italy) district of southern [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio), the southern part of [Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marche) and [Abruzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abruzzo), Molise, [Basilicata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilicata), northern [Calabria](44772.md), and northern and central [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia). In 1976, there were an estimated 7,047,399 [native speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) of this group of dialects.[^cite_note-Ethnologue_Napoletano-Calabrese-198]

Many of the most famous historic Italian songs known worldwide are written in the Neapolitan language, including ['O sole mio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'O_sole_mio), [Funiculì, Funiculà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicul%C3%AC,_Funicul%C3%A0), [Tu vuò fà l'americano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tu_vu%C3%B2_f%C3%A0_l'americano), and other classics that have played a major role in the international diffusion of Italian musical culture.

[Giordano Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giordano_Bruno)

Naples is one of the leading centres of [Italian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_literature). The history of the Neapolitan language was deeply entwined with that of the [Tuscan dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_dialects), which then became the current Italian language. The first written testimonies of the Italian language are the [Placiti Cassinensi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placiti_Cassinensi?action=edit\&redlink=1) legal documents, dated 960 A.D., preserved in the [Monte Cassino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Cassino) Abbey, which are, in fact, evidence of a language spoken in a southern dialect. The Tuscan poet [Giovanni Boccaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Boccaccio) lived for many years at the court of King [Robert the Wise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert,_King_of_Naples) and his successor [Joanna of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_I_of_Naples), using Naples as a setting for a number of his later novels. His works contain some words that are taken from Neapolitan instead of the corresponding Italian, e.g. "*testo*" (neap.: "*testa*"), which in Naples indicates a large [terracotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta) jar used to cultivate shrubs and little trees. King [Alfonso V of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_V_of_Aragon) stated in 1442 that the Neapolitan language was to be used instead of [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) in official documents.



Statue of [Giambattista Vico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_Vico)

Later Neapolitan was replaced by Italian in the first half of the 16th century,[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200] during Spanish domination. In 1458 the *[Accademia Pontaniana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Pontaniana)*, one of the first academies in Italy, was established in Naples as a free initiative by men of letters, science and literature. In 1480 the writer and poet [Jacopo Sannazzaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Sannazzaro) wrote the first pastoral romance, *Arcadia*, which influenced Italian literature. In 1634 [Giambattista Basile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_Basile) collected *[Lo Cunto de li Cunti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentamerone)* five books of ancient tales written in the Neapolitan dialect rather than Italian. Philosopher [Giordano Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giordano_Bruno), who theorised the existence of infinite solar systems and the infinity of the entire universe, completed his studies at the University of Naples. Due to philosophers such as [Giambattista Vico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_Vico), Naples became one of the centres of the Italian peninsula for historical and [philosophy of history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_history) studies.

[Jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisprudence) studies were enhanced in Naples thanks to eminent personalities of jurists like [Bernardo Tanucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Tanucci), [Gaetano Filangieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetano_Filangieri) and [Antonio Genovesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Genovesi). In the 18th century Naples, together with [Milan](36511.md), became one of the most important sites from which the [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) penetrated Italy. Poet and philosopher [Giacomo Leopardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Leopardi) visited the city in 1837 and died there. His works influenced [Francesco de Sanctis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_de_Sanctis), who studied in Naples and eventually became Minister of Instruction during the Italian kingdom. De Sanctis was one of the first literary critics to discover, study and diffuse the poems and literary works of the great poet from [Recanati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recanati).

Writer and journalist [Matilde Serao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilde_Serao) co-founded the newspaper [Il Mattino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Mattino) with her husband [Edoardo Scarfoglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edoardo_Scarfoglio) in 1892. Serao was an acclaimed novelist and writer during her day. Poet [Salvatore Di Giacomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Di_Giacomo) was one of the most famous writers in the Neapolitan dialect, and many of his poems were adapted to music, becoming famous Neapolitan songs. In the 20th century, philosophers like [Benedetto Croce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedetto_Croce) pursued the long tradition of philosophy studies in Naples, and personalities like jurists and lawyer [Enrico De Nicola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_De_Nicola) pursued legal and constitutional studies. De Nicola later helped to draft the modern [Constitution of the Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Italian_Republic) and was eventually elected to the office of President of the Italian Republic. Other noted Neapolitan writers and journalists include [Antonio De Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_De_Curtis), [Giancarlo Siani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giancarlo_Siani), [Roberto Saviano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Saviano) and [Elena Ferrante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Ferrante).[^cite_note-201] In Naples'44, An Intelligence Officer in the Italian Labyrinth (London, Eland, 2002), the acclaimed British travel writer Norman Lewis records the lives of the Napolitean people following the liberation of the city from Nazi forces in 1943.

Engraving of [Pulcinella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulcinella) in 1700

Naples was one of the centres of the peninsula from which originated the modern theatre genre as nowadays intended, evolving from 16th century *[commedia dell'arte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commedia_dell'arte)*. The masked character of [Pulcinella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulcinella) is a worldwide famous figure either as a theatrical character or [puppetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppetry) character.

The music Opera genre of *[opera buffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_buffa)* was created in Naples in the 18th century and then spread to Rome and northern Italy. In the period of [Belle Époque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_%C3%89poque), Naples rivalled Paris for its *[café-chantants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caf%C3%A9-chantants)*, and many famous Neapolitan songs were originally created to entertain the public in the cafès of Naples. Perhaps the most well-known song is "Ninì Tirabusciò". The history of how this song was born was dramatised in the eponymous comedy movie *[Ninì Tirabusciò: la donna che inventò la mossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nin%C3%AC_Tirabusci%C3%B2:_la_donna_che_invent%C3%B2_la_mossa)* starring [Monica Vitti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Vitti).

The Neapolitan popular genre of *[sceneggiata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sceneggiata)* is an important genre of modern folk theatre worldwide, dramatising common canon themes of thwarted love stories, comedies, tearjerker stories, commonly about honest people becoming [camorra](44945.md) outlaws due to unfortunate events. The Sceneggiata became very popular amongst Neapolitans and eventually one of the best-known genres of Italian cinematography thanks to actors and singers like [Mario Merola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Merola_\(singer\)) and [Nino D'Angelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nino_D'Angelo). Many writers and playwrights, such as [Raffaele Viviani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaele_Viviani), wrote comedies and dramas for this genre. Actors and comedians like [Eduardo Scarpetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Scarpetta) and then his sons [Eduardo De Filippo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_De_Filippo), [Peppino De Filippo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peppino_De_Filippo) and [Titina De Filippo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titina_De_Filippo) contributed to making the Neapolitan theatre. Eduardo's comedies and tragedies, such as *[Filumena Marturano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filumena_Marturano)* and *[Napoli milionaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Millions_of_Naples?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (which he also filmed as *[Side Street Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side_Street_Story)*), are well-known.

### Music

Main articles: [Music of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Naples), [Canzone Napoletana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canzone_Napoletana), and [Music conservatories of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_conservatories_of_Naples)



The interior of the [Teatro San Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_San_Carlo)

Naples has played an important role in the history of Western European art music for more than four centuries.[^cite_note-202] The first [music conservatories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_conservatories_of_Naples) were established in the city under Spanish rule in the 16th century. The San Pietro a Majella music conservatory, founded in 1826 by [Francesco I of Bourbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_I_of_the_Two_Sicilies), continues to operate today as both a prestigious centre of musical education and a musical museum.

During the late [Baroque period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_music), [Alessandro Scarlatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Scarlatti), the father of [Domenico Scarlatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Scarlatti), established the Neapolitan school of opera; this was in the form of *[opera seria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_seria)*, which was a new development for its time.[^cite_note-203] Another form of opera originating in Naples is *[opera buffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_buffa)*, a style of [comic opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_opera) strongly linked to [Battista Pergolesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Pergolesi) and [Piccinni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Piccinni); later contributors to the genre included [Rossini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioachino_Rossini) and [Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart).[^cite_note-204] The [Teatro di San Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_di_San_Carlo), built in 1737, is the oldest working theatre in Europe, and remains the operatic centre of Naples.[^cite_note-205]



Neapolitan [mandolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandolin)

The earliest [six-string guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-string_guitar) was created by the Neapolitan Gaetano Vinaccia in 1779; the instrument is now referred to as the [romantic guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_guitar). The Vinaccia family also developed the [mandolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandolin).[^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207] Influenced by the Spanish, Neapolitans became pioneers of classical guitar music, with [Ferdinando Carulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinando_Carulli) and [Mauro Giuliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauro_Giuliani) being prominent exponents.[^cite_note-208] Giuliani, who was actually from [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia) but lived and worked in Naples, is widely considered to be one of the greatest [guitar players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitarist) and composers of the 19th century, along with his [Catalan](6822.md) contemporary [Fernando Sor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Sor).[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210] Another Neapolitan musician of note was [opera singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_singer) [Enrico Caruso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Caruso), one of the most prominent opera [tenors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenor) of all time:[^cite_note-211] he was considered a man of the people in Naples, hailing from a working-class background.[^cite_note-212]

A popular traditional dance in [southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy) and Naples is the [Tarantella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarantella), which originated in [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia) and spread throughout the [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies). The Neapolitan tarantella is a [courtship dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtship_dance) performed by couples whose "rhythms, melodies, gestures, and accompanying songs are quite distinct", featuring faster, more cheerful music.

A notable element of popular Neapolitan music is the *[Canzone Napoletana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canzone_Napoletana)* style, essentially the [traditional music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_music) of the city, with a repertoire of hundreds of [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore) songs, some of which can be traced back to the 13th century.[^cite_note-festadipied-213] The genre became a formal institution in 1835, after the introduction of the annual Festival of [Piedigrotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedigrotta) songwriting competition.[^cite_note-festadipied-213] Some of the best-known recording artists in this field include [Roberto Murolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Murolo), [Sergio Bruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Bruni) and [Renato Carosone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renato_Carosone).[^cite_note-214] There are furthermore various forms of music popular in Naples but not well known outside it, such as *cantautore* ("singer-songwriter") and *sceneggiata*, which has been described as a musical soap opera; the most well-known exponent of this style is [Mario Merola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Merola_\(singer\)).[^cite_note-215]

[Totò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tot%C3%B2), a famous Neapolitan actor

Naples has had a significant influence on [Italian cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_cinema). Because of the city's relevance, many films and television shows are set (entirely or partially) in Naples. In addition to serving as the backdrop for several movies and shows, many talented celebrities (actors, actresses, directors, and producers) are originally from Naples.

Naples was the location for several early Italian cinema masterpieces. *[Assunta Spina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assunta_Spina_\(1915_film\))* (1915) was a silent film adapted from a theatrical drama by Neapolitan writer [Salvatore Di Giacomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Di_Giacomo). The film was directed by Neapolitan [Gustavo Serena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Serena). Serena also starred in the 1912 film *Romeo and Juliet*.[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218]

A list of some well-known films that take place (fully or partially) in Naples includes:[^cite_note-219]

- *[Shoeshine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoeshine_\(film\))* (1946), directed by Neapolitan, [Vittorio De Sica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_De_Sica)

- *[Hands over the City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hands_over_the_City)* (1963), directed by Neapolitan, [Francesco Rosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Rosi)

- *[Journey to Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_to_Italy)* (1954), directed by [Roberto Rossellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Rossellini)

- *[Marriage Italian Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_Italian_Style)* (1964), directed by Neapolitan, [Vittorio De Sica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_De_Sica)

- *[It Started in Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Started_in_Naples)* (1960), Directed by [Melville Shavelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melville_Shavelson)

- *[The Hand of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hand_of_God_\(film\))* (2021), Directed by [Paolo Sorrentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Sorrentino)

Naples is home to one of the first Italian colour films, *[Toto in Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toto_in_Color)* (1952), starring [Totò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tot%C3%B2) (Antonio de Curtis), a famous comedic actor born in Naples.[^cite_note-220]

Some notable comedies set in Naples include *Ieri, Oggi e Domani* (*[Yesterday, Today and Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yesterday,_Today_and_Tomorrow)*), by Vittorio De Sica, starring [Sophia Loren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophia_Loren) and [Marcello Mastroianni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcello_Mastroianni), *Adelina of Naples* (Academy Award-winning movie), *[It Started in Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Started_in_Naples)*, *[L'oro di Napoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'oro_di_Napoli)* again by Vittorio De Sica, dramatic movies like [Dino Risi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Risi)'s *[Scent of a Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scent_of_a_Woman_\(1974_film\))*, war movies like *[The Four Days of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Four_Days_of_Naples_\(film\))* by Sardinian director [Nanni Loy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanni_Loy), music and Sceneggiata movies like *[Zappatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zappatore)*, from the eponymous song by [Libero Bovio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libero_Bovio), starring singer and actor [Mario Merola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Merola_\(singer\)), crime movies like *[Il Camorrista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Camorrista)* with [Ben Gazzara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gazzara) playing the part of infamous [camorra](44945.md) boss [Raffaele Cutolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaele_Cutolo), and historical or costume movies like *[That Hamilton Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Hamilton_Woman)* starring [Vivien Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivien_Leigh) and [Laurence Olivier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Olivier).

More modern Neapolitan films include *[Ricomincio da tre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricomincio_da_tre)*, which depicts the misadventures of a young emigrant in the late 20th century. The 2008 Neapolitan language film *[Gomorrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomorrah_\(film\))*, based on the book by [Roberto Saviano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Saviano), explores the dark underbelly of the city of Naples through five intertwining stories about the powerful [Neapolitan crime syndicate](44945.md), as well as the [TV series of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomorrah_\(TV_series\)).

Several episodes of the animated series *[Tom and Jerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_and_Jerry)* also have references/influences from Naples. The song "[Santa Lucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Lucia_\(song\))" played by Tom Cat in *[Cat and Dupli-cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_and_Dupli-cat)* has its origins in Naples. "[Neapolitan Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_Mouse)" takes place in the same city.

The Japanese series [*JoJo's Bizarre Adventure*'s part 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JoJo's_Bizarre_Adventure_\(TV_series\)), Vento Aureo, takes place in the city.

Naples has appeared in episodes of TV serials such as *[The Sopranos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sopranos)* and the 1998 version of *[The Count of Monte Cristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Count_of_Monte_Cristo_\(1998_miniseries\))*, starring [Gérard Depardieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Depardieu).

### Tailoring

[Neapolitan tailoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_tailoring) was born as an attempt to loosen up the stiffness of English tailoring, which did not suit the Neapolitan lifestyle.[^cite_note-221] The Neapolitan jacket is shorter, lighter, quarter-lined or unlined, and has no shoulder padding.

## Sport



The [Stadio Diego Armando Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Diego_Armando_Maradona)



Wall painting of Maradona

[Football](10568.md) is by far the most popular sport in Naples. Brought to the city by the British during the early 20th century,[^cite_note-early_history-222] the sport is deeply embedded in local culture: it is popular at every level of society, from the *scugnizzi* ([street children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_children)) to wealthy professionals. The city's best known [football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_club_\(association_football\)) is [SSC Napoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSC_Napoli), which plays its home games at the [Stadio Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Diego_Armando_Maradona) in [Fuorigrotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuorigrotta). The club's stadium was renamed Stadio Diego Armando Maradona in honour of the Argentinian attacking midfielder who played for the club for seven years.[^cite_note-223] The team plays in [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A) and has won the *[Scudetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scudetto)* four times, the [Coppa Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppa_Italia) six times and the [Supercoppa Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercoppa_Italiana) three times. The team has also won the [UEFA Cup](232175.md),[^cite_note-calcionap-224] and once named [FIFA Player of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Player_of_the_Century) [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) among its players. Naples is the birthplace of numerous prominent professional footballers, including [Ciro Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciro_Ferrara) and [Fabio Cannavaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Cannavaro). Cannavaro was [captain of Italy's national team](362466.md) until 2010 and led the team to victory in the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup). He was consequently named [World Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Player_of_the_Year).

Some of the city's smaller clubs include [FC Neapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Neapolis) and [Internapoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internapoli_Camaldoli_S.S.D.), which play at the [Stadio Arturo Collana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Arturo_Collana). The city also has teams in a variety of other sports: [Eldo Napoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoli_Basket_\(2016\)) represents the city in basketball's [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A_\(basketball\)) and plays in the city of [Bagnoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagnoli). The city co-hosted the [EuroBasket 1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_1969). Partenope Rugby is the city's best-known [rugby union](25405.md) side: the team has won the rugby union [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A_\(rugby_union\)) twice. Other popular local sports include [futsal](188197.md), [water polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_polo), [horse racing](74711.md), sailing, fencing, [boxing](4243.md) and martial arts. The Accademia Nazionale di Scherma (National Academy and Fencing School of Naples) is the only place in Italy where the titles "Master of Sword" and "Master of [Kendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendo)" can be obtained.[^cite_note-fencing-225]
