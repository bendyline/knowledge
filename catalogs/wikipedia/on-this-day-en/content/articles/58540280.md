**David Joseph Malukas**[^cite_note-1] (born September 27, 2001) is an American [racing driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_driver) who competes in the [IndyCar Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series) driving the No. 12 [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet) for [Team Penske](2321219.md). He previously drove for [A.J. Foyt Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.J._Foyt_Racing) in 2025, [Meyer Shank Racing](30697656.md) in 2024, and [Dale Coyne Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Coyne_Racing) in 2022 and 2023.

## Early career

### Lower formula

In 2016 and the early part of 2017, Malukas competed in the [Formula 4 UAE Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_4_UAE_Championship) for Rasgaira Motorsports.[^cite_note-2] He did not contest the full season but still finished sixth in the championship.

Malukas would move to [ADAC Formula 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADAC_Formula_4) to compete for [Motopark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motopark_Academy) in 2017. There he would end up finishing nineteenth in the championship.

### U.S. F2000 National Championship

Along with his efforts in [ADAC Formula 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADAC_Formula_4), Malukas would compete in the [U.S. F2000 National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._F2000_National_Championship) in 2017 for [BN Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BN_Racing). At the first race at [Road America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_America), he took pole position and finished on the podium in second.

### Pro Mazda Championship

After competing part-time in the [U.S. F2000 National Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._F2000_National_Championship) the previous year, Malukas would move up to the [Pro Mazda Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Mazda_Championship) in 2018 again driving for [BN Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BN_Racing).[^cite_note-3] He would have an impressive season with three wins and six podiums to finish fourth in the championship.

### Formula Regional Americas Championship

Due to the [2020 Indy Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Indy_Lights) season being cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Malukas would compete in the [Formula Regional Americas Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Regional_Americas_Championship) for [HMD Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMD_Motorsports). He had a successful season claiming two wins and fifteen podiums to finish second in the championship.

### Indy Lights

In 2019, Malukas would once again move up a rung in the [Road to Indy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_to_Indy) ladder to the [Indy Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indy_Lights) series driving for BN Racing.[^cite_note-4] In August of that year, Malukas' father Henry acquired [BN Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BN_Racing)'s Indy Lights operation and established [HMD Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMD_Motorsports). Malukas would only have two podiums at [Circuit of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_of_the_Americas) and [Gateway Motorsports Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Motorsports_Park) respectively.

Malukas was set to compete in the series in 2020,[^cite_note-5] however the season was cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-6]

After 2020, Malukas returned to the series in 2021 once again with HMD Motorsports.[^cite_note-7] He would claim his maiden pole at the second race at Barber Motorsports Park and would go on to get his first win in the series.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Malukas would challenge rookie [Kyle Kirkwood](57671103.md) for the title all season but ultimately finished second in the standings behind Kirkwood. He would have his most impressive season in any category thus far with six poles, sixteen podiums and seven wins.[^cite_note-10]

## IndyCar

### Dale Coyne Racing (2022–2023)



Malukas driving for [Dale Coyne Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Coyne_Racing) at the [Indianapolis Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Motor_Speedway)

On December 15, 2021, it was announced that Malukas would drive the No. 18 entry full-time for [Dale Coyne Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Coyne_Racing) in partnership with [HMD Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMD_Motorsports) during the 2022 IndyCar Series season.[^cite_note-11] At Mid-Ohio, he finished a career-best ninth in his first top-ten of the season.[^cite_note-12] At the next race in Toronto, Malukas made it into the Firestone Fast 6 for a second time that season and qualified a career-best fifth.[^cite_note-13] Malukas took his first podium at [Gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bommarito_Automotive_Group_500), nearly passing leader [Josef Newgarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Newgarden) on the last lap and finishing second.[^cite_note-14]

In 2023, Malukas' second campaign with Dale Coyne started off with a top-ten finish in the chaotic season opener in [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_of_St._Petersburg), then saw him qualify ninth in the [2023 PPG 375](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_PPG_375) at [Texas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Motor_Speedway), and finish fourth in a multi-car battle for the win. His promising start went a bit awry, when he managed only two top-twenty results during the next six races in a row Following a last-place finish in the [Sonsio Grand Prix at Road America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Sonsio_Grand_Prix_at_Road_America), David shook off the bad luck by qualifying twelfth and recording a sixth place finish. The next stop at [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Honda_Indy_Toronto) would prove to be a let down, being credited with a twentieth place after retiring due to contact. Malukas went on to the double-header at [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hy-Vee_IndyCar_Race_Weekend) and recorded solid twelfth and eighth place finishes. The [Big Machine Music City Grand Prix at Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Big_Machine_Music_City_Grand_Prix) he qualified sixth, but suffered a mechanical failure, and was credited with a last-place finish. After a disappointing qualifying session, at the [Gallagher Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Gallagher_Grand_Prix), in Indianapolis, he moved up seven positions to finish sixteenth. Returning to [Gateway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Bommarito_Automotive_Group_500), the site of his breakout performance during the previous season, he came in third. At [Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BitNile.com_Grand_Prix_of_Portland), David qualified 24th, then worked his way forward to finish in eighth place. During the season finale in [Laguna Seca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Firestone_Grand_Prix_of_Monterey), he survived a shunt, which left him stranded in a gravel trap, then returned to finish twentieth.

### Arrow McLaren and other activities (2024)

On September 8, 2023, [Arrow McLaren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_McLaren) announced Malukas had been signed to a multi-year contract as driver of the No. 6 entry, beginning in 2024.[^cite_note-15] However, on the second weekend of February, Malukas tore ligaments and dislocated his left wrist in a [mountain biking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_biking) accident, and underwent hand surgery Tuesday, February 13. After missing four races, and with an unclear recovery timeline, he was released by the team on April 29, 2024.[^cite_note-16]

During his recovery from injury and the period without a race seat, Malukas took part in promotional and social media activities for the IndyCar Series. He covered various events, including the Indianapolis 500, which he stated helped him build more connections within the paddock.[^cite_note-\:1-17]

### Meyer Shank (2024)



Malukas driving for [Meyer Shank Racing](30697656.md) at [Mid-Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Ohio_Sports_Car_Course) in 2024

On June 7, 2024, it was announced that Malukas would be returning to IndyCar with [Meyer Shank Racing](30697656.md), replacing [Tom Blomqvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Blomqvist).[^cite_note-\:2-18] He debuted with the team at [Laguna Seca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Seca), during which he spun in early practice due to limited wrist mobility while adapting to a brace.[^cite_note-\:1-17][^cite_note-\:2-18][^cite_note-19]

### A. J. Foyt Racing (2025)

On August 13, 2024, it was announced that Malukas signed a multi-year deal with [A. J. Foyt Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Foyt_Racing) for the 2025 season and beyond.[^cite_note-20] He finished second after starting seventh at the [Indianapolis 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indianapolis_500). The following week, he qualified second for the [Detroit Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Chevrolet_Detroit_Grand_Prix), and ran amongst the top-five before contact with [Álex Palou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lex_Palou) resulted in a penalty, dropping him down the finishing order.

### Team Penske (2026–)



Malukas driving for [Team Penske](2321219.md) at the [2026 Good Ranchers 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Good_Ranchers_250) at [Phoenix Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Raceway).

On September 18, 2025, Malukas was announced to replace [Will Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Power) at [Team Penske](2321219.md) for 2026 on a multi-year agreement.[^cite_note-21] He started the [2026 season](80635394.md) with a 13th place finish at the [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Firestone_Grand_Prix_of_St._Petersburg). He scored his first career pole position and podium of the season at [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Good_Ranchers_250). During the [month of May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_500_traditions#Month_of_May), he finished second at the [Sonsio Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Sonsio_Grand_Prix). He then followed that up by finishing second at the [Indianapolis 500](80783201.md) in the race's closest ever finish, losing out to [Felix Rosenqvist](25501259.md) by 0.0233 seconds. On July 13, he went to the [White House](33057.md) to help promote the [Freedom 250 Grand Prix](82275853.md) along with [Álex Palou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lex_Palou) and Rosenqvist.



President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) (center) with three drivers involved in the race; from left to right: Malukas, [Álex Palou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lex_Palou) and [Felix Rosenqvist](25501259.md).

## Personal life

Malukas' parents emigrated from [Telšiai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel%C5%A1iai), Lithuania, in 1991.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] His parents later built a trucking company that expanded to a fleet of approximately 500–600 trucks.[^cite_note-\:1-17]

Malukas was born in [Chicago](6886.md) and is a United States citizen.[^cite_note-\:0-24] He has said in an interview that while also eligible for Lithuanian citizenship, he has not pursued it.[^cite_note-25] In addition to the American flag, Malukas marks his cars with the Lithuanian flag in honor of his ethnic heritage.[^cite_note-\:0-24]

In 2015, Malukas was one of the featured drivers in the *Kart Life* TV series, which provided a behind the scenes look into the world of young kart racers.[^cite_note-26]

In 2024, he moved to [Indianapolis](57707.md),[^cite_note-27] but relocated to [Charlotte, North Carolina](57447.md),[^cite_note-28] in the winter break before the start of the 2026 season.
