**North Kordofan** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): شمال كردفان, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Šamāl Kurdufān*) is one of the 18 [wilayat](579458.md) or states of [Sudan](27421.md). It has an area of 185,302 km<sup>2</sup> and an estimated population of 3,174,029 people (2018 est). [El Obeid](1481380.md) is the capital of the state. It borders [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern,_Sudan) to the north, [Khartoum](767612.md) and [White Nile](767622.md) to the east, [South Kordofan](767653.md) and [West Kordofan](767647.md) to the south, and [North Darfur](767666.md) to the west.

North Kordofan's climate is arid to semi-arid.[^cite_note-unicef-4]

## History

A [graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti) of a [Makurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makurian) king was discovered in Abu Negila[^cite_note-5] For centuries, North Kordofan was inhabited by nomads and pastoralists, mainly the Dar Hamid, Dar Hamar, Guamaa, Kababish, Bideriya, Shwehat and including the Yazeed tribes. The area has had almost continuous drought since the mid-1960s. Deforestation led to the destruction of the natural vegetation. NGOs working in the villages of Sudan tried to rectify the damage. They set up women's centres. These centres allowed many women gain an income. Training was introduced and a solar energy system set up. NGOs recognise that a need exists for longer projects requiring the kind of support that only can come from governments and large agencies.

### Sudanese civil war

At the beginning of the [Sudanese civil war](73556699.md) in April 2023 the [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF) captured the [El Obeid Airport](9265020.md) from the [Sudanese Army](27429.md) contingent. Throughout April and May, the Sudanese Army repelled several RSF assaults on the state capital. By June 2023, the RSF fully surrounded the city and [laid siege](74051914.md) to it.[^cite_note-6] The siege was lifted in February 2025, but resumed later that year. Amid warnings that a massacre like [the one in El-Fasher](81461601.md) could be repeated, the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council) adopted a statement on June 20 of 2026 expressing concern over the “imminent risk of mass atrocities” and demanding that the RSF immediately halt their attack on El Obeid.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] On June 23, seven European countries also called on the RSF in a joint statement to immediately halt their attack on the city. There were reportedly "credible signs of an imminent offensive."[^cite_note-9]

According to the [UNHCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHCR), people along the routes through Kordofan have been subjected to a documented pattern of executions, abductions, torture and abuse, sexual violence, and robbery.[^cite_note-10]

Due to the ensuing [refugee crisis](73638847.md) the state housed as of March 2026 around 231,000 [internally displaced persons](427408.md) (IDPs) according to the [International Organization for Migration](36893.md) (IOM) Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM).[^cite_note-11]

## Geography

### Climate

North Kordofan's climate is arid in the north and semi-arid in the south. The northern parts of the state receive 100 millimetres (3.9 in) of rain per year on average, while the southern parts receive up to 350 millimetres (14 in). The rainy season lasts from June to September. Rainfall is variable year to year, with no permanent rivers existing in the state. North Kordofan has been in state of drought almost continuously since the 1960s.[^cite_note-unicef-4]

### Mountains, hydrology and wildlife

North Kordofan is located in the southeastern [Sahara](325363.md). To the north in the [Northern State](767636.md) near the border with North Kordofan lies the [Gebel al-Ain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebel_al-Ain) mountain plateau, which stretches from southwest to northeast. Running south of this plateau, also in a southwest-to-northeast direction, runs the [Wadi al-Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_al-Malik), which flows into the [Nile](21244.md) near [Al Dabbah](26485047.md), outside North Kordofan. [Um Badr lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Um_Badr_lake?action=edit\&redlink=1) is situated within the wadi's catchment area, with the town [Umm Badr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_Badr) located on its western shore. The lake and its surroundings are designated as an [Important Bird Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Bird_Area) (IBA) by [BirdLife International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BirdLife_International); species found there include the [Nubian bustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_bustard) (classified as vulnerable by the [IUCN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN)) and the [Arabian bustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_bustard) (classified as near-threatened).[^cite_note-14] Another wadi is [Wadi Muqaddam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Muqaddam), located in the northeast of the state. Soil samples suggest that the [White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile) in earlier times flowed through it. The Kordofan region has four main aquifers. The groundwater lies at depths ranging from 241 to 310 meters and is used primarily for human consumption and watering livestock.[^cite_note-15]

In the far southeast of the state, south of the road from [Er Rahad](5775629.md) via [Umm Ruwaba](26735189.md) to the [White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile) near [Kosti](6671707.md), lies the [Khor Abu Habil Inner Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khor_Abu_Habil_Inner_Delta?action=edit\&redlink=1), an internationally important wetland designated under the [Ramsar Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_Convention) on April 10, 2022. During the rainy season between July and October, hundreds of temporary pools (known as "mayas") form amidst the dunes. This area, largely untouched by agriculture, also includes some remnants of forest and is an important habitat for waterbirds. Several endangered bird species, such as the critically endangered [Sociable lapwing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociable_lapwing) and the [Rüppell's vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCppell's_vulture), have also been seen here.[^cite_note-16]

## Economy



Market in El Obeid (2022)

The most productive agricultural lands are found in the east of the state, while livestock is raised in the semi-desert areas of the west. Due to long dry seasons, lower-income households typically engage in agriculture and wage labor from April to December. Outside this period, they pursue various non-agricultural activities to secure their livelihoods, such as odd jobs in urban centers or gold mining in more remote areas. Higher-income individuals generally derive their income from livestock farming and retail trade.[^cite_note-unicef-4] Traditionally, native [gum arabic trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gum_arabic_tree) (*Senegalia senegal*) are also tapped for [gum arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gum_arabic) production during the winter months from October to April. However, the northern limit of the trees and consequently the production zone has shifted southward from 15° N to 13° 45' N as of 2003 since the 1950s. Since these trees serve as a natural fertilizer in the region, this shift also impacts agriculture in the northern areas of the state.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

The state capital El Obeid is an important trading center through which trade between eastern and western Sudan flows.[^cite_note-unicef-4] The city is also home to an [oil refinery](195137.md) that, at full capacity, processes 15,000 [barrels](594043.md) per day, primarily into [heating oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heating_oil).[^cite_note-20] According to [Radio Dabanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Dabanga), the refinery's office was looted by RSF militias in June 2023.[^cite_note-21]
