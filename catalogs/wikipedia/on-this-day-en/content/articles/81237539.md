From 29 September to 4 October, the [Jammu Kashmir Joint Awami Action Committee](81214681.md) (JKJAAC) launched a region-wide shutter-down and wheel-jam strike across [Azad Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir) (AJK), demanding structural reforms, economic concessions, and an end to elite privileges.[^cite_note-auto2-5][^cite_note-auto1-6]&#x20;

The protest quickly escalated into violent clashes between demonstrators and security forces, resulting in 10 deaths and over 100 injuries.[^cite_note-Protests_end-3][^cite_note-auto-7][^cite_note-auto1-6][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-auto8-9] The government claimed to have accepted most of the demands, while protest leaders rejected such claims and called for continued protests until full implementation.[^cite_note-auto-7][^cite_note-auto2-5]&#x20;

## Background

The region of Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK), which is administered by Pakistan and often referred to as Azad Kashmir, has a history of periodic disputes over subsidies, governance, regional autonomy, and privileges granted to political elites. In [May 2024, protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Azad_Kashmir_protests) demanding subsidies for wheat and electricity escalated, resulting in the deaths of at least three protesters and a police officer. The government responded with promises of subsidies and reforms.[^cite_note-auto2-5][^cite_note-auto1-6][^cite_note-auto5-10]&#x20;

In 2025, the [Jammu Kashmir Joint Public Action Committee](81214681.md) (JKJAAC or sometimes AAC), a civil society coalition comprising traders, transporters, students, lawyers and other groups, re-emerged as a major driving force for protests in Azad Jammu and Kashmir.[^cite_note-auto5-10] In late September 2025, the JKJAAC announced a regional-level strike, sometimes referred to as a "lockdown", starting on 29 September, aimed at pressing a 38-point Charter of Demands.[^cite_note-auto7-11][^cite_note-auto5-10][^cite_note-auto4-12]&#x20;

## Demands

The demands included:&#x20;

- Abolition of special allowances and privileges for government officials.

  [^cite_note-auto5-10]

- Abolition of 12 seats in the [Azad Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Legislative_Assembly) reserved for Kashmiri migrants from [Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian-administered_Jammu_and_Kashmir).

  [^cite_note-auto5-10]

- Royalty from hydroelectric or energy projects in Azad Jammu and Kashmir.

  [^cite_note-auto5-10]

- Lower electricity tariffs and subsidized wheat/food assistance.

  [^cite_note-auto6-13]

- End of elite privileges and elimination of certain reserved seats in the government structure.

  [^cite_note-auto6-13]

## Timeline

### Pre-strike phase

- On 27 September, Prime Minister [Shahbaz Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahbaz_Sharif) publicly urged the JKJAAC to withdraw its call for an indefinite lockdown and return to talks.

  [^cite_note-auto7-11]

- The JKJAAC maintained its plan, saying the strike would begin on 29 September.

  [^cite_note-auto5-10]

- As preparations were underway, authorities imposed a communications blackout (internet and mobile services suspended) in parts of Azad Jammu and Kashmir from 29 September to prevent coordination of protests.

  [^cite_note-auto1-6]

### Strike begins

- On 29 September 2025, shutdown/wheel-jam strikes began in several cities of Azad Kashmir, including [Muzaffarabad](1119495.md), [Rawalakot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalakot), [Kotli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotli), [Mirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpur,_Azad_Kashmir), [Bhimber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimber), etc.

  [^cite_note-14]

  [^cite_note-15]

* Security was tightened as major roads and entry/exit routes were sealed, and law enforcement agencies were deployed in view of the unrest.

  [^cite_note-16]

- Rival rallies broke out in Muzaffarabad, a "peace rally" led by the Muslim Conference (a political group) clashed with JKJAAC protesters around [Neelum Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Neelum_Bridge\&action=edit\&redlink=1).

  [^cite_note-auto1-6]

* In the clash, police allegedly fired tear gas and, according to some eyewitnesses, fired live bullets. Several were injured and at least one protester (named Muhammad Sudhir) died of gunshot wounds.

  [^cite_note-auto1-6]

- The protests spread, and more clashes broke out in other parts of Azad Jammu and Kashmir in the following days.

  [^cite_note-auto4-12]

### Escalation and clashes

- On 1-2 October 2025, several deaths were reported in clashes between JKJAAC protesters and police in Muzaffarabad on 1 October. Four people were reportedly killed and over 100 were injured in the clashes. According to a police statement, three policemen and one civilian were among the dead.

  [^cite_note-auto6-13]

* Protest leaders rejected the government’s claims, saying that 12 of their supporters were killed and over 200 were injured.

  [^cite_note-auto5-10]

- Across Azad Jammu and Kashmir, at least nine people have been reported dead (including police and civilians) as a result of violent protests.

  [^cite_note-auto4-12]

* The government also announced that 172 police personnel had been injured in the protests, 12 of whom were in critical condition.

  [^cite_note-auto8-9]

- The government appealed to citizens to refrain from violence and misinformation. It warned that legal action would be taken against those involved in violent acts.

  [^cite_note-auto8-9]

## Casualties and injuries

Reports vary, but the protests resulted in casualties and property damage on both sides:&#x20;

### Killings

Three police officers and one civilian were killed in the Muzaffarabad clash.[^cite_note-auto-7] Protest leaders said that 12 JKJAAC supporters were killed.[^cite_note-auto5-10]&#x20;

Some media reports cite a total of 10 deaths in AJK (civilian + officials).[^cite_note-Protests_end-3]&#x20;

Some senior local government officials put the death toll at 15.[^cite_note-17]&#x20;

### Injuries

Over 100 people (police and civilians) were injured in the clashes in Muzaffarabad and across the region.[^cite_note-auto6-13] According to a statement from the AJK government, 172 policemen were injured, 12 of whom are in critical condition. Additional civilian injuries (reportedly \~50) have also been cited.[^cite_note-auto8-9]&#x20;

## Response and government reaction

- The [Government of Azad Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Azad_Kashmir) and federal officials said that 90 percent of the JKJAAC’s demands have already been accepted. They described the ongoing unrest as politically motivated.

  [^cite_note-auto6-13]

* The [Prime Minister of Azad Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Azad_Kashmir) [Chaudhry Anwarul Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhry_Anwarul_Haq) appealed to the JKJAAC to return to talks, warning that the violence would cost lives.

  [^cite_note-auto5-10]

- Security measures (internet/mobile shutdowns, deployment of security forces) remained in place during the protests to limit mobilization and communication.

  [^cite_note-auto1-6]

## Aftermath and continued developments

On 4 October, a peace agreement was reached, bringing an end to the protests.[^cite_note-Protests_end-3]&#x20;

The unrest sparked renewed discussions on structural reforms in the governance of Azad Kashmir, reserved seats, and the distribution of state resources.[^cite_note-auto5-10]&#x20;

The continued communication restrictions and the presence of law enforcement agencies have drawn criticism from civil society for restricting freedom of assembly and expression.[^cite_note-auto1-6]&#x20;
