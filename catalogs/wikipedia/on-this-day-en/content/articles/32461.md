The **foreign relations of Venezuela** had since the early twentieth century been particularly strong with the [United States](3434750.md). However, since the election of [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez) as [President of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Venezuela) in 1998, Venezuela's [foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez_administration) differed substantially from that of previous Venezuelan governments. This change in foreign policy direction no longer continues during the tenure of President [Delcy Rodríguez](44199228.md).

In 2001 Venezuela and Brazil opened a high-voltage power line between the two countries to supply electricity from Venezuela to energy-starved northern Brazil. The line provides cheap hydro-electric power to Brazil and also earns Venezuela tens of millions of dollars every year.[^cite_note-63] In 2007 Brazil and Venezuela pledged closer trade and energy ties, including building a new oil refinery in Brazil. The \$4.5bn refinery scheme to be completed in 2010 will be 40%-owned by Venezuela's state oil firm PDVSA, while Brazil's national oil firm Petrobras will hold the rest.[^cite_note-64] Venezuela purchased 1,500 tonnes of coffee beans from Brazil on 10 August 2009 after falling out with Colombia over its decision to allow an increased United States presence in its military bases.[^cite_note-Venezuela_shops_in_Argentina_after_Colombia_spat-60] On 18 June 2015, a mission of Brazilian senators led by Sen. [Aécio Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9cio_Neves) (mostly composed by opposition legislatives to Pres. [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff)) flew to Caracas with interest to visit Venezuelan prisoner [Leopoldo López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_L%C3%B3pez) and families of victims of the protests against President Nicolás Maduro. About a kilometer away from the [Simón Bolívar International Airport](8277069.md), the vehicle carrying the senators was prevented to continue the trip after being stopped and surrounded by government protesters.[^cite_note-65] Finally, the Brazilian senators decided to return to Brazil on the same day. ## Multilateral organizations

### Organisation of American States

By virtue of its geographical location, Venezuela became one of the members of the Organisation of American States (OAS) on "April 30, 1948" when they signed the "CHARTER OF THE ORGANIZATION OF AMERICAN STATES (A-41)".[^cite_note-179] On 21 December 1951, the treaty was ratified by Venezuela "IN ACCORDANCE WITH ARTICLE 145 OF THE CHARTER".[^cite_note-180]

In looking at the treaty, one would note that there are three parts with twenty-two chapters in total to the Treaty of the OAS.[^cite_note-oas.org-181] According to the OAS website. "Chapter XXI RATIFICATION AND ENTRY INTO FORCE, Article 141" the OAS Treaty or Charter "shall be registered with the Secretariat of the United Nations through the General Secretariat"[^cite_note-oas.org-181]

In accordance with "Chapter XIII THE INTER-AMERICAN COUNCIL FOR INTEGRAL DEVELOPMENT- Article 94 – The purpose of the Inter-American Council for Integral Development is to promote cooperation among the American States for the purpose of achieving integral development and, in particular, helping to eliminate extreme poverty, in accordance with the standards of the Charter, especially those set forth in Chapter VII with respect to the economic, social, educational, cultural, scientific, and technological fields."[^cite_note-182] To a large extent over the years, the people of Trinidad and Tobago have benefited from the generosity of the Venezuelans, whether it was due to their innate ability to give and share or whether it was provided by the articles of the Treaty as a means to share and for that they are grateful.

On 26 April 2017, Venezuela announced its intention to withdraw from the OAS.[^cite_note-183] Venezuelan [Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Minister_of_Venezuela) [Delcy Rodríguez](44199228.md) said that President Nicolás Maduro plans to publicly renounce Venezuela's membership on 27 April 2017. It will take two years for the country to formally leave. During this period, the country does not plan on participating in the OAS.[^cite_note-184]

#### Summits of the Americas

"The Summits of the Americas are institutionalized gatherings of the heads of state and government of the Western Hemisphere where leaders discuss common policy issues, affirm shared values and commit to concerted actions at the national and regional level to address continuing and new challenges faced in the Americas.[^cite_note-sota-185] To date there have been seven Summits of the Americas. There have also been at least two Special Meetings which were held between 1996 and 2004.

"At the Third Summit," which was held in Quebec, Canada in 2001 "leaders instructed their foreign ministers to prepare an Inter-American Democratic Charter which was adopted on September 11, 2001 in Lima".[^cite_note-sota-185]

The Fifth Summit of the Americas was held in Port of Spain, Trinidad and Tobago in 2009 was attended by Hugo Chávez on behalf of Venezuela. Also in attendance were Prime Minister Patrick Manning of Trinidad and Tobago and President Barack Obama of the United States of America.

The Seventh Summit of the Americas was held in Panama City, Panama on 10–11 April 2015.[^cite_note-summit-americas.org-186]

Peru will host the Eighth Summit of the Americas in 2018.[^cite_note-summit-americas.org-186]

With Venezuela having tribes of indigenous persons, given the geographical location of the country, recent developments in the oil and gas sector internationally and offshore, (such as Eliza I and II, the developments offshore of Suriname) the contributions of the Venezuelans to the OAS with respect to this area, that is, indigenous people may be significant going forward.[^cite_note-187]

The position of the OAS with respect to indigenous persons appears to be developing over the years. The Draft American Declaration of the Rights of the Indigenous Persons appears to be a working document. The last "Meeting for Negotiations in the Quest for Consensus on this area appeared to be Meeting Number (18) eighteen and is listed as being held in May 2015 according to the website.[^cite_note-189]

### Betancourt Doctrine

Main article: [Betancourt Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betancourt_Doctrine)

The [Betancourt Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betancourt_Doctrine), named after one of the first presidents of Venezuela's democratic era, Rómulo Betancourt "whereby Venezuela refused to maintain diplomatic relations with governments formed as a result of military coups, was adhered to by both administrations (first two presidents of Venezuela's democratic era). Although the doctrine was much praised, it gradually isolated Venezuela as most other Latin American nations became dominated by nonelected regimes."[^cite_note-190]

### Other international organisations to which Venezuela belongs

"In the democratic era, Venezuela has attempted to fulfill the principles of Simon Bolivar's ideals through a variety of means. It maintained active membership in the United Nations (UN) and its related agencies, other than this; the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC), the Organization of American States (OAS) and its related entities, and a host of other world and hemispheric organizations. In all these forums, Venezuela consistently aligned itself with other democracies" Venezuela and the United States belong to a number of the same international organizations, including the related agencies of the UN such as the International Monetary Fund, World Bank, and World Trade Organization.[^cite_note-191]

In October 2019, Venezuela competed for one of the two seats to the [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md), along with Brazil and Costa Rica, and was elected with 105 votes in a secret ballot by the 193-member [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md). Brazil was re-elected with 153 votes, while Costa Rica was not having garnered 96 votes and entering the month of the election as competition to Venezuela. The United States, [Lima Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Group) and human rights groups lobbied against Venezuela's election.[^cite_note-192]

When the UN General Assembly voted to add Venezuela to the [UN Human Rights Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Human_Rights_Council) in October 2019, US Ambassador to the UN [Kelly Craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Craft) wrote: "I am personally aggrieved that 105 countries voted in favor of this affront to human life and dignity. It provides ironclad proof that the Human Rights Council is broken, and reinforces why the United States withdrew."[^cite_note-auto6-193] Venezuela had been accused of withholding from the Venezuelan people [humanitarian aid](434679.md) delivered from other nations, and of manipulating its voters in exchange for food and medical care.[^cite_note-auto6-193] The council had been criticized regularly for admitting members who were themselves suspected of human rights violations.[^cite_note-auto6-193]

## International disputes

### Border dispute

Venezuela claims most of [Guyana](17238691.md) west of the [Essequibo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essequibo_River), in a dispute which dates back to the early nineteenth century and which saw the [Venezuela Crisis of 1895](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_Crisis_of_1895). It also has a [maritime boundary](29613061.md) dispute with [Colombia](5222.md) in the [Gulf of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Venezuela). The country also has an active maritime boundary dispute with [Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominica) over the [Isla Aves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Aves) archipelago located in the Caribbean.
