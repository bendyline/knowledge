The **Department of Health** (**DoH**, [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language): *An Roinn Sláinte*, pronounced [\[ənˠ ˈɾˠiːn̠ʲ ˈsˠl̪ˠaːn̠ʲtʲə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Irish), [Ulster Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Scots_language): *Männystrie o Poustie*) is a [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolved) Northern Irish government department in the [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive). The minister with overall responsibility for the department is the Minister of Health.

Until 9 May 2016, the department was previously called the **Department of Health, Social Services and Public Safety** ([Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language): *An Roinn Sláinte, Seirbhísí Sóisialta agus Sábháilteachta Poiblí*).

## Aim

DoH's overall aim and mission is to "improve the health and social well-being of the people of Northern Ireland."[^cite_note-3]

The department launched a new digital strategy in August 2022. It includes the Encompass programme which will create a unified health and care record for the province.[^cite_note-4]

## Responsibilities

The Minister, assisted by the department, makes policy and legislation in three broad areas:

- health and social care – hospitals, family practitioner services and community health and personal social services;

- public health – promoting and protecting the health and well-being of the population; and

- public safety – the [Northern Ireland Fire and Rescue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Fire_and_Rescue_Service) (commonly known by its former name, the **Northern Ireland Fire Brigade**) but not the [Police Service of Northern Ireland](294068.md) or [HM Coastguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Majesty's_Coastguard).

Some sensitive health policy issues are [reserved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_and_excepted_matters#List_of_key_reserved_matters) to Westminster and are therefore not devolved:[^cite_note-5]

- human [fertilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilisation) and [embryology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryology)

- human [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics)

- [surrogacy](1514981.md)

- [xenotransplantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenotransplantation)

In Northern Ireland, abortion law is a criminal justice matter and is devolved.[^cite_note-6] Since October 2019, abortion has been legal in Northern Ireland, with legal framework coming into effect in March 2020.

## History

Health policy in Northern Ireland was originally a responsibility of [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Northern_Ireland) and the [Ministry of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Home_Affairs_\(Northern_Ireland\)), which (similarly to the [Home Office](225695.md)) retained responsibility for policy areas not delegated to other ministries.

A separate [Ministry of Health and Local Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Health_and_Local_Government) was established in June 1944, as part of the [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state). In January 1965, that department was divided between the [Ministry of Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Development_\(Northern_Ireland\)) and the **Ministry of Health and Social Services**.

The latter ministry was renamed as the **Department of Health and Social Services** under [direct rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_rule_over_Northern_Ireland), introduced in March 1972. A health and social services ministry was also included in the [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive_\(1974\)) briefly established in 1974. The department was responsible for social security policy and its initials **DHSS** are still used locally to describe benefits and benefit claimants.

Following a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Northern_Irish_Belfast_Agreement_referendum) on the [Belfast Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfast_Agreement) on 23 May 1998 and the granting of [royal assent](153067.md) to the [Northern Ireland Act 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Act_1998) on 19 November 1998, a [Northern Ireland Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Assembly) and [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive) were established by the [United Kingdom Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Government) under Prime Minister [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair).

In December 1999, the Department of Health and Social Services was renamed as the Department of Health, Social Services and Public Safety. It gained responsibility for the [Northern Ireland Fire Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Fire_Brigade) from the [Department of the Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Environment_\(Northern_Ireland\)) but ceded social security to the [Department for Social Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Social_Development_\(Northern_Ireland\)). DHSSPS was therefore one of the six direct rule Northern Ireland departments to continue in existence following devolution, following the [Northern Ireland Act 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Act_1998) and [The Departments (Northern Ireland) Order 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Departments_\(Northern_Ireland\)_Order_1999).

A devolved minister took office on 2 December 1999. Devolution was suspended for four periods, during which the department came under the responsibility of [direct rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_rule_over_Northern_Ireland) ministers from the [Northern Ireland Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Office):

- between 12 February 2000

Since 8 May 2007, devolution has operated without interruption.
