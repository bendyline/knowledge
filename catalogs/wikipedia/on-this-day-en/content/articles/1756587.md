The **Coast Guard Administration of the Ocean Affairs Council** (**CGA**), also operating as the **ROC Coast Guard**, is charged with maintaining law and order; protecting the resources of the territorial waters of the Republic of China ([Taiwan](25734.md)), which surround [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan), [Penghu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penghu), [Kinmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinmen), [Matsu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsu_Islands), [Green Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Island,_Taiwan), [Orchid Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchid_Island), [Pratas Island](410487.md) (Tungsha/Dongsha), and [Nansha Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nansha_Islands); as well as providing a first line of defense along coastal areas against smugglers and illegal immigrants. The CGA is considered a civilian law enforcement agency under the administration of the [Ocean Affairs Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Affairs_Council) of the [Executive Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Yuan), though during emergencies it may be incorporated as part of the [Republic of China Armed Forces](30095.md).[^cite_note-2]

## Organization

The Coast Guard Administration is headed by one minister and three deputy ministers. The CGA includes eight departments, one office and five task forces, as well as a Maritime Patrol Directorate General and a Coastal Patrol Directorate General. Its jurisdiction covers the waters surrounding Kinmen, Matsu, Penghu, and the main island of Taiwan to ensure proper protection of the 1,819.8 kilometers coastline and 540,000 square kilometers of "Blue Territory," which is 15 times larger than the island of Taiwan.[^cite_note-CGA_Organization-3]

The Maritime Patrol Directorate General is responsible for all maritime patrols and operations at sea.[^cite_note-CGA_Organization-3][^cite_note-RAND_2017-4] The directorate consists of 16 Offshore Flotillas as well as the Northern, Southern, Central, and Eastern Flotilla Sectors.

The Coastal Patrol Directorate General is responsible for land based operations, primarily the patrolling of harbors, beaches and other coastal areas,[^cite_note-CGA_Organization-3][^cite_note-RAND_2017-4] and includes the Northern, Southern, Central, and Eastern Coastal Patrol Offices. There are several mobile investigative groups subordinated to four corresponding areas of responsibility of the Coastal Patrol Directorate General. All mobile investigative groups of the Coast Guard Administration are tasked to perform intelligence-gathering mission of State Security. While executing such intelligence-gathering function, The Coast Guard Administration is subjected to the supervisory and coordination from the [National Security Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Bureau_\(Republic_of_China\)).[^cite_note-5]

The [Special Task Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Task_Unit) is an elite special forces unit of the CGA similar to the [Military Police Special Services Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Police_Special_Services_Company) or the National Police Agency's [Thunder Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_Squad).[^cite_note-6] During the 36th annual [Han Kuang exercises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Kuang_exercises) they participated alongside special operations units from other branches in [anti-decapitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapitation_strike) drills.[^cite_note-7]

### Auxiliaries

The CGA has civilian coastal patrol volunteers which assist it. Volunteers provide assistance in search and rescue, border security, and marine debris removal. The number of auxiliaries has grown from 186 to a projected 6,000.[^cite_note-8]

## Scope

Article two of the Coast Guard Law splits the responsibilities of the CGA into three zones, their core area (Shoreline to the end of the Exclusive Economic Zone), Waters temporarily or tentatively within the area of law enforcement, and International waters fisheries patrol.[^cite_note-CGA_scope-9]

The "core area" includes all land within 500 meters of the high tide line, [Territorial waters](496983.md) (extending 12 nm from shoreline), the [Contiguous zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contiguous_zone) (extending 24 nm from shore), and the [Exclusive Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_Economic_Zone) (extending 200 nm from shore).[^cite_note-CGA_scope-9] "Waters temporarily or tentatively within the area of law enforcement" are waters within the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) were Taiwanese EEZs overlap with those of neighboring nations "where negotiations for delimitation have not yet reached a consensus."[^cite_note-CGA_scope-9]

The CGA conducts [fisheries patrols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitoring_control_and_surveillance) in [international waters](394357.md), particularly the north and midwest [Pacific Ocean](23070.md).[^cite_note-CGA_scope-9]

The CGA was established on 1 February 2000,[^cite_note-10] combining the Coast Guard Command (formerly under the Ministry of Defense), the Marine Police Bureau (formerly under the [National Police Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_Agency_\(Republic_of_China\)), Ministry of Interior), and several cutters from the Taiwan Directorate General of Customs, Ministry of Finance. The CGA formally unifies coastal and maritime law enforcement agencies.[^cite_note-11]

It has seen a great deal of action for a young agency, participating in numerous [search and rescue](172599.md) and anti-smuggling operations. The Coast Guard Administration was also recently involved in escorting Taiwanese fishing boats into waters disputed with Japan claimed by both sides as part of their exclusive economic zones.[^cite_note-RAND_2017-4]

In the late 2010s, China escalated to [grey-zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey-zone_\(international_relations\)) actions against Taiwan in an attempt to achieve unification with the self-governing island.[^cite_note-12] The Coast Guard Administration had to expand rapidly to meet the rising grey-zone challenge.[^cite_note-Focus_Taiwan_Feb_2021-13] China's grey-zone operations against Taiwan in the maritime domain are meant to establish presence while maintaining [plausible deniability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plausible_deniability).[^cite_note-14]

In May 2019 the CGA detained two Chinese fishing vessels for illegally fishing inside Taiwan's territorial waters. One vessel was 0.4 nautical miles off Taiwanese shores while the other was 2.1 nautical miles offshore.[^cite_note-15]

In May 2019 the CGA rescued six fishermen aboard a burning boat nineteen miles offshore.[^cite_note-16] Five fisherman were picked up by cutter while the most seriously injured was airlifted to hospital by helicopter. All fishermen survived the ordeal although three required hospitalization.[^cite_note-17]

As of 2019 the CGA planned to construct a total of 141 ships, including four 4000-tonne, six 1000-tonne, 12 600-tonne, 17 100-tonne, 52 35-tonne patrol ships and 50 coastal multi-purposed ships, by 2027.[^cite_note-FT_2019-18]

On March 1, 2020, three coast guard cutters clearing [illegal fishing](6346579.md) nets off Little Kinmen island were attacked by Chinese fishing boats which had to be repelled with warning shots from a [shotgun](26838.md).[^cite_note-Taipei_Times_2020-19]

On March 16, 2020, the patrol boats CP-1022 and CP-2006 of the 9th Offshore Flotilla based on [Kinmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinmen) were attacked by ten Chinese speedboats. They had been assisting a Kinmen County Government Fisheries Research Institute patrol boat in clearing fishing nets illegally left in Taiwanese waters by Chinese fishermen when they came under attack from the men in speedboats throwing rocks and bottles. During the incident CP-1022 was rammed at speed and lost the function of two of their three engines and its hull was damaged.[^cite_note-Taipei_Times_2020-19] The CGA responded to the attack using less lethal means including [stun grenades](662807.md) and [bean bag rounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean_bag_round) which caused the attacking boats to retreat.[^cite_note-20]

In July 2020 the CGA arrested all 18 crew members of a Chinese fishing vessel caught fishing illegally in Taiwanese waters. The interdiction followed an increase in illegal fishing in Taiwanese waters by Chinese fishing vessels.[^cite_note-21]

Between January and July 2020 the CGA chased 2,988 Chinese [sand dredging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_mining) vessels out of Taiwanese waters. In July 2020 the CGA seized a dredging vessel and arrested its eight crew as a warning to the rest.[^cite_note-22]

In August 2020 the CGA detained a small Chinese [oil tanker](15923603.md) which had illegally entered Taiwan's waters. The oil tanker was discovered during enhanced COVID-19 pandemic biosecurity patrols.[^cite_note-23]

In May 2021 the CGA detained a Chinese offshore supply vessel and its 12 crew. The vessel was caught trespassing in Taiwanese waters near [Penghu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penghu). It is believed that the supply ship was being used to deliver food and other supplies to offshore fishing fleets.[^cite_note-24]

In 2021 the Taiwanese cabinet approved a NT\$12.9 billion (US\$428.53 million) budget for six new 3,000-ton class patrol vessels.[^cite_note-25]

In May 2022 an Anping-class patrol ship launched a [Hsiung Feng II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsiung_Feng_II) anti-ship missile for the first time in a joint exercise with the Navy.[^cite_note-26]

Following an incident in 2025 which a Chinese owned vessel flying a [flag of convenience](402049.md) damaged an underwater telecommunications cable the CGA increased their monitoring of ships flying flags of convenience and increased the distance from shore at which they would be subject to routine boarding.[^cite_note-27] The captain of the vessel was later convicted on charges related to the incident and sentenced to three years in prison.[^cite_note-28]

## International cooperation

The CGA cooperates with Japan, the Philippines, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Vietnam in operations to counter human trafficking and drug smuggling. The ROC Coast Guard and the Japan Coast Guard conduct annual exercises and visits. The ROC Coast Guard and the Philippines Coast Guard have conducted tabletop exercises and drills. The ROC Coast Guard and the US Coast Guard cooperate on monitoring longline fishing, maritime law enforcement, human trafficking, and drug smuggling.[^cite_note-Glaser_and_Vitello_2015-29]

In 2010 the ROC Coast Guard held their first ever drill with China's [Maritime Search and Rescue Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Maritime_Safety_Administration). The drill, which simulated a ferry disaster between Kinmen and Xiamen, included 14 vessels, 3 helicopters, and 400 personnel. Due to the tense relations between the two countries participating forces used drill flags instead of their national flags and emblems. The second drill in 2012 involved 18 vessels and two helicopters from Taiwan, as well as 11 ships and one helicopter from China. A third drill in 2014 featured 33 vessels, four helicopters, and 550 personnel.[^cite_note-Glaser_and_Vitello_2015-29]

In 2020 the ROC Coast Guard and Chinese authorities worked out a standard operating procedure for dealing with illegal sand dredging by Chinese vessels. From implementation to December 2020 Chinese authorities had taken action in 64 cases and impounded 23 ships which demonstrated to the ROC Coast Guard that the Chinese were serious about cooperating on the issue.[^cite_note-30]

In March 2021 the ROC Coast Guard and the [US Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Coast_Guard) announced that they had signed a cooperation agreement, the agreement was promptly denounced by China.[^cite_note-31] In May 2021 US President Joe Biden praised the cooperation agreement while speaking at the commencement of the [United States Coast Guard Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Academy).[^cite_note-32] The first bilateral meeting under the agreement occurred in August 2021.[^cite_note-33]

Tuvalu and Taiwan signed a coast guard cooperation agreement in 2022. In 2023 Taiwan donated two new coast guard patrol vessels to Tuvalu.[^cite_note-34]

In July 2024, the ROC Coast Guard conducted a joint search and rescue drill with the [Japan Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Coast_Guard).[^cite_note-35]

In July 2025, the ROC Coast Guard signed a cooperation agreement with the [Somaliland Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Coast_Guard).[^cite_note-36]

In July 2026, the ROC Coast Guard and the [American Institute in Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_in_Taiwan) released photographs showing the [Chiayi-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiayi-class_patrol_vessel) *Taipei* sailing in formation with [*Midgett*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Midgett_\(WMSL-757\)) as part of maritime cooperation between Taiwan and the United States in the Taiwan Strait and the Western Pacific.[^cite_note-37]

In August 2026, Taiwan said the United States was training its coast guard in maritime patrols, law enforcement and small-boat operations after increased Chinese vessel activity around the island.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

## Fleet

In 2021 the CGA had more than 200 vessels.[^cite_note-Focus_Taiwan_Feb_2021-13]

In 2021 President Tsai Ing-wen ordered the name "Taiwan" to be prominently displayed on all vessels to avoid confusion with the People's Republic of China Coast Guard which is engaged in a [grey-zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey-zone_\(international_relations\)) campaign against Taiwan and other neighboring countries.[^cite_note-Focus_Taiwan_Feb_2021-13]

### Coastal surveillance

The CGA maintains a comprehensive coastal surveillance network. In 2021 the CGA allocated NT\$919.99 million (US\$33.33 million) to upgrade its coastal surveillance network and to add new gap filler radars as well as photoelectric sensors to the network.[^cite_note-55]

In 2025 following maritime boarder crossings by small boats and stowaways the Coast Guard Administration cooperated with the [National Security Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Bureau_\(Taiwan\)) to expand the network and to bring the sort of high tech solutions employed on the offshore islands near China to the main island of Taiwan. Deficiencies in the Coast Guard's coastal surveillance network were criticized by lawmakers who appropriated additional funds to the issue.[^cite_note-56]
