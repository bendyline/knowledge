**Aonishiki Arata** ([Japanese](15606.md): 安青錦 新大; born March 23, 2004 as **Danylo Yavhusishyn** [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Данило Явгусішин) is a [Ukrainian](31750.md) professional [sumo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumo) wrestler from [Vinnytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia), [Vinnytsia Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia_Oblast). He is the second Ukrainian to become a [professional sumo wrestler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikishi), after [Shishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shishi_Masaru) in 2020. He wrestles for [Ajigawa stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajigawa_stable_\(2022\)), at his highest rank, *[ōzeki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Czeki_\(sumo\)#%C5%8Czeki)*, which was the fastest promotion to the rank since the modern six-tournament schedule was introduced in 1958, accomplishing the feat in just 14 tournaments. He would then soon lose and immediately regain the rank over the course of the [July 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_in_sumo#July) tournament.

Aonishiki has posted double-digit wins in each of his first eight tournaments at *[sekitori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekitori)* status. In the top division, he won 11 matches in each of his first four tournaments before earning a [top division](985879.md) title on his fifth and sixth tournaments at that level of competition. Aonishiki earned a [special prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansh%C5%8D_\(sumo\)) in each of his first five top division tournaments, also earning a *[kinboshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinboshi)* on his third tournament.

## Early life and sumo background

Yavhusishyn started sumo at the age of seven,[^cite_note-nhk2-7] and placed third at the 2019 World Junior Sumo Championships.[^cite_note-nhk-8] He also has a [freestyle wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freestyle_wrestling) and [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) background.[^cite_note-asahikesho-9] He was inspired to try sumo after his seniors set up a sumo ring in his club. His interest grew thanks to the fast pace of the matches, which appealed to him.[^cite_note-numberjuly-10]

Yavhusishyn continued practicing sumo and won three gold medals at the Ukrainian national championships at the age of 17, with an unbeaten record.[^cite_note-Suspilne-5][^cite_note-kanal-6][^cite_note-baseball-11] At the same age, he finished ninth at the [2021 World Cadets Wrestling Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_World_Cadets_Wrestling_Championships) in the Men's 110.0 kg category.[^cite_note-12] At one point, he wondered about his professional future when the idea of becoming a professional wrestler took root in him after seeing an online video of the match between *[yokozuna](985879.md)* [Takanohana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takanohana_K%C5%8Dji) and then-*[ōzeki](985879.md#%C5%8Czeki)* [Asashōryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asash%C5%8Dry%C5%AB_Akinori) during the [2002 Autumn Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_in_sumo#Aki_basho).[^cite_note-numberjuly-10]

Yavhusishyn was accepted by a Ukrainian university, but because of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in February 2022, he decided to leave his country, first seeking [refuge](70199806.md) [in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum_in_Germany) before [arriving in Japan](42161679.md#Refugees_and_asylum_seekers) in April of the same year, using the friendship he had forged with Arata Yamanaka, [Kansai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_University) sumo club captain, an older amateur sumo wrestler with whom Yavhusishyn met at the 2019 World Junior Championships.[^cite_note-nhk-8][^cite_note-sponichi1-13][^cite_note-kyodofamily-14] In Japan, he was hosted at Yamanaka's apartment in [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe) ([Hyōgo Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%8Dgo_Prefecture)), where his host family spent their own money so that he could stay in Japan in the best possible conditions, helping him to cope with homesickness and loneliness, with Yavhusishyn calling them his "Japanese family".[^cite_note-asahiescaping-15]

Yavhusishyn trained with the sumo clubs of Kansai University and [Hōtoku Gakuen High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C5%8Dtoku_Gakuen_High_School) (Yamanaka's alma mater), despite not having student status.[^cite_note-asahikesho-9][^cite_note-kyodofamily-14][^cite_note-yahooiizuka-16] During this period, it was noticed that Yavhusishyn was already at a much higher level than his university counterparts, notably winning several matches against future [Isegahama stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isegahama_stable)'s wrestler [Toshinofuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshinofuji_Taisei), while the latter was competing at [Doshisha University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doshisha_University) after immigrating to Japan.[^cite_note-asahikesho-9] Yavhusishyn maintains a friendship with the club, by which he is nicknamed "Danya".[^cite_note-asahiescaping-15]

Through Kansai University sumo clubs, Yavhusishyn was introduced to Ajigawa (the former [Aminishiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aminishiki_Ry%C5%ABji)).[^cite_note-asahiescaping-15] In December 2022, Yavhusishyn was formally accepted as one of [Ajigawa stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajigawa_stable_\(2022\))'s first recruits.[^cite_note-baseball-11][^cite_note-sponichi1-13][^cite_note-kyodoaims-17] Initially, Ajigawa was reluctant to recruit Yavhusishyn, initially not wishing to recruit foreign wrestlers (subject to [recruitment restrictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikishi#Foreign-born_rikishi)) and concerned about the cultural and language barrier, but gave in to the young wrestler's determination.[^cite_note-nhk2-7]

## Professional debut

### Early career

Yavhusishyn officially joined Ajigawa stable's training camp before the July 2023 tournament, and took the new apprentice examination. After [obtaining a work visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikishi#Foreign-born_rikishi), he made his debut in the September tournament of the same year.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] He was given the *[shikona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikona)* **Aonishiki Arata** (安青錦 新大), a ring name created by merging the kanji for the color blue ([青](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%9D%92#Japanese)), referring to the [Ukrainian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ukraine), and the kanji meaning calm ([安](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%AE%89#Japanese)) and [brocade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocade) ([錦](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%8C%A6#Japanese)), from his master's own ring name (安美錦).[^cite_note-20] He himself chose to bear the first name Arata (新大), as it is that of Arata Yamanaka (山中新大), the former captain and now coach of the Kansai University sumo club that helped him find refuge in Japan.[^cite_note-asahikesho-9][^cite_note-sponichi1-13] Asked about this choice, Aonishiki replied "now I feel like we're fighting together".[^cite_note-kyodofamily-14]

During his first professional tournament, Aonishiki won the *jonokuchi* division.[^cite_note-sponichi1-13] At that point, he brought only the second championship to his stable after his stablemate Anōsho also won this division in July 2023.[^cite_note-21] In January 2024, he claimed his second championship with the *jonidan* division, after a playoff against [Kokonoe stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokonoe_stable)'s Chiyotaiko.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-kyodojonidan-23] In March 2024 tournament he was promoted to *sandanme*, but after six consecutive wins, he lost to [Kise stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kise_stable_\(2003\))'s Nagamura in his last match for the tournament, ending his 20 bout [winning streak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sumo_record_holders#Most_consecutive_wins_from_entry_into_sumo) since his debut.[^cite_note-24] For the May tournament he was promoted to *makushita* and finished with a 6–1 record. During the September tournament of the same year, Aonishiki rose to the top of the *makushita* division, and after the tournament it was reported that his score of 6–1 would normally be enough for promotion to the *jūryō* division.[^cite_note-25] His promotion for the November tournament was confirmed after the post-tournament [ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banzuke) meeting.[^cite_note-26]

### *Jūryō* promotion

Aonishiki is the first wrestler raised by former Aminishiki to reach *[sekitori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekitori)* status since the founding of his stable.[^cite_note-nhk2-7][^cite_note-jiji-27] He is also the second wrestler from Ukraine (after [Shishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shishi_Masaru)) to reach this rank, and the fifth fastest promotion since the introduction of the six-tournament system in 1958 after his debut (excluding *tsukedashi* system).[^cite_note-asahikesho-9][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

At the press conference for *jūryō* promotions, he admitted to being inspired by [his country's results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics) at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics), adding that he absolutely wanted to avoid showing an embarrassing level of sportsmanship.[^cite_note-jiji-27] He also commented that, "My master was promoted to *jūryō* at the age of 21. I wanted to make sure I didn't lose to him".[^cite_note-30] During his first *jūryō* tournament, Aonishiki recorded a double digit record.[^cite_note-yahooiizuka-16] Even though he is not a graduate from Kansai University, he was presented with a *[keshō-mawashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawashi#Kesh%C5%8D-mawashi)* by the university, because he trained there before his professional debut.[^cite_note-asahikesho-9][^cite_note-31] Another *keshō-mawashi* was presented to him following negotiations between one of his patrons, the president of the [Nakamura Keith Haring Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakamura_Keith_Haring_Collection) in [Hokuto, Yamanashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokuto,_Yamanashi). The apron features [Keith Haring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Haring)'s work "Earth, World", originally created in 1985 during the [Cold War](325329.md), representing a desire for world peace supported by people of different races and nationalities, echoing the war in Ukraine and a desire for world peace.[^cite_note-32]

During the 2025 January tournament, Aonishiki established himself as one of the leaders of the competition, rivalled also by Shishi, the only other Ukrainian wrestler in professional sumo. On Day 12, the two wrestlers faced off in the first match with two Ukrainian wrestlers in the history of the sport, with Shishi emerging victorious from the clash and taking sole leadership of the competition.[^cite_note-33] Although Aonishiki failed to win the championship, he solidified his chances of being promoted to *[makuuchi](985879.md)*, sumo's top division, for the March tournament.[^cite_note-34] After the tournament, Aonishiki was perceived by the press as the only European wrestler capable of reaching the top of the sport's hierarchy, despite the performances of his elder Shishi.[^cite_note-ukrweekly-35]

## *Makuuchi*

### Up and comer

Aonishiki was officially promoted to the [top division](985879.md) for the 2025 March tournament. This tied him with both [Takerufuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takerufuji_Mikiya) and the former [Jōkōryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dk%C5%8Dry%C5%AB_Takayuki) as the fastest to be promoted from debut to the top division, doing so in nine tournaments.[^cite_note-36] Coinciding with his promotion, his compatriot Shishi's return to the top division also makes their country, Ukraine, the seventh foreign country to have two *makuuchi*-ranked wrestlers in the history of professional sumo.[^cite_note-37]

Aonishiki downplayed the significance of this achievement, telling reporters: "I've just been following my stablemaster's instructions." He added that he wanted to give a strong performance, and would aim for double-digit wins and a [special prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansh%C5%8D_\(sumo\)) (*sanshō*) in his *makuuchi* debut.[^cite_note-38]

During the tournament, Aonishiki qualified as one of the most competent wrestlers, holding on to just three defeats until the twelfth day, recording a series of seven consecutive victories in the process.[^cite_note-39] On Day 6, he triumphed over his compatriot Shishi in the first *makuuchi* match between two Ukrainians in the history of the sport, claiming revenge on the latter after his *jūryō* championship loss in January.[^cite_note-40] On the final day, Aonishiki won his match against *sekiwake* [Ōhō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ch%C5%8D_K%C5%8Dnosuke), earning his eleventh victory by *[kirikaeshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite#Kirikaeshi)* ('twisting backward knee trip').[^cite_note-41] For his performance in the tournament, which ended on his birthday, Aonishiki was awarded the [Fighting Spirit prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansho_\(sumo\)), becoming the second wrestler to win such an award in the shortest time, behind [Takerufuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takerufuji_Mikiya).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]



Aonishiki during the 2025 May tournament.

During the 2025 May tournament, Aonishiki stood out as one of the leaders of the competition, recording seven victories and one defeat during the first half of the tournament, sharing the lead group after undefeated *ōzeki* [Ōnosato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cnosato_Daiki) with [Wakatakakage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakatakakage_Atsushi) and [Hakuōhō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haku%C5%8Dh%C5%8D_Tetsuya).[^cite_note-44] Aonishiki's winning streak came to an end, however, when he was defeated by *[komusubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komusubi)* Wakatakakage, a wrestler he admires, effectively taking him out of the title race with two defeats behind the tournament leader.[^cite_note-45] Although he struggled against the top-ranked wrestlers of the *[banzuke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banzuke)* (losing to *[sekiwake](985879.md)* [Daieishō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daieish%C5%8D_Hayato) and *[ōzeki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Czeki_\(sumo\))* [Kotozakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotozakura_Masakatsu_II)),[^cite_note-46] Aonishiki finished his second tournament with an eleventh victory, winning his second Fighting Spirit special prize after his victory over [Sadanoumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadanoumi_Takashi).[^cite_note-47] After his performances in his first two *makuuchi* tournaments, Aonishiki drew attention to a potential promotion to the *[san'yaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San'yaku)* ranks for the July tournament.[^cite_note-48] He just missed out on this potential promotion, instead being elevated to the top *maegashira* rank for July.[^cite_note-49]

During the 2025 July tournament in Nagoya, Aonishiki earned his first ever career *[kinboshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinboshi)* on the third day by defeating *yokozuna* [Hōshōryū](65166031.md).[^cite_note-50] This occurred in Aonishiki's 12th professional tournament, which set a new speed record for the fastest wrestler in the history of the six-tournament system (established in 1958) to get their first gold star. The previous record was 14 tournaments, which was set by former *ōzeki* [Konishiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konishiki_Yasokichi) in 1984 and matched by [Tomokaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomokaze_S%C5%8Ddai) in 2019.[^cite_note-51] By the midway point of the tournament he was one win off the lead, having won five matches against seven opponents ranked at *komusubi* or higher.[^cite_note-52] On Day 11 he moved into a share of the tournament lead.[^cite_note-53] He dropped out of the lead when he was defeated in his Day 14 contest by new *maegashira* [Kusano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusano_Naoya),[^cite_note-54] and was defeated by leader Kotoshoho himself the following day, who claimed his first top-division tournament as a result. Aonishiki was awarded runner-up in a five-way tie with [Atamifuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atamifuji), Kusano (who had also lost on the final day), Ōnosato, and [Tamawashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamawashi), also earning the Technique prize.[^cite_note-55] Prior to the start of the July tournament, [NHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK) released a documentary on Aonishiki titled *Blue Whirlwind: A Ukrainian sumo wrestler.*[^cite_note-56]

In September 2025, Aonishiki was promoted to the rank of *[komusubi](985879.md#Komusubi)*. With his promotion, he became the first Ukrainian to be promoted to *[san'yaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San'yaku)*, as well as the first *san'yaku* competitor for [Ajigawa stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajigawa_stable_\(2022\)). Having been promoted to *komusubi* in 12 tournaments, Aonishiki also set a new record for the fastest promotion to *san'yaku* since the introduction of the six-tournament system in 1958 (except wrestlers who entered sumo by way of *tsukedashi*), beating the 14 tournament record jointly held by former *yokozuna* [Asashōryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asash%C5%8Dry%C5%AB_Akinori) and former *ōzeki* [Konishiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konishiki_Yasokichi) and [Kotoōshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koto%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB_Katsunori).[^cite_note-57] His promotion also makes him the third wrestler in sumo history (excluding *tsukedashi*), after [Akebono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akebono_Tar%C5%8D) and Kotoōshū, to be promoted to the *san'yaku* ranks without ever having recorded more losses than wins since debut in the *jonokuchi* division.[^cite_note-58] During this tournament, he achieved on Day 9 his 100th victory since turning professional, becoming the fastest wrestler (excluding *tsukedashi* system) to reach this milestone since his debut, since the introduction of the seven-day tournament system for wrestlers in *makushita* and below in 1960.[^cite_note-59] Aonishiki finished the tournament with eleven wins, his fourth consecutive tournament in *makuuchi* with this score. For his performances, he was awarded the [Technique Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansh%C5%8D_\(sumo\)) for the second consecutive tournament.[^cite_note-60]

### Bid to *ōzeki* and first top-division championship

Aonishiki was promoted to the rank of *sekiwake* for the November tournament, once again setting some notable and historical achievements. In addition to being the first *sekiwake* wrestler for his stable and the first *sekiwake* wrestler from Ukraine, he was promoted to *sekiwake* in 13 tournaments, the fastest of any wrestler in modern sumo history (excluding *tsukedashi* competitors). This bested the previous record of 14 tournaments set by [Konishiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konishiki_Yasokichi). Additionally, he became the first new *sekiwake* since the 64th *yokozuna* [Akebono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akebono_Tar%C5%8D) in 1991 to secure winning records in every tournament while climbing from the bottom *jonokuchi* division to sumo's third-highest rank. Aonishiki told reporters after his *sekiwake* promotion that he was surprised and never imagined he would get this far, but wanted to aim even higher.[^cite_note-61]

With a fourth consecutive tournament with a score of eleven wins, several comments emerged about a potential promotion to the rank of *[ōzeki](985879.md#%C5%8Czeki)* after the November tournament.[^cite_note-62] More specifically, sumo commentator [Yasuo Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasuo_Fuji_\(journalist\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) expressed expectations for the wrestler, highlighting the lack of a second *ōzeki* on the *[banzuke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banzuke)*, his lack of *[make-koshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make-koshi)* throughout his career, and his recent victory over *yokozuna* [Hōshōryū](65166031.md).[^cite_note-63] Regarding the promotion, the head of the [Judging Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judge_\(sumo\)), Takadagawa (the former [Akinoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akinoshima_Katsumi)), commented that it might be too early, as normally the starting point for a promotion is the first tournament in the *san'yaku* ranks, but leaving open the question as to what score Aonishiki would achieve in November;[^cite_note-64] while Kumegawa (the former [Kotoinazuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotoinazuma_Yoshihiro)), another judge, commented that he would consider Aonishiki a potential candidate for promotion after November.[^cite_note-65]

During the tournament, he won his ninth victory on the tenth day in his match against [Tamawashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamawashi_Ichir%C5%8D), the oldest wrestler in the top division, who began his sumo career two months before Aonishiki was born.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] This match turned out to be the clash with the biggest age gap between competitors since [the war](32927.md), with nineteen years, four months, and seven days between the two wrestlers.[^cite_note-68] This record was then broken during the same tournament when Tamawashi faced [Fujinokawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujinokawa_Seig%C5%8D), with a twenty-one-year age difference between the two wrestlers.[^cite_note-69] With ten wins, Aonishiki was one of the tournament leaders alongside the two *yokozuna*. He faced [Ōnosato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cnosato_Daiki) on the thirteenth day. The outcome of the match, which saw the *yokozuna* emerge victorious, was controversial because Aonishiki and Ōnosato appeared to touch the ground and the outside of the ring at the same time without a *[mono-ii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judge_\(sumo\)#Mono-ii)* being called, prompting a reaction from the former [Mainoumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainoumi_Sh%C5%ABhei) (then an NHK commentator) who argued that a debate should have taken place. For his part, chief judge Takadagawa (the former Akinoshima) argued that since Aonishiki was in the air, he was the designated loser.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Aonishiki bounced back the following day, defeating *yokozuna* Hōshōryū (one of the wrestlers in contention for the title) for the third time in three career meetings. This also gave him his eleventh win, reaching the milestone of 33 wins in three tournaments normally required for promotion to the rank of *ōzeki*, and keeping him in the title race with Hōshōryū and Ōnosato.[^cite_note-72] On the final day, Aonishiki defeated *ōzeki* [Kotozakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotozakura_Masakatsu_II), qualifying him for a playoff against *yokozuna* Hōshōryū.[^cite_note-73] Aonishiki defeated the *yokozuna*, using an unconventional method in sumo by performing an Olympic wrestling quick duck under to get behind his opponent and bring him down by pressing on his knee.[^cite_note-74]

Aonishiki became at 21 years and 8 months the fourth youngest wrestler in sumo history to win a *makuuchi* tournament and made Ukraine the sixth foreign nation to win the title (after the United States, Mongolia, Bulgaria, Estonia, and Georgia).[^cite_note-75] With only fourteen tournaments since his debut, Aonishiki is also the second fastest wrestler (behind [Takerufuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takerufuji_Mikiya)) to win a top division trophy (excluding the *tsukedashi* system).[^cite_note-76] For his performances during the tournament, Aonishiki was awarded the [prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansh%C5%8D_\(sumo\)) for Outstanding Performance and Technique, thus equaling Ōnosato's record of winning a prize in each of his first five *makuuchi* tournaments.[^cite_note-77]

### Promotion to *ōzeki*

Just after Aonishiki's victory on Day 14, it was reported that the Judging Department of the Sumo Association would hold an extraordinary meeting on the final day of the tournament to consider a recommendation for *ōzeki* promotion.[^cite_note-78] After the tournament and Aonishiki's championship win, it was announced that the Judging Department formally issued their recommendation for promotion to chairman Hakkaku (the 61st *yokozuna* [Hokutoumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokutoumi_Nobuyoshi)), and that the Sumo Association board would meet on 26 November to discuss the promotion.[^cite_note-79] When he was informed about this in the customary [NHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK) victory ceremony interview, Aonishiki said that he was happy, "but there's [one more rank above *ōzeki*](985879.md#Yokozuna)" and he would be doing his best to get there. He also stated that he was nervous about his first playoff, saying: "I'm always a little nervous, but I was able to focus on my style of sumo."[^cite_note-80]

On 26 November 2025 the full Sumo Association board, in a meeting at the [Fukuoka Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Convention_Center), unanimously approved Aonishiki's *ōzeki* promotion.[^cite_note-81] In keeping with sumo custom, the board sent Asakayama (former *ōzeki* [Kaiō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai%C5%8D_Hiroyuki)) and Ōshima (former *sekiwake* [Kyokutenhō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyokutenh%C5%8D_Masaru)) to Ajigawa stable's training facility in [Kurume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurume) to personally inform Aonishiki of the promotion.[^cite_note-82] Aonishiki accepted, saying that he would strive to live up to his new rank and "aim even higher."[^cite_note-83]

Aonishiki's 14 tournaments from his debut to sumo's second-highest rank surpassed [Kotoōshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koto%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB_Katsunori)'s 19 tournaments for the fastest such promotion in modern sumo history, excluding *tsukedashi* competitors.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] The promotion also made him the fourth youngest wrestler to achieve this rank since the introduction of the six-tournament-a-year system (behind [Takanohana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takanohana_K%C5%8Dji), [Kitanoumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitanoumi_Toshimitsu), and [Hakuhō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakuh%C5%8D_Sh%C5%8D)). Furthermore, he is only the third wrestler in the history of the sport to earn promotion without ever suffering a *[make-koshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make-koshi)* since his debut in *[jonokuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonokuchi)* (behind [Musashiyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musashiyama_Takeshi) and [Haguroyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haguroyama_Masaji)) and the first to achieve this feat under the system of six tournaments per year. This promotion also makes him the first wrestler in 89 years (since [Futabayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futabayama_Sadaji)) to be directly promoted to the rank of *ōzeki* after winning a maiden *makuuchi* tournament as a newly promoted *sekiwake*.[^cite_note-sponichistats-86]

Immediately after the announcement of Aonishiki's promotion, and following global attention on his achievements, it was announced that a French documentary film, produced by the same team that produced *Les rêves ne meurent jamais* (Dreams Never Die) about [Yannick Bestaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yannick_Bestaven), would go into production and be released in 2027.[^cite_note-87] In December 2025 [Empress Masako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Masako) spoke about Aonishiki in her birthday message, saying that she was deeply moved by his story. The Empress referenced both Aonishiki and Japanese baseball player [Shohei Ohtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shohei_Ohtani), saying that the two gave courage and hope to many people by "opening up new possibilities through their daily efforts."[^cite_note-88]

### Second Emperor's Cup and *yokozuna* bid

Aonishiki got off to a strong start in his first tournament as an *ōzeki*, suffering only two losses by the halfway point of the tournament.[^cite_note-89] On Day 8, although defeated by [Kirishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishima_Tetsuo), he was granted an audience with Emperor [Naruhito](266108.md), [Empress Masako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Masako) and [Princess Aiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiko,_Princess_Toshi) who had attended the day's matches.[^cite_note-90] On Day 12 of the January tournament Aonishiki was seen wearing a different color of *[mawashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawashi)*, having switched from the blue one that he had been wearing for several tournaments to a black one. The black *mawashi* was worn in past competition by his stablemaster Ajigawa (former *sekiwake* [Aminishiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aminishiki_Ry%C5%ABji)), and had recently been presented to him as a gift. It is reportedly considered rare for a sumo wrestler to change his belt in the middle of a tournament. Aonishiki said at the time that he always wanted to wear the belt if he had the chance, and that it would be a waste not to wear it.[^cite_note-91] Entering the fifteenth and final day of competition, he and *maegashira*-ranked [Atamifuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atamifuji_Sakutar%C5%8D) were tied with 11 wins each. After Atamifuji won his match, Aonishiki–for the second tournament in a row–defeated *ōzeki* [Kotozakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotozakura_Masakatsu_II) to force a playoff.[^cite_note-92] In the deciding contest, Aonishiki took down Atamifuji using a [headlock throw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite#Kubinage) after being pressured towards the outer edges of the *[dohyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dohy%C5%8D)*. The championship made Aonishiki the first newly promoted *ōzeki* to win the Emperor's Cup since [Hakuhō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakuh%C5%8D_Sh%C5%8D) in May 2006, and also made him the first to achieve the feat of winning consecutive tournaments as a newly promoted *sekiwake* and a newly promoted *ōzeki* since [Futabayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futabayama_Sadaji) won consecutive tournaments in 1936 and 1937 (when only two tournaments were held per year).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-kyodo250125-94] Aonishiki became a candidate for promotion to the sport's highest rank of *yokozuna* at the March 2026 tournament, a possibility if he were to secure another championship or an equivalent performance.[^cite_note-kyodo250125-94] Commenting after the tournament, Aonishiki said he felt relaxed that it was over because "every one of the 15 days was tense." Regarding the potential *yokozuna* promotion, Aonishiki said that if there was an opportunity, he wanted to grab it as soon as possible.[^cite_note-95]

Aonishiki's *yokozuna* bid was put in jeopardy early on in the 2026 March tournament. By the end of the first week, he had already suffered four losses, leaving him virtually unable to win the championship.[^cite_note-96] During the second week, Aonishiki managed to stay in contention with a 7–7 record by defeating [Kirishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishima_Tetsuo), though Kirishima still clinched the championship that day thanks to his lead in wins over his competitors in the title race.[^cite_note-97] With Aonishiki aiming to finish with a winning record on the final day of the tournament, he absolutely had to win his last match against *yokozuna* [Hōshōryū](65166031.md). Aonishiki lost the match by *[kakenage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite#Kakenage)*, recording the first negative record of his career and placing him in *[kadoban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#Kadoban)* status for the May tournament.[^cite_note-98]

### Demotion, comeback to *ōzeki* and third Emperor's Cup

After the March 2026 tournament ended, it was revealed that Aonishiki had fractured the little toe on his left foot at some point during the tournament, also forcing Aonishiki to withdraw from the Spring Tour to focus on recovery.[^cite_note-99] At first, his recovery was going well; as he was training regularly and planning to participate in the *[sōken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#s%C5%8Dken)* training session taking place on May 1.[^cite_note-100] On May 6, however, news reports indicated that Aonishiki had been injured again, this time during a training session at [Arashio stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arashio_stable). According to reports, Aonishiki was thrown to the ground and was unable to get back up due to pain in his ankle, requiring medical assistance.[^cite_note-101] It was later announced that he would withdraw from the May tournament due to this ankle injury. Because Aonishiki was *[kadoban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#Kadoban)* from his losing record in March, his absence from the May tournament meant that demotion from *ōzeki* was a certainty. This was the first time he had ever withdrawn from a competition.[^cite_note-102]

When the *[banzuke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banzuke)* for the July tournament was unveiled, it was indeed confirmed that Aonishiki had been demoted to the rank of *[sekiwake](985879.md)*. As sumo rules allow, he could regain his rank immediately for the September tournament by recording 10 wins.[^cite_note-103] Before the tournament, Aonishiki and his master (the former *sekiwake* [Aminishiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aminishiki_Ry%C5%ABji)) made no secret of their hope not only to regain the rank of *ōzeki* but also to claim the [championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%ABsh%C5%8D).[^cite_note-104] Aonishiki performed well early in the tournament, racking up three consecutive wins since the start of the tournament.[^cite_note-105] On the fourth day, however, he suffered a loss to [Yoshinofuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshinofuji_Naoya).[^cite_note-106] The next day, he bounced back and won a match against [Kotozakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotozakura_Masakatsu_II).[^cite_note-107] On the ninth and tenth days, he won his bouts against the two *yokozuna*, [Ōnosato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cnosato_Daiki) and [Hōshōryū](65166031.md), respectively.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] Finally, on the eleventh day, Aonishiki earned his tenth victory against [Gōnoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C5%8Dnoyama_T%C5%8Dki) (a match that attracted a small amount of controversy),[^cite_note-110] stringing together seven consecutive wins and securing his return to the rank of *ōzeki* for the September tournament. This victory also ensured that he remained in the lead in the title race.[^cite_note-111] This achievement makes Aonishiki the seventh wrestler to regain the rank of *ōzeki* by winning ten bouts after an immediate demotion to the rank of *sekiwake* (the first since [Takakeishō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takakeish%C5%8D_Takanobu) in September 2019) and the tenth wrestler overall to regain that rank after being demoted from it.[^cite_note-112] Before the start of the tournament's final day, it was announced that Aonishiki would receive a fourth [special prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansh%C5%8D_\(sumo\)) for Technique for his performance during the tournament.[^cite_note-113] In the final bouts of the tournament, Aonishiki lost his match to [Atamifuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atamifuji_Sakutar%C5%8D), triggering a playoff involving the two wrestlers and [Kirishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishima_Tetsuo)—a first in nine tournaments.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] Aonishiki defeated Atamifuji before beating Kirishima and winning his third title. This championship victory makes Aonishiki the first wrestler in the history of the sport to be demoted from the *ōzeki* rank, regain that rank in a single tournament, and win the championship in the process.[^cite_note-116] Following repromotion to *ōzeki*, Aonishiki then joined the Summer Tour spanning August despite ongoing foot injury, engaging only in ritual, light exercise and fan interactions.[^cite_note-117]

In September, Aonishiki got off to a rough start in the tournament, posting a 3–3 record on the sixth day.[^cite_note-118] He nevertheless maintained his position among the contenders chasing [Ōnosato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cnosato_Daiki), the tournament leader, recording eight consecutive victories, ultimately leading to a showdown for the championship on the final day.[^cite_note-119] Ōnosato defeated Aonishiki by *[tsukidashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite#Tsukidashi)*, leaving him with a score of 11–4.[^cite_note-120]

## Fighting style

Aonishiki's most common *[kimarite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite)*, or winning technique, is *yori-kiri* ('frontal force out'), and he prefers a *migi-yotsu*, or left hand outside, right hand inside grip on his opponent's *[mawashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawashi)*.

Aonishiki does not have a large stature, but he makes up for this with his technical ability. In 2025, during his rise to the rank of *[ōzeki](985879.md#%C5%8Czeki)*, he was on average 7 cm (3 in) shorter and 36 kg (79 lb; 5.7 st) lighter than other members of the top division.[^cite_note-gunningtechnique-121]

Some commentators have stated that his mastery of hand positions and grappling techniques are the best that professional sumo has seen in the last ten years.[^cite_note-asahikesho-9][^cite_note-gunningtechnique-121] This technical ability sets him apart from previous high-ranking wrestlers of European origin, who typically relied on their size and physical strength.[^cite_note-ukrweekly-35][^cite_note-122]

During the *tachi-ai* (initial charge), one of Aonishiki's trademarks is to keep his hips high, unlike more common techniques of starting the bout as low as possible.[^cite_note-gunningtechnique-121] It was also noted that he possesses excellent stability, allowing him to stay low and gain advantage over his opponents by raising their center of gravity.[^cite_note-123] This stability greatly reduces the chances of defeating him with *[hatakikomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite#Hatakikomi)* ('slap down'), an occurrence that has happened only three times between his debut and his promotion to *ōzeki*, or *[hikiotoshi ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite#Hikiotoshi)* ('pull down'), with no occurrences.[^cite_note-gunningtechnique-121] On this subject, Takekuma (the former [Gōeidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C5%8Deid%C5%8D_G%C5%8Dtar%C5%8D)), praised him saying "you can't slap him down".[^cite_note-kyodo290725-124] His low stance is even seen as a trap for his opponents, instinctively triggering them to attempt to throw him to the ground, leaving their bodies exposed and their arms too high.[^cite_note-gunningtechnique-121]

Thanks to his technical skills, Aonishiki is often seen winning his fights with rare techniques, including *[kirikaeshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite#Kirikaeshi)* ('twisting backward knee trip'), *[okurinage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite#Okurinage)* ('rear throw down') and *[uchimusō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite#Uchimus%C5%8D)* ('inner thigh propping twist down').[^cite_note-nhk2-7] This latter became known as his trademark, due to his ease in deploying it in a division where it is difficult to use and the significant victories he has achieved over higher-ranked opponents (such as [Kotozakura II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotozakura_Masakatsu_II)) thanks to it.[^cite_note-gunningtechnique-121] When asked about his ease in using it, he replied that it was quite similar to what he knew from freestyle wrestling and that he enjoyed the destabilizing aspect of the technique.[^cite_note-125]

Aonishiki also puts in great effort when trying to gain weight, and also works on his strength training, lifting weights of 200 kg (440 lb; 31 st) in the [bench press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_press).[^cite_note-yahooiizuka-16] He is also known for his aptitude for fast, head-on attacks.[^cite_note-kyodoaims-17]

Since his amateur days, Aonishiki cites that he has been studying the style of wrestlers with the same build as himself, such as former *[yokozuna](985879.md)* [Wakanohana Masaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakanohana_Masaru) or his master (the former [Aminishiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aminishiki_Ry%C5%ABji)), one of the smallest yet recognized as one of the most technical wrestler in the 2000s and early 2010s.[^cite_note-gunningtechnique-121][^cite_note-nhk2-7][^cite_note-yahooiizuka-16][^cite_note-kyodoaims-17] He also studies the matches of former and [Chiyonofuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiyonofuji_Mitsugu).[^cite_note-126] Aonishiki is also inspired by [Wakatakakage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakatakakage_Atsushi), who helped him train in June 2024.[^cite_note-nhk2-7][^cite_note-127] Following Aonishiki's third straight 11-win tournament in the top division in July 2025, Kokonoe (former *ōzeki* [Chiyotaikai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiyotaikai_Ry%C5%ABji)) compared Aonishiki's style to that of Tamanoi (former *ōzeki* [Tochiazuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochiazuma_Daisuke)) when he was climbing up the *makuuchi* ranks.[^cite_note-kyodo290725-124]

Aonishiki also received praise from Asakayama (the former [Kaiō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai%C5%8D_Hiroyuki)), who noted his dedication and desire to improve during practice sessions.[^cite_note-kyodo290725-124] Prior to Aonishiki's November 2025 tournament victory, Kokonoe commented that Aonishiki was a "true powerhouse". He noted that Aonishiki excelled in fights against *yokozuna* (referring to his perfect 3-for-3 record against [Hōshōryū](65166031.md) at the time of his remark), and said that he had never seen a wrestler rise through the top division ranks with such consistency.[^cite_note-128]

## Personal life

Aonishiki has an older brother who stayed in Ukraine.[^cite_note-129] Both his parents have refugee status and now live in [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf), [Germany](11867.md), where they manage a [dry cleaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_cleaning) shop.[^cite_note-kyodojonidan-23][^cite_note-familystat-130] When it came to deciding to move to Japan, Aonishiki was able to count on the support of his parents, who encouraged him to obtain a visa.[^cite_note-asahiescaping-15] Aonishiki was reunited with his parents for the first time since he left home when he invited them to the 2025 May tournament in Tokyo and again when the Japan Sumo Association held its exhibition tournament in Paris in June 2026.[^cite_note-131]

Aonishiki is fluent in Ukrainian and Russian, and he speaks English at an intermediate level.[^cite_note-asahikesho-9][^cite_note-yahooiizuka-16][^cite_note-familystat-130] At the time of his recruitment into the professional world, it was noted that Aonishiki had already become fluent in Japanese, which he learned after arriving in Japan.[^cite_note-asahikesho-9][^cite_note-kyodoaims-17]
