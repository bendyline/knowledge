**Taiyuan**[^cite_note-7] ([Taiyuan Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_Chinese): [\[tʰai˦˥ ye˩˩\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Jin_Chinese)) is the capital of [Shanxi](81262.md), China. It is the political, economic, cultural, and international exchange center of the province, containing an industrial base focusing on energy and heavy chemicals. Throughout its long history, Taiyuan was the capital or provisional capital of many dynasties in China, hence the name *Lóngchéng* (龙城; *The City of the Dragon*).[^cite_note-太原市政府网站-历史沿革-8]

As of 2021, the city governed six districts and three counties, with a total area of 6,988 square kilometers and a permanent population of 5,390,957.[^cite_note-citypopulation.de-1]

Taiyuan is located roughly in the centre of Shanxi, with the [Fen River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fen_River) flowing through the centre of the city.[^cite_note-9]

## Etymology and names

The two Chinese characters of the city's name are [太](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%A4%AA#Chinese) (*tài*, "great") and [原](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%8E%9F#Chinese) (*yuán*, "plain"), referring to the location where the [Fen River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fen_River) leaves the mountains and enters a relatively flat plain. Throughout its long history, the city had various names, including *Bīngzhōu* (并州) (from which its abbreviated single-character name *Bīng* (并) is derived), *Jìnyáng* (晋阳), and *Lóngchéng* (龙城).[^cite_note-10]

During the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) and subsequent [Five Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms_period), the status of the city of Taiyuan was elevated to be the northern capital, hence the names *Běidū* (北都) and *Běijīng* (北京, different from present-day [Beijing](18603746.md)).[^cite_note-汉典-三京-11]

## History

Taiyuan is an ancient city with more than 2,500 years of urban history, dating back to 497 BC. It was the capital or secondary capital ([陪](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%99%AA)[都](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%83%BD), [别](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%88%AB)[都](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%83%BD)) of [Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_\(state\)), [Former Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Qin), [Eastern Wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Wei), [Northern Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Qi), [Northern Jin](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8C%97%E6%99%8B), [Later Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Tang), [Later Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Jin_\(Five_Dynasties\)), [Later Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Han_\(Five_Dynasties\)), and [Northern Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Han). Its strategic location and rich history make Taiyuan one of the economic, political, military, and cultural centers of northern China.[^cite_note-太原市政府网站-历史沿革-8]

### Pre-Qin dynasty history

From about 859 BC, the area around modern-day Taiyuan was occupied by the [Rong people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rong_people). In 662 BC, these were driven out by the [Di](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beidi) people.[^cite_note-先秦史籍中的太原-12]

In 497 BC, the first ancient city of Jinyang was built around the southern Jinyuan district of present-day Taiyuan by Dong Anyu ([董](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%91%A3#Chinese)[安](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%AE%89#Chinese)[于](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E4%BA%8E#Chinese)), who was a steward of [Zhao Jianzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Jianzi?action=edit\&redlink=1) (赵鞅), an upper-level official of the state of Jin.[^cite_note-太原市政府网站-历史沿革-8][^cite_note-13]

During the [Battle of Jinyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jinyang) in 453 BC, [Zhi Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhi_Yao) diverted the flow of the Fen River to inundate the city of Jinyang, causing significant damage to the Zhao. Later, [Zhao Xiangzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Xiangzi) alerted the Wei and Han, who both decided to ally with the Zhao. On the night of 8 May 453 BC, Zhao troops broke the dams of the Fen River and let the river flood the Zhi armies, which they eventually annihilated, with help from the Wei and Han.[^cite_note-14]

The [partition of Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Jin) happened in 403 BC, when the state of Jin, then a strong power in northern China, was divided into the three smaller states of [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_\(Warring_States\)), [Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_\(state\)), and [Wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_\(state\)). This event is the watershed between the [Spring and Autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period) and [Warring States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period) periods in Chinese history.

### Qin dynasty

In 248 BC, the [state of Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_\(state\)) attacked Zhao under General [Meng'ao](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%92%99%E9%AA%9C) and obtained the area around Jinyang. Qin set up the [Commandery of Taiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_Commandery) (太原郡), with the city of Jinyang as its administrative center. Although the name "Taiyuan" had appeared in historic records before, potentially referring to different regions in present-day southern and central [Shanxi](81262.md), this was the first time the name was officially used to refer to what is today Taiyuan.[^cite_note-先秦史籍中的太原-12]

Between 229 and 228 BC, the Qin general [Li Xin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Xin_\(Qin\)) led two armies from the cities of Taiyuan and Yunzhong to attack Zhao's northern commandery of Dai. Three months after General [Li Mu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Mu)'s death, Wang Jian, Li Xin, and Qiang Lei conquered Zhao.

In 221 BC, [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty) conquered the rest of China and officially started the nation's first imperial dynasty. Qin established 36 [commanderies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandery_\(China\)) on its territory, and Taiyuan was one of them, with Jinyang as its capital.[^cite_note-taiyuan.gov.cn-15]

### Jin dynasty and Sixteen Kingdoms

During the [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(265%E2%80%93420\)), Taiyuan was again transformed into a [vassal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_state). Following the ending of the Jin dynasty, [ethnic minority peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Barbarians) settled a series of short-lived sovereign states in northern China, commonly referred to as [Sixteen Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixteen_Kingdoms). Taiyuan was part of [Former Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Zhao), [Later Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Zhao), [Former Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Qin), [Former Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Yan), Former Qin again, [Western Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Yan), and [Later Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Yan), chronologically.

In 304, [Liu Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Yuan_\(Han_Zhao\)) founded the [Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiongnu) state of Former Zhao, whose army raided the area around Taiyuan for years and eventually conquered it in 316. In 319, Taiyuan became part of Later Zhao, founded by [Shi Le](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi_Le). It was then obtained by Former Yan in 358 and by Former Qin in 370.

Fu Jian died in 384. His son [Fu Pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_Pi) declared himself an emperor in 385, with Jinyang as his capital. The following year, he was defeated by the [Western Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Yan) prince [Murong Yong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murong_Yong), and Taiyuan became part of Western Yan.

In 581, [Emperor Wen of Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wen_of_Sui) founded the [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty). Jinyang was first the administrative center of Bing Zhou (并州), which was transformed into the Taiyuan Commandery.

### Tang dynasty

In 618, [Li Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Gaozu_of_Tang) founded the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty), which is generally considered to have been a golden age of Chinese civilization. Taiyuan expanded significantly, partly as it was the military base of the founding emperors Li Yuan and [Li Shimin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizong_of_Tang).[^cite_note-16]

In 690, [Wu Zetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Zetian) made Taiyuan the Northern Capital, ([北](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%8C%97)[都](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%83%BD); *Běidū*), one of three in the nation, along with [Chang'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'an) and [Luoyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoyang), as described in a poem by [Li Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Bai): "The king of heaven has three capitals, the Northern capital is one of them". ("天王三京, 北都居一").[^cite_note-汉典-三京-11] In 742, [Emperor Xuanzong of Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Xuanzong_of_Tang) changed the Northern Capital's name to *Beijing* ([北京](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%8C%97%E4%BA%AC))[^cite_note-17].

### Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms

In 923, [Li Cunxu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Cunxu), son of [Li Keyong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Keyong), founded [Later Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Tang), with the capital of [Daming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daming_County), and soon conquered most of northern China, ending Later Liang. Li Cunxu moved the capital from Daming to [Luoyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoyang), and Taiyuan was set as a provisional capital, under the name *Beidu* (北都, literally 'northern capital').[^cite_note-ZZTJ275-18]

In 936, [Shi Jingtang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi_Jingtang) established [Later Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Jin_\(Five_Dynasties\)) in Taiyuan, with help from the [Khitan Liao dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liao_dynasty). The next year, Shi Jingtang moved the capital to Luoyang and then to [Kaifeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaifeng), and Taiyuan became a provisional northern capital (*Beijing*) again.&#x20;

[Zhao Kuangyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizu_of_Song) (Emperor Taizu) established the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) and embarked on a campaign of Chinese re-unification. Using a power struggle at the [Northern Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Han) court, he moved against it in late 968.[^cite_note-song-19] By early 969, his armies encircled Taiyuan and defeated the reinforcements sent by the [Khitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people). However, an attempt to flood the city failed. The siege was lifted after three months, as heavy rains caused diseases within the besieging army, the supplies were running low, and another [Khitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people) relief force was advancing towards the city.[^cite_note-song-19]

Taizu launched the second invasion of [Northern Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Han) in September 969, but the armies were recalled after his death on 14 November.[^cite_note-song-19]



The hall of the holy mother in [Jinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinci), constructed from 1023 to 1032, during the Song dynasty

Taizu's brother [Emperor Taizong of Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizong_of_Song) subjugated the last independent kingdoms in the south of China by 978 and in 979 launched the third campaign against the Northern Han and its overlord, the [Khitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people) state of [Liao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liao_dynasty). Using the northwestern route instead of the southern (used in the previous campaigns), Taizong's armies defeated a major Liao force. Isolated, the Northern Han resisted for only fifteen days before surrendering. In contrast to the mild policies of his brother, Taizong dealt harshly with the city. He ordered the flooding of Taiyuan by releasing the Fen River and set the city on fire.[^cite_note-song-19] The former capital was subsequently downgraded from prefecture to county town status.

It was not until 982 that a new city was founded on the banks of the Fen River. The oldest existing building in Taiyuan today is the Temple of the Goddess ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): [圣](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%9C%A3)[母](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%AF%8D)[殿](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%AE%BF); [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): [聖](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%81%96)[母](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%AF%8D)[殿](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%AE%BF)), inside the [Jinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinci) complex. It was originally built in 1023 and reconstructed in 1102.

From 1027, one of two private markets for [Tangut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangut_people) goods, particularly salt, operated in Taiyuan.[^cite_note-20] During the Song period, many people, including the family of the chancellor [Wang Anshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Anshi), migrated south.[^cite_note-21]

The [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) emerged in 1206 under the leadership of [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan), and it expanded quickly. In 1218, Taiyuan was conquered by the Mongol army led by General [Muqali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muqali). In 1368, the [Hongwu Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongwu_Emperor) established the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), and Taiyuan was obtained from Yuan by General [Xu Da](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Da). The Ming dynasty installed the [Nine Military Garrisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Garrisons_of_the_Ming_dynasty) to defend the northern territory during the reign of the [Hongzhi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongzhi_Emperor), which included the garrison of Taiyuan ([太](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%A4%AA)[原](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%8E%9F)[镇](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%95%87)).

[Shanxi merchants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanxi_merchants) became prominent in Chinese business history from the beginning of the Ming dynasty, thanks to the logistical requirements of the military around the borders of northern Shanxi to defend against the remnant Mongol [Northern Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Yuan_dynasty).

### Qing

Throughout the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), international trade with Russia, especially of tea, and the creation and development of so-called draft banks, or *piaohao*, boosted the central Shanxi basin to become the financial center of Qing China. Even though most *piaohao* were based in the neighboring counties of [Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi_County,_Shanxi), [Taigu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taigu_County), and [Yuci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuci_District), Taiyuan became a significant trading center, due to its political and economic status in Shanxi.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

In 1900, the [Taiyuan massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_massacre) occurred, during which angry Chinese killed European children and their missionary parents.[^cite_note-24]

### Republican era



Taiyuan Cathedral, photographed by [Édouard Chavannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Chavannes) in 1907



Chinese soldiers and civilians celebrating the victory at [Pingxingguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pingxingguan) in 1937

The warlord [Yan Xishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan_Xishan) retained control of Shanxi from the [Xinhai Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_Revolution) in 1911 to the end of the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War) in 1949. Taiyuan consequently flourished as the center of his comparatively progressive province and experienced extensive industrial development. It was linked by rail both to the far southwest of Shanxi and to [Datong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datong) in the north. Until 1949, Yan's arsenal in Taiyuan was the only factory in China sufficiently advanced to produce [field artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_artillery). In 1923, Yan facilitated the construction of the province's first airport, [Taiyuan Chengbei Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_Chengbei_Airport), which allowed him to further develop a small air force.[^cite_note-Guangshe-25] Because Yan succeeded in keeping Shanxi uninvolved in most of the major battles between rival warlords that occurred in China during the 1910s and 1920s, Taiyuan was never taken from Yan by an invading army until the [Japanese occupied it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Taiyuan) in 1937.[^cite_note-GE289294-26]

Yan was aware of the threat posed by the Japanese, and in order to defend against their impending invasion of Shanxi, he entered into a secret "united front" agreement with the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) in November 1936. He then allowed agents under [Zhou Enlai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Enlai) to establish a secret headquarters in Taiyuan.[^cite_note-27] Yan, under the slogan "resistance against the enemy and defense of the soil", attempted to recruit young patriotic intellectuals to his government from across China. By 1936, Taiyuan had become a gathering point for anti-Japanese intellectuals who had fled from Beijing, [Tianjin](50821.md), and [Northeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_China).[^cite_note-28] A representative of the Japanese army, speaking of the final defense of Taiyuan, said that "nowhere in China have the Chinese fought so obstinately".[^cite_note-29]

From the occupation of Taiyuan to the Japanese surrender in 1945, the invaders continued to exploit Taiyuan's industries and resources to supply the Japanese army. After they surrendered surrendered to Yan Xishan, 10,000–15,000 Japanese troops, including both enlisted men and officers, decided to fight for Yan rather than return to Japan. Yan also retained the services of experienced and foreign-educated Japanese technicians and professional staff brought into Taiyuan by the Japanese to run the complex of industries that they had developed around the city.[^cite_note-30]

Taiyuan was the last area in Shanxi to resist Communist control during the final stages of the war. After Communist forces captured [Taiyuan Wusu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_Wusu_Airport), its defenders were trapped within the city, facing food shortages and low [morale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morale). [Taiyuan Qinxian Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_Qinxian_Airport) and an adjacent barracks was subsequently established in October 1948 in the Dayingpan area of the city, which was able to accommodate transport aircraft, while the barracks garrisoned the Second and Third Battalions. The existing Chengbei Airport was also reinforced, and two temporary backup airfields were established at Gelaogou and Honggou.[^cite_note-Qinxian1-31] The city was taken by Communist forces on 22 April 1949, after they surrounded Taiyuan and cut it off from all means of land and air supply. The capture required the support of 1,300 pieces of artillery.[^cite_note-32] Many [Nationalist](16903.md) officers subsequently committed suicide.[^cite_note-33]

## Geography



Satellite image of Taiyuan

Taiyuan lies on the [Fen River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fen_River) in the north of its fertile upper basin. The city is located at the center of the province, with an east–west span of 144 km (89 mi) and a north–south span of 107 km (66 mi).[^cite_note-shanxigov.cn-6][^cite_note-PRS-34] It commands the north–south route through the province as well as important natural lines of transportation through the [Taihang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taihang_Mountains) to [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei) in the east and to northern [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi) in the west.

### Natural resources

Taiyuan is abundant in natural resources such as [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron), [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), [silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silica), [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite), [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz), [phosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus), [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum), [mica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mica), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), and [gold](12240.md). It boasts high production of coal, iron, silica, and marble. The western satellite city of [Gujiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujiao) is the largest production site of [metallurgical coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgical_coal) in China. The tree population in Taiyuan is dominated by [coniferous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinophyta) forest, which includes [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine), [white pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pinus_species), [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce), and [cypress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress).[^cite_note-35]

### Climate

Taiyuan belongs to the warm temperate continental [monsoon](57630.md) climate, with long, dry, and cold winters, hot and humid summers, short and windy spring and autumn, and distinct dry and wet seasons.[^cite_note-36] The city experiences a [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *[BSk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Cold_semi-arid_climates)*). Springs are dry, with occasional [dust storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm), followed by early summer [heat waves](293270.md). Most of the year's rainfall is concentrated in July and August. Due to this aridity, there tends to be considerable [diurnal variation in temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation), except during the summer. The weather is much cooler than comparable-latitude cities such as [Shijiazhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shijiazhuang), due to the city's moderately high altitude. The monthly 24-hour average temperature ranges from −4.7 °C (23.5 °F) in January to 24.3 °C (75.7 °F) in July, while the annual mean is 10.9 °C (51.6 °F). Monthly sunshine ranges from 51 percent in July to 61 percent in May, with 2,493 hours of sunshine annually. Extremes since 1951 have ranged from −25.5 °C (−13.9 °F) to 39.4 °C (102.9 °F), though an unofficial record low of −29.5 °C (−21.1 °F) was noted on 7 January 1930.[^cite_note-37]

### Environment

The municipality of Taiyuan is 6,988 km<sup>2</sup> (2,698 sq mi). It had a forest area of 146,700 hectares and total grassland area of 422.5 km<sup>2</sup> (163.1 sq mi) in 2007.[^cite_note-Health_benefits_of_improving_air_quality_in_Taiyuan,_China-44] The forest area coverage rate in the six urban districts increased to 21.69% in 2015.[^cite_note-六城区森林覆盖率达21.69%-45]

#### Air pollution

Taiyuan has suffered from severe [air pollution](10934212.md), especially in the 1990s and the first decade of the 21st century,[^cite_note-46] and it was once listed among the ten most air-polluted cities in the world.[^cite_note-47] Air quality has been gradually improving, with increasing public awareness of air quality control and stricter and more detailed rules for pollution being applied. The city has taken action to combat air pollution, creating a "coal-free zone" of 1,460 km<sup>2</sup> in 2017.[^cite_note-\:1-48] This zone prevents most people and organizations from buying, selling, storing, transporting, burning, or using coal.[^cite_note-\:1-48] In 2019, the Taiyuan City Government expanded the size of this zone slightly, to a total of 1,574 km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-\:1-48]

## Demographics

As of the 2020 census, Taiyuan prefecture had a total population of 5,304,061 inhabitants on 6,959 km<sup>2</sup> (2,687 sq mi), of whom 4,529,141 lived in the six urban districts on 1,460 km<sup>2</sup> (560 sq mi).[^cite_note-Census2020-49]

## Economy



Taiyuan Riverside Sports Arena

Consistent with China's economic expansion throughout the 2010s, Taiyuan's economy has seen steady growth. In 2018, the city's GDP was worth 388.450 billion yuan, more than double what it was in 2010.[^cite_note-50] [Disposable income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_income) per capita was reported to be 31,031 yuan in 2018, a 7.2% increase from 2017.[^cite_note-51] In 2015, Taiyuan imported 4,085.130 million USD worth of goods[^cite_note-52] and exported the equivalent of 6,592.250 million USD.[^cite_note-53] Taiyuan's primary, secondary, and tertiary industries were worth 3.9 billion yuan, 105.2 billion yuan, and 132.2 billion yuan, respectively, in 2007.[^cite_note-Stat2013-54] Shanxi produces a quarter of China's coal, and Taiyuan is the location of the China Taiyuan Coal Transaction Center, which began trading in 2012.[^cite_note-55]

## Transportation

Taiyuan is a major transportation hub in northern China, with highways linking neighboring provincial capitals, and airlines to most other major Chinese cities and some international ones.

### Public transportation



A 1 route bus in Taiyuan

The [Taiyuan Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_Metro) opened in 2020. Line 1 launched in February 2025,[^cite_note-Feb2025M1-56] while Line 2 has been operating since December 2020.[^cite_note-xinhuanet.com-57]

In early 2016, the city began the conversion of all its 8,000 taxis to purely [electric vehicles](279350.md), initially using the [BYD e6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYD_e6).[^cite_note-58]

### Air



[Taiyuan Wusu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_Wusu_International_Airport)

The city's primary airport is [Taiyuan Wusu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_Wusu_International_Airport), which accommodates domestic airlines to major cities such as Beijing, [Shanghai](27643.md), and [Dalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalian).[^cite_note-toDalian-59] International flights are also available.[^cite_note-totaipei-60][^cite_note-todanang-61]

### Road

Taiyuan has a number of major roads, such as the [G5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G5_Beijing%E2%80%93Kunming_Expressway), [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20_Qingdao%E2%80%93Yinchuan_Expressway) (including [Shitai Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shitai_Expressway)), [G55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G55_Erenhot%E2%80%93Guangzhou_Expressway), G2001, [G307](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_307), [G108](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_108), and [G208](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_208).

### Rail



[Taiyuan railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_railway_station_\(Shanxi\))



[Taiyuan South railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_South_railway_station)

Major high-speed railways passing through Taiyuan include the [Shijiazhuang–Taiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shijiazhuang%E2%80%93Taiyuan_high-speed_railway), [Datong–Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datong%E2%80%93Xi'an_high-speed_railway), and [Taiyuan–Jiaozuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan%E2%80%93Jiaozuo_high-speed_railway). By high-speed rail, the travel time between Taiyuan and Beijing is less than three hours, on a distance of 600 km (370 mi).[^cite_note-62] The city's main train station is [Taiyuan South railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_South_railway_station). The conventional-speed [Taiyuan–Zhongwei–Yinchuan railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan%E2%80%93Zhongwei%E2%80%93Yinchuan_railway), opened in 2011, provides a direct connection with western Shanxi, northern [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi), [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia), and points further west.

## Tourism

Taiyuan is a modern city with only a few historic buildings remaining in the centre. The remnants of old Taiyuan can be found west of the central station, north of Fudong Street, and close to Wuyi Road.

One of the main tourist destinations is [Shanxi Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanxi_Museum), located on West Binhe Road, in downtown Taiyuan, which is among the largest museums in China.

The twin towers in [Yongzuo Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongzuo_Temple), which are featured on the city's emblem, are regarded as a symbol of Taiyuan. The temple is southeast of the city centre.

Chongshan Monastery, Longtan Park, and [Yingze Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yingze_Park), in the city centre, are popular tourist destinations.[^cite_note-66]

[Jinci Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinci_Temple) and [Christ's Church, Jinyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_Church,_Jinyuan), are both located in the [Jinyuan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinyuan_District) of southern Taiyuan. The district is also home to the Flying Bridge Across the Fish Pond, built during the Song dynasty.[^cite_note-67]

Along the West Mountain range in western Taiyuan are the [Tianlongshan Grottoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianlongshan_Grottoes), which were gradually built over centuries, beginning in the northern Qi dynasty. They contain thousands of Buddhist statues and artwork. The grottoes exist in a damaged state, with many of the sculptures missing. Many of them are housed in museums around the world. Researchers at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) initiated the Tianlongshan Caves Project in 2013, to pursue research and digital imaging of the caves and their sculptures.[^cite_note-68]

Not far from the Tianlongshan Grottoes are the [Longshan Grottoes](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%BE%99%E5%B1%B1%E7%9F%B3%E7%AA%9F), a Taoist cave complex. The main eight grottoes were carved in 1234–1239, during the Yuan dynasty.

## Education and research
