The **Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm Bo 105** is a light, twin-engine, multi-purpose [helicopter](8286923.md) developed by [Bölkow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6lkow) of [Ottobrunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottobrunn), West Germany. It was the first light twin-engine helicopter in the world, and the first rotorcraft that could perform [aerobatic maneuvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobatic_maneuver) such as inverted loops.[^cite_note-Moll_96-4][^cite_note-5] The Bo 105 features a [hingeless rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_rotor#Rigid) system, a pioneering innovation in helicopters when it was introduced into service in 1970. Production of the Bo 105 began at the then-recently merged [Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt-B%C3%B6lkow-Blohm) (MBB).

The main production facilities for producing the Bo 105 were located in Germany and Canada; due to the level of export sales encountered, additional manufacturing lines were set up in Spain, Indonesia, and the Philippines.[^cite_note-6] MBB, acquired by [DASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DASA) in 1989, merged its helicopter division with that of France's [Aérospatiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale) to form Eurocopter in 1992 (rebranded [Airbus Helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Helicopters) since). The latter continued production of the type until 2001. The Bo 105 was formally replaced in Eurocopter's product range by the newer [Eurocopter EC135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC135). By the close of production, over 1400 had been produced in Germany, and with license production 1640 had been produced in total.

## Development



The fourth prototype of the Bo 105, which first flew in 1969, on display at [Deutsches Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Museum)



Bo 105C at the 1973 [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show)

In 1964, development work began at Bölkow upon the helicopter that would become the Bo 105, although work on the hingeless [rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_rotor) it would use had begun earlier.[^cite_note-flight_1967-7] On 16 February 1967, the second Bo 105A prototype conducted its maiden flight at [Ottobrunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottobrunn) in Germany; the first public demonstration was held in May 1967.[^cite_note-eads-1-2] The test program was broken down into stages as the Bo 105 comprised a new airframe, new rotor system, and a new engine; thus the flying Bo 105 prototype was initially equipped with a main rotor from [Westland Helicopter's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Helicopters) [Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Scout) rotorcraft and a pair of [Allison Model 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allison_Model_250) [turboshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboshaft) engines instead of their production counterparts.[^cite_note-flight_1967-7] [Sud Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sud_Aviation) worked with Bölkow on developing the rotorcraft; an [Alouette II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_Alouette_II) helicopter was modified with the Bo 105's rotor and used to test its performance envelope. The third Bo 105 prototype was equipped with the initial production standard [MAN Turbo 6022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_Turbo_6022) turboshaft engines; six pre-production Bo 105s were constructed for testing.[^cite_note-flight_1967-7]



Offshore-configured Bo 105

On 13 October 1970, the [German Civil Aviation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftfahrt-Bundesamt) certified the Bo 105; initial deliveries for the first customers, [ADAC Air Rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADAC) and the [Bavarian State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Police), took place shortly thereafter. In 1972, further type certification was granted by the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) and the [Civil Aviation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Authority_\(United_Kingdom\)) (CAA), enabling export orders from the United States and Britain respectively, which soon followed.[^cite_note-euro_40-3] In 1972, an improved version of the rotorcraft with more powerful engines, the Bo 105C, was developed; this model quickly superseded the Bo 105A.[^cite_note-euro_40-3] On 25 September 1973, the prototype Bo 106 performed its first flight;[^cite_note-JAWA76_p73-8] the Bo 106 shared many similarities to the Bo 105, the principal difference being a widened cabin area, capable of seating three abreast in the front row and four abreast in the rear of the cabin. However, nothing further came of the Bo 106 project.[^cite_note-AI_may76_p246-9]

In 1976, the Bo 105CB, equipped with more powerful Allison 250-C20B engines, was introduced. The Bo 105C was further developed to become the Bo 105CBS, the primary change being a fuselage stretch of 10 inches to meet American demand for [emergency medical service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services) (EMS) operations; this version was often referred to as the Bo 105 Twin Jet in the United States. US aerospace firm [Boeing-Vertol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Rotorcraft_Systems) served as a partner in the type's production and further development, and marketed the BO 105 in the US.[^cite_note-wee_54-10][^cite_note-11] The Bo 105 CB and the Bo 105 CBS variants were also subject to [license manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licensed_production) agreements, leading to them being produced by the [Philippine Aerospace Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Aerospace_Development_Corporation) in the [Philippines](23440.md) which produced 44 units from 1977-1997,[^cite_note-12] [Indonesian Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Aerospace) (IPTN) in [Indonesia](14579.md), and [Construcciones Aeronáuticas SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construcciones_Aeron%C3%A1uticas_SA) (CASA) in [Spain](26667.md) in addition to the main production line in Germany.[^cite_note-euro_40-3]

In 1984, the Bo 105LS was developed with the enlarged fuselage of the Bo 105CBS combined with uprated Allison 250-C28C engines to increase the maximum take-off weight as well as hot-and-high flight performance; the Bo 105 LS was manufactured under a cooperative arrangement with [Eurocopter Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter).[^cite_note-euro_40-3] Improvements and modifications to the Bo 105 LS continued until 1995.[^cite_note-euro_40-3]

Production of the Bo 105 by [Eurocopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter) formally ended in 2001, principally due to the type having been superseded by the more modern [Eurocopter EC135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC135), itself a direct development from the Bo 105. By the end of production, 1,406 rotorcraft had been manufactured and delivered to operators in 55 nations worldwide.[^cite_note-euro_40-3] Including the license production in Canada, Indonesia, Philippines and Spain the total is about 1640; of that 1404 were made in Germany.[^cite_note-hamel-1]

## Design



A view of a Bo 105's engine, transmission, and main rotor

The Bo 105 has a reputation for having high levels of maneuverability; certain variants have been designed for aerobatic maneuvers and used for promotional purposes by operators, one such operator in this capacity being professional pilot [Aaron Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Fitzgerald?action=edit\&redlink=1), flying for [Red Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] During the 1970s, the Bo 105 was known for having a great useful load capacity and higher cruise speed than the majority of its competitors.[^cite_note-wee_54-56-15] While not being considered a visually attractive helicopter by some pilots, the Bo 105 was known for possessing steady, responsive controls and a good flight attitude.[^cite_note-Moll_105-16] Most models could perform steep dives, rolls, loops, turnovers, and various aerobatic maneuvers; according to MBB the Bo 105 is cleared for up to 3.5 positive [G force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_force) and one negative.[^cite_note-Moll_101-17] One benefit of the Bo 105's handling and control style is superior takeoff performance, including significant resistance to catastrophic [dynamic rollover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_rollover);[^cite_note-Moll_103-18] a combination of light weight and the twin-engined configuration enables a rapid ascent in a performance takeoff.[^cite_note-Moll_104-19]

Perhaps the most significant feature of the Bo 105 is its [rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_rotor) blades and rotor head. The rotor system is entirely [hingeless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinge), the rotor head consisting of a solid titanium block to which the four blades are bolted;[^cite_note-20] the flexibility of the rotor blades works to absorb movements typically requiring hinges in most helicopter rotor designs.[^cite_note-Moll_101-17] The rotor blades are made from reinforced-plastic glass-fiber composite material; the flexibility of the main rotor allows for active elements other than rotor pitch changes to be removed, greatly simplifying maintenance and extending blade lifespan.[^cite_note-wee_56-21] The reliability of the advanced rotor system is such that, in over six million operating hours across the fleet, there was a total of zero failures (as of 1991).[^cite_note-22] The rigid rotor design adopted on the Bo 105 has been partially responsible for the type's agility and responsiveness; it remained an uncommon feature on competing helicopters throughout the Bo 105's production life.[^cite_note-Moll_103-18]

Military operators would commonly operate the type at a very low altitude to minimise visibility to enemies, the Bo 105 being well matched to such operations, as the helicopter's flight qualities effectively removed or greatly minimised several of the hazards such a flight profile could pose to pilots.[^cite_note-23] When outfitted with optional auxiliary fuel tanks, a basic model Bo 105 had a flight endurance of roughly five hours under load.[^cite_note-Moll_104-19] In the event of a single engine failure, the Bo 105 could typically continue its flight, albeit with a reduction in cruise speed and range.[^cite_note-wee_56-21] Besides the two pilots, the cabin can be configured to accommodate up to three passengers on a single rear bench, which can be removed to make room for cargo or a stretcher, which can be loaded and unloaded via the large clamshell doors located at the rear of the fuselage.[^cite_note-wee_57-24] In a maritime context, the BO 105 can be equipped with auxiliary [fuel tanks](1071636.md), emergency flotation equipment, an inflatable [life raft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_raft), folding rotor blades and high skid landing gear.[^cite_note-25]

## Operational history



German Army Bo 105, 1986

In 1975, the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army) decided to procure a specialist anti-tank version armed with up to six [Euromissile HOT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromissile_HOT) missiles, designated as the Bo 105 PAH-1 (*Panzerabwehrhubschrauber*, "anti-tank helicopter"). A total of 212 Bo 105 PAH-1s were delivered between 1979 and 1984.[^cite_note-euro_40-3] German Army leaders saw the PAH-1 as a temporary measure only, having originally sought a more capable anti-tank helicopter; they were particularly dissatisfied with the PAH-1's inability to perform nighttime combat missions and its self-protection capabilities; ultimately, the [Eurocopter Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_Tiger) was developed as a replacement.[^cite_note-26] In 1977, the [German Ministry of Defence](2563857.md) selected the Bo 105C for its light observation helicopter (VBH) program, leading to the procurement of another 100 rotorcraft.[^cite_note-euro_40-3] The helicopter was retired from German Army service on 13 December 2016, the occasion was marked by an 18-aircraft formation flyby from their base in [Celle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celle_Air_Base). The Bohicans, as their pilots were called, had clocked over 1.38 million flying hours.[^cite_note-aviationweek23dec16-27]

During the 1970s, MBB issued a license to produce the Bo 105 to [Indonesian Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Aerospace) (IPTN) as part of a wider agreement to help develop Indonesia's aviation industry. Within ten years, manufacture of the Bo 105 had been entirely localised within IPTN's own facilities.[^cite_note-28] The Bo 105 has been used by various branches of the [Indonesian National Armed Forces](14650.md); military-operated Bo 105s have been reportedly used during the [1999 East Timorese crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_East_Timorese_crisis) and the [Papua conflict](21657843.md).[^cite_note-29] Indonesian Bo 105s have also participated in several large-scale joint exercises.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

The [Mexican Navy](1225277.md) operated a number of armed Bo 105s to perform [maritime reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_patrol_aircraft) duties; during the early 1980s, Mexico procured a number of a [Uribe-class patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uribe-class_patrol_vessel) which were equipped to handle the Bo 105 for off-shore operations.[^cite_note-32] In 2005, work was completed on a series of upgrades to 11 of the Mexican Navy's Bo 105s, having received [Forward looking infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_looking_infrared) (FLIR) sensors, [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) (GPS) receivers, [search radars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_configurations_and_types#Detection_and_search_radars), new rotor blades, armament pylons, and other changes.[^cite_note-33] In November 2014, Jet Rescue Air Ambulance inaugurated Mexico's first dedicated civil air ambulance rotorcraft using a Bo 105.[^cite_note-34]



A Mexican Navy Bo 105 on board [USS *Yorktown*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Yorktown_\(CG-48\)), 2002

During the late 1990s, the [Republic of Korea Army aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Korea_Army_aviation) procured a number of Bo 105s as part of an ongoing drive to improve the force's reconnaissance and surveillance capabilities; these were manufactured under a co-production arrangement between Eurocopter and the newly formed [Korea Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Aerospace_Industries) (KAI), receiving the designation *Korean Light Helicopter* (KLH), KAI also promoted the type to export customers.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

The Bo 105 gained widespread use by [emergency medical service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_service) (EMS) / [medevac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medevac) operators; in 1998, [Flying Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_\(magazine\)) reported that between the Bo 105 and the [MBB/Kawasaki BK 117](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBB/Kawasaki_BK_117) (a newer rotorcraft derived from the Bo 105), MBB held 35% of the EMS market share.[^cite_note-37] The first EMS-orientated Bo 105 in [Scotland](26994.md) entered service in 1989; in November 2015, the last Bo 105 air ambulance operated in Britain was retired, the type having typically been replaced by the newer [Eurocopter EC135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_EC135).[^cite_note-38] In 2009, the last Bo 105 to be produced was purchased by Canadian EMS specialist [Dam Helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dam_Helicopters?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

In May 2014, Airbus Helicopter reported that, worldwide, the Bo 105 had accumulated 8 million flight hours, and that approximately 700 Bo 105s remained in service.[^cite_note-41]

- On 17 August 2010 a [Philippine Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Navy) PADC/MBB Bo 105C serial 411 crashed near Zamboanga City with two casualties.

  [^cite_note-95]

  [^cite_note-96]

All occupants were uninjured.

## Specifications (Bo 105CB)

*Data from* Jane's All the World's Aircraft 1988-89[^cite_note-Janes_88_p93-4-113] Worldwide Equipment Guide – Volume 2: Airspace and Air Defense Systems[^cite_note-114]

**General characteristics**

- **Crew:** 1 or 2 pilots

- **Capacity:** 3 or 4 passengers

- **Length:** 11.86 m (38 ft 11 in) (total length - including rotors)

- **Height:** 3.00 m (9 ft 10 in)

- **[Airfoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airfoil):** NACA 23012

- **Empty weight:** 1,276 kg (2,813 lb)

- **Max takeoff weight:** 2,500 kg (5,512 lb)

- **Fuel capacity:** 570 L (150 US gal; 130 imp gal)

- **Powerplant:** 2 × [Allison 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allison_250)-C20B [turboshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboshaft) engines, 310 kW (420 shp) each

- **Main rotor diameter:** 9.84 m (32 ft 3 in)

- **Main rotor area:** 76.05 m

  2

  &#x20;(818.6 sq ft)

**Performance**

- **Maximum speed:** 242 km/h (150 mph, 131 kn)

- **Cruise speed:** 204 km/h (127 mph, 110 kn) (best range cruise)

- **[Never exceed speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_speeds#VNE):** 270 km/h (170 mph, 150 kn)

- **Range:** 657 km (408 mi, 355 nmi) at 1,525 m (5,000 ft) (standard fuel, maximum payload)

- **Ferry range:** 1,112 km (691 mi, 600 nmi) at 1,525 m (5,000 ft) (with auxiliary tanks)

- **Endurance:** 3 hr 30 min (standard fuel, maximum payload)

- **Service ceiling:** 5,200 m (17,000 ft)

- **Rate of climb:** 8.00 m/s (1,575 ft/min)

**Armament**\


- **Rockets:** 2 × 12 [SNEB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNEB) or 2 × 28 SNIA

- **Missiles:** 6 × [Euromissile HOT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromissile_HOT) (Bo 105 P) *or* 8 × [BGM-71 TOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGM-71_TOW) *or* 4 × [Air-to-Air Stinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-Air_Stinger)
