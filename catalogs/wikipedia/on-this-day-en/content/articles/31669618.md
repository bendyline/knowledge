# Video games in the United States



[E3 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E3_2010) at the [Los Angeles Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Convention_Center)

The [video game industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_industry) in the [United States](3434750.md) is one of the fastest-growing [entertainment industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_industry) in the country. The American video game industry is the largest video game industry in the world. According to a 2020 study released by the [Entertainment Software Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Software_Association) (ESA), the yearly economic output of the American video game industry in 2019 was \$90.3 billion, supporting over 429,000 American jobs. With an average yearly salary of about \$121,000, the latter figure includes over 143,000 individuals who are directly employed by the video game business. Additionally, activities connected to the [video game](5363.md) business generate \$12.6 billion in federal, state, and local taxes each year.[^cite_note-1] The [World Economic Forum](172185.md) estimates that, by 2025, the American gaming industry will reach \$42.3 billion while the worldwide gaming industry will possibly reach US\$270 billion.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The United States is one of the nations with the largest influence in the video game industry, with video games representing a significant part of its economy.



[BlizzCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlizzCon) in 2017

Major publishers headquartered in the United States are: [Sony Interactive Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Interactive_Entertainment), [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Gaming) (consist of [Xbox Game Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Game_Studios), [Bethesda Softworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethesda_Softworks) and [Activision Blizzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activision_Blizzard)), [Electronic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Arts), [Take-Two Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take-Two_Interactive), [Epic Games](573199.md), [Valve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valve_Corporation), [Warner Bros. Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Interactive_Entertainment), [Riot Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_Games), and others.[^cite_note-4] Major video game events such as [BlizzCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlizzCon), [QuakeCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QuakeCon), [Summer Game Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Game_Fest), and [PAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAX_\(event\)) are held every year in the US. For many years, [E3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E3), held annually in the US, was considered the biggest gaming expo of the year in terms of its importance and impact.[^cite_note-5] [The Game Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_Awards), [The New York Game Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Game_Awards), and [D.I.C.E. Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.I.C.E._Awards) are some of the most respected video game awards events in the video game industry. 103 million people watched [The Game Awards 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_Awards_2022) event alone.[^cite_note-6] The [Game Developers Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Developers_Conference) (GDC) is still the largest and one of the most important video game conferences for video game developers.[^cite_note-7]

In statistics collected by the ESA for the year 2013, a reported 58% of Americans play video games and the average American household now owns at least one dedicated [video game console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_console), [PC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_computer) or [smartphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone).\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)] According to estimates from [Nielsen Media Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research), approximately 45.7 million U.S. households in 2006 (or approximately 40 percent of approximately 114.4 million) owned a dedicated [home video game console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_video_game_console),[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] and by 2015, 51 percent of U.S. households owned a dedicated home video game console according to an [Entertainment Software Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Software_Association) annual industry report.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] The households that own these items play games most commonly on their console or PC. 36% of U.S. gamers play on their smartphones.\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)] 43% of video game consumers believe games give them the most value for their money compared to other common forms of entertainment such as movies or music.\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)] In 2011, the average American gamer spent an average of 13 hours per week playing video games.[^cite_note-13] In 2013, almost half of Americans who were gaming more than they did in 2010 spent less time playing board games, watching TV, going to the movies, and watching movies at home.\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)] When Americans game, 62% do so with others [online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_game) or in person, yet the other person is more likely to be a friend than a significant other or family member.\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)] The most common reason parents play video games with their children is as a fun family activity, or because they are asked to. 52% of parents believe video games are a positive part of their child's life, and 71% of parents with children under 18 see gaming as beneficial to mental stimulation or education.\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)]

The number of mobile game players in the US is higher than it has ever been at more than 191 million people, or 57.3% of the population.[^cite_note-14]

The average age of a U.S. gamer is 35, the average number of years a U.S. gamer has been playing games is 13.\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)] In 2021, it was reported that the age distribution of U.S. gamers were 20% under the 18 years old, 38% were in between 18 and 34 years old, 14% were in between 35 and 44 years old, 12% were in between 45 and 54 years old, 9% were in between 55 and 64 years old, and 7% were 65 years old or over.[^cite_note-15] The American gamer population is 54% male and 46% female. Of those females, women 18 and older account for a greater portion of the population than males younger than 18.\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)] The average female video game player is 44 years old, while the average male video game player is 35.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]&#x20;

## Market statistics

The best-selling [console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Console_game) [video game genres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_genres) of 2022 were [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_game), [shooters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooter_game), and [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_video_game). The [PC gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC_gaming) market's best-selling genres were [role-playing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role-playing_video_game), [strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategy_video_game), and [casual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casual_game). For [online games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_games), the most popular genres are [puzzle/trivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle_video_game), action/strategy, and casual/social games.\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)] While there are many American [video game developers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_developers) that have been producing games for years, [Japanese video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_video_games) and companies have regularly been listed in the annual lists of best sellers.[^cite_note-18] In December 2022, American consumers spent \$7.6 billion on video gaming content, hardware, and accessories, up 2% from the previous month and bringing total expenditure for the year to \$56.6 billion. The unit sales growth featured a similar drop with the report of 188 million units sold from 245.9 in 2021. U.S. gaming consumers spent a total of \$20.77 billion on the game industry alone and currently [hard copies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD+RW) of video games are still dominating in sales compared to [digital copies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_copy).\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)] 2022 saw inexorable expansion in the gaming sector, particularly in online and mobile gaming. A fundamental shift in mentality is also occurring, with games now being viewed as venues for creativity, self-expression, and socialization. While major IT firms are developing their own meta-universes, new gaming technologies such as AR, VR, and MR are changing the industry.[^cite_note-19]

## History

See also: [History of video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_video_games)

### 1940s

See also: [Early history of video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_history_of_video_games) and [Early mainframe games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_mainframe_games)

The beginning of video games can be traced to the year 1940, when American nuclear physicist [Edward Condon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Condon) designed a computer capable of playing the traditional game [Nim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nim). This device would have tens of thousands of people play it even though the computer won 90% of the time. Seven years later an American television pioneer, [Thomas T. Goldsmith, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_T._Goldsmith,_Jr.), patented an [oscilloscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscilloscope) displayed device that challenged players to fire a gun at a target.\[^cite_note-[www.icheg.org-62](http://www.icheg.org-62/)]

### 1950s

At the start of the 1950s another American, [Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon), wrote basic guidelines on [programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming) a computer.\[^cite_note-[www.icheg.org-62](http://www.icheg.org-62/)] Although *[OXO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OXO_\(video_game\))* was created in England by the year 1952, the findings and inventions of the Americans described helped make it possible.[^cite_note-63] The U.S. military dove into the computer age with the creation of a game titled *Hutspiel.* Considered a war gam&#x65;*, [Hutspiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutspiel)* depicted [NATO](21133.md) and [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) commanders waging war. The [IBM 701](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_701) computer received programs such as [Blackjack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackjack) and [Checkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkers). A later IBM model featured a [chess](5134.md) program that was capable of evaluating four [ply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ply_\(game_theory\)) ahead. The 1950s also included the largely forgotten [tennis](29773.md) game created by [Willy Higinbotham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Higinbotham) that anticipated the famous game *[Pong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pong).*\[^cite_note-[www.icheg.org-62](http://www.icheg.org-62/)]

### 1960s

The military continued to take part in video gaming in the 1960s when, shortly after the [Cuban Missile Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Missile_Crisis), the Defense Department created a war game known as *STAGE* (Simulation of Total Atomic Global Exchange). *STAGE* was created to be political propaganda that showcased how the U.S. would be victorious in a [Thermonuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear) war with the Soviet Union.\[^cite_note-[www.icheg.org-62](http://www.icheg.org-62/)] The idea of video games that were usable on televisions was conceived by the engineer [Ralph Baer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Baer) and with the help of a team, Baer completed two successful TV games in this decade. The first [interactive media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactive_media) computer game, *[Spacewar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacewar_\(video_game\))*, eventually had the future founders of [Atari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari) create an [arcade game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_game) of it titled *[Computer Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Space)* that became the first video arcade game ever released.\[^cite_note-[www.icheg.org-62\]\[^cite_note-www.infoplease.com-64](http://www.icheg.org-62]\[^cite_note-www.infoplease.com-64)]

### 1970s

See also: [History of arcade video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_arcade_video_games), [First generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_generation_of_video_game_consoles), and [Second generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_generation_of_video_game_consoles)

The 1970s included the birth of the [video game console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Game_Console). The first console released was titled *[Magnavox Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnavox_Odyssey)* and the foundation of Atari occurred around the same time, marking the start of *Pong's* development. Upon *Pong's* completion it became the hottest selling Christmas product of 1975. The evolution of the console was incredibly rapid. A few years after their invention, consoles received [microprocessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microprocessors) and programmable [ROM cartridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROM_cartridge) based games, allowing users the ability to change games by simply switching cartridges. Important consoles released at this time were the *[Telstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telstar_\(game_console\)), [Fairchild Channel F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Channel_F),&#x20;*&#x61;nd *[Atari 2600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_2600).* Arcade games also received advances with the game *[Space Invaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Invaders)*, which allowed high scores to be tracked and displayed. A year later the game *[Asteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroids_\(video_game\))&#x20;*&#x62;uilt on the idea and gave high scorers the ability to enter initials by their scores.[^cite_note-www\.icheg.org-62][^cite_note-www\.infoplease.com-64]

### 1980s

See also: [Third generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_generation_of_video_game_consoles) and [Fourth generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_generation_of_video_game_consoles)

The technological advances of the late 1970s led to the introduction of the [Intellivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellivision) in 1980, which featured better [video game graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_graphics) but a higher price tag. In two years, the [Commodore 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_64) changed the market by not only being the most powerful console of the time but also the cheapest. With the lowered prices, popularity of the video game industry continued to grow and the first video game magazine, *[Electronic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Games)*, was printed. However, attempts to copycat on the success of the Atari 2600 saturated the market, and the [video game crash of 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_crash_of_1983) decimated the industry in the United States. With the American-produced games on the downswing, [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo) successfully launched the [Nintendo Entertainment System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Entertainment_System) in America in 1985, revitalizing the market with the introduction of the [third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_generation_of_video_game_consoles) and [fourth generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_generation_of_video_game_consoles) of home consoles such as the [Master System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_System), [Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy), [Sega Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Genesis), [Atari 7800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_7800), and the [TurboGrafx-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TurboGrafx-16), with systems transitioning to support 3D graphics and support for optical media rather than cartridges.[^cite_note-www\.icheg.org-62][^cite_note-www\.infoplease.com-64]

### 1990s

See also: [Fifth generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_generation_of_video_game_consoles) and [Sixth generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_generation_of_video_game_consoles)

The 1990s saw the introduction of the [Super NES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_NES), [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_\(console\)), [Nintendo 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_64), [Tamagotchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamagotchi), and [Dreamcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamcast), whose sales brought the damaged video game industry back to life. During this decade, the PlayStation was considered the most popular console when its 20 millionth unit sold. In 1993, the video game industries' first debate began and its focus was on violence found in video games. This debate fueled Senator [Joseph Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Lieberman)'s desire to ban all violent games; from this investigation the [Entertainment Software Rating Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Software_Rating_Board) was created in 1994, giving all games a printed suggested age rating on their packaging.[^cite_note-www\.icheg.org-62][^cite_note-www\.infoplease.com-64][^cite_note-www\.g4tv.com-65]

### 2000s

Main article: [Seventh generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_generation_of_video_game_consoles)



[Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_\(console\)) (2001 console)

The 2000s further increased Sony's popularity when its [PlayStation 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2) video game console had such a high American consumer demand that it actually affected the console's availability to be purchased during the first few shipments; the PlayStation 2 remains the [best-selling console of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_game_consoles) in the United States and worldwide. [Microsoft](19001.md) and [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo) also saw this popularity with the release of their own [sixth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_generation_of_video_game_consoles) and [seventh generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_generation_of_video_game_consoles) of consoles, the [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_\(console\)) and [GameCube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameCube), respectively. Mass availability of the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) introduced online connectivity on consoles for multiplayer games as well as digital storefronts to sell games. Digital storefronts also enabled the growth of the [indie game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_game) market, expanding from computers onto consoles over this decade. [Motion control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_control)-enabled games, popularized by the [Wii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wii) console, grew in popularity.[^cite_note-www\.icheg.org-62][^cite_note-www\.infoplease.com-64] According to estimates from [Nielsen Media Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research), approximately 45.7 million U.S. households in 2006 (or approximately 40 percent of approximately 114.4 million) owned a dedicated home video game console.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

### 2010s

Main article: [Eighth generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_generation_of_video_game_consoles)



[PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4) designed by American video game designer [Mark Cerny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Cerny)

Within the 2010s, a larger shift towards casual and [mobile gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_gaming) on smartphones and tablets became significant, in part due to a wider demographic of video game players drawing in more female and older players.[^cite_note-68] The concept of [games as a service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_as_a_service), emerged as a trend for developers and publishers to have long-tail monetization of a game well after release. Continuing from the previous decade, a large number of [independently developed video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_video_game_development) emerged as games on par with those from major publishers, made easier to promote and distribute through digital storefronts on personal computers, consoles, and mobile store markets. All three major console manufacturers released [next generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_video_game_consoles_\(eighth_generation\)) consoles: [Xbox One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One), [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4), [Wii U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wii_U), and [Nintendo Switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Switch). Major developments in mixed reality games - both [augmented reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmented_reality) and [virtual reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_reality) - grew in popularity during the 2010s as the cost of required hardware dropped. [Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esport) became a significant market in the United States after its initial popularity in Eastern Asia countries. In 2015, 51 percent of U.S. households owned a home video game console according to an [Entertainment Software Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Software_Association) annual industry [report](1050798.md).[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

### 2020s

Main article: [Ninth generation of video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_generation_of_video_game_consoles)

Microsoft and Sony have released their successors to their eighth generation consoles in November 2020, the [Xbox Series X/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Series_X_and_Series_S) and [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5). Both systems support high-definition graphics, real-time ray-tracing, game streaming and cloud-based gaming. Nintendo has continued with their [Nintendo Switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Switch) at the beginning of this decade.

With the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and lockdown causing people to stay in their homes, people picked up video games which caused a big boom in sales throughout 2019 all the way into 2021. [The NPD Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_NPD_Group) reported that video game sales in North America in March 2020 were up 34% from those in March 2019, video game hardware up by 63%.[^cite_note-71] Game companies also saw this as an opportunity to expand what they could do to entertain, so [Epic Games](573199.md) hosted the first and second ever live in-game concert through *[Fortnite](57813623.md)*, first with [Marshmello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshmello) and second with "an in-game [Travis Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Scott) concert saw over 12 million concurrent views from players".[^cite_note-72]

&#x20;The American video game industry experienced significant resurgence in mergers and acquisitions between 2020 and 2024. Video game companies anticipated that the substantial growth observed during the pandemic would persist afterward, prompting many firms to explore mergers and acquisitions. Between 2020 and 2024, 8 out of the 20 [most expensive video game acquisitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_video_game_acquisitions) in history were made by American publishers, with major American publishers such as Microsoft Gaming, Sony Interactive Entertainment, Take-Two Interactive, and Electronic Arts each making at least one acquisition.





[Xbox Series X/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Series_X_and_Series_S)

There have been [widespread layoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%932024_video_game_industry_layoffs) in the US video game business starting in 2023 and going into 2024.[^cite_note-73] The layoffs were the result of multiple causes coming together, rather than occurring all at once. The COVID-19 epidemic drove up demand for video games.[^cite_note-74] In anticipation of continued growth, this prompted businesses to make large investments in staff expansion, mergers, and acquisitions. But when the market recovered and the world opened up, the quick development proved unsustainable, and businesses were forced to make cuts as their operating costs ballooned. According to Mat Piscatella, executive director of [Circana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_NPD_Group) (The NPD Group), the most optimistic forecast for the American video game industry in 2024 forecasts a possible decline of roughly 2%. On the other hand, a more negative outlook would predict a 10% decrease, with an even bigger drop possible if things become much worse.[^cite_note-75] DDM Games reported that the industry is presently going through a "reset phase." Businesses are using layoffs, divestitures, and closures as a means of reorganizing their operations. Since the growth surge brought on by the epidemic has receded, recalibration is now necessary.[^cite_note-76]

Similar to the [voice actor strike in 2016-2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%932017_video_game_voice_actor_strike), [SAG-AFTRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAG-AFTRA) initiated a [labor strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_SAG-AFTRA_video_game_strike) in July 2024 against all video game companies which were signed on the Internative Media Agreement due to concerns over lack of protections for video game performers, as well as the images and voices of other actors, from [AI](1164.md).[^cite_note-77] The strike lasted nearly a year, with the union and video game company reaching an agreement by June 2025 and union members voting in favor of the new agreement by July 2025.[^cite_note-78]

As a result of the [tariff policy in the second Trump administration](78448911.md) and the [global memory supply shortage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_memory_supply_shortage), all three console hardware manufacturers, Microsoft, Sony, and Nintendo, raised the price of their consoles in the U.S., generally citing "market conditions".[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

## Cultural impact on the global gaming industry

With [RPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role-playing_game) video game series such as *[Dungeons & Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeons_&_Dragons)*, *[The Elder Scrolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elder_Scrolls)*, and [*Fallout*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallout_\(franchise\)), and [first-person shooters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_shooter) series such as *[Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_\(franchise\))*, [Halo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_\(franchise\)), [*Half-Life*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Life_\(series\)), and *[BioShock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioShock_\(series\))*, the American video game industry has heavily influenced the global gaming industry. Some of the best-selling and most popular video games ever made such as [*Call of Duty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_of_Duty:_Modern_Warfare_II_\(2022_video_game\)), *[Fortnite](57813623.md)*, *[World of Warcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Warcraft)*, *[Overwatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwatch)*, *[League of Legends](21838352.md)*, *[Valorant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valorant)*, [*CSGO*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Strike:_Global_Offensive), *[Dota 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dota_2)*, *[Apex Legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apex_Legends)* and *[Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox)* were made in the United States. Some of the most revolutionary video games such as [*Skyrim*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elder_Scrolls_V:_Skyrim), [*Half-Life*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Life_\(video_game\)), and *BioShock*, were also made in the United States.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] Alongside video games, American companies such as [Epic Games](573199.md) have also contributed to the video game industry with high-technology. [Unreal Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Engine) and [Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_\(game_engine\)) are considered to be one of the best and most popular video game engines of all time.[^cite_note-84] With the rise of [Steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_\(service\)) in the mid-2010s and easy access to video game making tools and engines, it sparked the rise of [Indie games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_game).[^cite_note-85] The United States has some of the largest and most respected and popular video game news cites and journalists in the world. Some of them are [Game Informer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Informer), [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic), [IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN), [GameSpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameSpot), [GamesRadar+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GamesRadar+), [The Verge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Verge), [Kotaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotaku), [Polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon_\(website\)), and [Giantbomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Bomb).[^cite_note-aelieve.com-2023-86][^cite_note-makeuseof.com-87] Some of the most respected video game journalists, such as [Jason Schreier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Schreier), are also from the United States.[^cite_note-makeuseof.com-87][^cite_note-aelieve.com-2023-86]

### Criticisms

While the rise of American multiplayer games has grown the global video game industry, many video game journalists and gamers have heavily criticized some of the decisions and changes made by American companies, such as the addition of [micro-transactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtransaction) in video games.[^cite_note-89] After the release and huge success of *[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Breath_of_the_Wild)* in 2017, *[Elden Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elden_Ring)* in 2022 and *[Baldur's Gate 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldur's_Gate_3)* in 2023, complaints started pouring in that the American gaming industry was lacking far behind and not investing enough in innovation such as Japanese and European gaming companies.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

### Sony Interactive Entertainment

Main article: [Sony Interactive Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Interactive_Entertainment)

Sony Interactive Entertainment is gaming company operating as an independent subsidiary and video game publishing arm of multinational conglomerate [Sony Group Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Group_Corporation). It has its global headquarters in [San Mateo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Mateo,_California) and is incorporated in [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware). It has its own executive leadership team.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] In 2016, Sony Group moved Sony Interactive Entertainment's headquarters from [Tokyo](30057.md) to California.[^cite_note-121] With over 4,000 developers and 19 studios, Sony Interactive Entertainment is one of the biggest video game companies in the word. 10 out of 19 studios are American studios.[^cite_note-122] In 2022, Sony Interactive Entertainment made a major investment in America and acquired [Bungie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungie) for \$3.7 billion.[^cite_note-123] Sony Interactive Entertainment owns popular American video game studios such as [Naughty Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naughty_Dog), [Santa Monica Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Studio), [Insomniac Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insomniac_Games), [Sucker Punch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucker_Punch_Productions) and franchises such as [*God of War*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_of_War_\(franchise\)), [*The Last of Us*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_of_Us_\(franchise\)), and *[Uncharted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncharted)*.[^cite_note-124] [*God of War Ragnarok*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_of_War_Ragnar%C3%B6k) has sold over 5.1 million units in 1 week and 11 million units in 2 months, making it the fastest selling first-party game in PlayStation history.[^cite_note-125] In 2023 [Marvel's Spider-Man 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel's_Spider-Man_2) became the fastest-selling PlayStation first-party game of all time, selling 2.5 million units in 24 hours.[^cite_note-126]

### Take-Two Interactive

Main article: [Take-Two Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take-Two_Interactive)

In September 1993, Ryan Brant established the American video game holding firm Take-Two Interactive Software, Inc. in New York City. [Rockstar Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_Games) and [2K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2K_\(company\)), two significant publishing labels owned by Take-Two Interactive, both have internal game production teams. The business also established Ghost Story Games, rebranding Irrational Games, a former 2K firm. To establish itself in the market for mobile games, the company bought [Socialpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialpoint), Playdots, and [Nordeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordeus). Additionally, the company controls 50% of the professional esports league NBA 2K League.[^cite_note-127] In 2013, *Grand Theft Auto V* was the most popular game. Three days after its debut, when sales of the game surpassed \$1 billion, it set a record for fastest-selling entertainment product in history.[^cite_note-128] Take-Two's combined portfolio includes franchises such as *[BioShock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioShock_\(series\))*, *[Borderlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borderlands_\(series\))*, *[Grand Theft Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto)*, *[NBA 2K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_2K)*, [Max Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Payne) and *[Red Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Dead)* among others. In 2022, Take-Two Interactive acquired mobile video game company [Zynga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zynga) for \$12.7 billion.

### Electronic Arts (EA)

Main article: [Electronic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Arts)

Electronic Arts (EA) is the world's largest independent video game publisher by revenue and market value. American video game developer Electronic Arts is based in Redwood City, California. [Trip Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trip_Hawkins), an Apple employee, founded the business in May 1982. It was a pioneer in the early home computer gaming market and referred to the designers and programmers behind its games as "software artists". With 12,900 video game developers, Electronic Arts is one of the biggest video game publishers in the world.[^cite_note-129] [Respawn Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respawn_Entertainment), [BioWare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioWare), [Dice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DICE_\(company\)), [PopCap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PopCap_Games), are some of the studies under Electronic Arts. With the success of EA Sports and game series such as [*FIFA*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_\(video_game_series\)), [*NHL*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_\(video_game_series\)), [*NBA Live*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_\(video_game_series\)), *[Madden NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madden_NFL)*, *[Dragon Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Age)*, *[Mass Effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Effect)*, *[Dead Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Space)*, and [*Star Wars Jedi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Jedi:_Fallen_Order), Electronic Arts became one of the biggest video game companies in the world.

Riot Games was purchased by Tencent, a Chinese corporation, in 2011. Riot Games is one of the fastest growing American video game companies with over 5,500 developers. ## Video game mergers/acquisitions

The history of American video game company acquisitions is a long and complex one, spanning several decades and involving many different companies. Over the years, the video game industry has undergone numerous mergers, acquisitions, and consolidations, as companies seek to gain a competitive edge, expand their offerings, and diversify their portfolios.

One of the earliest major acquisitions in the video game industry took place in 1988, when Japanese electronics giant Sony acquired Columbia Pictures, a major Hollywood studio with a burgeoning video game division. The acquisition marked the first time that a major electronics manufacturer had acquired a major film studio, and it paved the way for Sony's eventual entry into the video game console market with the launch of the original PlayStation in 1994.[^cite_note-159]

In the years that followed, the video game industry saw a flurry of mergers and acquisitions as companies sought to capitalize on the growing popularity of video games. In 1995, Japanese video game giant Nintendo acquired a majority stake in the British/American software company [Rare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_\(company\)), which had developed several hit games for Nintendo's consoles. In 1999, French video game publisher [Atari SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_SA) acquired the U.S. company [GT Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GT_Interactive), which was best known for publishing the popular first-person shooter game *Doom*.

The early 2000s saw a wave of consolidation in the video game industry, as companies sought to cut costs and increase efficiency. In 2001, Microsoft entered the video game console market with the launch of the [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox), and it quickly began acquiring a number of studios and developers to bolster its offerings. In 2002, Microsoft acquired Rare from Nintendo, and in 2004 it acquired the video game developer Bungie, which had created the hugely popular *Halo* series.[^cite_note-160]

Meanwhile, other companies continued to make strategic acquisitions in the video game industry. In 2003, Electronic Arts, one of the largest video game publishers in the world, acquired the sports video game developer Tiburon, which had developed the popular *Madden NFL* franchise. In 2004, EA acquired the racing game developer Criterion Games, which had created the popular *Burnout* series.[^cite_note-161]

The trend of consolidation in the video game industry continued through the 2010s, as companies sought to build out their portfolios and expand their reach. In 2011, Sony acquired the cloud-based gaming service Gaikai, which it later used to launch its PlayStation Now subscription service.[^cite_note-162] In 2012, Disney acquired the video game developer and publisher LucasArts, as part of its acquisition of Lucasfilm.[^cite_note-163]

The 2020s saw a massive acceleration in video game company acquisitions. [ZeniMax Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZeniMax_Media) (Bethesda) was acquired by Microsoft in March 2021. In January 2022, Sony Interactive Entertainment announced its [Bungie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungie) acquisition.[^cite_note-Bankhurst-164] Take Two Interactive's acquisition of mobile video game maker [Zynga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zynga) is the second most expensive video game acquisition of all time.[^cite_note-videogameschronicle.com-165] The biggest video game acquisition of all time was Microsoft's Activision Blizzard merger, announced in February 2022.[^cite_note-166]

The video game industry is dominated by a handful of major players, including Microsoft, Sony, Nintendo, Electronic Arts, and Activision Blizzard. These companies have grown through a combination of organic growth and strategic acquisitions, and they continue to invest heavily in the development of new games and technologies.[^cite_note-167]

### Education training

Video game designers may work with computer graphics, animation, software design, programming and hardware, depending on their role. Relevant education can include courses or degrees in computer programming, computer engineering, software development, animation and computer graphics. Practical experience may be gained through internships, apprenticeships, freelance work or independent game development, which can also contribute to a professional portfolio.[^cite_note-electronics.howstuffworks.com-177]

### Job market

The U.S. video game industry continues to function as a vital source of employment. Currently, video game companies directly and indirectly employ more than 120,000 people in 34 states. The average compensation for direct employees is \$90,000, resulting in total national compensation of \$2.9 billion.[^cite_note-178]

The U.S. Department of Labor projected growth in software publishing employment, although this category is broader than video game design.[^cite_note-179] Salaries for game designers vary according to role, employer and location. An [International Game Developers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Game_Developers_Association) report listed entry-level salaries between \$50,000 to \$80,000 annually, with an average of \$57.600, while Learn Direct reported an entry-level figure of approximately \$37,000.[^cite_note-electronics.howstuffworks.com-177]

## Game ratings and government oversight

Main article: [Video game controversies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_controversies)

Prior to 1993, there was no standardized content rating body in the United States, but with games becoming more violent and with capabilities to show more realistic graphics, parents, politicians, and other concerned citizens called for government regulation of the industry. The [1993 congressional hearings on video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_congressional_hearings_on_video_games), putting the recently released *[Mortal Kombat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortal_Kombat_\(1992_video_game\))* and *[Night Trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Trap)* in the spotlight, drew attention to the industry's lack of a standardized rating system. While individual publishers such as Sega and Nintendo had their own methods of rating games, they were not standardized and allowed discrepancies between different console systems including sales of violent games to minors. Members of Congress threatened to pass legislation that would mandate government oversight of video games if the industry did not create its own solution.[^cite_note-183] The industry responded in 1994 by the formation of the trade group the Interactive Digital Software Association (IDSA), today known as the [Entertainment Software Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Software_Association) (ESA), and the creation of the voluntary [Entertainment Software Ratings Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Software_Ratings_Board) (ESRB) ratings system, a system that met the governmental concerns of the time.[^cite_note-184] The ESRB focused mostly on console games at its founding. Computer video game software used the [Recreational Software Advisory Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_Software_Advisory_Council) (RSAC) through 1999, but transitioned to use ESRB in 1999 while the RSAC became more focused on rating online content from the Internet.[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186]

Since 1993, several incidents of gun violence in the United States, such as [the Columbine School shooting of 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbine_High_School_massacre), put more blame on video games for inciting these crimes, thought there is [no conclusive proof that violent video games lead to violent behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_and_video_games). Under demands of parents and concerned citizens, federal and state governments have attempted to pass legislation that would enforce the ESRB rating systems for retail that would pose fines to retailers that sold mature-rated games to minors.\[^cite_note-[www.g4tv.com-65](http://www.g4tv.com-65/)][^cite_note-187] This came to a head in the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) case *[Brown v. Entertainment Merchants Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_v._Entertainment_Merchants_Association)*, which concluded in 2010 that video games were considered a form of protected speech, and regulation of their sales could only be mandated if the material passed the [Miller test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_test) for obscene material.\[^cite_note-[www.g4tv.com-65](http://www.g4tv.com-65/)][^cite_note-188]

The ESRB remains a voluntary system for rating video games in the United States, though nearly all major retail outlets will refuse to sell unrated games and will typically avoid selling those listed as "AO" for adults only. Retailers are voluntarily bound by the age ratings, though the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission), in 2013, found that the ESRB system had the best compliance of preventing sales of mature games to minors compared to the other American entertainment industries.[^cite_note-189] In addition to age ratings, the ESRB rating includes content descriptors (such as "Nudity", "Use of Drugs", and "Blood and Gore") to better describe the type of questionable material that may be in the game. The ESRB not only rates games after reviewing material submitted by the publisher, but also spot-checks games after release to make sure no additional content had been added after review, applying fines and penalties to publishers that do so. Notably, the ESRB was heavily involved over the [*Hot Coffee* mod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Coffee_mod), a [user mod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mod_\(video_gaming\)) of *[Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto:_San_Andreas)* that unlocked a sex scene that had been on the retail disc but otherwise inaccessible without the mod.[^cite_note-190] Currently 85% of American parents are aware of the [ESRB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESRB) rating system and many are finding parental controls on video game consoles useful.\[^cite_note-[www.theesa.com-8](http://www.theesa.com-8/)]

In the digital storefront space, including digital-only games and [downloadable content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downloadable_content) for retail games, the ESRB does not require ratings though encourages developers and publishers to utilize the self-assessment ratings tools provided by the [International Age Rating Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Age_Rating_Coalition) to assign their game a rating which can propagate to other national and regional ratings systems, such as the [Pan European Game Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_European_Game_Information) (PEGI) system.[^cite_note-191]

Arcade games in the United States are rated separated under a "Parental Advisory System" devised by the American Amusement Machine Association, the Amusement & Music Operators Association, and the International Association for the Leisure and Entertainment Industry, along with guidelines for where more mature games should be located in arcades and other code of conduct principles for arcade operators.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193]
