**Cortés** is one of the 18 [departments of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Honduras). The department covers an area of 3,954 km<sup>2</sup> and, in 2015, had an estimated population of 1,612,762, making it the most populous in Honduras. The [Merendón Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_del_Merend%C3%B3n) rise in western Cortés, but the department is mostly a tropical lowland, the [Sula Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sula_Valley), crossed by the [Ulúa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ul%C3%BAa_River) and the [Chamelecón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamelec%C3%B3n) river.

It was created in 1893 from parts of the departments of [Santa Bárbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_B%C3%A1rbara_Department,_Honduras) and [Yoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoro_Department). The departmental capital is [San Pedro Sula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Sula). Main cities also include [Choloma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choloma), [La Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Lima), [Villanueva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanueva,_Cort%C3%A9s), and the [sea ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_port) of [Puerto Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Cort%C3%A9s) and [Omoa](339891.md). The Atlantic coast of the Department of Cortés is known for its many excellent beaches.

Cortés is the economic heartland of Honduras, as the Sula Valley is the country's main agricultural and industrial region. US banana companies arrived in the area in the late 19th century, and established vast plantations, as well as infrastructure to ship the fruit to the United States. San Pedro Sula attracted substantial numbers of European, Central American, and [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) and Lebanese immigrants. Industry flourishes in the department, and Cortés today hosts most of the country's assembly plants, known as [maquilas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquiladora).
