A **substation** is a part of an electrical [generation](9540.md), [transmission](38824.md), and [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_distribution) system. Substations transform [voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage) from high to low, or the reverse, or perform any of several other important functions. Between the generating station and the consumer, electric power may flow through several substations at different voltage levels. A substation may include [transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer) to change voltage levels between high transmission voltages and lower distribution voltages, or at the interconnection of two different transmission voltages. They are a common component of the infrastructure. As of 2022, there were approximately 55,000 substations in the United States.[^cite_note-2] Substations are also occasionally known in some countries as **switchyards**.

Substations may be owned and operated by an electrical utility, or may be owned by a large industrial or commercial customer. Generally substations are unattended, relying on [SCADA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCADA) for remote supervision and control.

The word *substation* comes from the days before the distribution system became a [grid](38824.md). As central generation stations became larger, smaller generating plants were converted to distribution stations, receiving their energy supply from a larger plant instead of using their own generators. The first substations were connected to only one [power station](212141.md), where the generators were housed, and were subsidiaries of that power station.



220 kV/110 kV/20 kV station in Germany

## Construction

Substations may be designed and built by a [contractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_contractor) or alternately all phases of its development may be handled by the [electrical utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_utility). Most commonly, the utility does the engineering and procurement while hiring a contractor for actual construction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOkada2018334-3] Major design constraints for construction of substations include land availability and cost, limitations on the construction period, transportation restrictions, and the need to get the substation running quickly.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOkada2018335-4] Prefabrication is a common way to reduce the construction cost.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOkada2018341-5] For connecting the new substation, a partial outage at another substation may be required, but the utility often tries to minimize downtime.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOkada2018342-6]

### Transmission substation

A *transmission substation* connects two or more transmission lines.[^cite_note-Design_Guide_for_Rural_Substations-10] The simplest case is where all transmission lines have the same voltage. In such cases, the substation contains high-voltage switches that allow lines to be connected or isolated for fault clearance or maintenance. A transmission station may have [transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer) to convert between two transmission voltages, [voltage control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_compensation)/[power factor correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_factor#Power_factor_correction_\(PFC\)_in_non-linear_loads) devices such as capacitors, reactors or [static VAR compensators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_VAR_compensator) and equipment such as [phase shifting transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrature_booster) to control power flow between two adjacent power systems.



Minimal HV station in Germany

Transmission substations can range from simple to complex. A small "switching station" may be little more than a [bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busbar) plus some [circuit breakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_breaker#High-voltage_circuit_breakers). The largest transmission substations can cover a large area (several acres/hectares) with multiple voltage levels, many circuit breakers, and a large amount of protection and control equipment ([voltage and current transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_transformer), [relays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_protective_relay) and [SCADA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCADA) systems). Modern substations may be implemented using international standards such as [IEC Standard 61850](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEC_61850).

### Distribution substation



Transformer tower in Germany. Medium voltage supply at the front, low voltage output on the side.



A distribution substation in [Toronto](64646.md), Canada, disguised as a house, complete with a driveway, front walk and a mown lawn and shrubs in the front yard. A warning notice can be clearly seen on the "front door". Disguises for substations are common in many cities.

[^cite_note-11]

A *distribution substation* transfers power from the transmission system to the distribution system of an area.[^cite_note-Design_Guide_for_Rural_Substations-10] It is uneconomical to directly connect electricity consumers to the main transmission network, unless they use large amounts of power, so the distribution station reduces voltage to a level suitable for local distribution.[^cite_note-12]

The input for a distribution substation is typically at least two transmission or sub-transmission lines. Input voltage may be, for example, 115 kV, or whatever is common in the area. The output is a number of feeders. Distribution voltages are typically medium voltage, between 2.4 kV and 33 kV, depending on the size of the area served and the practices of the local utility. The feeders run along streets overhead (or underground, in some cases) and power the distribution transformers at or near the customer premises.

In addition to transforming voltage, distribution substations also isolate faults in either the transmission or distribution systems. Distribution substations are typically the points of [voltage regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_regulation), although on long distribution circuits (of several miles/kilometers), voltage regulation equipment may also be installed along the line.

The downtown areas of large cities feature complicated distribution substations, with high-voltage switching, and switching and backup systems on the low-voltage side. More typical distribution substations have a switch, one transformer, and minimal facilities on the low-voltage side.

### Collector substation

In [distributed generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_generation) projects such as a [wind farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_farm) or [photovoltaic power station](34779206.md), a collector substation may be required. It resembles a distribution substation although power flow is in the opposite direction, from many [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine) or [inverters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverters) up into the transmission grid. Usually for economy of construction the collector system operates around 35 kV, although some collector systems are 12 kV, and the collector substation steps up voltage to a transmission voltage for the grid. The collector substation can also provide [power factor correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_factor_correction) if it is needed, metering, and control of the wind farm. In some special cases a collector substation can also contain an HVDC converter station.

Collector substations also exist where multiple thermal or hydroelectric power plants of comparable output power are in proximity. Examples for such substations are [Brauweiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brauweiler) in Germany and [Hradec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hradec_substation) in the Czech Republic, where power is collected from nearby [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite)-fired power plants. If no transformers are required for increasing the voltage to transmission level, the substation is a switching station.

### Converter substations

Converter substations may be associated with [HVDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-voltage_direct_current) converter plants, [traction current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traction_current), or interconnected non-synchronous networks. These stations contain power electronic devices to change the frequency of current, or else convert from alternating to direct current or the reverse. Formerly [rotary converters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotary_converter) changed frequency to interconnect two systems; nowadays such substations are rare.

### Switching station

A switching station is a substation without transformers and operating only at a single voltage level. Switching stations are sometimes used as collector and distribution stations. Sometimes they are used for switching the current to back-up lines or for parallelizing circuits in case of failure. An example is the switching stations for the [Inga–Shaba HVDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inga%E2%80%93Shaba_HVDC) transmission line.

A switching station may also be known as a switchyard, and these are commonly located directly adjacent to or nearby a [power station](212141.md). In this case the generators from the power station supply their power into the yard onto the generator bus on one side of the yard, and the transmission lines take their power from a Feeder Bus on the other side of the yard.

An important function performed by a substation is [switching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switch#Power_switching), which is the connecting and disconnecting of transmission lines or other components to and from the system. Switching events may be planned or unplanned. A transmission line or other component may need to be de-energized for maintenance or for new construction, for example, adding or removing a transmission line or a transformer. To maintain reliability of supply, companies aim at keeping the system up and running while performing maintenance. All work to be performed, from routine testing to adding entirely new substations, should be done while keeping the whole system running.

Unplanned switching events are caused by a [fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(power_engineering\)) in a transmission line or any other component, for example:

- a line is hit by lightning and develops an [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_arc),

- a [tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_tower) is blown down by high wind.

The function of the switching station is to isolate the faulty portion of the system in the shortest possible time. De-energizing faulty equipment protects it from further damage, and isolating a fault helps keep the rest of the electrical grid operating with stability.[^cite_note-13]

### Railways

Main article: [Traction substation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traction_substation)

Electrified railways also use substations, often distribution substations. In some cases a conversion of the current type takes place, commonly with [rectifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifier) for [direct current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_current) (DC) trains, or [rotary converters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotary_converter) for trains using [alternating current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_current) (AC) at frequencies other than that of the public grid. Sometimes they are also transmission substations or collector substations if the railway network also operates its own grid and generators to supply the other stations.

### Mobile substation

A *mobile substation* is a substation on wheels, containing a transformer, breakers and buswork mounted on a self-contained [semi-trailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-trailer), meant to be pulled by a [truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tractor_unit). They are designed to be compact for travel on public roads, and are used for temporary backup in times of [natural disaster](58921.md) or [war](33158.md). Mobile substations are usually rated much lower than permanent installations, and may be built in several units to meet road travel limitations.[^cite_note-Mobile_Substations-14]

Substation design is aimed at minimizing cost while ensuring power availability and reliability, and enabling changes to the substation in the future.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201968-15]

Substations may be built outdoors, indoors, or underground or in a combination of these locations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlaubitz_et_al.2018375-16][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlaubitz_et_al.2018422-17]

### Location selection



Substations on the United States grid

Selection of the location of a substation must consider many factors. Sufficient land area is required for installation of equipment with necessary clearances for electrical safety, and for access to maintain large apparatus such as transformers. The site must have room for expansion due to load growth or planned transmission additions. Environmental effects of the substation must be considered, such as [drainage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage), noise and road traffic effects.

The substation site must be reasonably central to the distribution area to be served. The site must be secure from intrusion by passers-by, both to protect people from injury by electric shock or arcs, and to protect the electrical system from misoperation due to vandalism.

If not owned and operated by a utility company, substations are typically occupied on a long [lease](635681.md) such as a renewable 99-year lease, giving the utility company [security of tenure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_of_tenure).[^cite_note-18]

### Design diagrams



Tottenham Substation, set in wild parkland in North London.

The first step in planning a substation layout is the preparation of a [single-line diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-line_diagram), which shows in simplified form the switching and protection arrangement required, as well as the incoming supply lines and outgoing feeders or transmission lines. It is a usual practice by many electrical utilities to prepare one-line diagrams with principal elements (lines, switches, circuit breakers, transformers) arranged on the page similarly to the way the apparatus would be laid out in the actual station.[^cite_note-Design_Guide_for_Rural_Substations-10]

In a common design, incoming lines have a [disconnector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disconnector) and a [circuit breaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_breaker). In some cases, the lines will not have both, with either a switch or a circuit breaker being all that is considered necessary. A disconnect switch is used to provide isolation, since it cannot interrupt load current. A circuit breaker is used as a protection device to interrupt fault currents automatically, and may be used to switch loads on and off, or to cut off a line when power is flowing in the 'wrong' direction. When a large fault current flows through the circuit breaker, this is detected through the use of [current transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_transformer). The magnitude of the current transformer outputs may be used to trip the circuit breaker resulting in a disconnection of the load supplied by the circuit break from the feeding point. This seeks to isolate the fault point from the rest of the system, and allow the rest of the system to continue operating with minimal impact. Both switches and circuit breakers may be operated locally (within the substation) or remotely from a supervisory control center.

With [overhead transmission lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_transmission_line), the propagation of [lightning](61344.md) and switching [surges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_spike) can cause [insulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulator_\(electricity\)) failures into substation equipment. Line entrance [surge arrestors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surge_arrester) are used to protect substation equipment accordingly. Insulation Coordination studies are carried out extensively to ensure equipment failure (and associated [outages](296636.md)) is minimal.

Once past the switching components, the lines of a given voltage connect to one or more [buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busbar). These are sets of [busbars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busbar), usually in multiples of three, since [three-phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-phase) electrical power distribution is largely universal around the world.

The arrangement of switches, circuit breakers, and buses used affects the cost and reliability of the substation. For important substations a ring bus, double bus, or so-called "breaker and a half" setup can be used, so that the failure of any one circuit breaker does not interrupt power to other circuits, and so that parts of the substation may be de-energized for maintenance and repairs. Substations feeding only a single industrial load may have minimal switching provisions, especially for small installations.[^cite_note-STD11-19]



This single-line diagram illustrates the breaker-and-a-half configuration often used in switchyards of small utilities. In large utilities the double-bus-double-breaker configuration is often preferred.

### Safety



A warning sign commonly used at substations in the United Kingdom, warning of the danger of lethal electric shock

Because of the risk of electrical shock, substations are inherently dangerous to electrical workers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201941-20] To mitigate this hazard, substations are designed with various safety features.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201942-21] Live conductors and bare equipment are kept separate, either with protected equipment, or using screens or distance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201942-21] Based on the jurisdiction or company,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201946,_53-22] there are safety standards with minimum required clearance between different live equipment or conductors or between live metal and the ground, which often varies with higher clearance being required for higher voltages because of the greater ability to generate [flashover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashover).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201946–47-23] To this is added the necessary space for employees to work safely and vehicles to pass.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201948–49-24] Sometimes it is necessary to work on parts of the substation while energized, but employees must maintain a safe distance of at least 3 metres (9.8 ft).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201951-25] The aim to reduce substation footprints comes into conflict with ease of maintenance enhanced by including gaps where employees can safely work.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201954-26]

Underneath a substation, a mat or grid of conductors laid around 0.5 or 0.6 metres (1 ft 8 in or 2 ft 0 in) underground provides [grounding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_\(electricity\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201955–56-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENixon_et_al.2018313-28] This grid, which is typically copper although it may be [galvanized iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanized_iron) in some countries,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENixon_et_al.2018313-28] is used to ground circuits that are being worked on to prevent accidental re-energization while workers are in contact with a de-energized circuit.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201956-29] Often, earth rods are driven deeper into the ground from the grounding grid for lower resistance grounding,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENixon_et_al.2018313–314-30] and may be surrounded by [bentonite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentonite) or [marconite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marconite) to further reduce resistance and ensure effective grounding for the lifetime of the substation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENixon_et_al.2018314–315-31] Above ground, the grounding conductors may be steel, aluminum, or copper. They must be thick enough to carry the expected current of a fault for 1–3 seconds and remain undamaged.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENixon_et_al.2018313-28] Substation fences, typically at least 2 metres (6 ft 7 in) in height, both protect the public from electrical hazards and also protect the substation from vandalism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201964-32] Internal fences can also be incorporated to protect employees from areas that are unsafe when energized.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201965-33]

## Components

Substations generally have switching, protection and control equipment, and transformers. In a large substation, [circuit breakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_breaker) are used to interrupt any [short circuits](235898.md) or overload currents that may occur on the network. Smaller distribution stations may use [recloser circuit breakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autorecloser) or [fuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuse_\(electrical\)) for protection of distribution circuits. Substations themselves do not usually have generators, although a [power plant](212141.md) may have a substation nearby. Other devices such as [capacitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor), [voltage regulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_regulator), and [reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switchyard_reactor) may also be located at a substation.

Substations may be on the surface in fenced enclosures, underground, or special-purpose buildings. High-rise buildings may have several indoor substations. Indoor substations are usually found in urban areas to reduce the noise from transformers, improve appearance, or protect switchgear from extreme climate or pollution.

Substations often use [busbars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busbar) as conductors between electrical equipment. Busbars may be aluminum tubing 3–6 inches (76–152 mm) thick, or else wires (strain bus).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlume201681-34]

Outdoor, above-ground substation structures include wood pole, lattice metal tower, and tubular metal structures, although other variants are available. Where space is plentiful and appearance of the station is not a factor, steel lattice towers provide low-cost supports for transmission lines and apparatus. Low-profile substations may be specified in suburban areas where appearance is more critical. Indoor substations may be [gas insulated substations (GIS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas-insulated_switchgear) (at high voltages, with gas insulated switchgear), or use metal-enclosed or metal-clad switchgear at lower voltages. Urban and suburban indoor substations may be finished on the outside so as to blend in with other buildings in the area.

A *compact substation* is generally an outdoor substation built in a metal enclosure, in which each item of the electrical equipment is located very near to each other to create a relatively smaller footprint size of the substation.

### Switchgear

High-voltage [circuit breakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_breaker) are commonly used to interrupt the flow of current in substation equipment. - Air at atmospheric pressure (air-insulated switchgear (AIS)), which is the most common worldwide.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlaubitz2018365-40]

- Gas (gas circuit breaker (GCB) or gas-insulated switchgear (GIS)), most commonly [sulfur hexafluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_hexafluoride) (SF

- Mixed, including both gas and air insulation. Although it's the least common option

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTETwomey201812-37]

  &#x20;it can be useful when an air-insulated substation needs to be expanded but there is very limited location for additional construction.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTETwomey201813-38]

[Reclosers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recloser) are similar to breakers, and can be cheaper because they do not require separate protective relays. Often used in distribution, they often are programmed to trip when the amps exceed a certain amount over a period of time. Reclosers will attempt to re-energize the circuit after a delay. If unsuccessful for a few times, the recloser will have to be manually reset by an electrical worker.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlume201674-46]

### Capacitors

[Capacitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor) banks are used in substations to balance the lagging current draw from [inductive loads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_load) (such as motors, transformers, and some industrial equipment) with their [reactive load](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactive_load?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlume201683-47] Additional capacitor capacity may be needed if [dispersed generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispersed_generation) (such as small diesel generators, rooftop [photovoltaic solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_solar_panel), or [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine)) are added to the system.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201976-48] Capacitors can reduce the current in wires, helping stem system losses from [voltage drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_drop) or enabling extra power to be sent through the conductors. Capacitors may be left on in response to constant inductive load or turned on when inductive load is increased, such as in the summer for [air conditioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioner). The switching may be remote and can be done manually or automatically.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlume201683-47]

### Control rooms



Workers at a substation in Glasgow during World War I, monitoring and recording voltages. The machines on the right are [rotary converters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotary_converter).

Larger substations have control rooms for the equipment used to monitor, control, and protect the rest of the substation equipment. It often contains protective relays, meters, breaker controls, communications, batteries, and recorders that save detailed data about substation operations, particularly when there is any unusual activity, to help reconstruct what happened after the fact. These control rooms typically are heated and air conditioned to ensure the reliable operation of this equipment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlume201687–88-49] Additional equipment is necessary to handle power surges associated with intermittent renewable energy such as dispersed generation from wind or solar.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201977-50]

### Transformers

Most transformers lose between 5 and 1.5 percent of their input as heat and noise. Iron losses are no-load and constant whenever the transformer is energized, while copper and auxiliary losses are proportionate to the square of the current. Auxiliary losses are due to running fans and pumps[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENixon_et_al.2018321-51] which is noisy when the transformer is operating at maximum capacity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENixon_et_al.2018322-52] To reduce noise, enclosures are often built around the transformer and can also be added after the substation is built.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENixon_et_al.2018322-52]

Oil-based transformers are often built with bunded areas to prevent the escape of flaming or leaking oil. Fire separation areas or firewalls are built around the transformer to stop the spread of fire.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201962-53] Firefighting vehicles are allowed a path to access the area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFinn201963-54]

## Maintenance

Maintenance of substations involves inspections, data collection and analysis, and routine scheduled work. Using methods such as infrared scanning and dissolved gas analysis, it can be predicted when the substation will need maintenance and predict dangers before they materialize. Infrared technology finds hot spots in the substation where electrical energy is being converted to heat,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlume201688-55] which indicates a problem and can cause additional damage from the high heat. Dissolved gas analysis can tell when an oil-insulated transformer needs to have the oil filtered or replaced, and also detect other issues.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlume201689-56]

## Automation

Main article: [Power-system automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-system_automation)

Early electrical substations required manual switching or adjustment of equipment, and manual collection of data for load, energy consumption, and abnormal events. As the complexity of distribution networks grew, it became economically necessary to automate supervision and control of substations from a centrally attended point, to allow overall coordination in case of emergencies and to reduce operating costs. Early efforts to remote control substations used dedicated communication wires, often run alongside power circuits. [Power-line carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-line_communication), [microwave radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_transmission), [fiber optic](3372377.md) cables as well as dedicated wired remote control circuits have all been applied to [Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCADA) (SCADA) for substations. The development of the [microprocessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microprocessor) made for an exponential increase in the number of points that could be economically controlled and monitored. Today, standardized communication protocols such as [DNP3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNP3), [IEC 61850](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEC_61850) and [Modbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modbus), to list a few, are used to allow multiple intelligent electronic devices to communicate with each other and supervisory control centers. Distributed automatic control at substations is one element of the so-called [smart grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_grid).
