# Moonbase

For other uses, see [Moonbase (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonbase_\(disambiguation\)).



[Tranquility Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranquility_Base):

[^cite_note-y094-1]

&#x20;(L to R) Astronaut [Buzz Aldrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Aldrin), scientific equipment, [Apollo Lunar Module *Eagle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Module_Eagle); US flag and television camera in back (photo by [Neil Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Armstrong))

A **moonbase** (or **lunar base**) is a [human outpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_outpost) on or beneath the surface of the [Moon](19331.md).[^cite_note-Classes-2] More than a mere site of activity or temporary camp, moonbases are extraterrestrial bases, supporting [robotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncrewed_spaceflight) or [human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crewed_spaceflight) activity, by providing surface infrastructure. [Missions to the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_missions_to_the_Moon) have realized single-mission bases ([Tranquility Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranquility_Base) in 1969 being the first), as well as [some small permanent infrastructure](19331.md#Human_interaction_and_presence) like [lunar laser ranging installations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Laser_Ranging_Experiment).

Plans for establishing permanent multi-mission moonbases, with surface or sub-surface [research stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_station), are being proposed internationally. As of 2025, the two most advanced projects to establish moonbases have been pursued [multilaterally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateralism) as part of the U.S.-led [Artemis program](60758751.md), with its planned [Artemis Base Camp](60758751.md#Shelter_building_construction) and as the China-led [International Lunar Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Research_Station).[^cite_note-3] A broad, international infrastructure has been envisioned with the so-called [Moon Village concept](#Moon_Village_concept), and a general international regulatory regime for lunar activity has been called for by the 1979 signed [Moon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Treaty), and advocated with an [implementation agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Treaty#Implementation_agreement) since 2020.[^cite_note-The_Space_Review_2020-4][^cite_note-The_Space_Treaty_Institute_–_Dedicated_to_Peace_and_Sustainability_in_Outer_Space._Our_Mission-5]

The surface infrastructure of a base may consist of pre-integrated basic [landers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lander_\(spacecraft\)), as supporting stations for robotic [rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rover_\(space_exploration\)), or [habitation modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_habitat_\(facility\)#Extraterrestrial_surface_habitat) for crewed presence, or of surface assembled or in-situ derived and constructed surface stations for sustained [lunar habitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_habitation).[^cite_note-Classes-2] Lunar bases may work with lunar [space stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_station), which provide supporting infrastructure in [lunar orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_orbit).

The development of moonbases into permanent [extraterrestrial settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterrestrial_settlement) has been proposed.[^cite_note-Classes-2][^cite_note-o167-6] Broad [lunar colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_colonization) or [space colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_colonization#Colonialism) in general, particularly as laying territorial claims, which is prohibited by international [space law](743972.md), has been criticized for perpetuating [colonialism](7299.md).[^cite_note-Wall_2019-7]



[Apollo 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_12) astronaut [Pete Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Conrad) with [Surveyor 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveyor_3) and Apollo 12 *[Statio Cognitum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statio_Cognitum)* lunar base with the *Intrepid* lander and S-band antenna in the background, in a first ever [visit of a separate mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_rendezvous) beyond [Low Earth Orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Earth_Orbit)

## Past bases

Further information: [List of missions to the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_missions_to_the_Moon)

The U.S. [Apollo Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Program) established the first six temporary bases on the Moon, starting with [Tranquility Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranquility_Base) (1969).

Later robotic missions set up simple robotic temporary bases, rovers and [sample-return missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample-return_mission) leaving their landers behind, starting with [Luna 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_16) (1970).



Soviet stamp commemorating [Luna 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_16) (1970), with its return stage separating from its lander.

## Regulation

Main article: [Moon § Legal status](19331.md#Legal_status)

The 1967 [Outer Space Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Space_Treaty) defines the Moon and all outer space as the "[province of all mankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_heritage_of_mankind)".[^cite_note-unoosa_q6-9] It restricts the use of the Moon to peaceful purposes, explicitly banning military installations and [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_of_mass_destruction).[^cite_note-unoosa_q5-10] A majority of countries are parties of this treaty.[^cite_note-unoosa_q4-11]

The 1979 [Moon Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Treaty) was created to elaborate on the Outer Space Treaty, restricting the exploitation of the [Moon's resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_resources), particularly by any single nation, leaving it to a yet unspecified international regulatory regime.[^cite_note-The_Space_Review_2021-12] As of January 2020, the Moon Agreement has been signed and ratified by 18 nations,[^cite_note-unoosa_moon-13] none of which have [human spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight) capabilities.

Current lunar programs are [multilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateralism), with the US-led [Artemis program](60758751.md) and the China-led [International Lunar Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Research_Station). For broad international cooperation and coordination, the [International Lunar Exploration Working Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Exploration_Working_Group) (ILEWG), the [Moon Village Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Village_Association?action=edit\&redlink=1) (MVA) and more generally the [International Space Exploration Coordination Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Exploration_Coordination_Group?action=edit\&redlink=1) (ISECG) have been established.

Since 2020, countries have joined the U.S. in the [Artemis Accords](63968963.md), which are challenging the treaty. The U.S. has furthermore emphasized in a presidential [executive order](210541.md) ("Encouraging International Support for the Recovery and Use of Space Resources.") that "the United States does not view outer space as a 'global commons'" and calls the Moon Agreement "a failed attempt at constraining free enterprise."[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

With Australia signing and ratifying both the Moon Treaty in 1986 as well as the Artemis Accords in 2020, there has been a discussion if they can be harmonized.[^cite_note-Australian_Institute_of_International_Affairs_2021-16] In this light an *Implementation Agreement* for the Moon Treaty has been advocated for, as a way to compensate for the shortcomings of the Moon Treaty and to harmonize it with other laws and agreements such as the Artemis Accords, allowing it to be more widely accepted.[^cite_note-The_Space_Review_2020-4][^cite_note-The_Space_Treaty_Institute_–_Dedicated_to_Peace_and_Sustainability_in_Outer_Space._Our_Mission-5]

### Conservation

Further information: [Space sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_sustainability)

In the face of such increasing commercial and national interest, particularly prospecting territories, U.S. lawmakers have introduced in late 2020 specific regulation for the conservation of historic landing sites[^cite_note-HowStuffWorks_2021-17] and interest groups have argued for making such sites [World Heritage Sites](44940.md)[^cite_note-For_All_Moonkind-18] and zones of scientific value protected zones, all of which add to the legal availability and territorialization of the Moon.[^cite_note-Alvarez_2020_p.-19]

The United States has run several attempts to design and in some cases develop lunar outposts and the needed missions, the first being from 1959, with the upcoming Artemis missions being the most advanced.

### Current: Artemis Program

This section is an excerpt from [NASA lunar outpost concepts § Artemis Base Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_lunar_outpost_concepts#Artemis_Base_Camp).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=NASA_lunar_outpost_concepts\&action=edit)]

The US-led [Artemis Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_Program) has scheduled several crewed landings at the [Moon's south polar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole) region, starting with [Artemis IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_IV) planned for early 2028 at the earliest, setting up five temporary base camps with the [Human Landing Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Landing_System) (HLS) until [Artemis VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_Program#Artemis_flights) is planned to set up the fixed [Foundational Surface Habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_Program#Foundational_Surface_Habitat) (FSH) of the Artemis Base Camp in the 2030s.[^cite_note-NASA_lunar_outpost_concepts_o093-20][^cite_note-NASA_lunar_outpost_concepts_r096-21][^cite_note-NASA_lunar_outpost_concepts_j275-22] India was also invited to contribute to the Moonbase project.[^cite_note-23]



Concept art of Artemis Base Camp at the [Lunar south pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole) with a [Foundational Surface Habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-Purpose_Habitation_\(lunar_base\)) and a [Pressurized rover ("Mobile Habitat")](60758751.md#Pressurized_rover_\(%22Mobile_Habitat%22\))

#### Foundational Surface Habitat: Multi-Purpose Habitation module

Main article: [Multi-Purpose Habitation (lunar base)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-Purpose_Habitation_\(lunar_base\))

Under a bilateral Moon cooperation agreement between NASA and the Italian Space Agency ( known as [ASI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Space_Agency)) and within the [Artemis Accords](63968963.md), [Thales Alenia Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Alenia_Space) is designing a Foundational Surface Habitat, the [Multi-Purpose Habitation (MPH) module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-Purpose_Habitation_\(lunar_base\)). The MPH module will host Artemis astronauts and support both surface operations and scientific research experiments. It will operate both with and without the presence of a human crew and will have the ability to move on the surface.[^cite_note-24] In March 2026, ASI president Teodoro Valente indicated, "A long-standing space cooperation, now even deeper, between NASA and ASI will lead to the creation of a base camp on the Moon and an Italian astronaut walking on the lunar surface."[^cite_note-25]

## Moon Village concept



3D-printed lunar habitat (ESA, 2018)

The [European Space Agency](10363.md) (ESA) endorsed a **Moon Village** concept in 2015.[^cite_note-26] The "Village" is an international group of public and private investors, scientists, engineers, universities, and corporate actors coordinating to build and share infrastructure on the Moon and in [cislunar](177602.md) space. Moon Village promotes international cooperation and the [commercialization of space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_use_of_space).[^cite_note-Wörner_2016-27][^cite_note-LD_2016-28][^cite_note-Interview_2016-29]

The Moon Village Association is a nonprofit organization established in [Vienna](55866.md), Austria, in November 2017.[^cite_note-PS_Lunar_Village_2018-30][^cite_note-31] with the mission to create a global forum to build and maintain a permanent human settlement near the [lunar south pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole), taking advantage of near-continuous sunlight and nearby deposits of ice and other useful [volatiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatile_\(astrogeology\)).[^cite_note-PS_Lunar_Village_2018-30]

> The Moon Village is not one project or one program. It says, "Let's do it together."&#x20;

— [Jan Wörner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann-Dietrich_W%C3%B6rner)[^cite_note-32]

According to ESA, the Village's objective is to create a permanent sustainable presence on the surface of the Moon, whether robotic or crewed.[^cite_note-Wörner_2016-27][^cite_note-LD_2016-28] In 2017, ESA Director General [Jan Wörner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann-Dietrich_W%C3%B6rner), described the Village as "an understanding, not a single facility",[^cite_note-33] and a first step toward Martian exploration.[^cite_note-Wörner_2016-27][^cite_note-PhysOrg_Sep_2018-34] Wörner suggested the Moon Village as a successor to the orbiting [International Space Station](15043.md) (ISS).[^cite_note-PhysOrg_Sep_2018-34][^cite_note-Jesus_2017-35]



Inflatable lunar base with greenhouses, all radiation shielded through [regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regolith), while sunlight enters through mirrors (ESA, 2022)

[^cite_note-r625-36]

China has expressed interest in the Moon Village[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] as has NASA, as the Moon Village relates to NASA's proposed [Lunar Gateway](53648310.md) space station.[^cite_note-Interview_2016-29][^cite_note-39] The private aerospace company [Blue Origin](887418.md) has expressed early interest and [offered to develop a cargo lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_Blue_Moon) with a 4,500 kg (9,900 lb) capacity of usable payload.[^cite_note-sn20180529-40]

In 2018, the [Vienna University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Wien) received sponsorship from ESA for a design workshop on the topic of the Moon Village. 35 master students have developed hypothetical scenarios for a future Moon village. The cooperative concept of the workshop led to a number of new themes, such as multipurpose mobile infrastructure, an astro-scientist training campus on the Moon, an experimental research food lab, and a lunar recycling facility.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

## Chinese concept

Main article: [International Lunar Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Research_Station)



Map of the **International Lunar Research Station** (ILRS) states

In 2020, China proposed the **International Lunar Research Station** (ILRS), a somewhat similar proposal to the Moon Village, with the Russian space agency [Roscosmos](406573.md) and the ESA showing interest.[^cite_note-43] The first steps toward establishing the ILRS will be taken through [Phase IV of the Chinese Lunar Exploration Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Lunar_Exploration_Program#Phase_IV_\(robotic\):_Lunar_robotic_research_station), consisting of [Chang'e 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_6), [7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_7), and [8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_8), as well as the Russian missions, [Luna 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_25), [26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_26), and [27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_27). Long-term robotic and short-term crew missions at the ILRS are expected to begin in the early 2030s.[^cite_note-44] Roscosmos signed a memorandum of understanding on cooperation in the creation of the ILRS with CNSA on March 9, 2021.[^cite_note-CnsaRoscosmosLunarBase-45] There is a projected timeline stretching from the 2030s to 2045.[^cite_note-cnsa2045-46]

The [China National Space Administration](344618.md) ([CNSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNSA)) has commenced the [Chang'e program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_program) for exploring the Moon to investigate the prospect of [lunar mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_resources#Mining), specifically for mining [isotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope) [helium-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium-3) for use as an energy source on Earth.[^cite_note-Luan-47] CNSA director Luan Enjie has stated that humans must learn to leave [Earth](9228.md) and "set up a self-sufficient extraterrestrial homeland".[^cite_note-Luan-47]

On March 9, 2021, Russia and China signed a memorandum of understanding for the joint construction of the International Lunar Research Station (ILRS) around 2035.

## Soviet Union concepts (defunct)

### Lunny Poligon

Main article: [Lunny Poligon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunny_Poligon)

The [Russian Federal Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federal_Space_Agency) ([Roscosmos](406573.md)) has planned a fully robotic lunar base called **Lunny Poligon**. The project was planned for 2020, with an expected completion date of 2037. However, on March 9, 2021, [Russia](25391.md) turned to cooperate with [China](5405.md) and signed a memorandum of understanding for the joint construction of the International Lunar Research Station (ILRS).

### Zvezda

Main article: [Zvezda (moonbase)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvezda_\(moonbase\))

Zvezda moonbase ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): звезда, *"star"*) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) plan and project from 1962 to 1974 to construct a crewed moonbase as successor to the [N1-L3 crewed lunar expedition program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_crewed_lunar_programs). The project was ordered by the Soviet space chief [Sergei Korolyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Korolev) to [Vladimir Barmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Barmin)'s Spetcmash bureau. The project was named *DLB Lunar Base* in technical specifications and *Zvezda* in government documents. Unofficially, the project was called *Barmingrad* (Barmin's city) by its designers.

The realization of the project depended on key parts of the N1-L3 program – the [N-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_\(rocket\)) superheavy launcher, all 4 launches of which failed between 1969 and 1972. Zvezda moonbase was cancelled with the rest of the Soviet human lunar programs. All crewed Soviet lunar programs, including a Zvezda moonbase, had been classified as top secret and were only published in the [glasnost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasnost) epoch since 1990.

### Lunar Expeditionary Complex

This section is an excerpt from [Lunar Expeditionary Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Expeditionary_Complex).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Lunar_Expeditionary_Complex\&action=edit)]

The [LEK Lunar Expeditionary Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Expeditionary_Complex) was a lunar expedition and [Moon base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization_of_the_Moon) proposed by [Valentin Glushko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_Glushko) in 1974 as a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) response to the [United States](3434750.md)' [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program) and as a successor to the [Zvezda moonbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvezda_\(moonbase\)), which was based on the cancelled [N1-L3 crewed Moon expedition program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_human_lunar_programs). If implemented, it was intended to have been operational by 1980 and used for scientific and engineering research.

### Energia Lunar Expedition

This section is an excerpt from [Energia Lunar Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energia_Lunar_Expedition).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Energia_Lunar_Expedition\&action=edit)]

The [Energia Lunar Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energia_Lunar_Expedition) was a project that was proposed by [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) engineer [Valentin Glushko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_Glushko) in 1988 which would’ve aimed to create a [lunar base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_base)[^cite_note-48] using the [Energia rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energia_\(rocket\)). ## Other concepts



[Lunar lava tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_lava_tube) have been envisioned to house humans ([NASA](18426568.md) concept drawing of astronauts exploring a cave on the Moon, 1988).

In 1954, science fiction writer [Arthur C. Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_C._Clarke) proposed a lunar base of [inflatable modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflatable_space_habitat) covered in lunar dust for insulation.[^cite_note-base-49] A spaceship assembled in [low Earth orbit](47568.md) would launch to the Moon, and astronauts would set up the [igloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igloo)-like modules and an inflatable [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio) mast. Subsequent steps would include the establishment of a larger, permanent dome; an [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae)-based [air purifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_purifier); a [nuclear reactor](22151.md) for the provision of power; and [electromagnetic cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_driver) to launch [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo) and [fuel](21188370.md) to interplanetary vessels in space.

In 1959, John S. Rinehart suggested that the safest design would be a structure that could "\[float] in a stationary ocean of [dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust)", since there were, at the time this concept was outlined, theories that there could be mile-deep dust oceans on the Moon.[^cite_note-50] The proposed design consisted of a half-cylinder with half-domes at both ends, with a [micrometeoroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrometeoroid) shield placed above the base.

In 1962, John DeNike and Stanley Zahn published their idea of a sub-surface base located at the [Sea of Tranquility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_Tranquillitatis).[^cite_note-baseb-51] This base would house a crew of 21, in modules placed four meters below the surface, which was believed to provide [radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_ray) shielding on par with Earth's atmosphere. DeNike and Zahn favored [nuclear reactors](22151.md) for energy production, because they were more efficient than [solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_module), and would also overcome the problems with the long lunar nights. For the life support system, an algae-based gas exchanger was proposed.

As of 2006, the Japanese Aerospace Exploration Agency ([JAXA](716184.md)) planned a human lunar landing around 2020 that would lead to a crewed lunar base by 2030; however, there was no budget yet for this project.[^cite_note-52]

In 2007, Jim Burke, of the [International Space University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_University) in France, said people should plan to preserve humanity's culture in the event of a civilization-stopping asteroid impact with Earth. A Lunar [Noah's Ark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah's_Ark) was proposed.[^cite_note-53] Subsequent planning may be taken up by the [International Lunar Exploration Working Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Exploration_Working_Group) (ILEWG).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

In 2010, *The Moon Capital Competition* offered a prize for a design of a lunar habitat intended to be an underground international commercial center capable of supporting a residential staff of 60 people and their families. The Moon Capital is intended to be self-sufficient with respect to food and other material required for life support. Prize money was provided primarily by the [Boston Society of Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Society_of_Architects), [Google Lunar X Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Lunar_X_Prize) and The New England Council of the [American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Aeronautics_and_Astronautics).[^cite_note-57]

## Surface infrastructure

Further information: [Moon § Infrastructure](19331.md#Infrastructure), and [List of active Solar System probes § Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_Solar_System_probes#Moon)



[Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) Lunar [Laser Ranging Retroreflector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_Ranging_Retroreflector)

Besides temporary infrastructure of lunar missions, some of the permanently placed [artificial objects on the Moon](537458.md) have already fulfilled long-term purposes. Since 1969, [retroreflectors have been installed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_retroreflectors_on_the_Moon) on the surface of the Moon and are actively used for [lunar laser ranging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_laser_ranging). Equatorial sites have been described as easier for landing and launch and allowing constant communication with Earth, although long lunar nights pose challenges for power.[^cite_note-58]

In the 2010s, the [International Lunar Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Network) consisting of robotic instruments placed around the moon was also proposed.

## Orbital infrastructure

Lunar orbital spacecraft have been supporting lunar bases since the use of the [Apollo command and service module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_command_and_service_module) in lunar orbit.

Today an increasing number of lunar satellites provide different services to surface missions and possibly to lunar bases.

The current lunar landing [Artemis program](60758751.md) was to be supported by the since shelved [Lunar Gateway](53648310.md).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]
