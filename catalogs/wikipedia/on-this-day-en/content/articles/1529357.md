**Gyumri** ([Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Գյումրի,[^cite_note-3] pronounced [\[ɡjumˈɾi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Armenian)) is an urban municipal community and the [second-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Armenia) in [Armenia](10918072.md), serving as the administrative center of [Shirak Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirak_Province) in the northwestern part of the country. By the end of the 19th century, when the city was known as **Alexandropol**,[^cite_note-4] it became the largest city of Russian-ruled [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) with a population above that of [Yerevan](34352.md). The city became renowned as a cultural hub, while also carrying significance as a major center of Russian troops during Russo-Turkish wars of the 19th century.

The city underwent a tumultuous period during and after [World War I](4764461.md). While Russian forces withdrew from the [South Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caucasus) due to the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution), the city became host to large numbers of Armenian refugees fleeing the [Armenian genocide](140376.md), in particular hosting 22,000 orphaned children in around 170 orphanage buildings. It was renamed **Leninakan**[^cite_note-5] during the Soviet period and became a major industrial and textile center in [Soviet Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Armenia).[^cite_note-History-6] The city's population rapidly grew to above 200,000 prior to the [1988 Spitak earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Armenian_earthquake), when it was devastated, with the city's population being reduced to 121,976 as of the 2011 census. The city was renamed Gyumri under modern independent Armenia soon after the breakup of the Soviet Union, and post-earthquake reconstruction efforts continue. As of the 2022 census, the population of the city was 112,301.[^cite_note-2022_Census-1]

Today, Gyumri continues to grapple with the lasting effects of the 1988 earthquake, but remains known as the cultural hub of Armenia due to the many artists and craftsman who originated from the city. The city is also notable for the large [Kumayri historic district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumayri_historic_district) that managed to mostly survive the 1988 earthquake, dating back mainly to the 19th century and being one of the few surviving places in the world with authentic urban [Armenian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_architecture).[^cite_note-History-6]

## Name

The area of modern-day Gyumri was originally known as **Kumayri** ([Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Կումայրի) during the period of the Kingdom of [Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu). Over time, the name became disrupted through phonetic changes to Kumri, then Gumri, and finally **Gyumri**. In 1837, Gyumri was renamed **Alexandropol** after Czar Nicholas I's wife, Princess [Alexandra Fyodorovna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Fyodorovna_\(Charlotte_of_Prussia\)). Between 1924 and 1990, the city was known as **Leninakan** in honor of [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin). Following independence, the original name **Kumayri** was used until 1992, when **Gyumri** was chosen as the name of the city.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-History-6]

Archaeological excavations conducted throughout the Soviet period have shown that the area of modern-day Gyumri has been populated since at least the third millennium BC. The area was mentioned as **Kumayri** in the historic [Urartian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) inscriptions dating back to the 8th century BC.[^cite_note-8] Kumayri may be identical with the city of *Gymnias* or *Gumnias* (Γυμνιάς in [greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language)) mentioned by [Xenophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophon) in his [*Anabasis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabasis_\(Xenophon\)).[^cite_note-9]

At the decline of the [Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) Kingdom by the second half of the 6th century BC, Kumayri became part of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire). The remains of a royal settlement found just to the south of Gyumri near the village of [Beniamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beniamin) dating back to the 5th to 2nd centuries BC, are a great example of the Achemenid influence in the region. However, at the beginning of the 5th century BC, Kumayri became part of the [Satrapy of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrapy_of_Armenia) under the rule of the [Orontids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontid_Dynasty). An alternative theory suggests that Kumayri has been formed as an urban settlement in the late 5th century BC, ca. 401 BC, by [Greek](42056.md) colonists.[^cite_note-10]

Later in 331 BC, the entire territory was included in the [Ayrarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrarat) province of [Ancient Armenian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)) as part of the Shirak canton. Between 190 BC and 1 AD Kumayri was under the rule of the [Artaxiad dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artaxiad_dynasty) of Armenia. During the 1st century AD, Shirak was granted to the [Kamsarakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamsarakan) family, who ruled over Kumayri during the [Arsacid Kingdom of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia).[^cite_note-11]

### Medieval period

Following the partition of Armenia in 387 between the Byzantines and the Persians, and as a result of the fall of the Arsacid Kingdom of Armenia in 428, Shirak including Kumayri became part of the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) of Persia. In 658 AD, at the height of the Arab Islamic invasions, Kumayri was conquered during the [Muslim conquest of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia) to become part of the [Emirate of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arminiya) under the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate).[^cite_note-12]

Kumayri was a significant and quite-developed urban settlement during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). According to the Armenian scholar *Ghevond the Historian*, the town was a center of the Armenian rebellion led by *Artavazd Mamikonian* against the Islamic Arab [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate), between 733 and 755. After 2 centuries of Islamic rule over Armenia, the [Bagratids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratid_dynasty_of_Armenia) declared independence in 885 establishing the [Bagratid Kingdom of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratid_Kingdom_of_Armenia).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Kumayri entered e new era of growth and progress, particularly when the nearby city of Ani became the capital of the kingdom in 961. By the second half of the 10th century, Kumayri was under the influence of the Armenian [Pahlavuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavuni) family, who were descendants of the Kamsarakans. The Pahlavunis had a great contribution in the progress of Shirak with the foundation of many fortresses, monastic complexes, educational institutions, etc.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]



[Marmashen Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmashen_Monastery) 6 kilometres (3.7 miles) northwest of Gyumri (10th century)

After the fall of Armenia to the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) in 1045 and later to the [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire) invaders in 1064. Under the foreign rulers, the town had gradually lost its significance during the following centuries, until the establishment of the [Zakarid Principality of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarid_Armenia) in 1201 under the [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Georgia) protectorate. During the Zakarid rule, the Eastern Armenian territories, mainly Lori and Shirak, entered into a new period of growth and stability, becoming a trade center between the east and the west. After the Mongols captured [Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani) in 1236, Armenia turned into a [Mongol protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Armenia) as part of the [Ilkhanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate), and the Zakarids became vassals to the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire). After the fall of the Ilkhanate in the mid-14th century, the Zakarid princes ruled over Lori, Shirak, and Ararat plain until 1360 when they fell to the invading Turkic tribes.[^cite_note-The_Turco-Mongol_Invasions-17]

By the last quarter of the 14th century, the [Ag Qoyunlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ag_Qoyunlu) Sunni [Oghuz Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oghuz_Turks) tribe took over Armenia, including Shirak. In 1400, [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) invaded Armenia and Georgia, and captured more than 60,000 of the survived local people as slaves. Many districts including Shirak were depopulated.[^cite_note-The_Turco-Mongol_Invasions-17] In 1410, Armenia fell under the control of the [Kara Koyunlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Koyunlu) Shia Oghuz Turkic tribe. According to the Armenian historian [Thomas of Metsoph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_of_Metsoph), although the Kara Koyunlu levied heavy taxes against the Armenians, the early years of their rule were relatively peaceful and some reconstruction of towns took place.[^cite_note-18]

### Persian and Russian rules

Main article: [Kumayri historic district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumayri_historic_district)







Saint Alexandra the Martyr's Russian Orthodox church, built in 1837–42

In 1501, most of the [Eastern Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) territories including Kumayri were conquered by the emerging [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) of Iran led by Shah [Ismail I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I).[^cite_note-19] Soon after in 1502, Kumayri became part of the newly formed *Erivan Beglarbegi*, a new administrative territory of Iran formed by the Safavids. During the first half of the 18th century, Kumayri became part of the [Erivan Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erivan_Khanate) under the rule of the [Afsharid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_dynasty) and later under the [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) of Persia.



[Diorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diorama) of old Alexandropol with the [Holy Saviour's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Saviour's_Church,_Gyumri) (1859–1873)

In June 1804, the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) forces controlled over Shirak region at the beginning of the [Russo-Persian War of 1804 and 1813](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1804%E2%80%931813\)). Kumayri became officially part of the Russian Empire at the [Treaty of Gulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gulistan) signed on 1 January 1813 between Imperial Russia and Qajar Persia.

During the period of Russian rule, Gyumri became one of the developing cities in the [Transcaucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasus). In 1829, in the aftermath of the [Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War,_1828-1829), there was a big influx of Armenian population, as around 3,000 families who had migrated from territories in the Ottoman Empire, in particular from the towns of [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars), [Erzurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzurum), and [Doğubeyazıt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C4%9Fubayaz%C4%B1t)- settled in and around Gyumri. The Russian poet [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin) visited Gyumri during his journey to [Erzurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzurum) in 1829.



Surb Nshan Church of 1870

In 1837 Russian [Tsar Nicholas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_I_of_Russia) arrived in Gyumri and changed the name into **Alexandropol**. The name was chosen in honour of Tsar Nicholas I's wife, Princess [Charlotte of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Feodorovna_\(Charlotte_of_Prussia\)), who had changed her name to *Alexandra Fyodorovna* after converting to [Orthodox Christianity](10186.md).

A major [Russian](25391.md) fortress was built on the site in 1837. Alexandropol was finally formed as a town in 1840 to become the center of the newly established [Alexandropol Uyezd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandropol_Uyezd), experiencing rapid growth during its first decade. In 1849, the Alexandropol Uyezd became part of the [Erivan Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erivan_Governorate). The town was an important outpost for the Imperial Russian armed forces in the [Transcaucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasia) where their military barracks were established (e.g., at Poligons, Severski, Kazachi Post). The Russians built the [Sev Berd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sev_Berd) fortress at the western edge of the city during the 1830s in response to the [Russo-Turkish War of 1828–1829](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1828%E2%80%931829\)).

Alexandropol had been quickly transformed to become one of the major centers of the Russian troops during the [Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)). After the establishment of the railway station in 1899, Alexandropol witnessed significant growth and became the largest city in Eastern Armenia. By the end of the 19th century, Alexandropol was home to 430 shopping stores, several workshops, cultural institutions, a girl's gymnasium, a commercial school, a theater, and leather, bear, and soap enterprises.[^cite_note-20]

### Modern history



Alexandropol in 1901

In 1902, the first bank in the city was opened. Until the Sovietization of Armenia in 1920, Alexandropol had 31 manufacturing centers including beer, soap, textile, etc. After the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) of 1917 and the Russian withdrawal from the [South Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caucasus), the Ottoman forces launched a new offensive capturing the city of Alexandropol on 11 May 1918, during the [Caucasus Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Campaign) in [World War I](4764461.md). However, the Ottomans withdrew from the city on 24 December 1918, as stipulated by the [Armistice of Mudros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros). On 6 December 1918, the Armenian army reoccupied the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDer_Matossian202015-21]

The newly established [Republic of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) proclaimed on 28 May 1918, included the city of Alexandropol. On 10 May 1920, the local Bolshevik Armenians aided by the Muslim population, attempted a [coup d'état](25914154.md) in Alexandropol against the Dashnak government of Armenia. The [uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Uprising) was suppressed by the Armenian government on May 14 and its leaders were executed. However, during [another Turkish invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish-Armenian_War), Turkish troops attacked Alexandropol and occupied the city on 7 November 1920. Armenia was forced to sign the [Treaty of Alexandropol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alexandropol) on December 3 to stop the Turkish advance towards Yerevan, however a concurrent Soviet invasion led to the fall of the Armenian government on December 2. The Turkish forces withdrew from Alexandropol after the [Treaty of Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kars) was signed in October 1921 by the unrecognized Soviet and Turkish governments.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHovannisian1967198-22]



Rizhkov Street in central Gyumri

Under [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) rule, the name of the city was changed in 1924 to Leninakan after the deceased Soviet leader [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin). The city suffered an [earthquake in 1926](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Kars_earthquake), when many of its significant buildings were destroyed including the Greek church of Saint George.

Leninakan became a major industrial center in the [Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) and its second-largest city, after the capital Yerevan. The city suffered major damage during the [1988 Armenian earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Armenian_earthquake), which devastated many parts of the country. The earthquake occurred along a known thrust fault with a length of 60 kilometers (37 mi). Its [strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_and_dip) was parallel to the Caucasus range and dipped to the north-northeast. [Bruce Bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Bolt), a seismologist and a professor of earth and planetary science at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), walked the fault scarp in 1992 and found that the vertical displacement measured 1 m (3 ft 3 in) along most of the length with the southwest end reaching 1.6 m (5 ft 3 in).[^cite_note-bolt-23]

The earthquake had a disastrous impact on the city, as many buildings are still not recovered. As of 2014, according to some news websites, between 4,000 and 5,000 residents of Gyumri remain homeless, although there are no official figures provided by the local authorities of the city.[^cite_note-24]



Gyumri City Hall at Vardanants Square

At the time of the [breakup of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_the_Soviet_Union), the city was renamed *Kumayri* from 1990 until 1992 when it was finally given the name *Gyumri*. The [Russian 102nd Military Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_102nd_Military_Base) is located in the city.[^cite_note-25]

Gyumri was celebrated as the Capital of Culture of the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md) for 2013. Major events took place in the city on 30 June 2013.[^cite_note-aph_mayraqaxaq-26]

On 12 January 2015, Valery Permyakov, a serviceman from the Russian 102nd Military Base, [murdered seven members of an Armenian family in Gyumri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Gyumri_massacre).[^cite_note-iwpr-27]

On 25 June 2016, [Pope Francis](1687680.md) delivered a Holy Mass at Gyumri's [Vartanants Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vartanants_Square). Catholicos of All Armenians [Garegin II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garegin_II) also took part in the ceremony.[^cite_note-28]

## Geography and climate



Gyumri with [Mount Aragats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Aragats) in the background

Gyumri is 126 kilometres (78 miles) north of the capital [Yerevan](34352.md) at the central part of the [Shirak plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirak_Plain). It has an approximate height of 1,550 metres (5,090 feet) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level), the high altitude line being 1,500 metres (4,900 feet). The [Akhurian River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhurian_River) passes through the western suburbs. The Shirak plateau is surrounded with the [Pambak Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pambak_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1) from the east and [Aragats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Aragats) volcanic range from the south. The city of Gyumri is 196 kilometres (122 miles) away from the [Black Sea](3386.md). The surrounding lands near the city are rich in [tufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufa), [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) and [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay).

Gyumri has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm-summer_humid_continental) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfb*), characterized by freezing and snowy winters and warm summers. The annual precipitation averages 510 millimetres (20 inches).

### Religion

[Cathedral of the Holy Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Holy_Martyrs,_Gyumri)

The majority of the population in Gyumri belongs to the [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church). The [Cathedral of the Holy Mother of God of Gyumri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Holy_Mother_of_God,_Gyumri)—also known as the Cathedral of the Seven Wounds of the Holy Mother of God—is the seat of the [Diocese of Shirak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Shirak) of the Armenian Church.[^cite_note-42]

The [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church), a minority in Armenia, is under the jurisdiction of the [Ordinariate of Armenia, Georgia, Russia, and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinariate_for_the_faithful_of_eastern_rite), based in the [Cathedral of the Holy Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Holy_Martyrs,_Gyumri) in Gyumri.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] As a consequence, the aid organisation of the Armenian Catholic Church, [Armenian Caritas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Caritas), is also headquartered in Gyumri. There are around 16,000 Armenian Catholics in the [Shirak Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirak_Province).

The presence of the small Russian Orthodox community along with the Russian military base personnel is marked with the [Saint Alexandra the Martyr's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Alexandra_the_Martyr's_Church?action=edit\&redlink=1) (within the Russian base), the Church of Saint Michael the Archangel and the Church of Saint Arsenije.[^cite_note-45]

However, many historic churches in Gyumri were either ruined or destroyed, including:

- Dprevank Monastery and the Basilic Church of old Kumayri dating back to the 7th century: it was the first ever church built in old Kumayri. However, the monastic complex was completely destroyed in 1852, during the construction of Russian military barracks.

- Saint George Greek Orthodox Church, opened in 1850. It was completely destroyed in 1933–34.

- Holy Mother of God Armenian Catholic Church, built between 1849 and 1854. Although standing, the building was turned into a private residence during the Soviet period.

- Russian church of the Seversky 18th Dragoon Regiment, built in 1856. It was consecrated in 1901 and destroyed during the Soviet period.

- Russian church of the Caucasian 7th Rifle Regiment, built during the 1850s. It was completely destroyed during the Soviet period.

- Russian church of the Caucasian 8th Rifle Regiment, built during the 1850s. It was completely destroyed during the Soviet period.

- Russian church of the Baku 154th Infantry Regiment, built during the 1850s. It was completely destroyed during the Soviet period.

As of 2017, Gyumri is home to the following church buildings:[^cite_note-46]

- [Saint Alexandra the Martyr's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Alexandra_the_Martyr's_Church?action=edit\&redlink=1) within the complex of the Russian military base, built in 1837–42. It was completely renovated and reopened on May 8, 2008.

- [Church of the Holy Saviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Saviour,_Gyumri) or Surp Amenaprkich, constructed between 1859–1873: designed to resemble the [Cathedral of Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Ani). The church was heavily damaged by the 1988 Spitak earthquake and is currently under reconstruction.

- Surp Nshan or Holy Sign Church: Opened in 1870.

- Saint Gregory the Illuminator's Church, built between 1875 and 1880.

- Saint Michael the Archangel Russian Orthodox Church, locally known as *Plplan Zham* (the Shimmering Chapel), built between 1875 and 1880.

- [Cathedral of the Holy Mother of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Holy_Mother_of_God,_Gyumri): also known as Seven Wounds of the Holy Mother of God, constructed between 1873–1884. Currently, it is the seat of the [Diocese of Shirak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Shirak) of the Armenian Apostolic Church.

- Saint Arseny Russian Orthodox Church, built during the 1870s and opened in 1910. It is locally known as the church of Kazachi Post.

- Saint Hripsime Chapel, opened in 1992.

- Saint Jacob of Nisibis Church: or Surp Hakob Mtsbinetsi Church, opened in 2005.

- Surp Sarkis Chapel, built in 2008 and opened in 2011.

- Surp Minas Chapel, opened in 2013.

- [Cathedral of the Holy Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Holy_Martyrs,_Gyumri) of the Catholic Armenians, opened in 2015.

## Culture

### Museums and art



Aslamazyan Sisters House-Museum



[Hovhannes Shiraz House-Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovhannes_Shiraz_House_Museum)

Gyumri is home to many prominent museums of Armenia, including the House-Museums of sculptor [Sergey Merkurov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Merkurov), poets [Avetik Isahakyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avetik_Isahakyan) and [Hovhannes Shiraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovhannes_Shiraz), and actor [Mher Mkrtchyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frunzik_Mkrtchyan). The [Aslamazyan Sisters Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallery_of_Mariam_and_Eranuhi_Aslamazyan_Sisters), built in the 1880s, is home to more than 700 drawings, paintings and other works of the Aslamazyan sisters who were the Soviet-era artists. The [Dzitoghtsyan Museum of Social Life and National Architecture of Gyumri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzitoghtsyan_Museum_of_National_Architecture) is an old mansion, housing collections related to both history and the everyday-life of Gyumri, as well as paintings and other works of art.

Throughout centuries, Kumayri-Gyumri was labelled as the "city of crafts and arts",[^cite_note-Reporter-47] being famous for its schools, theaters and [gusans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusans).



"October" cinema hall

In 1865, an amateur theatre group in Gyumri performed H. Karinyan's "Shushanik". In 1912, Gyumri was home to the first opera show ever staged in Armenia, when composer [Armen Tigranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armen_Tigranian) presented [Anoush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anoush_\(opera\)) to the public in Alexandropol. In 1923, the first Armenian opera theatre was opened in Gyumri (where the first [ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet) performance in Armenia took place in 1924[^cite_note-48]), while the Vardan Ajemian State Drama Theatre was founded in 1928. Prominent directors [Ruben Simonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruben_Simonov) and [Vardan Ajemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vardan_Ajemian), actors [Mher Mkrtchyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frunzik_Mkrtchyan), [Azat Sherents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azat_Sherents) and [Varduhi Varderesyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varduhi_Varderesyan) worked in theatre. The theatre's new building was opened in 1972. The artistic director is [Nikolay Tsaturyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Tsaturyan). Gyumri is known for its 19th-century architecture and [urban constructions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning).[^cite_note-Reporter-47]

The first printing house of Gyumri was founded in 1876 by G. Sanoyan and operated until 1918. It published literary works (including [Avetik Isahakyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avetik_Isahakyan)'s first book), calendars, textbooks. Another printing house, Ayg (founded 1892), published historical books and the first periodical of Gyumri, *Akhuryan*.[^cite_note-49]

In 1987, several artists — including Arman Grigoryan, Sev (Hendrik Khachatryan), Karen Andreasyan and others — set up an exhibition in the Artists' Union of Armenia's building, in the conference hall on the third floor.[^cite_note-50] The resulting *Third Floor* ([Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Երորդ Հարկ) modern art group has been described as "[anti-Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Soviet), [anti-Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Establishment) and in some ways anti-art, a clever appropriation of and comment on several movements, including [performance art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_art) and American [Pop Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_Art)."[^cite_note-51] By 1994, the group dispersed as its [utopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopia) mood faltered in the wake of post-[independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Armenia\)) [hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation) and [power outages](296636.md).[^cite_note-52]

Gyumri is home to the Gyumri Biennial, organized by the artist Azat Sargsyan and the Gyumri Center of Contemporary Art (GCCA).[^cite_note-53] Gyumri was officially declared [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md) cultural capital in 2013.[^cite_note-54]

### Music



A statue of two *[gusans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusans)* in Gyumri, depicting [Sheram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheram) and [Jivani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jivani)

The city of Gyumri has a great contribution in [Armenian folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_folk_music). Throughout the 19th century, Alexandrapol was considered the center of folk and traditional Armenian music. The musical culture of Alexandrapol has greatly influenced the art of [Jivani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jivani), who is considered the founder of modern Armenian folk music during the 19th century. Another 19th-century [ashik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashik) [Sheram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheram) who was born in Alexandropol, is one of the earliest [gusans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusans) of traditional Armenian music in the modern history of Armenia. He is one of the most celebrated Armenian composers of folk music.

The mystic philosopher of Alexandropol [George Gurdjieff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gurdjieff) has produced many influential works of music during the 20th century.

Different genres of music became popular in the city during the 2nd half of the 20th century. [Rock](25423.md), [folk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_rock) and [ethnic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_rock) are widely popular through the local famous rock band [Bambir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambir), active since 1978.[^cite_note-55]

In 1986, the [Gyumri State Orchestra of Folk Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_State_Orchestra_of_Folk_Instruments?action=edit\&redlink=1) was founded, followed by the [Gyumri State Symphonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_State_Symphonic_Orchestra?action=edit\&redlink=1) founded in 1993. In 1997, the [KOHAR Symphony Orchestra and Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOHAR_Symphony_Orchestra_and_Choir) was founded in Gyumri through the efforts of the Lebanese-Armenian philanthropist Harout Khatchadourian. Soon after, KOHAR became one of the most celebrated choirs in Armenia as well as throughout the [Armenian diaspora](214413.md).[^cite_note-56]

Influenced by Gurdjieff, the Armenian musician [Levon Eskenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Eskenian?action=edit\&redlink=1) founded [The Gurdjieff Ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gurdjieff_Ensemble) in 2008. The award-winning ensemble gathers many of Armenia's leading practitioners of traditional music, performing on [duduk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duduk), [sring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sring), [kamancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamancha), [oud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oud), [kanōn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monochord), [santur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santur), [tar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tar_\(string_instrument\)), [saz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%9Flama), [daf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daf), [dhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhol), and [tombak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tombak).[^cite_note-57]

The *Renaissance* international music festival of Gyumri is held annually since 2009.

In 2011 [WhoCares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhoCares), a [supergroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergroup_\(music\)) formed by [Ian Gillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Gillan) and [Tony Iommi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Iommi) with the participation of a great number of rock artists, raised money to build the "Octet" music school in Gyumri (opened two years later).[^cite_note-58]

### Local customs



Rentable horse-drawn [carriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carriage) at the center of Gyumri

The residents are Gyumri are widely known as conservative people. Traditions and local customs are widely preserved by the local citizens. It is very common among Armenians to refer to the *dignity of Gyumri* ([Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Գյումրվա թասիբ *Gyumrva tasib*).[^cite_note-65]

Gyumri is considered to be the "laughter and humor capital" of Armenia.[^cite_note-66] The jokes and anecdotes of local humorists like *Jgher Khachik* and *[Poloz Mukuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poloz_Mukuch)* are widely known by the local citizens. Many works have been published to narrate about the legacy and heritage of the humor in Gyumri.[^cite_note-67]

The city celebrates the "Gyumri Day" annually on the first Sunday of October. It is marked with many cultural and entertainment activities.

## Transportation

### Air transportation



[Shirak Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirak_Airport)

Gyumri is served by the international [Shirak Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirak_Airport), about 5 kilometres (3.1 miles) to the southeast of the city center. It was inaugurated in 1961 and is the second largest airport in Armenia. At the beginning of 2006, the government of Armenia felt the importance of having a second international airport, when adverse weather conditions meant that many flights had to be diverted from Yerevan's [Zvartnots International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvartnots_International_Airport) into Gyumri's Shirak Airport. New air traffic control equipment allowed airport workers to identify planes in a 400-kilometre (250-mile) radius.[^cite_note-68]

Following moderate use in 2005 and 2006 during which annual passenger traffic was at about 46,000 and several hundred aircraft movements took place each year, the airport's activity quickly declined again to the point where in 2016 passenger traffic amounted to only 12,421 and a mere 54 aircraft movements took place. However, in the beginning of 2017, as part of new efforts to develop Gyumri and its tourism industry, the government focused on revitalizing the airport. Multiple new airlines began operating flights to the airport, including [Taron Avia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taron_Avia), a new Armenian airline based in Gyumri. In order attract more customers, the Ministry of Nature Protection made meteorological services free for all airlines flying to Gyumri, lowering ticket costs.[^cite_note-69] The [Gyumri Technology Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_Technology_Center) also participated in helping revitalize the airport by adding interior design details to improve the airport's look.[^cite_note-70]

### Railway



[Gyumri Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_Railway_Station)

The railway junction of Gyumri is the oldest and the largest one in Armenia. It was formed in 1897 and the first railway link to Alexandropol that connected the city with Tiflis was completed in 1899. The rail line was then extended from Alexandropol to Yerevan (in 1902), Kars (in 1902), [Jolfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolfa,_Iran_\(city\)) (in 1906), and [Tabriz](439791.md). As a result, Alexandropol became an important rail hub.

As of 2017, the [Gyumri Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_Railway_Station) operates regular trips to Yerevan and [Batumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batumi). The South Caucasus Railway CJSC, is the current operator of the railway sector in Armenia.[^cite_note-71]

### Public vans and taxis

Public transport is dominated by the private sector in Gyumri. Public transit is mainly served by public vans, locally-known as *[marshrutka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshrutka)*. Most of the *marshrutkas* Russian-made [GAZelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAZelle) vans with 13 seats that operate with certain routes and stops. As of 2017, the one-way trip fee is AMD 100 (around US\$0.25). Passengers need to pay the money directly to the driver when getting in the vehicle, with no established ticketing system.

The central station of the city serves as bus terminal for inter-city transport, serving outbound routes towards other major cities and towns in Armenia, as well as cities in [Georgia](48768.md). The M-7 Motorway passes across the Shirak Province from east to west, connecting the city of Gyumri with the rest of Armenia.

Armenia is among the top 10 safest countries where one can wander around and go home alone safely at night. Taxis are available in the city at any time of the day or night.[^cite_note-72]

## Economy



The old brewery in Gyumri, opened in 1898

During the pre-Soviet era, Alexandropol was considered the third-largest trade and cultural center in Transcaucasia after [Tiflis](42298.md) and [Baku](4566.md) ([Yerevan](34352.md) would not rise to prominence until being proclaimed as the capital of independent [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) in 1918 and the [Armenian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_SSR) in 1920).[^cite_note-73] At the end of the 19th century, the population of Alexandropol had grown to 32,100 inhabitants, with a majority of Armenians.



[Poloz Mukuch Beerhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poloz_Mukuch_Beerhouse) at Jivani Street

The economy of Gyumri is mainly based on industry and construction. However, tourism and banking services are also among the developed sectors in the city.

The industrial sector in the provincial center Gyumri includes the production of building materials (tufa and basalt), hosiery and textile manufacturing, food processing and dairy products, alcoholic drinks, electronic machines, etc. The largest industrial plant in Gyumri is the *Gyumri-Beer* Brewery opened in 1972. The factory produces a variety of [lager beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lager_beer) under the brands *Gyumri*, *Ararat* and *Aleksandrapol*.[^cite_note-74] The city is also home to the "Factory of Bending Machinses" opened in 1912, the "Arshaluys" hosiery manufacturing enterprise established in 1926, the "Karhat" machine tools plant opened in 1959, the "Chap Chemical LLC" since 1999, the "Armtex Group" clothing factory since 2000, and the "Lentex" hosiery manufacturing plant is operating since 2001. Other industrial firms of the city include the "Aleqpol" factory for dairy products, the "Anusharan" confectionery plant, and the "Gold Plast" plant for building materials.

The nearby village of [Akhuryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhuryan) is home to the "Lusastgh-Sugar" factory (opened in 2010), the largest sugar producers in the Southern Caucasus region.

## Education



Progress [Gyumri University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_University?action=edit\&redlink=1) at the [Independence Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Square,_Gyumri)



[Gyumri Technology Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_Technology_Center)

Gyumri has a large number of educational institutions, following the capital Yerevan in the number of educational institutions. It is considered the cultural and educational center of northern Armenia.

As of 2017, Gyumri is home to the following higher educational centers:

- [Shirak State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirak_State_University) named after [Mikael Nalbandian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Nalbandian), opened in 1934 and is currently home to 7 faculties.

- Gyumri campus of the [National Polytechnic University of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Polytechnic_University_of_Armenia), operating since 1959 with 2 faculties:

  [^cite_note-75]

  - Faculty of Technologies and sectoral economics,

  - Faculty of Natural sciences and communication systems.

- Gyumri campus of [Komitas State Conservatory of Yerevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komitas_State_Conservatory_of_Yerevan), operating since 1988.

- Progress Gyumri University, opened in 1990.

- Shirakatsy campus of [Haybusak University of Yerevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haybusak_University_of_Yerevan), operating since 1991.

- Imastaser Anania Shirakatsi University, opened in 1992.

- Gyumri campus of [Armenian State University of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_State_University_of_Economics), operating since 1997.

- Gyumri campus of [Yerevan State Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan_State_Academy_of_Fine_Arts), operating since 1997.

- Gyumri campus of [Yerevan State Institute of Theatre and Cinematography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan_State_Institute_of_Theatre_and_Cinematography), operating since 1997.

In 2014, the [Gyumri Technology Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_Technology_Center) was opened in the city, in an attempt to turn Gyumri into a regional and international center of information and high technologies.[^cite_note-76]

The Gyumri branch of [Tumo Center for Creative Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumo_Center_for_Creative_Technologies) was opened in May 2015, following a fundraising initiative by [Shant TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shant_TV).[^cite_note-77] After three years of renovations, the center was moved to its permanent location, the historic theater building of Gyumri, in 2020.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

As of 2017, the city is home to 47 public education schools, 23 pre-school kindergartens, as well as 7 special schools for music.

## Sport

### Football



[Gyumri City Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_City_Stadium)

Gyumri is home to the Armenian [football](10568.md) club [FC Shirak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shirak). They play their home games at the [Gyumri City Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_City_Stadium), the oldest football stadium in Armenia dating back to 1924. Shirak are one of the most popular football teams in Armenia, having won the championship of the [Armenian Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Premier_League) four times, with the most recent one in the [2012–13 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Armenian_Premier_League). Shirak have also won the [Armenian Independence Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Independence_Cup) once. The native of Gyumri and former Shirak player [Artur Petrosyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Petrosyan) was the all-time leading scorer for the [Armenia national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_national_football_team) until his record was surpassed by [Henrikh Mkhitaryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrikh_Mkhitaryan) in 2013.

[Aragats FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragats_FC) was the second football club that represented the city. However, the club was dissolved in 2002 due to financial difficulties.&#x20;



[Gyumri Football Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_Football_Academy)

The [Gyumri Football Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri_Football_Academy) of the [Football Federation of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Armenia) was opened on 13 September 2014. It is home to four natural-grass and two artificial turf regular-sized football training pitches.[^cite_note-80]

### Futsal

Futsal is also very popular in Gyumri. Being one of the most successful Futsal teams in Armenia, FC Gyumri played at the [Armenian Futsal Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Futsal_Premier_League). Starting from 2017–18 season, FC Gyumri became known as *Shirak Futsal*, representing the futsal branch of [FC Shirak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shirak). The newly founded *Sh.S.U. Futsal* -representing the [Shirak State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirak_State_University)- will also participate in the domestic league competition. The futsal teams of Gyumri regularly play their home games at the Armen Sargsyan Sports Hall, located in the Ani district at the northwest of the city.

### Olympic individual sports

Gyumri has made a major contribution to the sporting life of Armenia. Many Olympic and world champion wrestlers, weightlifters and boxers are from Gyumri. The city is notable for its worldwide champions in individual sports, such as [Robert Emmiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Emmiyan) in long jump, [Yurik Vardanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yurik_Vardanyan) and [Nazik Avdalyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazik_Avdalyan) in weightlifting and [Artur Aleksanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Aleksanyan) in [Greco-Roman wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_wrestling).

Many special sport schools are serving the young generation of Gyumri such as the Robert Emmiyan school of athletics, Levon Ishtoyan football school, Tigran Petrosian school of chess, Ludvig Mnatsakanyan school of winter sports, Artur Aleksanyan school of wrestling, Yurik Vardanyan school of weightlifting, Aleksan Haobyan school of tennis and table tennis, as well as other special schools for boxing, artistic gymnastics, sambo-judo, fencing, and chess. The city is also home to the Gyumri Swimming Complex. The National Federation of Black Belts of [Aikido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aikido) (NFBBA) is based in Gyumri since its establishment in 2012.

The Gyumri State Sports College of Olympic Reserve and Gyumri School of Sport Masters are among the prominent sport schools in Armenia that produced many champions in several individual sports.
