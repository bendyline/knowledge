**Jakarta**,[^cite_note-9] officially the **Special Capital Region of Jakarta**,[^cite_note-10] is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_Indonesia) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indonesian_cities_by_population) of [Indonesia](14579.md), with administrative status equivalent to a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia). It lies on the northwestern coast of [Java](69336.md), borders the provinces of [West Java](314356.md) and [Banten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banten), and faces the [Java Sea](467143.md) to the north. Jakarta itself covers about 662 square kilometres (256 square miles), but the wider [Jakarta metropolitan area](1340014.md)—locally known as *Jabodetabek*—is among the largest urban agglomerations in the world by area. By population, the metro area is [the most populous urban area in the world](14649921.md) with a population of over 40 million. Jakarta is Indonesia's political, economic, and cultural centre and contains many national institutions, corporate headquarters, and the secretariat of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations ([ASEAN](106539.md)).

The area that is now Jakarta has been inhabited since at least the early centuries of the Common Era and was long associated with [Sunda Kelapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Kelapa), the port of the [Sunda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Kingdom). In 1527, the settlement was renamed Jayakarta after being captured by forces of the [Demak Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demak_Sultanate). The [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) (VOC) seized the city in 1619 and rebuilt it as [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies), which served as the centre of [VOC power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_the_Dutch_East_Indies) and subsequently of [Dutch colonial rule](23476997.md) in the Indonesian archipelago for more than three centuries. After the [Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies) during the Second World War and [Indonesia's declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence) in 1945, the city took the name Jakarta and became the capital of the new republic.

The [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network) classifies Jakarta as an alpha world city. It is Indonesia's main financial and commercial centre and [one of Southeast Asia's largest urban economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ASEAN_country_subdivisions_by_GDP). Its economy is concentrated in finance, trade, business services, media, and international diplomacy. Rapid urbanisation since the mid-20th century expanded Jakarta into a metropolitan region and drew migrants from across Indonesia.

Jakarta has no ethnic majority. Its population includes large communities of [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Betawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_people), [Sundanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundanese_people), [Chinese Indonesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians), and migrants from many other parts of Indonesia. [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) is the official language and the main language of public life, while Betawi culture grew out of the mixing of local, Chinese, Indian, Arab, and European influences during the colonial period. Jakarta has persistent urban problems, including traffic congestion, air pollution, flooding, and land subsidence, which helped prompt the national government's decision to [relocate Indonesia's future capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_Indonesia#Relocation_to_Nusantara) to [Nusantara](69803286.md) in [East Kalimantan](453651.md).

## Etymology

The area now known as Jakarta has had several names. During the period of the [Sunda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Kingdom), its harbour was known as *Kalapa* or *Sunda Kalapa*, one of the kingdom's principal ports on the north coast of western Java.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom20172–3-11] A 16th-century account by Portuguese apothecary [Tomé Pires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A9_Pires) referred to the harbour as *Calapa*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPires1990172-12]

The name *Jayakarta* is traditionally linked to the conquest of Kalapa in 1527 by Islamic forces said to have been led by [Fatahillah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatahillah), but surviving records do not confirm that he renamed the settlement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom20173-2] The name is of [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) roots and translates to "victory" or "victorious deed". Early European sources recorded related forms including *Iacarta*, *Xacatra*, and *Jacatra*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom201717-13][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPires1990172-12][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"The_capital's_'childhood'_names"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2019-14]

After the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) (VOC) seized and destroyed Jayakarta in 1619, it rebuilt the settlement as *[Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies)*, named for the [Batavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavi_\(Germanic_tribe\)), a Germanic people regarded by the Dutch as their ancestors.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom201718-15] Batavia remained the city's name until the Japanese occupation in 1942, when the authorities renamed it Jakarta and used the designation *Jakaruta Tokubetsu-shi* ([Japanese](15606.md): ジャカルタ特別市, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Jakarta Special Municipality').[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom201720–21-16][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"The_capital's_'childhood'_names"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2019-14] Following Indonesian independence, Jakarta became the city's formal name.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"The_capital's_'childhood'_names"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2019-14]

### Early settlements and Sunda Kelapa

Further information: [Sunda Kelapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Kelapa)

Tall, weathered rectangular stone monument displayed upright in a museum, with historical exhibits visible behind it.

[Luso-Sundanese *padrão*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luso-Sundanese_padr%C3%A3o), a monument commemorating a treaty between the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) and the [Sunda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Kingdom)

The [Buni culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buni_culture) was a prehistoric pottery culture found in the coastal areas of northern and western Java. It is dated to roughly 400 BC–AD 100, with evidence suggesting that it may have continued until about AD 500.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Batujaya_Temple_complex_listed_as_national_cultural_heritage"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2019-17] Written evidence from this period includes the mid-fifth-century [Tugu inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tugu_inscription), found near [Tanjung Priok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Priok) in present-day North Jakarta. The inscription mentions the *Candrabhaga* and *Gomati* waterways and mentions a King Purnawarman dugging the *Gomati* river. But it is unclear who was responsible for the *Candrabhaga* or how the two projects were related.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENoorduynVerstappen1972298-18]

By the 10th century, the [Sunda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Kingdom) had emerged in western Java and ruled the area around present-day Jakarta.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom201713-19] The Chinese work *[Chu-fan-chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Fan_Zhi)* describes *Sin-t'o* as having a harbour and producing pepper,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhao191170-20] with the work's translators [Friedrich Hirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hirth) and [William Woodville Rockhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Woodville_Rockhill) identifying the place meant as Sunda in western Java.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhao191171-21] By the early 16th century, Kalapa was one of the Sunda Kingdom's leading ports.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom201713-19] Tomé Pires described it as Sunda's most important trading port, drawing ships from Sumatra, Malacca, Java and elsewhere.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPires1990172-12]

In March 1513, the Portuguese captain at Malacca sent four ships to Java to obtain spices.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPires1990xxv-22] The Sunda Kingdom concluded a [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luso_Sundanese_padr%C3%A3o) with Portugal on 21 August 1522.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPires1990172-12] It allowed the Portuguese to build a fortress at Kalapa,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPires1990172-12] as Sunda sought Portuguese support amid pressure from the [sultanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate) of [Cirebon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Cirebon) and [Demak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demak_Sultanate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom201716-23][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReid2010433-24] In 1527, Islamic forces captured Kalapa, but sources differ over whether [Fatahillah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatahillah) or [Sunan Gunung Jati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunan_Gunungjati) led the conquest.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom201717-13][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReid2010433-24] After the conquest, Kalapa became a dependency of the [Banten Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banten_Sultanate) and was recorded in foreign sources as *Iacarta*, *Xacatra* or *Jacatra*. Banten later developed into a major maritime power in western Java.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGultom201717-13][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReid2010433-24]

### Batavia under Dutch rule

Further information: [Batavia, Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies)

Historical illustration of a waterfront gate beside a canal, with groups of people on the shore, small boats on the water, and a wooden bridge spanning the canal.

The Nieuwe Poort in Batavia, 1682

In the early 17th century, Jayakarta was under Banten's control and became an object of rivalry between [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) (VOC) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) (EIC) trading interests.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnörr201446,_124-25] Following a siege, Dutch naval officer [Jan Pieterszoon Coen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Pieterszoon_Coen) returned with reinforcements in May 1619, overran Jayakarta and burned the royal residence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlussé202518-26] The VOC established its Asian headquarters on the site and subsequently named the settlement Batavia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEvan_der_Brug200045-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndraprahastaDerudder20194-3][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlussé202518-26]

Batavia developed as a walled canal city on the low coastal plain at the mouth of the [Ciliwung river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciliwung).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEvan_der_Brug200045,_47-28] However, the canals flowed poorly and accumulated sediment, and rapid coastal silting contributed to unhealthy conditions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEvan_der_Brug200047-29] After the 1730s, Batavia became markedly less healthy, and wealthy residents increasingly moved away from the old town.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEvan_der_Brug200047–48-30]

Despite these health problems, Batavia remained the VOC's main administrative centre in Asia and an important trading port.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEvan_der_Brug200045-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndraprahastaDerudder20194-3] Chinese residents were involved in Batavia's trade, agriculture, crafts, and construction, and lived both within the city and in the surrounding countryside (*Ommelanden*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKusno2025296–299-31][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOktarinaKurniawan20214-32] In 1740, unrest among Chinese labourers in the *Ommelanden* and the VOC's response escalated into a [massacre of Chinese residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1740_Batavia_massacre) in Batavia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKusno2025300-33][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOktarinaKurniawan202112–13-34] After the massacre, Chinese settlement began settling outside the city walls. In March 1741, the VOC designated a Chinese quarter at *Diestpoort*, south of Batavia, in an area later known as [Glodok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glodok).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKusno2025300-33][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOktarinaKurniawan202113-35]

By the early 19th century, residents were moving away from the unhealthy old town, and the move to *Weltevreden* (present-day [Central Jakarta](2728760.md)) sped up Batavia's southward growth.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndraprahastaDerudder20194-3][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENasGrijns20007–8-36] *Weltevreden* developed around government buildings and spacious residences with gardens, while the lower old town remained a commercial district that included the Chinese quarter.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENasGrijns20008-37] Subsequent growth extended Batavia southward to [Menteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menteng) and incorporated *[Meester Cornelis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatinegara)* (present-day Jatinegara).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENasGrijns20008-37] In March 1942, Dutch forces [surrendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies_campaign#Surrender) Batavia to [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), and the [Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies) authorities renamed the city Jakarta.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"The_capital's_'childhood'_names"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2019-14][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndraprahastaDerudder20194-3]

### Jakarta in independent Indonesia

Black-and-white photograph of Sukarno in white clothing and a black peci, standing before a crowd and reading from a paper, with men flanking him.

[Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) reading the [Proclamation of Indonesian Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence) at Jalan Pegangsaan Timur No. 56, Menteng

Indonesia's independence was proclaimed in Jakarta on 17 August 1945.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERicklefs2001260-38] During the first months of the [Indonesian National Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution), authority in Jakarta was contested among Indonesian, Japanese and Dutch officials. British forces intervened to restore order and a Dutch political takeover was initially blocked by the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFormichi201282–83-39] As the British consolidated control of Jakarta, the Republican government moved to [Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogyakarta), which became its capital in early 1946.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFakih202347-40] Jakarta was made the capital again in 1950 after the federal [United States of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Indonesia) was replaced by the unitary republic of Indonesia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERicklefs2001285-41]

Under first President [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno), Jakarta became the focus of major nation-building projects, some of which were part of preparations for the [Asian Games in 1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Asian_Games). These projects included the [National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Monument_\(Indonesia\)), [Hotel Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Indonesia), [Sarinah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarinah), the [Senayan sports complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelora_Bung_Karno_Sports_Complex), and the [Thamrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalan_MH_Thamrin_\(Jakarta\))–Sudirman axis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPadawangi201410–11-42][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPrasetyoDanisworo2015205–206-43] In 1964, new legislation formally reaffirmed the Special Capital Region of Jakarta as Indonesia's capital.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Declaration_That_the_Special_Capital_Region_of_Greater_Jakarta_Shall_Remain_the_Capital_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia_under_the_Name_Jakarta"._Audit_Board_of_Indonesia,_1964-44]

The [violence and political upheaval of 1965–66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_to_the_New_Order) marked General [Suharto](217513.md)'s rise to power and the beginning of the [New Order](2864794.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECribb2002551-45] Under Governor [Ali Sadikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Sadikin) (1966–77), Jakarta expanded the *Kampung* Improvement Program to improve basic services in dense *[kampungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampong)*, but modernisation also displaced some poor neighbourhoods.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIrawaty201828–29,_41-46][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESimone201437–38-47] From the 1980s, deregulation and greater private and foreign investment accelerated high-rise construction along Thamrin-Sudirman and large industrial, commercial and residential projects on the metropolitan outskirts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPrasetyoDanisworo2015206–207-48]

The [Asian financial crisis in 1997–98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis) intensified [student protests and widespread violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_1998_Indonesia_riots) in Jakarta. After mounting political pressure, President [Suharto resigned on 21 May 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Suharto).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVickers2013209,_211–212-49] In the *[Reformasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_era_in_Indonesia)* era, decentralisation changed Jakarta's local governance, and residents directly [elected the governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Jakarta_gubernatorial_election) for the first time in 2007.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Jakarta_holds_historic_election"._BBC_News,_2007-50] In 2022, the central government laid the legal groundwork for moving the national capital from Jakarta to [Nusantara](69803286.md). The capital's legal functions remain in Jakarta until a presidential decree formally transfers them to Nusantara.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Law_Number_3_of_2022_concerning_the_National_Capital"._Audit_Board_of_Indonesia,_2022-51]

## Geography

A vast pale-gray urban area spreads inland from the north coast of Java, surrounded by greener rural land and mountains to the south, with the Java Sea at the top.

Satellite image of the [Jakarta metropolitan area](1340014.md) and the surrounding [western Java](314356.md), acquired by [Sentinel-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel-2).

Jakarta covers about 662 square kilometres (256 sq mi) of land and 6,977 square kilometres (2,694 sq mi) of sea area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotoBela_NegaraTasriKamil201820-52] Jakarta's urban area extends beyond the provincial boundary into the wider [metropolitan region](1340014.md), commonly called *Jabodetabek*, which includes surrounding cities and regencies in [West Java](314356.md) and [Banten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banten).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinezMasron20202-53][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFirman2014369-54] Daily commuting links Jakarta with surrounding municipalities, which are [Bogor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogor), [Depok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depok), [Tangerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangerang), and [Bekasi](1915107.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETjahjonoKusumaSeptiawan2020585-55]

The city lies on Java's northwestern coast at the mouth of the [Ciliwung river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciliwung), facing [Jakarta Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Bay).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinezMasron20202-53] Except for some hilly areas in the south, Jakarta spreads across low, flat terrain. Much of Jakarta lies on an alluvial plain, with elevations ranging from below sea level to about 50 metres (160 feet).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinezMasron20202-53][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotoBela_NegaraTasriKamil201820-52] Thirteen rivers flow through the city from the south toward Jakarta Bay, including the [Ciliwung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciliwung_River), [Angke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angke_River), [Sunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunter_River), and [Grogol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grogol_River) rivers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinezMasron20202-53][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotoBela_NegaraTasriKamil201820-52] The alluvial plain was historically swampy, and seasonal flooding is common,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinezMasron20202-53] which is caused by heavy rain and runoff from upstream areas. In addition, high tides, land subsidence, waste-clogged drainage and limited river capacity can worsen inundation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotoBela_NegaraTasriKamil201819–21-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOctaviantiCharles20181103-57][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPriyambodohoKureYagiJanuriyadi20211-58]

Jakarta's flood-control policy has long favoured engineered measures, including canals, dredging, pumping stations and coastal defences.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOctaviantiCharles20181102,_1107-59][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPadawangiDouglass2015517,_521,_528-60] Land subsidence is a major driver of coastal-flood risk in northern Jakarta.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBottSchöneIllignerHaghshenas_Haghighi20215–6-61] Groundwater extraction and the weight of buildings and infrastructure are among the human causes of subsidence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBottSchöneIllignerHaghshenas_Haghighi20213-62][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETakagiEstebanMikamiPratama20211-63] Beyond flooding, Jakarta also faces severe river-water and air pollution, with air pollution imposing substantial health burdens.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELuoKangApipZhou20191-64]

### Climate

A rainy city street lined with tall office buildings and trees, with cars, motorcycles, and a truck traveling on the wet road as city lights and vehicles reflect on the wet pavement.

[Drizzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drizzle) in Jakarta

Jakarta has a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) with distinct wet and dry seasons.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESiswantovan_Oldenborghvan_der_SchrierJilderda20163215–3216-65] Rainfall is much heavier from December to March, while June to September is relatively dry.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESiswantovan_Oldenborghvan_der_SchrierJilderda20163216-66] Flooding is common during the rainy season. Heavy rain and upstream runoff can overwhelm rivers and drains, while high tides, land subsidence and urban development can further increase flood risk.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPriyambodohoKureYagiJanuriyadi20213-67][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPadawangiDouglass2015523-68][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUNFCCC20233–4-69]

Jakarta remains warm throughout the year, with monthly means between 26 °C (78.8 °F) and 28 °C (82.4 °F) and the yearly average around 27 °C (80.6 °F).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKagabuDelinomLubisShimada201070-70] Average daily highs are about 32 °C (89.6 °F), while average daily lows are about 24.8 °C (76.6 °F).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESiswantoNuryantoFerdiansyahPrastiwi20233-71] Over the long term, Jakarta has also become warmer, with its annual mean temperature rising by about 1.6 °C (2.9 °F) per century between 1866 and 2012.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESiswantovan_Oldenborghvan_der_SchrierJilderda20163214-72]

## Cityscape

### Architecture

See also: [Colonial architecture in Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_architecture_in_Jakarta) and [List of tallest buildings in Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Jakarta)

Traditional Betawi wooden house with a deep shaded veranda, dark carved railings, and a steep red-tiled roof, beside a paved courtyard with tropical plants.

[Rumah Kebaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumah_Kebaya) built with [Betawi architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_people#Architecture) at [Taman Mini Indonesia Indah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taman_Mini_Indonesia_Indah)

Jakarta's built environment includes Betawi vernacular architecture, colonial-era buildings, post-independence monuments and modern high-rises. Betawi houses are rooted in Malay architectural traditions, with decorative influences from Chinese, Dutch, Portuguese and Arab cultures.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELakawa201586,_89-78] Their wide eaves, large openings, and open layouts are also suited to the tropical climate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHidayatSuhendar20181-79]

Jakarta's colonial architecture developed from the VOC-built core of Batavia through the 19th-century expansion to *Weltevreden* and further growth in the early 20th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKehoe20151,_4-80][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPasschier20066-81][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllisa201813,_16-82] By the mid-19th century, *Weltevreden* had become the colonial administrative centre, with government buildings, elite residences, churches, shops and hotels.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllisa201816,_19–20-83]

In the early 20th century, planned neighbourhoods such as [Menteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menteng) combined garden-city layouts with houses adapted to tropical conditions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPasschier200613–14-84] Menteng, developed in the 1910s, was planned as a healthy residential district for the middle class. The early houses combined climate-adapted features such as broad eaves and open ventilation with modernist and [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) design.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Building_on_the_Past"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2012-85]

After independence, Indonesia's first President [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) used major public works as part of nation-building, including the [National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Monument_\(Indonesia\)), the [Senayan sports complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelora_Bung_Karno_Stadium), and new major roads.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESopandi201723–24-86] The [national legislative complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPR/DPR/DPD_building) began as the [CONEFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONEFO) project in 1965. Its main Nusantara Building features a dome formed from two curved sections.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELuciusSantosaMartokusumoNugraha2024294–295-87] From the late 20th century onward, high-rise development increasingly altered Jakarta's skyline, especially the [Golden Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Triangle_of_Jakarta).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPuspitasariKwon20172-88]

### Parks and public spaces

Elevated covered walkway and vine-covered structures surrounded by dense green vegetation at Ragunan Zoo.

[Ragunan Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragunan_Zoo) in southern Jakarta, one of the city's major green and recreational areas.

Public parks and green open spaces occupy a limited share of Jakarta's land area. Green open space (*ruang terbuka hijau*, RTH) covered about 5.18% of Jakarta in 2023, well below the 30% minimum set by national spatial-planning law.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKusumaningtyas202497-89] Since 2015, the city has also developed child-friendly integrated public spaces (*ruang publik terpadu ramah anak*, RPTRA), neighbourhood facilities intended for play, social activity, and community use.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaragihToyyibahKurniawanKusuma20241–2-90]

[Merdeka Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merdeka_Square,_Jakarta) (*Medan Merdeka*) in Central Jakarta surrounds the [National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Monument_\(Indonesia\)) (Monas). Formerly the colonial *Koningsplein* (King's Square) in *Weltevreden*, it is now a government-managed public green space used for leisure, sport, and political activities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGamalRohmahLarasatiPiyaputri2024188,_193-91] Nearby [Lapangan Banteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapangan_Banteng), located beside [Istiqlal Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istiqlal_Mosque) and [Jakarta Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Cathedral), is a public-history and commemorative space that includes the [West Irian Liberation Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Irian_Liberation_Monument) and accommodates also everyday public use.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Lapangan_Banteng_gets_facelift"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2018-92][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaseliWipranataSantosoSuryadjaja20231814-93]

Other public spaces include [Suropati Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taman_Suropati) and [Menteng Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menteng_Park) in Menteng,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFitriantySantosaErnawati2025184-94] while [Kalijodo Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalijodo_Park) has been redeveloped as green and child-friendly public space.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESutantoJunadi20181-95] Larger recreational sites include [Ancol Dreamland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancol_Dreamland), [Ragunan Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragunan_Zoo), and [Taman Mini Indonesia Indah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taman_Mini_Indonesia_Indah), which was conceived as a cultural showcase of Indonesia's diversity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Recreational_Areas_and_Playgrounds"._Government_of_Jakarta,_2022-96][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHitchcock200545-97]

Migration has been an important source of Jakarta's population growth.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMulyana201220-103][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHadiTirtosudarmo201624-104] Jakarta experienced substantial migration after Indonesian independence in 1945 and has drawn people from across the Indonesian archipelago, including many seeking work.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMulyana201221-105][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHadiTirtosudarmo201623-106] In 1961, 51% of Jakarta's population had been born in the city, while 46.7% had been born in other Indonesian provinces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECastles1967172-107]

Recent population estimates for Jakarta differ because they use different definitions and geographic boundaries. In the first half of 2025, Jakarta's civil-registration records counted 11,010,514 residents registered at addresses in the province.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Understanding_the_Difference_in_Jakarta_Population_Data:_UN_Data_vs._Population_and_Civil_Registration_Office_Data"._Jakarta_Population_and_Civil_Registration_Office,_2025-5] The [United Nations](31769.md), using its Degree of Urbanization definition, estimated Jakarta's 2025 population at 41.9 million.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations_Department_of_Economic_and_Social_Affairs202533,_107-108] The population of the metropolitan area has grown from 11.4 million people in 1980,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERustiadiPravitasariSetiawanMulya20212-109] and urban growth has also continued into Jakarta's suburbs, including Bogor, Bekasi, and Tangerang.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERustiadiPravitasariSetiawanMulya20217-110]

### Ethnicity

Further information: [Betawi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_people)

In the [2010 Indonesian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Indonesian_census), no ethnic group made up a majority of Jakarta's Indonesian citizens. [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) were the largest group, followed by Betawi, [Sundanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundanese_people), and [Chinese Indonesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians). Batak, Minangkabau, Malay, and other groups were also recorded.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStatistics_Indonesia201236–41-111] Migration from other parts of Indonesia has contributed to Jakarta's ethnic composition.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHadiTirtosudarmo201627-112] Among the larger groups, Chinese Indonesians have a long established presence in Jakarta, dating back to colonial Batavia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOktarinaKurniawan20216-113]

The [Betawi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_people) are regarded as Jakarta's indigenous ethnic group, but the community itself formed in colonial Batavia from people of varied origins.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnörr201411-114] Early Batavia's population came from many parts of Asia and the Indonesian archipelago.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECastles1967155-115] Everyday contact across ethnic lines, including intermarriage, helped forge a shared Betawi identity. Islam was also an important bond across these groups.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnörr201453–57-116] Today, Betawi communities live in central and peripheral parts of Jakarta and in nearby Bekasi and Tangerang.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnörr201411-114]

### Language

See also: [Betawi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_language) and [Indonesian slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_slang)

Roadside sign amid trees reading "NYOK BARENG-BARENG KITE JAGE DAN KITE BANGUN JAKARTA TIMUR", with red-and-white Indonesian flag decorations below.

A sign encouraging development in East Jakarta written in the [Betawi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_language).

The [Indonesian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) is widely spoken across Jakarta, as it is the national and official language of Indonesia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"1945_Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia"._Ministry_of_Law_and_Human_Rights_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia,_2019-117] It is also a language of wider communication across ethnic groups.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaauw200912-118] The [Betawi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_language) embodies Jakarta's long history of contact among different cultural groups.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESiregarHamzah2024274-119] Betawi Malay and colloquial Jakartan Indonesian are distinct varieties. The latter has been strongly influenced by Betawi Malay and has spread beyond Jakarta.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESneddon20064–5-120]

Some Jakarta communities continue to use regional languages in family and social settings, including Toba Batak and Minangkabau.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESimanjuntakSusenoRamdhoniSutrisno2022109-121][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHastutiOswari2020248-122] Among young Chinese Indonesians surveyed in West and North Jakarta, everyday family language use has shifted toward Indonesian, while Chinese heritage languages are maintained unevenly across households.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYulianaYanti2023241,_251-123]

### Education

White colonial-era institutional building with tall shuttered windows and a broad tiled roof, partly framed by trees and flowering gardens.

The [University of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Indonesia)'s Faculty of Medicine occupies the Salemba building to which STOVIA moved in 1919.

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPols2024168-124]

Jakarta and its surrounding metropolitan area are home to several major public universities, including the [University of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Indonesia), the [State University of Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_Jakarta), and [Syarif Hidayatullah State Islamic University Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syarif_Hidayatullah_State_Islamic_University_Jakarta).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESoetjiptoSedaNoorWardani2014293-125] The city also has a large private higher-education sector,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStatistics_Indonesia_DKI_Jakarta2026205,_207-126] and the provincial government's list of international schools includes Jakarta Intercultural School and Australian Independent School.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDKI_Jakarta_Provincial_Government20244-127]

Formal medical training in Batavia began in 1851 with a two-year course preparing young Javanese men for vaccination work. The programme grew in length and scope over time. The [STOVIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOVIA) moved to Salemba, Weltevreden in 1919, and the higher-level Batavia Medical School began taking students in 1927.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPols2024163,_167,_174-128]

Jakarta is one of Indonesia's largest higher-education centres. In 2025, 241 institutions under the [Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Higher_Education,_Science,_and_Technology_\(Indonesia\)) enrolled 799,378 students.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStatistics_Indonesia_DKI_Jakarta2026205,_207-126] Under the primary and secondary education ministry, Jakarta had 2,223 primary, 1,074 junior secondary, 510 senior secondary, and 553 vocational schools in the same year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStatistics_Indonesia_DKI_Jakarta2026175-129] School participation, including non-formal equivalents, was nearly universal among 7–12- and 13–15-year-olds, but fell to just under nine in ten among 16–18-year-olds.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStatistics_Indonesia_DKI_Jakarta2026213-130] The Jakarta Smart Card (KJP Plus) programme is intended to help students from low-income families complete compulsory schooling.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"KJP_(Jakarta_Smart_Card)"._Government_of_Jakarta,_2023-131]

### Religion

Aerial view of Istiqlal Mosque, showing its large white dome, rectangular prayer hall, and tall minaret amid central Jakarta buildings.

[Istiqlal Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istiqlal_Mosque,_Jakarta) is the largest [mosque](19894.md) in Southeast Asia.

According to the [Ministry of Religious Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Religious_Affairs_\(Indonesia\)) data in 2025, 83.85% of Jakarta's population was [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Indonesia). [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Indonesia) accounted for 12.51%, followed by [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Indonesia) (3.44%), [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Indonesia) (0.18%), [Confucianis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_for_the_Confucian_Religion_in_Indonesia) (0.02%), and a small number registered under other religions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Population_by_Religion"._Ministry_of_Religious_Affairs,_2025-132]

Several national Islamic organisations have offices in Jakarta. The [Indonesian Ulema Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Ulema_Council) (MUI) and [Nahdlatul Ulama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahdlatul_Ulama)'s central board (PBNU) are based in Central Jakarta.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"MUI_Profile"._Indonesian_Ulema_Council-133][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Contact_Us_-_NU_Online"._NU_Online-134] [Muhammadiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammadiyah) is formally based in Yogyakarta, but its central leadership operates from offices in both Yogyakarta and Jakarta.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Muhammadiyah_Bylaws"._Muhammadiyah-135] The [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Jakarta) is a metropolitan archdiocese with [Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Bandung) and [Bogor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Bogor) as its [suffragan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan_bishop) dioceses.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Metropolitan_Archdiocese_of_Jakarta,_Indonesia"._GCatholic.org-136]

[Istiqlal Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istiqlal_Mosque,_Jakarta), the largest [mosque](19894.md) in Southeast Asia, stands opposite [Jakarta Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Cathedral), and their proximity has long been presented as a symbol of interfaith tolerance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKochValiyevZaini2018194–195-137] On Fridays, [Islamic prayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_prayer) usually involve large congregation of Muslims at mosques that on occasion extend into adjacent or nearby places.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYuniasihEllisa202611–15-138] In addition, during major religious holidays, shopping malls often display temporary [Eid al-Fitr](154944.md)- and [Christmas](6237.md)-themed decorations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"The_architecture_of_fluid_identities"._Inside_Indonesia,_2014-139]

## Economy

Jakarta skyline with clusters of modern high-rise office towers rising behind a tree-lined roadway.

The [Sudirman Central Business District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudirman_Central_Business_District) (SCBD) is a prominent business centre in Jakarta.

Under Jakarta's 2024 special-region law, the city's post-capital-transfer role is defined as a national economic centre and global city, with activities spanning trade, services, finance, and national, regional, and global business.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Law_Number_2_of_2024_concerning_the_Special_Region_of_Jakarta_Province"._Audit_Board_of_Indonesia,_2024-140] The economic significance of Jakarta developed over centuries, from Sunda Kelapa's role as a trading port to Batavia's commercial activities, and later to the wider metropolitan area's place in national and global economic networks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndraprahastaDerudder20193–4,_7-141]

In 2024, Jakarta's GRDP was Rp 3,679.36 trillion at current prices, while real economic output grew by 4.90% from 2023. Wholesale and retail trade, including motor-vehicle and motorcycle repair, accounted for 18.01% of Jakarta's economy in 2024, the largest industry share, while household consumption accounted for 62.03% on the expenditure side.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Jakarta's_Economic_Growth_in_the_Fourth_Quarter_of_2024"._Statistics_Indonesia_-_DKI_Jakarta_Province,_2025-142] Jakarta recorded Rp 241.9 trillion in realised investment in the same year, the second-largest provincial total in Indonesia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"The_Jakarta_Investment_Realization_Recap_2024"._Jakarta_Investment_Centre,_2025-143]

Services dominate Jakarta's economy. The city is Indonesia's financial and trade centre, and the wider metropolitan area is a prime location for specialised business services.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKurniaŽenkaČervenka20265-144][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndraprahastaDerudder20196-145] Global firms in these services generally base their Indonesian headquarters or representative offices in Jakarta, and the metropolitan area also hosts many headquarters of large domestic and multinational companies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndraprahastaDerudder20196-145] The city is also a centre of national administration, concentrated around the [Medan Merdeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merdeka_Square,_Jakarta)-[Gambir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambir,_Jakarta) area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKurniaŽenkaČervenka202610-146] Jakarta is the economic centre of *[Jabodetabek](1340014.md)*, which had 4.4 million commuters in 2023, 81.9% of whom travelled for work.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHidayatAsvaliantinaAjiMaulana20241,_10-147]

### Shopping

See also: [List of shopping malls in Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_shopping_malls_in_Jakarta)

Interior of a large multi-level shopping mall with glass-fronted stores, escalators, open balconies, and a promotional display in the central atrium.

[Grand Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Indonesia_Shopping_Town) shopping mall.

Jakarta has a large retail sector, with modern shopping centres operating alongside traditional markets. According to a late 2024 data, Jakarta had about 4.81 million square metres of [shopping-centre retail space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_malls_in_Jakarta).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECushman_&_Wakefield20242-148] [Grand Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Indonesia) and [Plaza Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Indonesia) are among Jakarta's high-end shopping centres.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Inside_the_bubble:_the_air-conditioned_alternate_reality_of_Jakarta's_megamalls"._The_Guardian,_2016-149] Beyond shopping, Jakarta's malls are also used as air-conditioned social and leisure spaces, partly substituting for scarce public space. However, access to these privately controlled spaces is unequal.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Inside_the_bubble:_the_air-conditioned_alternate_reality_of_Jakarta's_megamalls"._The_Guardian,_2016-149][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrzegorzewski20251-150]

Traditional markets (*pasar*) are also a part of the city's retail life.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUtariSoesiloHaruki202413-151] Examples include [Tanah Abang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanah_Abang), a major centre for wholesale textile trade,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOktarinaKurniawanLukito20252-152] and [Glodok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glodok) where shops and market activity are part of the area's Chinese Indonesian urban character.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBudiantoTadeusDjaukarEkomadyo2023395-153] [Pasar Baru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasar_Baru) is a historic commercial district with a multicultural character, known for fashion and clothing, footwear and bags, textile and tailoring businesses, and jewellery.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESholihahHeath2016247,_249–251-154]

### Tourism

See also: [Jakarta Tourism and Culture Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Tourism_and_Culture_Office)

Aerial view of a small tropical island covered with dense trees and low-rise buildings, surrounded by turquoise water, piers, and boats.

*[Untung Jawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untung_Jawa_\(Amsterdam_Island\))* Island, part of the [Thousand Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Islands_\(Indonesia\)) north of Jakarta's coast.

Jakarta is one of Indonesia's busiest entry points for foreign tourists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuprionoTopowijonoMusadieq2017119-155] In 2024, it recorded about 2.5 million international tourist visits, representing 18.2% of Indonesia's total.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Number_of_International_Tourist_Arrivals_in_DKI_Jakarta_and_Indonesia,_2024"._Statistics_Indonesia_–_DKI_Jakarta_Province,_2025-156] In the first quarter of 2025, Jakarta recorded about 23.2 million domestic tourist trips.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Growth_of_Tourism_in_DKI_Jakarta_Province,_March_2025"._Statistics_Indonesia_–_DKI_Jakarta_Province,_2025-157] Input-output analysis found that tourism-related demand affects economic output, household income and employment, with transport and warehousing, accommodation and food services, and information and communication showing some of the largest multiplier effects.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosyadiAvilianiIqbal2023157-158]

Many foreign visitors arrive in Jakarta for business or transit.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuprionoTopowijonoMusadieq2017119-155] A 2016 study of foreign visitors identified several lifestyle segments, including one combining cultural interests with shopping.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESrihadiHartoyoSukandarSoehadi201632-159] Shopping centres also form part of Jakarta's urban tourism, with some combining retail with green space, galleries, events and other leisure facilities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuryadjaja20127–8-160]

Jakarta's tourist attractions include the [National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Monument_\(Indonesia\)) (*Monas*), the [Jakarta Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Tua_Jakarta) (*Kota Tua*), and [Sunda Kelapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Kelapa) harbour. The 132-metre Monas in [Merdeka Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merdeka_Square,_Jakarta) commemorates Indonesia's struggle for independence and has an observation deck overlooking the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Historical_Tourism"._Government_of_Jakarta,_2021-161] *Kota Tua* preserves elements of colonial Batavia, including canals, former VOC warehouses and the old Town Hall.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"The_Old_Town_of_Jakarta_(Formerly_old_Batavia)_and_4_Outlying_Islands_(Onrust,_Kelor,_Cipir_dan_Bidadari)"._UNESCO_World_Heritage_Centre,_2015-162] To the north, the historic [Sunda Kelapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Kelapa) harbour is another attraction, which is still used by traditional inter-island vessels.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Historical_Tourism"._Government_of_Jakarta,_2021-161]

## Culture

Close-up of two colorful Betawi ondel-ondel giant puppets, one with a pale face and one with a red face, featuring painted facial details and large radiating headdresses made of multicolored decorative spikes.

*[Ondel-ondel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondel-ondel)*, a large traditional Betawi puppet.

Jakarta's cultural life showcases a population influenced by long-standing migration from across Indonesia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHadiTirtosudarmo201623-106][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinezMasron20206-163] Betawi traditions are part of local culture, along with cultural practices of communities from other parts of Indonesia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHadiTirtosudarmo201625–27-164][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinezMasron20206–7-165]

### Arts and festivals

Betawi performing arts include traditions such as *[tanjidor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjidor)*, *[lenong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenong)*, and *[ondel-ondel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondel-ondel)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHadijahHenuk2025214,_217–218,_224–225,_227-166] *Lebaran Betawi* is held as a public celebration and educational event intended to introduce and preserve Betawi culture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPetricia202283-167] Condet was designated a Betawi cultural heritage area in 1976, but the designation was revoked in 1988. More recent plans have again promoted Betawi cultural activities there.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENurizkiHerlambangRahardjo20202771–2772-168]

[Wayang orang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayang_orang) Bharata stages regular performances at the Bharata Purwa theatre in Senen,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"'Wayang_Orang_Bharata'_strives_to_preserve_traditional_culture"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2016-169] while [Aula Simfonia Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aula_Simfonia_Jakarta) is a major venue for orchestral and Western classical music and is regularly used by professional ensembles.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndita202291,_146-170] [Taman Ismail Marzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taman_Ismail_Marzuki) in Menteng hosts art exhibitions, dance and music performances, cultural programmes, and astronomy activities linked to the [Jakarta Planetarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Planetarium_and_Observatory).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFitriantySantosaErnawati2025202-171] Jakarta also hosts recurring cultural events, such as [Jakarta Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Fashion_Week) and the [Java Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_Jazz_Festival).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELuvaas2013205-172][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBonneauParantika2016283-173] International cultural institutions in the city include the [Japan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Foundation)'s Jakarta centre and [Erasmus Huis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Huis), the Netherlands' cultural centre.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"The_Japan_Foundation,_Jakarta"._The_Japan_Foundation-174][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Erasmus_Huis"._Netherlands_and_You-175]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Betawi cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_cuisine)

A bowl of creamy soto Betawi topped with pieces of meat, tomato, herbs, and crackers, served on a wooden placemat beside a plate piled with extra crackers. To the right is a wooden cutting board leafy herbs, chopped green onions, and red chilies on it. Two whole tomatoes are in the foreground.

*[Soto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soto_\(food\)#Varieties) [betawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_people)*, a Jakarta-area variant of soto.

[Betawi cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_cuisine) draws on cultural influences from other parts of the archipelago and from overseas, including Malay, Chinese, Arab, Portuguese, and Dutch traditions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKartikaKartika2022723,_728-176] One example is *[Soto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soto_\(food\)#Varieties) [betawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betawi_people)* a Jakarta-area *soto* made with beef and offal in a broth containing coconut milk or cow's milk.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYudhistiraFatmawati20207-177]

*[Wartegs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warung#Varieties)* are one prevalent example of a street-side eatery, as they serve simple, inexpensive meals to a wide range of Jakarta residents and workers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMeilasari-SugianaSusantoNovianti2019187–188-178] Several districts are known for concentrations of food vendors and restaurants. The [Blok M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blok_M) area has developed into a popular culinary destination, particularly among younger visitors,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKulsumTjkrosaputro2025300-179] while in [Glodok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glodok), Chinese and traditional foods are part of Chinatown's appeal as a culinary destination.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChristalynSarudin20241-180]

### Sports

Interior of Jakarta International Stadium during a football match, showing the illuminated pitch, sweeping tiers of orange and gray seating, spectators, and the large roof structure overhead.

The [Jakarta International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_International_Stadium) in [Tanjung Priok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Priok), North Jakarta. With a seating capacity of 82,000, it is Indonesia's biggest stadium.

Jakarta has hosted major international sporting events, including the [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Asian_Games) and [2018 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Asian_Games), which it co-hosted with [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Jakarta_1962"._Olympic_Council_of_Asia-181][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Jakarta_-_Palembang_2018"._Olympic_Council_of_Asia-182] The [Gelora Bung Karno Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelora_Bung_Karno_Stadium) was also one of the venues for the [2007 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Asian_Cup_venue:_Gelora_Bungkarno_Stadium"._ABC_News,_2007-183] The larger [Gelora Bung Karno complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelora_Bung_Karno_Sports_Complex) also contains an aquatic stadium, Istora indoor arena, tennis venues, football fields, and other sports facilities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"GBK_-_Sports_Complex"._Gelora_Bung_Karno-184] Other major venues include the [Jakarta International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_International_Stadium), a retractable-roof football stadium inaugurated in 2022,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Indonesia:_Jakarta_International_Stadium_inaugurated!"._Stadium_DB,_2022-185] and the [Jakarta International Velodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_International_Velodrome) in Rawamangun, which was used for the 2018 Asian Games.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Jakpost_guide_to_Asian_Games_2018"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2018-186]

Jakarta residents also use streets and public spaces for exercise and organised events. During [Jakarta Car-Free Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car-free_days), sections of Sudirman and Thamrin are closed to motor traffic and used for exercise and family activities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Jakarta_Car_Free_Day:_Exercise_and_Socialise"._Now_Jakarta,_2019-187] while the [Jakarta Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Marathon) has been held since 2013.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"IAAF_Approves_Jakarta_Marathon's_Route"._Tempo,_2013-188] The city has also hosted international motorsport, including the first [Jakarta ePrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_ePrix) that was held in 2022 at the [Jakarta International e-Prix Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_International_e-Prix_Circuit) at Ancol Beach.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Last_year_in_Jakarta..."._ABB_FIA_Formula_E_World_Championship,_2023-189]

### Media and entertainment

A tall TV broadcasting tower rises behind a TVRI building, with a large antenna at the top against a cloudy sky.

The main [TV tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVRI_Tower) of [TVRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVRI) at its headquarters in Jakarta.

See also: [List of newspapers in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_Indonesia), [List of radio stations in Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_radio_stations_in_Jakarta), and [List of television stations in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_stations_in_Indonesia)

Printed news in Jakarta dates back to colonial Batavia, where *Bataviase Nouvelles* first appeared in 1744 and seem to have circulated mainly among VOC employees and Europeans.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdam198417–18-190] Jakarta is home to the headquarters of most kinds of Indonesian media organisation. The concentration differs by sector, with television ownership focused particularly in the capital and radio and newspaper ownership more concentrated in Java generally.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasdukiD'Haenens20222246,_2253-191] Reforms after 1998 expanded the broadcasting system to include public and community media along with commercial outlets, but large private groups based in Jakarta remained dominant.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMasdukiD'Haenens20222241,_2252–2253-192]

Around 70% of [Indonesia's cinema industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Indonesia) is concentrated in the metropolitan area, where much of the country's filmmaking and editing equipment is concentrated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBritish_CouncilJakarta_Creative_Economy_Committee202342-193] The local film ecosystem includes the [Jakarta Film Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Film_Week) and Producer Lab, a government-supported programme for Indonesian film producers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBritish_CouncilJakarta_Creative_Economy_Committee202341-194] Jakarta is also a major publishing centre, with about 25% of Indonesia's publishers based in the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBritish_CouncilJakarta_Creative_Economy_Committee202311-195] In recent years, several creative subsectors have used digital platforms as part of their public-facing activities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBritish_CouncilJakarta_Creative_Economy_Committee20239-196]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Governor of Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Jakarta)

Jakarta City Hall complex with a large fountain and potted plants in the foreground, an Indonesian flag flying at center, and modern office buildings rising behind.

The [Jakarta City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_City_Hall)

Jakarta is a special autonomous region at the provincial level.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Law_Number_2_of_2024_concerning_the_Special_Region_of_Jakarta_Province"._Audit_Board_of_Indonesia,_2024-140] Formerly a municipality of the [West Java](314356.md) province, Jakarta became a "[first-level region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia)" (*Daerah Tingkat I*) in 1957, and later acquired the status of a Special Capital Region (*Daerah Khusus Ibukota*, DKI) in 1961.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"History_of_Jakarta"._Government_of_Jakarta-197] Under the city's 2024 special-region law, Jakarta remains the national capital until a presidential decree formalises the transfer to [Nusantara](69803286.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Law_Number_2_of_2024_concerning_the_Special_Region_of_Jakarta_Province"._Audit_Board_of_Indonesia,_2024-140]

Jakarta's provincial government is headed by a directly elected [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Jakarta) and [vice governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Governor_of_Jakarta), while the [Jakarta Regional People's Representative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Regional_People's_Representative_Council) has legislative, budgetary, and oversight roles.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Law_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia_Number_29_of_2007_concerning_the_Governance_of_the_Special_Capital_Region_of_Jakarta_as_the_Capital_of_the_Unitary_State_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia"._Ministry_of_Finance_Legal_Documentation_and_Information_Network,_2007-198] The administrative cities and the Thousand Islands administrative regency are headed by mayors and a regent appointed by the governor rather than elected locally.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESapiiAponnoThoriq202591-199] At the national level, Jakarta elects 21 [DPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Representative_Council) members from three electoral districts and four [DPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Representative_Council) members.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKPU202422–24-200][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDPR_RI2014124-201]

Jakarta derives most of its provincial revenue from local sources. In 2024, realised revenue was Rp72.95 trillion, including Rp50.74 trillion in locally generated revenue (*pendapatan asli daerah*, PAD) and Rp21.62 trillion in transfer revenue.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDKI_Jakarta_Province_Official_Portal202569-202] Basic-service functions accounted for 56.33% of provincial spending, with education and health being the largest components.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDKI_Jakarta_Province_Official_Portal202515-203] Jakarta also accounted for about half of Indonesia's cumulative subnational borrowing during the 2016–2021 period, with gross cumulative loans of Rp37 trillion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIhsanRahmadantiIlman202320-204]

### Administrative divisions

See also: [List of districts of Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Jakarta)

Map of Jakarta divided into its five administrative cities, showing district boundaries, roads, and major location markers. The Thousand Islands Regency shown in an inset at lower left.

Map of Jakarta's administrative cities, with the Thousand Islands Regency shown in a lower-left inset.

Jakarta is divided into five administrative cities (*kota administrasi*) and one administrative regency (*kabupaten administrasi*). The administrative cities are [Central Jakarta](2728760.md), [West Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Jakarta), [South Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jakarta), [East Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Jakarta), and [North Jakarta](2728733.md), while the [Thousand Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Islands_\(Indonesia\)) form the province's sole administrative regency.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDKI_Jakarta_Provincial_Government20212-205] The five administrative cities were established by gubernatorial decree in 1966.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGovernor_of_Jakarta19662-206] In 2001, the [Thousand Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Islands_\(Indonesia\)), then part of North Jakarta, was elevated to an administrative regency to improve public services and welfare and the management of the islands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Government_Regulation_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia_No._55_of_2001_on_the_Establishment_of_the_Thousand_Islands_Administrative_Regency_in_the_Special_Capital_Region_of_Jakarta"._Audit_Board_of_Indonesia,_2001-207]

Each administrative city is headed by a mayor, and the Thousand Islands by a regent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinezMasron20202-53] The five cities and the regency are made up of [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Indonesia) (*kecamatan*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDKI_Jakarta_Provincial_Government20212-205] Unlike autonomous cities and regencies elsewhere in Indonesia, Jakarta's administrative cities and regency are non-autonomous parts of the provincial administration. City and regency councils assist their mayors or regent, but the units do not have their own DPRD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUzdah202445,_47-208]

### Public safety

Policing in Jakarta is handled by [Greater Jakarta Metropolitan Regional Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Jakarta_Metropolitan_Regional_Police) (*Polda Metro Jaya*), the regional police force of the [Indonesian National Police](18251498.md). Its jurisdiction covers Jakarta and nearby areas including Depok, Tangerang city and regency, South Tangerang, and Bekasi city and regency. The force is led by a regional police chief with the rank of inspector general.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Profile_-_Tribrata_News_Polda_Metro_Jaya"._Tribrata_News-209]

Jakarta Siaga 112 is a free, 24-hour emergency call service operated by the Jakarta's disaster management agency (BPBD).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGovernor_of_Jakarta20171-210][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Jakarta_Siaga_112_Emergency_Call_Service"._Jakarta_Regional_Disaster_Management_Agency-211] It integrates emergency-call services from provincial agencies, police and other organizations, forwarding reports on fires, medical emergencies, crime, disasters and other incidents to the relevant service.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGovernor_of_Jakarta20173,_5–6-212]

Two examples of public transport in Jakarta, the [KRL Commuterline](15039992.md) and the [Transjakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transjakarta) bus

The Jakarta metro area is heavily dependent on private road transport and experiences severe traffic congestion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWorld_Bank202210-213] Jakarta is served by the [Jakarta Inner Ring Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Inner_Ring_Road) and [Jakarta Outer Ring Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_Outer_Ring_Road), as well as toll roads linking the city with [Tangerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangerang), [Cikampek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cikampek), [Bogor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogor) and other surrounding areas.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Toll_Road_Concession_Business"._PT_Jasa_Marga_(Persero)_Tbk-214] To reduce congestion, Jakarta uses an [odd-even licence-plate restriction system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd%E2%80%93even_rationing) on 16 main roads.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAzniPrihantoroSaputra202431,_33-215]

Jakarta's public transport network includes [Transjakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transjakarta), the [Jakarta MRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_MRT), [Jakarta LRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_LRT), [Jabodebek LRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabodebek_LRT), [KRL Commuterline](15039992.md), and the [airport rail link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soekarno%E2%80%93Hatta_Airport_Commuter_Line).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"President_opens_Jabodebek_LRT"._Railway_Gazette_International,_2023-216][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETUMI20232-217] Since 2018, Jakarta has expanded fare and station integration and improved walking and cycling access to public transport.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEITDP20213,_9,_11-218] In 2021, Jakarta received the Sustainable Transport Award for work on integrated and resilient transport systems.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEITDP20212-219] The wider metropolitan area also has large daily commuter flows between Jakarta and Bogor, [Depok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depok), Tangerang, and [Bekasi](1915107.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Commuter_Statistics_of_Greater_Jakarta:_Results_of_the_2023_Jabodetabek_Commuter_Survey"._Statistics_Indonesia,_2024-220]

The primary airport for the metropolitan area is the [Soekarno–Hatta International Airport](1203751.md), while [Halim Perdanakusuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halim_Perdanakusuma) also accommodates commercial flights along with military purposes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarjonoSembiringPasaribu2023292-221][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"At_Halim_military_airbase,_defense_comes_after_business"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2016-222] [Port of Tanjung Priok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tanjung_Priok) in North Jakarta is Indonesia's largest and busiest port, while the Muara Angke harbour provides boat services to the [Thousand Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Islands_\(Indonesia\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESusantonoBerawiMirajPetroceany2015101-223][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Want_Vacation?_Here_are_Boat_Fares_to_Seribu_Islands"._Berita_Jakarta,_2022-224]

### Healthcare

White hospital building with tower-like roof sections decorated with Indonesian flags and red-and-white bunting. A covered entrance, parked cars, and a driveway is shown in front.

[Dr. Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Cipto_Mangunkusumo_Hospital) in Jakarta

As elsewhere in Indonesia, Jakarta's healthcare system includes public and private hospitals, clinics, and community health centres (*[puskesmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puskesmas)*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMahendradhataTrisnantoroListyadewiSoewondo2017151–152,_156-225] In late 2012, then governor [Joko Widodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Widodo) introduced the *Kartu Jakarta Sehat* (Healthy Jakarta Card, KJS), a provincial programme intended to expand access to medical care through public health facilities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Jokowi_set_to_launch_'Jakarta_Health_Card'"._The_Jakarta_Post,_2012-226] The programme merged into the national *[Jaminan Kesehatan Nasional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaminan_Kesehatan_Nasional)* (JKN) system in January 2014, administered by *[BPJS Kesehatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BPJS_Kesehatan)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPisani2017267,_272-227] A 2025 Jakarta planning document reported that universal health coverage in the city had reached 98.5% in 2023.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBappeda202538-228]

A 2023 assessment estimated that air pollution in Jakarta contributes annually to more than 10,000 premature deaths and 5,000 hospital admissions, as well as over 7,000 cases of adverse health effects among children.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"7_Things_to_Know_About_Jakarta's_Air_Pollution_Crisis"._World_Resources_Institute_Indonesia,_2023-229] In the 2023 Indonesia Health Survey, 3.9% of residents aged 15 or over had doctor-diagnosed [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes), the highest provincial rate in the country, while 31.8% of residents aged over 18 were [obese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Key_Results_of_the_2023_Indonesia_Health_Survey"._Ministry_of_Health_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia-230]

### Water supply

Further information: [Water privatisation in Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_privatisation_in_Jakarta)

Jakarta granted two 25-year concessions to private water operators in 1997, PAM [Lyonnaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_\(company,_1997%E2%80%932008\)) Jaya and [Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_Water) PAM Jaya (renamed Aetra in 2007). Both companies operated in the eastern and western part of the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEADB20133-231] PAM Jaya resumed full operation in 2023 after private management ended.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"PAM_Jaya_Starts_100%_Independent_Operations_Today"._Government_of_Jakarta,_2023-232] The West Tarum Canal, which carries water from the [Jatiluhur Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatiluhur_Dam) and the [Citarum River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citarum_River), supplied about 80% of Jakarta's bulk water as of 2014.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHadipuroRusmadiLatifEkaningdyah2014369-233]

Piped-water coverage had reached about 82% of Jakarta's population by 2026.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Jakarta,_the_Sinking_Metropolis_Where_Millions_Still_Struggle_to_Access_Piped_Water"._The_Diplomat,_2026-234] Historically, access was uneven, with poorer households facing barriers to direct network connections.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBakkerKooyShofianiMartijn20081905-235] Groundwater has long supplied a substantial share of the city's water needs, but heavy extraction is linked to land subsidence and salinisation. Waste-water contamination also affects shallow groundwater.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKooyWalterPrabaharyaka2018111–112-236] In 2024, non-revenue water, the difference between water distributed and water billed to customers, stood at 46.2%, and the city's reduction programme prioritises rehabilitating ageing distribution pipelines.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"NRW_Reduction"._Government_of_Jakarta-237][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Contracting_Tools_for_Non-Revenue_Water_Reduction"._World_Bank-238]
