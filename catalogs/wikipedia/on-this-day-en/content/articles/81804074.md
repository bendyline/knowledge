**Nnena Kalu** ([//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/24/LL-Q33578\_%28ibo%29-Vivian_Amalachukwu-Nnena_Kalu.wav/LL-Q33578\_%28ibo%29-Vivian_Amalachukwu-Nnena_Kalu.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/24/LL-Q33578_%28ibo%29-Vivian_Amalachukwu-Nnena_Kalu.wav/LL-Q33578_%28ibo%29-Vivian_Amalachukwu-Nnena_Kalu.wav.mp3)[Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_language) pronunciation<sup>ⓘ</sup>; born 1966)[^cite_note-artnet-1] is a British artist who won the [2025 Turner Prize](81784513.md).[^cite_note-GuardianTurnerWin-3][^cite_note-nyt-2]

## Early life

Kalu was born in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) to Nigerian parents in 1966, and moved to [Wandsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandsworth) in London at a young age.[^cite_note-4] She is [autistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autistic) and has limited verbal communication.[^cite_note-5]

She began making art in the 1980s[^cite_note-nyt-2] at Hill House day centre in [Tooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tooting), in south London.[^cite_note-GuardianTurnerWin-3]

## Work

Kalu began making sculptures around 2010, after making flat artworks for years. She begins with a bundle of paper, cloth, or another base structure, and then elaborates it - often compulsively - by wrapping, layering, and binding materials such as ropes, strips of fabric, unspooled [VHS cassette tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHS), netting and [rubbish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbage).[^cite_note-CNNWin-6][^cite_note-\:1-7] Her sculptures are usually made of found materials.[^cite_note-ArtNetNom-8] In 2013, she began making distinctive drawings which have been compared to whirlpools, usually completed in pairs or trios,[^cite_note-CNNWin-6] sometimes fours or sixes, but not alone. Kalu creates the drawings together, often with her eyes closed.[^cite_note-CAS-9]

Critics have focused on the physicality of her work, comparing her sculptures to bodies[^cite_note-WorthyWinner-10] and "disembowelled organs".[^cite_note-\:1-7]

## Career

In 1999, Kalu began working as an artist at ActionSpace in Clapham, an organization which assists artists with learning disabilities.[^cite_note-bbc-11]

In 2016, her works were shown in [Belgium](3343.md) alongside artists such as [Laure Prouvost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laure_Prouvost), who won the Turner Prize in 2013;[^cite_note-GuardianTurnerWin-3] at the 2018 [Glasgow International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_International);[^cite_note-\:0-12] [Humber Street Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber_Street_Gallery);[^cite_note-\:0-12][^cite_note-13] and at [Studio Voltaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_Voltaire).[^cite_note-14] Her first commercial show was in 2024, at Arcadia Missa in London. The gallery is her official representative.[^cite_note-CNNWin-6]

*Creations of Care*,[^cite_note-CAS-9] Kalu's first major institutional show, was held in 2025 at the [Kunsthall Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsthall_Stavanger?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Norway.[^cite_note-GuardianTurnerWin-3] Later in 2025, she won the [Turner Prize](149045.md), the most prominent British art award.[^cite_note-bbc-11] The jury nominated her for her work in *Conversations* at the [Walker Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker_Art_Gallery) in [Liverpool](18081.md), and *Hanging Sculpture 1 to 10* at Manifesta 15 in [Barcelona](4443.md).[^cite_note-15] The BBC described her winning works as drawings of tornado-like swirls, and brightly coloured sculptures that are wrapped haphazardly with layers of materials such as ribbon, string, card and VHS tape.[^cite_note-bbc-11]

She was the first artist with a learning disability to win the prize,[^cite_note-bbc-11] and her facilitator and studio manager, Charlotte Hollinshead, made a speech on her behalf, in which she said that Kalu "has faced an incredible amount of discrimination" and hoped that award would help "smash the prejudice away."[^cite_note-GuardianTurnerWin-3]

Kalu's works have been included as part of the [Arts Council Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_Council_Collection)[^cite_note-16] and the collection of [The Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tate).[^cite_note-17]
