**Ismaïla Sarr** (born 25 February 1998) is a Senegalese professional [footballer](10568.md) who plays as a [winger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winger_\(association_football\)) or [forward](428681.md) for [Premier League](11250.md) club [Crystal Palace](385313.md) and the [Senegal national team](1017436.md).

Sarr was a youth academy graduate of Senegalese club [Génération Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9n%C3%A9ration_Foot), and made his senior debut for [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) club [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metz) in 2016. He subsequently spent two seasons at [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Rennais_FC), winning the [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Coupe_de_France) in 2019 and scoring the winning penalty in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Coupe_de_France_final), before moving to Premier League club [Watford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford_F.C.), where he was named Player of the Season in 2021. He departed in 2023 and spent a season back in France with [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille), before returning to England with Crystal Palace, where he won the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_FA_Cup) in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FA_Cup_final), his first season. In 2026, he was [top goalscorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_UEFA_Conference_League#Top_goalscorers) and named [best player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Conference_League#Player_of_the_Season) as Palace won the [UEFA Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_UEFA_Conference_League_final).

Sarr made his senior debut for Senegal in 2016, and went on to represent his side at the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [2018](13327177.md), [2022](17742072.md) and [2026](36581929.md), as well as at the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and [2025](57061770.md), winning the 2021 tournament.

## Club career

### Early career

Born in [Saint-Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Louis,_Senegal), Senegal, Sarr started his football career with Senegalese football club [Génération Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9n%C3%A9ration_Foot).

### Metz

On 13 July 2016, Sarr signed his first professional contract with [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metz) for five years.[^cite_note-2] He made his league debut as a substitute in a 3–2 win against [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille_OSC) on 13 August, in Metz's first match of the [2016–17 Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Ligue_1) season.[^cite_note-3] He made his first professional start in a 3–0 win at [Nantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Nantes) on 11 September.[^cite_note-fcnantes-4]

On 8 February 2017, Sarr scored his first [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) goal in a 2–1 win against [Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijon_FCO).[^cite_note-fcnantes-4] He went on to score four more goals in his final eight appearances of the season, ending [2016–17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_FC_Metz_season) with five goals and five assists from 31 league appearances.[^cite_note-fcnantes-4]

### Rennes

On 26 July 2017, Sarr signed a four-year contract with Ligue 1 side [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Rennais_FC). The transfer fee paid to Metz was reported as €17 or 20 million, depending on the source.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] Sarr claimed that he had chosen to sign for Rennes ahead of [Barcelona](68187.md), saying: "I could have gone to Spain and join the great Barcelona, but leaving Metz to join them was too early. I saw Rennes as a club that will help me progress and reach great heights".[^cite_note-7]

Sarr made his debut for Rennes in the team's [2017–18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Stade_Rennais_F.C._season) season opener against [Troyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ES_Troyes_AC) on 5 August 2017 and scored his first goal against [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Toulouse) on 26 August.[^cite_note-ubereats-8]

His first season at the [Breton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany) club was curtailed by a [malleolus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malleolus) fracture sustained in a match against [Saint-Étienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Saint-%C3%89tienne) on 24 September.[^cite_note-9] On 28 September, it was announced by coach [Christian Gourcuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Gourcuff) that Sarr's injury would require surgery that would prevent him from playing until 2018.[^cite_note-10]

Sarr ended the season with five goals and five assists from 24 [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Ligue_1) appearances[^cite_note-ubereats-8] as Rennes finished in fifth place and qualified for the [2018–19 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Europa_League).[^cite_note-11]

During the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Stade_Rennais_F.C._season), Sarr made his [UEFA Europa League](232175.md) debut, scoring the opening goal of a 2–1 against [Czech](5321.md) club [FK Jablonec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Jablonec) on 20 September 2018. This goal was later included on a shortlist of ten for UEFA Goal of the Season.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-athletic-13] On 13 December 2018, he scored twice, as Rennes clinched qualified for the knockout phase with a 2–0 home win over [FC Astana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Astana).[^cite_note-14] His fourth Europa League goal of the season came from a header against [Arsenal](2174.md) in a 3–1 round of 16 first leg win on 7 March 2019.[^cite_note-15] However, on 14 March, Rennes lost the second leg in [London](17867.md) 3–0 and were eliminated.[^cite_note-16]

In [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Ligue_1), Sarr scored eight goals and registered six assists.[^cite_note-athletic-13] His form saw him nominated for the Ligue 1 Young Player of the Season award.[^cite_note-athletic-13]

He also made a significant contribution to Rennes winning their first [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) in 48 years.[^cite_note-17] On 6 January 2019, he scored the equalising goal in a 2–2 draw with [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Brestois_29) and scored in the subsequent [penalty shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shootout) that took the team through to the fourth round of [the tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Coupe_de_France).[^cite_note-18] On 2 April, he assisted Senegalese teammate [M'Baye Niang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M'Baye_Niang)'s opening goal in an eventual 3–2 semi-final win against [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais).[^cite_note-19] Rennes went on to face [Paris Saint Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint_Germain_F.C.) at the [Stade de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_France) in the [2019 Coupe de France Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Coupe_de_France_Final) on 27 April, with Sarr scoring the winning kick of the penalty shootout after the underdogs had recovered from a 2–0 deficit to draw 2–2 during regulation time.[^cite_note-20]

Overall, Sarr scored 18 goals and registered 16 assists from 77 appearances for Rennes.[^cite_note-bein-21]

### Watford

On 8 August 2019, Sarr joined [Premier League](11250.md) club [Watford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford_F.C.) on a five-year contract. The transfer fee paid to Rennes was a club record for Watford[^cite_note-22] and reported as "in the region of €30 million".[^cite_note-23]

Sarr made his debut for Watford as a substitute in a 3–1 [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Premier_League) defeat to [West Ham United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ham_United) at [Vicarage Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicarage_Road) on 24 August 2019.[^cite_note-24] Three days later, on his first start for Watford, he scored his first goal in English football in an [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_EFL_Cup) tie against [Coventry City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry_City_F.C.).[^cite_note-25] On 30 November 2019, Sarr scored his first Premier League goal in a 2–1 loss at [Southampton](81576.md).[^cite_note-26] On 22 December, he was voted [man of the match](906698.md) in Watford's 2–0 home win over [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) – a match in which he scored the opening goal and was fouled for the [penalty kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_kick) that was converted by captain [Troy Deeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Deeney).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] On 29 February 2020, Sarr scored twice and assisted Deeney's third goal in Watford's 3–0 home victory over defending [European Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Champions_League) [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), handing them their first defeat of the [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Liverpool_F.C._season) and ending the club's 44-match unbeaten league run.[^cite_note-29] On 26 July, Watford were relegated to the [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) after losing 3–2 to Arsenal at the [Emirates Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_Stadium) in their final match of the season.[^cite_note-30] Sarr ended [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Watford_F.C._season) with five goals and four assists in the Premier League.[^cite_note-best-31]

During [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Watford_F.C._season), Sarr scored thirteen goals and provided ten assists,[^cite_note-century-32] winning Watford's Player of the Season.[^cite_note-33] On 24 April 2021, he scored the only goal of a 1–0 win over [Millwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_F.C.) which ensured Watford's promotion back to the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Premier_League).[^cite_note-34]

Sarr started [2021–22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Watford_F.C._season) by scoring five goals in the opening twelve [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Premier_League) matches.[^cite_note-35] This included scoring once in a 3–2 win against [Aston Villa](42173.md) in the opening game of the season,[^cite_note-36] twice in a 3–1 win at [Norwich City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich_City_F.C.),[^cite_note-37] once in a 1–1 draw at home to [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.),[^cite_note-38] and once in a 4–1 home win over Manchester United.[^cite_note-39] However, Sarr struggled with injury for the rest of the season[^cite_note-best-31][^cite_note-century-32][^cite_note-40] and did not score in his final ten appearances[^cite_note-best-31] as Watford were relegated for the second time in three seasons.[^cite_note-ath-41][^cite_note-42]

On 8 August 2022, Sarr scored a goal from his own half in a 1–1 draw with [West Bromwich Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C.) at [The Hawthorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hawthorns).[^cite_note-43] The goal was later awarded [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_EFL_Championship) Goal of the Season.[^cite_note-ath-41] On 5 October, he played his 100th match for Watford, scoring in a 2–1 loss to [Swansea City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea_City_A.F.C.).[^cite_note-century-32] Sarr ended [the season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Watford_F.C._season) with 10 goals from 39 matches in all competitions.[^cite_note-farewell-44]

During his four seasons at Watford, he scored a 34 goals from 131 appearances.[^cite_note-farewell-44]

### Marseille

On 24 July 2023, Sarr joined French club [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille) for an undisclosed fee.[^cite_note-farewell-44][^cite_note-45]

Sarr made his debut for Marseille in a 1–0 loss to [Panathinaikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panathinaikos_F.C.) in a [UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_UEFA_Champions_League) qualifier on 9 August.[^cite_note-46] He scored his first goal in a 2–0 [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Ligue_1) win over Brest at the [Stade Vélodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_V%C3%A9lodrome) on 26 August.[^cite_note-47]

After an impressive start on his return to France, recording five goal involvements in his first nine matches, Sarr struggled with form and fitness for most of the [2023–24 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Olympique_de_Marseille_season).[^cite_note-48] Overall, he registered three goals and three assists from 15 league starts, in addition to two goals and three assists in the club's run to the [UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_UEFA_Europa_League) semi-finals, where they were beaten 4–1 on aggregate by eventual winners [Atalanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atalanta_B.C.).[^cite_note-49]

### Crystal Palace

On 1 August 2024, Sarr returned to the Premier League, signing for [Crystal Palace](385313.md).[^cite_note-50] He was assigned the number 7 shirt, previously worn by [Michael Olise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Olise), who had departed for [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich).[^cite_note-51]

Sarr made his debut for Crystal Palace as an 84th-minute substitute for [Chris Richards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Richards_\(soccer\)) in their opening match of the [2024–25 Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Premier_League) season, a 2–1 loss at [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford_F.C.) on 18 August.[^cite_note-52]

On 27 August, he scored his first goal for the club in a 4–0 [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_EFL_Cup) win over [Norwich City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich_City_F.C.); the goal, initially attributed to [Eberechi Eze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eberechi_Eze), was awarded to Sarr a week later.[^cite_note-53]

His first league goal for the *Eagles* came in a 2–2 draw at Aston Villa on 23 November. After scoring in the fourth minute, he went on to record an assist for [Justin Devenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Devenny) in first-half stoppage time.[^cite_note-54] On 15 December, he scored twice and assisted a goal for [Trevoh Chalobah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevoh_Chalobah) in Palace's 3–1 win at [rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_&_Hove_Albion_F.C.%E2%80%93Crystal_Palace_F.C._rivalry) [Brighton & Hove Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_&_Hove_Albion_F.C.), winning the Man of the Match award.[^cite_note-55]

During Palace's run to the [2025 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FA_Cup_final), Sarr scored the team's second goal in the 3–0 quarter-final win at [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.)[^cite_note-56] and then scored two goals and assisted the other in the 3–0 semi-final defeat of Aston Villa at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md).[^cite_note-57] On 17 May 2025, Sarr played in the FA Cup final and won the [FA Cup](11237.md) as Crystal Palace beat [Manchester City](165813.md) 1–0 to win their first major trophy.[^cite_note-58] Sarr followed this up by scoring in the [2025 FA Community Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FA_Community_Shield) which Palace won against Liverpool.[^cite_note-59]

On 30 April 2026, Sarr scored the fastest goal in the [UEFA Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Conference_League) after 21 seconds in a 3–1 away win over [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) in the [semi-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_UEFA_Conference_League_knockout_phase) first leg, surpassing previous record of [Ferdy Druijf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdy_Druijf).[^cite_note-60] A few weeks later, on 10 May, he scored his 20th goal of the season in all competitions in a 2–2 draw with [Everton](91155.md), becoming the first Crystal Palace player to reach that mark since [Glenn Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Murray) did so in the 2012–13 season.[^cite_note-61] On 28 May, he played all 90 minutes in the [2026 UEFA Conference League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_UEFA_Conference_League_final) as Palace won 1–0 against [Rayo Vallecano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayo_Vallecano).[^cite_note-62]

## International career



Sarr playing for Senegal in 2021 Africa Cup of Nations

Sarr has represented his country with the [Senegal U23 national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_national_under-23_football_team). In 2015, he played in [2015 Africa U-23 Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_U-23_Cup_of_Nations). At that time, his age was only 17. He contributed that Senegal finished the competition in a fourth place, playing three games.

He played for the first time with [Senegal national team](1017436.md) for a match against [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_football_team) in September 2016 in [Dakar](44251.md) (2–0) for [2017 Africa Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification), for which Senegal had already qualified. As a symbol, he came into play in the 67th minute in place of the former resident of [Génération Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9n%C3%A9ration_Foot) and [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metz), [Sadio Mané](34758489.md).[^cite_note-63] On 8 January 2017, Sarr scored his first international goal in a [friendly match](977090.md) against [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team) in [Stade Municipal de Kintélé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Municipal_de_Kint%C3%A9l%C3%A9), [Brazzaville](52710.md).

In May 2018, he was named in Senegal's 23-man squad for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia.[^cite_note-64] He started all three group matches at the tournament[^cite_note-65] as Senegal beat [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) 2–1, drew 2–2 with [Japan](993546.md) and lost 1–0 to [Colombia](680175.md).[^cite_note-66]

In [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Sarr was part of the Senegal squad that reached the final of the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md), for only the second time in the nation's history.[^cite_note-67] He played the full 90 minutes of the final on 19 July 2019, as they lost 1–0 to [Algeria](1029528.md).[^cite_note-68]

Sarr was part of [Senegal's squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_squads#Senegal) for the [2021 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations); the Lions of Teranga went on to win the tournament for the first time in their history.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] He scored once in a 3–1 win over [Equatorial Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea_national_football_team) at the quarter-final stage.[^cite_note-71]

He was appointed a [Grand Officer of the National Order of the Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Order_of_the_Lion) by [President of Senegal](450099.md) [Macky Sall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macky_Sall) following the nation's victory at the tournament.[^cite_note-reuters-72]

Sarr started all four of Senegal's matches at the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md), scoring in a 2–1 win over [Ecuador](1016797.md) that qualified the nation for the round of 16 for the first time since its debut in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-73]

In December 2023, he was named in Senegal's squad for the postponed [2023 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) held in the [Ivory Coast](57744.md).[^cite_note-74] He started all four matches for the team, registering an assist in a 3–0 win over [The Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia_national_football_team),[^cite_note-75] and both scoring and assisting once in a 3–1 win over [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team).[^cite_note-76]

### 2026 FIFA World Cup

On 21 May 2026, Sarr was selected by Senegal's coach [Pape Thiaw](5728292.md) in the 26-man squad for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-77]

In Senegal's second group-stage match, he netted a brace in a 3–2 defeat against [Norway](203277.md), becoming the country's joint top scorer at the World Cup with three goals, alongside [Papa Bouba Diop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa_Bouba_Diop).[^cite_note-78] A week later, on 26 June, he netted a goal and provided an assist in a 5–0 victory over [Iraq](1039652.md), becoming Senegal's all-time top scorer at the World Cup with four goals and the third African player to score three goals in the group stage, following Papa Bouba Diop and [Ismael Saibari](65163271.md).[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

He scored in the [first knockout-stage](81667041.md) match against [Belgium](679716.md) on 1 July, which ended in a 3–2 defeat after extra time.[^cite_note-81] With that goal, he became only the second African player, after [Roger Milla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Milla), to score four goals in a single World Cup campaign, taking also his overall World Cup tally to five goals, moving behind only [Asamoah Gyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asamoah_Gyan) among African goalscorers in the tournament's history.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] He also became the second African player to score in three consecutive World Cup matches after Ismael Saibari.[^cite_note-84]
