**Bačka Palanka** ([Serbian Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Cyrillic_alphabet): Бачка Паланка, pronounced [\[bâːtʃkaː pǎlaːnka\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Serbo-Croatian); [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language): *Palánka*) is a town and municipality located in the [South Bačka District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Ba%C4%8Dka_District) of the autonomous province of [Vojvodina](176801.md), [Serbia](29265.md). It is situated on the left bank of the [Danube](21209639.md). In 2022 the town had a total population of 25,476, while Bačka Palanka municipality had 48,265 inhabitants.&#x20;

## Name

In [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language), the town is known as Бачка Паланка or *Bačka Palanka*, in [Slovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_language) as *Báčska Palanka*, in [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language) as *Bačka Palanka*, in [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language) as *Bácspalánka*, in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) as *Plankenburg* and in [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) as *Küçük Hisar*.&#x20;

Its name means "a town in [Bačka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%8Dka)" in [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language). The word "[palanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palanka_\(fortification\))" itself originates from [Turkish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language). This word was also adopted by Serbs and it is used in the Serbian language with the same meaning. Older Serbian names for this town were Palanka (Паланка), Stara Palanka (Стара Паланка), Nova Palanka (Нова Паланка) and Nemačka Palanka (Немачка Паланка).&#x20;

## History



Lithograph of Palanka and surroundings, 1826



[Church of St. John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_St._John_the_Baptist,_Ba%C4%8Dka_Palanka)

Archeologists have proved that people have lived in the area for centuries. There are many archaeological objects from the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age), [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) period.&#x20;

In the 11th century, this area was populated by [Hungarians](17106358.md) and [Serbs](59512.md).[^cite_note-backapalanka1-4] Bačka Palanka is first mentioned as a settlement in 1486, as a suburb of [Ilok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilok) called *Iločka*. Until the 16th century, this area was administered by the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary).&#x20;

At the beginning of the 16th century, the village was the property of a landowner [Laurence of Ilok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_of_Ilok), a duke of [Syrmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrmia). It was destroyed by the [Ottomans](22278.md) after The [Battle of Mohács](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moh%C3%A1cs) in 1526, but was then rebuilt as a small [Ottoman](22278.md) [fortress](204118.md) named Palanka. During the Ottoman administration (16th-17th century), Palanka was mostly populated by ethnic Serbs.[^cite_note-5]&#x20;

In 1687 Palanka was absorbed into the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) and more Orthodox [Serbs](59512.md) settled here. Palanka was then mentioned as a small town with 167 houses, all of which were Serb (1720 census data).[^cite_note-6] Later, [Germans](152735.md), [Slovaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks) and [Hungarians](17106358.md) settled here as well.[^cite_note-backapalanka1-4]&#x20;

It was part of the Habsburg [Military Frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Frontier) from 1702 to 1744. Nova Palanka (New Palanka) was founded between 1765 and 1770, 2 kilometers away from the original Palanka (which then became known as Stara Palanka - Old Palanka) and Nemačka Palanka (German Palanka) was founded by [Danube Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Swabians) in 1783. Those three towns became one city, Bačka Palanka, in the 20th century.&#x20;

Palanka's industrial development started in 1765, when a brick plant was built. It got its first post office in 1828. In 1875, one of the first libraries in [Vojvodina](176801.md) was opened. In 1884 the Sintelon company was founded. In 1886, the first state school started functioning. By 1894, a railway was built from Bačka Palanka to [Feketić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feketi%C4%87) and a first [phone call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_call) was made to [Novi Sad](113288.md).&#x20;

In the second half of the 19th century, the German population became more numerous than the Serbian.[^cite_note-7] According to the 1910 census, the population of Stara Palanka was mostly Serb, while populations of Nova Palanka and Nemačka Palanka were mostly German (Danube Swabian). A large number of them immigrated to North America both before and after World War II. Trenton, New Jersey was the most popular settling place for these German Palankaers.[^cite_note-8]&#x20;

The city was under Habsburg administration until 1918, when it became part of the [Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbs,_Croats_and_Slovenes) (later renamed as [Yugoslavia](34244.md)). During the [World War II](32927.md) (from 1941 to 1944), it was under [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) ([Horty Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_\(1920%E2%80%9346\))) occupation. In 1944, one part of Bačka Palanka's citizens of German ethnicity left the city, together with the defeated German army. As a consequence of the war, the site of a post-[World War II](32927.md) work camp for the remaining [ethnic Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Germans) ([Danube Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Swabians)) was formed here under a new [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) administration. After the work camps were dissolved (in 1948), the remaining German population was expelled from Yugoslavia in an [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing). To replace the Germans, 3,609 (mostly Serb) colonists were settled in the town after World War II. Most of them originated from [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md).&#x20;

During the 1990s, about 5,000 refugees from Croatia and Bosnia moved to Bačka Palanka because of the [Yugoslav wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_wars). During the [NATO](21133.md) bombing in 1999, Bačka Palanka was bombed by NATO twice, on 2 and 27 April. On both occasions the target was the [Ilok–Bačka Palanka Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilok%E2%80%93Ba%C4%8Dka_Palanka_Bridge). It was only damaged, but not destroyed.&#x20;

In 2002, 4 [tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton) of [fish stew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisherman%27s_soup) were cooked at Kaloš Čarda at the Bager Lake in Bačka Palanka and it entered the [Guinness Book of Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records).&#x20;

## Tourism

Tourism is developed in Bačka Palanka. The city is famous for Tikvara, a [natural monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_monument). Tikvara is a Danube lake. Tikvara covers an area of 5 square kilometres (1.9 square miles). The Tikvara Resort complex is built along the lake for practicing various sports, recreational and entertaining activities. There are 33 [archeological sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) in the municipality. It is also known for [Karađorđevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara%C4%91or%C4%91evo_\(Ba%C4%8Dka_Palanka\)), which has a hunting ground and horse farm. People enjoy racing derbies in Karađorđevo. At the time of Serbia's emancipation, engraver Marko Vujatović worked on the intricate [iconostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconostasis) of the Serbian Orthodox Church of [St. John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist). Bačka Palanka's Serbian church is one of the oldest churches in [Vojvodina](176801.md) some built on foundations of temples of even earlier times. The north of the town is the Bagremara forest.&#x20;

## Economy and industry

Industry in Bačka Palanka started developing in the 18th century. The first companies were built in the second half of the 18th century. A brick plant was opened in 1765, and a tobacco storehouse was opened a year later. In 1974 the *Bridge of Youth* was built to connect Bačka Palanka and [Ilok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilok) in Croatia. Today, Bačka Palanka falls among the ten most developed municipalities in Vojvodina. Bačka Palanka municipality is an agricultural and industrial center. The main industries are [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food), [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy), [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile), [electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics) and [machine industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_industry). The most famous factories are Enia, Sintelon, [Tarkett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarkett_d.o.o.), [Nectar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nectar_d.o.o.), Dunavprevoz, AD Bačka, [Carlsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Srbija), Marina, Majevica, Plattner, Žitoprodukt, Budućnost and others.&#x20;

## Sports



[Stadion Slavko Maletin Vava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Slavko_Maletin_Vava), where FK Bačka plays

Sport is very popular in Bačka Palanka. People enjoy swimming in Lake Tikvara and cycling down the banks of The Danube. [Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports) include [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_handball), football ([soccer](10568.md)), [basketball](3921.md) and [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate). There is a [chess](5134.md) tournament every year, Bačka Palanka Open.&#x20;
