**Prague** ([/ˈprɑːɡ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*PRAHG*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)  [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/01/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Zazollo-Prague.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Zazollo-Prague.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/01/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Zazollo-Prague.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Zazollo-Prague.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): *Praha* [\[ˈpraɦa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Czech) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c7/Cs-Praha.ogg/Cs-Praha.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c7/Cs-Praha.ogg/Cs-Praha.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>)[^cite_note-8] is the [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_the_Czech_Republic) of the [Czech Republic](5321.md)[^cite_note-\:1-9] and the historical capital of [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia). Located on the [Vltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vltava) River, the city has a population of about 1.4 million, making it the [twelfth-largest city in the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_European_Union_by_population_within_city_limits). Its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_metropolitan_area) is home to approximately 2.3 million people.

Prague is a historical city with [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture), [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Gothic_architecture), [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Renaissance_architecture), and [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Baroque_architecture) architecture. It was the capital of the [Kingdom of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia) and residence of several [Holy Roman Emperors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor), most notably [Charles IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) (r. 1346–1378) and [Rudolf II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) (r. 1575–1611).[^cite_note-\:1-9] It was an important city to the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) and [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary). The city played major roles in the [Bohemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Reformation) and [Protestant Reformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War), and in 20th-century history as the capital of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) between the World Wars and the post-war [Communist era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_era_of_Czechoslovakia).[^cite_note-10]

Prague is home to a number of cultural attractions including [Prague Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Castle), [Charles Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bridge), [Old Town Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Square) with the [Prague astronomical clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_astronomical_clock), the [Jewish Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josefov), [Petřín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet%C5%99%C3%ADn) hill, and [Vyšehrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vy%C5%A1ehrad). Since 1992, the historic centre of Prague has been included in the [UNESCO](21786641.md) list of [World Heritage Sites](44940.md). The city has more than ten major museums, along with numerous theatres, galleries, cinemas, and other historical exhibits. An extensive modern public transportation system connects the city. It is home to a wide range of public and private schools, including [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University), the oldest university in [Central Europe](5188.md).

Prague is classified as a "Beta+" [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) according to [GaWC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GaWC) studies.[^cite_note-11] Its rich history makes it a popular tourist destination, and as of 2017, the city receives more than 8.5 million international visitors annually. In 2017, Prague was listed as the fifth most visited European city after [London](17867.md), [Paris](22989.md), [Rome](25458.md), and [Istanbul](3391396.md).[^cite_note-12]

## Etymology and names

See also: [Names of European cities in different languages (M–P) § P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_European_cities_in_different_languages_\(M%E2%80%93P\)#P)

According to a popular legend, which first appeared in *[Chronica Boemorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronica_Boemorum)* (1119–1125) of [Cosmas of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmas_of_Prague), the Czech name *Praha* is derived from the Czech word *práh* (meaning '[threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshold_\(door\))'). In the 18th century, [Josef Dobrovský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Dobrovsk%C3%BD)'s theory was accepted, according to which the name is derived from the old [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages) word *práh*. This word means '[ford](30432825.md)' or '[rapid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapids)', referring to the city's origin at a crossing point of the Vltava river and to the rapids of the Vltava. Since the 19th century, the accepted theory has been that the name is derived from the old Czech verb *pražiti* ('to burn') and from *vypražený les* ('burned forest'), referring to a forest that was burned down so a settlement could be established there. In the mid-20th century, linguist Antonín Profous corrected the theory by saying that the name is indeed derived from *pražiti*, but this word also means 'to roast' (when speaking about the sun), and the name denoted a hot, arid place (related to the [shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale) bedrock where the Prague Castle was founded).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Prague is nicknamed the *"City of a Hundred [Spires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spire),"* based on a count by 19th-century mathematician [Bernard Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Bolzano); today's count is estimated by the Prague Information Service at 500.[^cite_note-15] Other nicknames for Prague also include the Golden City, the Mother of Cities and the Heart of Europe.[^cite_note-16]

The local Jewish community, which belongs to one of the oldest continuously existing in the world, has described the city as עיר ואם בישראל *Ir va-em be-yisrael*, "The city and mother in Israel".[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## History

Main article: [History of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Prague)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Prague).

Prague has grown from a settlement stretching from [Prague Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Castle) in the north to the fort of [Vyšehrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vy%C5%A1ehrad) in the south, to become the capital of a modern European country. With archaeological deposits over 10m deep, the city serves as a model for the implementation of comprehensive regulations for the protection of archaeological heritage throughout the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-19]

### Early history



The mythological princess [Libuše](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libu%C5%A1e) prophesies the glory of Prague.

The region was settled as early as the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) age.[^cite_note-Demetz1997-20] Jewish chronicler [David Solomon Ganz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gans), citing [Cyriacus Spangenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyriacus_Spangenberg), claimed that the city was founded as Boihaem in c. 1306 [BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Domini) by an ancient king, Boyya.[^cite_note-ganz-21]

Around the fifth and fourth century BC, a [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) tribe appeared in the area, later establishing settlements, including the largest Celtic [oppidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppidum) in [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia), Závist, in a present-day [Dolní Břežany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doln%C3%AD_B%C5%99e%C5%BEany) south of Prague, and naming the region of Bohemia, which means "home of the Boii people".[^cite_note-Demetz1997-20][^cite_note-bohemiaradio-22] In the last century BC, the Celts were slowly driven away by [Germanic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) ([Marcomanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcomanni), [Quadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadi), [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) and possibly the [Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebi)), leading some to place the seat of the [Marcomanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcomanni) king, [Maroboduus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroboduus), in Závist.[^cite_note-marobudradio-23][^cite_note-ganz-21] Around the area where present-day Prague stands, the 2nd century map drawn by Roman geographer [Ptolemaios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy) mentioned a Germanic city called *Casurgis*.[^cite_note-24]

In the late 5th century AD, during the great [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period) following the collapse of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire), the Germanic tribes living in Bohemia moved westwards and, probably in the 6th century, the [Slavic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavs) settled the Central Bohemian Region. In the following three centuries, the [Czech tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs) built several fortified settlements in the area, most notably in the [Šárka valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divok%C3%A1_%C5%A0%C3%A1rka), [Butovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinonice), and [Levý Hradec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev%C3%BD_Hradec).[^cite_note-Demetz1997-20]



A model representing [Prague Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Castle) and its surroundings in the 10th century

The construction of what came to be known as [Prague Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Castle) began near the end of the 9th century, expanding a fortified settlement that had existed on the site since the year 800.[^cite_note-25] The first masonry under Prague Castle dates from the year 885 at the latest.[^cite_note-26] The other prominent Prague fort, the Přemyslid fort [Vyšehrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vy%C5%A1ehrad), was founded in the 10th century, some 70 years later than Prague Castle.[^cite_note-27] Prague Castle is dominated by the [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vitus_Cathedral), which began construction in 1344, but was not completed until the 20th century.[^cite_note-28]

The legendary origins of Prague attribute its foundation to the 8th-century Czech duchess and prophetess [Libuše](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libu%C5%A1e) and her husband, [Přemysl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%99emysl,_the_Ploughman), founder of the [Přemyslid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%99emyslid_dynasty). Legend says that Libuše, prophesied from her castle at Vyšehrad, came out on a rocky cliff high above the Vltava and prophesied: "I see a great city whose glory will touch the stars". She ordered a castle and a town called Praha to be built on the site.[^cite_note-Demetz1997-20]

The region became the seat of the [dukes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke), and later [kings of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Bohemia). Under Duke of Bohemia [Boleslaus II the Pious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boleslaus_II,_Duke_of_Bohemia) the area became a [bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) in 973.[^cite_note-29] Until Prague was elevated to [archbishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishopric) in 1344, it was under the jurisdiction of the [Archbishopric of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Mainz).[^cite_note-30]

Prague was an important seat for trading where merchants from across Europe settled, including many Jews, as recalled in 965 by the [Hispano-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) merchant and traveller [Abraham ben Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_ben_Jacob).[^cite_note-31] The [Old New Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_New_Synagogue) of 1270 still stands in the city. Prague was also once home to a [slave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) market.[^cite_note-32]

At the site of the ford in the Vltava river, King [Vladislaus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladislaus_II,_Duke_of_Bohemia) had the first bridge built in 1170, the Judith Bridge (Juditin most), named in honour of his wife [Judith of Thuringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_of_Thuringia).[^cite_note-\:0-33] This bridge was destroyed by a flood in 1342, but some of the original foundation stones of that bridge remain in the river. It was rebuilt and named the Charles Bridge.[^cite_note-\:0-33]

In 1257, under King [Ottokar II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottokar_II_of_Bohemia), [Malá Strana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%A1_Strana) ('lesser quarter') was founded in Prague on the site of an older village in what would become the [Hradčany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrad%C4%8Dany) (Prague Castle) area.[^cite_note-34] This was the district of the German people, who had the right to administer the law autonomously, pursuant to [Magdeburg rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg_rights).[^cite_note-35] The new district was on the bank opposite of the [Staré Město](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_\(Prague\)) ("Old Town"), which had [borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough) status and was bordered by a line of walls and fortifications.

### Late Middle Ages



The current [St. Vitus Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vitus_Cathedral) in Prague was founded in 1344.

Prague flourished during the 14th-century reign (1346–1378) of [Charles IV, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) and the king of [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia) of the new [Luxembourg dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Luxembourg). As King of Bohemia and Holy Roman Emperor, he transformed Prague into an imperial capital. In the 1470s, Prague had around 70,000 inhabitants and with an area of 360 ha (\~1.4 square miles) it was the third-largest city in the Holy Roman Empire.[^cite_note-36]

Charles IV ordered the building of the [New Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Town,_Prague) (Nové Město) adjacent to the [Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_\(Prague\)) and laid out the design himself. The Charles Bridge, replacing the Judith Bridge destroyed in the flood just before his reign, was erected to connect the east bank districts to the Malá Strana and castle area. In 1347, he founded [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University), the [oldest university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation) in Central Europe.[^cite_note-37]

His father [John of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Bohemia) began construction of the [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) [Saint Vitus Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vitus_Cathedral), within the largest of the Prague Castle courtyards, on the site of the Romanesque rotunda. Prague was elevated to an archbishopric in 1344,[^cite_note-38] the year the cathedral was begun.

The city had a [mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_\(coin\)) and was a centre of trade for German and Italian bankers and merchants. The social order, however, became more turbulent due to the rising power of the [craft guilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_guilds) (themselves often torn by internal conflicts), and the increasing number of poor.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

The Hunger Wall, a substantial fortification wall south of Malá Strana and the castle area, was built during a famine in the 1360s. The work is reputed to have been ordered by Charles IV as a means of providing employment and food to the workers and their families.[^cite_note-41]

Charles IV died in 1378. During the reign of his son, King [Wenceslaus IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslaus,_King_of_the_Romans) (1378–1419), a period of intense turmoil ensued. During Easter 1389, members of the Prague clergy announced that Jews had desecrated the host (Eucharistic wafer), and the clergy encouraged mobs to pillage, ransack, and burn the Jewish quarter. Nearly the entire Jewish population of Prague (ca 750 people) was murdered.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]



The [Prague astronomical clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_astronomical_clock) was first installed in 1410, making it the third-oldest astronomical clock in the world and the oldest one still working.

[Jan Hus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Hus), a theologian and [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_\(academia\)) at Charles University, preached in Prague. In 1402, he began giving sermons in the [Bethlehem Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Chapel). Inspired by [John Wycliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wycliffe), these sermons focused on what were seen as radical reforms of a corrupt Church. Having become too dangerous for the political and religious establishment, Hus was summoned to the [Council of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Constance), put on trial for [heresy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heresy), and burned at the stake in [Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstanz) in 1415.

Four years later, Prague experienced its [first defenestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenestrations_of_Prague), when the people rebelled under the command of the Prague priest [Jan Želivský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_%C5%BDelivsk%C3%BD). Hus' death, coupled with Czech proto-nationalism and [proto-Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Protestantism), had spurred the [Hussite Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussite_Wars). Peasant rebels, led by the general [Jan Žižka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_%C5%BDi%C5%BEka), along with Hussite troops from Prague, defeated Emperor [Sigismund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) in the [Battle of Vítkov Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_V%C3%ADtkov_Hill) in 1420.

During the [Hussite Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussite_Wars), when Prague was attacked by "Crusader" and mercenary forces, the city militia fought bravely under the Prague Banner. This swallow-tailed banner is approximately 4 by 6 ft (1.2 by 1.8 m), with a red field sprinkled with small white fleurs-de-lis, and a silver old Town Coat-of-Arms in the centre. The words "PÁN BŮH POMOC NAŠE" (The Lord is our Relief/Help) appeared above the coat-of-arms, with a Hussite chalice centred on the top. Near the swallow-tails is a crescent-shaped golden sun with rays protruding.

One of these banners was captured by Swedish troops during the [Battle of Prague (1648)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Prague_\(1648\)) when they captured the western bank of the [Vltava river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vltava_river) and were repulsed from the eastern bank; it was placed in the [Royal Military Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Army_Museum) in [Stockholm](26741.md). Although this flag still exists, it is in very poor condition. They also took the [Codex Gigas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Gigas) and the [Codex Argenteus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Argenteus). The earliest evidence indicates that a [gonfalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonfalon) with a municipal charge painted on it was used for the Old Town as early as 1419. Since this city militia flag was in use before 1477 and during the Hussite Wars, it is the oldest still preserved municipal flag of Bohemia.

In the following two centuries, Prague strengthened its role as a merchant city. Many noteworthy Gothic buildings[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] were erected and [Vladislav Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladislav_Hall) of the Prague Castle was added.

### Habsburg era



Depiction of the "Prague Banner" (municipal flag dated to the 16th century)

[^cite_note-46]



The [coat of arms of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Prague) (1649)

[^cite_note-Vojtisek-1]

In 1526, the Bohemian estates elected [Ferdinand I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) of the [House of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg). The fervent Catholicism of its members brought them into conflict in Bohemia, and then in Prague, where Protestant ideas were gaining popularity.[^cite_note-47] These problems were not preeminent under Holy Roman Emperor [Rudolf II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), elected King of Bohemia in 1576, who chose Prague as his home. He lived in Prague Castle, where his court welcomed not only astrologers and magicians but also scientists, musicians, and artists. Rudolf was an art lover as well, and Prague became a capital of European culture. This was a prosperous period for the city: famous people living there in that age include the astronomers [Tycho Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe) and [Johannes Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kepler), the painter [Arcimboldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Arcimboldo), the alchemists [Edward Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Kelley) and [John Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dee), the poet [Elizabeth Jane Weston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Jane_Weston), and others.

In 1618, the famous [second defenestration of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenestrations_of_Prague) provoked the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War), a particularly harsh period for Prague and Bohemia. [Ferdinand II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) of Habsburg was deposed, and his place as King of Bohemia taken by [Frederick V, Elector Palatine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_V,_Elector_Palatine); however, his army was crushed in the [Battle of White Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_White_Mountain) (1620) not far from the city. Following this, in 1621, was the execution of 27 Czech Protestant leaders (involved in the uprising) in Old Town Square and the exile of many others. Prague was forcibly converted back to [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism), followed by the rest of the Czech lands. The city suffered subsequently during the war under an attack by [Electorate of Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Saxony) (1631) and during the [Battle of Prague (1648)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Prague_\(1648\)).[^cite_note-48] Prague began a steady decline, which reduced the population from the 60,000 it had had in the years before the war to 20,000. In the second half of the 17th century, Prague's population began to grow again. [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_the_Czech_Republic) had been in Prague since the end of the 10th century and, by 1708, they accounted for about a quarter of Prague's population.[^cite_note-49]

In 1689, a great fire devastated Prague, which spurred a renovation and a rebuilding of the city. In 1713–14, a major outbreak of [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_\(disease\)) hit Prague one last time, killing 12,000 to 13,000 people.[^cite_note-50]



[Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franti%C5%A1ek_Palack%C3%BD_Monument,_Prague) to [František Palacký](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franti%C5%A1ek_Palack%C3%BD), a significant member of the Czech National Revival, created by [Stanislav Sucharda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislav_Sucharda)

[^cite_note-51]

In 1744, during the [Austrian War of Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_War_of_Succession), [Frederick the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_the_Great) of Prussia invaded Bohemia. He took Prague after a severe and prolonged siege in the course of which a large part of the town was destroyed.[^cite_note-EB1911-52] [Empress Maria Theresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa) expelled the Jews from Prague in 1745; though she rescinded the expulsion in 1748, the proportion of Jewish residents in the city never recovered.[^cite_note-53] In 1757, during the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War), [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) bombardment[^cite_note-EB1911-52] destroyed more than one-quarter of the city and heavily damaged St. Vitus Cathedral. However, a month later, Frederick the Great was defeated and forced to retreat from Bohemia.

The economy of Prague continued to improve during the 18th century. The population increased to 80,000 inhabitants by 1771. Many rich merchants and nobles enhanced the city with a host of palaces, churches, and gardens full of art and music, creating the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) city features.

In 1784, under [Joseph II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), the four municipalities of Malá Strana, Nové Město, Staré Město, and Hradčany were merged into a single entity. The Jewish district, called [Josefov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josefov), was included only in 1850. The [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) produced great changes and developments in Prague, as new factories could take advantage of the coal mines and ironworks of the nearby regions. The first general hospital was founded in 1789, with a reconstructed building of the Institute for Noble Women in the Church of the Holy Angels (now [Všeobecná fakultní nemocnice v Praze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C5%A1eobecn%C3%A1_fakultn%C3%AD_nemocnice_v_Praze?action=edit\&redlink=1)) used for it. The first suburb, [Karlín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl%C3%ADn), was created in 1817, and twenty years later the population exceeded 100,000.

[The revolutions in Europe in 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848) also touched Prague, but they were fiercely suppressed. In the following years, the [Czech National Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_National_Revival) began its rise, until it gained the majority in the town council in 1861. Prague had a large number of German speakers in 1848, but by 1880 the number of German speakers had decreased to 14 percent (42,000), and by 1910 to 6.7 percent (37,000), due to a massive increase in the city's overall population caused by the influx of [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs) from the rest of Bohemia and [Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravia) and the increasing prestige and importance of the Czech language as part of the Czech National Revival. In 1891, the city council ordered that all German inscriptions be removed in Prague, while Czechs at the same time demanded equal status for the Czech and German languages in Bohemia and Moravia.[^cite_note-54]

### 20th century



[Statue of Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Garrigue_Masaryk,_Prague) near [Prague Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Castle)

#### First Czechoslovak Republic

Main article: [First Czechoslovak Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Czechoslovak_Republic)

World War I ended with the defeat of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire) and the creation of Czechoslovakia. Prague was chosen as its capital and Prague Castle as the seat of the president [Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Masaryk). At this time, Prague was a true European capital with a highly developed industry. By 1930, the population had risen to 850,000.

#### Second World War



The Red Army [enters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_offensive) Prague in May 1945.

Further information: [German occupation of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Czechoslovakia)

[Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler) ordered the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_\(Wehrmacht\)) to enter Prague on 15 March 1939, and from Prague Castle proclaimed [Bohemia and Moravia a German protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia). For most of its history, Prague had been a multi-ethnic city[^cite_note-TOT1-55] with important Czech, German and (mostly native German-speaking) Jewish populations.[^cite_note-56] From 1939, when the country was occupied by [Nazi Germany](21212.md), Hitler took over Prague Castle. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), most Jews were [deported and killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) by the Germans. In 1942, Prague was witness to the assassination of one of the most powerful men in [Nazi Germany](21212.md)—[Reinhard Heydrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhard_Heydrich)—during [Operation Anthropoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Anthropoid), accomplished by Czechoslovak national heroes [Jozef Gabčík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jozef_Gab%C4%8D%C3%ADk) and [Jan Kubiš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kubi%C5%A1). Hitler ordered bloody reprisals.[^cite_note-57]

In February 1945, [Prague suffered several bombing raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_Bombing_of_Prague) by the [US Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces). 701 people were killed, more than 1,000 people were injured and some buildings, factories and historic landmarks ([Emmaus Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmaus_Monastery), [Faust House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faust_House_\(Prague\)), [Vinohrady Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinohrady_Synagogue?action=edit\&redlink=1)) were destroyed.[^cite_note-Bombing-58] Many historic structures in Prague, however, escaped the destruction of the war and the damage was small compared to the destruction of many other cities in that time. According to American pilots, it was the result of a navigational mistake. In March, a deliberate raid targeted military factories in Prague, killing about 370 people.[^cite_note-59]

On 5 May 1945, two days before Germany capitulated, an [uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_uprising) against Germany occurred. Several thousand Czechs were killed in four days of bloody street fighting, with many atrocities committed by both sides. Fought concurrently with the Prague uprising, the [Prague offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_offensive) significantly helped the [liberation of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Czechoslovakia_in_1945). At daybreak on 9 May, the [3rd Shock Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Shock_Army) of the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) took the capital city almost unopposed. The majority (about 50,000 people) of the German population of Prague either fled or were [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Germans_from_Czechoslovakia) by the [Beneš decrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bene%C5%A1_decrees) in the aftermath of the war.

#### Cold War

Main articles: [History of Czechoslovakia (1948–1989)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Czechoslovakia_\(1948%E2%80%931989\)) and [Czechoslovak Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Socialist_Republic)



Velvet Revolution in November 1989

Prague was a city in a country under the military, economic, and political control of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (see [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain) and [COMECON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COMECON)). The world's largest [Stalin Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin_Monument_\(Prague\)) was unveiled on [Letná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letn%C3%A1) hill in 1955 and destroyed in 1962. The 4th Czechoslovak Writers' Congress, held in the city in June 1967, took a strong position against the regime.[^cite_note-60] On 31 October 1967 students demonstrated at [Strahov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strahov_\(district_of_Prague\)). This spurred the new secretary of the [Czechoslovak Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Communist_Party), [Alexander Dubček](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dub%C4%8Dek), to proclaim a new deal in his city's and country's life, starting the short-lived season of the "[socialism with a human face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_with_a_human_face)". It was the [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring), which aimed at the renovation of political institutions in a democratic way. The other [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) member countries, except [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania) and [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Albania), were led by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) to repress these reforms through the [invasion of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact_invasion_of_Czechoslovakia) and the capital, Prague, on 21 August 1968. The invasion, chiefly by infantry and tanks, effectively suppressed any further attempts at reform. The military occupation of Czechoslovakia by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) would end only in 1991.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] [Jan Palach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Palach) and [Jan Zajíc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Zaj%C3%ADc) committed suicide by [self-immolation](916669.md) in January and February 1969 to protest against the "[normalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalization_\(Czechoslovakia\))" of the country.

### After the Velvet Revolution



Prague high-rise buildings at [Pankrác](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankr%C3%A1c)

In 1989, after riot police beat back a peaceful student demonstration, the [Velvet Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Revolution) crowded the streets of Prague, and the capital of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_and_Slovak_Federative_Republic) benefited greatly from the new mood. In 1992, the [Historic Centre of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Centre_of_Prague) and its monuments were inscribed as a cultural [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md). In 1993, after the [Velvet Divorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Divorce), Prague became the capital city of the new Czech Republic. Since the 1990s, high-rise buildings have been built in Prague in large quantities. In the late 1990s, Prague again became an important cultural centre of Europe and was notably influenced by [globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalisation).[^cite_note-63] In 2000, the [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF) and [World Bank](36752.md) summits took place in Prague and [anti-globalisation riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-globalization_protests_in_Prague) took place here. In 2002, Prague suffered from [widespread floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_European_floods) that damaged buildings and its underground transport system.

In 2012, Prague's main airport, [Prague Ruzyně International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Ruzyn%C4%9B_International_Airport) was renamed Václav Havel Airport, in honour of the Czech Republic's former first president [Václav Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel), who died previously in late 2011.&#x20;

Prague [launched a bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_bid_for_the_2016_Summer_Olympics) for the [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics),[^cite_note-64] but failed to make the candidate city [shortlist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortlist). In June 2009, as the result of financial pressures from the [global recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession), Prague's officials chose to cancel the city's planned bid for the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-65]

On 21 December 2023, [a mass shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Prague_shootings) took place at [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) in central Prague. Fifteen people were killed and 25 were injured. It was the deadliest mass murder in the history of the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-66]

## Geography

Prague is situated on the [Vltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vltava) River. The [Berounka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berounka) flows into the Vltava in the suburbs of [Lahovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahovice). There are 99 watercourses in Prague with a total length of 360 km (220 mi). The longest streams are Rokytka and Botič.[^cite_note-water-67]

There are 4 reservoirs, 47 fishponds, 33 retention reservoirs, and dry polders in the city. The largest pond is Velký Počernický rybník with 41.76 ha (103.2 acres).[^cite_note-water-67] The largest body of water is Hostivař Reservoir with 42 hectares (103.8 acres).[^cite_note-68]

[In terms of geomorphological division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphological_division_of_the_Czech_Republic), most of Prague is located in the [Prague Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Plateau). In the south, the city's territory extends into the [Hořovice Uplands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%C5%99ovice_Uplands?action=edit\&redlink=1), in the north it extends into the [Central Elbe Table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Elbe_Table) lowland. The highest point is the top of the hill Teleček on the western border of Prague, at 399 m (1,309 ft) above sea level. Notable hills in the centre of Prague are Petřín with 327 m (1,073 ft) and Vítkov with 270 m (890 ft). The lowest point is the Vltava in [Suchdol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suchdol_\(Prague\)) at the place where it leaves the city, at 172 m (564 ft).[^cite_note-69]

Prague is located approximately at [50°5′N 14°25′E  /  50.083°N 14.417°E  / 50.083; 14.417](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Prague\&params=50_5_N_14_25_E_). Prague is approximately at the same latitude as [Frankfurt](10992.md), Germany;[^cite_note-70] [Paris](22989.md), France;[^cite_note-71] and [Vancouver](32706.md), Canada.[^cite_note-72] The northernmost point is at [50°10′39″N 14°31′37″E  /  50.17750°N 14.52694°E  / 50.17750; 14.52694](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Prague\&params=50_10_39_N_14_31_37_E_), the southernmost point is at [49°56′31″N 14°23′44″E  /  49.94194°N 14.39556°E  / 49.94194; 14.39556](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Prague\&params=49_56_31_N_14_23_44_E_), the westernmost point is at [50°6′14″N 14°13′31″E  /  50.10389°N 14.22528°E  / 50.10389; 14.22528](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Prague\&params=50_6_14_N_14_13_31_E_), and the easternmost point is at [50°5′14″N 14°42′23″E  /  50.08722°N 14.70639°E  / 50.08722; 14.70639](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Prague\&params=50_5_14_N_14_42_23_E_).[^cite_note-73] Farthest geographical points of the city territory are marked physically by so-called [Prague Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Poles).

### Climate



Prague seen from [satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPOT_\(satellite\))

Prague has an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*; [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dobk*)[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] bordering on a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dfb*), defined as such by the 0 °C (32 °F) isotherm.[^cite_note-76] The winters are relatively cold with average temperatures at about freezing point (0 °C), and with very little sunshine. Snow cover can be common between mid-November and late March, although snow accumulations of more than 200 mm (8 in) are infrequent. There are also a few periods of mild temperatures in winter. Summers usually bring plenty of sunshine and temperatures around 25 °C (77 °F), but there has been a considerable increase in days with over 30 °C (86 °F) in later years. Nights can be quite cool even in summer, though. Precipitation in Prague is low by Czech standards (about 500 mm per year) since it is located in the [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) of the [Sudetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudetes) and other mountain ranges. The driest season is usually winter, while late spring and summer can bring quite heavy rain, especially in the form of thundershowers. The number of hours of average sunshine has increased over time. [Temperature inversions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inversion_\(meteorology\)) are relatively common between mid-October and mid-March, bringing foggy, cold days and sometimes moderate air pollution. Prague is also a windy city with common sustained western winds and an average wind speed of 16 km/h (10 mph) that often helps break temperature inversions and clear the air in cold months.&#x20;

## Administration and politics

### Administrative division



Map of Prague cadastral areas and administrative districts

Main article: [Districts of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Prague)

Prague is the capital of the Czech Republic and, as such, is the regular seat of its central authorities. Since 24 November 1990, it is *[de facto](43594.md)* again a statutory city, but has a specific status of the municipality and the region at the same time. Prague also houses the administrative institutions of the [Central Bohemian Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bohemian_Region).



Mayor Bohuslav Svoboda

Until 1949, all administrative districts of Prague were formed by the whole one or more cadastral units, municipalities, or towns. Since 1949, there has been a fundamental change in the administrative division. Since then, the boundaries of many urban districts, administrative districts, and city districts are independent of the boundaries of cadastral territories, and some cadastral territories are thus divided into administrative and self-governing parts of the city. [Cadastral area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadastre) (for example, [Vinohrady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinohrady) and [Smíchov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sm%C3%ADchov)) are still relevant, especially for the registration of land and real estate and house numbering.

Prague is divided into 10 municipal districts (1–10), 22 administrative districts (1–22), 57 municipal parts, and 112 cadastral areas.



Prague New City Hall

### City government

Prague is administered by the autonomous [Prague City Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_City_Assembly), which consists of 65 members and is elected through municipal elections. As of Dec. 20, 18 members of the council were from the centre-right [Together Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPOLU_\(Czech_Republic\)) group, which governs the city in conjunction with 12 members of the [Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Pirate_Party) (centre to centre-left) and 5 members of the [Mayors and Independents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayors_and_Independents) group (centre to centre-right).[^cite_note-k218-83][^cite_note-\:4-84] Thus, the governing coalition has a comfortable majority in the assembly with 35 of 65 seats. Opposition parties include [ANO 2011](40808475.md) (right-wing populist) with 14 members, [Prague Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Together) (centre) with 11 votes, [Freedom and Direct Democracy](46867556.md) (right-wing to far-right) with 3 members, and 2 unaffiliated Independents.[^cite_note-\:4-84]

The executive body of Prague, elected by the Assembly, is [Prague City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_City_Council). The municipal office of Prague is at [Prague City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_City_Hall) and has 11 members, including the [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Prague). It prepares proposals for the Assembly meetings and ensures that adopted resolutions are fulfilled. The [Mayor of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Prague) is [Civic Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Democratic_Party_\(Czech_Republic\)) member [Bohuslav Svoboda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohuslav_Svoboda).

## Demographics



Prague population pyramid in 2021

### 2011 census

Even though the official population of Prague hovers around 1.3 million as of the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Czech_census), the city's real population is much higher due to only 65% of its residents having registered in the city.[^cite_note-86] Data taken from mobile phone movements around the city suggest that the real population of Prague is closer to 1.9 or 2.0 million, with an additional 300,000 to 400,000 commuters coming to the city on weekdays for work, education, or commerce.[^cite_note-87]

About 14% of the city's residents were born outside the Czech Republic, the highest ratio anywhere in the country.[^cite_note-88] However, 64.8% of the city's population self-identified as ethnically [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs), which is slightly higher than the national average of 63.7%. Almost 29% of respondents declined to answer the question on ethnicity at all, so it may be assumed that the real percentage of ethnic Czechs in Prague is considerably higher. The largest ethnic minority are [Slovaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks_in_the_Czech_Republic), followed by Ukrainians and Russians.[^cite_note-auto-89]

Prague's population is the oldest and best-educated in the country. It has the lowest proportion of children. Only 10.8% of census respondents claimed adherence to a religion; the majority of these were [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholics).[^cite_note-auto-89]

The city is traditionally one of the cultural centres of Europe, hosting many cultural events. Some of the significant cultural institutions include the [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_\(Prague\)) (*Národní Divadlo*) and the [Estates Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates_Theatre) (*Stavovské or Tylovo* or *Nosticovo divadlo*), where the premières of [Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart)'s *[Don Giovanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Giovanni)* and *[La clemenza di Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_clemenza_di_Tito)* were held. Other major cultural institutions are the [Rudolfinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolfinum), which is home to the [Czech Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Philharmonic), and the [Municipal House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_House), which is home to the [Prague Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Symphony_Orchestra). The [Prague State Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Opera_\(Prague\)) (Státní opera) performs at the Smetana Theatre.

The city has many world-class museums, including the [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_\(Prague\)) (*Národní muzeum*), the [Museum of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Prague_Museum) (*Muzeum Prahy*), the [Jewish Museum in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Museum_in_Prague), the [Alfons Mucha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonse_Mucha) Museum, the [Prague Postal Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Postal_Museum), the African-Prague Museum, the [Museum of Decorative Arts in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Decorative_Arts_in_Prague), the [Náprstek Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A1prstek_Museum) (*Náprstkovo muzeum*), the [Josef Sudek Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Sudek_Gallery) and [The Josef Sudek Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Josef_Sudek_Studio), the [National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clementinum), the [National Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_Prague), which manages the largest collection of art in the Czech Republic and the Kunsthalle Praha, the newest museum in the city.[^cite_note-94]

There are hundreds of concert halls, galleries, cinemas, and music clubs in the city. It hosts [music festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_festivals) including the [Prague Spring International Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring_International_Music_Festival), the [Prague Autumn International Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Autumn_International_Music_Festival), the [Prague International Organ Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_International_Organ_Festival), the Dvořák Prague International Music Festival,[^cite_note-95] and the [Prague International Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_International_Jazz_Festival). Film festivals include Bohemia Film Awards, the [Febiofest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Febiofest), the [One World Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_Film_Festival), and Echoes of the [Karlovy Vary International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovy_Vary_International_Film_Festival). The city also hosts the [Prague Writers' Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Writers'_Festival), the Prague Folklore Days, Prague Advent Choral Meeting the [Summer Shakespeare Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Shakespeare_Festival),[^cite_note-96] the [Prague Fringe Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Fringe_Festival), the [World Roma Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Roma_Festival), as well as the hundreds of [Vernissages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernissage) and [fashion shows](8312977.md).

With the growth of low-cost airlines in Europe, Prague has become a weekend city destination, allowing tourists to visit its museums and cultural sites as well as try its Czech beers and cuisine.

## Economy



[Žižkov Television Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDi%C5%BEkov_Television_Tower) with crawling "[babies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babies_\(%C4%8Cern%C3%BD\))"

Prague's economy accounts for 25% of the Czech GDP,[^cite_note-CDAP-106] making it the highest performing regional economy of the country. As of 2021, its GDP per capita in [purchasing power standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) is €58,216, making it the [third best performing region in the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_Union_regions_by_GDP) at 203 per cent of the EU-27 average in 2021.[^cite_note-107]

Prague employs almost a fifth of the entire Czech workforce, and its wages are significantly above average (≈+20%). In 4Q/2020, during the pandemic, average salaries available in Prague reached CZK 45.944 (≈[€](9472.md)1,800) per month, an annual increase of 4%, which was nevertheless lower than the national increase of 6.5% both in nominal and real terms. (Inflation in the Czech Republic was 3.2% in 4Q/2020.)[^cite_note-RHDP-108][^cite_note-109] Since 1990, the city's economic structure has shifted from industrial to service-oriented. Industry is present in sectors such as pharmaceuticals, printing, food processing, the manufacture of transport equipment, computer technology, and electrical engineering. In the service sector, financial and commercial services, trade, restaurants, hospitality, and public administration are the most significant. [Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_\(economics\)) account for around 80 per cent of employment. There are 800,000 employees in Prague, including 120,000 commuters.[^cite_note-CDAP-106] The number of (legally registered) foreign residents in Prague has been increasing despite the country's economic downturn. As of March 2010, 148,035 foreign workers were reported to be living in the city, making up about 18 per cent of the workforce, up from 131,132 in 2008.[^cite_note-110] Approximately one-fifth of all investment in the Czech Republic takes place in the city.



Na příkopě, the most expensive street among the states of V4



Tourism is a significant part of the city's economy.

Almost one-half of the national income from tourism is spent in Prague. The city offers approximately 73,000 beds in accommodation facilities, most of which were built after 1990, including almost 51,000 beds in hotels and boarding houses.

From the late 1990s to the late 2000s, the city was a common filming location for international productions such as Hollywood and Bollywood motion pictures. A combination of architecture, low costs, and the existing motion picture infrastructure has proven attractive to international film production companies.

The modern economy of Prague is largely service and export-based, and, in a 2010 survey, the city was named the best city in Central and Eastern Europe (CEE) for business.[^cite_note-111]

In 2005, Prague was deemed among the three best cities in Central and Eastern Europe according to *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)*'s livability rankings.[^cite_note-112] The city was named as a top-tier nexus city for innovation across multiple sectors of the global innovation economy, placing 29th globally out of 289 cities, ahead of [Brussels](3708.md) and [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) for innovation in 2010.[^cite_note-113]

[Na příkopě](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na_p%C5%99%C3%ADkop%C4%9B) is the most expensive street among all the states of the [V4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visegr%C3%A1d_Group).[^cite_note-114] In 2017, with the amount of rent €2,640 (CZK 67,480) per square metre per year, ranked on 22nd place among the most expensive streets in the world.[^cite_note-115] The second most expensive is Pařížská street.

In the Eurostat research, Prague ranked fifth among Europe's 271 regions in terms of gross domestic product per inhabitant, achieving 172 per cent of the EU average. It ranked just above Paris and well above the country as a whole, which achieved 80 per cent of the EU average.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

Since [the fall of the Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989), Prague has become one of the world's most popular tourist destinations. Prague [suffered considerably less](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Prague) damage during [World War II](32927.md) than some other major cities in the region, allowing most of its historic architecture to stay true to form. It contains one of the world's most pristine and varied collections of architecture, from [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_art), to [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture), [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance), [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque), [Rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo), [Neo-Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Revival_architecture), [Neo-Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival_architecture), [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau), [Cubist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism), [Neo-Classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism) and ultra-modern.

Prague is classified as a "Beta+" [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) according to [GaWC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GaWC) studies, comparable to [Vienna](55866.md), [Manila](184334.md) and [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)[^cite_note-119] Prague ranked sixth in the [Tripadvisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripadvisor) world list of best destinations in 2016.[^cite_note-tripadvisor.com-120] Its rich history makes it a popular tourist destination, and the city receives more than 8.4 million international visitors annually, as of 2017. Furthermore, the city was ranked 7th in the world [ICCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_and_Convention_Association) Destination Performance Index, measuring performance of conference tourism in 2021.[^cite_note-121] As of December 2024 the city is ranked 3rd among the best places to visit during Christmas.[^cite_note-122]

An iconic part of the city that made a comeback in 2025 is Prague's Matějská pouť (St. Matthew's fun fair) [Cyclone roller coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mat%C4%9Bjsk%C3%A1_pou%C5%A5_s_Cyclone_roller_coaster). Originally constructed in 1963 it was an attraction at the Výstaviště fairgrounds. In 2018, it was removed due to urban revitalisation plans. The Štaubert family, custodians of the ride, undertook an extensive restoration, dismantling and refurbishing the structure in Chotouchov near [Kolín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kol%C3%ADn). The renovated Cyclone restarted for the 2025 Matějská pouť.[^cite_note-123]

Prague is also home to [Klementinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clementinum), the oldest meteorological station in the Czech Republic with a continuous series of daily temperature measurements since 1775, although occasional measurements began another 20 years earlier. Klementinum is most likely the second-oldest meteorological station in Europe at which temperatures have been measured continuously to this day.&#x20;

## Science, research and hi-tech centres

See also: [Category\:Science and technology in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Science_and_technology_in_the_Czech_Republic)



[EUSPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Agency_for_the_Space_Programme) headquarters of the [Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_\(satellite_navigation\)) system in Prague's [Holešovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hole%C5%A1ovice)

The Prague metropolitan area is an important centre of research. It is the seat of 39 out of 54 institutes of the [Czech Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Academy_of_Sciences), including the largest ones, the Institute of Physics, the Institute of Microbiology, and the Institute of Organic Chemistry and Biochemistry. It is also a seat of 10 public research institutes, four [business incubators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_incubator) and large hospitals performing [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) activities such as the [Motol University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motol_University_Hospital) or [Institute for Clinical and Experimental Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Clinical_and_Experimental_Medicine), which was the largest transplant centre in [Europe](9239.md) as of 2019.[^cite_note-134] Universities seated in Prague (see section *Colleges and Universities*) also represent important centres of science and research activities.

As of 2008, there were 13,000 researchers (out of 30,000 in the country, counted in full-time equivalents), representing a 3% share of Prague's economically active population. Gross expenditure on research and development accounted for €901.3 million (41.5% of the country's total).[^cite_note-135]

Some well-known multinational companies have established research and development facilities in Prague, among them [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens), [Honeywell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeywell), [Oracle](22591.md), [Microsoft](19001.md), and [Broadcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcom_Inc.).

Prague was selected to host the administration of the EU satellite navigation system [Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_\(satellite_navigation\)). It started to provide its first services in December 2016, and full completion is expected by 2020.

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Prague)



Škoda 15 T, tram of the Prague tram system

The public transport infrastructure consists of the heavily used [Prague Integrated Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Integrated_Transport) (PID, *Pražská integrovaná doprava*) system, with the [Prague Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Metro) (lines [A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_A_\(Prague_Metro\)), [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_B_\(Prague_Metro\)), and [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_C_\(Prague_Metro\))) at the network's core. The metro's total length is 65 km (40 mi) with 61 stations in total.&#x20;

Part of the PID are also the [Prague tram system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Prague), [Prague bus service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses_in_Prague), [commuter trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esko_Prague), [funiculars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular), and seven [ferries](50771.md). Prague has one of the highest rates of public transport usage in the world,[^cite_note-136] with 1.2 billion passenger journeys per year.



[Barrandov Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrandov_Bridge), part of the Prague Inner Ring Road

Inner and outer ring roads carry the main flow of traffic leads through the centre of the city, and around its perimeter. The Inner Ring Road (The City Ring "MO") surrounds central Prague. It is the longest city tunnel in Europe with a length of 5.5 km (3.4 mi), and five interchanges have been completed to relieve congestion in the north-western part of Prague.&#x20;

The [D0 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D0_motorway_\(Czech_Republic\)) (Outer Ring Road) which, when finished, will connect all major motorways that meet each other in the Prague region and provide faster transit without the necessity to drive through the city. So far 39 km (24 mi), out of a total planned 83 km (52 mi), is in operation. As of 2021, the next 12 km (7 mi) section between [Modletice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modletice) and [Běchovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C4%9Bchovice) is planned to be completed in 2025.[^cite_note-137]

The city forms the hub of the [České dráhy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cesk%C3%A9_dr%C3%A1hy) system, with services to all parts of the country and abroad. The railway system links Prague with major European cities. Travel times range between 2 hours to Dresden and 13 hours to Zurich.[^cite_note-138]

Prague's main international railway station is [Praha hlavní nádraží](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praha_hlavn%C3%AD_n%C3%A1dra%C5%BE%C3%AD);[^cite_note-139] rail services are also available from other main stations: [Praha Masarykovo nádraží](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praha_Masarykovo_n%C3%A1dra%C5%BE%C3%AD), [Praha-Holešovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praha-Hole%C5%A1ovice_railway_station) and [Praha-Smíchov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praha-Sm%C3%ADchov_railway_station), in addition to suburban stations. Commuter rail services operate under the name [Esko Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esko_Prague), which is part of the PID.



[Václav Havel Airport Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel_Airport_Prague) is one of the busiest airports in [central Europe](5188.md), carrying 17.8 million passengers in 2025.

Prague is served by one international airport: [Václav Havel Airport Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel_Airport_Prague), the largest airport in the Czech Republic and one of the largest and busiest airports in Central and Eastern Europe. Other airports in Prague include the city's original airport of [Prague-Kbely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague-Kbely_Airport) in the [eponymous district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_19), which is serviced by the [Czech Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Air_Force). It also houses the [Kbely Aviation Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kbely_Aviation_Museum). The nearby Letňany Airport is mainly used for private aviation and aeroclub aviation.

A small percentage of people commute by [bike in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Prague), depending on the season. Cycling is very common as a sport or recreation.[^cite_note-140] Bikesharing is partially connected to the public transportation network and subsidised by the city.[^cite_note-141]

## International relations



[Petřín Lookout Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet%C5%99%C3%ADn_Lookout_Tower), an observation tower inspired by the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower) in Paris, France, and built at Petřín hill

The city of Prague maintains its own EU delegation in [Brussels](3708.md) called Prague House.[^cite_note-145]

Prague was the location of [U.S. President](24113.md) [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama)'s speech on 5 April 2009, which led to the [New START](26709878.md) treaty with Russia, signed in Prague on 8 April 2010.[^cite_note-146]

The annual conference [Forum 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_2000), which was founded by former Czech President [Václav Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel), Japanese philanthropist [Yōhei Sasakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%8Dhei_Sasakawa), and Nobel Peace Prize laureate [Elie Wiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Wiesel) in 1996, is held in Prague. Its main objective is "to identify the key issues facing civilization and to explore ways to prevent the escalation of conflicts that have religion, culture, or ethnicity as their primary components", and also intends to promote democracy in non-democratic countries and to support civil society. Conferences have attracted several prominent thinkers, Nobel laureates, former and acting politicians, business leaders, and other individuals like: [Frederik Willem de Klerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._W._de_Klerk), [Bill Clinton](3356.md), [Nicholas Winton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Winton), [Oscar Arias Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Arias_S%C3%A1nchez), [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama), [Hans Küng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_K%C3%BCng), [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres) and [Madeleine Albright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeleine_Albright).

### Namesakes

Several settlements in the United States are derived from or similar to the name of Prague. In many of these cases, Czech emigration has left several namesake cities scattered over the globe, with a notable concentration in the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World). These settlements include [Praha, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praha,_Texas),[^cite_note-157] [Prague, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague,_Oklahoma),[^cite_note-158] [Prague, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague,_Nebraska),[^cite_note-159] and [New Prague, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Prague,_Minnesota).[^cite_note-160]

Additionally, [Kłodzko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%82odzko) is sometimes referred to as "Little Prague" ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Klein-Prag*). Although now in [Poland](22936.md), it had been traditionally a part of [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia) until 1763 when it became part of [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia).[^cite_note-161]
