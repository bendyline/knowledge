The **Maldives**,[^cite_note-9] officially the **Republic of Maldives**,[^cite_note-10] and historically known as the **Maldive Islands**, is an [archipelagic country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelagic_state) in [South Asia](21566765.md), located in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), near the southeastern boundary of the [Arabian Sea](2747.md). The Maldives is located southwest of [India](14533.md) and [Sri Lanka](26750.md), about 750 kilometres (470 miles; 400 nautical miles) from the Asian continent's mainland. The Maldives' chain of [26 atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atolls_of_the_Maldives) stretches across the equator from [Ihavandhippolhu Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atolls_of_the_Maldives#Ihavandhippolhu) in the north to [Addu Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seenu_Atoll) in the south.

The Maldives is the smallest [country in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_and_dependent_territories_in_Asia). Its land area is only 298 square kilometres (115 sq mi), but this is spread over roughly 90,000 square kilometres (35,000 sq mi) of the sea, making it one of the world's most spatially dispersed sovereign states. With a population of 515,132 in the 2022 census, it is the second [least populous country in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_countries_by_population) and the [ninth-smallest country by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area), but also one of the [most densely populated countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population_density). The Maldives has an average ground-level elevation of around 1.5 metres (4 ft 11 in) above sea level,[^cite_note-guardian.co.uk-11] and a [highest natural point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_elevation_extremes_by_country) of only 2.4 metres (7 ft 10 in), making it the world's lowest-lying country. Some sources state the highest point, [Mount Villingili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villingili_\(Addu_Atoll\)), as 5.1 metres or 17 feet.[^cite_note-guardian.co.uk-11]

[Malé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%A9) is the capital and the most populated city, traditionally called the "King's Island", where the ancient [royal dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Maldivian_monarchs) ruled from its central location.[^cite_note-12] The Maldives has been inhabited for over 2,500 years. Documented contact with the outside world began around 947 CE when [Arab](2185.md) travellers began visiting the islands. In the 12th century, partly due to the importance of the Arabs and [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) as traders in the Indian Ocean, Islam reached the Maldivian Archipelago.[^cite_note-13] The Maldives was soon consolidated as a [sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_the_Maldive_Islands), developing strong commercial and cultural ties with Asia and Africa. From the mid-16th century, the region came under the increasing influence of European [colonial powers](7299.md), with the Maldives becoming a British [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) in 1887. [Independence from the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_the_Maldives) came in 1965, and a [presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) was established in 1968 with an elected People's Majlis. The ensuing decades have seen political instability, efforts at democratic reform,[^cite_note-14] and environmental challenges posed by [climate change and rising sea levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_the_Maldives).[^cite_note-15] The Maldives became a founding member of the [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation) (SAARC).

[Fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry_in_the_Maldives) has historically been the dominant economic activity, and remains the second largest sector, behind the rapidly growing [tourism](413341.md) industry. The Maldives rates "high" on the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index),[^cite_note-HDI-16] with a *per capita* income significantly higher than other SAARC nations.[^cite_note-17] The World Bank classifies the Maldives as having an upper-[middle income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class) economy.[^cite_note-18]

The Maldives is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md), the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations), the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md), and the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement), and is a Dialogue Partner of the [Shanghai Cooperation Organisation](152422.md).[^cite_note-19] It temporarily withdrew from the Commonwealth in October 2016 after being threatened with expulsion from the organisation for its human rights infringements and democratic backsliding.[^cite_note-guardian_2016-20] It was readmitted to the Commonwealth on 1 February 2020 after showing evidence of reform and functioning democratic processes.[^cite_note-guardian_2020-21]

## Etymology

See also: [Names of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_the_Maldives)

According to legends, the first settlers of The Maldives were people known as Dheyvis.[^cite_note-\:12-22] The first Kingdom of the Maldives was known as Kingdom of Dheeva Maari.[^cite_note-23] During the 3rd century BCE visit of emissaries, it was noted that the Maldives was known as Dheeva Mahal.[^cite_note-\:12-22]

During c. 1100 – 1166, the Maldives was also referred to as Diva Kudha, and the Laccadive [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) which was a part of the Maldives was then referred to as Diva Kanbar by the scholar and polymath [al-Biruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Biruni).[^cite_note-24]

The name [*Maldives*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Maldives) may also derive from [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) माला *mālā* ([garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garland)) and द्वीप *dvīpa* (island),[^cite_note-Hogendorn-25] or මාල දිවයින *Maala Divaina* ("Necklace Islands") in [Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language).[^cite_note-ParanavitanaPrematilleka1978-26] The Maldivian people are called *Dhivehin*. The word *Dheeb/Deeb* (archaic Dhivehi, related to [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) द्वीप, *dvīpa*) means "island", and *Dhives* (*Dhivehin*) means "islanders" (i.e., Maldivians).[^cite_note-27] In Tamil, "garland of islands" can be translated as *mālaittīvu* (மாலைத்தீவு).[^cite_note-28]

The Sri Lankan chronicle Mahavamsa mentions an island designated as *Mahiladiva* ("Island of Women", महिलादिभ) in Pali, likely arising from an erroneous translation of the Sanskrit term, signifying "garland".

Jan Hogendorn, professor of economics at [Colby College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colby_College), theorised that the name Maldives derives from the Sanskrit *mālādvīpa* (मालाद्वीप), meaning "garland of islands".[^cite_note-Hogendorn-25] In [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam), "garland of islands" can be translated as *māladvīpŭ* (മാലദ്വീപ്).[^cite_note-29] In Kannada, "garland of islands" can be translated as *māledvīpa* (ಮಾಲೆದ್ವೀಪ).[^cite_note-30] None of these names are mentioned in any literature, however, classical Sanskrit texts dating back to the [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period) mention the "Hundred Thousand Islands" (*[Lakṣadvīpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshadweep)*), a generic name which would include not only the Maldives, but also the [Laccadives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laccadives), [Aminidivi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aminidivi) Islands, [Minicoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minicoy), and the [Chagos island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagos_Archipelago) groups.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Medieval Muslim travellers such as [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) called the islands *_Maḥal Dībīyāt_* (محل ديبية) from the Arabic word *_maḥal_* ("palace"), which must be how the [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people) traveller interpreted the name of Malé, having been through Muslim North India, where [Perso-Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perso-Arabic) words were introduced to the local vocabulary.[^cite_note-33] This is the name currently inscribed on the scroll in the Maldives [state emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem_of_Maldives).[^cite_note-MVEmblems-34] The classical Persian/Arabic name for Maldives is *Dibajat*.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The Dutch referred to the islands as the *Maldivische Eilanden* (pronounced [\[mɑlˈdivisə ˈʔɛilɑndə(n)\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch)),[^cite_note-WWW\.IBPUS.COM_69-37] while the British [anglicised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicised) the local name for the islands first to the "Maldive Islands" and later to "Maldives".[^cite_note-WWW\.IBPUS.COM_69-37]

In a conversational book published in 1563, [Garcia de Orta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garcia_de_Orta) writes: "I must tell you that I have heard it said that the natives do not call it Maldiva but Nalediva. In the Malabar language, *nale* means four, and *diva* means island. So that in that language, the word signifies 'four islands', while we, corrupting the name, call it Maldiva."[^cite_note-38]

The local name for Maldives by the Maldivian people in [Dhivehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldivian_language) language is *Dhivehi Raajje*, (ދިވެހިރާއްޖެ).[^cite_note-39]

## History

Main article: [History of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Maldives)

### Ancient history and settlement

Main article: [History of the Maldives § Early Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Maldives#Early_Age)

In the 6th–5th century BCE, kingdoms already existed in the modern-day Maldives.[^cite_note-\:12-22] The country has an established history of over 2,500 years according to historical evidence and legends.[^cite_note-40]

The *[Mahāvaṃsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C4%81va%E1%B9%83sa)* (300 BCE) has records of people from Sri Lanka emigrating to the Maldives.[^cite_note-\:12-22] Assuming that cowrie shells come from the Maldives, historians believe that there may have been people living in the Maldives during the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation) (3300–1300 BCE).[^cite_note-41] Several artefacts show the presence of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) in the country before the Islamic period.[^cite_note-\:12-22]

According to the book *Kitāb fi āthār Mīdhu al-qādimah* (كتاب في آثار ميذو القديمة) (*On the Ancient Ruins of Meedhoo*), written in the 17th century in Arabic by Allama Ahmed Shihabuddine (Allama Shihab al-Din) of [Meedhoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meedhoo_\(Addu\)) in Addu Atoll, the first settlers of the Maldives were people known as Dheyvis.[^cite_note-\:12-22] They are said to be from [Kalibangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalibangan) in India.[^cite_note-\:12-22] The time of their arrival is unknown but before Emperor [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka)'s kingdom in 269–232 BCE. Shihabuddin's story tallies remarkably well with the recorded history of South Asia and that of the copperplate document of the Maldives known as [Lōmāfānu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C5%8Dm%C4%81f%C4%81nu).[^cite_note-\:12-22]

The ancient history of the Maldives is told in copperplates, ancient scripts carved on coral artefacts, traditions, language, and different ethnicities of Maldivians.[^cite_note-\:12-22] The *Maapanansa*,[^cite_note-\:12-22] the copper plates on which the history of the first [kings of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Maldivian_monarchs) from the Solar Dynasty was recorded, were lost early.

A 4th-century notice written by [Ammianus Marcellinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus) (362 CE) speaks of gifts sent to the Roman emperor [Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_\(emperor\)) by a deputation from the nation of Divi. The name Divi is very similar to Dheyvi who were the first settlers of the Maldives.[^cite_note-\:12-22]

The first Maldivians did not leave any archaeological artefacts. Their buildings were probably built of wood, palm fronds, and other perishable materials, which would have quickly decayed in the salt and wind of the tropical climate. Moreover, chiefs or headmen did not reside in elaborate stone palaces, nor did their religion require the construction of large temples or compounds.[^cite_note-42]

Comparative studies of Maldivian oral, linguistic, and cultural traditions confirm that the first settlers were people from the southern shores of the neighbouring [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent),[^cite_note-autogenerated2-43] including the [Giraavaru people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giraavaru_people), mentioned in ancient legends and local folklore about the establishment of the capital and kingly rule in Malé.[^cite_note-44]

A strong underlying layer of [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_peoples) and [North Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Indian_culture) cultures survives in Maldivian society, with a clear [Elu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elu) substratum in the language, which also appears in place names, kinship terms, poetry, dance, and religious beliefs.[^cite_note-Maloney,_Clarence-45] The North Indian system was brought by the original [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people) from [Sri Lanka](26750.md). [Malabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_region) and [Pandya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandya) seafaring culture led to the settlement of the Islands by [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils) and [Malabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabars) seafarers.[^cite_note-Maloney,_Clarence-45]

[Isdhoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isdhoo_\(Laamu_Atoll\)) Lōmāfānu is the oldest [copper-plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C5%8Dm%C4%81f%C4%81nu) book to have been discovered in Maldives to date. The book was written in 1194 CE (590 AH) in the Evēla form of the [Divehi akuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhivehi_Writing_Systems), during the reign of [Siri Fennaadheettha Mahaa Radun (Dhinei Kalaminja)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhinei_of_the_Maldives).

Despite being just mentioned briefly in most history books, the 1,400 year-long Buddhist period has a foundational importance in the history of the Maldives. It was during this period that the culture of Maldives both developed and flourished, a culture that survives today. The Maldivian [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhivehi_language), early Maldive scripts, architecture, ruling institutions, customs, and manners of the Maldivians originated at the time when Maldives were a Buddhist kingdom.[^cite_note-46]

Buddhism probably spread to the Maldives in the 3rd century BCE at the time of Emperor [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka)'s expansion and became the dominant religion of the people of the Maldives until the 12th century. Archeological evidence from an ancient Buddhist monastery in [Kaashidhoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaashidhoo_\(Kaafu_Atoll\)) has been dated between 205 and 560 CE, based on the radiocarbon dating of shell deposits unearthed from the foundations of [stupas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupa) and other structures in the monastery.[^cite_note-mikkelsen2000-47] The ancient Maldivian Kings promoted [Buddhism](3267529.md), and the first Maldive writings and artistic achievements, in the form of highly developed sculpture and architecture, originate from that period. Nearly all archaeological remains in the Maldives are from Buddhist [stupas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupa) and monasteries, and all artefacts found to date display characteristic Buddhist iconography.

### Islamic period

See also: [History of the Maldives § Islamic Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Maldives#Islamic_Period), [Islam in Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Maldives), [List of Maldivian monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Maldivian_monarchs), and [Sultanate of the Maldive Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_the_Maldive_Islands)

The importance of the Arabs as traders in the Indian Ocean by the 12th century may partly explain why the last Buddhist king of Maldives, [Dhovemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhovemi_of_the_Maldives), converted to Islam in 1153 (or 1193). Adopting the Muslim title of Sultan Muhammad al-Adil, he initiated a series of six Islamic dynasties that lasted until 1932 when the [sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maldives) became elective. The formal title of the sultan up to 1965 was, *Sultan of Land and Sea, Lord of the twelve-thousand islands and Sultan of the Maldives* which came with the style *[Highness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highness)*.

A Moroccan traveller named Abu al-Barakat Yusuf al-Barbari is traditionally cited for this conversion.[^cite_note-\:2-48] According to the story told to [Ibn Battutah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battutah), a mosque was built with the inscription: 'The Sultan Ahmad Shanurazah accepted Islam at the hand of Abu al-Barakat Yusuf al-Barbari.'[^cite_note-\:2-48][^cite_note-49] The folk tale regarding the spread of Islam by al-Barbari involved a local belief to an oceanic deity named [Rannamaari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rannamaari). According to the legend, Rannamaari demanded the sacrifice of a female virgin each month by the locals. This tradition was practiced for a long time, until al-Barbari visited Maldives and managed to convert the local ruler and convinced him along with his subjects to abandon the Rannamari worship.[^cite_note-StephanieSmith-50][^cite_note-XavierRannamari-51] According to [Norwegian](21241.md) archaeologist [Egil Mikkelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egil_Mikkelsen?action=edit\&redlink=1), it is likely the myth of Rannamari was heavily influenced by [Buddhist tantric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajrayana) rituals and beliefs that were practiced in the islands at the time.[^cite_note-MikkelsenRannaanari-52]

Some scholars have suggested the possibility of Ibn Battuta misreading Maldive texts, and having a bias towards the North African, Maghrebi narrative of this Shaykh, instead of the Persian origins account that was known as well at the time.[^cite_note-53] Others have it that he may have been from the Persian town of [Tabriz](439791.md).[^cite_note-auto-54] This interpretation, held by the more reliable local historical chronicles, Raadavalhi and Taarikh,[^cite_note-Visweswaran2011-55][^cite_note-sw-56] is that Abu al-Barakat Yusuf al-Barbari was Abdul Barakat Yusuf Shams ud-Dīn at-Tabrīzī, also locally known as Tabrīzugefānu.[^cite_note-57] In the Arabic script the words al-Barbari and al-Tabrizi are very much alike, since at the time, Arabic had several consonants that looked identical and could only be differentiated by overall context (this has since changed by the addition of dots above or below letters to clarify pronunciation. For example, the letter "B" in modern Arabic has a dot below, whereas the letter "T" looks identical except there are two dots above it). "ٮوسڡ الٮٮرٮرى" could be read as "Yusuf at-Tabrizi" or "Yusuf al-Barbari".[^cite_note-58]

The venerated tomb of the scholar now stands on the grounds of [Medhu Ziyaaraiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muliaage#Medhu_Ziyaaraiy), across the street from the Friday Mosque, or [Hukuru Miskiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%A9_Friday_Mosque), in Malé. Originally built in 1153 and re-built in 1658,[^cite_note-Yoosuf-59] this is one of the oldest surviving mosques in the Maldives. Following the Islamic concept that before Islam there was the time of [Jahiliya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahiliyyah) (ignorance), in the history books used by Maldivians the [introduction of Islam](#Introduction_of_Islam) at the end of the 12th century is considered the cornerstone of the country's history. Nonetheless, the cultural influence of Buddhism remains, a reality directly experienced by Ibn Battuta during his nine months there sometime between 1341 and 1345, serving as a chief judge and marrying into the royal family of [Omar I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_I_of_the_Maldives).[^cite_note-60] For he became embroiled in local politics and left when his strict judgements in the laissez-faire island kingdom began to chafe with its rulers. In particular, he was angered at the local women going about with no clothing above the waist— a cultural epithet of the region at the time- was seen as a violation of Middle Eastern Islamic rules of modesty—and the locals taking no notice when he complained.[^cite_note-61]

Compared to the other areas of South Asia, the conversion of the Maldives to Islam happened relatively late. The Maldives remained a Buddhist kingdom for another 500 years. Arabic became the prime language of administration (instead of Persian and Urdu), and the [Maliki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maliki) school of jurisprudence was introduced, both hinting at direct contact with the core of the Arab world.

Middle Eastern seafarers had just begun to take over the Indian Ocean trade routes in the 10th century and found the Maldives to be an important link in those routes as the first landfall for traders from [Basra](57580.md) sailing to Southeast Asia. Trade involved mainly [cowrie shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowrie_shells)—widely used as a form of currency throughout Asia and parts of the East African coast—and coir fibre. The [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate), where cowrie shells were used as legal tender, was one of the principal trading partners of the Maldives. The Bengal–Maldives cowry shell trade was the largest shell currency trade network in history.[^cite_note-62]

The other essential product of the Maldives was [coir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coir), the fibre of the dried [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut) [husk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husk), resistant to saltwater. It stitched together and rigged the [dhows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhow) that plied the Indian Ocean. Maldivian coir was exported to [Sindh](28972.md), [China](5405.md), [Yemen](350939.md), and the [Persian Gulf](24761.md).

### Protectorate period



[Portuguese presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) in Maldives was established in 1558, by order of [Constantino of Braganza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantino_of_Braganza), Viceroy of [Portuguese India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India).

In 1558, the Portuguese established a small garrison with a *Viador* (*Viyazoaru*), or overseer of a [factory (trading post)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_\(trading_post\)) in the Maldives, which they administered from their main colony in [Goa](40010153.md). Their attempts to forcefully impose Christianity with the threat of death provoked a local revolt led by [Muhammad Thakurufaanu al-A'uẓam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Thakurufaanu_al-Auzam), his two brothers, and [Dhuvaafaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhuvaafaru) Dhandahele, who fifteen years later drove the Portuguese out of the Maldives. This event is now commemorated as [Qaumee Dhuvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaumee_Dhuvas) (lit. 'National Day'). It is celebrated on the 1st of [Rabi' al-Awwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi'_al-Awwal), the third month of [Hijri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Calendar) (Islamic) calendar.[^cite_note-qaumiyyath-63]



16th-century Portuguese illustration from the [Códice Casanatense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3dice_Casanatense), depicting workers



18th-century map by [Pierre Mortier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Mortier) from the [Netherlands](21148.md), depicting with detail the islands of Maldives

In the mid-17th century, the Dutch, who had replaced the Portuguese as the dominant power in [Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceylon), established hegemony over Maldivian affairs without involving themselves directly in local matters, which were governed according to centuries-old Islamic customs.

The British expelled the Dutch from Ceylon in 1796 and included the Maldives as a [British protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_protectorate). The status of the Maldives as a British protectorate was officially recorded in an 1887 agreement in which the sultan [Muhammad Mueenuddeen II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Mueenuddeen_II) accepted British influence over Maldivian external relations and defence while retaining home rule, which continued to be regulated by [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) traditional institutions in exchange for an [annual tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldivian_annual_tribute). The status of the islands was akin to other British protectorates in the Indian Ocean region, including [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar) and the [Trucial States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trucial_States).



&#x20;17th-century Portuguese drawing of the fortress of Maldives and the archipelago. In Antonio Bocarro's book of Fortress (1632)

[^cite_note-64]

In the British period, the Sultan's powers were taken over by the Chief Minister, much to the chagrin of the British Governor-General who continued to deal with the ineffectual Sultan. Consequently, Britain encouraged the development of a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy), and the first Constitution was proclaimed in 1932. However, the new arrangements favoured neither the Sultan nor the Chief Minister, but rather a young crop of British-educated reformists. As a result, angry mobs were instigated against the Constitution which was publicly torn up.

The Maldives remained a British crown protectorate until 1953 when the sultanate was suspended and the First Republic was declared under the short-lived presidency of [Mohamed Amin Didi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Amin_Didi). While serving as prime minister during the 1940s, Didi nationalised the fish export industry. As president, he is remembered as a reformer of the education system and an advocate of [women's rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_rights). Conservatives in Malé ousted his government, and during a riot over food shortages, Didi was beaten by a mob and died on a nearby island.



An [RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF) [Short Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Sunderland) moored in the lagoon at Addu Atoll, during WWII

Beginning in the 1950s, the political history in the Maldives was largely influenced by the British military presence on the islands. In 1954, the restoration of the sultanate perpetuated the rule of the past. Two years later, the United Kingdom obtained permission to reestablish its wartime [RAF Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Gan) airfield in the southernmost Addu Atoll, employing hundreds of locals. In 1957, however, the new prime minister, [Ibrahim Nasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Nasir), called for a review of the agreement. Nasir was challenged in 1959 by a local secessionist movement in the three southernmost atolls that benefited economically from the British presence on [Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_\(Addu_Atoll\)). This group cut ties with the Maldivian government and formed an independent state, the [United Suvadive Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Suvadive_Republic) with [Abdullah Afeef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Afeef) as president and [Hithadhoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hithadhoo_\(Seenu_Atoll\)) as its capital. One year later the Suvadive Republic was scrapped after Nasir sent gunboats from Malé with government police, and Abdullah Afeef went into exile. Meanwhile, in 1960 the Maldives allowed the United Kingdom to continue to use both the [Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Gan) and the Hithadhoo facilities for thirty years, with the payment of £750,000 from 1960 to 1965 for the Maldives' economic development. The base was closed in 1976 as part of the larger British withdrawal of permanently stationed forces '[East of Suez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Suez)'.[^cite_note-gan.philliptsmall.me.uk-65]

### Independence and republic

Main article: [Independence of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_the_Maldives)

&#x20;When the British became increasingly unable to continue their colonial hold on Asia and were losing their colonies to the indigenous populations who wanted freedom, on 26 July 1965 an agreement was signed on behalf of the Sultan by Ibrahim Nasir Rannabandeyri Kilegefan, Prime Minister, and on behalf of the British government by [Sir Michael Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Walker_\(diplomat\)), British Ambassador-designate to the Maldive Islands, which formally ended the British authority on the defence and external affairs of the Maldives.[^cite_note-66] The islands thus achieved independence, with the ceremony taking place at the British High Commissioner's Residence in [Colombo](56636.md). After this, the sultanate continued for another three years under [Sir Muhammad Fareed Didi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Fareed_Didi), who declared himself King upon independence.[^cite_note-67]



Flag of the [Sultan of Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Maldivian_monarchs)



Prime Minister Ibrahim Nasir signs independence agreement with the British on 26 July 1965.

On 15 November 1967, a vote was taken in parliament to decide whether the Maldives should continue as a constitutional monarchy or become a republic.[^cite_note-68] Of the 44 members of parliament, 40 voted in favour of a republic. On 15 March 1968, a [national referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Maldivian_constitutional_referendum) was held on the question, and 93.34% of those taking part voted in favour of establishing a republic.[^cite_note-69] The republic was declared on 11 November 1968, thus ending the 853-year-old monarchy, which was replaced by a republic under the presidency of Ibrahim Nasir.[^cite_note-70]

[Tourism](413341.md) began to be developed on the [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) by the beginning of the 1970s.[^cite_note-71] The first resort in the Maldives was [Kurumba Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurumba_Maldives) which welcomed the first guests on 3 October 1972.[^cite_note-72] The first accurate census was held in December 1977 and showed 142,832 people living in the Maldives.[^cite_note-73]

Political infighting during the 1970s between Nasir's faction and other political figures led to the 1975 arrest and exile of elected prime minister [Ahmed Zaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Zaki_\(politician\)) to a remote [atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoll).[^cite_note-74] Economic decline followed the closure of the [British airfield at Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_International_Airport) and the collapse of the market for dried fish, an important export. With support for his administration faltering, Nasir fled to Singapore in 1978, with millions of dollars from the treasury.[^cite_note-75]

[Maumoon Abdul Gayoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maumoon_Abdul_Gayoom) began his 30-year role as president in 1978, winning six consecutive elections without opposition. His election was seen as ushering in a period of political stability and economic development given Maumoon's priority to develop the poorer islands. Tourism flourished and increased foreign contact spurred development. However, Maumoon's rule was controversial, with some critics saying Maumoon was an autocrat who quelled dissent by limiting freedoms and practising political favouritism.[^cite_note-cnn_sinking-76]

A series of coup attempts (in 1980, 1983, and 1988) by Nasir supporters and business interests tried to topple the government without success. While the first two attempts met with little success, the [1988 coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Maldives_coup_attempt) involved a roughly 80-strong mercenary force of the [PLOTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Organisation_of_Tamil_Eelam) who seized the airport and caused Maumoon to flee from house to house until the intervention of 1,600 [Indian troops](7171338.md) airlifted into Malé restored order.

The November 1988 coup d'état was headed by Ibrahim Lutfee, a businessman, and Sikka Ahmed Ismail Manik, the father of the former first lady of the Maldives [Fazna Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazna_Ahmed).[^cite_note-77] The attackers were defeated by then [National Security Services](1115368.md).[^cite_note-three-78] On the night of 3 November 1988, the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) airlifted a [parachute battalion group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parachute_Regiment_\(India\)#Operation_Cactus) from [Agra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra) and flew them over 2,000 kilometres (1,200 mi) to the Maldives.[^cite_note-three-78] By the time Indian armed forces reached the Maldives, the mercenary forces has already left Malé on the hijacked ship MV Progress Light.[^cite_note-three-78] The Indian [paratroopers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratroopers) landed at [Hulhulé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulhul%C3%A9_Island) and secured the airfield and restored the government rule at Malé within hours.[^cite_note-three-78] The brief operation labelled *Operation Cactus*, also involved the [Indian Navy](604522.md) that assisted in capturing the freighter MV Progress Light and rescued the hostages and crew.[^cite_note-three-78]

### 21st century

Main article: [History of the Maldives § 21st century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Maldives#21st_century)

&#x20;The Maldives [were devastated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effect_of_the_2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_on_the_Maldives) by a [tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami) on 26 December 2004, following the [Indian Ocean earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake). Only nine islands were reported to have escaped any flooding,[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] while fifty-seven islands faced serious damage to critical infrastructure, fourteen islands had to be totally evacuated, and six islands were destroyed. A further twenty-one resort islands were forced to close because of tsunami damage. The total damage was estimated at more than US\$400 million, or some 62% of the GDP.[^cite_note-81] 102 Maldivians and 6 foreigners reportedly died in the tsunami.[^cite_note-cnn_sinking-76] The destructive impact of the waves on the low-lying islands was mitigated by the fact there was no continental shelf or land mass upon which the waves could gain height. The tallest waves were reported to be 14 feet (4.3 m) high.[^cite_note-82]



[17th SAARC summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_SAARC_summit) in [Addu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addu_City), Maldives

During the later part of Maumoon's rule, independent political movements emerged in the Maldives, which challenged the then-ruling [Dhivehi Rayyithunge Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhivehi_Rayyithunge_Party) (Maldivian People's Party, MPP) and demanded democratic reform. The dissident journalist and activist [Mohamed Nasheed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Nasheed) founded the [Maldivian Democratic Party](673147.md) (MDP) in 2003 and pressured Maumoon into allowing gradual political reforms.[^cite_note-brownpoliticalreview\.org-83] In 2008, a new constitution was approved and the [first direct presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Maldivian_presidential_election) occurred, which were won by Nasheed in the second round. His administration faced many challenges, including the huge debt left by the previous government, the economic downturn following the 2004 tsunami, overspending using overprinting of local currency (the [rufiyaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufiyaa)), unemployment, corruption, and increasing drug use.[^cite_note-84] Taxation on goods was imposed for the first time in the country, and import duties were reduced on many goods and services. Universal health insurance ([Aasandha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aasandha)) and social welfare benefits were given to those aged 65 years or older, single parents, and those with special needs.[^cite_note-cnn_sinking-76]

Social and [political unrest grew in late 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%932013_Maldives_political_crisis), following opposition campaigns in the name of protecting Islam. Nasheed controversially resigned from office after a large number of police and army mutinied in February 2012. Nasheed's vice-president, [Mohamed Waheed Hassan Manik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Waheed_Hassan), was sworn in as president.[^cite_note-85] Nasheed was later arrested,[^cite_note-86] convicted of terrorism, and sentenced to 13 years. The trial was widely seen as flawed and political. The [UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_Group_on_Arbitrary_Detention) called for Nasheed's immediate release.[^cite_note-87]

The [2013 Maldivian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Maldivian_presidential_election) results were highly contested. Former president Nasheed won the most votes in the first round, but the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Maldives) annulled it despite the positive assessment of international election observers. In the re-run vote [Abdulla Yameen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulla_Yameen), the half-brother of the former president Maumoon, assumed the presidency.[^cite_note-brownpoliticalreview\.org-83] Yameen survived an assassination attempt in late 2015.[^cite_note-88] Vice-president [Mohamed Jameel Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Jameel_Ahmed) was removed from office after a [no confidence motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_confidence_motion) from the People's Majlis, it was alleged that he was conspiring with opposition political parties and planning riots.[^cite_note-89] Vice-president [Ahmed Adeeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Adeeb) was later arrested together with 17 supporters for "public order offences" and the government instituted a broader crackdown against his accomplices. A [state of emergency](298608.md) was later declared ahead of a planned anti-government rally,[^cite_note-90] and the People's Majlis (parliament) accelerated the removal of Adeeb.[^cite_note-majlis.gov.mv-91][^cite_note-92]

[In the 2018 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Maldivian_presidential_election), [Ibrahim Mohamed Solih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Mohamed_Solih) won the most votes, and was sworn in as the Maldives' new president in November 2018. Adeeb was freed by courts in Male in July 2019 after his conviction on charges of terrorism and corruption was overruled but was placed under a [travel ban](26647363.md) after the state prosecutor appealed the order in a corruption and money laundering case. Adeeb escaped in a [tugboat](216434.md) to [seek asylum](276005.md) in India. It is understood that the [Indian Coast Guard](786383.md) escorted the tugboat to the International Maritime Boundary Line (IMBL) and he was then "transferred" to a [Maldivian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldivian_Coast_Guard) ship, where officials took him into custody.[^cite_note-93] Former president Abdulla Yameen was sentenced to five years in prison in November 2019 for money laundering. The [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_the_Maldives) upheld the jail sentence in January 2021.[^cite_note-94] However, Supreme Court overturned Yameen's conviction in November 2021.[^cite_note-95]

In the [2023 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Maldivian_presidential_election), [People's National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_National_Congress_\(Maldives\)) (PNC) candidate [Mohamed Muizzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Muizzu) won the second-round runoff of the Maldives presidential election, beating incumbent president, Ibrahim Solih, with 54% of the vote.[^cite_note-96] On 17 October 2023, Mohamed Muizzu was sworn in as the eighth President of the Republic of Maldives.[^cite_note-97] Mohamed Muizzu is widely seen to be pro-China, souring relations with India.[^cite_note-98] In 2024, ex-President Abdulla Yameen Abdul Gayoom was freed from his 11-year conviction and the High Court ordered a new trial.[^cite_note-99]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Maldives)

&#x20;The Maldives consists of 1,192 [coral islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_island) grouped in a double chain of 26 atolls, that stretch along a length of 871 kilometres (541 miles) north to south, 130 kilometres (81 miles) east to west, spread over roughly 90,000 square kilometres (35,000 sq mi), of which only 298 km<sup>2</sup> (115 sq mi) is dry land, making this one of the world's most dispersed countries. It lies between latitudes [1°S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_parallel_south) and [8°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_parallel_north), and longitudes [72°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72nd_meridian_east) and [74°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/74th_meridian_east). The atolls are composed of live [coral reefs](87410.md) and [sand bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_bar), situated atop a submarine ridge 960 kilometres (600 mi) long that rises abruptly from the depths of the Indian Ocean and runs north to south.



Maalhosmadulu Atoll seen from space. [Northern Maalhosmadulu Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Maalhosmadulu_Atoll) and [Southern Maalhosmadulu Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Maalhosmadulu_Atoll) can be seen in this picture.



Dark clouds bringing heavy rain, common in the rainy season

Only near the southern end of this natural coral barricade do two open passages permit safe ship navigation from one side of the Indian Ocean to the other through the territorial waters of the Maldives. For administrative purposes, the Maldivian government organised these atolls into 21 [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_Maldives). The largest island of the Maldives is that of [Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_\(Laamu_Atoll\)), which belongs to Laamu Atoll or Hahdhummathi Maldives. In Addu Atoll, the westernmost islands are connected by roads over the reef (collectively called Link Road), and the total length of the road is 14 km (9 mi).

The Maldives is the lowest country in the world, with maximum and average natural ground levels of only 2.4 metres (7 ft 10 in) and 1.5 metres (4 ft 11 in) above sea level, respectively. In areas where construction exists, however, this has been increased to several metres. More than 80 per cent of the country's land is composed of coral islands which rise less than one metre above sea level.[^cite_note-100] As a result, the Maldives are in danger of being submerged due to [rising sea levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise). The [Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change](15030.md) has warned that, at current rates, sea-level rise would be high enough to make the Maldives uninhabitable by 2100.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

### Climate



A beach of [Bathala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathala_\(island\)) island

The Maldives has a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) (Am) under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), which is affected by the large landmass of South Asia to the north. Because the Maldives has the lowest elevation of any country in the world, the temperature is constantly hot and often humid. The presence of this landmass causes differential heating of land and water. These factors set off a rush of moisture-rich air from the Indian Ocean over South Asia, resulting in the southwest [monsoon](57630.md). Two seasons dominate the Maldives' weather: the dry season associated with the winter northeastern monsoon and the rainy season associated with the southwest monsoon which brings strong winds and storms.[^cite_note-Climate-103]

The shift from the dry northeast monsoon to the moist southwest monsoon occurs during April and May. During this period, the southwest winds contribute to the formation of the southwest monsoon, which reaches the Maldives at the beginning of June and lasts until the end of November. However, the weather patterns of the Maldives do not always conform to the monsoon patterns of South Asia. The annual rainfall averages 254 centimetres (100 in) in the north and 381 centimetres (150 in) in the south.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-Climate-103]

The monsoonal influence is greater in the north of the Maldives than in the south, more influenced by the [equatorial currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Counter_Current).

The average high temperature is 31.5 degrees Celsius and the average low temperature is 26.4 degrees Celsius.[^cite_note-Climate-103]

### Sea level rise

Main article: [Climate change in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_the_Maldives)

See also: [Effects of climate change on island nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_island_nations) and [The Island President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Island_President)

More than 80% of the Maldives' 1,190 coral islands sit less than 1 meter above sea level, making it the lowest-lying country in the world, and particularly at risk to sea level rise.[^cite_note-\:3-107] The [NASA Earth Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Earth_Observatory) estimates that the global sea level rises 3 to 4 milimeters per year with [NOAA](37876.md) estimating that the rate of annual sea level rise has doubled since the 20th century from 0.06 inches per year to 0.14 inches per year from 2006 to 2015.[^cite_note-\:3-107][^cite_note-108] A study published in the journal Coastal Futures which analysed an abnormal swell event in the Maldives in 2022 found that extreme flood events, which usually happen every 25 years, could become more frequent and hazardous due to rising sea levels.[^cite_note-109] According to researchers from the [University of Southampton](98078.md), the Maldives are the third most endangered island nation due to flooding from [climate change](5042951.md) as a percentage of population.[^cite_note-110]

In 2020, a three-year study at the [University of Plymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Plymouth) which looked at the Maldives and the [Marshall Islands](19147.md), found that tides move sediment to create a higher elevation, a morphological response that the researchers suggested could help low-lying islands adjust to sea level rise and keep the islands habitable. The research also reported that [sea walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawall) were compromising islands' ability to adjust to rising sea levels and that island drowning is an inevitable outcome for islands with coastal structures like sea walls.[^cite_note-111] Hideki Kanamaru, natural resources officer with the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) in Asia-Pacific, said the study provided a "new perspective" on how island nations could tackle the challenge of sea-level rise, and that even if islands can adapt naturally to higher seas by raising their own crests, humans still needed to double down on global warming and protection for island populations.[^cite_note-\:1-112]

In 2008, the then-president [Nasheed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Nasheed) announced plans to look into purchasing land on higher ground in India, Sri Lanka, and Australia as an insurance policy against sea level rise. The president explained his intentions: "We do not want to leave the Maldives, but we also do not want to be [climate refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_migration) living in tents for decades".[^cite_note-Guardian-113] However, these plans never materialised and successive leadership opted to instead reinforce of the country's current islands with [seawalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawall) and [construct new islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation) on higher ground.[^cite_note-114]

At the 2009 International Climate Talks, Nasheed stated that:

> For us swearing off fossil fuels is not only the right thing to do, but it is also in our economic self-interest... Pioneering countries will free themselves from the unpredictable price of foreign oil; they will capitalise on the new green economy of the future, and they will enhance their moral standing giving them greater political influence on the world stage.[^cite_note-115]

Former president [Mohamed Nasheed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Nasheed) said in 2012 "If carbon emissions continue at the rate they are climbing today, my country will be underwater in seven years."[^cite_note-Guilfordian-116] He has called for more [climate change mitigation](2119179.md) action while on the American television shows *[The Daily Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Show)*[^cite_note-117] and the *[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman)*,[^cite_note-Guilfordian-116] and hosted "the world's first underwater cabinet meeting" in 2009 to raise awareness of the threats posed by climate change.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-\:1-112] Concerns over rising sea levels have also been expressed by Nasheed's predecessor, [Maumoon Abdul Gayoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maumoon_Abdul_Gayoom).[^cite_note-119]

### Environment

Environmental issues other than [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) include bad waste disposal and [sand theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_theft). Although the Maldives are kept relatively pristine and little [litter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litter) can be found on the islands, most [waste disposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management) sites are often substandard. The bulk of the waste from Malé and nearby resorts in the Maldives is disposed of at [Thilafushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thilafushi), an industrial island on top of a lagoon reclaimed in the early '90s to sort waste management issues which had plagued the capital and surrounding islands.[^cite_note-120]

[31 protected areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Protected_Areas_of_Maldives) are administered by the Ministry of Climate Change, Environment and Energy and the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) of Maldives.[^cite_note-Maldives_Protected_Areas-121]

### Marine ecosystem



Maldives soft coral



[Oriental sweetlips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_sweetlips) (*Plectorhinchus vittatus*) at [Meeru Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meeru_Island), North Male Atoll

The waters surrounding the Maldives contain diverse marine life, including various [corals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral), over 2,000 species of fish,[^cite_note-122] and rare species of biological and commercial significance, with [tuna fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna_fisheries) representing a longstanding traditional resource. Freshwater fish such as the [milkfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkfish) ([Chanos chanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkfish)) and various smaller species thrive within ponds, marshes and other limited freshwater habitats. In the 1970s, a [United Nations](31769.md) agency facilitated the introduction of the [tilapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilapia) or mouth-breeder.

The Maldives have different habitats including [deep sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_sea), shallow coast, and reef ecosystems, fringing [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove), wetlands, and dry land. 187 species of coral form the [coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reefs). This area of the Indian Ocean, alone, houses 1,100 [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) of fish, 5 species of [sea turtle](142905.md), 21 species of whale and dolphin, 400 species of [mollusc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusc), and 83 species of [echinoderms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echinoderms). The area is also populated by several crustacean species: 120 [copepods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copepod), and 15 [amphipods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphipod), as well as more than 145 [crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crab) and 48 [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp) species.[^cite_note-123]

Among the many marine families represented are [pufferfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pufferfish), [fusiliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesionidae), [jackfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carangidae), [lionfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionfish), [oriental sweetlips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_sweetlips), [reef sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reef_sharks), [groupers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouper), eels, [snappers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutjanidae), [bannerfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannerfish), [batfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogcocephalidae), [humphead wrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphead_wrasse), [spotted eagle rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_eagle_ray), [scorpionfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpionfish), [lobsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster), [nudibranches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nudibranches), [angelfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomacanthidae), [butterflyfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterflyfish), [squirrelfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrelfish), [soldierfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldierfish), [glassfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_glassfish), [surgeonfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgeonfish), [unicornfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naso_\(fish\)), [triggerfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triggerfish), [Napoleon wrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_wrasse), and [barracuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barracuda).[^cite_note-scuba-124][^cite_note-125]

These coral reefs are home to various marine ecosystems that include species such as [planktonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planktonic) organisms and [whale sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_shark). Five species of [sponge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge) have displayed anti-[tumor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumor) and anti-[cancer](105219.md) properties.[^cite_note-Coral_reefs-126]

In 1998, sea-temperature warming of as much as 5 °C (9.0 °F) due to a single [El Niño phenomenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o-Southern_Oscillation) event caused [coral bleaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_bleaching) and killed two-thirds of the nation's coral reefs.[^cite_note-Wheatley-127]

To induce the regrowth of the reefs, scientists placed electrified cones anywhere from 20–60 feet (6.1–18.3 m) below the surface to provide a substrate for larval coral attachment. In 2004, scientists witnessed corals regenerating. Corals began to eject pink-orange eggs and sperm. The growth of these electrified corals was five times faster than untreated corals.[^cite_note-Wheatley-127] Scientist Azeez Hakim stated:

> Before 1998, we never thought that this reef would die. We had always taken for granted that these animals would be there, that this reef would be there forever. El Niño gave us a wake-up call that these things are not going to be there forever. Not only this, but they also act as a natural barrier against tropical storms, floods, and tsunamis. Seaweeds grow on the skeletons of dead coral.

— [^cite_note-scuba-124]

Again, in 2016, the coral reefs of the Maldives experienced a [severe bleaching incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_bleaching#Maldives). Up to 95% of coral around some islands have died, and, even after six months, 100% of young coral transplants died. The surface water temperatures reached an all-time high in 2016, at 31 degrees Celsius in May.[^cite_note-128]

Recent scientific studies suggest that the faunistic composition can vary greatly between neighbour atolls, especially in terms of [benthic fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benthic_fauna). Differences in terms of fishing pressure (including [poaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching)) could be the cause.[^cite_note-Ducarme-2016-V36-129]

The [wildlife of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_the_Maldives) includes the [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora) and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna) of the [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_of_the_Maldives), reefs, and the surrounding ocean. Recent scientific studies suggest that the fauna varies greatly between [atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atolls_of_the_Maldives) following a north–south gradient, but important differences between neighbouring atolls were also found (especially in terms of sea animals), which may be linked to differences in fishing pressure – including poaching.[^cite_note-Ducarme-2016-V36-129]

The terrestrial habitats of the Maldives are confronted with a significant threat as extensive development encroaches swiftly upon the limited land resources. Once seldom frequented, previously uninhabited islands now teeter on the brink of extinction, virtually devoid of untouched expanses. Over recent decades of intensive development, numerous natural environments crucial to indigenous species have suffered severe endangerment or outright destruction.

Coral reef habitats have been damaged, as the pressure for land has brought about the creation of artificial islands. Some reefs have been filled with rubble with little regard for the changes in the currents on the reef shelf and how the new pattern would affect coral growth and its related life forms on the reef edges.[^cite_note-130] Mangroves thrive in brackish or muddy regions of the Maldives. The archipelago hosts fourteen species spanning ten genera, among which is the fern Acrostichum aureum, indigenous to these islands.[^cite_note-Hingun-2015-131]



*[Butorides striata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butorides_striata)* in the Maldives

Due to their diminutive size, land-dwelling reptiles are scarce on the Maldivian islands. Among the limited terrestrial reptilian inhabitants are a species of [gecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gecko) and the oriental garden lizard ([Calotes versicolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calotes_versicolor)), alongside the white-spotted supple skink ([Riopa albopunctata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riopa_albopunctata)), the Indian wolf snake ([Lycodon aulicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycodon_aulicus)), and the Brahmin blind snake ([Ramphotyphlops braminus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramphotyphlops_braminus)).

In the surrounding seas, however, a more diverse array of reptilian life thrives. Maldivian beaches serve as nesting grounds for the green sea turtle ([Chelonia mydas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelonia_mydas)), the [hawksbill sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawksbill_sea_turtle), and the [leatherback sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leatherback_sea_turtle). Furthermore, [saltwater crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_crocodiles) have been reported to occasionally reach the islands, taking residence in marshy regions.[^cite_note-132]

The location of this Indian Ocean archipelago means that its avifauna is mainly restricted to [pelagic birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelagic_bird).[^cite_note-133] Most of the species are Eurasian migratory birds, only a few being typically associated with the Indian sub-continent. Some, like the [frigatebird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigatebird), are seasonal. Some birds dwell in marshes and island bush, like the [grey heron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_heron) and the [moorhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorhen). [White terns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_terns) are found occasionally on the southern islands due to their rich habitats.[^cite_note-134]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Maldives)



[Mohamed Muizzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Muizzu), [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Maldives) since 2023



[Hussain Mohamed Latheef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussain_Mohamed_Latheef), [Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_Maldives) since 2023

The Maldives is a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [constitutional republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic), with extensive influence of the president as [head of government](37570.md) and [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state). The president heads the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) and appoints the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_Maldives) which is approved by the [People's Majlis (Parliament)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis_of_the_Maldives). He leads the [armed forces](1115368.md). The current president, serving since 17 November 2023, is [Mohamed Muizzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Muizzu).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] [President of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Maldives) and Members of the [unicameral](204304.md) Majlis serve five-year terms.[^cite_note-137] The total number of members are determined by atoll populations. At the [2024 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Maldivian_parliamentary_election), the [People's National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_National_Congress_\(Maldives\)) (PNC) won a super-majority over the 93 constituencies.[^cite_note-138]

The republican constitution came into force in 1968 and was amended in 1970, 1972, and 1975.[^cite_note-139] On 27 November 1997 it was replaced by another Constitution assented to by then-President [Maumoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maumoon_Abdul_Gayoom). This Constitution came into force on 1 January 1998.[^cite_note-140] The current [Constitution of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Maldives) was ratified by President Maumoon on 7 August 2008, and came into effect immediately, replacing and repealing the constitution of 1998. This new constitution includes a judiciary run by an independent commission, and independent commissions to oversee elections and fight corruption. It also reduces the executive powers vested under the president and strengthens the parliament. All state that the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Maldives) is head of state, head of government, and Commander-in-Chief of the [armed forces](1115368.md) of the Maldives.

In 2018, the then ruling [Progressive Party of Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_of_Maldives) (PPM)'s tensions with opposition parties and the subsequent crackdown was termed as an "assault on democracy" by the [UN Human Rights](635790.md) chief.[^cite_note-141]



President Ibrahim Mohamed Solih congratulates [Mohamed Nasheed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Nasheed) Speaker of the People's Majlis in May 2019

In the April 2019 parliamentary [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Maldivian_parliamentary_election) The [Maldivian Democratic Party](673147.md) (MDP) of President Ibrahim Mohamed Solih won a landslide victory. It took 65 of 87 seats of the parliament.[^cite_note-142] This was the first time a single party was able to get such a high number of seats in the parliament in Maldivian history.[^cite_note-143]

[Order of Nishanizzuddeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Distinguished_Rule_of_Izzuddin) is the Maldives' highest civilian honour that can be bestowed upon a person. It is awarded by the president, usually in an elaborate ceremony.[^cite_note-144]

In April 2024, President Mohamed Muizzu's pro-China People's National Congress (PNC) won 66 seats in the 2024 [Maldivian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldivian_parliamentary_election,_2024), while its allies took nine, giving the president the backing of 75 legislators in the 93-member house, meaning a super-majority and enough to change the constitution.[^cite_note-145]

### Administrative divisions

Main article: [Administrative divisions of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_Maldives)



Each administrative atoll is marked, along with the [thaana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaana) letter used to identify the atoll. Natural atolls are labelled in light blue.

The Maldives has twenty-six natural [atolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atolls_of_the_Maldives) and few island groups on isolated reefs, all of which have been divided into twenty-one administrative divisions (17 administrative atolls and cities of Malé, Addu, [Fuvahmulah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuvahmulah), [Thinadhoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinadhoo_\(Huvadhu_Atoll\)), and [Kulhudhuffushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulhudhuffushi)).[^cite_note-146]

Each atoll is administered by an elected Atoll Council. The islands are administered by an elected Island Council.

In addition to a name, every administrative division is identified by the Maldivian code letters, such as "[Haa Alif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haa_Alif)" for [Thiladhunmati Uthuruburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thiladhunmathi_Atoll) (Thiladhunmathi North); and by a Latin code letter. The first corresponds to the geographical Maldivian name of the atoll; the second is a code adopted for convenience. As certain islands in different atolls have the same name, for administrative purposes this code is quoted before the name of the island, for example: Baa Funadhoo, Kaafu Funadhoo, Gaafu-Alifu Funadhoo. Since most atolls have very long geographical names it is also used whenever the long name is inconvenient, for example in the atoll website names.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-147]

The introduction of code-letter names has been a source of much puzzlement and misunderstanding, especially among foreigners. Many people have come to think that the code letter of the administrative atoll is its new name and that it has replaced its geographical name. Under such circumstances, it is hard to know which is the correct name to use.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-147]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_Maldives)

Since 1996, the Maldives has been the official progress monitor of the [Indian Ocean Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_Commission). In 2002, the Maldives began to express interest in the commission but as of 2008 had not applied for membership. The Maldives' interest relates to its identity as a small island state, especially economic development and environmental preservation, and its desire for closer relations with France, a main actor in the IOC region.

Maldives is a founding member of the [South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation) ([SAARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Association_for_Regional_Cooperation)). The republic joined the Commonwealth in 1982, some 17 years after gaining independence from the United Kingdom. In October 2016, the Maldives announced its withdrawal from the Commonwealth[^cite_note-148] in protest at allegations of human rights abuse and failing democracy.[^cite_note-guardian_2016-20] The Maldives enjoys close ties with Commonwealth members [Seychelles](27288.md) and [Mauritius](19201.md). The Maldives and [Comoros](5403.md) are also both members of the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md). Following his election as president in 2018, Ibrahim Mohamed Solih's administration sought to rejoin the Commonwealth after showing evidence of democratic reform,[^cite_note-149] leading to its readmission on 1 February 2020.[^cite_note-guardian_2020-21]

As a result of sanctions imposed upon the [Russian oligarchs](1209554.md) by the West in response to the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), many of them sought refuge for their mega-yachts in the Maldives due to the absence of an extradition treaty with the United States and other countries.[^cite_note-150]

Following a cabinet meeting, in June 2024, the government of the Maldives decided to ban [Israeli passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_passport) holders from entering the country, as a response to the ongoing [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]

### Military

Main article: [Maldives National Defence Force](1115368.md)



Maldivian soldiers at a presidential address in February 2024

The Maldives National Defence Force is the combined security organisation responsible for defending the security and sovereignty of the Maldives, having the primary task of being responsible for attending to all internal and external security needs of the Maldives, including the protection of the [Exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) (EEZ) and the maintenance of peace and security.[^cite_note-154] The MNDF component branches are the [Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldivian_Coast_Guard), [Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MNDF_Marine_Corps), [Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Forces_\(Maldives\)), Service Corps, Defence Intelligence Service, Military Police, Corps of Engineers, Special Protection Group, Medical Corps, Adjutant General's Corps, Air Corps, and Fire and Rescue Service. The Maldives has an arrangement with India allowing cooperation on radar coverage.

As a water-bound nation, much of its security concerns life at sea. Almost 99% of the country is covered by sea and the remaining 1% land is scattered over an area of 800 km (497 mi) × 120 km (75 mi), with the largest island being not more than 8 km<sup>2</sup> (3 sq mi). Therefore, the duties assigned to the MNDF of maintaining surveillance over the Maldives' waters and providing protection against foreign intruders poaching in the EEZ and territorial waters, are immense tasks from both logistical and economic viewpoints. The Coast Guard plays a vital role in carrying out these functions. To provide timely security its patrol boats are stationed at various MNDF Regional Headquarters. The Coast Guard is also assigned to respond to maritime distress calls and to conduct search and rescue operations promptly. In 2019, the Maldives signed the UN [treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).[^cite_note-155]

### Law

See also: [Judiciary of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_the_Maldives) and [Law enforcement in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_the_Maldives)

According to the [Constitution of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Maldives), "the judges are independent, and subject only to the Constitution and the law. When deciding matters on which the Constitution or the law is silent, judges must consider Islamic [Shari'ah](28840.md)".[^cite_note-156]

Islam is the official religion of the Maldives and open practice of any other religion is forbidden.[^cite_note-157] The 2008 constitution says that the republic "is based on the principles of Islam" and that "no law contrary to any principle of Islam can be applied". Non-Muslims are prohibited from becoming citizens.[^cite_note-Hirschl2011-158]

The requirement to adhere to a particular religion and prohibition of public worship following other religions is contrary to Article 18 of the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights) and Article 18 of the [International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Covenant_on_Civil_and_Political_Rights) to which the Maldives has recently become party[^cite_note-159] and was addressed in the Maldives' reservation in adhering to the Covenant claiming that "The application of the principles set out in Article 18 of the Covenant shall be without prejudice to the Constitution of the Republic of Maldives."[^cite_note-160]

A new [penal code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_code) came into effect on 16 July 2015, replacing the 1968 law, the first modern, comprehensive penal code to incorporate the major tenets and principles of Islamic law.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

[Same-sex relations](5488304.md) are illegal in the Maldives, although tourist resorts typically operate as exceptions to this law.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]

### Human rights

Main article: [Human rights in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_the_Maldives)

Human rights in the Maldives is a contentious issue. In its 2011 [Freedom in the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_in_the_World) report, [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) declared the Maldives "Partly Free", claiming a reform process that had made headway in 2009 and 2010 had stalled.[^cite_note-FH_2011-166] The [United States Bureau of Democracy, Human Rights and Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Democracy,_Human_Rights,_and_Labor) claims in their 2012 report on human rights practices in the country that the most significant problems are corruption, lack of [religious freedom](46006.md), abuse, and unequal treatment of women.[^cite_note-State_2012-167]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Maldives)



Malé harbour

Historically, the Maldives provided enormous quantities of [cowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowry) shells, [an international currency of the early ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_money). From the 2nd century CE, the islands were known as the "Money Isles" by the Arabs.[^cite_note-168] *[Monetaria moneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetaria_moneta)* was used for centuries as a currency in Africa, and huge amounts of [Maldivian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldive_Islands) cowries were introduced into Africa by western nations during the period of [slave trade](49005.md).[^cite_note-169] The cowry is now the symbol of the Maldives [Monetary Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_Monetary_Authority).

In the early 1970s and 1980s, the Maldives was one of the world's 20 poorest countries, with a population of 100,000.[^cite_note-170] The economy at the time was largely dependent on fisheries and trading local goods such as [coir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coir) rope, [ambergris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambergris) (Maavaharu), and [coco de mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_de_mer) (Tavakkaashi) with neighbouring countries and [East Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian) countries.[^cite_note-171]

The Maldivian government began a largely successful [economic reform](2134402.md) programme in the 1980s, initiated by lifting import quotas and giving more opportunities to the private sector. At that time, the tourism sector was in its infant stage but would later play a significant role in the nation's development. Agriculture and manufacturing continue to play lesser roles in the economy, constrained by the limited availability of cultivable land and the shortage of domestic labour.

### Tourism

Main articles: [Tourism in the Maldives](413341.md), [Diving in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_in_the_Maldives), and [List of mosques in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mosques_in_the_Maldives)



Filitheyo island beach with tall palm trees and blue fresh lagoons

The Maldives remained largely unknown to tourists until the early 1970s. Only 200 islands are home to its 382,751 inhabitants.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] The other islands are used entirely for economic purposes, of which tourism and agriculture are the most dominant. [Tourism](413341.md) accounts for 28% of the GDP and more than 60% of the Maldives' foreign exchange receipts. Over 90% of government tax revenue comes from import duties and tourism-related taxes.

The development of tourism fostered the overall growth of the [country's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Maldives). It created direct and indirect employment and income generation opportunities in other related industries. The first tourist resorts were opened in 1972 with Bandos Island Resort and Kurumba Village (the current name is Kurumba Maldives),[^cite_note-174] which transformed the Maldives' economy.



The [resort island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_island) of [Landaa Giraavaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landaa_Giraavaru) (Baa atoll)

According to the [Ministry of Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Tourism_\(Maldives\)), the emergence of tourism in 1972 transformed the economy, moving rapidly from dependence on fisheries to tourism. In just three and a half decades, the industry became the main source of income. Tourism was also the country's biggest foreign currency earner and the single largest contributor to the GDP. As of 2008, 89 resorts in the Maldives offered over 17,000 beds and hosted over 600,000 tourists annually.[^cite_note-175] In 2019, over 1.7 million visitors came to the islands.[^cite_note-176]

The number of resorts increased from 2 to 92 between 1972 and 2007. As of 2007, over 8,380,000 tourists had visited the Maldives.[^cite_note-177]

The country has [six heritage Maldivian coral mosques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mosques_in_the_Maldives) listed as [UNESCO](21786641.md) tentative sites.[^cite_note-178]

#### Visitors

See also: [§ Transportation](#Transportation), and [Visa policy of Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_policy_of_Maldives)



Sign outside the Velana International Airport

Visitors to the Maldives do not need to apply for a visa pre-arrival, regardless of their country of origin, provided they have a passport with at least 1 month validity, a complete [travel itinerary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_itinerary) (including return journey confirmed tickets, prepaid confirmed booking at a registered facility,[^cite_note-179] proof of financial means for sufficient funds to support the stay in the Maldives, and entry requirements for their onward destinations—such as visa and passport validity), a Traveller Declaration form (which must be filled out and submitted by all foreigners arriving in the Maldives within 96 hours of the flight), and a Yellow Fever Vaccination Certificate, if applicable.[^cite_note-180] However, entry is denied to Israeli passports.[^cite_note-doi-181]

Most visitors arrive at [Velana International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velana_International_Airport), on [Hulhulé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulhul%C3%A9) Island, adjacent to the capital Malé. The airport is served by flights to and from [India](14533.md), [Sri Lanka](26750.md), [Doha](26214389.md), [Dubai](211583.md), [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md), [Singapore](27318.md), [Dhaka](56656.md), [Istanbul](3391396.md), and major airports in [South-East Asia](28741.md) like [Kuala Lumpur International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur_International_Airport) in Malaysia, as well as charters from Europe like [Charles De Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport) in France. [Gan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_International_Airport), on the southern atoll of Addu, also serves an international flight to [Malpensa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Malpensa_Airport) in Milan several times a week. [British Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways) offers direct flights to the Maldives from [Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Airport).[^cite_note-182]

### Fishing industry

Main article: [Fishing industry in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry_in_the_Maldives)



A mechanised traditional inter-island dhoni stripped of its sails

For many centuries the Maldivian economy was entirely dependent on fishing and other [marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean) products. Fishing remains the main occupation of the people and the government gives priority to the fisheries sector.

The [mechanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanisation) of the [traditional fishing boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_fishing_boat) called *[dhoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhoni_\(fishing_vessel\))* in 1974 was a major milestone in the development of the fisheries industry. A fish canning plant was installed on [Felivaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felivaru) in 1977, as a joint venture with a Japanese firm. In 1979, a Fisheries Advisory Board was set up with the mandate of advising the government on policy guidelines for the overall development of the fisheries sector. Manpower development programmes began in the early 1980s, and fisheries education was incorporated into the school curriculum. [Fish aggregating devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_aggregating_device) and navigational aids were located at various strategic points. Moreover, the opening up of the [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) (EEZ) of the Maldives for fisheries has further enhanced the growth of the fisheries sector.

As of 2010, fisheries contributed over 15% of the country's GDP and engaged about 30% of the country's workforce. Fisheries were also the second-largest foreign exchange earner after [tourism](#Tourism).

### Transportation

Main articles: [Transport in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_the_Maldives) and [List of airports in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_the_Maldives)

&#x20;\


[Velana International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velana_International_Airport) is the principal gateway to the Maldives; it is adjacent to the capital city Malé and is connected by [Sinamalé Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinamal%C3%A9_Bridge). International travel is available on government-owned [Island Aviation Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Aviation_Services) (branded as Maldivian), which operates [DHC-6 Twin Otter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHC-6_Twin_Otter) seaplanes and to nearly all Maldivian domestic airports with several [Bombardier Dash 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Dash_8) aircraft, and one [Airbus A320](164933.md) with international service to India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Malaysia, Thailand, and China.

In the Maldives, there are three main ways to travel between islands: by domestic flight, by [seaplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane), or by boat.[^cite_note-183] For several years two seaplane companies were operating: TMA ([Trans Maldivian Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_Maldivian_Airways)) and [Maldivian Air Taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldivian_Air_Taxi), but these merged in 2013 under the name TMA. The seaplane fleet is entirely made up of DHC-6 Twin Otters. There is also another airline, [Villa Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyme_\(Villa_Air\)), which operates using [ATR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_\(aircraft_manufacturer\)) planes to domestic airports, principally [Villa-Maamigili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_International_Airport_Maamigili), [Dharavandhoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharavandhoo_Airport) and some others. [Manta Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manta_Air) began its first scheduled seaplane service in 2019. Its seaplane fleet is made up of DHC-6 Twin Otter aircraft. In addition to the seaplane service, [Manta Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manta_Air) uses [ATR 72–600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_\(aircraft_manufacturer\)) aircraft to operate domestic flights to [Dhaalu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaalu_Airport), [Dharavandhoo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharavandhoo_Airport) and [Kooddoo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kooddoo_Airport) from the main [Velana International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velana_International_Airport).[^cite_note-184] Depending on the distance of the destination island from the airport, resorts organise [speedboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedboat) transfers or [seaplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane) flights directly to the resort island jetty for their guests. Several daily flights operate from [Velana International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velana_International_Airport) to the 18 domestic and international airports in the country. Scheduled ferries also operate from Malé to many of the atolls. The traditional Maldivian boat is called a [dhoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhoni_\(fishing_vessel\)), one of the oldest known sea vessels in the Maldives.[^cite_note-185] Speedboats and seaplanes tend to be more expensive, while travel by dhoni, although slower, is relatively cheaper and convenient.

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Maldives)

The largest and the only ethnic group in the country are [Maldivians](23582208.md) (*Dhivehin*), native to the historic region of the Maldive Islands comprising today's Republic of Maldives and the island of [Minicoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minicoy) in [Union territory of Lakshadweep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshadweep), India. They share the same culture and speak the [Dhivehi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhivehi_language). They are principally an [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples) people, having traces of Middle Eastern, South Asian, [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) and African genes in the population.

In the past, there was also a small [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils) population known as the [Giraavaru people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giraavaru_people). This group has now been almost completely assimilated into the larger Maldivian society but were native to the island of [Giraavaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giraavaru_\(Kaafu_Atoll\)), which was evacuated in 1968 due to heavy erosion of the island.[^cite_note-189] A small [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people) population in the Maldives also made up about 0.7% of the total population in 2000.[^cite_note-190]

Some social stratification exists on the islands. It is not rigid, since rank is based on varied factors, including occupation, wealth, Islamic virtue, and family ties. Instead of a complex [caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste) system, there was merely a distinction between noble (bēfulhu) and common people in the Maldives. Members of the social elite are concentrated in Malé.

The population doubled by 1978, and the [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) rate peaked at 3.4% in 1985. By the 2006 census, the population had reached 298,968,[^cite_note-191] although the census in 2000 showed that the population growth rate had declined to 1.9%. [Life expectancy at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) stood at 46 years in 1978, and later rose to 72. Infant mortality has declined from 12.7% in 1977 to 1.2% today, and adult literacy reached 99%. Combined school enrolment reached the high 90s. The population was projected to have reached 317,280 in 2010.[^cite_note-192]

The 2022 Population, Housing and Economic Census listed the total population in the Maldives as 515,132 of which 382,639 were resident Maldivians and 132,493 were resident foreigners, with the foreign-born population comprising 26% of the total population. The six largest nationalities among migrants were Bangladeshi (56.4 percent), Indian (24.9 percent), Sri Lankan (8.6 percent), Nepali (3.9 percent), Indonesian (1.7 percent), and Filipino (1.3 percent).[^cite_note-\:0-193][^cite_note-194]

An increasing share of the Maldivian population is concentrated in Male. In 2022, approximately 41% of the population resided in the capital, compared to just 27% in 2000.[^cite_note-\:0-193]

### Languages

See also: [Maldivian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldivian_language)

&#x20;The official and national language is [Dhivehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldivian_language), an [Indo-Aryan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages) closely related to the [Sinhala language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language) of Sri Lanka. The first known script used to write Dhivehi is the *[eveyla akuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eveyla_akuru)* script, which is found in the historical recording of kings (*raadhavalhi*). Later a script called *[Dhives Akuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhives_Akuru)* was used for a long period. The present-day script is called [Thaana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaana) and is written from right to left. [Thaana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaana) is derived from a mix of the old indigenous script of [Dhives Akuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhives_Akuru) and [Arabic abjad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script). Thaana is said to have been introduced during the reign of [Mohamed Thakurufaanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Thakurufaanu_al-Auzam).



Thaana script

English is widely spoken by the locals of the Maldives:[^cite_note-195] "Following the nation's opening to the outside world, the introduction of English as a medium of instruction at the secondary and tertiary levels of education, and its government's recognition of the opportunities offered through tourism, English has now firmly established itself in the country."[^cite_note-196]

Otherwise, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) is taught in schools and mosques, as [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) is the [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion). The Maldivian population has formal or informal education in the reading, writing, and pronunciation of the Arabic language, as part of the compulsory religious education for all primary and secondary school students.[^cite_note-stategovreligion-1]

#### Thikijehi Thaana

These additional letters were added to the Thaana alphabet by adding dots ([*nukuthaa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuqta)) to existing letters, to allow for transliteration of Arabic loanwords, as previously Arabic loanwords were written using the Arabic script. Their usage is inconsistent, and becoming less frequent as the spelling changes to reflect pronunciation by Maldivians, rather than the original Arabic pronunciation, as the words get absorbed into the Maldivian language.

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Maldives)

After the long [Buddhist period of Maldivian history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_the_Maldives),[^cite_note-197] Muslim traders introduced Islam. Maldivians converted to Islam by the mid-12th century. The islands have had a long history of [Sufic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) orders, as can be seen in the history of the country such as the building of tombs. They were used until as recently as the 1980s for seeking the help of buried [saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint). They can be seen next to some old mosques and are considered a part of the Maldives's [cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage).

Other aspects of [tassawuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tassawuf), such as ritualised [dhikr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhikr) ceremonies called Maulūdu ([Mawlid](1285650.md)) – the [liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgy) of which included recitations and certain supplications in a melodic tone – existed until very recent times. These Maulūdu festivals were held in ornate tents specially built for the occasion. At present Islam is the official religion of the entire population, as adherence to it is required for citizenship.[^cite_note-stategovreligion-1]

According to Arab traveller [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta), the person responsible for this conversion was a Sunni Muslim visitor named Abu al-Barakat Yusuf al-Barbari, sailing from what is today [Morocco](19291.md). He is also referred to as Tabrizugefaanu. His venerated tomb now stands on the grounds of Medhu Ziyaaraiy, across the street from the Friday Mosque, or [Hukuru Miskiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%A9_Friday_Mosque), in Malé. Originally built in 1153 and re-built in 1658,[^cite_note-Yoosuf-59] this is one of the country's oldest surviving mosques.

[Felix Wilfred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Wilfred) notes that the number of Christians in the Maldives as 1,400 or 0.4% of the country's population.[^cite_note-198]

Since the adoption of the [2008 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Maldives#2008_Constitution), Islam is established as the country's state religion and Maldivian citizens are required by law to follow [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam).[^cite_note-stategovreligion-1] However, in 2020, independent studies found that approximately 0.29% of the population is Christian (roughly split between [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) and [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant)).[^cite_note-199]

### Education

Main article: [Education in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Maldives)

The [Maldives National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_National_University) is one of the country's institutions of higher education.[^cite_note-200][^cite_note-201] In 1973, the Allied Health Services Training Centre (the forerunner of the Faculty of Health Sciences) was established by the [Ministry of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_\(Maldives\)).[^cite_note-202] The Vocational Training Centre was established in 1974, providing training for mechanical and electrical trades.[^cite_note-203] In 1984, the Institute for Teacher Education was created and the School of Hotel and Catering Services was established in 1987 to provide trained personnel for the tourist industry.[^cite_note-204] In 1991, the Institute of Management and Administration was created to train staff for public and private services. In 1998, the Maldives College of Higher Education was founded. The Institute of Shar'ah and Law was founded in January 1999. In 2000 the college launched its first-degree programme, Bachelor of Arts. On 17 January 2011, the Maldives National University Act was passed by the President of Maldives; Maldives National University was named on 15 February 2011. In 2015 under a Presidential decree the College of Islamic Studies was changed into the Islamic University of Maldives (IUM).[^cite_note-205]

The Maldivian government now offers 3 different scholarships to students who have completed their higher secondary education with results above a certain threshold, with ranks of the scholarship received depending on the merits achieved by students on their year 12 examinations.[^cite_note-206]

### Health

Main article: [Health in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_the_Maldives)

The Human Rights Measurement Initiative reports that the Maldives is meeting 5.1 out of 10 of the expected fulfilment for the right to health considering its income level.[^cite_note-Hrm-207] Specifically for children's health rights, The Maldives attains 98.0% of the anticipated level based on its current income.[^cite_note-Hrm-207] Regarding adult health rights, the country achieves 99.7% of the expected fulfilment considering its income level. However, in terms of reproductive health rights, the Maldives falls into the "very bad" category, as it fulfills only 18.2% of the expected achievement based on its available resources.[^cite_note-208]

[Life expectancy at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy_at_birth) in the Maldives was 77 years in 2011.[^cite_note-209] Infant mortality fell from 34 per 1,000 in 1990 to 15 in 2004. There is an increasing disparity between health in the capital and on the other islands. There is also a problem of [malnutrition](258979.md). Imported food is expensive.[^cite_note-210]

On 24 May 2021, the Maldives had the world's fastest-growing [COVID-19 outbreak](62750956.md), with the highest number of infections per million people over the prior 7 and 14 days, according to data compiled by [Bloomberg](3057255.md).[^cite_note-211] Doctors warned that increasing demand for COVID-19 care could hinder their ability to handle other health emergencies in the Maldives.[^cite_note-212] The reason for the outbreak was the [Delta variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2_Delta_variant).

&#x20;The culture of the Maldives is influenced by the cultures of the people of different ethnicities who have settled on the islands throughout the times.



[Maahefun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maahefun) Festival in [Fuvahmulah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuvahmulah)

Since the 12th century CE, there were also influences from [Arabia](47858.md) in the language and culture of the Maldives because of the conversion to Islam and its location as a crossroads in the central Indian Ocean.[^cite_note-213] This was due to the long trading history between the Far East and the Middle East.

Reflective of this is the fact that the Maldives has had the highest national divorce rate in the world for many decades. This, it is hypothesised, is due to a combination of liberal Islamic rules about divorce and the relatively loose marital bonds that have been identified as common in non- and semi-sedentary peoples without a history of fully developed agrarian property and kinship relations.[^cite_note-214]

### Media

Main articles: [Television Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_Maldives), [Voice of Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_Maldives), and [List of newspapers in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_the_Maldives)

[PSM news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSM_news) serves as the country's main media, owned by the [government of the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Maldives).[^cite_note-215] The newspaper was founded on 3 May 2017, in the celebration of [World Press Freedom Day](220703.md).[^cite_note-216] The Maldives has been ranked one–hundred in the [World Press Freedom Index](8264708.md) 2023 and 106 in 2024.[^cite_note-217] The country's first daily newspaper, [Haveeru Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haveeru_Daily) News was the first and longest–serving newspaper in the history of the Maldives, which was registered on 28 December 1978, and dissolved in 2016.[^cite_note-218] Article 28 of the Maldives [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_Constitution) guarantees freedom of the press and stipulates that:

> No person shall be compelled to disclose the source of any information that is espoused, disseminated, or published by that person.[^cite_note-219]

However, this protection is compromised by the Evidence Act, which came into effect in January 2023 and grants courts the authority to compel journalists to reveal their confidential sources. Maldives Media Council (MMC) and Maldives [Journalists Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_Journalists_Association) (MJA) serve as crucial watchdogs in addressing and combating these threats. Newspapers *Sun Online*, *[Mihaaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihaaru)* and its English edition named *[The Edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Edition),* and *Avas* serve as well–known private news outlets.[^cite_note-220]

### Sports



[Flyboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyboard) in Maldives

Sports are deeply ingrained in the culture of the Maldives, with various activities based on traditional pastimes and modern sporting pursuits. Given its geography of scattered islands surrounded by the Indian Ocean, [water sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_sports) naturally hold a prominent position. [Surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing), in particular, has gained international recognition. Locations such as the atolls of North and South [Malé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%A9), [Thulusdhoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thulusdhoo), and [Himmafushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himmafushi) offer ideal surfing conditions.[^cite_note-221] Additionally, diving and snorkelling are immensely popular.[^cite_note-222]



[Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_national_football_team) against [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_national_football_team) at the [2021 SAFF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_SAFF_Championship)

[Football in the Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_the_Maldives) is one of the most widely played and passionately-followed sports in the country.[^cite_note-223] The Maldives [national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_national_football_team) competes in regional and international tournaments. The country has its own domestic football league, the [Dhivehi Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhivehi_Premier_League), featuring clubs from various atolls vying for supremacy.[^cite_note-224] Matches often draw large crowds. [Futsal](188197.md) is also popular, especially among younger generations, with numerous indoor facilities providing spaces for friendly matches and competitive leagues.

Bodu Beru, a rhythmic drumming and [dance performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_performance), often accompanies events for traditional sports such as "Baibalaa", a game resembling [volleyball](32558.md) but played with a woven ball made from dried [coconut palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_palm) leaves, and "Fenei Bashi", a form of [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling).
