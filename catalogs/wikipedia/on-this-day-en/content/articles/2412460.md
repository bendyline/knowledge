**Airsoft guns** (**ASG** or *AEG*) are [air guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_guns) used in [airsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airsoft) sports. They are a special type of low-power [smoothbore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoothbore) [guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun) designed to shoot non-metallic spherical [projectiles](144553.md) ([airsoft pellets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airsoft_pellet)) often colloquially (but incorrectly) referred to as "[BBs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BB_\(ammunition\))", which are typically made of (but not limited to) [plastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic) or [biodegradable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodegradable_polymer) [resin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resin) materials. Airsoft gun [powerplants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerplant) are designed to have low [muzzle energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzzle_energy) ratings (generally between 1.0 to 2.5 [joules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule) or 0.74–1.84 [ft⋅lb<sub>f</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot-pound_\(energy\))) and the pellet balls have significantly less [effective range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_range), [penetrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penetration_\(weaponry\)) and [stopping power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stopping_power) than conventional [airgun pellets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airgun_pellet) and [shots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shot_\(pellet\)), and these characteristics make airsoft guns generally safe as [toy guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toy_gun) for [competitive sporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competitive_sport) and [recreational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreation) purposes as long as adequate [protective gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protective_gear) (e.g. [protective eyewear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protective_eyewear), [face shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_shield), firm [clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing)) are worn over the more vulnerable exposed parts of the body.

Depending on the mechanism for pellet propulsion, airsoft guns fall into two broad groups just like conventional airguns. One group is spring-piston guns, which utilize [elastic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_energy) of a [coil spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coil_spring) to propel [piston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston_pump) [air pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pump) that is either manually [cocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sear_\(firearm\)) (i.e. "spring guns", commonly referred to as "*springers*", and are the simplest, cheapest and generally of low quality); or automatically cycled by a [battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_battery)-powered [electric motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_motor) [gearbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gearbox) (i.e. "automatic electric guns" or AEGs). The other group is [pneumatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumatic) guns (i.e. "gas guns"), which operate by [bash valve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bash_valve)-controlled pressure release of prefilled [bottled gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottled_gas) such as [compressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_gas) [propane](23643.md) mixed with [silicone oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicone_oil) (commonly known as "Green Gas") or [CO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) canisters ([Powerlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerlet).). Pneumatic guns can be cycled either manually by hand-pulling back the [bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolt_\(firearms\)) or [slide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistol_slide), which loads the subsequent shot; or automatically by having the released propellant gas also pushing back the [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_\(firearms\)) (i.e. "gas blowback guns" or GBBs).

Airsoft guns are often designed to aesthetically resemble real [firearms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearms), which provides more [realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism) but also makes it difficult to distinguish them from actual lethal weapons. Despite some jurisdictions issuing a mandate of bright [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(colour\))-colored [muzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzzle_\(firearms\)) tips for better identification, the strong aesthetic resemblance has led to incidents of [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) misidentifying airsoft gun handlers as [active shooters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_shooter) with real firearms and subsequently [shooting them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_shooting) under the [probable cause](146751.md) and [plausible deniability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plausible_deniability) of thinking they thus pose a deadly threat.[^cite_note-1]

## Usage



[Systema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systema_Engineering) Professional Training Weapon System M4A1 MAX



Soldiers with the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army) 187th Ordnance Battalion prepared to clear a room during urban combat training at the battalion's Field Training Exercise site. The soldiers were armed with [airsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airsoft) weapons as part of a pilot program in 2009.

See also: [Airsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airsoft) and [MilSim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MilSim)

Airsoft in the past was used almost solely for recreational purposes, but in 2012, gas blowback (GBB) airsoft technology became adopted by US federal and state institutions as an affordable and reliable tactical training tool for [close quarters battles](356000.md). In 2018, the [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) officially adopted [SIG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIG_Sauer#SIG_Air_brand)-branded P229 airsoft pistol for training.[^cite_note-Matthew_Cox-2][^cite_note-Matthew_Moss-3] The GBB guns allow for correct weapon manipulation drills, [muscle memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_memory) training, [stress inoculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_behavioral_therapy#Stress_inoculation_training) and [force-on-force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposing_force) simulations for a fraction of the cost of conventional bolt conversion kits that use marking cartridges with [wax bullets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wax_bullet) from training ammunition manufacturers such as UTM and Simunition. Airsoft guns also allow basic and advanced shooter training in a safer environment by reducing the risks of accidental injury or death from a [negligent discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negligent_discharge).

There are clubs, teams, and even athletic associations devoted to airsoft events around the world. Europe is home to some of the largest events, with skirmishes of over 2,000 people participating. In North America, in 2012 alone, [Fulda Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulda_Gap) Airsoft Game in [Taylorsville, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylorsville,_North_Carolina) had over 1,100 participants, and Operation Lion Claws Military Simulation Series (OLCMSS) had 800 people attend at [George Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Air_Force_Base) in [Victorville, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorville,_California). American Mil-sim, Black Sheep, and Ballahack also host large games. Events may include a variety of equipment ranging from small arms to armored vehicles.[^cite_note-4]

In many countries, every airsoft gun owner and active enthusiast must be affiliated with an accredited airsoft association or federation. Most airsoft players host games at a registered field where combat situations are simulated using airsoft weaponry like replica [pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistol), [submachine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submachine_guns), [carbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbine)/[assault rifles](18950865.md), [DMRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_marksman_rifle)/[sniper rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sniper_rifle), [light machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_machine_gun), [grenades](20353360.md) and [landmines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmine). Great variety and profusion of [militaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militaria) is usually used. [Historical reenactment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_reenactment) of famous war situations is another favorite of many airsoft players and clubs.[^cite_note-5] In addition, a number of companies such as [Systema Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systema_Engineering) and Celsius Technology manufacture ultra-realistic high-velocity airsoft rifles designed specifically for the police and military for non-lethal training purposes. People today may also use airsoft guns as [props](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatrical_property) for [film making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_making), particularly after the [*Rust* shooting incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_shooting_incident).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

An Airsoft Two-Tone Gun is an Airsoft replica which has been sprayed over 51% a bright, non military colour, so that it complies with the Violent Crimes Reduction Act (VCRA, 2006), allowing anyone to buy a replica without having to prove that they have a legitimate use for it.[^cite_note-8]

## Types

### Spring-powered



Airsoft [Makarov pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makarov_pistol) spring system

**Spring-powered airsoft guns** (or "**air-cocking guns**" as called by [Tokyo Marui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Marui)) are [single-shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-shot) devices that use the [elastic potential energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_potential_energy) stored within a compressed [coil spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coil_spring) to drive a [piston air pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston_pump), which is released upon trigger-pull and rapidly pressurizes the air within the pump cylinder to in turn "blow" pellets down the [gun barrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_barrel). These guns are almost identical (though simplified and underpowered) in design to [spring-piston air guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_gun#Spring-piston) and have the same operating principles. The user must manually recompress the spring under [stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_\(mechanics\)) prior to each shot, typically by pulling back the [slide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistol_slide) (pistols), [bolt handle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocking_handle) (rifles) or [forend pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump_action) (shotguns) on the weapon, which cocks and readies the gun. Because of this, spring guns are incapable of [automatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_firearm) or [semi-automatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-automatic_firearm) firing by design.[^cite_note-9]

Spring-powered airsoft guns are generally not as powerful as gas-powered ones, but are more powerful than electric airsoft guns because stiffer springs can often be used without the worry of overloading any motor-gearbox, although some spring [shotguns](26838.md) and [bolt-action rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolt-action_rifle) can be very powerful with [muzzle velocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzzle_velocity) up to 400–700 ft/s (120–210 m/s). Spring guns are generally inexpensive (except the high-power sniper rifles and shotguns), and may not last long (depending on the build quality) due to the tension exerted on the gun parts by the recoiling of a powerful spring. However, many spring guns can be modified and upgraded to last longer and shoot more powerfully.[^cite_note-10]

Low-end spring guns tend to be much cheaper than their electric equivalents due to their lack of electrical components ([electric motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_motor)/[actuator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuator), spring-[gearbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gearbox) assembly, battery and battery charger). These guns are less common in competitions because their rate-of-fire is a disadvantage against automatic guns in close combat, and they do not provide enough accuracy and power for long-range use. There are some exceptions, however, as higher-end spring-powered airsoft rifles can be quite expensive; these guns are typically suited for "marksman" applications in airsoft matches and provide competitive muzzle velocities. Additionally, pump shotguns are sometimes used for both short and long range engagements.

In colder weather, spring pistols are more reliable than gas-powered pistols and even the batteries on automatic electric pistols (AEPs) both of which can be adversely affected by extremely low temperatures. This represents one of the major advantages of spring airsoft gun, as it can be fired in practically any situation without relying on [batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_\(electricity\)) or [bottled gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottled_gas). This independence from external power sources causes some players to favor spring-powered guns. Spring guns are also less susceptible to the effects of water, where a battery-powered gun could [short-circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-circuit) and malfunction when wet.[^cite_note-11]

Because of their low price, availability, and simplicity, spring guns tend to act as training guns for new players. In the UK, they are affectionately known as "springers" and were often a player's introduction to the sport due to the entry-level cost in comparison to AEG and GBB weapons. Some veteran airsoft players still rely on [sniper rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sniper_rifle) and [shotgun](26838.md)-type spring guns as a primary weapon due to reliability, high power, accuracy, low noise, and ease of repair compared to AEGs and GBB guns.

### &#x20;Battery-powered

#### Automatic electric guns (AEGs)



An illustration of the working of a Version 2 electric motor gearbox



A Dboys M4A1 AEG



An ARES L85 AEG customized with a Daniel Defense [rail interface system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_Integration_System), replica [ACOG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Combat_Optical_Gunsight) sight, Grip Pod and [Magpul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magpul) PTS EMAG.

Electrically powered airsoft guns use a spring-loaded piston pump just like spring guns, but instead of manual operation they typically use portable [rechargeable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rechargeable_battery) [battery packs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_pack) to power an internal [electric motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_motor), which powers a [gearbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gearbox), which in turn compresses the pump spring, propels the pellets via the compression of air, and loads the next pellet in cyclic fashion. [Selective fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_fire) options among [automatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_firearm), 3-round [burst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burst_fire) and [semi-automatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-automatic_firearm) operations are all possible, which gives these guns the popular name "**automatic electric guns**", or **AEGs**.[^cite_note-airsplat-12] They are the most commonly used and widely available type of airsoft gun.[^cite_note-13]

AEGs were developed in [Japan](15573.md) and the Japanese company [Tokyo Marui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Marui) is credited with creating the original gearbox system. In a Tokyo Marui AEG, the motor drives a [train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_train) of three [gears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears) mounted inside a gearbox, which then loads a pump piston against a spring. Once the spring is released, it pushes the piston plunger forward through the pump cylinder to propel a pellet resting within the [chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_\(firearms\)) forward through the barrel and out of the [muzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzzle_\(firearms\)). Many manufacturers have now more or less replicated this basic model, adding reinforced parts or minor improvements.[^cite_note-14]

The electric airsoft guns were powered primarily by [nickel-metal hydride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel-metal_hydride) (NiMH) batteries with varying [voltages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage) and [milliampere-hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milliampere-hour) ratings. The most common battery is an 8.4 V large [battery pack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_pack), with a capacity between 2200 and 5000 mAh. Also available are "mini" and "stick" batteries, which generally have 900 \~ 1600 mAh capacities. Voltages for NiMH batteries range from 7.2 V to 12 V. The usual rule of thumb is that the higher the mAh rating, the longer the battery lasts; the higher the voltage, the higher the rate of fire.[^cite_note-BatteryLord-15] Recently, however, the more energy-dense [lithium polymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_polymer) (Li-Po) batteries are becoming more popular in the airsoft world, since they last longer, have higher mAh and voltages, and can be charged more frequently without concerns for [voltage depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage_depression), while at the same time being small and lightweight.[^cite_note-16]

The most common AEGs are the [AR-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AR-15%E2%80%93style_rifle) series (such as [M16 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M16_rifle) and [M4 carbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_carbine), sometimes referred to as the [ArmaLite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArmaLite) or [Colt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colt's_Manufacturing_Company) series), the [H\&K MP5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_MP5) series, and the AK or [Kalashnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalashnikov_Concern) series. Also increasingly popular is the [H\&K G36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_G36) and, more recently, the [FN P90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN_P90) and [H\&K MP7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_MP7). Subsequently, numerous parts for repairs and modifications are commonly available for these rifles.

#### Low-powered electric guns (LPEGs)



(LPEG) Assault rifle [AK-74M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-74) replica by CYMA

Some airsoft guns are called *low-powered electric guns* (LPEGs) to distinguish them from the original, more expensive and more powerful AEGs even though their mechanical/electrical design and operation is similar.[^cite_note-\:1-17]

#### Medium-power electric guns (MPEGs)

MPEGs are the middle ground between LPEGs and AEGs.

Some companies, like UTG with their popular [MP5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_MP5) and [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47) models, have improved their quality to such an extent that some models are now considered more affordable AEGs.

#### Electric blowbacks

Electric blowbacks, also known as EBBs, are typically available at lower prices than their battery-powered counterparts. They generally run on AAA or AA batteries.[^cite_note-\:1-17] Most models utilizing this system are rifles. EBBs simulate the blowback action of a real pistol or rifle. Essentially an AEG in design, EBBs are just as powerful. However, a drawback to having the realistic recoil feature is the tendency of the battery to quickly deplete. Additionally, blowbacks can cause extra stress on the gear box which may result in a shorter life span.

#### Automatic electric pistols

Automatic electric pistols, abbreviated AEPs, were first introduced by [Tokyo Marui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Marui) in 2005 with their [Glock 18C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glock_18#Variants) (followed later by a [Beretta 93R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beretta_93R) model). They were the first handguns to incorporate an electrically powered system that is capable of fully automatic operation.[^cite_note-\:1-17]

In cold weather, AEPs are often considered better sidearms than gas powered pistols, because batteries are not as badly affected by frigid weather. Gases like CO<sub>2</sub> and [green gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_gas) are stored in liquid form and require heat in order to vaporize. A gas pistol at 10 °F (−12 °C) will usually only get one to two usable shots from a full magazine, and even will be at reduced power because of the lowered pressure of the gas.

Because the AEP gearbox and battery are smaller, the velocity of AEP BBs (usually between 200 and 280 ft/s (85.3 m/s)) is relatively slow by the standards of airsoft simulations, rendering them useful only for close-range simulation. However, the advanced hop up units on these new guns tend to compensate for the low power and can produce an effective range comparable to those of an AEG. CYMA has made a clone Glock 18C, which is a lower priced alternative.

An AEP differs from electric blow-backs because the AEP has a fixed slide (in which there is no external movement of the slide during operation), while an EBB simulates the "blow back" action in the slide experienced in a real pistol or Gas Blow Back (GBB). An AEP, however, has much more power and accuracy.

One of the newer AEP-styled guns is the Marui replica of the Heckler & Koch [MP7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MP7). It is considerably larger than either of the other guns, and can be upgraded to a much higher power through the use of an external battery, but uses the same system as the AEP, so the classification is ambiguous. It is slightly more powerful than the others and is a suitable choice for [CQB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CQB) (Close Quarter Battle) games due to its small size and decent barrel- to gun-length ratio.

Some [semi-automatic pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-automatic_pistol) can be modified to be automatic pistols. To make them more effective, they use rechargeable batteries supplied with the gun, which can be replaced with a larger battery to increase their [ROF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_of_fire).

Due to restrictions on size, either the electric motor or batteries have to occupy space in the hand grip, reducing the available space for a magazine. Because of this, most AEPs do not use a full size magazine found in most gas powered pistols. They are typically constructed almost entirely of plastic and have a light, toy-like feeling to them.

### Gas-powered

#### Gas blowback



A gas blowback [Heckler & Koch USP Compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_USP#USP_Compact) replica made by KSC



A gas no-blowback [Makarov pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makarov_pistol) replica by KWC

Gas-powered airsoft guns use the [pneumatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumatic) [potential energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_energy) stored within [compressed gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_gas) to drive the shooting mechanism, and thus operate according to an entirely different design principle to spring- or electrically powered airsoft guns. The most common type seen is the gas blowback (GBB) guns. These gas guns use an internal [canister](1179236.md) (usually within the [magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magazine_\(firearms\))) that upon trigger-pull releases the prefilled [bottled gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottled_gas) via a series of valves to propel the pellet and generate a [blowback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blowback_\(firearms\)), which simulates [recoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recoil) and cyclically loads the next shot. They are capable of both automatic and semi-automatic operation.[^cite_note-18]

The most common gases [^cite_note-19][^cite_note-gas-20] used are "[green gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_gas)" and [propane](23643.md) which requires an adaptor. [HFC-134a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HFC-134a) is also commonly used, particularly with pistols which have plastic sliders due to the lower pressure giving a smaller chance of damage to the weaker slide. Less commonly used gases include "red gas" (which is actually [HCFC-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HCFC-22)), [CO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) and [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen)/high-pressure air. However, it is unlawful to use HCFC-22 as a propellant in the US, as it is a Class II [ozone-depleting substance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone-depleting_substance) and its use as an aerosol propellant has been banned since January 1994 under section 610(d) of Clean Air Act.[^cite_note-epa-21] Red gas is usually avoided unless the airsoft gun has undergone modification, as its relatively high critical pressure can cause damage to the airsoft gun, such as breakage of the slide or bolt. CO<sub>2</sub>, nitrogen, and high pressure air are less common because they need to be stored at higher pressures than "green gas" or HFC-134a.

The first ever gas powered airsoft guns were commonly referred to as "classic" guns, owing to their age. These guns were most commonly powered by liquid propellants such as [R-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dichlorodifluoromethane) (which was marketed by the Japanese as FLON-12 or [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont) tradename Freon 12) [CFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorofluorocarbon) feeding system with a majority of the configurations containing two tanks, one containing the R-12 and one used as an expansion tank, and the gun itself. R-12 was a commonly used refrigerant for car air conditioning and refrigerators, and is considered a highly potent ozone-depleting substance and listed as a Class I Ozone Depleting Substance by the US EPA. Its use as a general purpose aerosol propellant has been banned by the US EPA since March 1978 under 43 FR 11301 for use in aerosol use with a very few exceptions.[^cite_note-epa1-22] Its use is also banned in many countries under United Nations treaties. On December 31, 2008, the use of CFCs for medical inhalers was banned.

Later users modified these old guns to be powered by regulated CO<sub>2</sub> canisters or nitrogen/high pressure air bottles to increase power and consistency. However, these guns have largely been superseded by the newer and more versatile AEGs, or automatic electric guns. One of the reasons for this is because the most commonly available propellant, R-12, is costly. Additionally, at high flow rates, liquid propellants tend to cool down, eventually freezing. As cooldown progresses, the rate of fire gradually decreases until the gun ceases operation. The user would then be forced to wait for the propellant to warm up again. CO<sub>2</sub> is not affected as badly by this tendency, and nitrogen/high pressure air is immune to it. Furthermore, if liquid propellant is introduced into the gun's mechanism, rubber parts can freeze and eventually damage the gun. However, it is unlikely for this to occur since once the gas is released from the containing cylinder it instantly turns back into its gaseous state, and expands rapidly. It is doubtful whether the retained pressure behind the BB before it begins to accelerate down the barrel is enough to keep the gas in a liquid form. Also, any gun that is expected to be exposed to the intense cold of de-pressurizing gas should have materials that can handle it.

Gas power tends to be used in airsoft pistols where size constraints make electric-powered mechanisms impractical. Other instances where gas is favored are where adjustable velocities are required or where a blowback feature is desired. A blowback feature is a mechanism which cycles a slide or bolt to better simulate a real firearm's operation. Because of the mechanical complexities involved with distributing and regulating gas, these guns have largely given way to electric guns for less specialized applications, however, they still remain favorable amongst most airsofters. They are not just limited to pistols; submachine guns, sniper rifles and assault rifles commonly use gas mechanisms. Whilst the submachine gun replicas typically feature a blowback mechanism similar to the pistol replicas, sniper rifle replicas usually omit the blowback mechanism in favor of reduced [recoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recoil) and increased muzzle velocity.

Along with using gas to power guns, it is also applied for use in replica grenades. These grenades are either projectiles, fired from a grenade launcher such as the [M203](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M203) or [GP-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GP-30), or throwable. The shells work on the system of an internal piston, filled with gas. Either a series of BB's or in some cases a rubber or soft foam head is seated in or on top of the shell. When the pressure is released the projectile(s) are shot from the launcher sent downrange.

In the case of the throwable grenades, inside the grenade there is a similar piston to the one used in the shells, but is on a literal "timer" that allows the user to clear the area of effect. BBs or powder acts as the projectile in the case of these grenades. Currently both types of grenades are not very common, mostly because grenade launchers are quite expensive and the throwable grenades are not very reliable.

#### High pressure air systems

High pressure air (HPA) systems are a type of pneumatic airsoft weapon that use externally supplied high pressure air instead of internal gas canisters like the majority of gas-operated airsoft guns. They work by using a separate high-pressure [air tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_vessel) that is connected to the airsoft gun with a hose, which is connected with a [pneumatic motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumatic_motor) inside the gun (called an "engine") at where the gearbox would be in a normal electric airsoft gun. The engine is powered by a fire control unit that can adjust to the desired rates of fire as well as the [dwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timing_light) that determines how much air is released with each individual shot.[^cite_note-23]

- **AEG hybrid operation**: The magazine is loaded with shell cases, each containing a single plastic pellet. These shell casings can have a small red cap, the same as those found in any child's toy [cap gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_gun) placed on the top of them. These hybrid guns feature an electrically powered, full blowback system and operate on a "round-per-shell" basis such that for every pellet fired, a shell casing is ejected and the cap is fired providing a realistic sound and smoke effect. Since its debut, the only hybrid guns seen on the market are TOP M4A1, as well as [M1 Garand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_Garand), [Kar98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kar98), and other rifle models. These guns are the least common type of gun on the market today and are generally used by collectors and re-enactors rather than skirmishers.

- **Gas blowback hybrid operation**: Hybrid gas blowback airsoft guns are quite similar to hybrid AEGs and their operations are similar to ordinary gas blowback airsoft guns. A single 6 mm pellet is still loaded to a shell casing. Then it is placed into a magazine. The airsoft replica itself also has a tank for compressed gas as propellant (such as [green gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_gas)). So as the [slide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slide_\(firearms\))/[bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolt_\(firearms\)) is pulled back, it loads a shell into the chamber. As the trigger is pulled, it releases a small burst of propellant and the pellet is forced out the barrel.

Airsoft guns shoot [plastic pellets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airsoft_pellets) at velocities from 30 m/s (98 ft/s) for a low-end spring pistol, to 200 m/s (660 ft/s) for heavily upgraded customized sniper rifles. Most non-upgraded AEGs are in the middle, producing velocities from 90 m/s (300 ft/s) to 120 m/s (390 ft/s). The internal components of most guns can be upgraded which can increase the pellet velocity significantly. Using heavier pellets (.25 g, .3 g, etc.) will significantly reduce the gun's muzzle velocity, but can increase accuracy at range and reduce susceptibility to wind drift. Lighter pellets have less kinetic energy than their heavier pellets, despite their higher exit velocity. Decreasing the pellet's weight does not generally increase its range.

A common upgrade done by players is in the "[hop-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hop-up_\(airsoft\))" system, featured in most mid- to high-end AEGs, as well as gas guns and spring sniper rifles. In this system, the wall of a rubber tube, called a bucking, is forced into the upper path of the pellet right before it begins flying down the inner barrel. This contact imparts [backspin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backspin), which in turn gives the pellet a [Magnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_effect) [lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_\(force\)) to maintain a flatter trajectory for a longer period of time. This is adjusted by screws or gears that cause the bucking to only show a small or large presence in the barrel. Different degrees of firmness of the rubber are considered when a hop-up is being upgraded.

## Safety

See also: [Legal issues in airsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_issues_in_airsoft)

Airsoft is safe when played with proper protective gear. Most airsoft guns on the market are usually below 350 ft/s (110 m/s), but projectiles expelled from any type of airsoft gun can travel as slow as 65 ft/s (20 m/s) to more than 700 ft/s (210 m/s) and are capable of breaking skin at 350–400 ft/s (110–120 m/s). For example, skirmish play in the United Kingdom has a maximum of 350 ft/s, with some airsoft locations having a limit as low as 290 ft/s.[^cite_note-24] If under 300 ft/s (91 m/s), the hit would have to be within a short range. Blood can be drawn, but injuries that do occur are predominantly superficial. Full-seal protective eyewear (goggles or glasses) is widely considered the minimum protection for airsoft players, as the eyes may be injured by any type of impact. The least amount of protection a player should seek will meet or exceed ANSI/ISEA Z87.1 standards, which indicates the eyewear is rated for ballistic strikes. Mesh eyewear has also seen some limited use by players due to low cost and its inability to fog, although it has drawn some criticism for its non-impact rated construction and vulnerability to debris. Some impacts (particularly at close range with powerful guns) are capable of cracking or damaging teeth. Dentists have reported broken teeth that require root canal and crowns to repair damage. A face mask (like that used for paintball) is recommended to protect eyes and teeth. Metallic mesh masks and mouth guards have recently seen popular use.

There are [legal issues in airsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_issues_in_airsoft) as well as several rules imposed in a game by game basis. Most indoor airsoft fields only allow up to 350 ft/s (110 m/s), and most outdoor fields begin capping near the 410 ft/s (125 m/s) for rifles and 525 ft/s (160 m/s) for long-range guns such as sniper rifles. Most outdoor fields also impose a minimum engagement distance for guns firing over a certain range, normally for squad support weapons and sniper rifles. In order for an airsoft gun to cause any serious injury, it would have to be well over these limits and in close proximity. To even reach such speeds, the gun would have to be highly modified. So it is therefore unlikely to cause permanent or serious damage with any stock airsoft gun. The use of metallic BBs, or any foreign objects, is very dangerous for the user and other people and property in close vicinity, and may damage the airsoft gun as well. However, specially designed and built metallic 6 mm BBs for airsoft guns can be found on the market. These metallic BBs should not be used for airsoft play because they can break through goggles and other safety gear. Plastic BBs can be a mixture of PLA and Plastic causing less damage to persons.[^cite_note-25]

Although airsoft guns in the United States are generally sold with a 6 mm (0.24in.) or longer orange tip on the barrel in order to distinguish them from real firearms, this is not in fact required by federal law. There is some controversy on this topic as [Title 15 of the Code of Federal Regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_15_of_the_Code_of_Federal_Regulations), on foreign commerce and trade, stipulates that "no person shall manufacture, enter into commerce, ship, transport, or receive any toy, look-alike, or imitation firearm" without approved markings; these may include an orange tip, orange barrel plug, brightly colored exterior of the whole toy, or transparent construction (part 272.2, formerly part 1150.2). However, section 272.1 (formerly 1150.1) clearly indicates that these restrictions shall not apply to "traditional b-b, paint-ball, or pellet-firing air guns that expel a projectile through the force of compressed air, compressed gas or mechanical spring action, or any combination thereof."[^cite_note-26] Local laws may differ by jurisdiction. Full or partial preventive painting of airsoft guns as a legal obligation to avoid confusion of the airsoft replicas with real lethal weapons is in practice in several jurisdictions around the world.

A common controversy referred to as "pumping" or "roping" is the act of purposely firing 20+ airsoft rounds at one player until they run away or submit in pain. Popular videos such as *Rip Kid*[^cite_note-27] and *SC Trip 2015*[^cite_note-28] have both documented the act of pumping.

## Trademark problems

Some airsoft guns can be such accurate replicas that they violate [intellectual property](14724.md) laws (specifically those regarding [trademarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trademark)), most notably some models from [Tokyo Marui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Marui) bearing [Colt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colt_Manufacturing_Company) or [Heckler & Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch) trademarks that may not be imported into the United States. Certain companies such as Classic Army or ICS avoid this problem by licensing their replicas from the original manufacturers, such as [ArmaLite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArmaLite) by license from ActionSportGames or [Olympic Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Arms). The airsoft company ActionSportGames has licensed trademark rights from many well-known firearm manufacturers, such as Armalite, [Dan Wesson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Wesson_Firearms), [CZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cesk%C3%A1_zbrojovka_firearms), [Steyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steyr_Mannlicher), STI, [B\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCgger_&_Thomet) and [Franchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franchi_\(firearms\)).

Another company who licence the designs of gun companies is Evolution International. It has a portfolio of exclusive licenses from ADC [Armi Dallera Custom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armi_Dallera_Custom), [TangoDown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TangoDown?action=edit\&redlink=1), Z-M Weapons, DSR precision, Lone Star Tactical, and SAR. In addition, there have been reports of companies taking action in defense of their intellectual property rights. Some end users have made attempts to sell their guns, some in the style of [Glock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glock) pistols, only to find Glock blocking the sale and threatening legal action. In addition to these actions, Glock, as well as [Heckler & Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch), have blocked the sale, trade and distribution of replicas bearing resemblance to their products. Recently, Glock gave the licensing to the Glock name and likeness to Elite Force/Umarex to produce replica models of the real Glock series of firearms.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

The [United States Coast Guard](32223.md), which has long used the [.40 caliber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.40_S\&W) [SIG P229](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIG_P229) as its duty sidearm, announced on November 5, 2018, that it would acquire the SIG Air ProForce P229 airsoft pistol (then produced under [brand licensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_licensing) by French airsoft manufacturer CyberGun) as a training pistol for cadets and guardsmen.[^cite_note-Matthew_Cox-2][^cite_note-Matthew_Moss-3] In response, SIG Air, [SIG Sauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIG_Sauer)'s airgun division, announced on January 17, 2019, that it was introducing its own in-house ProForce airsoft line for professional training, with the initial offerings include the [M17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIG_Sauer_M17) and P229 airsoft pistol.[^cite_note-42] SIG Air also announced that "we are rapidly expanding the SIG AIR business, and it is important to us to assume full control to ensure all SIG Air products are of the highest quality", and it would no longer be licensing the "SIG" brand or trademarks for sale by commercial airsoft manufacturers.[^cite_note-43]
