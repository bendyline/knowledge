The **Bell 412** is a [utility helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_helicopter) of the [Huey family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huey_family) manufactured by [Bell Helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Helicopter). It is a development of the [Bell 212](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_212), with the major difference being the composite four-blade main rotor. It is a twin-turbine helicopter that has been popular on the civilian and military markets, and major users include Canada, Italy, and Japan. Several hundred have been produced since its introduction in 1979, and several iterations of upgrades and variations have been produced, such as with upgraded cockpit electronics.

It has been manufactured under license by [Agusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusta) in Italy as the **Agusta-Bell AB412**, in Indonesia by [Indonesian Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Aerospace), and in Japan by [Fuji Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru_Corporation). The **[CH-146 Griffon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_CH-146_Griffon)**, a Bell 412 variant for the Canadian Armed Forces, is manufactured in Bell-Textron's main commercial factory in Mirabel, Quebec.

## Design and development



Bell 412CF looking forward from the tail



Bell 412 on approach

Development began in the late 1970s, with two Bell 212s being converted into 412 prototypes. An advanced four-blade main rotor with a smaller diameter replaced the 212's two-blade rotor. A Bell 412 prototype first flew in August 1979. The initial model was certified in January 1981, with deliveries commencing in the same month.[^cite_note-airl_412-2] The 412 model was followed by the **412SP** (Special Performance) version, which featured a larger fuel capacity, a higher takeoff weight, and optional seating arrangements. In 1991, the **412HP** (High Performance) variant with improved transmission replaced the SP version in production.[^cite_note-airl_412-2]



A Chilean Air Force Bell 412

In the early 2000s, Bell offered the Bell 412EP as its Bell 412 LUH entrant in the U.S. Army Light Utility Helicopter program, where it competed against several other light helicopters for an order.[^cite_note-3]

The current production version, **412EP** (Enhanced Performance), is equipped with a dual digital automatic flight control system. In 2013, Bell introduced the **412EPI**, which includes an [electronic (digital) engine control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FADEC) upgrade for the PT6T-9 engine, and a [glass cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_cockpit) display system similar to the one installed on the Bell 429. Also featured is a [Garmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmin) touchscreen navigation system, plus the BLR Strake and Fast Fin upgrades for improved hover performance.[^cite_note-4]

The helicopter is powered by the Pratt and Whitney Canada twin-pack power plant with two turboshaft engines and has achieved the lowest in-flight shut-down rate of aircraft turboshaft engines. If one engine is shut down, a single engine can produce emergency power for 30 minutes.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

The aircraft has been produced in Montreal, Canada, in Italy under license by Agusta, in Indonesia by Indonesian Aerospace, and also in Japan by Subaru (Fuji Heavy Industries).[^cite_note-6] The Bell 412 was also initially manufactured in Texas, USA.[^cite_note-7]

The emergency medical configuration can carry 6 patients/wounded and two attendants, or two stretchers and up to four attendants.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

By 2022, over 1,300 Bell 412 helicopters had been delivered.[^cite_note-auto-1]

## Operators

The Bell 412 is used by private and commercial operators. It is particularly popular in the oil industries, military, law enforcement, and for [medevac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medevac) use. Around 2022-23, the unit cost for a 412EPX model was between US\$14.5 million[^cite_note-amr1-21] and US\$16 million.[^cite_note-ab1-22]

## Incidents and accidents

- On April 4, 1991, a Bell 412SP [collided in mid-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Merion_mid-air_collision) with a [Piper Aerostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_Aerostar) over [Lower Merion Township, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Merion_Township,_Pennsylvania), during an improvised attempt to inspect the Aerostar's landing gear. The accident killed all five people on both aircraft, including Aerostar passenger and United States senator [John Heinz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Heinz), along with two children on the ground. The collision was attributed to poor judgment by the pilots of both aircraft.

  [^cite_note-74]

  [^cite_note-75]

- On December 10, 2006, Bell 412SP medical helicopter [Mercy Air 2 crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Mercy_Air_Bell_412_crash) in mountainous terrain near [Hesperia, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesperia,_California). All three crew members on board died. The accident was attributed to [controlled flight into terrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_flight_into_terrain) in low visibility.

  [^cite_note-76]

  [^cite_note-77]

- On December 7, 2023, a Bell 412EP operated by the Guyanese military [went down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Guyana_Defence_Force_helicopter_crash) carrying 2 crew and 5 senior officers at the border between Venezuela and Guyana during bad weather.

  [^cite_note-78]

  [^cite_note-79]

- On July 29th 2026, A Bell 412 helicopter Carrying 7 people on board operating on a firefighting flight crashed into a hillside in western Brazil. All on board were killed. The aircraft was destroyed by fire.
