**Deir ez-Zor Governorate** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مُحافظة دير الزور, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Muḥāfaẓat Dayr az-Zawr*) is one of the fourteen [governorates](690220.md) of [Syria](7515849.md). It is situated in eastern Syria, bordering [Iraq](7515928.md). It has an area of 33,060 km<sup>2</sup> (12,760 sq mi) and a population of 1,239,000 (2011 estimate).[^cite_note-CityPop-1] The capital is [Deir ez-Zor](1429146.md). It is divided roughly equally from northwest to southeast by the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates).

## History

&#x20;On 6 September 2007, Israel attacked and destroyed a facility in the governorate that Israel claimed was a nuclear site[^cite_note-ynetnews.com-2][^cite_note-Observer-3][^cite_note-4] in [Operation Outside the Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Outside_the_Box). The complex was suspected of holding nuclear materials from [North Korea](21255.md).[^cite_note-5] In 2011, the IAEA confirmed it had been a nuclear weapons site.[^cite_note-6]

### Syrian civil war

In the course of [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), as the [Syrian Arab Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Arab_Armed_Forces) concentrated its forces on wresting back control of Aleppo, rebels slowly gained ground in the eastern tribal heartland, aiming to control the country's 200,000 barrel-a-day oil output.[^cite_note-reudeir-7] In August 2012, units of the [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) (FSA) targeted the remaining isolated outposts of the Syrian Army forces in north-east Syria, where the FSA controls all the main roads. There were said to be only three Army outposts left in Deir ez-Zor province countryside and they were under attack. On 30 November 2012, Syrian troops withdrew from [Omar oil field](75129437.md), one of the last regime positions east of Deir ez-Zor city near the Iraqi border. This meant that the rebels controlled the country's major fields. This happened after Syrian troops lost the [Conoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conoco) gas reserve on 27 November. The insurgents took control of an oil field for the first time on 4 November when they overran Al-Ward, the most important in the province. After also losing control of Al-Jofra field also in November, the army controlled not more than five fields, all located to the west of Deir ez-Zor city.[^cite_note-8] Residents in Deir ez-Zor used crude oil for heating and agriculture for lack of fuel.[^cite_note-9] On 1 January 2013, it was reported that two thirds of Deir ez-Zor Governorate was under rebel control.[^cite_note-jazjanfirst-10]

#### Siege of Deir ez-Zor

See also: [Siege of Deir ez-Zor (2014–2017)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Deir_ez-Zor_\(2014%E2%80%932017\))

On 11 April 2014, [Islamic State](9087364.md) (ISIS) withdrew from Abu Kamal to the T2 oil site, where a Syria-Iraq pipeline runs.[^cite_note-lepoint.fr-11] On 3 July, the Syrian Organisation for Human Rights (SOHR) said that all towns and villages on the route from Abu Kamal to Al-Bab, passing through [Raqqa governorate](5317143.md), were now under ISIL control. Only the provincial capital Deir ez-Zor and the military airport were outside of it. The city of Deir ez-Zor was split between [al-Assad](364813.md)'s forces and an amalgam of rebel groups.[^cite_note-ndtv.com-12] In 2014 ISIL militants massacred an estimated 900 members of the [Al-Shaitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shaitat) tribe in the governorate, following resistance to the group's control of the area.[^cite_note-13] In early 2016, fighters from the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) entered the governorate following the [Al-Shaddadi offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shaddadi_offensive_\(2016\)). Until October 2017, ISIL controlled all of the countryside, while Syrian Government forces held out in the capital. On 14 October 2017, Russia confirmed that Al-Mayadeen was [recaptured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Mayadin_offensive) by the Syrian army amid a major offensive. The military fully secured Deir ez-Zor city on 3 November [^cite_note-14] and on 17 November 2017, ISIL surrendered the island of Hawijat Kati, bringing all areas around the city under Army control following the [two-month offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_offensive_\(September%E2%80%93November_2017\)).[^cite_note-15] On 23 October 2017, the Syrian troops began an [offensive towards Abu Kamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Abu_Kamal_offensive), being ISIL's last stronghold in the governorate. The city was captured on 19 November 2017 after changing hands three times.[^cite_note-16]

#### ISIL defeat and insurgency in Deir ez-Zor

Main article: [Deir ez-Zor campaign (2017–2019)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_campaign_\(2017%E2%80%932019\))

During the Deir ez-Zor campaign ISIL was militarily and territorially defeated by the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) (SDF).

Main article: [Eastern Syria insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Syria_insurgency)

In 2018, ISIL started to conduct insurgent attacks against SDF forces in Deir ez-Zor.

#### Post-Assad era

On 26 February 2025, four SDF fighters and eight civilians were killed in a Turkish airstrike on military positions of SDF.[^cite_note-17]

The [Syrian transitional government](79584988.md) engaged in various armed altercations with the SDF in the months after the [March 10 agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_peace_process#March_10_agreement), predominantly in Deir ez-Zor Governorate.[^cite_note-18] On 18 January 2026, the [Syrian armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_armed_forces) captured the entire governorate from the SDF during the [2026 northeastern Syria offensive](82106178.md).

## Demographics

[Arabs](2185.md) constitute about 90% of the population of the governorate, a vast majority being [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam). About a thousand [Christians](18337522.md) live in the governorate as well.[^cite_note-19]

The most significant Arab tribes are the Bu Kamal, the Shuwayt, [Al-Shaitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shaitat), Bakir, Bu Kamil, Mashahda, Bu Khabour, Qaraan and Bu Hassan who all are part of the 'Egaidat confederation. Beside the 'Egaidat confederation, other tribes include the [Al-Baggara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Baggara), Abeed, Kul’ayeen, and Albu Saraya.[^cite_note-20]
