**Puerto Princesa**,[^cite_note-8] officially the **City of Puerto Princesa** ([Cuyonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyonon_language): *Siyudad i'ang Puerto Princesa*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Lungsod ng Puerto Princesa*), is a [highly urbanized city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Legal_classification) in the [Mimaropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimaropa) region of the [Philippines](23440.md). According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 316,384 people.[^cite_note-9]

It is a city located in the western [Philippine province](165100.md) of [Palawan](240395.md) and is the [westernmost city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines) in the [Philippines](23440.md). Though the seat of government and capital of the province, the city itself is one of 38 [independent cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Independent_cities) within the Philippines not controlled by the province in which it is geographically located and is therefore an independent area located within Palawan for its geographical and statistical purposes by the [Philippine Statistics Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority). It is the largest city in the province of Palawan and the [Mimaropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimaropa) region.

It is the least densely populated city in the Philippines with 110 inhabitants per square kilometer (280 inhabitants/mi<sup>2</sup>). In terms of land area, the city is the second largest geographically after [Davao City](489401.md) with an area of 2,381.02 square kilometers (919.32 sq mi).[^cite_note-PSA-NSCB-ListofCities-10] Puerto Princesa is the location of the Philippines' [Western Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFP_Western_Command) headquarters.[^cite_note-11]

Today, Puerto Princesa is a [tourist city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_city) with beach resorts and seafood restaurants.[^cite_note-WildAsiaOrg-12]

## Etymology

The name *Puerto Princesa* has several possible origins. It is said to have been attributed by locals to a princess-like maiden who roams the place on certain nights of the year, while other accounts attribute its geographical advantage as a seaport which is a naturally protected area due to its surrounding mountains, and is characterized by a depth able to accommodate any size of shipping vessel.[^cite_note-13]

There are two official versions about the actual origin of the name. The first is that place was originally named Port of the Princess ([Spanish](26825.md): *Puerto de la Princesa*) after [Infanta Eulalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infanta_Eulalia_of_Spain), one of the princesses born to [Isabella II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_II_of_Spain) and her consort, [Francis, Duke of Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_As%C3%ADs,_Duke_of_C%C3%A1diz).[^cite_note-eulalia-3][^cite_note-galliera-15] Another version, however, states that the place was originally named Port Asuncion ([Spanish](26825.md): *Puerto de Asunción*), allegedly named after another daughter of Isabella II and the Duke of Cadiz. In this version, the princess suffered an untimely death, which prompted the Queen to change the name to *Puerto de la Princesa*, and was eventually shortened to Puerto Princesa. However, this second version is likely to be incorrect, as Isabella and Francis did not have a daughter named Asunción.[^cite_note-mb-16]

## History

### Spanish era



A 1904 map of Puerto Princesa

Spanish colonists founded the settlement on 4 March 1872 as Puerto de la Princesa by Ezequiel Moreno, in the course of their exploration of the province. As they scanned the Palawan shoreline for a capital site, they came upon a hill with steep declivity. Rowing to shore, they surveyed the hill and discovered an extensive plateau which they decided as ideal for settlement. In the same year, the community of Renampacan (now Inagawan) was founded.[^cite_note-\:0-17]

Soon after, Fr. Antonio Muro levelled a portion of the hill to make way for a chapel (that section is now occupied by the [Roman Catholic Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Princesa_Cathedral), the P.C. Barracks and the Rizal Park, the Old Municipal Building used to be there, as well as an Elementary School). The first mass celebrated in Puerto Princesa took place at a site where a marker now stands.

In May 1872, the [Port of Puerto Princesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Puerto_Princesa) became the center of Spanish naval operations in the area because the Bay met all the navy's requirements. Royal decrees later provided incentives to settlers, and by 1883 the settlement had flourished into a town of twelve roads, a hospital and well-built port.

In 1894, Puerto Princesa was recognized by government authorities as one of the most beautiful towns in the country by virtue of the orderly distribution of streets, buildings and houses as well as the cleanliness of the community.[^cite_note-18]

### American era

Main articles: [Invasion of Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Palawan) and [Palawan Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan_Massacre)

In 1911, the New American Administration made Puerto Princesa the seat of the Palawan Provincial Government with Major John Brown as Lieutenant Governor.

In the year 1936, Governor Heginio Mendoza made a directive on the transfer of the Palawan High School (currently Palawan National School) from the island municipality of Cuyo to the central place of the province, which was the Municipality of Puerto Princesa.[^cite_note-19]

### World War II



Plaza Cuartel, the site of the infamous [Palawan Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan_Massacre) committed by the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army)

During the [Pacific campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) of [World War II](32927.md) and the [Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Philippines), the village was largely abandoned. On 1 January 1942, Japanese planes began bombing Puerto Princesa and shot several bullets. On 18 May of the same year, Japanese troops landed and occupied Puerto Princesa City.[^cite_note-\:0-17][^cite_note-20] After the establishment of the Second Philippine Republic on 14 October 1943, President [Jose P. Laurel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_P._Laurel) appointed Major Patricio Fernandez as the Provincial Governor of Palawan, and Severino Vicente as the mayor of Puerto Princesa. For the most part, it was mostly Puerto Princesa and Aborlan that were within the Japanese-occupied zone, while the rest were under heavy guerrilla influence or full guerrilla control. On 7 January 1944, Ex-Governor and guerrilla commander Dr. Higinio Mendoza as well as 15 other patriots were brutally massacred by Japanese forces. In the same year on 20 October, American planes bombed the town, causing widespread destruction. The [Filipino Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) barracks was the scene of the [Palawan Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan_Massacre) on 14 December, just before liberation with the allied [Invasion of Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Palawan).[^cite_note-\:0-17][^cite_note-Bob-21]

On 28 February 1945, US forces landed in Puerto Princesa and on 4 March, the [Commonwealth Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) was restored in Palawan.[^cite_note-\:0-17]

### Post-World War II

In 1951, the barrios of Tinitian, Caramay, Rizal, Del Pilar, Malcampo, Tumarbong, Taradungan, Ilian, and Capayas were separated to form the town of [Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxas,_Palawan).[^cite_note-PhilippineLawInfo-RA615-22]

In 1955, the [sitios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitio) of Materingen, Tandayag, Nasedoc, and Panlawagan were separated from the barrio of Maroyogon and elevated into a barrio.[^cite_note-PhilippineLawInfo-RA1234-23]

In 1956, the sitios of Calagbenguen, Tarabanan, Bendoyan, Talabigan, Tagbuan, and Langogan were constituted into the barrio of Concepcion.[^cite_note-PhilippineLawInfo-RA1527-24]

In 1957, the barrio of *Tapul* was renamed to *Salvacion*.[^cite_note-PhilippineLawInfo-RA2024-25]

On 21 June 1969, San Vicente was created from Puerto Princesa and [Taytay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taytay,_Palawan), with the barrios of Caruray and the current poblacion of the town being separated from Puerto Princesa while the barrios of New Agutaya and Binga were separated from Taytay.

### Cityhood

Main article: [Cities of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines)

The town was converted into a city on 1 January 1970, under *Republic Act 5906* as amended by *P.D. 437*,[^cite_note-TheCorpusJuris-RA5906-26][^cite_note-GovPH-PD437-27] through the effort of then Congressman [Ramon Mitra, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Mitra,_Jr.) Feliberto R. Oliveros, Jr., who then became the first City Mayor. In 1987, the port of Puerto Princesa was put under the administration of the Philippine Ports Authority, expanding the city's importance nationally and advancing its infrastructure.[^cite_note-28]

#### Highly urbanized city

On March 26, 2007, through Proclamation No. 1264, the city of Puerto Princesa was converted into a highly urbanized city. A plebiscite was held on 21 July 2007, where majority of residents voted in its favor. In 2011, the President launched a nationwide campaign for the inclusion of Puerto Princesa's underground river into the New Seven Wonders of Nature.[^cite_note-29] This campaign came into fruition when the [Puerto Princesa Subterranean River National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Princesa_Subterranean_River_National_Park), the city's top heritage site, was recognized internationally as one of the New Seven Wonders of Nature in 2012.[^cite_note-30]

### Contemporary

In May 2001, [Abu Sayyaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sayyaf) gunmen entered the luxury Dos Palmas Resort in [Honda Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Bay) just off the coast of Puerto Princesa and [kidnapped 20 people from the resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dos_Palmas_kidnappings), including four resort staff and three Americans.[^cite_note-31]

On the morning of 24 January 2011, broadcast journalist and former politician [Gerry Ortega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Ortega) was at an "ukay-ukay" (used clothing) thrift store in Puerto Princesa when he was murdered by a gunman.[^cite_note-affidavit-32][^cite_note-kapihan1-33][^cite_note-cpj-34][^cite_note-cohen-35] After a brief chase, police apprehended the alleged shooter, Marlon Recamata,[^cite_note-affidavit-32] who confessed to the crime at the Puerto Princesa Police Office and implicated three other persons.[^cite_note-kapihan1-33] In 2015, former Palawan governor [Mario Joel Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Joel_Reyes) was arrested in [Thailand](30128.md) after being charged by the city court in connection with the crime.[^cite_note-RapplerBolledo-36] Various courts went back and forth in ordering the arrest or non-arrest of Reyes, with him being released from jail in 2018 upon orders of the [Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeals_of_the_Philippines) but ultimately ordered rearrested in 2023, with him surrendering to the [National Bureau of Investigation](2489044.md) in [Metro Manila](72340.md) in September 2024.[^cite_note-RapplerBolledo-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

## Geography



Sabang Beach

Puerto Princesa is located in the mid-section of [Palawan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan_\(island\)). It is bound to the east by the [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea), to the west by the [South China Sea](74209.md), to the north by the municipalities of [San Vicente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vicente,_Palawan) and [Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxas,_Palawan), and to the south by the municipality of [Aborlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aborlan). It is approximately 306 nautical miles (567 km) from the Philippine capital of [Manila](184334.md), 205 nautical miles (380 km) from [Panay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panay) and 250 nautical miles (460 km) from [Zamboanga City](25167165.md) on the southern Philippine island of [Mindanao](20546.md).

### Climate

Puerto Princesa features a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) Aw). It is usually wet from May to December, with very little rain from January to April. Average temperature is 27.43 °C (81.37 °F) while the annual average rainfall is 1,563.8 millimetres (61.57 in) per year. It is warm and humid all year round. On 23 April 2025 the town reached 37.0 °C (98.6 °F) for the first time in history.&#x20;

[Immaculate Conception Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Princesa_Cathedral) and Rizal Park

In the 2024 census, the population of Puerto Princesa was 316,384 people,[^cite_note-47] with a density of 130 inhabitants per square kilometer or 340 inhabitants per square mile.

Waves of migrants from other Philippine provinces, and even other countries, have turned Puerto Princesa into a melting pot of various cultures. Among the original inhabitants are the Cuyonons who have a rich legacy of folklore and traditions. Indigenous groups include the [Tagbanwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagbanwa_people), Palawanos, Molbogs and [Bataks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_people_\(Philippines\)), each group with its distinct culture and system of beliefs.

Total inhabitants number 307,079 (as of 2020), of which three-quarter of the population resides in the city proper, an urban settlement on the shores of Puerto Princesa Bay. Although the predominant language is [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), [Cuyonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyonon_language) is widely spoken and used throughout the whole city, as well as [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language), other [Visayan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayan_languages), and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English).

Puerto Princesa is known as the "Eco-Tourism Center of the Philippines".[^cite_note-DSWDGovPH-Palawan-1] In recent years, the city has seen an increase in the number of tourists bringing with them trade and businesses for the city. Many hotels ranging from basic to five-star luxury accommodations have been developed since the 1990s to cater to a growing number of foreign and local tourists in the city.

There are also a number of restaurants, bars and shopping malls, including the [Robinsons Place Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinsons_Place_Palawan), NCCC Mall Palawan, Unitop Mall Puerto Princesa, as well as the recently opened [SM City Puerto Princesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_City_Puerto_Princesa).

## Transportation



[Puerto Princesa International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Princesa_International_Airport)

### Air

The [Puerto Princesa International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Princesa_International_Airport) is within the city proper. Puerto Princesa is accessible by direct flights to and from the major cities of the Philippines, such as [Manila](184334.md), [Cebu](285868.md), [Davao](489401.md), [Iloilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iloilo_City), and [Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_International_Airport), as well as other parts of Palawan, such as [Cuyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyo,_Palawan), [Busuanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busuanga,_Palawan), [San Vicente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vicente,_Palawan), and [El Nido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Nido,_Palawan).

### Sea

The city is served by domestic passenger ferries to Cuyo, Manila, [Coron](498917.md) and Iloilo at the [Port of Puerto Princesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Puerto_Princesa).

### Land



[Tricycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricycle) within the city

The main modes of transport are via [tricycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricycles), [jeepneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeepneys) and vans-for-hire (or PUVs/public utility vehicles). [Taxis](22026933.md) started operating since April 2015, plying through the city center and nearby tourist destinations. Provincial buses and jeepneys operate from the San Jose terminal located 7 kilometers (4.3 mi) north of the city center off the National Highway.

#### E-tricycle

Then-Puerto Princesa Mayor [Edward S. Hagedorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_S._Hagedorn) unveiled the environment friendly and economical [electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric)-powered "Trikebayan" (which does not emit any noise or [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide)) at the Kapihan sa Sulo forum, Sulo Hotel, [Quezon City](284223.md). The Trikebayan costs only [₱](227672.md)48 or \$1.20 per day to operate, while a gasoline-powered [tricycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricycle) operation would cost ₱200.[^cite_note-Philstar-Tricycles-58] Rolly Concepcion, who conceptualized the Trikebayan, said that converting a tricycle engine to electric costs ₱68,000. The rechargeable battery under the passenger seat can run for 12 hours.[^cite_note-Inquirer-Tricycle-59] There was a dealership for these trikes on the north highway but it closed down in 2011.

Although Puerto Princesa has this bold plan for electric vehicles, the municipal government and tourist office has stated (when asked by a tourist in August 2011), that it has no published or announced plan for providing for the current and future needs and safety of pedestrians or bicycle riders. Spaces for walking and bicycling from one place to another are not being considered.

## Government



Old flag of Puerto Princesa

Elected and appointed public officials have governed Puerto Princesa, with a strong mayor-council government. The city political government is composed of the mayor, vice mayor, ten councilors, one [Sangguniang Kabataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Kabataan) (SK) Federation representative, an Association of Barangay Captains (ABC) representative and Indigenous People's Mandatory Representative (IPMR).
