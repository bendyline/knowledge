# Two-hour marathon







**From left to right:**&#x20;

- [Sabastian Sawe](73093597.md), the first athlete to break the two-hour marathon barrier with record-eligible conditions (2026 [London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Marathon), 1\:59\:30)

- [Yomif Kejelcha](46932262.md) also ran the 2026 London Marathon in under two hours, coming in second at 1\:59\:41

- [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge) ran the [Ineos 1\:59 Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ineos_1\:59_Challenge), a 2019 exhibition race, in 1\:59\:40

The **two-hour marathon**, also referred to as the **sub-two-hour marathon**, is the physical and psychological barrier of completing the [marathon](49132.md), a distance of 42.195 kilometres (26.219 mi), in under two hours.

Originally thought to be impossible,[^cite_note-1] various attempts were made to break the mark after the marathon distance was defined in the early 20th century. Counting all conditions, the barrier was first broken by [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge) in 2019 during the [Ineos 1\:59 Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ineos_1\:59_Challenge), an exhibition race where Kipchoge used pacing and fueling techniques that made the attempt ineligible for ratification by [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics). It was first broken in record-eligible conditions by both [Sabastian Sawe](73093597.md) and [Yomif Kejelcha](46932262.md) at the [2026 London Marathon](83030339.md), who ran times of 1\:59\:30 and 1\:59\:41 respectively.

## History

The [marathon](49132.md) distance, as then understood to be a race of about 25 miles (40 km), was first run at [the 1896 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1896_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_marathon) as an ode to [Pheidippides' run at the Battle of Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marathon#Marathon_run) in 490 BC.[^cite_note-2] [Spyridon Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spyridon_Louis) won the race in 2 hours, 58 minutes, and 50 seconds, becoming the first to break the 3-hour barrier before the modern marathon race distance was set at 42.195 kilometres (26.219 mi) in 1908 and standardized in 1921.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

In May 1960, US marathon champion [Buddy Edelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Edelen) told the Metropolitan Track Writers' Association that it "won't be long" until the marathon is run under two hours. He said it would most likely be done by a [sub-four-minute miler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_minute_mile) who moved to the roads after losing their sharpness on the track, and that it would require a fast opening mile and strength to continue with that pace.[^cite_note-5] (Though the first sub-two marathon runner [Sabastian Sawe](73093597.md) had not run under four minutes in the mile, the second runner [Yomif Kejelcha](46932262.md) had previously held the [indoor mile world record](854191.md#Men_Indoor_IAAF_era) time of 3\:47.01 before moving to the roads.[^cite_note-6])

Following Japanese runner [Morio Shigematsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morio_Shigematsu)'s 2\:12\:00 marathon world record in 1965, [Sydney Wooderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Wooderson) stated in the *[Liverpool Echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Echo)*, "Obviously it is now going to be possible for a marathon to be run inside two hours – and bang will go yet another barrier which would have brought only derision if any of us had made such a prediction 25 years ago". The mark was predicted to have been possible in a race between Shigematsu and then-Olympic champion [Abebe Bikila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abebe_Bikila) under ideal conditions.[^cite_note-7]

Several predictions have been made about when the first marathon under two hours would be achieved. In 2011, [University of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montreal) professor Francois Peronnet projected that the first sub-two-hour marathon would be run in the year 2028.[^cite_note-8] In 2019, American exercise physiologist [Michael Joyner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Joyner) predicted that the two-hour marathon would most likely be broken in May 2032.[^cite_note-9] Others doubted it could be done; in 2013, [LetsRun.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LetsRun.com)'s Robert Johnson stated that "humans aren't close to doing it" and predicted that the two-hour marathon would not be broken during his lifetime.[^cite_note-10]

The first high-profile time-trial attempt at the two-hour barrier was made by [Nike, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) at the [Breaking2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking2) event in May 2017. Held on [Formula One](10854.md)'s [Monza Circuit](952083.md), the event featured three runners in [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge), [Zersenay Tadese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zersenay_Tadese), and [Lelisa Desisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelisa_Desisa). Kipchoge faded to 2\:00\:25 after remaining on-pace through 30 kilometres (19 mi), though the mark was not record-eligible as his human [pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacer_\(running\)) were allowed to enter and exit the race at will (in contravention of [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics) rules that pacers can only block wind for as long as they can run from the beginning of the race).[^cite_note-11]

Two years later, a similar time-trial event was staged in [Vienna](55866.md) at the [Ineos 1\:59 Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ineos_1\:59_Challenge), this time only featuring Kipchoge with a team of 41 pacemakers. The two-hour barrier was broken with a 1\:59\:40 finishing time, ending with Kipchoge performing a [negative split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_split) after a 59\:59 first half. Like the Breaking2 event, the race was not sanctioned by [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics) and did not count as a [marathon world record](310762.md).[^cite_note-ineos-12]

In April 2026, [Sabastian Sawe](73093597.md) and [Yomif Kejelcha](46932262.md) became the first runners to break the two-hour barrier with record-eligible conditions at the [2026 London Marathon](83030339.md). Sawe, the defending champion, ran 1\:59\:30 to set a three-minute [personal record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_record), while Kejelcha, an accomplished track runner, ran 1\:59\:41 for 2nd in his marathon debut.[^cite_note-13]

## Wheelchair performances

In 1980, [Curt Brinkman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curt_Brinkman) became the first [wheelchair racer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelchair_racer) to complete the marathon distance in under two hours with a 1\:55\:00 time. He performed the feat at the 1980 [Boston Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Marathon), beating the previous wheelchair world record of 2 hours and 34 minutes and also becoming the first wheelchair athlete to beat the able-bodied runners at the Boston Marathon.[^cite_note-14] Danish wheelchair racer [Connie Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connie_Hansen) became the first woman to complete the marathon in under two hours at the [1989 Boston Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Boston_Marathon), beating [Candace Cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candace_Cable)'s 2\:02\:15 mark set the previous year.[^cite_note-15] As of 2026, the fastest men's and women's wheelchair marathons are 1\:17\:47 by [Marcel Hug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Hug) of Switzerland and 1\:34\:16 by [Catherine Debrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Debrunner) of Switzerland respectively.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Factors

Various factors have been proposed as necessary to run a sub-two-hour marathon. A team led by Andrew Jones of the [University of Exeter](33719893.md) suggested three primary physiological factors in 2020: a high [VO<sub>2</sub> max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VO2_max), excellent [running economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_economy), and a high [lactate turn point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactic_acid).[^cite_note-18]

### Footwear

The first [carbon fiber-plated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_fibers) running shoes were developed by American footwear company [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) and debuted as a prototype at the [2016 United States Olympic trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_Olympic_trials_\(marathon\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). Over the following years, the technology was eventually adapted by other brands, creating a class of footwear dubbed [super shoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_shoes) that led to improvements in most [road running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_running) world records.[^cite_note-19] The shoes have been compared to [full-body suits in competitive swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competitive_swimwear#Bodyskin), which were banned by [World Aquatics](317029.md) in 2010 after their performance impact was demonstrated.[^cite_note-20]

The shoes served to increase the amount of bounce and energy recovery received by athletes. Sawe's record-breaking run used a newly-released shoe from [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) – the Adios Pro Evo 3 – which weighed 97 grams (3.4 oz) for a men's shoe of US [size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoe_size) 9.5, less than leading super shoes from Nike, while still maintaining a large 39-millimetre stack height. Other athletes who wore the shoe at the [2026 London Marathon](83030339.md) also broke records, including runner-up [Yomif Kejelcha](46932262.md) who also ran under two hours.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]
