**Zagreb** ([/ˈzɑːɡrɛb/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*ZAH-greb*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-7] Croatian: [\[zǎːɡreb\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Serbo-Croatian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/42/Hr-Zagreb.ogg/Hr-Zagreb.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/42/Hr-Zagreb.ogg/Hr-Zagreb.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>[^cite_note-9])[^cite_note-10] is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Croatia#List_of_cities_and_towns) of [Croatia](5573.md).[^cite_note-11] It is in the [north of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Croatia), along the [Sava](587845.md) River, at the southern slopes of the [Medvednica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvednica) mountain. Zagreb stands near the international border between Croatia and [Slovenia](27338.md) at an elevation of approximately 158 m (518 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level).[^cite_note-12] At the 2021 census, the city itself had a population of 767,131,[^cite_note-Census_2021-3] while the population of the [Zagreb metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_metropolitan_area) is 1,086,528.

The oldest settlement in the vicinity of the city was the Roman [Andautonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andautonia), in today's [Šćitarjevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0%C4%87itarjevo).[^cite_note-13] The historical record of the name "Zagreb" dates from 1134, in reference to the foundation of the settlement at [Kaptol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaptol,_Zagreb) in 1094. Zagreb became a [free royal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_royal_city) in 1242.[^cite_note-14] In 1851, [Janko Kamauf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janko_Kamauf) became Zagreb's [first mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Zagreb).[^cite_note-15] Zagreb has special status as a [Croatian administrative division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Croatia)—it comprises a consolidated city-county (but separate from [Zagreb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_County)),[^cite_note-16] and is administratively subdivided into [17 city districts](#City_districts).[^cite_note-17] Most of the city districts lie at a low elevation along the [valley of the river Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posavina), whereas northern and northeastern city districts, such as [Podsljeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podsljeme)[^cite_note-18] and [Sesvete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesvete)[^cite_note-19] districts are spread out across the foothills of the [Medvednica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvednica) mountain,[^cite_note-20] making the city's geography rather diverse. The city spans about 30 km from east to west, while stretching about 20 km from north to south.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Zagreb ranks as a [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city), with a 'Beta-' rating from the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network).[^cite_note-23]

The transport connections, concentration of industry, and scientific and research institutions and industrial tradition underlie Zagreb's leading economic position in Croatia.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Zagreb is the seat of the [central government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_government), [administrative bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_administration), and almost all [government ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Croatia).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Almost all of the [largest Croatian companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Croatian_companies), [media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media), and scientific institutions have their headquarters in the city. Zagreb is the most important transport hub in Croatia: here [Central Europe](5188.md), the Mediterranean and [Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe) meet, making the Zagreb area the centre of the road, rail and air networks of [Croatia](5573.md). It is a city known for its diverse economy, high [quality of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_living), [museums](#Museums), sporting, and entertainment events. Major branches of Zagreb's economy include [high-tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-tech) industries and the [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector).

## Name

The etymology of the name *Zagreb* is unclear. It was used for the united city only from 1852, but it had been in use as the name of the [Zagreb Diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_bishopric) since the 12th century and was increasingly used for the city in the 17th century.[^cite_note-30] The name is first recorded in a charter by [Felician, Archbishop of Esztergom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felician_\(archbishop_of_Esztergom\)), dated 1134, mentioned as *Zagrabiensem episcopatum*.[^cite_note-31]

The name is probably derived from Proto-Slavic word [\*grębъ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction\:Proto-Slavic/gr%C4%99b%D1%8A#Proto-Slavic) which means "hill" or "uplift". An Old Croatian reconstructed name [Zagrębъ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction\:Proto-Slavic/Zagr%C4%99b%D1%8A#Proto-Slavic) is manifested through the city's former German name, **Agram**.[^cite_note-Gluhak_1999-32] Some linguists (*e.g.* [Nada Klaić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nada_Klai%C4%87), Miroslav Kravar) propose a [metathesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metathesis_\(linguistics\)) of \**Zabreg*, which would originate from Old Slavic *breg* (see Proto-Slavic [\*bergъ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction\:Proto-Slavic/berg%D1%8A#Proto-Slavic)) in the sense of "riverbank", referring to River Sava. This metathesis has been attested in [Kajkavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajkavian),[^cite_note-Gračanin_2012-33] but the meaning of "riverbank" is lost in modern Croatian and [folk etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology) associates it instead with *breg* "hill", ostensibly referring to Medvednica. Hungarian linguist Gyula Décsy similarly uses metathesis to construct \**Chaprakov(o)*, a putative Slavicisation of a Hungarian [hypocorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypocorism) for "[Cyprian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprian)", similar to the etymology of [Csepreg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csepreg), Hungary.[^cite_note-Desy1990-34] The most likely derivation is \**Zagrębъ* in the sense of "embankment" or "rampart", *i.e.* remains of the 1st millennium fortifications on [Grič](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradec,_Zagreb).[^cite_note-Gračanin_2012-33][^cite_note-Gluhak_1999-32]

In [Middle Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Latin) and [Modern Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Latin), Zagreb is known as *Agranum* (the name of an unrelated Arabian city in [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo)),[^cite_note-35] *Zagrabia*[^cite_note-36] or *Mons Graecensis* (also *Mons Crecensis*, in reference to [Grič (Gradec)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradec,_Zagreb)).

The most common folk etymology of the name of the city has been from the verb stem *[\*za-](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction\:Proto-Slavic/za-#Proto-Slavic)*_[grab-](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction\:Proto-Slavic/grob%D1%8A#Proto-Slavic)_, meaning "to scoop" or "to dig". A folk legend illustrating this derivation, attested but discarded as a serious etymology by [Ivan Tkalčić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Tkal%C4%8Di%C4%87), ties the name to a drought of the early 14th century, during which [Augustin Kažotić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Ka%C5%BEoti%C4%87) (c. 1260–1323) is said to have dug a well which miraculously produced water.[^cite_note-37] In another legend,[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] a city governor is thirsty and orders a girl named Manda to "scoop" water from the [Manduševac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Jela%C4%8Di%C4%87_Square#History) well (nowadays a fountain in Ban Jelačić Square), using the imperative: *Zagrabi, Mando!* ("Scoop, Manda!").[^cite_note-43]

## History

Main article: [History of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Zagreb)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Zagreb).



Ruins of the ancient Roman town [Andautonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andautonia) near Zagreb

The oldest known settlement located near present-day Zagreb, the Roman town of [Andautonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andautonia), now [Ščitarjevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0%C4%8Ditarjevo), existed between the 1st and the 5th centuries AD.[^cite_note-andautonia-44]

The first recorded appearance of the name "Zagreb" dates from 1094, at which time the city existed as two different city centres: the smaller, eastern [Kaptol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaptol,_Zagreb), inhabited mainly by [clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy) and housing [Zagreb Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Cathedral), and the larger, western [Gradec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradec,_Zagreb), inhabited mainly by craftsmen and merchants. In 1851 the [Ban of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_of_Croatia), [Josip Jelačić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Jela%C4%8Di%C4%87), united Gradec and Kaptol; the name of the main city square, [Ban Jelačić Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Jela%C4%8Di%C4%87_Square) honors him.[^cite_note-Ban_Josip_Jelačić-45]

While Croatia formed part of [Yugoslavia](34244.md) (1918 to 1991), Zagreb remained an important economic centre of that country, and was the second largest city. After [Croatia declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_independence_referendum,_1991) from Yugoslavia in 1991, the [Parliament of the Republic of Croatia](398696.md) proclaimed Zagreb as the capital of the Republic of Croatia.[^cite_note-Sluzbeni_List-46]

### Early Zagreb

The history of Zagreb dates as far back as 1094 when the [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) [King Ladislaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladislaus_I_of_Hungary), returning from his campaign against the [Kingdom of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Croatia_\(medieval\)), founded a diocese. Alongside the bishop's [see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_see), the canonical settlement [Kaptol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaptol,_Zagreb) developed north of [Zagreb Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Cathedral), as did the fortified settlement [Gradec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradec,_Zagreb) on the neighbouring hill, with the border between the two formed by the Medveščak stream.[^cite_note-47] Today the latter is Zagreb's [Upper Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gornji_grad,_Zagreb) (*Gornji grad*) and is one of the best-preserved urban nuclei in Croatia. Both settlements came under [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe) attack in 1242.[^cite_note-48] As a sign of gratitude for offering him a safe haven from the Mongols, the Croatian and Hungarian [King Béla IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9la_IV_of_Hungary) granted Gradec the [Golden Bull of 1242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Bull_of_1242), which gave its citizens exemption from county rule and autonomy, as well as their own [judicial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

### Relationship between Kaptol and Gradec throughout history

See also: [Kaptol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaptol_\(Zagreb\)) and [Gradec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradec,_Zagreb)

The development of Kaptol began in 1094 after the foundation of the [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese), while the growth of Gradec began after the [Golden Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Bull_of_1242) was issued in 1242. In the history of the city of Zagreb, there have been numerous conflicts between Gradec and Kaptol, mainly due to disputed issues of rent collection and due to disputed properties.

The first known conflicts took place in the middle of the 13th century and continued with interruptions until 1667. Because of the conflict, it was recorded that the Bishop of Kaptol excommunicated the residents of Gradec twice.

In the conflicts between Gradec and Kaptol, there were several massacres of the citizens, destruction of houses and looting of citizens. In 1850, Gradec and Kaptol, with surrounding settlements, were united into a single settlement, today's city of Zagreb.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

### 16th to 18th centuries

See also: [Hundred Years' Croatian–Ottoman War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Years'_Croatian%E2%80%93Ottoman_War)



Modern Zagreb's town core emerged from the Upper Town [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) settlements of [Gradec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradec,_Zagreb) and [Kaptol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaptol,_Zagreb). Picture from 1689

There were numerous connections between the Kaptol diocese and the free sovereign town of Gradec for both economic and political reasons, but they were not known as an integrated city, even as Zagreb became the political centre, and the regional Sabor ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Congregatio Regnorum Croatiae, Dalmatiae et Slavoniae*) representing Croatia, [Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonia) and [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia), first convened at Gradec. Zagreb became the Croatian capital in 1557, with city also being chosen as the seat of the [Ban of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_of_Croatia) in 1621 under ban [Nikola IX Frankopan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_IX_Frankopan).[^cite_note-56]

At the invitation of the [Croatian Parliament](398696.md), the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) came to Zagreb and built the first [grammar school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar_school),[^cite_note-57] the [St. Catherine's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Alexandria) Church (built 1620-1632[^cite_note-58]) and monastery. In 1669, they founded an academy where philosophy, theology, and law were taught, the forerunner of today's [University of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zagreb).

During the 17th and 18th centuries, Zagreb was badly devastated by fire and by [the plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague). In 1776, the royal council (government) moved from [Varaždin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vara%C5%BEdin) to Zagreb and during the reign of the Emperor [Joseph II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) Zagreb became the headquarters of the Varaždin and [Karlovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovac) general command.[^cite_note-HRT-59]

### 19th to mid-20th century

See also: [Zagreb in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_in_World_War_II)



Mass protests in Zagreb against the unification of the [State of Slovenes, Croats and Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Slovenes,_Croats_and_Serbs) with the Kingdom of Serbia in 1918



[Croatian Partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Partisans) enter [Ban Jelačić Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Jela%C4%8Di%C4%87_Square) on May 9, 1945, national celebration on the square

In the 19th century, Zagreb was the centre of the [Croatian National Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrian_movement) and saw the foundation of important cultural and historic institutions. In 1850, the town was united under its first [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Zagreb) – [Janko Kamauf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janko_Kamauf).[^cite_note-HRT-59]

The first railway line to connect Zagreb with [Zidani Most](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zidani_Most) and [Sisak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisak) opened in 1862 and in 1863 Zagreb received a [gasworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gasworks). Since 1 January 1877, the [Grič cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gri%C4%8D_cannon) fires daily from the [Lotrščak Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotr%C5%A1%C4%8Dak_Tower) on [Grič](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gri%C4%8D,_Zagreb) to mark midday. The Zagreb [waterworks](684928.md) opened in 1878.

After the [1880 Zagreb earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1880_Zagreb_earthquake),[^cite_note-60] up to the 1914 outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md), development flourished and the town received the characteristic layout which it has today. The first horse-drawn [tram](30733.md) dated from 1891. The construction of railway lines enabled the old suburbs to merge gradually into [Donji Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donji_grad_\(Zagreb\)), characterized by a regular block pattern that prevails in [Central European](5188.md) cities. This bustling core includes many imposing buildings, monuments, and parks as well as a multitude of museums, theatres, and cinemas. An [electric-power plant](212141.md) was built in 1907.

The first half of the 20th century saw a considerable expansion of Zagreb. Before World War I, the city expanded and neighborhoods like [Stara Peščenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stara_Pe%C5%A1%C4%8Denica) in the east and [Črnomerec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Crnomerec) in the west grew up. The Rokov perivoj neighbourhood, noted for its [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) features, was established at the start of the century.[^cite_note-61]

After the war, [working-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) districts such as [Trnje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trnje,_Zagreb) emerged between the railway and the [Sava](587845.md), whereas the construction of residential districts on the hills of the southern slopes of [Medvednica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvednica) was completed [between the two World Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period).

In the 1920s, the population of Zagreb increased by 70 percent – the largest demographic boom in the history of the town. In 1926, the first [radio station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_broadcasting) in the region began broadcasting from Zagreb, and in 1947 the [Zagreb Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Fair) opened.[^cite_note-HRT-59]

During World War II, Zagreb became the capital of the [Independent State of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_State_of_Croatia) (1941–1945), which was backed by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) and by the [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism_in_Italy). The history of [Zagreb in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_in_World_War_II) became rife with incidents of régime terror and resistance sabotage - the [Ustaša](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usta%C5%A1a) régime had thousands of people executed during the war in and near the city. [Partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisans_\(Yugoslavia\)) took the city at the end of the war. From 1945 until 1990, Zagreb functioned as the capital of the [Socialist Republic of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Croatia), one of the six constituent socialist republics of the [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia).

### Contemporary era



[Urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) of Zagreb

The area between the railway and the Sava river witnessed a new construction-boom after World War II. After the mid-1950s, construction of new residential areas south of the Sava river began, resulting in [Novi Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Zagreb) (Croatian for *New Zagreb*), originally called "Južni Zagreb" (*Southern Zagreb*).[^cite_note-juzni_zagreb-62] From 1999 Novi Zagreb has comprised two [city districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_districts): [Novi Zagreb – zapad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Zagreb_%E2%80%93_zapad) (*New Zagreb – West*) and [Novi Zagreb – istok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Zagreb_%E2%80%93_istok) (*New Zagreb – East*)

The city also expanded westward and eastward, incorporating [Dubrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrava,_Zagreb), Podsused, [Jarun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarun), [Blato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blato,_Zagreb), and other settlements.

The cargo railway hub and the [international airport](1519705.md) ([Pleso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Airport)) were built south of the Sava river. The largest [industrial zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_district) (Žitnjak) in the south-eastern part of the city, represents an extension of the industrial zones on the eastern outskirts of the city, between the Sava and the Prigorje region. Zagreb hosted the [Summer Universiade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Summer_Universiade) in 1987.[^cite_note-HRT-59] This event initiated the creation of pedestrian-only zones in the city centre and extensive new sport infrastructure, lacking until then, all around the city.

During the 1991–1995 [Croatian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_War_of_Independence), the city saw some [sporadic fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Barracks) around its [JNA](536643.md) [army barracks](1033489.md), but escaped major damage. In May 1995, it was targeted by Serb [rocket artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_artillery) in two [rocket attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_rocket_attacks) which killed seven civilians and wounded many.

An urbanized area connects Zagreb with the surrounding towns of [Zaprešić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapre%C5%A1i%C4%87), [Samobor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samobor), [Dugo Selo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugo_Selo), and [Velika Gorica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velika_Gorica). Sesvete was the first and the closest area to become a part of the [agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomeration) and is already included in the City of Zagreb for administrative purposes and now forms the easternmost city district.[^cite_note-63]

### Climate

The climate of Zagreb is classified as a [humid temperate climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_temperate_climate). It is further classified as [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) (Köppen: *Cfb*) bordering on a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (Köppen: *Cfa*).



Satellite view of Zagreb



Aerial view of [Zagreb's downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donji_grad,_Zagreb)

Zagreb has four distinct seasons. Summers are generally warm, sometimes hot. In late May it gets significantly warmer, temperatures start rising and it often becomes very warm or even hot with occasional afternoon and evening thunderstorms. Heatwaves can occur but are short-lived. Temperatures rise above 30 °C (86 °F) on average on 14.6 days in summer. During summertime, rainfall is abundant, often due to thunderstorms. With 840 mm of precipitation per year, Zagreb is Europe's ninth wettest capital, receiving less precipitation than [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_City) but more than [Brussels](3708.md), [Paris](22989.md) or [London](17867.md). Compared to these cities, however, Zagreb has fewer rainy days, but the annual rainfall is higher due to heavier showers occurring mainly in late spring and summer. Autumn in its early stage often brings pleasant and sunny weather with occasional episodes of rain later in the season. Late autumn is characterized by a mild increase in the number of rainy days and a gradual decrease in daily temperature averages. Morning fog is common from mid-October to January, with northern city districts at the foothills of the [Medvednica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvednica) mountain, as well as south-central districts along the [Sava](587845.md) river, being more prone to longer fog accumulation. Winters are relatively cold, bringing overcast skies and a precipitation decrease pattern. February is the driest month, averaging 39 mm of precipitation. On average there are 29 days with snowfall, with the first snow usually falling in early December. However, in recent years, the number of days with snowfall in wintertime has decreased considerably. Spring is characterized by often pleasant but changeable weather. As the season progresses, sunny days become more frequent, bringing higher temperatures. Sometimes cold spells can occur as well, mostly in the season's early stages. The average daily mean temperature in the winter is around 1 °C (34 °F) (from December to February) and the average temperature in the summer is 20 °C (68.0 °F).[^cite_note-normals-64] The highest recorded temperature at the Maksimir weather station was 40.4 °C (104.7 °F) in July 1950, and lowest was −27.3 °C (−17.1 °F) in February 1956.[^cite_note-HRMETEO-65] A temperature of −30.5 °C (−22.9 °F) was recorded on the since defunct Borongaj Airfield in February 1940.[^cite_note-66]

Since records began in 1981, the highest temperature recorded at the Rim weather station in Zagreb was 38.4 °C (101.1 °F), on 8 August 2018.[^cite_note-67] The coldest temperature was −19.4 °C (−2.9 °F), on 12 January 1985.[^cite_note-68]

### Cityscape



Panoramic view from Zagreb's [Upper town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gornji_Grad%E2%80%93Medve%C5%A1%C4%8Dak), with (from left to right) [St. Francis of Assisi Church](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franjeva%C4%8Dki%20samostan%20i%20crkva%20na%20Kaptolu), [Šalata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0alata), [Zagreb Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Cathedral) and [St. Mary's Church](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDupna%20crkva%20sv.%20Marije%20na%20Dolcu)

The most important historical [high-rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-rise) constructions are [Neboder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neboder) (1958) on [Ban Jelačić Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Jela%C4%8Di%C4%87_Square), [Cibona Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cibona_Tower) (1987), and [Zagrepčanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagrep%C4%8Danka) (1976) on Savska Street, [Mamutica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamutica) in Travno ([Novi Zagreb – istok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Zagreb_%E2%80%93_istok) district, built in 1974) and [Zagreb TV Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_TV_Tower) on [Sljeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvednica) (built in 1973).[^cite_note-72]

In the 2000s, the City Assembly approved a new plan that allowed for the many recent high-rise buildings in Zagreb, such as the Almeria Tower, [Eurotower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurotower_\(Zagreb\)), [HOTO Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOTO_Tower), [Zagrebtower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagrebtower), [Sky Office Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Office_Tower) and the [tallest high-rise building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallest_buildings_in_Croatia) in Zagreb [Strojarska Business Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strojarska_Business_Center).[^cite_note-skyoffice-73][^cite_note-empoirs-74]

In [Novi Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Zagreb), the neighbourhoods of [Blato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blato,_Zagreb) and [Lanište](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lani%C5%A1te,_Croatia) expanded significantly, including the [Zagreb Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Zagreb) and the adjoining business centre.[^cite_note-blato_lanište-75]

Due to a long-standing restriction that forbade the construction of 10-story or higher buildings, most of Zagreb's high-rise buildings date from the 1970s and 1980s and new apartment buildings on the outskirts of the city are usually 4–8 floors tall. Exceptions to the restriction have been made in recent years, such as permitting the construction of high-rise buildings in Lanište or Kajzerica.[^cite_note-javno-76]

### Surroundings

Further information: [Zagreb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_County), [Prigorje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prigorje), [Hrvatsko Zagorje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrvatsko_Zagorje), and [Turopolje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turopolje)



[Medvedgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvedgrad) fortress

The wider Zagreb area has been continuously inhabited since the prehistoric period, as witnessed by archaeological findings in the [Veternica cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veternica_cave) from the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) and excavation of the remains of the Roman Andautonia near the present village of Šćitarjevo.

many former villages on the slopes of Medvednica, Šestine, Gračani, and Remete, maintain their traditions, including folk costumes, Šestine umbrellas, and [gingerbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gingerbread) products.

To the north is the [Medvednica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvednica) mountain ([Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language): *Zagrebačka gora*), with its highest peak [Sljeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvednica) (1,035 m), where one of the tallest structures in Croatia, [Zagreb TV Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_TV_Tower) is located. The Sava and the [Kupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kupa_\(river\)) valleys are to the south of Zagreb, and the region of [Hrvatsko Zagorje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrvatsko_Zagorje) is located on the other (northern) side of the Medvednica hill. In mid-January 2005, Sljeme held its first [World Ski Championship tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Alpine_World_Ski_Championships).



[Zagreb TV Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_TV_Tower)

From the summit, weather permitting, the vista reaches as far as [Velebit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velebit) Range along Croatia's rocky northern coast, as well as the snow-capped peaks of the towering [Julian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Alps) in neighbouring [Slovenia](27338.md). There are several lodging villages, offering accommodation and restaurants for hikers. Skiers visit Sljeme, which has four ski-runs, three ski-lifts, and a chairlift.

The old [Medvedgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvedgrad), a recently restored medieval [burg](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Special\:Search/burg) was built in the 13th century on Medvednica hill. It overlooks the western part of the city and also hosts the *Shrine of the Homeland*, a memorial with an [eternal flame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_flame), where Croatia pays reverence to all its heroes fallen for [homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland) in its history, customarily on [national holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday). The ruined medieval fortress [Susedgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susedgrad) is located on the far-western side of Medvednica hill. It has been abandoned since the early 17th century, but it is visited during the year.

Zagreb occasionally experiences earthquakes, due to the proximity of [Žumberak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDumberak)-[Medvednica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvednica) fault zone.[^cite_note-77] It is classified as an area of high seismic activity.[^cite_note-embassy_earthquake-78] The area around Medvednica was the epicentre of the [1880 Zagreb earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1880_Zagreb_earthquake) (magnitude 6.3), and the area is known for occasional landslide threatening houses in the area.[^cite_note-landslide_survey_croatian-79] The proximity of strong seismic sources presents a real danger of strong earthquakes.[^cite_note-landslide_survey_croatian-79] Croatian Chief of [Office of Emergency Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Emergency_Management) Pavle Kalinić stated Zagreb experiences around 400 earthquakes a year, most of them being imperceptible. However, in case of a strong earthquake, it is expected that 3,000 people would die and up to 15,000 would be wounded.[^cite_note-80] In 2020 the city experienced a [5.5 magnitude earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Zagreb_earthquake), which damaged various buildings in the historic downtown area. The city's iconic cathedral lost the cross of one of its towers. This earthquake was the strongest one to affect the city since the destructive [1880 Zagreb earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1880_Zagreb_earthquake).

[Zagreb metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_metropolitan_area) population is slightly above one million inhabitants,[^cite_note-CityMayors-81] as it includes the [Zagreb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_County).[^cite_note-zg_cc-82] Zagreb metropolitan area makes approximately a quarter of the total [population of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Croatia). After the easing of [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) restrictions, thousands of foreign workers immigrated to Zagreb due to a shortage of labour in Croatia. These workers primarily come from countries such as [Nepal](171166.md), [the Philippines](23440.md), [India](14533.md), and [Bangladesh](3454.md), as well as some European countries including [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), [Serbia](29265.md), [Kosovo](17391.md) and [North Macedonia](23564616.md).[^cite_note-85] However, the majority of these workers are on temporary work permits, and might not plan on staying in the country permanently, mostly due to language skills and citizenship requirements.

## Politics and government

See also: [Politics of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Croatia)



[Croatian Parliament](398696.md) on [St. Mark's Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_Square,_Zagreb)



[Banski dvori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banski_dvori), seat of the [Government of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Croatia)

Zagreb is the capital of the [Republic of Croatia](5573.md), its political centre and the centre of various state institutions.

On the [St. Mark's Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_Square,_Zagreb) are the seats of the [Government of the Republic of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Croatia) in the [Banski Dvori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banski_Dvori) complex, the [Croatian Parliament](398696.md) (*Sabor*), as well as the [Constitutional Court of the Republic of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Croatia). Various ministries and state agencies are located in the wider area of the City of Zagreb.

### City governance

According to the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Croatia), the city of Zagreb, as the capital of Croatia, has a special status. As such, Zagreb performs [self-governing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance) public affairs of both [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Croatia) and [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Croatia). It is also the seat of the [Zagreb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_County) which encircles Zagreb. The city administration bodies are the [Zagreb City Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Assembly) as the representative body and the [Mayor of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Zagreb) who is the executive head of the city. The current mayor is [Tomislav Tomašević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomislav_Toma%C5%A1evi%C4%87) ([We Can!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_can!_\(Croatia\))), elected in the [2021 Zagreb local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Zagreb_local_elections), the second round of which was held on 30 May 2021. There are two deputy mayors elected from the same list, [Danijela Dolenec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danijela_Dolenec) and [Luka Korlaet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luka_Korlaet).[^cite_note-92]



[Zagreb Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Assembly)



City hall

The 47 representatives of the City Assembly is elected for a four-year term on the basis of [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) in [direct elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_elections) by [secret ballot](479210.md) using [proportional system](23298.md) with [d'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method) in a manner specified by law. The president and vice-presidents of the assembly are elected by the representatives. All changes to urban planning documents and the budget must be passed through the assembly.

The [Office of the Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Zagreb) and the deputies are elected on a four-year term. Before 2009, the mayor was elected by the City Assembly, but was changed to direct elections by [majoritarian vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_vote) with a [two-round system](25932.md). The mayor, along with the deputies, may be recalled by a referendum according to the law from not less than 20% of all electors or two-thirds of the assembly. The mayor is responsible for the state administration due to the city's special status, including appointing all heads of city offices and services. The city administration is divided into 12 city offices which are responsible for managing everything from [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) to culture, the City institute for the Protection of Cultural and Natural Monuments, System Information and Technical Affairs Service, City Administration Expert Service and the City Assembly Expert Service, which is managed by a secretary appointed by the assembly.[^cite_note-93] The city owns [Zagreb Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Holding), a city enterprise which operates many branches carrying out all operating tasks,[^cite_note-94] and [Zagreb Electric Tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Electric_Tram), the city transit authority.[^cite_note-95]

Local government is organized into 17 [city districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Zagreb) represented by City District Councils which are elected by the district's residents.[^cite_note-96] Districts have to adopt plans for the maintenance of infrastructure and for community programs and projects, and can propose changes to urban development documents. The city is further subdivided into 218 local committees, the second level of local self-government. They are in charge of organizing cultural or other events in the area and listening to the problems of citizens living there.[^cite_note-97] Since 2024, as part of [participatory budgeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participatory_budgeting), residents can propose and discuss new projects in their neighborhoods.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

#### Minority councils and representatives

Directly elected minority councils and representatives are tasked with consulting tasks for the local or regional authorities in which they are advocating for minority rights and interests, integration into public life and participation in the management of local affairs.[^cite_note-T-Portal-100] At the [2023 Croatian national minorities councils and representatives elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Croatian_national_minorities_councils_and_representatives_elections) [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_of_Croatia), [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks_of_Croatia), [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs_of_Croatia), [Hungarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarians_of_Croatia), [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonians_of_Croatia), [Montenegrins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrins_of_Croatia), [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Croatia), [Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenes_of_Croatia) and [Serbs of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Croatia) each fulfilled legal requirements to elect 25 members minority councils of the City of Zagreb while [Bulgarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarians_in_Croatia), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_of_Croatia), [Pannonian Rusyns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Rusyns), [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_of_Croatia), [Slovaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks_of_Croatia), [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians_of_Croatia), [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_Croatia), [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_of_Croatia) and [Jews of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews_of_Croatia) elected individual representatives, with a representative of the [Germans of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_of_Croatia) remaining unelected due to the lack of candidates.[^cite_note-GZ-Manjine-2023-101]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Croatia)

### Tourism



View of the [St. Mark's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_Church,_Zagreb) with the famous colourful roof representing the coat of arms of the [Triune Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triune_Kingdom) of [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Croatia_\(Habsburg\)), [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dalmatia) and [Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Slavonia) (left), and the City of Zagreb (right)

Zagreb is an important tourist centre, not only in terms of passengers travelling from the rest of Europe to the Adriatic Sea but also as a travel destination itself. Since the end of the [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Croatia), it has attracted close to a million visitors annually, mainly from Austria, Germany, and Italy, and in recent years many tourists from far east (South Korea, Japan, China, and last two years, from India). Due to its location and tourism boom during [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), Zagreb saw an increase in need of accommodation, therefore the first registered hotel, [Palace Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_Hotel,_Zagreb) was built in 1907.[^cite_note-122] Today it is an important tourist destination, not only in Croatia, but considering the whole region of southeastern Europe. There are many interesting sights and happenings for tourists to attend in Zagreb, for example, the two statues of Saint George, one at the [Republic of Croatia Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Croatia_Square), the other at the [Stone Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Gate), where the image of the Virgin Mary is said to be the only thing that did not burn in the 17th-century fire. Also, there is an art installation starting in the Bogovićeva Street, called [Nine Views](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Views). Zagreb is also famous for its award-winning [Christmas market](1340652.md) that had been named the one in Europe for three consecutive years (2015, 2016 and 2017) by *European Best Destinations*.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

The capital is also known for its many restaurants[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] that serve not only traditional [Croatian food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_cuisine) and classic dishes. In addition to that, a lot of [international hotel chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_chained-brand_hotels) are offering their accommodations in Zagreb.

The historical part of the city to the north of [Ban Jelačić Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Jela%C4%8Di%C4%87_Square) is composed of the [Gornji Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradec,_Zagreb) and [Kaptol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaptol,_Zagreb), a medieval urban complex of churches, palaces, museums, galleries and government buildings that are popular with tourists on sightseeing tours. The historic district can be reached on foot, starting from the Ban Jelačić Square, the centre of Zagreb, or by a [funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular) on nearby Tomićeva Street. Each Saturday, (from April until the end of September), on [St. Mark's Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_Square,_Zagreb) in the Upper Town, tourists can meet members of the Order of The Silver Dragon (*Red Srebrnog Zmaja*), who reenact famous historical conflicts between [Gradec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradec,_Zagreb) and [Kaptol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaptol,_Zagreb).

Some famous Zagreb souvenirs are the [tie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necktie) or *cravat*, an accessory named after Croats who wore characteristic scarves around their necks in the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War) in the 17th century, and the [ball-point pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballpoint_pen), a tool developed from the inventions by [Slavoljub Eduard Penkala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavoljub_Eduard_Penkala), an inventor and a citizen of Zagreb.

In 2010 more than 600,000[^cite_note-127] tourists visited the city, with a 10%[^cite_note-128] increase seen in 2011. In 2012 a total of 675,707 tourists[^cite_note-129] visited the city. A record number of tourists visited Zagreb in 2017, 1,286,087, up 16% compared to the year before, which generated 2,263,758 overnight stays, up 14.8%.

Domestic dishes include [turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_as_food), [duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck_\(meat\)) or [goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose) with *[mlinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlinci)* (flat pasta, soaked in roast juices), famous z*agrebački odrezak* (type of [cordon bleu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordon_bleu_\(dish\))), *faširanci* (fried minced meat mixed with onions and bread crumbs),[^cite_note-130] and *[sarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarma_\(food\))* ([sauerkraut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerkraut) rolls filed with minced pork meat and rice, served with [mashed potato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashed_potato)). Some are made out of permanent materials to be sold as souvenirs.[^cite_note-134]

Zagreb is home to two [Michelin star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_star) restaurants.[^cite_note-135] Many restaurants offer various specialties of [national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_cuisine) and [international cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_cuisine).[^cite_note-136] Bakeries sell regional pastries like [burek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burek), strudels and [pies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pie). ### Cultural institutions



[Esplanade Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esplanade_Zagreb_Hotel)



[Ilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilica_\(street\)), the famous shopping street of the city



[Tkalčićeva Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tkal%C4%8Di%C4%87eva_Street), commonly known as "Tkalča" has many cafes, bars and restaurants of local and foreign cuisine

Zagreb's museums reflect the history, art, and culture not only of Zagreb and Croatia, but also of Europe and the world. Around thirty collections in museums and galleries comprise more than 3.6 million various exhibits, excluding church and private collections.

The [Archaeological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum,_Zagreb) collections, today consisting of nearly 450,000 varied [archaeological](18951655.md) artefacts and monuments, have been gathered over the years from many different sources. These holdings include evidence of Croatian presence in the area.[^cite_note-zageb_archeo-141] The most famous are the Egyptian collection, the [Zagreb mummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Linteus) and bandages with the oldest [Etruscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Italic_alphabet) inscription in the world (*[Liber Linteus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liber_Linteus) Zagrabiensis*), as well as the [numismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_collecting) collection.

The [Modern Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Gallery,_Zagreb) ([Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language): *Moderna galerija*) holds the most important and comprehensive collection of paintings, sculptures and drawings by 19th- and 20th-century Croatian artists. The collection numbers more than 10,000 works of art, housed since 1934 in the historic Vranyczany Palace in the centre of Zagreb, overlooking the Zrinjevac Park. A secondary gallery is the Josip Račić Studio.[^cite_note-moderngallery-142]

The [Croatian Natural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Natural_History_Museum) holds one of the world's most important collections of [Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) remains found at one site.[^cite_note-zageb_nat-143] These are the remains, stone weapons, and tools of prehistoric *[Krapina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krapina) man*. The holdings of the Croatian Natural History Museum comprise more than 250,000 specimens distributed among various collections.

The [Technical Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_Museum,_Zagreb) was founded in 1954 and it maintains the oldest preserved machine in the area, dating from 1830, which is still operational. The museum exhibits numerous historic aircraft, cars, machinery and equipment. There are some distinct sections in the museum: the Planetarium, the Apisarium, the Mine (model of mines for coal, iron and [non-ferrous metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrous), about 300 m (980 ft) long), and the [Nikola Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Tesla) study.[^cite_note-zageb_tech-144][^cite_note-145]

The [Museum of the City of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_City_Museum) was established in 1907 by the Association of the Braća Hrvatskog Zmaja. It is located in a restored monumental complex (Popov toranj, the Observatory, Zakmardi Granary) of the former Convent of the [Poor Clares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_Clares), of 1650.[^cite_note-zageb_citymus-146] The Museum deals with topics from the cultural, artistic, economic and [political history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_history) of the city spanning from Roman finds to the [modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_history). The holdings comprise over 80,000 items arranged systematically into collections of artistic and mundane objects characteristic of the city and its history.

The [Arts and Crafts Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Arts_and_Crafts,_Zagreb) was founded in 1880 with the intention of preserving the works of art and craft against the new predominance of industrial products. With its 160,000 exhibits, the Arts and Crafts Museum is a national-level museum for artistic production and the history of [material culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_culture) in Croatia.[^cite_note-zageb_artsandcrafts-147]



[Mimara Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimara_Museum)



[Institute of Lexicography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miroslav_Krle%C5%BEa_Institute_of_Lexicography)

The [Ethnographic Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnographic_Museum,_Zagreb) was founded in 1919. It lies in the fine Secession building of the one-time Trades Hall of 1903. The ample holdings of about 80,000 items cover the ethnographic heritage of Croatia, classified in three cultural zones: the Pannonian, Dinaric and Adriatic.[^cite_note-zageb_ethnographic-148]

The [Mimara Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimara_Museum) an [art museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_museum), that was founded with a donation from [Ante Topić Mimara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ante_Topi%C4%87_Mimara) and opened to the public in 1987. It is located in a late 19th-century [neo-Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Revival_architecture) palace.[^cite_note-zageb_mim-149] The holdings comprise 3,750 works of art of various techniques and materials, and different cultures and civilizations, including paintings from great European masters like: [Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubens), [Velázquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez), [Renoir](24546.md), [Goya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Goya), [Hieronymus Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieronymus_Bosch), [Veronese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Veronese), [Manet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Manet), [Degas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Degas), [van Dyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_van_Dyck) and many others.

The [Croatian Museum of Naïve Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Museum_of_Na%C3%AFve_Art) is one of the first museums of [naïve art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na%C3%AFve_art) in the world. The museum holds works of Croatian naïve expression of the 20th century. It is located in the 18th-century Raffay Palace in the [Gornji Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gornji_Grad_%E2%80%93_Medve%C5%A1%C4%8Dak). The museum holdings consist of almost 2000 works of art – paintings, sculptures, drawings, and prints, mainly by Croatians but also by other well-known world artists.[^cite_note-zagreb_naïve-150] From time to time, the museum organizes topics and retrospective exhibitions by naïve artists, expert meetings and educational workshops and playrooms.



[Meštrović Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me%C5%A1trovi%C4%87_Pavilion)



[Museum of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Contemporary_Art,_Zagreb)

The [Museum of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Contemporary_Art,_Zagreb) was founded in 1954. Its new building hosts a rich collection of Croatian and international contemporary visual art which has been collected throughout the decades from the nineteen-fifties until today. The museum is located in the centre of [Novi Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Zagreb) and opened in 2009. The old location is now part of the Kulmer Palace in the [Gornji Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gornji_Grad_%E2%80%93_Medve%C5%A1%C4%8Dak).[^cite_note-zagreb_contemporary-151]

The Institute for Contemporary Art (*Institut za suvremenu umjetnost*), successor to the Soros Center for Contemporary Art – Zagreb (SCCA – Zagreb), was founded in 1993, and registered as an independent nonprofit organization in 1998. It was founded and run by [art historians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_historian), curators, artists, photographers, designers, publishers, academics, and journalists, and initially located at the Museum of Contemporary Art. After moving a number of times, the institute has a gallery at the Academia Moderna. Its aims are to promote contemporary Croatian artists and the [visual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts) and other creative arts; to start documenting contemporary artists; and to build a body of contemporary art. It established the Radoslav Putar Award in 2002.[^cite_note-152]

The [Strossmayer Gallery of Old Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strossmayer_Gallery_of_Old_Masters) offers permanent holdings presenting European paintings from the 14th to 19th centuries,[^cite_note-zageb_stross-153] and the [Ivan Meštrović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Me%C5%A1trovi%C4%87) Studio, with sculptures, drawings, lithography portfolios and other items, was a donation of this great artist to his homeland. The Museum and Gallery Center introduces on various occasions the Croatian and foreign cultural and artistic heritage.

The [Art Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Pavilion,_Zagreb) by Viennese architects Hellmer and Fellmer who were the most famous designers of theatres in Central Europe is a [neo-classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism) exhibition complex and one of the landmarks of the downtown. The exhibitions are also held in the Meštrović building on the [Square of the Victims of Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_of_the_Victims_of_Fascism) – the Home of Croatian Fine Artists. The World Center "Wonder of Croatian Naïve Art" exhibits masterpieces of Croatian naïve art as well as the works of a new generation of artists. The Modern Gallery comprises all relevant fine artists of the 19th and 20th centuries. The [Museum of Broken Relationships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Broken_Relationships) at 2 Ćirilometodska holds people's mementos of past relationships.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] It is the first private museum in the country.[^cite_note-157] [Lauba House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauba_House) presents works from the [Filip Trade Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Trade_Collection), a large private collection of modern and contemporary Croatian art and current artistic production.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]



[Zagreb Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Botanical_Garden)



[Zagreb Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Zoo)

[Other museums and galleries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Croatia) are also found in the Croatian School Museum, the Croatian Hunting Museum, the Croatian Sports Museum, the Croatian Post and Telecommunications Museum, the HAZU ([Croatian Academy of Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts)) Glyptotheque (collection of monuments), and the HAZU Graphics Cabinet.

There are five [castles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle) in Zagreb: Dvorac Brezovica, Kašina (Castrum antiquum Paganorum), Medvedgrad, Susedgrad and Kulmerovi dvori.[^cite_note-160]

[Zagreb Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Zoo) is a 7-hectare [zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo) located in [Maksimir Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksimir_Park) opened in 1925.[^cite_note-161] It holds 6754 animals from more than 370 species from all around the world,[^cite_note-162] and is the most visited zoo in Croatia with almost half of million visitors in 2023.[^cite_note-163]

### Performing arts



[Croatian National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_National_Theatre_in_Zagreb)



[Vatroslav Lisinski Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatroslav_Lisinski_Concert_Hall)

There are about 20 permanent or seasonal theatres and stages. The [Croatian National Theater in Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_National_Theatre_in_Zagreb) was built in 1895 and opened by emperor [Franz Joseph I of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I_of_Austria). The most renowned [concert hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_concert_halls) named "[Vatroslav Lisinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatroslav_Lisinski_Concert_Hall)", after the [composer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatroslav_Lisinski) of the first Croatian opera, was built in 1973.

The [World Theatre Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Theatre_Festival_\(Zagreb\)) and [International Puppet Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Puppet_Festival) both take place in Zagreb in September and October.[^cite_note-zagrebfests-164]

*Animafest*, the World Festival of [Animated Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animation), takes place every even-numbered year, and the *Music Biennale*, the international festival of [avant-garde music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde_music), every odd-numbered year. In the summer, theatre performances and concerts, mostly in the Upper Town, are organized either indoors or outdoors. The stage on Opatovina hosts the *Zagreb Histrionic Summer* theatre events.

Zagreb is also the host of *Zagrebfest*, the oldest Croatian [pop-music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music) [festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_festival), as well as of several traditional international sports events and tournaments. The *Day of the City of Zagreb* on 16 November is celebrated every year with special festivities, especially on the [Jarun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarun) lake in the southwestern part of the city.

#### Parks

Zagreb is home to numerous sports and recreational parks. Recreational Sports Center Jarun, situated on the Jarun Lake in the southwest of the city, has shingle beaches, a [regatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regatta) course used for training and sports competitions, a [jogging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogging) and a bike lane around the lake, and several restaurants and [night clubs](19321330.md). Its sports and recreation opportunities include swimming, sunbathing, waterskiing, angling, and other [water sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_water_sports), for which it is most popular, but also [beach volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_volleyball), football, basketball, handball, table tennis, and [mini-golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniature_golf).[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166] To the east in [Novi Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Zagreb) lays [Bundek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundek), a park with two small lakes near the Sava river, which were created in the 60s because of heavy excavations in the area, from which the material was used to build the [Zagreb Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Fair) and the Youth Bridge. The location had then been used until the 1970s, when it went into neglect until its complete renovation in 2006.[^cite_note-167] In the north-east is [Maksimir Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksimir_Park), an urban forest that was gifted to the city by bishop [Maksimilijan Vrhovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksimilijan_Vrhovac). Because of its many meadows and lakes, which are well connected by a series of pathways, it is a popular place for walking, running and cycling.[^cite_note-168]

#### Sports

In northern [Trešnjevka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tre%C5%A1njevka) lays [Dom sportova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Sportova), a sports centre that features six halls, of which the largest two have a seating capacity of 5,000 and 3,100 people.[^cite_note-169] This centre is used for various sports: basketball, handball, volleyball, hockey, gymnastics, tennis, and others, as well as music events.[^cite_note-170] [Sports Park Mladost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mladost_\(sports_society\)), situated on the embankment of the Sava river, has an [Olympic-size swimming pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic-size_swimming_pool), smaller indoor and outdoor swimming pools, a sunbathing terrace, 16 tennis courts as well as [basketball](3921.md), [volleyball](32558.md), [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_handball), [football](10568.md) and [field hockey](10886.md) courts. A volleyball sports hall is within the park. Sports and recreational centre "Šalata", located in the neighborhood [Šalata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0alata) only a couple hundred meters from the [Ban Jelačić Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Jela%C4%8Di%C4%87_Square), includes nine [tennis courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_court), four of which are covered in the winter to protect from the cold, as well as swimming pools which are open during the summer, basketball courts and football fields. Many of the fields are used by sports clubs. Outdoor [ice skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_skating) is available during the winter.[^cite_note-171] Maksimir Tennis Center, located in the neighborhood of [Ravnice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravnice,_Zagreb), east of [downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donji_grad_\(Zagreb\)), consists of 22 outside and 4 inside tennis courts, and one [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\)) court.[^cite_note-172] There are currently seven pool complexes across the city.[^cite_note-173] Since the city doesn't have many skate parks, skaters can only skate in a few locations in Zagreb, them being [Jarun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarun), [Špansko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0pansko) and [Vrapče](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrap%C4%8De) (which is a DIY skate park built by volunteers).[^cite_note-174] [Zagreb Hippodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Hippodrome) offers recreational [horseback riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrianism) and [horse races](74711.md), able to house 175 horses.[^cite_note-175] It has been used for mass gatherings and concerts.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177]

[Arena Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Zagreb) is the main and the biggest arena in the city, with a total of 16,500 seats. It hosted the [2009 World Men's Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Men's_Handball_Championship).[^cite_note-178] The [Dražen Petrović Basketball Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dra%C5%BEen_Petrovi%C4%87_Basketball_Hall) is the home arena of [Cibona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Cibona), one of the main basketball clubs in the city,[^cite_note-179] with a seating capacity of 5017 people.[^cite_note-180] It is located next to the 94 m (308 ft) tall [Cibona Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cibona_Tower).[^cite_note-181] The 35,123-seat [Maksimir Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Maksimir) is the largest stadium in the city and home to [Dinamo Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNK_Dinamo_Zagreb).[^cite_note-182] Opened in 1912, it has undergone many transformations, but was never completely finished.[^cite_note-183] The stadium is located next to the Svetice Recreational and Sports Complex and Maksimir Park.[^cite_note-184]

Most notable sports clubs include football teams [GNK Dinamo Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNK_Dinamo_Zagreb)[^cite_note-185] and [NK Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NK_Zagreb),[^cite_note-186] basketball clubs [KK Cibona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Cibona), [KK Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Zagreb), and [KK Cedevita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Cedevita),[^cite_note-187] ice hockey club [KHL Medveščak Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHL_Medve%C5%A1%C4%8Dak_Zagreb),[^cite_note-188] handball team [RK Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RK_Zagreb),[^cite_note-189] and water polo club [HAVK Mladost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAVK_Mladost).[^cite_note-190]

### Religion









Clockwise from top left: [Zagreb Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Cathedral), [Synagogue of the Jewish Community of Zagreb](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinagoga%20%C5%BDidovske%20op%C4%87ine%20Zagreb), [Zagreb Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Mosque) and [Serbian Orthodox Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Transfiguration_of_the_Lord,_Zagreb) with statue of [Petar Preradović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petar_Preradovi%C4%87)

The [Archdiocese of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Zagreb) is a [metropolitan see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) of the [Catholic Church in Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Croatia), serving as its religious centre. The Catholic Church is the largest religious organization in Zagreb, Catholicism being the predominant religion of Croatia, with over 1,1 million adherents.[^cite_note-191] Zagreb is also the [episcopal see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_see) of the [Metropolitanate of Zagreb and Ljubljana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitanate_of_Zagreb_and_Ljubljana) of the [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy_in_Croatia). [Islamic religious organization of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Croatia) has the see in Zagreb. President is Mufti Aziz Hasanović. There used to be a mosque in the [Meštrović Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me%C5%A1trovi%C4%87_Pavilion) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_in_World_War_II)[^cite_note-192] at the [Square of the Victims of Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_of_the_Victims_of_Fascism), but it was relocated to the neighborhood of Borovje in [Peščenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C5%A1%C4%8Denica_%E2%80%93_%C5%BDitnjak). Mainstream Protestant churches have also been present in Zagreb – [Evangelical Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Church,_Zagreb) and Reformed Christian (Calvinist) Church. [The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) is also present in the Zagreb neighborhood of [Jarun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarun) whereas [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) have their headquarters in Central Zagreb.[^cite_note-193] In total there are around 40 non-Catholic religious organizations and denominations in Zagreb with their headquarters and places of worship across the city making it a large and diverse multicultural community. There is also significant [Jewish history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Croatia) through [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_the_Independent_State_of_Croatia).

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Zagreb)

### Highways

Main article: [Highways in Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highways_in_Croatia)



[Zagreb bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_bypass) existing and planned routes

Zagreb is the hub of five major Croatian highways. The [A6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A6_\(Croatia\)) highway was upgraded in October 2008 and leads [from Zagreb to Rijeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A7_\(Croatia\)#Route_description), forming a part of the Pan-European Corridor Vb. The upgrade coincided with the opening of the [bridge over the Mura river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zrinski_Bridge) on the [A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4_\(Croatia\)) and the completion of the Hungarian [M7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M7_motorway_\(Hungary\)), which marked the opening of the first freeway corridor between [Rijeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijeka) and [Budapest](36787.md).[^cite_note-arz-209] The [A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_\(Croatia\)) starts at the [Lučko interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C4%8Dko_interchange) and [concurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrency_\(road\)) with the A6 up to the [Bosiljevo 2 interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosiljevo_2_interchange), connecting Zagreb and [Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split,_Croatia). A further extension of the A1 up to [Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrovnik) is under construction. Both highways are tolled by the Croatian highway authorities [Hrvatske autoceste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrvatske_autoceste) and [Autocesta Rijeka - Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocesta_Rijeka_-_Zagreb).

The [A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_\(Croatia\)) highway (formerly named [Bratstvo i jedinstvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brotherhood_and_unity)) was the showpiece of Croatia in the [SFRY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). It is the oldest Croatian highway.[^cite_note-bratstvo-enotnost-210][^cite_note-RTS-vremeplov-211] A3 forms a part of the [Pan-European Corridor X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_X). The highway starts at the [Bregana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bregana) [border crossing](288329.md), bypasses Zagreb forming the southern arch of the [Zagreb bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_bypass), and ends at [Lipovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipovac,_Vukovar-Srijem_County) near the Bajakovo border crossing. It continues in [Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe) in the direction of [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East). This highway is tolled except for the stretch between Bobovica and [Ivanja Reka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivanja_Reka_interchange) interchanges.[^cite_note-HAC-A3-prospekt-212]

The [A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2_\(Croatia\)) highway is a part of the [Corridor Xa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_Xa).[^cite_note-corridors-213] It connects Zagreb and the frequently congested [Macelj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macelj) border crossing, forming a near-continuous motorway-level link between Zagreb and [Western Europe](33800.md).[^cite_note-2000loan-214] Forming a part of the Corridor Vb, highway [A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4_\(Croatia\)) starts in Zagreb forming the northeastern wing of the [Zagreb bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_bypass) and leads to Hungary until the [Goričan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gori%C4%8Dan) border crossing. It is often used highway around Zagreb.[^cite_note-215]

The railway and the A3 highway along the [Sava](587845.md) river that extend to [Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonia) (towards [Slavonski Brod](149285.md), [Vinkovci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinkovci), [Osijek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osijek) and [Vukovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukovar)) are some of the busiest traffic corridors in the country.[^cite_note-zageb_tran-216] The railway running along the [Sutla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutla) river and the A2 highway (Zagreb-[Macelj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macelj)) running through [Zagorje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagora_\(Croatia\)), as well as traffic connections with the Pannonian region and Hungary (the [Zagorje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrvatsko_Zagorje) railroad, the roads and railway to [Varaždin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vara%C5%BEdin) – [Čakovec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cakovec) and [Koprivnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koprivnica)) are linked with truck routes.[^cite_note-hž-217] The southern railway connection to [Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split,_Croatia) operates on a high-speed [tilting trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilting_train) line via the [Lika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lika) region (renovated in 2004 to allow for a five-hour journey); a faster line along the [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_\(Sava\)) river valley is in use only up to the border between Croatia and [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md).[^cite_note-hž-217][^cite_note-vjesnik_tilting_trains-218]

### Cycling

The conditions for [cycling](5931.md) in Zagreb are favourable because of its flat terrain under the Medvednica mountain[^cite_note-233] and little snowfall during winter.[^cite_note-234] Zagreb has warm summers and cold winters, which can make cycling more difficult.[^cite_note-235] The biking infrastructure in the city are a series of unconnected bike lanes that start and end abruptly instead of forming a connected network. Most of the existing lanes are located on sidewalks and are indicated by a painted line.[^cite_note-236] The city had a [Nextbike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nextbike) [bike-sharing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike-sharing_system) from 2013 to 2025,[^cite_note-237] when it was replaced by a €11.6 million city-funded *Bajs* system.[^cite_note-238] It is maintained by a Nextbike licensed partner *Public bicycle system*, since they were the only ones to make an offe&#x72;*.*[^cite_note-239]

[Public transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) in the city is organized in multiple layers: the inner parts of the city are mostly covered by [trams](30733.md), while the outer city areas and suburbs are linked with buses and rapid transit [commuter rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Commuter_Rail). The public transportation company [Zagreb Electric Tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Electric_Tram) operates all tram and city bus lines, and most of the suburban bus lines.[^cite_note-Javni_prijevoz_u_Zagrebu-240] The [Zagreb Funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Funicular) (*uspinjača*), which was built in the 19th century and is only long 66 meters, is a popular [tourist attraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_attraction). It was built before the Jubilee Economic-Forestry Exhibition to easily travel between the growing lower part of the city and the administrative and political centre on the Upper Town.[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242] To the north, Sljeme, the peak of the Medvednica mountain, is accessible by a [gondola lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Cable_Car). The ride lasts for just over 16 minutes and has an elevation of 754 m.[^cite_note-243] [Zagreb Bus Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Bus_Station) also serves as important hub for [bus transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_transport), which is very well connected with rest of the Europe.

The national rail operator [Croatian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Railways) runs a network of urban and suburban train lines in the metropolitan Zagreb area and is a [government-owned corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government-owned_corporation).

The [taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab) market has been [liberalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalization) in early 2018,[^cite_note-244] and numerous transport companies (including [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber)) have been allowed to enter the market. Despite this liberalization, taxi prices were prevented from increasing too much via legislation that [capped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_controls) them on one tenth of the average monthly [salary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salary).[^cite_note-245]

#### Tram network

Main article: [Trams in Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Zagreb)

Zagreb has an extensive [tram](30733.md) network with 15 day and 4 night lines covering much of the inner- and middle-suburbs of the city.[^cite_note-Javni_prijevoz_u_Zagrebu-240][^cite_note-246] The first tram line was opened on 5 September 1891 and trams have been serving as a vital component of Zagreb mass transit ever since.[^cite_note-247] Many new lines were introduced in the coming years, and by 1911, the whole network was electrified.[^cite_note-248] New tracks were continuously added to the network throughout the 20th century,[^cite_note-249] even during [World War II](32927.md) under the leadership of Dragutin Mandl.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251] The last expansion happened in 2000.[^cite_note-252] The average speed of trams is only 13 km/h (8.08 mph), one of the slowest in Europe.[^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255] On narrower streets, the tracks are either shared with car traffic or separated by a painted yellow line, which can still be used by taxis, buses and emergency vehicles, which can slow down trams during [rush hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_hour), whereas on larger avenues the tracks are situated inside [green belts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_track).[^cite_note-256]

The rolling stock consists of the newer Croatian-built [Crotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crotram) [TMK 2100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMK_2100), [TMK 2200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMK_2200), [TMK 2300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMK_2300) and [TMK 2400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMK_2400) tram types, and older [Tatra T4YU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatra_T4) and [KT4YU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatra_KT4) types.[^cite_note-257] The city also bought 11 used [GT6M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADtranz_low_floor_tram) trams to replace the older [TMK 201](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMK_201) trams.[^cite_note-258] Trams transported 121.83 million people in 2024, or roughly 333,773 a day.[^cite_note-259]

### Rail

The train tracks form a circle around the inner-city and have branches going in four directions: Zaprešić, [Dugo Selo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugo_Selo), [Jastrebarsko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jastrebarsko) and [Velika Gorica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velika_Gorica). Zagreb is connected with all major cities in Croatia, including Vinkovci, [Karlovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovac), [Rijeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijeka) and [Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split,_Croatia),[^cite_note-\:0-260][^cite_note-261] as well with cities in other European countries - [Ljubljana](42374.md), [Budapest](36787.md), [Munich](19058.md), [Vienna](55866.md) and [Zurich](40334603.md). A 2024 [Greenpeace](12233.md) study found out that Zagreb had much unused potential in connecting with other major European cities, instead prioritizing air travel.[^cite_note-262]

#### Commuter rail

The [commuter rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Zagreb#Commuter_rail) in Zagreb has existed since 1992, with the introduction of the [Savski Marof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savski_Marof) - Zagreb - Dugo Selo line.[^cite_note-263] In 2005, suburban rail services were increased to a 15-minute frequency serving the middle and outer suburbs of Zagreb, primarily in the east–west direction and to the southern districts. This has enhanced the commuting opportunities across the city.[^cite_note-suburban_rail-264] The cities connected by the suburban lines include [Harmica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmica,_Zagreb_County), Savski Marof, Zaprešić, Dugo Selo and Velika Gorica.[^cite_note-\:0-260] The passenger trains ride on the same tracks as [freight trains](70161362.md), which can cause delays.[^cite_note-265] Zagreb Electric Tram and [HŽ Putnički prijevoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C5%BD_Putni%C4%8Dki_prijevoz) offer combined monthly and yearly ticket passes with discounted prices.[^cite_note-266]

### Air traffic

Main article: [Zagreb Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Airport)



Franjo Tuđman Airport terminal

[Zagreb Franjo Tuđman Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **ZAG**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **LDZA**) is the main Croatian international airport, a 17 km (11 mi) drive southeast of Zagreb in the city of [Velika Gorica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velika_Gorica). The airport is also the main Croatian airbase featuring a fighter squadron, helicopters, as well as military and freight [transport aircraft](2375692.md).[^cite_note-zageb_pleso-267] The airport had 3.45 million passengers in 2019 with a new passenger terminal being opened in late March 2017 that can accommodate up to 5.5 million passengers, and in 2025 it hit record number of passengers with 4.72 million, making it [the busiest airport in Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Croatia) and [one of the busiest airports in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Europe). Zagreb Airport is the only airport in Croatia that has direct flights with [Canada](5042916.md), and one of two (with [Dubrovnik Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrovnik_Airport)) to have direct flights with [North America](21139.md).[^cite_note-268] The airport is served by a bus line from [Eugen Kvaternik Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_Kvaternik_Square).[^cite_note-269]

Zagreb also has a second, smaller airport, [Lučko Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C4%8Dko_Airfield) ([ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **LDZL**). It is home to sports airplanes and a Croatian special police unit, as well as being a military helicopter airbase. Lučko used to be the main airport of Zagreb from 1947 to 1959.[^cite_note-270]

A third, small grass airfield, Buševec, is located just outside [Velika Gorica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velika_Gorica). It is primarily used for sports purposes.[^cite_note-buševec-271]

### University of Zagreb

Main article: [University of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zagreb)

Further information: [List of universities in Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Croatia)



[Faculty of Science, University of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Science,_University_of_Zagreb)



[Croatian Academy of Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts)



[National and University Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_and_University_Library_in_Zagreb)

Founded in 1669, the University of Zagreb is the oldest continuously operating university in Croatia and one of the largest[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280][^cite_note-281][^cite_note-282] and oldest universities in the Southeastern Europe. Ever since its foundation, the university has been continually growing and developing and now consists of 29 faculties, three art academies and the Croatian Studies Centre. More than 200,000 students have attained the [Bachelor's degree](188874.md) at the university, which has also assigned 18,000 [Master's](170346.md) and 8,000 [Doctor's degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate).[^cite_note-zg_brief-283] As of 2011, the University of Zagreb is ranked among 500 Best Universities of the world by the Shanghai Academic Ranking of World Universities.

Zagreb is the seat of two private universities: the [Catholic University of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Croatia) and the Libertas International University. It also hosts numerous public and private polytechnics, colleges and higher professional schools.
