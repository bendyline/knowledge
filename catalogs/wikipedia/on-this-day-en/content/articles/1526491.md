The **Bukhman** **International Booker Prize** (formerly known as the **Man Booker International Prize**, and the **International Booker Prize** prior to 2026) is an international [literary award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_award) hosted in the [United Kingdom](31717.md). The introduction of the International Prize to complement the [Man Booker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Booker_Prize), as the [Booker Prize](4446.md) was then known, was announced in June 2004.[^cite_note-Debate-1] Sponsored by the [Man Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Group), from 2005 until 2015 the award was given every two years to a living author of any nationality for a body of work published in English or generally available in English translation.[^cite_note-Times-2] It rewarded one author's "continued creativity, development and overall contribution to fiction on the world stage",[^cite_note-Spark-3] and was a recognition of the writer's body of work rather than any one title.

Since 2016, the award has been given annually to a single novel or collection of short stories, translated into English and published in the United Kingdom or Ireland, with a £50,000 prize for the winning title, shared equally between author and translator.[^cite_note-shaffi-4][^cite_note-5] From 2027 onwards, the prize will be doubled to £100,000.

Crankstart, the [charitable foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charitable_foundation) of [Sir Michael Moritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Moritz) and his wife Harriet Heyman, began supporting The Booker Prizes on 1 June 2019. From this date, the prizes were known as The Booker Prize and The International Booker Prize. Of their support for The Booker Prize Foundation and the prizes, Moritz commented: "Neither of us can imagine a day where we don’t spend time reading a book. The Booker Prizes are ways of spreading the word about the insights, discoveries, pleasures and joy that spring from great fiction". Bukhman Philanthropies, the charitable foundation of [Dmitry Bukhman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Bukhman) and his wife Daria, began supporting the International Booker Prize in July 2026, and the prize was renamed to the Bukhman International Booker Prize.

## History

### Pre-2016

Whereas the Man Booker Prize was open only to writers from the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations), Ireland, and Zimbabwe, the International Prize was open to writers of any nationality whose work was available in English, including translations.[^cite_note-Atwood-6] The award was worth £60,000 and given every two years to a living author for their entire body of literature, in a similar way to the [Nobel Prize for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_for_Literature).[^cite_note-Spark-3] The Man Booker International Prize also allowed for a separate award for translating. If applicable, the winning author could direct the organization to present a prize sum of £15,000 to their translator.[^cite_note-Quit-7]

The 2005 inaugural winner of the international prize was Albanian writer [Ismail Kadare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Kadare). Praising its concerted judgement, the journalist Hephzibah Anderson noted that the Man Booker International Prize was "fast becoming the more significant award, appearing an ever more competent alternative to the Nobel".[^cite_note-8]

### 2016 onwards

In July 2015 it was announced that the [*Independent* Foreign Fiction Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Foreign_Fiction_Prize) would be disbanded and its prize money would be folded into the Man Booker International Prize.[^cite_note-shaffi-4] Starting with the 2016 award, the Man Booker International Prize became annual and changed its remit to perform the same role as the *Independent* prize: awarding books of fiction translated into English, in order to encourage the publishing and reading of quality works in translation and to highlight the work of translators.[^cite_note-orthofer-15] The £50,000 prize is split between author and translator. Each shortlisted author and translator receives £2,500.[^cite_note-16] Judges select a longlist of 12 or 13 books in March ("the Booker Dozen"), followed by a shortlist of six in April,[^cite_note-17] with the winner announced in May.[^cite_note-18]

In July 2026, it was announced that [Bukhman Philanthropies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Bukhman#Philanthropy) would sponsor the prize for the next 10 years. The prize was renamed the Bukhman International Booker Prize, and the winning prize was increased from £50,000 to £100,000. The shortlist prize remained the same. [^cite_note-19]

### 2016

The nominees for the seventh Man Booker International Prize were announced on 14 April 2016.[^cite_note-obs-52] The six nominees were chosen from a longlist of thirteen.[^cite_note-SCain-53][^cite_note-54] Han Kang became the first Korean author to win the prize and, under the new format for 2016, Smith became the first translator to share the prize. British journalist [Boyd Tonkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyd_Tonkin), who chaired the judging panel, said that the decision was unanimous. He also said of the book "in a style both lyrical and lacerating, it reveals the impact of this great refusal both on the heroine herself and on those around her. This compact, exquisite and disturbing book will linger long in the minds, and maybe the dreams, of its readers."[^cite_note-55]

### 2017

The longlist for the eighth Man Booker International Prize was announced on 14 March 2017, and the shortlist on 20 April 2017. The winner was announced on 14 June 2017.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] David Grossman became the first Israeli author to win the prize, sharing the £50,000 award with translator Jessica Cohen. Nick Barley, who is the director of the [Edinburgh International Book Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Book_Festival), described the book as "an ambitious high-wire act of a novel \[that] shines a spotlight on the effects of grief, without any hint of sentimentality. The central character is challenging and flawed, but completely compelling." The novel won over 126 other contenders.[^cite_note-58]

### 2024

The longlist was announced on 11 March 2024, the shortlist on 9 April 2024, and the winner on 21 May 2024, at a ceremony at [Tate Modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate_Modern) in London, sponsored by [Maison Valentino](15399668.md). The judging panel was chaired by Canadian writer and broadcaster [Eleanor Wachtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Wachtel), and consisted of Mojave American poet [Natalie Diaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Diaz), Sri Lankan British novelist [Romesh Gunesekera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romesh_Gunesekera), South African artist [William Kentridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kentridge), and American writer, editor and translator Aaron Robertson. On choosing the six shortlisted books, Eleanor Wachtel said, "Our shortlist, while implicitly optimistic, engages with current realities of racism and oppression, global violence and ecological disaster."[^cite_note-72] The winner was [Jenny Erpenbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Erpenbeck) for her novel *[Kairos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairos_\(novel\))*, translated from the German by [Michael Hofmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Hofmann).[^cite_note-Gdn2024-73] The judges' decision marked the first occasion the prize was won by either a German writer or a male translator.[^cite_note-Gdn2024-73]

### 2025

The longlist was announced on 25 February 2025[^cite_note-Guardian20250225-74][^cite_note-75] and the shortlist was published on 8 April 2025.[^cite_note-2025shortlist-76] The judging panel was chaired by English writer [Max Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Porter_\(writer\)) and also consisted of Nigerian poet, director and photographer [Caleb Femi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Femi), writer and publishing director of *[Wasafiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasafiri)* Sana Goyal, South Korean writer and translator [Anton Hur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Hur), and English singer-songwriter [Beth Orton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Orton). The winner, [Banu Mushtaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Mushtaq)'s *[Heart Lamp: Selected Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_Lamp:_Selected_Stories)*, was announced on 20 May 2025.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

All 13 writers on the longlist were first-time nominees. The works selected for 2025 included several other firsts: the prize's first nominated translation from [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) (*Heart Lamp*); the first nomination of a Romanian author ([Cărtărescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_C%C4%83rt%C4%83rescu)); and the first nomination for an Iraqi translator ([Antoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinan_Antoon)). Translator [Sophie Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Hughes) appeared on the longlist for a record fifth time and on the shortlist for a record third time.[^cite_note-Guardian20250225-74][^cite_note-2025shortlist-76] [Deepa Bhasthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepa_Bhasthi) was the first Indian translator to win the award.[^cite_note-80]

### 2026

The longlist was announced on 24 February 2026[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] and the shortlist on 31 March 2026.[^cite_note-83] The winner, Yáng Shuāng-zǐ's *[Taiwan Travelogue](80179283.md)*, was announced on 19 May 2026 at the [Tate Modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate_Modern) in London.[^cite_note-84] *Taiwan Travelogue* is the first book written in Mandarin and the first book by a Taiwanese author to win.[^cite_note-85] The judging panel was chaired by British writer [Natasha Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Brown_\(author\)) alongside Oxford professor and mathematician [Marcus du Sautoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_du_Sautoy), International Booker Prize-shortlisted translator [Sophie Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Hughes), Kenyan writer and editor [Troy Onyango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Onyango), and Indian novelist and columnist [Nilanjana Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilanjana_Roy).&#x20;

In 2026, *The Vegetarian* was voted the readers' favourite of all the Booker winners in the last ten years.[^cite_note-86]

### 2027

In July 2026, it was announced that American writer [Katie Kitamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Kitamura) would chair the judging panel for 2027; she will be joined by writer [Patrick McGuinness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_McGuinness), British-Ghanaian writer [Caleb Azumah Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Azumah_Nelson), Danish writer [Olga Ravn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Ravn) and American actress [Tessa Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessa_Thompson). The longlist will be announced on 16 March 2027, and the shortlist will be announced on 15 April 2027. The winning book will be announced in May 2027.[^cite_note-87]
