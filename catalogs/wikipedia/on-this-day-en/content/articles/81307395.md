On 9 October 2025, the Islamist political party [Tehreek-e-Labbaik Pakistan](55761171.md) (TLP) launched a protest march from [Lahore](125315.md) and [Faisalabad](401880.md) toward [Islamabad](51153.md) under the banner of the **Labbaik Ya Aqsa Million March**. The movement called for a rally outside the [US Embassy in Islamabad](32553327.md) in solidarity with the [Palestinians](23267.md) amid the ongoing [Gaza genocide](75668427.md). The planned march triggered a major security response: authorities imposed [Section 144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_144) in [Rawalpindi](343524.md) to restrict public gatherings, suspended 3G/4G mobile data services in [Islamabad](51153.md) and Rawalpindi, and placed [shipping containers](22849124.md) alongside a heavy police deployment to seal off the [Red Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Zone_\(Islamabad\)) around the capital. Clashes erupted in Lahore when Punjab Police raided the TLP headquarters on Multan Road to arrest party leader [Saad Rizvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saad_Rizvi). Police used [tear gas](1202646.md) and live fire to disperse demonstrators. According to reports, at least 11 TLP workers were killed and dozens more were injured during the live fire and shelling by the police.[^cite_note-6] The government maintained that the TLP had not obtained the required permission for the march, attributing the unrest to internal party disputes.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-auto-8][^cite_note-auto3-9][^cite_note-auto1-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] [Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf](1651925.md) member [Sheikh Waqas Akram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Waqas_Akram) has compared the incident to [Model Town Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_Town_Massacre) and has said the government could not carry out a transparent probe into the massacre, hence independent observers and human rights organisations must be included to ensure a fair investigation.[^cite_note-14]

In the aftermath, the Pakistani government banned the TLP under the Anti-Terrorism Act in late October 2025.[^cite_note-15]

## Background

The group has used marches and protests in the past, chanting slogans such as "Labbayk Ya Aqsa" (which roughly translates to "I am here for [Al-Aqsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa)") and "Pro-Palestinian." The "Labbaik Ya Aqsa Million March" continues this tradition. Sources reported that the TLP leadership used inflammatory speeches and rhetoric, which authorities deemed abusive or provocative. That leading the way in measures predicting widespread unrest, authorities imposed [Section 144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_144) (ban on assemblies), suspended mobile internet (3G/4G) services in Islamabad and Rawalpindi, placed shipping containers to block roads leading to the capital, and detained TLP activists.[^cite_note-auto3-9][^cite_note-auto2-16][^cite_note-auto1-10][^cite_note-auto-8]

## Timeline

### October 8–9

Preparations intensified ahead of the march. Authorities in [Rawalpindi](343524.md) imposed Section 144, prohibiting public gatherings, and detained around 117 key TLP activists. Containers were placed near the [Faizabad Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faizabad_Interchange) and other major routes, while security was tightened across the [twin cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad%E2%80%93Rawalpindi).[^cite_note-auto2-16]

### October 9

Mobile internet services (3G/4G) were suspended in Islamabad and Rawalpindi from midnight to 10 pm on October 9.[^cite_note-auto3-9] Clashes erupted in Lahore after a police raid on the TLP headquarters aimed at arresting its leader, [Saad Rizvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saad_Rizvi). TLP supporters responded with stone pelting and iron rods, prompting police to use tear gas. At least three policemen were injured.[^cite_note-17]

### October 10

The march was scheduled for October 10, 2025. Authorities had already closed major roads and sealed off the [Red Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Zone_\(Islamabad\)) around [Islamabad](51153.md).[^cite_note-auto1-10]

### October 11

TLP supporters regrouped near [Muridke](19493547.md) and announced their intent to continue the march toward Islamabad. Clashes were reported along the [GT Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GT_Road) corridor, leaving several injured. Police detained over 100 activists following the unrest.[^cite_note-18]

### October 12

The standoff escalated as protesters camped in Muridke. According to officials, dozens of police officers were injured, while TLP claimed multiple fatalities among its members. The Punjab government deployed additional police and Rangers, and roads toward Islamabad remained sealed.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### October 13

Violence escalated near [Lahore](125315.md) and [Muridke](19493547.md) as police attempted to disperse the advancing TLP march. According to officials, at least five people, including a police [SHO](41402072.md), were killed and dozens were injured when clashes broke out between security forces and protesters. Police used tear gas and aerial firing to contain the crowd, while TLP claimed multiple deaths among its members.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

### October 14

The Punjab government extended the enforcement of [Section 144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_144) in [Rawalpindi](343524.md) and [Islamabad](51153.md) for an additional 48 hours amid continued tensions. Internet and mobile data services remained suspended across the [twin cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad%E2%80%93Rawalpindi), and heavy police deployment continued along the [GT Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Road) to prevent further TLP advances. TLP leaders accused the authorities of carrying out an "unprovoked crackdown", while officials claimed the party had refused to negotiate peacefully.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Aftermath

Following the dispersal, the federal government banned Tehreek-e-Labbaik Pakistan (TLP) on 24 October 2025, declaring it a proscribed organization under the [Anti-Terrorism Act of 1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Terrorism_Act_1997), citing involvement in terrorism.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] The Punjab government moved to freeze TLP assets and properties, and transferred control of associated mosques and seminaries.[^cite_note-38]

TLP challenged the ban notification in court, arguing it was issued without due process.[^cite_note-39] TLP leader Saad Rizvi and others were placed on a no-fly list amid ongoing investigations.[^cite_note-40]

It was discovered in 2026 that a key party figure, Inayatul Haq Shah, along with his family members and other accomplices, had used 1,800 different accounts to finance the TLP. At least [PKR](588111.md) 13.4 million in cash was withdrawn from a madrassa's account on 13 and 14 October 2025.[^cite_note-41]
