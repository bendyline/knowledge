The **Cleveland Cavaliers**, often referred to as the **Cavs**, are an American professional [basketball](3921.md) team based in [Cleveland](5951.md). The Cavaliers compete in the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA) as a member of the [Central Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Division_\(NBA\)) of the [Eastern Conference](1305847.md). The team began play as an [expansion team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_team) in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970%E2%80%9371_NBA_season), along with the [Portland Trail Blazers](72897.md) and [Buffalo Braves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Braves). Home games were first held at [Cleveland Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Arena) from 1970 to 1974, followed by the [Richfield Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richfield_Coliseum) from 1974 to 1994. Since 1994, the Cavs have played home games at [Rocket Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Arena) in [downtown Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Cleveland), which is shared with the [Cleveland Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Monsters) of the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League). [Dan Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Gilbert) has owned the team since March 2005.

The Cavaliers opened their inaugural season by losing their first 15 games and struggled in their early years, placing no better than sixth in the Eastern Conference during their first five seasons. The team won their first Central Division title in 1976, which also marked the first winning season and playoff appearance in franchise history, where they advanced to the Eastern Conference Finals. The franchise was purchased by [Ted Stepien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Stepien) in 1980. Stepien's tenure as owner was marked by six coaching changes, questionable trades and [draft](835946.md) decisions, and poor attendance, leading to \$15 million in financial losses. The Cavs went 66–180 over the course of those three seasons and endured a 24-game losing streak spanning the 1981–82 and 1982–83 seasons.

[George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gund_III) and [Gordon Gund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Gund) purchased the franchise in 1983. During the latter half of the 1980s and through much of the 1990s, the Cavs were a regular playoff contender – led by players such as [Mark Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Price) and [Brad Daugherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Daugherty_\(basketball\)) – and advanced to the Eastern Conference Finals in 1992. After the team's playoff appearance in the 1997–98 season, however, the Cavs had six consecutive losing seasons with no playoff action. Cleveland was awarded with the top overall pick in the [2003 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NBA_draft), and they selected [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James). Behind James and [Zydrunas Ilgauskas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zydrunas_Ilgauskas), the Cavaliers again became a regular playoff contender by 2005. They made their first appearance in the [NBA Finals](890793.md) in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_Finals) after winning the first Eastern Conference championship in franchise history. After failing to return to the NBA Finals in the ensuing three seasons, James joined the [Miami Heat](72850.md) in 2010. As a result, the Cavaliers finished the 2010–11 season last in the conference, enduring a 26-game losing streak, the [third-longest in NBA history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Basketball_Association_longest_losing_streaks). Between 2010 and 2014, however, the team won the top pick in the [NBA draft lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_draft_lottery) three times – first in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NBA_draft) where they selected [Kyrie Irving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrie_Irving), and again in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NBA_draft) and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NBA_draft).

After four seasons with the Heat and having won back-to-back titles in the 2011–12 and 2012–13 seasons, James returned to the Cavs in 2014 and led the team to four straight NBA Finals appearances. In [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season), the Cavaliers won their first [NBA Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NBA_champions), marking [Cleveland's first major sports title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_sports_curse) since 1964. The [2016 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NBA_Finals) victory over the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Golden_State_Warriors_season) marked the first time in Finals history a team had come back to win the series after trailing 3–1. The Cavaliers have 25 playoff appearances and have won eight Central Division titles, five Eastern Conference titles and one NBA title.

## History

Main article: [History of the Cleveland Cavaliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Cleveland_Cavaliers)

The Cavaliers began play in the [1970–71 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970%E2%80%9371_NBA_season) as an expansion team.[^cite_note-10] They set losing records in each of their first five seasons before winning their first division title in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The 1976 team was led by [Austin Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Carr), [Bobby "Bingo" Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingo_Smith), [Jim Chones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Chones), [Dick Snyder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Snyder), [Nate Thurmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Thurmond) and head coach [Bill Fitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Fitch).[^cite_note-18] It was remembered for the "[Miracle at Richfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_at_Richfield)", in which the Cavaliers defeated the [Washington Bullets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Bullets), 4–3, in the Eastern Conference Semifinals.[^cite_note-\:0-19] They won Game 7, 87–85, on a shot by Snyder with four seconds to go.[^cite_note-\:0-19] The Cavaliers moved on to the Eastern Conference Finals for the first time,[^cite_note-\:0-19] but were without Chones after he broke his toe before the series opener.[^cite_note-20] As a result, the Cavaliers went on to lose, 4–2, to the [Boston Celtics](43376.md).[^cite_note-21] They made playoff appearances in the following two seasons before a six-year playoff drought.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-\:1-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]



Cavaliers forward and Akron native [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James), who was the first overall pick of the [2003 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NBA_draft). A perennial [NBA All-Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_All-Star) and a four-time [NBA MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_MVP) winner, he led the team to its first NBA Finals in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_Finals) and their first championship in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NBA_Finals).

The early 1980s were marked by [Ted Stepien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Stepien)'s ownership. During Stepien's tenure, the Cavaliers made a practice of trading future draft picks for marginal veteran players.[^cite_note-nytimes2-30] His most notable deal sent a 1982 first-round pick to the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) in exchange for [Don Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Ford) and the 22nd overall pick in 1980.[^cite_note-31] As a result of Stepien's dealings, the NBA introduced the "Stepien Rule", which prohibits teams from trading first-round draft picks in successive seasons.[^cite_note-nytimes2-30][^cite_note-thoughtco2-32] The Cavaliers went 66–180, dropped to the bottom of the league in attendance and lost \$15 million during Stepien's three years as the owner.[^cite_note-nytimes2-30] The Cavs went through six coaches during this span, including four during the 1981–82 season.[^cite_note-nytimes2-30] The team finished 15–67, and between March and November 1982, the team had a 24-game losing streak, which, at the time, was the [NBA's longest losing streak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Basketball_Association_longest_losing_streaks).[^cite_note-\:1-27][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] [George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gund_III) and [Gordon Gund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Gund) purchased the Cavaliers from Stepien in 1983.[^cite_note-thoughtco2-32][^cite_note-37]

The Cavaliers made the playoffs 10 times between 1985 and 1998.[^cite_note-\:22-38] The [1988–89](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season) season was the Cavaliers' best season to date, finishing the regular season at 57–25 with a team that included [Brad Daugherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Daugherty_\(basketball\)), [Mark Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Price), [Ron Harper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Harper) and [Larry Nance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Nance), and head coach [Lenny Wilkens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Wilkens).[^cite_note-\:22-38][^cite_note-39] They had their second 57-win season in [1991–92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season) and reached the Eastern Conference Finals that year.[^cite_note-\:22-38][^cite_note-40] However, between 1999 and 2005, the Cavaliers failed to make a playoff appearance.[^cite_note-\:22-38] The Cavaliers finished the [2002–03 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season) 17–65, tied for the worst record in the NBA.[^cite_note-41]



[Dan Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Gilbert), who has owned the Cavaliers since 2005, with the team winning the 2016 NBA Championship under his watch.

The Cavaliers had the No. 1 pick in the [2003 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NBA_draft), selecting future NBA MVP [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James), a native of nearby [Akron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron,_Ohio).[^cite_note-42] On January 3, 2005, the team was sold to businessman [Dan Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Gilbert) for \$375 million,[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] and the deal was finalized on March 1, 2005.[^cite_note-46] That year, the team also hired head coach [Mike Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Brown_\(basketball,_born_1970\)) and general manager [Danny Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Ferry).[^cite_note-47] The Cavaliers built a team around James and [Žydrūnas Ilgauskas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDydr%C5%ABnas_Ilgauskas) by adding players such as [Drew Gooden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Gooden_\(basketball\)), [Larry Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Hughes) and [Anderson Varajao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Varajao).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Under this new leadership, the Cavaliers made five straight playoffs from 2006 to 2010, advancing to at least the second round each time.[^cite_note-\:22-38] The [2006–07 Cavaliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season) advanced to the franchise's first [NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_Finals), but were swept by the [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] The [2008–09 Cavaliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season) won a franchise-record 66 games, including a franchise-best 39–2 record at home, but lost the Eastern Conference Finals to the [Orlando Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Magic).[^cite_note-\:22-38] Despite the addition of four-time NBA champion [Shaquille O'Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaquille_O'Neal),[^cite_note-52] the [2009–10 Cavaliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season) were unable to return to the Eastern Conference Finals after losing to the [Boston Celtics](43376.md) in the second round.[^cite_note-\:22-38]

With the Cavaliers out of the playoffs, the focus turned to James' impending free agency.[^cite_note-53] On July 8, 2010, James announced in a nationally televised one-hour special titled *[The Decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decision_\(TV_special\))* that he would be signing with the [Miami Heat](72850.md).[^cite_note-54] The repercussions of this announcement left many in the city of Cleveland infuriated and feeling betrayed.[^cite_note-55] After a 19–win season in [2010–11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season), the Cavaliers began a rebuild around [Kyrie Irving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrie_Irving), whom they selected first overall in the [2011 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NBA_draft).[^cite_note-\:22-38][^cite_note-56]

In 2014, James returned to the Cavaliers after four seasons in Miami. While the Heat had a 224–88 record during James' four-year tenure and won NBA titles in 2012 and 2013, the Cavaliers went 97–215 and missed the playoffs each season.[^cite_note-57] The Cavaliers made several moves to build a championship-contending team around James, most notably acquiring power forward [Kevin Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Love) from the [Minnesota Timberwolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Timberwolves), which created what many fans and media referred to as a "Big Three" with James, Love and Irving.[^cite_note-58] The LeBron-led Cavaliers made four consecutive Finals appearances from [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NBA_Finals) to [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NBA_Finals) – all against the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) – winning in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-\:22-38][^cite_note-\:3-59] The 2016 NBA Championship marked the Cavaliers' first title in franchise history, as they became the first team to come back from a 3–1 deficit to win the Finals.[^cite_note-\:3-59] It was also Cleveland's first championship in major professional sports since the [1964 Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Cleveland_Browns_season), signaling the end of the so-called [Cleveland sports curse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_sports_curse).[^cite_note-60]

The Cavaliers' roster went through many changes in the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season), most notably the trade of Irving to the [Boston Celtics](43376.md) in exchange for [Isaiah Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Thomas_\(basketball\)) and other assets.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] Thomas was later traded to the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) as part of a trade deadline overhaul that saw the Cavaliers add [Jordan Clarkson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Clarkson), [Larry Nance Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Nance_Jr.) (son of Cavs legend [Larry Nance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Nance)) and others.[^cite_note-63] The following offseason, James declined his player option to rejoin the team, instead signing with the Lakers.[^cite_note-64] In the following two seasons, the team recorded only 19 wins and failed to make the playoffs.[^cite_note-\:22-38]

In the first three seasons since James left the second time, the Cavaliers won a total of 60 games, missed the playoffs each season and had four different head coaches: [Tyronn Lue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyronn_Lue), [Larry Drew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Drew), [John Beilein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Beilein) and by this time, [J.B. Bickerstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.B._Bickerstaff), who was promoted to head coach for the 2020–21 season after he had been an assistant on Belien's staff the previous season.[^cite_note-\:22-38][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]



[Donovan Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donovan_Mitchell), who was traded to the Cavaliers on September 1, 2022 and has been an All-Star in all four seasons he has played for the Cavs.

In the [2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NBA_season), the Cavs finished 44–38 and eighth in the Eastern Conference, and qualified for the [NBA play-in tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_play-in_tournament), marking the first time the team played in the event. This also marks the team's first postseason basketball since 2017–18 and the first time a non-LeBron-led team qualified for postseason play since [1997–98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_NBA_season). The team, however, did not make it out of the play-in tournament, losing the game to the [Brooklyn Nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets) and then to the [Atlanta Hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Hawks), ending their season.

On March 26, 2023, the Cavaliers clinched a spot in the NBA playoffs, making this the first time since [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NBA_playoffs) that the team entered the playoffs, with a 108–91 win over the [Houston Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Rockets). This was also the first time since [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_NBA_playoffs) that the team entered the playoffs without LeBron James.[^cite_note-67] They wound up losing their first round playoff series to the [New York Knicks](72855.md) four games to one.[^cite_note-68]

On October 25, 2023, it was announced that [defensive end](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_end) [Myles Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myles_Garrett) of the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns) had purchased a minority ownership stake in the Cavaliers. Along with his ownership stake, Garrett would serve as an ambassador for the team.[^cite_note-69]

The Cavaliers qualified the 2024 NBA Playoffs as the 4th seed in the Eastern Conference, defeating the Orlando Magic 4–3 in the first round, but losing to the Boston Celtics 4–1 in the Conference semifinals.[^cite_note-70]



Current head coach [Kenny Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Atkinson)

On November 6th, 2024 the Cavaliers beat their all-time franchise "season start" win-streak, beating the New Orleans Pelicans 131-122 for a season record of 9-0.[^cite_note-71]

After a 128-114 win against the [Charlotte Hornets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets) on November 17, 2024, the Cavaliers achieved a 15-0 start, which is tied for the second-best start in franchise history, and the Cavaliers are the fourth team to achieve this milestone, after the 2015-2016 [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Golden_State_Warriors_season) team, the 1993-1994 [Houston Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_Houston_Rockets_season) team, and the 1948-1949 [Washington Capitols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948%E2%80%9349_Washington_Capitols_season) team.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] The 15-0 start is also the Cavaliers’ longest winning streak in franchise history.[^cite_note-74]

On November 19, 2024, the [Boston Celtics](43376.md) ended the Cavaliers 15–0 start and 15 game winning streak when they won 120–117, giving the Cavaliers their first loss of the season.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

## Logos and uniforms

### Original wine and mustard/metallic gold

When the Cleveland Cavaliers debuted in the NBA in 1970, the team's original jersey colors were [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_\(color\)) and gold. The first jerseys featured the feathered treatment of the letter "C" in "Cavaliers". In 1974, they changed into the classic block lettering and checkerboard pattern that was synonymous to the "Miracle of Richfield" team of 1976. In 1980, the gold shade was changed from yellowish to metallic, and the uniforms removed the checkerboard pattern and placed the stripes above "Cleveland" and below the uniform number, the only time the city name was featured in both home and away jerseys.

The original logo was that of a swashbuckling cavalier looking right with a sword pointing, surrounded by the team name and a basketball. A modernized swashbuckling cavalier logo was later used by the Cavaliers' [NBA Development League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Development_League) affiliate, the [Canton Charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_Charge). The gold checkerboard uniforms were used as throwbacks in the 2004–05 season to commemorate the 30th anniversary of the "Miracle of Richfield" team, while the gold "feathered C" uniforms were used again in the 2008–09 season as a buildup to the then-upcoming 40th season of the Cavaliers. The "Miracle of Richfield" gold uniforms were used again in the 2015–16 season on special "Hardwood Classic" nights to commemorate the Miracle of Richfield team's 40th-anniversary celebration.

### Blue and orange

In the 1983–84 season, the colors were changed to burnt orange, blue and white. The first Cavaliers uniform under the new scheme featured the Cavaliers logo (with a "V" in the shape of a hoop and circle above as basketball) in an arched pattern and the player name sewn onto the back shoulder as a patch, with orange being the primary color in both the away and home uniforms. However, in the 1987–88 season, orange was relegated as a secondary color, and blue was used instead as the primary for the away and home uniforms; minor changes in the 1989–90 season include the city name on the blue away uniforms. The drop shadows were also removed. The orange version of the uniform was used again in the 2006–07 and 2016–17 seasons, as part of the respective 20th and 30th anniversaries of the 1986–87 team.[^cite_note-78] The blue versions were worn in the 2009–10 season as part of the franchise's 40th anniversary and as a tribute to the 1988–89 team.

### Blue, black and orange

Coinciding with the move to Gund Arena in the 1994–95 season, the Cavaliers changed logos and uniforms, adding black in addition to the already existing blue, orange and white colors. The uniforms feature a blue splash in the abdomen area in front. From 1994 to 1997, the word "CAVS" on the home uniforms was orange with black lines, while the numbers are in black with white lines; "CLEVELAND" on the road uniforms was also orange with black lines, while the numbers are in white with a black line. From 1997 to 1999, the numbers and lettering were slightly tweaked: The word "CAVS" and the numbers on the home uniforms were black with orange lines, while the word "CLEVELAND" and the numbers on the road uniforms were white with orange lines. In the latter iteration, the blue splash was moved from the right leg to the left leg, surrounding "CLEVELAND" on the home uniforms and "CAVS" on the road uniforms, with a minor change in striping.

In the 1999–2000 season, the Cavaliers opted to go for a cleaner look, eliminating the splash and adding an orange and blue line that runs through the shorts. The home jerseys feature the team nickname and the uniform numbers are in blue with black lines, while the away jerseys featured the city name and the uniform numbers in white with blue lines; these jerseys were used until the 2002–03 season. The logo used in this period was of a basketball on its way down a net, surrounded by a black square and the word "CAVS" in blue with black line below.

The 1994–1996 black uniforms were revived for the 2019–20 season as part of the Cavaliers' 50th season, as well as the 25th anniversary of the move to Gund Arena (now Rocket Arena).[^cite_note-79]

### Wine, metallic gold and navy

The Cavaliers switched to a modified version of the team's classic wine-and-gold scheme in the 2003–04 season: metallic gold and a crimson shade of wine with navy blue added. The home uniform was white, with the word "Cavaliers" in wine lettering with gold trim on the front, the player's name in wine lettering with gold trim on the back, the player's numbers in navy blue, and wine-and-gold trim on the sides. The team's standard road uniform was wine-colored, with the word "Cleveland", the player's name and the player's numbers all in white lettering with gold trim, as well as white and gold trim on the sides. The team's alternate jersey was navy blue with the word "Cleveland", the player's name and the player's numbers all in white lettering with gold trim, as well as a wine, gold and navy blue checkerboard trim. The checkerboard trim was a tribute to the original Cavaliers uniforms from the 1970s. The navy alternates were first worn in the 2005–06 season and for a majority of its existence, the Cavaliers primarily wore them during road playoff games. The logo used was a gold sword piercing through the words "Cleveland Cavaliers" in white and navy trim, with a wine basketball surrounding it.

The navy blue alternate from 2005–2010 was brought back as its "Classic" uniform in the 2025–26 season, paying tribute to the franchise's first NBA Finals appearance in the 2006–07 season.

### Wine, mustard gold and navy

The Cavaliers debuted new uniforms before the start of the [2010–11 NBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_NBA_season) to coincide with the team returning to the original shades of wine and gold used from 1970 to 1983.[^cite_note-80] The home uniform is white with a wine-and-gold horizontal stripe trim on the collar, sleeves, waistband and pant legs, the word "CAVALIERS" (in block style lettering) in wine on the front of the jersey with wine lettering for the name and number, and white shoes and socks. The road uniform is wine-colored with the same stripe trim, "Cleveland" in gold on the front of the jersey, gold lettering on the name and number, and black shoes and socks. An alternate jersey – gold with "CAVS" in wine on the front of the jersey, wine lettering on the name and number, white socks and shoes, and the same stripe trim as the other uniforms[^cite_note-81] – was added for the 2012–13 season. All uniforms have the team motto – "All for one. One for all." – stitched on the inside of the collar and the secondary "Sword C" logo on the side of the pant legs. The logo used is the same piercing sword logo, updated to the classic wine-and-gold scheme.

For the 2014–15 season, a second alternate jersey – and fourth uniform overall – was added, which is navy blue (a callback to the 1987–1994 style) with "CAVS" and the player's number in wine with gold trim, the player's name on the back of the jersey in gold and the "Sword C" logo on the side of the pant legs.[^cite_note-82]

### Wine, mustard gold, navy and black



50th anniversary logo used during 2019–20 season

Two alternate jerseys were unveiled prior to the start of the 2015–16 season.[^cite_note-CavsAltUnis2015-83] The second wine uniform is similar to their regular road jersey, except that it features the arched mid-1980s Cavs logo and white numerals in gold trim. A black sleeved uniform features the wine "C" logo in front and was famously worn in the title-clinching Game 7 of the [2016 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] Their logos for the 2017–18 season newly included the color black to commemorate the victory.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

Beginning in the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NBA_season), all NBA teams switched to [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.)-designed white "Association" uniforms and "Icon" uniforms in the respective team's primary color. The Cavaliers' Icon uniforms were wine-colored with "Cleveland" across the front and the player's name in gold lettering with navy blue numerals both in the front and in the back. The white Association uniforms have "Cavs" across the front, with wine color letters and numbers both front and back. All teams have the choice of which uniform to wear for any home game. Also included on the Cavs' jerseys is a small [Goodyear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_Tire_and_Rubber_Company) "Wingfoot" logo, as part of a sponsorship deal with the team.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

Nike also provides a third, alternate uniform called the "Statement" uniform. The Cavaliers' Statement uniform is black with dark gray pinstripes, a wine-colored "C" trimmed in gold on the front, names in gold letters on the back, wine-colored/gold-trimmed numbers on the front and back, and gold-colored Nike and Goodyear logos on the front. In the same vein as the logo on their 2017–18 jerseys, the black jersey is a nod to the team's former black-sleeved jerseys that they wore when they won Game 7 of the 2016 Finals.[^cite_note-90]

### Wine, metallic gold and black



Cavaliers' "V-net" logo - a modified version of similar logos used in the 1980s and 1990s that the team began using in 2022.

For the [2022–23 NBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NBA_season), the Cavaliers unveiled an updated logo and color scheme, removing navy blue while reverting to the metallic gold first used from 1980 to 1983, and from 2003 to 2010. The "C-sword" alternate logo was retired, and an updated "V-net" logo, merging the styles of the 1983–1994 and 1994–2003 logos with the current typeface, was also unveiled.[^cite_note-CavsModernLogos-3][^cite_note-CavsBringBackGold-4]

With the new logo, the team also introduced three coinciding new uniforms.

The white Association uniform features a wine-colored "V-net" logo trimmed in gold across the front, wine names and numbers trimmed in gold, and a wine primary "Cavs C" logo (outlined in gold) on left leg of the shorts.

The wine Icon uniform features "Cleveland" in gold lettering on the chest, gold names and numbers, and the "Cavs C" logo in wine with a gold outline on the left leg of the shorts.

The black Statement uniform – a nod to the black uniforms worn during the team's 2016 NBA championship run – displays a wine "Cavs C" logo outlined in gold on the front of the jersey, gold names and numbers, and a gold "V-net" logo on the left leg of the shorts.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

### CavFanatic uniforms

From the 2008–09 to the 2011–12 season, the Cavaliers wore special "mash-up" uniforms – combining the style from one era with the color scheme of another – on select "CavFanatic Nights".

- 2008–09: The team wore the original "feathered C" uniforms, but with the 1994–2003 shade of blue combined with the classic wine-and-gold coloring.

- 2009–10: The team wore their 1987–1989 uniforms, but in the classic wine and gold from the "Miracle of Richfield" era.

- 2010–11: The team wore the 2005–10 checkerboard alternate uniforms, but in the 1994–2003 color scheme of blue, black and orange.

- 2011–12: The team wore navy uniforms with wine-and-gold lettering; these were similar to the 2014–17 navy alternates, but with a different jersey and shorts striping.

## Cleveland Clinic Courts

Cleveland Clinic Courts, the team's practice facility and team headquarters, is located in suburban [Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence,_Ohio). The 50,000-square-foot (4,600 m<sup>2</sup>) building opened in 2007 and includes two full-size basketball courts, a weight room, a team room, offices, medical facilities, and kitchen and dining facilities. Naming rights are held by the [Cleveland Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Clinic), which is the team's official healthcare partner. Prior to the opening of Cleveland Clinic Courts, the team used the practice court located on the club level of [Rocket Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Arena).[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] In honor of the Cavs winning the NBA Championship, the city of Independence renamed the section of Brecksville Road leading to the team's practice facility "Cavaliers Way" in November 2016.[^cite_note-108]

## Media

**Radio**

[WTAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTAM) (1100 AM/106.9 FM) and [WMMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WMMS) (100.7 FM) currently serve as the flagship stations for the [Cavaliers AudioVerse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavaliers_AudioVerse).[^cite_note-124] Tim Alcorn serves as the announcer, with WTAM morning co-host/sports director Mike Snyder hosting the pregame/halftime/postgame shows. Former [Ohio State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State) standout and NBA player [Brad Sellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Sellers) joins Snyder for the postgame show.[^cite_note-125]

[Spanish language](26825.md) broadcasts of home games air on [WJMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WJMO) 1300, with Rafael Hernandez Brito on the call.[^cite_note-126]

**TV**

Starting in the [2026–27 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season), Cavaliers games will air on the [DAZN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAZN) [streaming service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_service), with 15 games simulcast over-the-air on [Gray Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_Media) Cleveland sister stations [WOIO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WOIO) channel 19 ([CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS)), [WUAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WUAB) channel 43 ([My Network TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Network_TV)), and [WTCL-LD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTCL-LD)/[WOHZ-CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WOHZ-CD)/W28FG-D channel 22 ([RESN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Entertainment_Sports_Network)).[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] The broadcast team includes play-by-play announcer [John Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Michael_\(broadcaster\)), sideline reporter Serena Winters, and former Cavalier [Brad Daugherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Daugherty_\(basketball\)) as analyst. Any nationally televised games will not be streamed by DAZN due to network exclusivity rights.

Cayleigh Griffin and former Cavaliers guard [Austin Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Carr) host the pregame/halftime/postgame shows, with former Cavaliers guard [Iman Shumpert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iman_Shumpert) serving as a contributor.

## Mascots



Current mascots Sir C.C. (left) and Moondog (right)



Mid 1990s/early 2000s-era mascot Whammer

### Current

The Cavaliers have two official mascots: Moondog and Sir C.C. The former was inspired by [Cleveland](5951.md) radio DJ [Alan Freed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Freed), who popularized the phrase "rock and roll" and had called himself "[Moondog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Freed#WJW_Cleveland)". Moondog was an NBA All-Star selection in 2003 and 2004, and he made his first appearance on November 5, 2003. Sir C.C., a [swashbuckler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swashbuckler) character, debuted during a game on November 27, 2010.[^cite_note-129]

### Past

During the 1990s and early 2000s, the Cavs had a polar bear mascot named Whammer, who was introduced on November 9, 1995. He still makes occasional appearances throughout the season at Cavaliers games.
