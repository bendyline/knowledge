**Göppingen** (German pronunciation: [\[ˈɡœpɪŋən\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/ce/De-G%C3%B6ppingen.ogg/De-G%C3%B6ppingen.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/ce/De-G%C3%B6ppingen.ogg/De-G%C3%B6ppingen.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Swabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_language): *Geppenge* or [Swabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_language): *Gebbenga*) is a town in southern [Germany](11867.md), part of the [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) Region of [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md). It is the capital of the [district Göppingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goeppingen_\(district\)). Göppingen is home to the toy company [Märklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A4rklin) and hosts the headquarters of [TeamViewer AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeamViewer_AG).

## Geography



Districts of Göppingen

Göppingen is situated at the bottom of the [Hohenstaufen mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenstaufen_mountain), in the valley of the river [Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fils_\(river\)). The districts of Göppingen are Bartenbach, [Bezgenriet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezgenriet), Faurndau, Göppingen, [Hohenstaufen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenstaufen_\(G%C3%B6ppingen\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), Holzheim, Jebenhausen and Maitis.

## History

Tradition holds that the city was founded by an [Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni) leader called Geppo sometime in the 3rd or 4th century. A disastrous fire on August 25, 1782 destroyed most of the town, but it was immediately rebuilt. Industrialisation during the 19th century made the area into a centre of industry. The importance of such industry is still seen in the town in the present day in companies such as [Märklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A4rklin) and [Schuler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuler_Group).

### Jewish community

Göppingen and the nearby village of Jebenhausen were home to a thriving Jewish community from 1777 until the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust). In 1777, the Baron von Liebenstein issued a "Letter of Protection" granting Elias Gutmann and other Jews permission to settle in Jebenhausen. The community grew and peaked in the mid-1800s, with the population center shifting from Jebenhausen to Göppingen and a substantial emigration of Jewish families to America by the late 19th Century.[^cite_note-4] The Göppingen synagogue, constructed in 1881, was destroyed during the [Kristallnacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristallnacht) from 9–10 November 1938.[^cite_note-5] Detailed records of the communities were collected by Rabbi Dr. Aron Tänzer, who served the community from 1907-1937, and who was also a driving force behind the establishment of the secular city library in Göppingen.[^cite_note-6] A comprehensive Jewish community history, *Die Geschichte der Juden in Jebenhausen und Göppingen*, was first produced by Tänzer in 1927, on the 150th anniversary of the settlement of Jebenhausen, and re-released in 1988 by the city of Göppingen with updated material added by Karl-Heinz Ruess.[^cite_note-7] The Jewish Museum in Jebenhausen, opened in 1992, documents and preserves the history of the Jewish community of Jebenhausen and Göppingen.[^cite_note-8]

### Cooke Barracks

In 1930, a civilian air field was built north of Göppingen. This was acquired by the [Luftwaffe](17885.md) (German Air Force) in 1935 and expanded into Fliegerhorst Kaserne. From 1945 through 1949, [displaced persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displaced_person) and refugees were housed in the [kaserne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaserne). In 1949, it was renamed Cooke Barracks in honor of Charles H. Cooke, Jr. who had been posthumously awarded the Silver Star and Soldier's Medal for gallantry in action. In late 1950, the [VII Corps (US)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VII_Corps_\(United_States\)) had been reactivated in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) and U.S. Army units began to be stationed at Cooke Barracks. The barracks began to be expanded and was used as the [28th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) headquarters. The 28th Infantry was redesignated as the [9th Infantry Division (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) in 1954 and was replaced by the [8th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) in 1956. They were then replaced by the [4th Armored Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Armored_Division_\(United_States\)) in 1957, which was redesignated as the [1st Armored Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Armored_Division_\(United_States\)) in 1971 and moved to Hindenburg Kaserne in [Ansbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansbach) in 1972. The [1st Infantry Division (Forward)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) moved from [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg) in 1972 until they were deactivated in 1991. There was a school for military dependents (Kindergarten through eighth grade) within walking distance of the family housing. Teachers at this school were credentialed U.S. educators employed by DoDDs (Department of Defense Dependents Schools). There was also a local civilian employed to teach the German language, songs, and customs. (Frau Lore Bader) Cooke Barracks was returned to the German government in 1992. The barracks were again used to house refugees for some time, then returned to civilian use.[^cite_note-9]

## Sports

Local sports club [Frisch Auf Göppingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frisch_Auf_G%C3%B6ppingen) currently play in the German first [team handball](13730.md) men's top division [Handball-Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handball-Bundesliga).

## Media

Göppingen has its own daily newspaper called "[Neue Württembergische Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCdwest_Presse)".[^cite_note-10] The "[Stuttgarter Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgarter_Zeitung)" also has a local editorial office in Göppingen. In addition, Göppingen has its own local TV station, the "[Filstalwelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filstalwelle?action=edit\&redlink=1)". It can be received in the local cable network and via the web.[^cite_note-11] Göppingen also has "Radio Fips" which is a radio station operated by a non-profit association.
