The **Inter-Services Intelligence** (**ISI**)[^cite_note-1] is the [foreign intelligence agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_intelligence_agency) of Pakistan. It is responsible for [counterintelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterintelligence), [espionage](10501.md) and conducting [covert operations](613453.md) around the world. The main objective of the ISI is to covertly collect and analyse intelligence from overseas that is deemed relevant to Pakistan's national security and interests. The ISI reports to its agency executive which is the [Director-General of Inter-Services Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director-General_of_Inter-Services_Intelligence). It is primarily focused on providing foreign intelligence to the [Government of Pakistan](1897833.md) and the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md). It is part of the [Pakistani intelligence community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_intelligence_community).

The ISI primarily consists of both serving military and civilian [intelligence officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_officer) drawn on [secondment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondment) from the three intelligence services of the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md): the [MI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_\(Pakistan\)), [NI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Intelligence_\(Pakistan\)), and [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Intelligence_\(Pakistan\)) and the [Intelligence Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Bureau_\(Pakistan\)) (IB), [Federal Investigation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Investigation_Agency) (FIA), and the [Counter Terrorism Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_Terrorism_Department_\(Pakistan\)) (CTD).

Since 1971, it has been formally headed by a serving [three-star general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-star_rank) of the Pakistan Army, who is appointed by the [Prime Minister of Pakistan](461896.md) in consultation with the [Chief of Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)), who recommends three officers for the position. As of 30 September 2024, the ISI is headed by [Lt. Gen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_general_\(Pakistan\)). [Asim Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asim_Malik).[^cite_note-2] The Director-General reports directly to both the Prime Minister and the Chief of Army Staff.

Initially relatively unknown outside of Pakistan in the years following its inception, the agency gained global recognition and fame in the 1980s when it backed the [Afghan mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen) against the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) during the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) in the former [Democratic Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan). Over the course of the conflict, the ISI worked in close coordination with the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) of the [United States](3434750.md) and the [Secret Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Intelligence_Service) of the [United Kingdom](31717.md) to run [Operation Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cyclone), a program to train and fund the mujahideen in Afghanistan with support from [China](5405.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), and other [Muslim nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Following the dissolution of the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan in 1992 along with the Soviet Union, the [ISI provided strategic support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Services_Intelligence_activities_in_Afghanistan) and intelligence to the [Taliban](30635.md) against the [Northern Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alliance) during the [Afghan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1992%E2%80%931996\)) in the 1990s.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-Sky-7][^cite_note-8] The ISI has strong links with [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) groups, particularly in [Afghanistan](737.md) and [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-BBC-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-firstpost.com-13][^cite_note-rediff.com-14] The [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) (FBI), in their first ever open acknowledgement in 2011 in US Court, said that the Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI) sponsors and oversees the [insurgency in Kashmir](1915235.md) by arming separatist militant groups.[^cite_note-firstpost.com-13][^cite_note-rediff.com-14]

## History

The Inter-Services Intelligence was established in 1948 by officers of the Pakistan Army after the [First Kashmir War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Kashmir_War), the first of several [Indo-Pakistani wars and conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts). It was the brainchild of [Brigadier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier) [Syed Shahid Hamid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Shahid_Hamid) who became its first [Director-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director-General_of_Inter-Services_Intelligence).[^cite_note-15]

The Kashmir War had exposed weaknesses in intelligence gathering, sharing, and coordination between military branches and Pakistani [Intelligence Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Bureau_\(Pakistan\)) (IB) and [Military Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_of_Pakistan) (MI). The ISI was thus established to be operated by officers from the newly independent Pakistan's three main military services, and to specialize in the collection, analysis, and assessment of external military and non-military intelligence.[^cite_note-16]

Naval [Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander) [S. M. Ahsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._M._Ahsan), who served as Deputy Director [Naval Intelligence of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Intelligence_of_Pakistan) and helped formulate ISI procedure, undertook and managed the recruitment and expansion of the ISI. After the [1958 coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Pakistani_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), all national intelligence agencies were directly controlled by the president and [Chief Martial Law Administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Martial_Law_Administrator). The maintenance of national security, which was the principal function of these agencies, resulted in the consolidation of the Ayub regime. Any criticism of the regime was seen as a threat to national security.[^cite_note-17]

The ISI is headquartered in Pakistan's capital, [Islamabad](51153.md). The complex consists of various low-rise buildings separated by lawns and fountains. The entrance to the complex is next to a private hospital. [Declan Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declan_Walsh_\(journalist\)) from [The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian) stated that the entrance is "suitably discreet: no sign, just a plainclothes officer packing a pistol who directs visitors through a chicane of barriers, soldiers, and sniffer dogs".[^cite_note-TNI-18] Walsh noted that the complex "resembles a well-funded private university" and that the buildings are "neatly tended," the lawns are "smooth," and the fountains are "tinkling." He described the central building, which houses the director general's office on the top floor, as "a modern structure with a round, echoing lobby".[^cite_note-Walshreally-19]

On 5 July 1977 through [Operation Fair Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Fair_Play), the ISI began collecting intelligence on the [Pakistan Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Pakistan) and the [Pakistan Peoples Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Peoples_Party).[^cite_note-ISI_History_2-20] The [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) in the 1980s saw the enhancement of the ISI's [covert operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covert_operations). A special Afghanistan section known as the SS Directorate was created under the command of Brigadier Mohammed Yousaf to oversee day-to-day operations in Afghanistan. The ISI's [black ops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_ops) unit is the [Covert Action Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covert_Action_Division). Officers from the [Covert Action Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covert_Action_Division) received training in the United States, and "many covert action experts of the CIA were attached to the ISI to guide it in its operations against Soviet troops by using the Afghan Mujahideen".[^cite_note-21]

Many analysts (mainly Indian and American) believe that the ISI provides support to militant groups, though others think these allegations remain unsubstantiated.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-CSIS-23]

The ISI has often been accused of playing a role in major [terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_attacks) across India including [militancy in Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_in_Jammu_and_Kashmir), the [July 2006 Mumbai Train Bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_July_2006_Mumbai_train_bombings),[^cite_note-24] the [2001 Indian Parliament attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Indian_Parliament_attack),[^cite_note-25] the [2006 Varanasi bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Varanasi_bombings), the [August 2007 Hyderabad bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25_August_2007_Hyderabad_bombings),[^cite_note-26] and the [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

The ISI has been accused of supporting [Taliban](30635.md) forces[^cite_note-Pakistan's_shadowy_secret_service-29] and recruiting and training [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen)[^cite_note-30] to fight in Afghanistan[^cite_note-31] and Kashmir. Based on communication interceptions, US intelligence agencies concluded Pakistan's ISI was behind the attack on the Indian embassy in Kabul on 7 July 2008, a charge that the governments of India and Afghanistan had laid previously.[^cite_note-32] It is believed to be aiding these organizations in eradicating perceived enemies or those opposed to their cause.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Satellite imagery from the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md)[^cite_note-35] suggested the existence of several training camps in Pakistan, with at least one militant admitting to being trained in the country. As part of the ongoing [Kashmir conflict](3030955.md), Pakistan is alleged to be backing separatist militias.[^cite_note-36] Many [nonpartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonpartisanism) sources believe that officials within Pakistan's military and the ISI sympathies with and aid Islamist militant and insurgent groups, saying that the ISI has provided covert, but well-documented support to separatist groups active in Kashmir, including [Jaish-e-Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaish-e-Mohammed).[^cite_note-37]

General [Javed Nasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javed_Nasir) confessed to assisting the [besieged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) Bosnian Muslims.[^cite_note-38] The [National Intelligence Coordination Committee (NICC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Intelligence_Coordination_Committee_\(Pakistan\)) of Pakistan is headed by the [Director-General of Inter-Services Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director-General_of_Inter-Services_Intelligence). The overarching intelligence coordination body was given assent by the [Prime Minister of Pakistan](461896.md) in November 2020. It held its inaugural session on 24 June 2021, marking the date the committee became functional.[^cite_note-39]

## Structure

A director-general, who is traditionally a serving lieutenant general in the Pakistan Army, heads the ISI.[^cite_note-govt_notify-40] Three deputy director generals, who are serving two-star military officers, report directly to the director general with each deputy heading three wings respectively:[^cite_note-41]

- Internal Wing – responsible for domestic intelligence, domestic [counter-intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-intelligence), counter-espionage, and counter-terrorism.

- External Wing – responsible for external intelligence, external counter-intelligence, and espionage.

- Foreign Relations Wing – responsible for diplomatic intelligence and foreign relations intelligence.

Military officers of the three branches of the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md) and paramilitary forces such as [ANF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Narcotics_Force), [ASF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_Security_Force), [Pakistan Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Rangers), [Frontier Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Corps), [Gilgit-Baltistan Scouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit-Baltistan_Scouts) and [Maritime Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Maritime_Security_Agency) as well as civilian officers from the [Federal Investigation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Investigation_Agency) (FIA), [Federal Board of Revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Board_of_Revenue_\(Pakistan\)) (FBR), [Pakistan Customs](26447288.md), [Police](9371583.md), [Judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_judiciary) and [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Pakistan\)) make up ISI's general staff. They are recruited on deputations for three to four years and enhance the ISI's professional competence. According to some experts, the ISI is the largest intelligence agency in the world in terms of total staff. While the total number has never been made public, experts estimate around 10,000 officers and staff, which does not include informants or assets.[^cite_note-FAS-42]

### Departments

- [Covert Action Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covert_Action_Division): Its roles are similar to the [Special Activities Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Activities_Division) of the CIA and a handful of officers are trained by that division. The division has been active since the 1960s.

  [^cite_note-Lancer_Publishers_&_Distributors-43]

- Joint Intelligence X: Coordinates the other departments in the ISI.

  [^cite_note-FAS-42]

  &#x20;Intelligence and information gathered from the other departments are sent to JIX which prepares and processes the information and from there prepares reports which are presented.

- Joint Intelligence Bureau: Responsible for gathering anti-state intelligence and fake drugs, fake currency, and TTP.

  [^cite_note-FAS-42]

- Joint Counterintelligence Bureau: Focused on foreign intelligence agencies.

- Joint Intelligence North: Exclusively responsible for the [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) region and [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md).

  [^cite_note-FAS-42]

- Joint Intelligence Miscellaneous: Responsible for espionage, including offensive intelligence operations, in other countries.

  [^cite_note-FAS-42]

- Joint Signal Intelligence Bureau: Operates intelligence collections along the India-Pakistan border.

  [^cite_note-FAS-42]

  &#x20;The JSIB is the [ELINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELINT), [COMINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COMINT), and [SIGINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGINT) directorate that is charged with diverting attacks from foreign non-communications [electromagnetic radiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation) emanating from sources other than nuclear detonations or radioactive sources.

  [^cite_note-FAS-42]

- Joint Intelligence Technical: Deals with development of science and technology to advance Pakistani intelligence gathering. The directorate is charged with taking steps against [electronic warfare](82272.md) attacks in Pakistan.

  [^cite_note-FAS-42]

  &#x20;Without any exception, officers from this division are reported to be engineer officers and military scientists who deal with the military [promotion of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_funding_of_science) and technology.

  [^cite_note-FAS-42]

  &#x20;There are also separate explosives and [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_warfare) and [biological warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_warfare) sections.

  [^cite_note-FAS-42]

- SS Directorate: Comprises officers from the Special Services Group. It monitors the activities of terrorist groups that operate against Pakistan. It is comparable to the [National Clandestine Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Clandestine_Service) (NCS) of the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA), and is responsible for special operations against terrorists.

- Political Internal Division: Monitors the financial funding of the [right-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Pakistan) political science sphere against [left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_Pakistan) political science circles. This department was involved in providing funds to anti-left wing forces during the general elections of [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Pakistani_presidential_election), [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Pakistani_general_election), [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_Restoration_of_Democracy), [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Pakistani_general_election), and [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Pakistani_general_election).

  [^cite_note-44]

  &#x20;The department has been inactive since March 2012 with the new director general taking operational charge of the ISI.

## Director Generals

Main article: [Director-General of Inter-Services Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director-General_of_Inter-Services_Intelligence)

The Director General of the ISI is among the most powerful posts in Pakistan.[^cite_note-govt_notify-40] For example, according to Mohammad Sohail, shares at the [Pakistan Stock Exchange](49066780.md) went down in October 2021 over concerns regarding the appointment of the ISI chief. The benchmark KSE-100 index fell 1.51%.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] According to retired air marshal Shahzad Chaudhry, three to four names are provided by the [Chief of Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)), and the prime minister selects the director general from that list,[^cite_note-spymaster-49] and the appointed serves for two to three years.[^cite_note-spymaster-49] Before 2021, the appointment process of the Director-General followed no formal protocol other than verbal discussion between the Prime Minister and the head of the army.[^cite_note-50]

[Walter Cawthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Cawthorn) was the first head of the ISI. His successor, [Syed Shahid Hamid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Shahid_Hamid) is said to have supported [Ayub Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan)'s rise to power.[^cite_note-Pakistan_ISI-51] After his retirement, he helped President [Muhammed Zia-ul-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Zia-ul-Haq).[^cite_note-52]

## Recruitment and training

Both civilians and members of the armed forces can join the ISI. For civilians, recruitment is advertised and handled by both the [Federal Public Services Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Public_Services_Commission) (FPSC); they are considered employees of the Ministry of Defence. The FPSC conducts examinations that test the candidate's knowledge of current affairs, English, and various analytical abilities. Based on the results, the FPSC shortlists the candidates and sends the list to the ISI who conduct the initial background checks. Selected candidates are then invited for an interview which is conducted by a joint committee comprising both ISI and FPSC officials, and are then sent to the Defence Services Intelligence Academy (DSIA) for six months of training. The candidates are transferred to different sections for open source information where they serve for five years. After five years of basic service, officers are entrusted with sensitive jobs and considered part of the core team.[^cite_note-57]

## Reception

Critics of the ISI say that it has become a [state within a state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_within_a_state) and not accountable enough. Some analysts say that it is because intelligence agencies around the world remain secretive. Critics argue the institution should be more accountable to the president or the prime minister.[^cite_note-BBC_News-132] The Pakistani government disbanded the ISI's political wing in 2008 after its discovery.[^cite_note-133]

### U.S. government

During the [Cold War](325329.md), the ISI and the CIA worked together to send [spy planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_aircraft) over the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-134] The two organisations also worked closely during the Soviet–Afghan War supporting groups such as [Gulbuddin Hekmatyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulbuddin_Hekmatyar)'s [Hezb-i Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezb-i_Islami) and [Jalaluddin Haqqani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalaluddin_Haqqani), leader of the [Haqqani network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haqqani_network).[^cite_note-Neamatollah_Nojumi-135]

Some report the ISI and CIA stepped up cooperation in the aftermath of the [9/11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11_attacks) to kill and capture senior [Al Qaeda](1921.md) leaders such as Sheikh Younis Al Mauritan and [Khalid Shaikh Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_Shaikh_Mohammed), the planner of the 9/11 attacks who was residing in Pakistan. Pakistan claims that around 100 top level al-Qaeda leaders/operators were killed or arrested by the ISI.[^cite_note-136] Secretary of State [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) said Pakistan is paying a "big price for supporting the U.S. war against terror groups. \[...] as they have made these adjustments in their own assessment of their national interests, they're paying a big price for it."[^cite_note-137]

Other senior international officials maintain that senior Al Qaeda leaders such as bin Laden have been hidden by the ISI in major settled areas of Pakistan with the full knowledge of the Pakistani military leadership.[^cite_note-Guardian-138] A December 2011 analysis report by the [Jamestown Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown_Foundation) came to the conclusion that

> &#x20;In spite of denials by the Pakistani military, evidence is emerging that elements within the Pakistani military harbored Osama bin Laden with the knowledge of former army chief General [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf) and possibly former Chief of Army Staff (COAS) General [Ashfaq Pervez Kayani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashfaq_Pervez_Kayani). Former Pakistani General [Ziauddin Butt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziauddin_Butt) (a.k.a. General Ziauddin Khawaja) revealed at a conference on Pakistani-U.S. relations in October 2011 that according to his knowledge the then former Director-General of Intelligence Bureau of Pakistan (2004–2008), Brigadier Ijaz Shah (retd.), had kept Osama bin Laden in an Intelligence Bureau safe house in Abbottabad.[^cite_note-Jamestown_Foundation-139]

Pakistani general [Ziauddin Butt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziauddin_Butt) said bin Laden had been hidden in [Abbottabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbottabad) by the ISI "with the full knowledge" of General [Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf)[^cite_note-Jamestown_Foundation-139] but later denied making any such statement, saying his words were altered by the media, he said: "It is the hobby of the Western media to distort the facts for their own purposes."[^cite_note-Ashraf_Javed-140] U.S. military officials have increasingly said they do not notify Pakistani officials before conducting operations against the Afghan Taliban or Al Qaeda, because they fear Pakistani officials may tip them off.[^cite_note-Kissinger_on_foxnews-141] International officials have accused the ISI of continuing to support and even lead the Taliban during the [2001-2021 War in Afghanistan](19666611.md). As [Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md), [Mike Mullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mullen) stated:

> &#x20;The fact remains that the Quetta Shura \[Taliban] and the Haqqani Network operate from Pakistan with impunity ... Extremist organizations serving as proxies of the government of Pakistan are attacking Afghan troops and civilians as well as US soldiers. ... For example, we believe the Haqqani Network—which has long enjoyed the support and protection of the Pakistani government ... is, in many ways, a strategic arm of Pakistan's Inter-Services Intelligence Agency.[^cite_note-longwarjournal1-142]

The [Associated Press](18935732.md) reported that "the president said Mullen's statement 'expressed frustration' over the insurgent safe havens in Pakistan. But Obama said 'the intelligence is not as clear as we might like in terms of what exactly that relationship is.' Obama added that whether Pakistan's ties with the Haqqani network are active or passive, Pakistan has to deal with it."[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]

The [Guantanamo Bay files leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_files_leak) showed that the US authorities unofficially consider the ISI a terrorist organization that was equally as dangerous as Al Qaeda and the Taliban, and many allegations of it supporting terrorist activities have been made.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-Burke-146]

In 2017, General [Joseph Dunford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Dunford), [chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md), accused the ISI of having ties to terror groups.[^cite_note-147] In a Senate hearing, Dunford told members of the U.S. Senate: "It is clear to me that the ISI has connections with terrorist groups."[^cite_note-148]

### Indian government

India has accused the ISI of plotting the [1993 Bombay bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Bombay_bombings).[^cite_note-ISI-149] According to the [United States diplomatic cables leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak), the ISI had previously shared intelligence information with [Israel](9282173.md) regarding possible terrorist attacks against Jewish and Israeli sites in India in late 2008.[^cite_note-150] The ISI is also accused of supporting pro independence militias in [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md)[^cite_note-151] while Pakistan denies all such claims,[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] or says it gives them moral support only.[^cite_note-Martin-155]

## Controversies

The ISI has been accused of using [designated terrorist groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_designated_terrorist_organizations) and [militants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militant_\(word\)) to conduct [proxy wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxy_wars) against its neighbors.[^cite_note-Bajoria-156][^cite_note-Laruelle-157][^cite_note-Hussain-158] According to Grant Holt and David H. Gray, "The agency specializes in utilizing terrorist organizations as proxies for Pakistani foreign policy, covert action abroad, and controlling domestic politics."[^cite_note-Holt-159] [James Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_J.F._Forest) says, "There has been increasing proof from counter-terrorism organizations that militants and the Taliban continue to receive assistance from the ISI, as well as the establishment of camps to train terrorists on Pakistani territory."[^cite_note-Forest-160] All external operations are carried out under the supervision of the ISI's S Wing.[^cite_note-McGrath-161] [Joint Intelligence/North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Intelligence/North) is responsible for conducting operations in Jammu and Kashmir and Afghanistan.[^cite_note-Camp-162] The Joint Signal Intelligence Bureau (JSIB) provides support with communications to groups in Jammu and Kashmir.[^cite_note-Camp-162] According to [Daniel Benjamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Benjamin) and [Steven Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Simon), both former members of the [United States National Security Council](44026.md), the ISI acted as a "kind of terrorist conveyor belt" radicalizing young men in the Madrassas in Pakistan and delivering them to training camps affiliated with or run by Al-Qaeda and from there moving them into Jammu and Kashmir to launch attacks.[^cite_note-Caldwell-163]

### Support for militants

Since the 1990s, the ISI began communicating with the jihadists who emerged from the conflict against the Soviet Union in Afghanistan, and by 2000 most militant groups operating in Kashmir were based in Pakistan or were pro-Pakistan. These groups are used to conduct a [low-intensity conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-intensity_conflict) against India.[^cite_note-Zahab-164] According to [Stephen P. Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_P._Cohen) and John Wilson, the ISI's aid to and creation of designated terrorist groups and religious extremist groups is well-documented.[^cite_note-Cohen-165][^cite_note-Wilson1-166] The ISI has been accused of having close ties to [Lashkar-e-Taiba](649718.md), who carried out the attacks in Mumbai in 2008.[^cite_note-Green-167] The organisation has also given aid to [Hizbul Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hizbul_Mujahideen).[^cite_note-Sisk-168] Terrorism expert Gus Martin said, "The ISI has a long history of supporting designated terrorist groups and pro-Independence groups operating in Punjab and Jammu and Kashmir which fight against Indian interests."[^cite_note-Martin-155][^cite_note-Palmer-169] The ISI also helped with the founding of the group [Jaish-e-Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaish-e-Mohammed).[^cite_note-Wilson2-170]

#### Hizbul Mujahideen

The group Hizbul Mujahideen was created as the Kashmiri branch of [Jamaat-i-Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-i-Islami).[^cite_note-Juergensmeyer-171] It was reported that JI founded Hizbul Mujahideen at the request of the ISI to counter the [Jammu and Kashmir Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Liberation_Front) who are advocates for the independence of [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir).[^cite_note-Hizb-ul-Mujahideen-172] The failure of 1987 elections in Kashmir, and afterwards the arrest of Muhammad Yusuf, a.k.a. [Syed Salahuddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Salahuddin), led to the events that created armed struggle in the valley.

#### Al-Badr

There have been three incarnations of Al-Badr. According to Tomsen, the ISI, in conjunction with [Jamaat-e-Islami Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaat-e-Islami_Pakistan), formed the first Al-Badr, who resisted the Indian-trained influx of [Mukti Bahini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukti_Bahini) in Bangladesh in the 1970s.[^cite_note-Tomsen,240-173][^cite_note-Schmid_(Editor)-174]

#### Al-Qaeda and bin Laden

Main articles: [Civil war in Afghanistan (1989–1992)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_war_in_Afghanistan_\(1989%E2%80%931992\)) and [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War)

The ISI supported Al-Qaeda during the war along with the CIA against the Soviet government, through the Taliban, and it is believed by some that there is still contact between Al-Qaeda and the ISI.[^cite_note-Aubrey-175] An assessment by British Intelligence in 2000 into Al-Qaeda training camps in Afghanistan showed the ISI were playing an active role in some of them.[^cite_note-Atkins-176] In 2002, it was alleged that when the Egyptian investigators tracked down Al-Qaeda member Ahmed Said Khadr in Pakistan, the Egyptian authorities informed Pakistani authorities of his location. However, the Afghan Taliban at night came in a car and took Khadir along with them to Afghanistan. The next day, Pakistani authorities claimed they were unable to capture Khadir.[^cite_note-177] The leak in 2012 of [e-mails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Stratfor_email_leak) from [Stratfor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratfor) claimed papers captured during all the compounds during [the raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Osama_bin_Laden) in Abbottabad on Osama bin Laden's compound showed up to 12 ISI officials knew where he was and that Bin Laden had been in regular contact with the ISI.[^cite_note-McElroy-178]

Despite the [allegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegations_of_support_system_in_Pakistan_for_Osama_bin_Laden), [Steve Coll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Coll) stated that as of 2019 there is no direct evidence showing Pakistani knowledge of bin Laden's presence in Abbottabad, even by a rogue or compartmented faction within the government, other than the circumstantial fact of bin Laden's compound being located near (albeit not directly visible from) the [Pakistan Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Military_Academy). Documents captured from the Abbottabad compound generally show that bin Laden was wary of contact with ISI and Pakistani police, especially in light of Pakistan's role in the arrest of [Khalid Sheikh Mohammed](190884.md); it has also been suggested that the \$25 million U.S. reward for information leading to bin Laden would have been enticing to Pakistani officers given their reputation for corruption. The compound itself, although unusually tall, was less conspicuous than sometimes envisaged by Americans, given the common local habit of walling off homes for protection against violence or to ensure the privacy of female family members.[^cite_note-179]

Al-Qaeda has repeatedly labelled ISI their enemy, and claimed the Pakistani military and intelligence are their main targets in Pakistan.[^cite_note-180] In 2019, [Ayman al-Zawahari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahari) labelled ISI and the Pakistani military a "puppet" of the United States in a video message.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

#### Harkat-ul-Mujahideen

The [Harkat-ul-Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harkat-ul-Mujahideen) was founded in the 1980s by the ISI to fight against Indian interests.[^cite_note-O._Riedel-183]

#### Jammu and Kashmir

in 1984, under the orders of Zia-ul-Haq, the ISI prepared for a rebellion, which was to be set in motion in 1991.[^cite_note-Haqqani-184]

#### Haqqani network

The ISI allegedly have links to the Haqqani network[^cite_note-Cordesman-185] and contributed to their funding.[^cite_note-Shanty-186] It is widely believed the [suicide attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Indian_embassy_bombing_in_Kabul) on the Indian embassy in Kabul in 2008 was planned with the help of the ISI.[^cite_note-Williams-187] A report in 2008 from the US [director of National Intelligence](456082.md) stated that the ISI provides intelligence and funding to help with attacks against the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force), the Afghan government, and Indian targets.[^cite_note-Aid-188] On 5 November 2014, Lieutenant-General Joseph Anderson, a senior commander for US and NATO forces in Afghanistan, said that the Haqqani network is now "fractured" like the Taliban in a Pentagon-hosted video briefing from Afghanistan. "They are fractured. They are fractured like the Taliban is. That's based pretty much on Pakistan's operations in North Waziristan this entire summer-fall," he said, acknowledging the effectiveness of Pakistan's [military offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Zarb-e-Azb) in North Waziristan. "That has very much disrupted their efforts in Afghanistan and has caused them to be less effective in terms of their ability to pull off an attack in Kabul," Anderson added.[^cite_note-189]

### Insubordination controversies

On 6 October 2016, the daily newspaper *[Dawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_\(newspaper\))* published a report about a government meeting allegedly arranged by Sharif. The article detailed a presentation by Foreign Secretary [Aizaz Chaudhry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aizaz_Ahmad_Chaudhry) about international pressure to crack down on Pakistan's extremist segments such as Masood Azhar, the Jaish-i-Mohmmad, Hafiz Saeed, the Lashkar-e-Taiba, and the Haqqani network. According to Ghazi Salahuddin of *[The News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_News_International),* controversy ensued after the October meeting and the *Dawn* report, which lingered until May 2016.[^cite_note-isolation-190][^cite_note-dangerous-191] During the October 2016 meeting, Punjab chief minister [Shehbaz Sharif](6502561.md) allegedly revealed that, whenever action had been taken against certain extremist groups by civilian authorities, the security agency had worked secretly to free the arrested parties.[^cite_note-isolation-190][^cite_note-dangerous-191] According to Salahuddin Ghazi, information minister [Pervaiz Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervaiz_Rashid) lost his portfolio over the *Dawn* news leak, and a government notification was released about the civilian government's decision after the meeting. On 29 April 2017, the director general released a tweet that said: "Notification on Dawn Leak is incomplete and not in line with recommendations by the Inquiry Board. Notification is rejected." Ghazi stated that a meeting was eventually held between the prime minister and the chief of army staff, and a press conference was held to announce the decision to withdraw the tweet.[^cite_note-dangerous-191]

#### 2021 disagreement over appointment of ISI Chief

Pakistan's mainstream media reported on the October 2021 constitutional rift between civil and armed wings over the appointment of the director general post only after ministers spoke on the matter.[^cite_note-Govt_stalemate-192][^cite_note-ISI_Chief-193] On 6 October 2021, the Pakistan military's media affairs wing announced the replacement of [Faiz Hameed](61059085.md) with [Nadeem Anjum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadeem_Anjum).[^cite_note-194] After two days, it became apparent on social media that the federal government of Pakistan had yet to issue any formal notification for the appointment of the new director general.[^cite_note-Govt_stalemate-192] Rumors became more substantiated when Hameed attended the National Security Committee meeting instead of the expected new director general.[^cite_note-Govt_stalemate-192][^cite_note-ISI_Chief-193]

On 13 October 2021, Information Minister [Fawad Chaudhry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fawad_Chaudhry) informed media that the process of appointing a new director general was in progress, and that the selection is Prime Minister [Imran Khan](413184.md)'s prerogative. He also noted that the army chief and the prime minister agreed on following correct procedures of appointment according to the Constitution.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196][^cite_note-govt_notify-40]
