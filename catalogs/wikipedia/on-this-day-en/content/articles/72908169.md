Guinea and Sierra Leone

The **[Guinea](12176.md)–[Sierra Leone](27308.md) relations** are foreign relations between two neighbouring [West African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African) nations. After gaining independence (Guinea in 1958[^cite_note-DW-1] and Sierra Leone in 1961[^cite_note-Murtala_Mohammed_Kamara-2]) initial relations between the two countries in the [Cold War](325329.md) context turned to be tense due to conservative strategy of Sierra Leone (advocating strong links with the former [Metropole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropole)) and much more [Pan-African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Africanism) and pro-[socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist) leanings of Guinea.[^cite_note-Sesay-3]

In 1960 Sierra Leone invited [President of Guinea](392253.md) [Ahmed Sékou Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_S%C3%A9kou_Tour%C3%A9) to visit [Freetown](56661.md) (his first official state visit since 1958 independence) where he together with [Chief Minister of Sierra Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Sierra_Leone) [Milton Margai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Margai) described their countries as "sister states" and expressed their commitment for further cooperation with Milton Margai visiting Guinea next year on his return trip.[^cite_note-Sesay-3] In 1961 Guinea was among the countries that officially sponsored Sierra Leone's candidacy for membership in the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-Sesay-3] Initial tense relations nevertheless improved more significantly only after [Albert Margai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Margai) got into power in Sierra Leone in 1964 leading to 1967 Mutual Defence Pact.[^cite_note-Sesay-3] Two countries signed the Framework Agreement on Cooperation on October 10, 1964 to which specific agreements on trade, payments, telecommunications, transport, cultural exchange and legal convention were attached.[^cite_note-HronikaBelgrade-4] [Siaka Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siaka_Stevens) took power in Sierra Leone in 1967 with Guinean troops intervening per request to help him stay in power in what was the first intervention by one sub-Saharan African state in another in 1971.[^cite_note-Sesay-3] At the time of intervention Sierra Leone left the [Commonwealth realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm) and became a republic.[^cite_note-Sesay-3]

In 2001, during [Sierra Leone's civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Civil_War), the Republic of Guinea sent troops into the city of [Yenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenga)[^cite_note-Gberie-5] to help the [army of Sierra Leone](27316.md) suppress the rebel [Revolutionary United Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_United_Front). After the rebels were quashed, the Guinean soldiers remained in Yenga. Prior to the civil war Yenga was administered by the [Kailahun District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailahun_District) of Sierra Leone.[^cite_note-Konneh-6] In 2002 Sierra Leone and Guinea signed an agreement than Yenga would be returned to Sierra Leone, as soon as Guinea's border could be secured.[^cite_note-Gberie-5] The dispute was officially "resolved" in 2019 when President [Ahmad Tejan Kabbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Tejan_Kabbah) announced that "Conakry has now affirmed that Yenga is a part of Sierra Leone".[^cite_note-7] However, in early 2021, President [Julius Maada Bio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Maada_Bio) reported to the 58th [Ecowas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) summit that "The issue remains unresolved and our Guinean counterparts have continued to encroach on Sierra Leone's land and sea borders".[^cite_note-8]
