The **HQ-9** ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 红旗-9; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 紅旗-9; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Hóng Qí-9*; lit. 'Red Flag-9'; [NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name): **CH-SA-9**)[^cite_note-IISS_2025-7] is a long-range [semi-active radar homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-active_radar_homing) (SARH) [surface-to-air missile](182664.md) (SAM) developed by the [People's Republic of China](5405.md).[^cite_note-aspj_mccabe-3][^cite_note-hopkins_scs_dahm_p6-4] The naval variant is the **HHQ-9** ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 海红旗-9; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 海紅旗-9; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Hǎi Hóng Qí-9*; lit. 'Sea Red Flag-9').[^cite_note-aspj_mccabe-3] The HQ-9 and its export variants are developed by [China Aerospace Science and Industry Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Aerospace_Science_and_Industry_Corporation) (CASIC).[^cite_note-janes_producer-2]

## Description



HQ-9 Surface-to-air missiles



PLAAF HQ-9B launcher vehicle

The HQ-9 development program started in the 1970s, with full-scale development beginning in the late 1980s.[^cite_note-红旗9衍生自S-300？专家：引进前早已研发-8]

According to Western analysts, the HQ-9 is "equivalent"[^cite_note-rusi_iads_bronk-9] to or a derivative of the Russian [S-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-300_missile_system), which China started importing in 1993.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-aspj_mccabe-3][^cite_note-hopkins_scs_dahm_p6-4] Justin Bronk of the [Royal United Services Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_United_Services_Institute) describes the missile as a "hybrid design based on the Russian SA-20 but with radar, seeker head and [C2](7092305.md) elements heavily influenced by American and Israeli technology."[^cite_note-rusi_iads_bronk-9]

The missile uses [track-via-missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track-via-missile) (TVM) guidance combining inertial guidance, mid-course uplink, and terminal active radar.[^cite_note-11] The TVM used on earlier missiles may have been developed from a [United States](3434750.md) [MIM-104 Patriot](52024.md) missile purchased from [Israel](9282173.md) or [Germany](11867.md).[^cite_note-janes_woody_fisher-6]

According to a 2001 article from *Defence International*, the HQ-9 is 6.8 m. long with a mass of nearly two tons. The diameters of the first and second stages are 700 mm and 560 mm, respectively. The warhead mass is 180 kg, and the maximum speed is [Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_number) 4.2. The HQ-9 may use [fire-control radars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_radar) from other Chinese SAM systems.[^cite_note-DIIC-12]

## Foreign interest

### Turkey

The HQ-9 was a contender in Turkey's T-LORAMIDS program, and it was reportedly selected as the winner in September 2013.[^cite_note-23] The United States responded by blocking funds to integrate the Chinese system into NATO defenses.[^cite_note-24] However, through 2013 there was no confirmation that the deal had been finalized.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-DefenseUpdate-27] In February 2015, the [Grand National Assembly of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey) was informed by the Ministry of National Defence that the evaluation of bids was complete and that the chosen system would be used by Turkey without integration with NATO; the system was not explicitly named. However, other Turkish officials reported that no winner had been selected.[^cite_note-reuters_2015-02-19-28] Later in the month, Turkish officials revealed that negotiations were ongoing with multiple bidders; the Chinese bid had not yet satisfied requirements concerning technology transfer.[^cite_note-reuters_2015-02-26-29] In March 2015, a [China Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Daily) article reported that it was "well-known that the Chinese FD-2000 system, a HQ-9 model for export, was chosen for the contract with Turkey in 2013" based on comments made by a CPMIEC representative at the 2015 [Langkawi International Maritime and Aerospace Exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_International_Maritime_and_Aerospace_Exhibition); the article was misleadingly called "Missile sale to Turkey confirmed."[^cite_note-chinadaily_2015-03-19-30] In November 2015, Turkey confirmed it would not purchase the HQ-9, opting for an indigenously developed system instead.[^cite_note-reuters_2015-11-18-31]

### Iran

In late 2025, after the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War) in June, a member of the [Islamic Consultative Assembly](383276.md) claimed that Iran had received the HQ-9.[^cite_note-32] In July, the [Chinese embassy to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_China,_Tel_Aviv) denied that China was supplying Iran with surface-to-air missiles.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-bbc_iran-34] Reports of the HQ-9 in Iranian service in 2025 may have been [disinformation](142690.md).[^cite_note-bbc_iran-34] In March 2026, multiple analysts and experts interviewed by the outlet ‘’Domino Theory’’ reportedly stated that there was no evidence of any deployment or delivery of the HQ-9 from China to Iran, despite earlier, primarily sensationalist reports claiming that not only was the HQ-9 in Iranian service, but also that it had completely failed and was neutralised immediately in early hours of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md). The report further noted that there were “no pictures of the HQ-9B in Iranian service, no satellite images that suggest it has been deployed, and nothing from the U.S. or Israeli governments to say their armed forces have encountered it in combat”.[^cite_note-35]

## Operating history

### China

China has deployed HQ-9s near or in disputed territory. Missiles were deployed in July 2015 to [Hotan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotan) in [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang), close to [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) across the [Line of Actual Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Actual_Control),[^cite_note-kashmir2k15-36] and in February 2016 to [Woody Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Island_\(South_China_Sea\)) in the [disputed](32005536.md) [South China Sea](74209.md).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

The naval variant, the HHQ-9, was deployed by the PLAN by 2009. It was first carried by the [Type 052C destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_052C_destroyer).[^cite_note-oni_2009-39]

### Pakistan

The [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) operates the HQ-9/P variant.[^cite_note-janes_pak_hq9p-22] Negotiations for the purchase of the HQ-9 and HQ-16 by Pakistan began in early 2015.[^cite_note-paki-40] The system officially entered service on October 14, 2021.[^cite_note-janes_pak_hq9p-22] The HQ-9's first combat use was during the [2025 India–Pakistan conflict](79896622.md).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-clary_2025-05-28-42]

### Egypt

It was reported that Egypt had acquired HQ-9B systems in mid-2025 by the Middle East Monitor,[^cite_note-\:0-43] and in December 2025, it was reported that the Egyptians had deployed the system as part of an A2/AD system in the Sinai near Israel in a major move to boost aerial defenses not done since 1979.[^cite_note-44]

### Serbia

In 2026, it was reported that Serbia was procuring the FD-2000B (HQ-9B) instead of the [S-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-400_missile_system) in order to complement its existing inventory of FK-3 ([HQ-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HQ-22)E) batteries.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

### Algeria

Algeria has ordered at least 4 FQ-2000 (HQ-9AE) batteries for its [Territorial Air Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Air_Defence_Forces) in order to complement its existing S-300 and S-400 air defence batteries.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

### Morocco

Moroco initially showed interest in the HQ-9 in 2012 and finalized an agreement for the purchase of 4 batteries of the HQ-BE (FD-2000B) variant.[^cite_note-50] Moroccan forces underwent training for the system in early 2021, with deliveries of the system beginning in late 2021.[^cite_note-\:1-51][^cite_note-\:2-52][^cite_note-\:3-53] The deliveries came just after the U.S had approved of [MIM-104 Patriot](52024.md) sales to Morocco.[^cite_note-\:3-53] The first FD-2000B (HQ-9BE) batteries received by Morocco were deployed to a military base near the city of [Sidi Yahya El Gharb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidi_Yahya_El_Gharb).[^cite_note-\:3-53][^cite_note-\:2-52]
