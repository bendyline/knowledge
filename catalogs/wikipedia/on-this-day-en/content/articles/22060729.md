**Vancouver Whitecaps Football Club** is a Canadian professional [soccer](10568.md) club based in [Vancouver](32706.md). The Whitecaps compete in [Major League Soccer](71802.md) (MLS) as a member of the [Western Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Conference_\(MLS\)). The club was founded on March 18, 2009, and began play in 2011 as the 17th team to enter Major League Soccer. They are a [phoenix club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_club_\(association_football\)) and the third team to use the ["Whitecaps" name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC), which originated in 1974 as a member of the [North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League) and was revived in 2001 for a [second-division team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps_\(1986%E2%80%932010\)) that last played in the 2010 [USSF Division 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSF_Division_2_Professional_League). The club has been owned and managed by the same group since they were a second-division franchise.

In the 2012 season, the team became the first Canadian team to qualify for the [MLS Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Cup_playoffs). The Whitecaps have won five [Canadian Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Championship), and have advanced to both the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup) and [MLS Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Cup_playoffs) finals in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC_season), losing to [Cruz Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_Azul) and [Inter Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Miami) respectively. Vancouver competes against longtime Pacific Northwest rivals [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Sounders_FC) and [Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers) in the [Cascadia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascadia_Cup), a fan-created trophy awarded based on MLS regular season results.

In 2025, the Whitecaps attracted international attention for the signing of German superstar and [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) winner [Thomas Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_M%C3%BCller) on a free transfer. Other notable Whitecaps players include former American international [Jay DeMerit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_DeMerit), the club's first player and captain; [Camilo Sanvezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilo_Sanvezzo), the 2013 [MLS Golden Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Golden_Boot) winner; and current [Canadian men's national team](723688.md) captain [Alphonso Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonso_Davies), a [homegrown player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homegrown_Player_Rule_\(Major_League_Soccer\)).

## History

Main article: [History of Vancouver Whitecaps FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC)

An ownership group in Vancouver were granted the seventeenth Major League Soccer franchise on March 18, 2009, by MLS commissioner [Don Garber](1878607.md).[^cite_note-7] While no name was provided at the Vancouver announcement, over a year later the club confirmed it would keep the Whitecaps name, which had been first used in 1974.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The [original Whitecaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps_\(1974%E2%80%931984\)) played from 1974 to 1984 in the [North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League) and won one league championship, [Soccer Bowl '79](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_Bowl_'79).[^cite_note-12] After the league folded, a new team named the [Vancouver 86ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps_\(1986%E2%80%932010\)) was founded in 1986 and played in various minor leagues. It adopted the Whitecaps name in 2001.[^cite_note-13]

The team sold the first 5,000 season ticket deposits 48 hours after they became available to the public. Remaining season tickets were made available to season ticket holders for the USSF 2 Whitecaps before becoming available to non-season ticket holders.[^cite_note-14] In preparation for its first MLS season, the Whitecaps brought in executive talent from around the world. On November 24, 2009, [Paul Barber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Barber_\(soccer_administrator\)), former [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) executive, was announced to join the club as CEO. Others joining him included former [D.C. United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.C._United) head coach [Tom Soehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Soehn) as Director of Operations and Dutch national Richard Grootscholten as the Technical Director and head coach of the residency program.

As the head coach of the [USL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Soccer_Leagues) and later [USSF Division 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSF_Division_2_Professional_League) [Vancouver Whitecaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps_\(1986%E2%80%932010\)), former [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_national_football_team) international [Teitur Thordarson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teitur_Thordarson) was confirmed as head coach on September 2, 2010, for the inaugural MLS season.[^cite_note-15] He was subsequently relieved of his duties on May 30, 2011, after the Whitecaps won just one of their first twelve matches. [Tom Soehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Soehn), the Whitecaps director of soccer operations, replaced Thordarson on an interim basis.[^cite_note-ttfired-16]

### First years

The Whitecaps began playing in the [2011 MLS season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Major_League_Soccer_season) with their first match on March 19, 2011, against rival Canadians [Toronto FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_FC), which they won 4–2. The first goal in the Whitecaps' MLS era was scored by [Eric Hassli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Hassli).[^cite_note-17] After their winning start the Whitecaps struggled, and failed to secure another victory in their next 11 MLS games, drawing six and losing five. In the aftermath of their 1–1 draw with the [New York Red Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Red_Bulls) on May 30 head coach Teitur Thordarson was fired.[^cite_note-18] [Tom Soehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Soehn) took over coaching duties for the remainder of the 2011 season, while [Martin Rennie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Rennie_\(football_manager\)) was announced as the new permanent head coach on August 9, taking over officially on November 2.[^cite_note-19] The team managed to attract 15,500 season ticket holders in its first MLS season and 13,000 for the second.[^cite_note-20]

On March 3, 2012, the Whitecaps won their first minor, pre-season cup at the [2012 Walt Disney World Pro Soccer Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Walt_Disney_World_Pro_Soccer_Classic) defeating Toronto FC 1–0 thanks to a goal by [Camilo Sanvezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilo_Sanvezzo). The Whitecaps finished the regular season with 11 wins, 3 losses, and 10 ties positioning the club, fifth in the Western Conference and 11th on the league overall table. On October 21, 2012, the Whitecaps became the first Canadian team to earn a spot in the MLS playoffs.[^cite_note-21] Vancouver were eliminated in the knockout round.

In the 2013 season, Vancouver finished in seventh in the Western Conference, 13th in the league table with 13 wins, 12 losses, and 9 ties in the regular season. They were not able to qualify for the post season, in the playoffs as they had accomplished in the season prior. Two days after the end of the 2013 MLS regular season, Rennie's contract was not renewed sparking a search for the next head coach.[^cite_note-22] In their off-season, the Whitecaps were in the midst of controversy with one of their then players, Camilo, who had played for the team since their inaugural campaign, after the Brazilian went on to join Liga MX club [Querétaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quer%C3%A9taro_F.C.). The Mexican club believed that he was no longer under contract, while the Whitecaps reported that he was still on a contract with Vancouver.[^cite_note-Mexican_media-23] The scandal was resolved with the Liga MX club paying a transfer fee from Vancouver to acquire the Brazilian forward.[^cite_note-24]

In October 2014, the Whitecaps qualified for the 2015–16 [CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_League) for the first time as a result of becoming the highest ranked Canadian team in the 2014 MLS season and due to a reformatting of the [Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Championship) in the following season. A week later they qualified for the MLS playoffs for the second time, an achievement unmatched by any Canadian team.[^cite_note-25]

### First continental seasons

In 2015, the Whitecaps would go on to have their best-ever regular season, finishing the season with 53 points. They also went on to win the [Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Championship) for the [first time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Canadian_Championship), defeating Montreal 2–0 in the second leg to win the final 4–2 on aggregate. In the [2015 MLS Cup Playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_MLS_Cup_Playoffs) and [2014–15 CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_CONCACAF_Champions_League), however, they did not achieve the same success, getting eliminated in the group stage of the CONCACAF Champions League and the conference semi-final of the playoffs.

In 2016, the Whitecaps took a step back, finishing the regular season with 39 points. In the [2016 Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Canadian_Championship), they reached the final, but lost 2–2 on away goals to Toronto FC. They did go on to win the [Cascadia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascadia_Cup) on the final day of the regular season, beating the [Portland Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers) 4–1.

During the year of 2017, there were major improvement for the Whitecaps, as they made the [CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_League) semi-finals for the [first time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_CONCACAF_Champions_League), but were eliminated by [Tigres UANL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigres_UANL). They finished the regular season with 52 points, making the playoffs for the fourth time in their history. However, despite recording their first-ever playoff win against the [San Jose Earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Earthquakes) in the knockout round, they were eliminated by [Seattle Sounders FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Sounders_FC) in the conference semi-final.

In 2018, the Whitecaps would record the fourth-worst defensive record of the season, conceding 67 goals. As a result of poor form in the regular season, coach [Carl Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Robinson) was relieved of his duties on September 25, 2018. He was replaced by interim [Craig Dalrymple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Dalrymple) for the remainder of the regular season. The Whitecaps finished the season with 47 points and would miss the playoffs for the second time in three seasons.

2019 saw a new-look squad for the Whitecaps, as key players like [Alphonso Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonso_Davies), [Kendall Waston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_Waston), [Kei Kamara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_Kamara), and [Cristian Techera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Techera) had all departed following the 2018 season. The team would also appoint a new manager prior to the season, naming [Marc Dos Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Dos_Santos) to take the club forward. However, they would once again go on to miss the playoffs, finishing the season last place in the Western Conference. They were also eliminated by [Cavalry FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry_FC) in the [2019 Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Canadian_Championship), becoming the first MLS team to get eliminated by a [Canadian Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Premier_League) side in Canadian Championship history.

### 2020–2024

2020 was the club's 10th season in Major League Soccer. However, on March 12, 2020, after only two games played, Major League Soccer suspended their season as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic. The Whitecaps' season resumed on July 15, 2020, when they played the [San Jose Earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Earthquakes) in their first match of the [MLS is Back Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_is_Back_Tournament). Despite losses to the San Jose Earthquakes and Seattle Sounders FC, a 2–0 win against the [Chicago Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Fire_FC) was enough for the team to qualify to the round of 16. However, the Whitecaps would ultimately lose to [Sporting Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporting_Kansas_City) 3–1 on penalties after a scoreless 120 minutes. From August 18 to September 16, 2020, the Whitecaps would play six matches against fellow Canadian MLS teams [Toronto FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_FC) and [Montreal Impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CF_Montreal), that would serve as both regular season matches as well as qualifiers for the [2020 Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Canadian_Championship). The Whitecaps would not qualify for the Canadian Championship, finishing at the bottom of the qualification table with only 6 points from 6 games. From September 19, 2020, until the end of the season, the Whitecaps would play the rest of their home matches at [Providence Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Park) in [Portland, Oregon](23503.md). They would finish the season with 27 points from 23 matches played, missing the playoffs for the third season in a row.

2021 saw much greater success for the Whitecaps. The season did not start off well initially, with head coach Marc Dos Santos being relieved of his duties on August 27, 2021, as a result of his team sitting below the playoff line after 20 games, as well as once again getting eliminated by [Pacific FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_FC) in the [2021 Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_Championship). However, under interim head coach [Vanni Sartini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanni_Sartini), the team would go unbeaten in 12 of their last 14 games to finish the season, making the playoffs for the first time in four years. In the playoffs, the Whitecaps were eliminated in the first round by Sporting Kansas City, losing by a scoreline of 3–1. On November 30, 2021, Sartini was named as the club's new head coach.

In 2022, the Whitecaps would go on to have mixed success. They missed the playoffs for the fourth time in five years, finishing the season with 43 points. However, they won the [2022 Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Canadian_Championship), beating Toronto FC 5–3 on penalties after a 1–1 draw following regulation and extra time. It was the first time that the Whitecaps won the [Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Championship) since their first victory in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Canadian_Championship). The Canadian Championship victory also meant that the Whitecaps would qualify for the [2023 CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Champions_League).

In the [2023 CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_CONCACAF_Champions_League), the Whitecaps defeated [Real C.D. España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_C.D._Espa%C3%B1a) to advance to the quarterfinals, but were ultimately eliminated by [Los Angeles FC](44255607.md). In the [regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Major_League_Soccer_season), the team would finish with 48 points, qualifying for the playoffs. They were once again eliminated by Los Angeles FC, in a two-game sweep. In the [2023 Leagues Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Leagues_Cup), their inaugural appearance in the competition, the Whitecaps advanced to the Round of 32, but were eliminated by Tigres UANL. For the second consecutive season, the Whitecaps won the Canadian Championship for the [third time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Canadian_Championship), beating [CF Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CF_Montr%C3%A9al) 2–1 in the final. As a result, they qualified for the [2024 CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup).

The [2024 CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup) saw the Whitecaps face Tigres UANL, a team that the Whitecaps previously lost to 4–1 on aggregate in the [2016–17 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_CONCACAF_Champions_League). The Whitecaps would once again get eliminated by the [Liga MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX) side, by the same 4–1 aggregate scoreline. In the [regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Major_League_Soccer_season), the team finished with 47 points, qualifying for the [wild-card round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_MLS_Cup_playoffs#Wild_card_round) of the playoffs where they defeated the [Portland Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers) in their wild-card match 5–0. In a Round 1 rematch with Los Angeles FC, the Whitecaps were again eliminated, this time in three games. In the [2024 Leagues Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Leagues_Cup), the Whitecaps advanced to the Round of 32 before being eliminated by [Pumas UNAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Universidad_Nacional). The Whitecaps won their third consecutive Canadian Championship, winning the [2024 Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Canadian_Championship) by defeating Toronto FC 4–2 on penalties after a scoreless draw, qualifying them for the [2025 CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup).

### 2025 season: Three finals

The Whitecaps began the [2025 CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup) with a 3–2 aggregate win over Costa Rican side [Saprissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saprissa). In the Round of 16, they met [CF Monterrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CF_Monterrey) led by Sergio Ramos and advanced on away goals (3–3 agg.). They also defeated their quarterfinals opponent [UNAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNAM) by virtue of away goals (3–3 agg.), after a late stoppage-time equalizer by [Tristan Blackmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_Blackmon). Facing the MLS Supporters Shield holders [Inter Miami CF](41853326.md), a star-studded side led by [Lionel Messi](2150841.md), The Whitecaps upset Miami by winning 5–1 on aggregate, advancing to their first-ever [Champions Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup_final). The Whitecaps traveled to Mexico City and lost 5–0 to [Cruz Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_Azul) in the match.

The [2025 Canadian Championship final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Canadian_Championship_final) was held on October 1, 2025, at BC Place in Vancouver, where Vancouver Whitecaps FC defeated [Vancouver FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_FC) 4–2 to claim their fourth consecutive Voyageurs Cup, the longest championship streak in the tournament's modern era. Midfielder [Ali Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Ahmed_\(soccer\)) was named tournament MVP after scoring twice in the final, while [Thomas Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_M%C3%BCller) added a goal and an assist, marking the 300th goal of his career and the 35th trophy of his career, making him the most decorated German footballer of all time. Team captain [Ryan Gauld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gauld), returning from a seven-month injury absence, sealed the victory with the Whitecaps' fourth goal. Despite goals from [Thierno Bah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierno_Bah_\(soccer,_born_2004\)) and [Nicolás Mezquida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Mezquida) for Vancouver FC, the Whitecaps dominated possession and outshot their Canadian Premier League opponents 20–4.[^cite_note-26]

The club had their most successful regular season to date in the MLS-era. On August 6, 2025, the Whitecaps gained international attention due to the signing of German superstar and [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) winner [Thomas Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_M%C3%BCller) in free agency after a storied 17-season club career at [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayern_Munich), being a key centrepiece towards the team's multiple trophies.[^cite_note-27] Boosted by the addition of Müller, the Whitecaps went on a ten-game unbeaten streak in all competitions, ending with a 1–2 loss to [FC Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dallas) at home in the final regular season match of the season. The Whitecaps finished the [2025 regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Major_League_Soccer_season) with a MLS-era record 63 points, maintaining a top-3 position in the Western Conference during the entire season. After sweeping FC Dallas in Round 1 of the [2025 MLS Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_MLS_Cup_playoffs#Round_One), the Whitecaps hosted a memorable conference semifinals match versus Los Angeles FC, who eliminated Vancouver from the playoffs the previous two years. With an MLS-era record 53,957 fans in attendance, the Whitecaps defeated LAFC in penalties despite Vancouver playing two men short for the final 11 minutes of stoppage time following a red card to Tristan Blackmon and an injury to [Belal Halbouni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belal_Halbouni). The Whitecaps won their first Western Conference title, defeating expansion club [San Diego FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_FC) in the conference finals to advance to their first [MLS Cup](483246.md), where they lost to Inter Miami 1–3.

## Colours and badge

On June 8, 2010, it was officially announced the club would continue using the "Whitecaps" name, but with a redesigned logo.[^cite_note-28] The name alludes to the geographic features surrounding the city: [snow-capped mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Shore_Mountains) to the north and the Pacific Ocean's [white-capped waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_wave) to the west.

The official club colours include navy blue ("deep sea"), white, and light blue ("Whitecaps blue").[^cite_note-29] The "deep sea" blue represents the maritime landscape of the Vancouver area and the "Whitecaps blue" indicates the reflection of the [North Shore Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Shore_Mountains) in the Pacific Ocean. The lighter shade of blue also alludes to the primary colour of the original Whitecaps, winners of [Soccer Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_Bowl) 1979. The silver outline pays homage to the team's championship victories since 1974.[^cite_note-30]

On June 10, 2010, the Whitecaps [strip package](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_\(association_football\)) was unveiled with [Bell Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Canada) serving as the inaugural jersey sponsor.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The home shirt is white with horizontal, navy blue pinstripes; the stripes broaden slightly from bottom to top. The secondary shirt is deep blue with an embossed, interlocking diamond pattern which is also deep blue and is reflective in the light.

On June 14, 2012, the Whitecaps unveiled a [third kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_jersey). The third kit is predominantly "[arbutus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbutus_menziesii) brown", with sky blue accents, which reflects the unique land full of deep roots and the high-reaching arms of the temperate rainforests of British Columbia.[^cite_note-33]

In 2019 they used a redesigned version of their 1979 kit as a 40th anniversary tribute to the team winning the [1979 NASL Soccer Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_Bowl_'79).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

In 2023, the club unveiled the Bloodlines Jersey, highlighting "\[the] powerful thread that connects us all". This highlighted a partnership with Telus to support Canadian Blood Services, including active community figures and blood donors such as Joban Bal among others involved in Canadian blood donation efforts.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

In 2024, they released The 50 Jersey, on the club's 50th anniversary. This honoured the club's status as the longest running professional soccer club in Canada and the United States, creating a modern twist on the original crest during the inaugural season in 1974.[^cite_note-38]

In 2026 the Whitecaps unveiled a third jersey as part of the [MLS](71802.md) Archive collection. As a celebration of the 40th anniversary of the [Vancouver 86ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_86ers) the Archive jersey uses the colours of the 86ers, and a chest mark and jock tag with 86ers inspired designs.[^cite_note-39]

## Stadium



White sheets are used to artificially reduce the capacity of BC Place for most Whitecaps FC matches.



Marquee matches use both the upper and lower bowls.



Exterior view of BC Place, May 2026

The Whitecaps plays its home matches since 2011 at [BC Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place) in [Vancouver](32706.md), which it shares with the [BC Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Lions) of the [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League).[^cite_note-BCPlace1-40][^cite_note-41] The 54,500-seat [multi-purpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_stadium) domed stadium opened in 1983 and was designed for both [Canadian football](6963.md) and soccer. It underwent a major renovation between 2009 and 2011 to replace the roof with the world's largest cable-supported [retractable roof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retractable_roof);[^cite_note-renovation-42] the renovation also included the installation of white sails (known as the "secondary roof") to close off the upper bowl and reduce capacity to around 22,120 for most Whitecaps matches.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] BC Place's surface is [polytan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytan) [artificial turf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_turf), which is certified by [FIFA](11049.md) with a 2-star rating.[^cite_note-renovation-42][^cite_note-45]



The National Soccer Development Centre on the grounds of the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia)

The club played most of its [inaugural season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC_season) at [Empire Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Field), a temporary stadium built at the former site of [Empire Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Stadium_\(Vancouver\)) to house the Whitecaps and the BC Lions while BC Place was being renovated.[^cite_note-Empire-46] Empire Field was a 27,500-seat multi-purpose stadium that featured FIFA 1-star rated [FieldTurf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FieldTurf).[^cite_note-Empire-46] The team played its final match at Empire Field on September 24, 2011, a 3–1 loss to [Seattle Sounders FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Sounders_FC).[^cite_note-47] The following week, the Whitecaps played their first match at BC Place, a 1–0 loss to [Portland Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers) on October 2, 2011.[^cite_note-48]

Club ownership initially hoped to build [Whitecaps Waterfront Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitecaps_Waterfront_Stadium) in [Gastown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastown) in time for the 2016 season. The proposal was abandoned after facing opposition from urbsnists and local residents.[^cite_note-BCPlace1-40]

Near the end of the [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC_season) and at the beginning of the [2021 seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC_season), the club was forced to play their home matches in the United States due to [the Canadian government's response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Canada) to limit cross-border travel during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_association_football). The club shared facilities with the [Portland Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers) at [Providence Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Park) in Portland, Oregon, where they played one of their rivalry matches as the home team.[^cite_note-49] In 2021, the club played most of their home matches at [Rio Tinto Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Tinto_Stadium) in [Sandy, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy,_Utah).[^cite_note-rsl.com-50]

In April 2025, it was announced that the Whitecaps and the City of Vancouver were having high level talks about building a new stadium at [Hastings Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Park).[^cite_note-51]

The club initially did not have a permanent training centre, opting instead to use existing facilities around [Greater Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Vancouver).[^cite_note-training-52] The Whitecaps partnered with the Government of British Columbia and the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) (UBC) to build the \$32.5 million National Soccer Development Centre on the UBC campus,[^cite_note-training-52] which opened on September 22, 2017.[^cite_note-53]

## Club culture

### Supporters

Main article: [Vancouver Southsiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Southsiders)

The largest Whitecaps supporters group is known as the Southsiders. The group began in 1999 when fans of the Vancouver 86ers began congregating in the pitch-level [beer garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_garden) behind the goal at the south end of [Swangard Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swangard_Stadium).[^cite_note-Province_2010-07-21-54][^cite_note-Vancouver_Sun_2008-09-06-55]

The Southsiders' relationship with the team's ownership has not always been amicable, but improved since the announcement of an MLS expansion team.[^cite_note-Province_2010-07-21-54] Images of the Southsiders are featured prominently in Whitecaps' marketing campaigns and the group's board was invited to the invite-only launch of the kits and logo to be used in MLS.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] The expansion has also increased membership to over 1200 and 100 paid members by July 2010.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-Vancouver_Sun_2010-07-17-59] Southsider supporters were primarily located in the southeast corner (sections 249–254) of BC Place stadium; the majority of the group later relocated to the new general admission section at BC Place in half of section 253 and all of section 254 that was introduced for the 2020 MLS season.

The three biggest supporter groups are the Vancouver Southsiders, Curva Collective and the Rain City Brigade.[^cite_note-60] There are several sub-groups that have emerged, members of which are sometimes also members of the three larger groups. There are generally considered to be 6 separate Whitecaps supporters groups. Founded after the 2011 season, Curva Collective focuses on visual displays and was previously located in the southwest "curve" in sections 203 and 204 at BC Place.[^cite_note-CurvaCollective-61][^cite_note-62] Rain City Brigade was established in 2010, occupying part of Section 201 and marching on matchdays from Library Square.[^cite_note-63]

The Whitecaps are also home to North America's first ever all youth supporter group, Vancouver Albion. They stand in section 254, and have become one of the 'Caps biggest groups with over 100 members. The Prawnsiders have existed since the Whitecaps' Swangard years, but organized formally at the beginning of the inaugural MLS season in 2011.[^cite_note-\:0-64] The name "Prawnsiders" comes from "prawn-sandwich brigade", a term often used to describe soccer supporters who sit in the more expensive seats.[^cite_note-\:0-64] They are primarily located in sections 244 and 245.[^cite_note-\:0-64] South Sisters provide a positive meeting space for Vancouver Whitecaps supporters who identify as female, LGBTQ2+, or allies. The group was officially formed in 2019.[^cite_note-65] Couch Ultras, named for their origins as a home-based group, organizes tifo displays at BC Place for the Whitecaps and Canadian national teams.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

In 2020, a new general admissions supporters section (named the Village Stand) was introduced in half of Section 253 and all of Section 254, adjacent to the tunnel where the players enter the pitch.[^cite_note-General_Admission-68] Membership in a supporters group is not required and seating is unassigned. The majority of the Vancouver Southsiders and Curva Collective are located here. Standing and chanting are permitted throughout the match in this' section.[^cite_note-General_Admission-68]

### Mascot

The official mascot for the Whitecaps is Spike, a [belted kingfisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belted_kingfisher), a bird common to the Vancouver area.[^cite_note-69] In late spring of 2026, Spike was given a makeover, introducing us to Spike 2.0.

## Rivalries

### Cascadia Cup

See also: [Cascadia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascadia_Cup)

The Vancouver Whitecaps have longstanding rivalries with both the [Portland Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers) and [Seattle Sounders FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Sounders_FC). The rivalries predate MLS and have been an integral part of the soccer culture in the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest). Matches between these three teams are heated as each of these teams are well-supported by their respective cities.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

**Portland Timbers**

Main article: [Portland Timbers–Vancouver Whitecaps rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers%E2%80%93Vancouver_Whitecaps_rivalry)

The Portland Timbers are one of the Whitecaps' biggest and longtime rivals, with an antagonistic history between the clubs going back to 1975 in the original [North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_\(1968%E2%80%931984\)).[^cite_note-72] In the [A-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-League_\(1995%E2%80%932004\)) and [USL First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_First_Division) Portland and Vancouver clashed in crucial, and often physical matches during the late 2000s, with the clubs facing each other in memorable playoff duels in 2007, 2009, and 2010.[^cite_note-73] The two clubs played for the 100th time in 2017, and the rivalry is one of the most-played in US soccer history.

**Seattle Sounders FC**

Main article: [Seattle Sounders–Vancouver Whitecaps rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Sounders%E2%80%93Vancouver_Whitecaps_rivalry)

The rivalry with the Seattle Sounders FC trace back to the 1970s [North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_\(1968%E2%80%931984\)) and have endured through multiple league incarnations, including the [USL First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_First_Division) and [MLS](71802.md) since 2011. As of mid-2025, the teams have met over 160 times. Vancouver's most sustained winning run came in the early 1980s, while Seattle's longest unbeaten stretch occurred between 2017 and 2022.

### Canadian rivalries

See also: [Canadian Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Championship) and [CF Montréal–Vancouver Whitecaps FC rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CF_Montr%C3%A9al%E2%80%93Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC_rivalry)

The Vancouver Whitecaps also have rivalries with [Toronto FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_FC) and the [CF Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CF_Montreal). Vancouver's first game in MLS was against Toronto in an attempt by the league to spur a rivalry between the two Canadian teams.[^cite_note-74] Montreal was a rival in the second division. The three teams have played each other during [Voyageurs Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyageurs_Cup) competitions.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

## Broadcasting

All Whitecaps matches are broadcast on television and radio. Through the 2013 season, [Sportsnet Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet) and [Sportsnet One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet_One) nationally broadcast all "regional" Whitecaps games not televised by [TSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network) or [TSN2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSN2) as part of its national package of MLS games, broadcasting 24 games per season.[^cite_note-Rogers_deal-77] Regional matches were called by Craig MacEwen, who does [play-by-play](1662121.md), and former Vancouver 86ers goalkeeper [Paul Dolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dolan_\(soccer\)), who provides [colour commentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_commentator).[^cite_note-78] Dolan replaced former Vancouver Whitecaps midfielder [Martin Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Nash_\(soccer\)), who provided colour commentary during the Whitecaps FC inaugural season.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-TSN_deal-80][^cite_note-81] In January 2014, TSN (which is owned by [Bell Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Media), a subsidiary of the Whitecaps' founding sponsor [Bell Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Canada)) announced that it would take over broadcast rights to these "regional" Whitecaps games beginning in the [2014 Major League Soccer season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Major_League_Soccer_season). In 2014, selected games aired on [CIVT-DT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIVT-DT) and [CIVI-DT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIVI-DT) due to scheduling conflicts.[^cite_note-bell-mlsprwhitecaps-82][^cite_note-tsn-whitecapsdeal-83] As of 2021, TSN streams all 34 regular season games, with playoff games if qualified. The current broadcasting team features [Blake Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Price) doing play-by-play, and Dolan as colour commentator, after Peter Schaad was relieved of his play-by-play duty in April 2021.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] Since 2023, the only regional broadcast is CKGO-AM, as TSN will only carry selected Whitecaps matches due to the new worldwide MLS broadcast deal with Apple TV.

On radio, Whitecaps games are primarily broadcast on [CKNW.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKNW) with Asa Rehman[^cite_note-whitecapsradio-86] and [Colin Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Miller_\(soccer,_born_1964\)).[^cite_note-corus-whitecapsdeal-87] Until the end of the 2016 season, matches broadcast on radio had play-by-play duties shared between Schaad and Scott Rintoul, and by former Vancouver Whitecaps midfielder [David Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Norman_\(soccer\)), who provides colour commentary.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-David_Norman-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] Norman replaced Paul Dolan prior to the 2012 MLS season, after Dolan joined the Sportsnet broadcasting team.[^cite_note-Radio_deal-92][^cite_note-David_Norman-89]

## Ownership



Supporters marching on Robson Street in April 2026 for "Save The Caps" as the teams future in Vancouver is uncertain.

The club is owned by a group of four investors: Greg Kerfoot, [Steve Luczo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_J._Luczo), [Jeff Mallett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Mallett), and [Steve Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Nash).[^cite_note-ownership-93] The group has a collective [net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_worth) over \$2 billion.[^cite_note-kerfoot-94] Kerfoot has been the majority owner of the Whitecaps since 2002, when he saved the club from contraction after previous owner David Stadnyk left the club, selling it to [United Soccer Leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Soccer_Leagues).[^cite_note-ownership-93][^cite_note-kerfoot-94][^cite_note-vanmag-95] Mallett, a former [chief operating officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_operating_officer) (COO) of [Yahoo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!), was raised in [Victoria, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia), and played for several collegiate soccer teams.[^cite_note-ownership-93] He later purchased minority stakes in the [San Francisco Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants) of [Major League Baseball](38776.md) and English soccer club [Derby County F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_County_F.C.)[^cite_note-ownership-93] Having first met at a charity soccer event in 2005, Mallett partnered with Steve Nash—a two-time [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA) [Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) who was also raised in Victoria—to put together a bid for a minority share of English soccer club [Tottenham Hotspur F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) in 2008.[^cite_note-ownership-93] After that transaction fell through, the duo contacted Kerfoot about a minority stake in the club.[^cite_note-ownership-93] Nash is the older brother of former Whitecaps midfielder [Martin Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Nash_\(soccer\)).[^cite_note-vanmag-95] The fourth partner, Steve Luczo, is the president, chairman, and CEO of [Seagate Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagate_Technology) and a partner in [Boston Basketball Partners L.L.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Basketball_Partners_L.L.C.), a group which owns the NBA's [Boston Celtics](43376.md).[^cite_note-ownership-93] Luczo met Kerfoot while the two were both employed by Seagate Technology, and Kerfoot contacted Luczo proposing he become part of the club's MLS bid.[^cite_note-96] In 2009, the group paid a \$35 million expansion fee to MLS for the right to join the league.[^cite_note-ownership-93] As of 2023, the club is valued at \$410 million by [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes).[^cite_note-97]

In April 2026, a formal offer to buy the team and move it to [Las Vegas](47737.md) was received from Grant Gustavson and a group of investors. Gustavson is the son of [Public Storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Storage) heir and billionaire [Tamara Gustavson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_Gustavson).[^cite_note-98]

## Affiliated teams

### Whitecaps FC 2

Main article: [Whitecaps FC 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitecaps_FC_2)

Whitecaps FC 2 is the club's reserve team that began playing in the newly established [MLS Next Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Next_Pro) league in 2022.[^cite_note-101]

The team previously played in the [United Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Soccer_League_\(2010\)) from 2015 to 2017 until the team was temporarily disbanded in favour of a partnership with 2018 USL expansion club [Fresno FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresno_FC).[^cite_note-Whitecaps_FC_launch_USL_PRO_team,_provide_update_on_National_Soccer_Development_Centre_at_UBC-102]

### Vancouver Rise FC

Main article: [Vancouver Rise FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Rise_FC)

Vancouver Rise FC is a women's club that competes in the [Northern Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Super_League), the top flight of the [Canadian women's soccer league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_soccer_league_system#Women). The Rise were one of the first two clubs to join the Northern Super League, and are a spiritual successor to the Whitecaps' [former women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC_\(women\)). The club was co-developed with the league itself, in an effort to retain female players from the Whitecaps' [academy team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitecaps_FC_Academy#Women's), and provide equity between the organization's men's and women's programs.

### Whitecaps FC Academy

Main article: [Whitecaps FC Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitecaps_FC_Academy)

[Whitecaps FC Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitecaps_FC_Academy), formerly known as the Whitecaps Residency program,[^cite_note-103] is the [youth academy and development system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_system) of Vancouver Whitecaps FC that was established in 2007. The academy fields men's and women's teams in [League1 British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League1_British_Columbia), which commenced in 2022.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

### Defunct teams

**Vancouver Whitecaps FC (women)**

Main article: [Vancouver Whitecaps FC (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC_\(women\))

The Whitecaps organization owned and operated a women's team in the [USL W-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_W-League_\(1995%E2%80%932015\)) from 2001 to 2012 that played at Swangard Stadium.[^cite_note-Province-WLeague-106] Originally named the Vancouver Breakers, the club was renamed the Whitecaps in 2003 under the ownership of Greg Kerfoot.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] The team won the W-League championship twice (in 2004 and 2006) and finished as runners-up in 2010.[^cite_note-Province-WLeague-106] In its place, the Whitecaps launched an [EXCEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Soccer_National_Development_Centres) youth development program for girls in partnership with B.C. Soccer and Canada Soccer.[^cite_note-109]

In 2019, several former Whitecaps Women players published allegations of sexual abuse and misconduct from coaches at the club.[^cite_note-110] The claims centered around behaviour by head coach [Bob Birarda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Birarda?action=edit\&redlink=1), who was fired in 2008 after an internal investigation, and [Hubert Busby Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Busby_Jr.), who coached the team from 2011 to 2012.[^cite_note-CBC-Investigation-111] Several supporters groups for the men's team organized protests and walk-outs during matches in April and May 2019 in support of an independent investigation into the allegations.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] At one protest in May, they were joined by visiting Portland Timbers fans.[^cite_note-114]

In late 2021, MLS announced an independent investigation and review into the conduct of both coaches as well as the Whitecaps organization.[^cite_note-CBC-Investigation-111][^cite_note-Guardian-Misconduct-115] The investigation found that the Whitecaps' response "was appropriate" and "adhered to all of the \[internal] investigator's recommendations".[^cite_note-Guardian-Misconduct-115][^cite_note-116] A parallel investigation into Canada Soccer's actions found that the allegations of Birarda's behaviour with the under-20 team were "mishandled" by CSA.[^cite_note-117]

**Fresno FC**

Main article: [Fresno FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresno_FC)

In November 2017, Vancouver Whitecaps FC entered into an official [USL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship) affiliation agreement with [Fresno FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresno_FC), a newly established USL expansion team set to begin play in the 2018 season.

The partnership between Vancouver Whitecaps FC and Fresno FC effectively ended when Fresno FC ceased operations on October 29, 2019.
