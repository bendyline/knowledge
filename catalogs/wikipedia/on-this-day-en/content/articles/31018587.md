**Patrick O'Donovan** (born 21 March 1977) is an Irish [Fine Gael](11252.md) politician who has served as [Minister for Culture, Communications and Sport](493027.md) since January 2025. He previously served as [Minister for Further and Higher Education, Research, Innovation and Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Further_and_Higher_Education,_Research,_Innovation_and_Science) from 2024 to 2025 and a [Minister of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_\(Ireland\)) from 2016 to 2024. He has been a [Teachta Dála](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachta_D%C3%A1la) (TD) for the [Limerick County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick_County_\(D%C3%A1il_constituency\)) constituency since 2016, and from 2011 to 2016 for the [Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick_County_\(D%C3%A1il_constituency\)) constituency.[^cite_note-elecs_irl-1]

## Personal life

He was born and raised in [Newcastle West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_West), County Limerick. He has a degree in chemistry from [University College Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Cork). After working as an analytical chemist and industry consultant, he returned to college obtaining a Graduate Diploma in Education from [Mary Immaculate College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Immaculate_College), Limerick.[^cite_note-RTE_20250126-2] He later worked as a primary school teacher. In 2014, he married Eileen Keary; and they have three children.[^cite_note-RTE_20250126-2]

## Early career

He was a member of [Limerick County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick_County_Council) for the [Newcastle West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_West) local electoral area from 2003 to 2011.[^cite_note-elecs_irl-1][^cite_note-oireachtas_db-3]

In January 2014, he called for "tougher controls on the use of open source internet browsers and payment systems" which he claimed allowed users to remain anonymous in the illegal trade of drugs, weapons and pornography.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## In government

### Minister of State

On 19 May 2016, following the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election) and the formation of [minority Fine Gael government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_Government_of_Ireland) led by [Enda Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enda_Kenny), O'Donovan was appointed as [Minister of State at the Department of Transport, Tourism and Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_at_the_Department_of_Transport) with responsibility for Tourism and Sport.[^cite_note-6]

On 20 June 2017, following the appointment of [Leo Varadkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Varadkar) as Taoiseach, he was appointed by the [new government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Government_of_Ireland) as [Minister of State at the Department of Public Expenditure and Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_at_the_Department_of_Public_Expenditure,_Infrastructure,_Public_Service_Reform_and_Digitalisation) and [at the Department of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_at_the_Department_of_Finance) with responsibility for Public Procurement, Open Government and eGovernment.[^cite_note-7] In August 2017, he claimed in an interview with the *[Sunday Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_Independent_\(Ireland\))*, that the [Provisional IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_IRA) were responsible for the [Dublin and Monaghan bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_and_Monaghan_bombings).[^cite_note-8] Fine Gael declined to comment on the matter.[^cite_note-9]

On 1 July 2020, he was appointed by the [new government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Government_of_Ireland) formed after the [2020 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Irish_general_election) led by [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin) as Minister of State at the Department of Public Expenditure and Reform, with responsibility for the [Office of Public Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Public_Works).[^cite_note-10]

In December 2022, following the appointment of Leo Varadkar as taoiseach, he was re-appointed to the same post, as well as the post of [Minister of State at the Department of Tourism, Culture, Arts, Gaeltacht, Sport and Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_at_the_Department_of_Culture,_Communications_and_Sport) with special responsibility for the [Gaeltacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaeltacht).[^cite_note-11] On 29 June 2023 he was hospitalised after collapsing in the chamber of Dáil Éireann.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Ten weeks later he had recovered sufficiently to resume his post.[^cite_note-14] He later ascribed the incident to undiagnosed [photosensitive epilepsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosensitive_epilepsy).[^cite_note-15]

### Minister for Further and Higher Education, Research, Innovation and Science

On 9 April 2024, O'Donovan was appointed as [Minister for Further and Higher Education, Research, Innovation and Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Further_and_Higher_Education,_Research,_Innovation_and_Science) following the appointment of [Simon Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Harris) as Taoiseach.[^cite_note-16]

He was appointed by Fine Gael as director of elections for Daniel Butler in the [2024 Limerick mayoral election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Limerick_mayoral_election).[^cite_note-17]

### Minister for Culture, Communications and Sport

#### 2025

On 23 January 2025, O'Donovan was appointed as [Minister for Culture, Communications and Sport](493027.md) in the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35th_government_of_Ireland) led by [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin), following the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Irish_general_election).[^cite_note-18]

In December 2025, O'Donovan proposed new measures requiring [social media](5897742.md) users to [verify their age](2636394.md) with a [government smartphone app](5719877.md) before accessing platforms such as [Facebook](7529378.md) and [Instagram](31591547.md) in order to protect children from [pornographic material](5710507.md) and other adult content online.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] These proposals were met with strong criticism from [civil liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_liberties) and [digital privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_privacy) campaigners, as well as legal and digital experts. Critics described the plans as disproportionate, intrusive and potentially illegal, however O'Donovan responded by emphasising the importance of [child protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_protection).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

On 19 December 2025, O'Donovan accused one of his predecessors, [Shane Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Ross), of setting back the role of women in [Irish sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Ireland) by "about 20 years".[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

#### 2026

On 4 January, O'Donovan and [Simon Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Harris) announced the introduction of a new [tax credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_credit) for “unscripted programmes”,[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] which would include the likes of [game shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_show) and [reality TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_television).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

On 8 January 2026, O'Donovan faced criticism from fellow [TDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachta_D%C3%A1la) and the Dublin Rape Crisis Centre after claiming that [X (formerly Twitter)](9988187.md) was not responsible for [non-consensual sexual images](62590682.md) and [child sexual abuse images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_Sexual_Abuse_Imagery) created by the platform's [AI tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ai_tool), [Grok](75223933.md), at the request of users. He had instead argued that the users requesting the creation of the images were solely responsible.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The following day, O'Donovan announced that he had deactivated his X account due to his concerns about the use of Grok to generate said material.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]On 13 January, he claimed that his comments about X and Grok had been “taken out of context”.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] On 16 January, during an interview on [RTÉ Radio 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Radio_1), O'Donovan rejected claims that the government had not been taking the issue seriously, describing the accusation as "rubbish".[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

In March 2026, an investigation carried out by the [OCCRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_Crime_and_Corruption_Reporting_Project) and its partners found that [Aughinish Alumina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aughinish_Alumina), a [Russian](25391.md)-owned [alumina](141888.md) refinery in [Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick),[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] indirectly supplies [materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_material) to [sanctioned](70568070.md) [Russian arms manufacturers](12513462.md).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] This contradicted statements made by O'Donovan during a Dáil debate in April 2022 that the refinery is "not in any way connected to a war machine", and "not connected, as some people might want to suggest, to any sort of Russian empire”.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

On 13 April, O'Donovan said that he would be asking [Coimisiún na Meán](73295318.md) to review whether [media coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_coverage) of recent [fuel protests](82911459.md) had been [balanced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalistic_objectivity);[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] this was strongly criticised by the [National Union of Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Journalists), who described the comments as "sinister and deeply disturbing".[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] During a debate in the Dáil the following day,[^cite_note-58] [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)) leader [Ivana Bacik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivana_Bacik) also criticised him when she compared him to the outgoing [Hungarian](13275.md) prime minister [Viktor Orban](750582.md).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] O'Donovan later repudiated the comparison, saying that "If I was ... a woman, I don’t think she’d have said it."[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] He was also strongly criticised by other TDs,[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] including members of [Fine Gael](11252.md),[^cite_note-67] while [Tánaiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1naiste) and Fine Gael leader [Simon Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Harris) distanced himself from O'Donovan's comments.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] Following a meeting with officials from Coimisiún na Meán on 14 April, a spokesperson for O'Donovan said that the Minister was no longer seeking a media coverage review despite his comments the day before.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] On 17 April, O'Donovan admitted that he had "made a hames" of what he was trying to say when calling for a review of media coverage of the fuel protests,[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] but defended his record as minister, and said he planned to propose a new [bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_bill_\(law\)) related to [media independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_independence) in the future.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]
