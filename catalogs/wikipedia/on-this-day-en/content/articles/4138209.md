**Cautín Province** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Provincia de Cautín*) is one of two [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Chile) in the southern [Chilean](5489.md) [region](299550.md) of [La Araucanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Araucan%C3%ADa_Region) (IX), bounded on the north by [Arauco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arauco_Province) and [Malleco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malleco_Province) provinces, on the east by [Argentina](18951905.md), on the south by [Valdivia Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdivia_Province), and on the west by the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). Its population at the 2012 census was of 692,582. The most important [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_Chile) are [Temuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temuco), [Villarrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villarrica,_Chile), [Padre Las Casas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Las_Casas,_Chile), and [Nueva Imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Imperial). Cattle, forestry, and agriculture make up most of Cautin's economy. Its climate is humid, rainy in winter, and generally warm in summer.

## History

Cautin province once formed part of the territory occupied by the [Araucanian natives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araucan%C3%ADa_\(historic_region\)), and its present political existence dates from 1887.[^cite_note-4] Cautín Province was the last area to be taken by Chile during the [occupation of the Araucanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Araucan%C3%ADa).&#x20;

In the late 19th century, the Chilean government endorsed a large-scale immigration and settlement program for the area, often by endorsing land allotment advertisement to German settlers from [Germany](11867.md), [Austria](26964606.md), and [Switzerland](26748.md), from where most of the new arrivals came. Beginning in the mid-19th century, with the [German Revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Revolutions), immigrants were often fleeing political upheaval and poor economies, seeking a new place to live. (Read [German colonization of South Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_colonization_of_Valdivia,_Osorno_and_Llanquihue))&#x20;

In 1903, a fleet of 88 [Canarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarians) families—400 persons—arrived in [Budi Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budi_Lake) (and currently have more than 1,000 modern descendants) as a response to the government's call to populate the region, and signed contracts for the benefit of a private company. While many [Canarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle%C3%B1o) obeyed their servitude, some of those who disobeyed the provisions of repopulation tried to escape their agreements and were arrested, or the indigenous Mapuche people took pity on the plight of these individuals who were on their former lands. The Mapuche welcomed some of them, and joined their demonstrations, in the so-called "revolt of the Canarians"; many Canarians integrated into the Mapuche population, thus adding to the large mestizo population that exists in Chile.[^cite_note-5]

Cautin is known for the [1971 Agrarian revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cautin_Agrarian_Revolt_\(1971\)).

## Tourism

The province of Cautín is known for its lakes and beaches. Perhaps best known internationally is the town of [Pucón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puc%C3%B3n) with its many recreational amenities on [Villarrica Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villarrica_Lake) under the backdrop of the [Villarrica Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villarrica_\(volcano\)). South of Villarrica on [Calafquén Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calafqu%C3%A9n_Lake) lies [Licán Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lic%C3%A1n_Ray), another lakeside resort town. [Saavedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saavedra,_Chile) is a popular beach on the Pacific coast.

## Economy

Cautín lies within the temperate agricultural and forest region of the south, and produces [cereal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal) crops such as wheat and oats. In [Carahue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carahue), the potato cultivation has a significant position. In addition, cattle production is remarkable.

## Geography and demography

According to the 2002 census by the [National Statistics Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Statistics_Institute_\(Chile\)) (*INE*), the province spans an area of 18,409 km<sup>2</sup> (7,108 sq mi)[^cite_note-INE-1] and had a population of 667,920 inhabitants (0 men and 0 women), giving it a population density of 36.3/km<sup>2</sup> (94/sq mi). It is the third most populated province in the country after [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Province,_Chile) and [Concepción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concepci%C3%B3n_Province,_Chile). Of these, 449,147 (67.2%) lived in urban areas and 218,773 (32.8%) in rural areas. Between the 1992 and 2002 censuses, the population grew by 15.5% (89,715 persons).[^cite_note-INE-1]
