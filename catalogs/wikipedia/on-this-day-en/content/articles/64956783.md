The [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) that started in 2020 and ended in May 2023 significantly impacted liturgical celebrations of the [Catholic Church](606848.md) worldwide. The Pontifical Foundation [Aid to the Church in Need](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_to_the_Church_in_Need) (ACN) stated that the pandemic was not "just a medical, social and economic problem, but also a pastoral problem", which led ACN to start encouraging a special program for the actions of priests and [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_\(Catholicism\)) against the virus spread.[^cite_note-ACN-1]

## Public masses



Countries where there were suspensions of Catholic Masses with the presence of the people during the COVID-19 pandemics, at regional level (in red) or national level (in burgundy).

In March 2020, all public [Masses](5684685.md) were suspended in Vatican City and Italy due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Italy). These suspensions began in late-February in the Archdioceses of [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Milan) and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Venice) and were extended to the rest of the Italian peninsula on 8 March.[^cite_note-2] Setting an example for churches unable to celebrate public Masses due to the lockdown, Pope Francis began livestreaming daily Mass from his residence at [Domus Sanctae Marthae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domus_Sanctae_Marthae) on 9 March.[^cite_note-3] Outside Italy, Mass in cities around the world were suspended in the days that followed.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] At the height of the outbreak in Italy,[^cite_note-6] on 27 March, Pope Francis imparted the *[Urbi et Orbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbi_et_Orbi)* blessing, normally reserved for Christmas and Easter, from an empty [Saint Peter's Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter's_Square) following a prayer for the health of all the world.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] For the prayer service, Francis brought the crucifix from [San Marcello al Corso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marcello_al_Corso) which had processed through the streets of Rome during the [miraculous plague cure of 1522](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraculous_plague_cure_of_1522).[^cite_note-9] The prayer service concluded with [Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benediction_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament), as church bells were rung and [sirens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siren_\(alarm\)) blared across Rome.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The spread of COVID-19 soon slowed in Italy, and public Masses were allowed to resume on 18 May, with Pope Francis celebrating his last daily live-stream Mass and first public Mass since [the lockdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdown_in_Italy) on the centenary of the birth of Pope John Paul II.[^cite_note-12]

All over the world, many churches suspended the presence of the faithful in their Masses, and resorted to virtual services for broadcasting the celebrations, such as [live streaming](18996908.md) or other ones, like television and radio.[^cite_note-Brasil-13][^cite_note-Parke-14] The Vatican announced that the [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week) celebrations in [Rome](25458.md), which take place at the end of [Lent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lent) would be canceled. Some dioceses ordered their churches to be closed to the public, while other dioceses such as the [Archdiocese of New York](245234.md) canceled Masses but kept churches open for prayer.[^cite_note-Burke-15] In Spain, many cities canceled their [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week) festivities. Holy Week is usually celebrated with parades and significant collections with tourism; in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville), it was the first time that events were canceled since 1933.[^cite_note-16] Due to the interruption of several Catholic religious activities (if not their totality), [Pope Francis](1687680.md) greatly encouraged the prayer of the [Holy Rosary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary).[^cite_note-17]

## Commentary on vaccine

On 26 February 2021, the [Archdiocese of New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_New_Orleans) issued a statement calling the [Janssen COVID-19 vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janssen_COVID-19_vaccine) "morally compromised," as it uses an abortion-derived cell line in vaccine production.[^cite_note-18] According to [The Washington Post](102226.md), the [Catholic Church](606848.md) embraced [COVID-19 vaccines](63319438.md), and "Catholic religious leaders across the United States are supporting coronavirus vaccination".[^cite_note-19] On 10 January 2022, [Pope Francis](1687680.md) issued a statement on [COVID-19 vaccines](63319438.md). He stated that [COVID-19 vaccination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccination) was a "moral obligation" and denounced "how people had been swayed by 'baseless information' to refuse one of the most effective measures to save lives".[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

## Impact on finances

The 2020 fiscal report for the Vatican showed a decrease in revenue of about 50%, but the [Institute for the Works of Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Works_of_Religion) contributed more income. Expenses were reduced by \$3.88 million. The Vatican also planned to increase their [liquid capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_capital) in response to market uncertainty to avoid selling Church assets in unfavorable market conditions.[^cite_note-23]
