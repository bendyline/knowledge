Presidential elections are scheduled to be held in Bulgaria on 25 October 2026.[^cite_note-3] Incumbent president [Iliana Iotova](34677899.md) is eligible and has declared her intention to run for a full term.

## Electoral system

The [president of Bulgaria](335070.md) is elected through a [two-round system](25932.md); if no candidate receives a majority of the vote in the first round or a candidate receives a majority of the vote but voter turnout is below 50%, a second round run-off is held between the top two candidates.[^cite_note-4] No candidate has ever won in the first round. Since 2016, a [none of the above](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/None_of_the_above) option is also present on the ballot.[^cite_note-5] In order to stand as a candidate, a person must be a Bulgarian citizen holding no other nationality, be at least 40 years of age by election day and have resided in the country for the previous five years.[^cite_note-6]

## Background

Former presidents [Georgi Parvanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgi_Parvanov) and [Rumen Radev](51575780.md) (now [Prime Minister](335071.md)) are ineligible to be elected to a third term, as the [Bulgarian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bulgaria) prohibits any person from being elected as president more than twice.

## Candidates

### Registered candidates

Ten parties and 27 initiative committees submitted documents to participate in the elections for President and Vice President of the Republic on October 25, 2026. One party was denied registration, while another withdrew its registration. Three initiative committees were also denied registration. Consequently, a total of 32 participants remained registered: 8 parties and 25 initiative committees.[^cite_note-7] Of these, one party and three initiative committees did not nominate candidate pairs and one initiative committee was canceled by CEC; ultimately, 27 candidate pairs remained registered and participated in the draw to determine their ballot numbers on the unified ballot.
