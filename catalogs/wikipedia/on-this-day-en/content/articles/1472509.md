The **British diaspora in Africa** is a population group broadly defined as English-speaking people of mainly (but not only) [British](19097669.md) descent who live in or were born in the [African continent](5334607.md). The majority live in [South Africa](17416221.md) and other [Southern African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa) countries in which English is a primary language, including [Zimbabwe](34399.md), [Namibia](21292.md), [Kenya](188171.md), [Botswana](3464.md) and [Zambia](34415.md). Their [first language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) is usually [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language). The term 'British diaspora in Africa' is usually used to refer to [White Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Africans_of_European_ancestry) of British ancestry - [anglicised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicisation) native Africans are not thought to be a part of the British diaspora.

## History

Further information: [History of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_South_Africa), [History of Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Zimbabwe), and [History of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Kenya)

### Colonialism

See also: [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) and [Territorial evolution of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_the_British_Empire)



[David Livingstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Livingstone) (taken in 1864) left [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) for [Africa](5334607.md) in 1840



[Cecil Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Rhodes) planned to link [Cape Town](6653.md) to [Cairo](6293.md)

Although there were earlier British settlements at ports along the [West African](67393.md) coast to facilitate the British [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md), more permanent British settlement in [Africa](5334607.md) did not begin in earnest until the end of the eighteenth century, at the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope) in [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa). British settlement in the Cape gained momentum following the second British occupation of the [Dutch Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Cape_Colony) in 1806. The government encouraged [British settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1820_Settlers) in [Albany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_South_Africa) ("Settler Country") in 1820 in order to consolidate the British [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony)'s eastern frontier during the [Cape Frontier Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_Wars) against the [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_people).[^cite_note-6] The Crown proclaimed [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Natal) in southeastern Africa as a [British colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_colony) in 1843. Following the defeat of the [Boers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer) in the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War) in 1902, Britain annexed the [Boer Republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer_Republics) of the [Transvaal Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Republic) and the [Orange Free State](180208.md).

[Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people) medical [missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) [David Livingstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Livingstone) became known for his exploration of the African continent. He is believed to have been the first [European](9239.md) to set eyes on [Victoria Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Falls,_Zambia) in 1855. He is a key character in African history, being one of the first well-known Britons to believe his heart was in Africa.

In the late nineteenth century, the discovery of [gold](12240.md) in the [Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witwatersrand) and [diamonds](8082.md) in [Kimberley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberley,_Northern_Cape) encouraged further settlement by the British, Australians, New Zealanders, Americans and Canadians. The search for [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) [resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource) also drove expansion north. Mining magnate [Cecil Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Rhodes) dreamed of a British Africa linked from [Cape Town](6653.md) to [Cairo](6293.md). The [British South Africa Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_South_Africa_Company), which he founded in 1889, [controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_Rhodesia) the territory named [Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia_\(name\)) after him; this later became known as [(Southern) Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Rhodesia) and [Northern Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Rhodesia) (now [Zimbabwe](34399.md) and [Zambia](34415.md), respectively). Simultaneously, British settlers began expansion into the fertile uplands (the "[White Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Highlands)") of [British East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa_Protectorate) (now [Kenya](188171.md)).

As a result of the rise of nationalist and anti-colonial movements throughout the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), in the aftermath of [World War II](32927.md) [decolonisation of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization_of_Africa) took place. Ethnic Africans were overwhelmingly the majority of population in the British colonies and [protectorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) and had long been denied equivalent political and economic power. These former colonies eventually became [self-governing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance). The Cold War powers entered into the conflicts in this period. Often aided by [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) expertise and weapons, [black nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_nationalism) [guerrilla](12720.md) forces such as the [Mau Mau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mau_Mau_Uprising) in Kenya, [ZANU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_African_National_Union) in [Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia) and [MK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umkhonto_we_Sizwe) in [South Africa](17416221.md) fought for majority rule, which normally meant "[one man, one vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_man,_one_vote)".

### Zimbabwe



Unveiling a statue of [Cecil Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Rhodes) in [Bulawayo](355237.md) in 1909

The ruling white minority in Southern Rhodesia [unilaterally declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia's_Unilateral_Declaration_of_Independence) as Rhodesia in 1965 but no provisions were made to incorporate the [Indigenous African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Africa) majority as political equals. [Civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesian_Bush_War) lasted until 1979, as black nationalists fought against the white-dominated government.  In 1980, the [first democratic general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Rhodesian_general_election,_1980) was held in what was now [independent](62450.md) [Zimbabwe](34399.md) and the country joined the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations). Subsequently, the country's white population declined sharply – thousands were intimidated, attacked, and driven off their property. Because of patterns of discrimination, whites had held the majority of property previously occupied by indigenous groups.[^cite_note-7] Charged with abusing [human rights](13831.md) and undermining [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy), President [Robert Mugabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mugabe) and other Zimbabwean individuals and entities were subjected to a wide range of economic and political sanctions by the United States and other western nations.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In 2002 Zimbabwe was suspended from the Commonwealth due to [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Zimbabwe) and [electoral fraud](382165.md).[^cite_note-10] In 2003, Zimbabwe voluntarily terminated its Commonwealth membership.[^cite_note-11]

Northern Rhodesia became a separate nation, Zambia.

#### White minority rule

See also: [History of South Africa (1910–1948)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_South_Africa_\(1910%E2%80%931948\)) and [Apartheid in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid_in_South_Africa)

In 1910, the two separate [British colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonies) and two [Boer republics](364762.md) in Southern Africa united to form the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa), which was governed as a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) within the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) under [white minority rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_minority). In 1926, the [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration_of_1926) ended the oversight of the [Dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion) from Britain, leading South Africa to become a founding member of the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations), as a [realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm). Five years later, the Act of the [Statute of Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Westminster_1931) formalized this full sovereignty.[^cite_note-CommonwealthSA-12] The majority of the British diaspora supported the [United Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Party_\(South_Africa\)), led by [J. B. M. Hertzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._M._Hertzog) and [Jan Smuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Smuts), while it was the ruling party between 1934 and 1948, and its various successors up to the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(South_Africa\)), the predecessor of the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)).[^cite_note-13] The United Party favoured close relations with the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and the Commonwealth, unlike the [Nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(South_Africa\)).

The [Afrikaners](18947965.md), who solely ruled the country from 1948 to 1994, entrenched a system of racial segregation known as [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid_in_South_Africa), established a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) in 1961, and withdrew from the Commonwealth.[^cite_note-CommonwealthSA-12] In 1955, 33,000 Dutch (34.8%), Germans (33.7%), French (13.2%), people of colour (7%), British (5.2%), unknown origin (3.5%), other Europeans (2.6%) in [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal_Province), which had an [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_English)-speaking majority of white voters, signed the [Natal Covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Covenant#Natal_Covenant) against the establishment of a republic.[^cite_note-14] Many of the British diaspora voted "No" in the [1960 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_republic_referendum,_1960) of white voters, but it was approved by a narrow margin and resulted in the establishment of a republic. The Natal majority voted against the republic and some residents called for secession from the Union after the referendum.[^cite_note-15]

#### Democracy

See also: [History of South Africa (1994–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_South_Africa_\(1994%E2%80%93present\))

In 1994, South Africa held its [first universal democratic general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_general_election,_1994), marking the end of apartheid and white minority rule, and rejoined the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).[^cite_note-CommonwealthSA-12] The majority of the British diaspora support the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)), which is the official opposition to the ruling [African National Congress](2503.md) and an increasingly [multiracial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial) party.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]



[Mark Shuttleworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Shuttleworth) is the first [South African](17416221.md) in space

The British diaspora population declined starting in the early 1990s as a result of a low [birth rate](515898.md) relative to that of other population groups and emigration. Reasons for emigration included [crime](6551455.md), [corruption](18331902.md), poor service delivery and [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action#South_Africa).[^cite_note-Haynie-20][^cite_note-Wende-21] A crude estimate of the British diaspora population is the number of [white South Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_South_African) who speak English as a first language, representing 1.6 million people, 36% of the white population group and 3% of the total population in the [South African National Census of 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Census_of_2011). This number is an overstatement as it includes people of other ancestral origins who have [assimilated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation) into the white English-speaking population. The English-speaking population is largest in the [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md) province and in cities such as [Johannesburg](58893.md) and [Cape Town](6653.md).[^cite_note-Census2011-1]

Despite the high emigration rate, many people of British descent continue to settle in South Africa, including many South African-born people who have returned home since the late-1990s, especially after the [2008 global economic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession).[^cite_note-Haynie-20][^cite_note-Wende-21] South Africa has been a top destination for British retirees,[^cite_note-22] and many [White Zimbabweans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people_in_Zimbabwe) of British descent settled in South Africa after Zimbabwean independence; some as a result of forced removal from their property. Over 200,000 [British citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_nationality_law) live in South Africa, including more than 38,000 people who are being paid a [UK State Pension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_State_Pension).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

### Global presence

See also: [South African diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_diaspora) and [Zimbabwean diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwean_diaspora)

A significant number of the British diaspora in Africa have emigrated to other [Commonwealth countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_Commonwealth_of_Nations) such as the [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Australia](4689264.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md) and [Canada](5042916.md). Others have settled in countries such as the [United States](3434750.md), [Ireland](14560.md) and [France](5843419.md).

## Culture

See also: [Culture of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_South_Africa)

White Africans generally enjoy the outdoors lifestyle and sports. The *[braai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_variations_of_barbecue#South_Africa)* is a popular way to get together with friends and family. Other popular pastimes include: visiting game reserves, hiking, camping and recreational fishing. There is a particular appreciation of country life and farming. Farmers themselves generally prefer holiday houses at the coast. In other ways, the culture of the British diaspora derives from their British ancestry. Afternoon tea – in fact, tea at any time of day – is still widespread as are hobbies such as gardening and reading. Families who live in rural areas are usually familiar with horseriding and shooting. White South African culture was encapsulated in the 1970s [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet) radio [jingle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingle) "Braaivleis, rugby, sunny skies and Chevrolet" based on the United States slogan "Baseball, hot dogs, apple pie and Chevrolet".[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Nationwide [television in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_South_Africa) was only introduced as lately as 1976, and many older South Africans of British descent had little exposure to British television and humour as a result of an [Equity union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_\(trade_union\)) ban on British television programme sales to South Africa under apartheid.[^cite_note-27]

### Language

See also: [South African English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_English), [List of South African English regionalisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_African_English_regionalisms), and [List of South African slang words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_African_slang_words)



Heart of the [Rhodes University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_University) campus

Many White Africans speak a unique dialect of English, developed by interaction with other local languages. [South African English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_English) is influenced by [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language), [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans), [Bantu languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages), [Khoisan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoisan_languages), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) and [languages of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India). The considerable Dutch and Afrikaans influence can be seen from words such as *braai*, *trek*, *lekker* and *ja* in common usage. Some [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_language) and [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_language) words, such as *[shongololo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millipede)*, *[muti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muti)*, *[ubuntu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubuntu_\(philosophy\))* and *fundi* (meaning an "expert"), are also commonly used. South African slang is used by many younger South Africans, with older people not using it regularly. The common greeting "howzit!" comes from the Afrikaans *hoezit!* (or "how is it?"); it can be likened to the US "howdy", the Canadian "hey, eh?" or the Australian "g'day".

[Zimbabwean English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwean_English) (ZimEng) shares many similarities with southern hemisphere English dialects (Australian, New Zealand, and South African), yet is distinct from its closest relative, [South African English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_English). Traditionally Zimbabwean English was predominately influenced by [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English), with the minor influence of Afrikaans (compared to South Africa) and Indigenous African languages, generally used to describe flora and fauna, with terms such as [kopje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopje), [dassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassie) and *bundu* (Shona for bush).[^cite_note-\:0-28] This dialect came to be known as *Rhodesian English*, typified by speakers such as Prime Minister [Ian Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Douglas_Smith) and [P.K. van der Byl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.K._van_der_Byl). After Zimbabwean independence from the UK in 1980, this dialect sharply fell out of favour and came to be regarded as an archaic, non-productive dialect, only spoken by the oldest generation of [White Zimbabweans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Zimbabweans) (including nostalgic [Rhodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodie) and [whenwes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whenwe)). Zimbabwean English evolved with the changing social, economic and political conditions in which Blacks and Whites interacted in Zimbabwe; with the old, conservative Rhodesian accent being effectively replaced by the more neutral and prestigious sounding *cultivated* [private school](612857.md) accent, which ironically retains some of its features.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Today, the main languages spoken in are English, Shona and Ndebele. Only 3.5%, mainly the White, Indian, coloured (mixed race) and foreign-born minorities, consider English their native language. The vast majority of English speakers are Black Zimbabweans, who are [bilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilingual) or even trilingual with Bantu languages, such as [Shona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shona_language) (75%), Ndebele (18%) and the other minority languages, and thus these speakers have an outsize role in influencing the direction of Zimbabwean English, despite traditional native speakers maintaining an important influence.[^cite_note-31]

Much like [Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_English), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_English) and [South African English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_English), spoken English exists on a continuum from *broad*, *general* to *cultivated* ([broad and general accents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_and_general_accents)), based on an individual's background particularly, class and income and historically, ethnicity.[^cite_note-32] Affluent, middle class and [highly educated](612857.md) Zimbabweans speak in a cultivated accent, influenced by older forms of [southern British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_in_southern_England), the now archaic Rhodesian English and [South African English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_English). The cultivated accent is sometimes humorously mocked by other speakers for its nasality and alleged pretentiousness, with speakers derided as the so-called *nose brigades*.[^cite_note-33] [Robert Mugabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mugabe), [Brendan Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Taylor), [Pommie Mbangwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pommie_Mbangwa), [Dave Houghton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Houghton_\(cricketer\)) and journalists Peter Ndoro and Sophie Chamboko are notable speakers of a cultivated accent.[^cite_note-34] Rural and urban working class speakers, on the other hand are heavily influenced by their native languages (these groups are also mocked as *SRBs* whose accents betray their *strong rural background*. [Lower middle class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_middle_class) black Zimbabweans are generally the most prominent in the mainstream media, fall in a spectrum between the two accents.[^cite_note-35] Speakers of this *general* Zimbabwean accent include [Morgan Tsvangirai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Tsvangirai), [Evan Mawarire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Mawarire), [Simba Makoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simba_Makoni) and [Tatenda Taibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatenda_Taibu). English is spoken by virtually all in the cities, but less so in rural areas.[^cite_note-\:0-28] Today English, the official language, enjoys status dominance and is the language of instruction in education, commerce, the government and the majority of the media.

[Rhodes University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_University) in [Grahamstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grahamstown) houses the Dictionary Unit for South African English.[^cite_note-36] The fourth edition of *A Dictionary of South African English* was published in 1991,[^cite_note-37] and the second edition of the *Oxford South African Concise Dictionary* was published in 2010.[^cite_note-38] The English Academy of Southern Africa, founded in 1961, is dedicated to promoting the effective use of English as a dynamic language in Southern Africa.[^cite_note-39]

### Literature

The British diaspora in Africa has a long literary tradition, and has produced a number of notable [novelists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novelist) and [poets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet), including [Doris Lessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doris_Lessing), [Olive Schreiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_Schreiner), [Guy Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Butler_\(poet\)) and [Roy Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Campbell_\(poet\)). A traditional South African storybook is [Percy FitzPatrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Percy_FitzPatrick)'s *[Jock of the Bushveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jock_of_the_Bushveld)*, which describes his journey as a wagon driver with his dog Jock. Other significant African writers of British descent are: [Nadine Gordimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadine_Gordimer), [Alan Paton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Paton), [Peter Godwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Godwin_\(writer\)), [Alexandra Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Fuller) and [Bryce Courtenay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryce_Courtenay).

### Arts

The British diaspora has influenced modern African arts, and has often incorporated other African cultures. [Athol Fugard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athol_Fugard) is a significant playwright. Born of an [Irish Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Catholic) father and an Afrikaner mother, he has always described himself as an Afrikaner but he wrote in English to reach a larger audience. [Sharlto Copley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharlto_Copley) is a significant film actor, producer and director. He starred in the [Oscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award)-nominated [science fiction film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_film) *[District 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_9)*, which was an international box office hit and received widespread critical acclaim. *District 9* drew heavily on metaphoric references to South Africa's apartheid history as well as including many other more direct references to South African and other African culture. Despite being of British descent, Copley plays an Afrikaner bureaucrat who experiences a similar oppression to that he once imposed on alien refugees. He also starred in the [film remake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A-Team_\(film\)) of the 1980s television show *[The A-Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A-Team)*.

### Music

Notable African musicians of British descent include: [Dave Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Matthews), who emigrated to the United States, and [Johnny Clegg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Clegg). [Wrex Tarr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrex_Tarr) performed the distinctly [Rhodesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesian) comedy song "Cocky Robin" based on [Chilapalapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanagalo). [John Edmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Edmond) was a popular singer, songwriter, entertainer and storyteller during the [Rhodesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesian) [Bush War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesian_Bush_War). [Seether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seether) is a [post-grunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-grunge) band founded by South Africans, which now includes Americans.

### Education

The British diaspora and their forebears have been extensively involved in the founding and development of numerous educational institutions across Africa.

#### Universities

There are four universities in South Africa that were established by the British diaspora, which admitted limited numbers of [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people) students under apartheid. The [South African College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_College) was founded in 1829 and later split into the [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town) and the [South African College Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_College_Schools). The [University of Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Natal) merged with the [University of Durban-Westville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Durban-Westville) to form the [University of KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_KwaZulu-Natal). The [University of the Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Witwatersrand) was founded in [Kimberley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberley,_Northern_Cape) in 1896 as the [South African School of Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_School_of_Mines,_Kimberley) and is now based in [Johannesburg](58893.md). Finally, [Rhodes University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_University) was established in 1904 with an initial grant from the [Rhodes Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_House#The_Rhodes_Trust).

#### Schools

There are two categories of schools founded by the British diaspora or British missionaries, those originally intended for the education of the children of the British diaspora and those founded for the education of the indigenous population.

The first category includes both notable [private schools](612857.md) such as [St. George's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George's_College,_Harare) in [Harare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harare), [Peterhouse Boys' School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse_Boys'_School) in [Marondera](2272458.md), the [Diocesan College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocesan_College) in [Cape Town](6653.md), the [Wykeham Collegiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wykeham_Collegiate) in [Pietermaritzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietermaritzburg) and [St John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College_\(Johannesburg,_South_Africa\)) in Johannesburg and prestigious [government schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school) such as [Maritzburg College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritzburg_College) in Pietermaritzburg, [King Edward VII School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VII_School_\(Johannesburg\)) in Johannesburg and [Prince Edward School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_School) in [Harare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harare).

The second category of schools includes South African institutions such as the [Lovedale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovedale_\(South_Africa\)) educational institution in the [Eastern Cape](313645.md), which was responsible for the education of many notable Africans including [Thabo Mbeki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabo_Mbeki), [Chris Hani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hani) and [Seretse Khama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seretse_Khama),[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] [Tiger Kloof Educational Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Kloof_Educational_Institute) in the [North West province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_\(South_African_province\)),[^cite_note-42] and St Matthew's High School outside [Keiskammahoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keiskammahoek) in the Eastern Cape. Many of these institutions were adversely impacted by the [Bantu Education Act of 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_Education_Act,_1953), and the Historic Schools Restoration Project championed by former [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Church_of_Southern_Africa) [Archbishop of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Archbishop_of_Cape_Town) [Njongonkulu Ndungane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Njongonkulu_Ndungane) aims to transform under-resourced historically significant schools into sustainable centres of cultural and educational excellence.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

### Sport



[Rory Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rory_Byrne) with [Michael Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Schumacher)'s car for the [2005 Formula One season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Formula_One_season)

[Cricket](25675557.md), [football](10568.md), [rugby union](25405.md), [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league), [tennis](29773.md), [golf](19568112.md) and [cycling](857311.md) are generally considered to be the most popular sports among the British diaspora.

[Cricket](25675557.md) in Africa and particularly Zimbabwe has been dominated by the people of British heritage. Up until recently, the majority of Zimbabwean players were from the British diaspora, including: [Andy Flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Flower), [Heath Streak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath_Streak), [Brendan Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Taylor) and [Ray Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Price_\(cricketer\)). Cricket in [South Africa](17416221.md) also traditionally features the British diaspora, including former national [Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket) captain [Graeme Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graeme_Smith) and bowler [Shaun Pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaun_Pollock). The [England cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_cricket_team) has often included many players of Southern African heritage in their ranks such as brothers [Sam Curran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Curran) and [Tom Curran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Curran_\(cricketer\)), [Gary Ballance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Ballance) and [Andrew Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Strauss). The England cricket team of 2010 that retained the [2010–11 Ashes series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ashes) in Australia, for example, received significant contributions from South African captain [Andrew Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Strauss), wicketkeeper [Matt Prior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Prior), batsman [Kevin Pietersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Pietersen), batsman [Jonathan Trott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Trott) and coach [Andy Flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Flower).

A few examples of the notable contributions of the British diaspora to South African rugby union are those made by [Kitch Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitch_Christie), the coach who led the [Springboks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_rugby_union_team) to victory in the [1995 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Rugby_World_Cup), [Bobby Skinstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Skinstad) and [Percy Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Montgomery), the Springboks' all-time leader in [appearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_\(sport\)) and points.

Members of the British diaspora have also had notable success in African [rallying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rallying), while former Rhodesia in particular produced several world champion [motorcycle road racers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing) including [Jim Redman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Redman) and [Kork Ballington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kork_Ballington).

Four-time [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France) winner [Chris Froome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Froome) was born in [Kenya](188171.md), and grew up in [South Africa](17416221.md).

[^cite_note-45]

[^cite_note-46]

The majority of white South Africans and Zimbabweans identify themselves as primarily *South African* and *Zimbabwean* respectively, regardless of their first language or ancestry.[^cite_note-47] The term *English-speaking South African* (ESSA) is sometimes used to distinguish anglophone South Africans from the rest of the population, particularly [Afrikaners](18947965.md). Additionally, the inclusive term *Zimbo* or *Anglo-Zimbabweans* are terms sometimes used by academics to distance themselves, from the [Rhodesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Rhodesia) era, though the latter term overlaps with and can cause confusion with the large community of [Britons of Zimbabwean descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabweans_in_the_United_Kingdom).[^cite_note-48] Along with [Anglo African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo_African) these terms are somewhat analogous to those used in other English-speaking countries such as [White Anglo Saxon Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Anglo_Saxon_Protestant), [English Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canadian), [Anglo-Celtic Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Celtic_Australian) and [English New Zealander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_New_Zealander).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

[Colloquial terms for the British in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_names_for_the_British#Africa) which are considered derogatory include the [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans) term *rooinek* (literally "red neck", probably from the stereotype that they sunburn relatively easily although unrelated to the [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) term *[redneck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redneck)*),[^cite_note-51] the [Australian term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_English_vocabulary) *pommy*, 'Beberu' in Kenya which means he-goat.[^cite_note-52]

The term *[Anglo-African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo#Africa)* has been used historically to describe people living in the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) in Africa,[^cite_note-53] although it has also been used to [self-identify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_\(social_science\)) by people of mixed British and indigenous African ancestry.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] *The Anglo-African Who's Who and Biographical Sketch-Book* published in [London](17867.md) in 1905 contains details of prominent British and Afrikaner people in Africa at that time.[^cite_note-58]

'Cape Brit' is another term sometimes used to refer to South Africans of British descent. It refers to the Cape Colony where the immigrants to whom many South Africans can trace their origins from settled during its time as British colony. The term is considered an equivalent of 'Cape Dutch'.
