# Secondary school

"High school" redirects here. For other uses, see [High School (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_School_\(disambiguation\)).

"Senior high" redirects here. For the Filipino drama, see [*Senior High* (TV series)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_High_\(TV_series\)).



[Staples High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staples_High_School) in [Westport, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westport,_Connecticut), US

A **secondary school**, **high school**, or **senior school**, is an institution that provides students with [secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education). Some secondary schools provide both *lower secondary education* (ages 11 to 14) and *upper secondary education* (ages 14 to 18), i.e., both levels 2 and 3 of the [ISCED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Standard_Classification_of_Education) scale, but these can also be provided in separate schools. There may be other variations in the provision: for example, children in [Australia](4689264.md), [Hong Kong](13404.md), and [Spain](26667.md) change from the primary to secondary systems a year later at the age of 12, with the ISCED's first year of lower secondary being the last year of primary provision.

In the [US](3434750.md), most local secondary education systems have separate [middle schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school#United_States) and [high schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_in_the_United_States). Middle schools are usually from grades 6–8 or 7–8 (serving students ages 11 to 14), and high schools are typically from grades 9–12 (serving students ages 14 to 18). In the [United Kingdom](31717.md), most secondary [state schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school) and [privately funded schools](612857.md) accommodate pupils between the ages of 11 and 16 or between 11 and 18, and some [UK](31717.md) private schools, i.e. [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_\(United_Kingdom\)), admit pupils between the ages of 13 and 18.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] In South Africa, primary school runs from grade 1-7 and high school from grade 8-12, with no middle school.

Secondary schools follow on from [primary schools](25058.md) and prepare for [vocational](56551.md) or [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education). In high and middle income countries, attendance is usually [compulsory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education) for students at least until age 16. The organisations, buildings, and terminology are more or less unique in each country.[^cite_note-ISCED-4][^cite_note-5]

## Levels of education

In the [ISCED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Standard_Classification_of_Education) 2014 education scale,[^cite_note-ISCED2011-6] levels 2 and 3 correspond to secondary education which are as follows:

Lower secondary education

First stage of secondary education building on primary education, typically with a more subject-oriented curriculum. Students are generally around 11 – 15 years old.

[^cite_note-ISCED2011-6]

Upper secondary education

Second stage of secondary education and final stage of formal education for students typically aged 15 – 18, preparing for tertiary/adult education or providing skills relevant to employment, usually with an increased range of subject options and streams.

[^cite_note-ISCED2011-6]

## Legal framework

Schools exist within a strict [legal framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_framework) where they may be answerable to their government through local authorities and their stakeholders. In England there are six general types of state-funded schools running in parallel to the private sector. The state takes an interest in safeguarding issues in all schools. All state-funded schools in England are legally required to have a website where they must publish details of their governance, finance, curriculum intent and staff and pupil protection policies to comply with *The School Information (England) (Amendment) Regulations 2012 and 2016*. [Ofsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofsted) monitors these.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

## Theoretical framework

School building design does not happen in isolation. The building or school campus needs to accommodate:

- Curriculum content

- Teaching methods

- Costs

- Education within the political framework

- Use of school building (also in the community setting)

- Constraints imposed by the site

- Design philosophy

Each country will have a different education system and priorities.[^cite_note-20] Schools need to accommodate students, staff, storage, mechanical and electrical systems, support staff, ancillary staff, and administration. The number of rooms required can be determined from the predicted roll of the school and the area needed.

According to standards used in the United Kingdom, a general classroom for 30 students needs to be 55 m<sup>2</sup>, or more generously 62 m<sup>2</sup>. A general art room for 30 students needs to be 83 m<sup>2</sup>, but 104 m<sup>2</sup> for 3D textile work. A drama studio or a specialist science laboratory for 30 needs to be 90 m<sup>2</sup>. Examples are given on how this can be configured for a 1,200 place secondary (practical specialism).[^cite_note-21] and 1,850 place secondary school.[^cite_note-22]

## Size

The ideal size for a typical comprehensive high school is large enough to offer a variety of classes, but small enough that students develop a sense of community.[^cite_note-LeeSmith1997-23][^cite_note-24] Some research has suggested that [academic achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_achievement) is best when there are about 150 to 250 students in each grade level, and that above a total school size of 2,000 for a secondary school, academic achievement and the sense of school community decline substantially.[^cite_note-LeeSmith1997-23]

Arguments in favor of smaller schools include having a shared experience of school (e.g., everyone takes the same classes, because the school is too small to offer alternatives), higher average academic achievement, and lower [educational inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_inequality).[^cite_note-Lee2000-25]

Arguments in favor of larger schools tend to focus on [economy of scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_scale).[^cite_note-Lee2000-25] For example, a single basketball court could serve a school with 200 students just as well as a school with 500 students, so construction and maintenance costs, on a per-student basis, can be lower for larger schools. However, cost savings from larger schools have generally not materialized, as larger schools require more administrative support staff, and rural areas see the potential savings offset by increased transportation costs.[^cite_note-Lee2000-25]

Larger schools can also support more specialization, such as splitting students into advanced, average, and basic [tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracking_\(education\)), offering a greater variety of classes, or sponsoring a greater number of [extra-curricular activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra-curricular_activities).[^cite_note-Lee2000-25] (Some of these benefits can also be achieved through smaller but specialized schools, such as a dedicated [special school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_school) for students with disabilities or a [magnet school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet_school) for students with a particular subject-matter interest.)

In terms of structure, organization, and relationships, larger schools tend to be more [hierarchical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchical_organization) and [bureaucratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucratic), with fewer and weaker personal connections and more rigidly defined, unvarying roles for all staff.[^cite_note-Lee2000-25] Teachers find that large schools result in more information to process in the larger environment (e.g., announcements about 100 programs instead of just 10) and that as individuals they form fewer relationships with teachers outside of their primary subject area.[^cite_note-Lee2000-25] Smaller schools have less [social isolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_isolation) and more engagement.[^cite_note-Lee2000-25] These effects cannot be entirely overcome through implementation of a [house system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_system) or "[school within a school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School-within-a-School)" programs.[^cite_note-Lee2000-25]

## Building design specifications

The building providing the education has to fulfill the needs of: students, teachers, non-teaching support staff, administrators, and the community. It has to meet general government building guidelines, health requirements, minimal functional requirements for classrooms, toilets and showers, electricity and services, preparation and storage of textbooks, and basic teaching aids.[^cite_note-za-26] An optimum secondary school will meet the minimum conditions and will have:

- adequately-sized classrooms;

- specialized teaching spaces;

- a staff preparation room;

- an administration block;

- multipurpose classrooms;

- a general purpose school hall;

- laboratories for science, technology, mathematics, and life sciences, as may be required;

- adequate equipment;

- a [library](17727.md) or library stocks that are regularly renewed; and

- [computer rooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_lab) or media centres.

  [^cite_note-za-26]

A secondary school may have a [canteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cafeteria), serving a set of foods to students, and storage where the equipment of a school is kept.

Government accountants having read the advice then publish minimum guidelines on schools. These enable environmental modelling and establishing building costs. Future design plans are audited to ensure that these standards are met but not exceeded. Government ministries continue to press for the "minimum" space and cost standards to be reduced.

The UK government published this downwardly revised space formula in 2014. It said the floor area should be 1050 m<sup>2</sup> (+ 350 m<sup>2</sup> if there is a sixth form) + 6.3 m<sup>2</sup>/pupil place for 11- to 15-year-olds + 7 m<sup>2</sup>/pupil place for students above 16 years old. The external finishes were to be downgraded to meet a build cost of £1113/m<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-27]

- [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Macau): *escola secundária* (中學 zung1 hok6): schools with secondary sections have *ensino secundário* (中學教育 zung1 hok6 gaau3 juk6)

- [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Malaysia): *secondary school* or *sekolah menengah*, sometimes *high school* is used

- [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Malta): *skola sekondarja* or *secondary school*

- [Mexico](1179798.md): *educación secundaria y preparatoria*

- [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Mongolia): *бүрэн дунд сургууль*

- [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Morocco): In Arabic: junior: *Madrasa I'dadia Ta'hilia* (*مدرسة إعدادية تأهيلية* / *preparative qualificative school*); senior: *Madrasa I'dadia Thanawia* (*مدرسة إعدادية ثانوية* / *preparative secondary school*) – in French: *lycée*

- [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Netherlands): *middelbare school* or *voortgezet onderwijs*

- [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_New_Zealand): *high school*, *college*, or *secondary school*

- [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Nigeria): *secondary school*, *junior* or *senior secondary school*

- [Norway](3037898.md): *videregående skole*

- [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Pakistan): *secondary school*, *higher secondary school*

- [Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Paraguay): *educación media*

- [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Peru): *educación secundaria* or *escuela secundaria*

- [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Philippines): *mataas na paaralan*, can be divided into *junior high school* (grades 7–10) and *senior high school* (grades 11–12)

- [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Poland):&#x20;

  - generally: *szkoła średnia* or *szkoła ponadpodstawowa* (*szkoła ponadgimnazjalna* during the existence of *gimnazjum* middle schools)

  - specifically: *[liceum ogólnokształcące](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liceum_og%C3%B3lnokszta%C5%82c%C4%85ce)* (comprehensive secondary school, grades 9–12), *[technikum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technikum_\(Polish_education\))* (technical secondary school, grades 9-13)

- [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Portugal): *2º ciclo do ensino básico* (grades 5–6), *3º ciclo do ensino básico* (grades 7–9), and *ensino secundário* or *liceu* (grades 10–12)

- [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Romania): *gimnaziu* (grades 5–8), *liceu* (grades 9–12)

- [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Russia): *средняя школа* (literally *middle school*); *junior middle school* (compulsory, grades 5–9), *senior middle school* (voluntary, grades 10–11)

- [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Serbia): *gymnasium* (four years), *[professional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional) schools* (four years), *[vocational schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_school)* (three or four years)

- [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Slovakia) : *gymnázium* (i.e. gymnasium, also translated as [grammar school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar_school) or [high school](554992.md))

- [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Slovenia): *gimnazija* (gymnasium), *srednja šola* (literally *middle school*)

- [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_South_Africa): *high school* or *hoërskool*

- [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_South_Korea): 중고등학교 (中高等學校・chung'godŭnghakkyo), 중등교육 (*chungdŭng'gyoyuk*; literally *middle education*), comprising 중학교 (*chunghakkyo*; the lower secondary school, grades 7–9, referred to as "middle school grades 1–3") and 고등학교 (*kodŭnghakkyo*; the upper secondary school, grades 10–12, referred to as "high school grades 1–3")

- [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Spain): *educación secundaria*, composed of two cycles: *E.S.O.* (*Educación Secundaria Obligatoria*, compulsory secondary education, four years, 7th to 10th grade) and *bachillerato* (non-compulsory secondary education, to years, 11th and 12th grade); [formerly (for those born until 31 December 1983)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ley%20General%20de%20Educaci%C3%B3n%20\(Espa%C3%B1a\)), primary education comprised up to the 8th grade and the secondary education was composed of two non-compulsory cycles: *B.U.P.* (*Bachillerato Unificado Polivalente*, three years, 9th to 11th grade) and *C.O.U.* (*Curso de Orientación Universitaria*, one year, 12th grade)

- [Sri Lanka](4560910.md): *junior secondary school*, *senior secondary school*

- [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Sweden): *gymnasium*

- [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Switzerland): *gymnasium*, *secondary school*, *collège* or *lycée*

- [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Taiwan): *junior high school&#x20;*(國民中學), *senior high school&#x20;*(高級中學), *vocational high school* (高級職業中學), *military school* (軍校), and *complete high school* (完全中學)

- [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Thailand): *matthayommasueksa* (มัธยมศึกษา; literally *secondary education*)

- [Trinidad and Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Trinidad_and_Tobago): *secondary school*, forms 1–5 (five years) or forms 1–6 (seven years)

- [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Turkey): *lise*

- [Ukraine](5231751.md): *gymnasium* (compulsory, grades 5–9), *lyceum* (voluntary, grades 10–12)

- [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_United_Kingdom)&#x20;

  - [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_England) and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Wales): *secondary school* (may be referred to as *high school*)

  - [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Northern_Ireland): *secondary school* or *grammar school*

  - [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Scotland): *high school* or *academy*

- [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education_in_the_United_States): *[high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_\(North_America\))* (usually grades 9–12 but sometimes 10–12, also called *senior high school*), is always considered secondary education; *junior high school* or *intermediate school* or *[middle school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school)* (grades 6–8, 7–8, 6–9, 7–9, or other variations) are sometimes considered secondary education.

- [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Uruguay): *liceo* or *secundaria* (three years of compulsory education: *ciclo básico*; and three years of specialization: *bachillerato diversificado*, into: humanities (law or economics), biology (medicine or agronomy), science (engineering or rrchitecture), and art

- [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Venezuela): *bachillerato*

- [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Vietnam): *trung học cơ sở* (abbreviated *THCS*, literally *basic middle school*, equivalent to junior high school in the U.S.); *trung học phổ thông* (abbreviated *THPT*, literally *general middle school*, equivalent to senior high school in the U.S.)
