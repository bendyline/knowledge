# Hangar

For other uses, see [Hangar (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangar_\(disambiguation\)).

Not to be confused with [Hanger (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanger_\(disambiguation\)).



A cutaway diagram of a hangar



A [Royal Air Force](25679.md) station hangar

A **hangar** is a building or structure designed to hold [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft) or [spacecraft](37910.md). Hangars are built of metal, wood, or concrete. The word *hangar* comes from Middle French *hanghart* ("enclosure near a house"), of Germanic origin, from Frankish \**haimgard* ("home-enclosure", "fence around a group of houses"), from \**haim* ("home, village, hamlet") and *gard* ("yard"). The term, *gard*, comes from the Old Norse *garðr* ("enclosure, garden").

Hangars are used for protection from the weather, direct sunlight and for maintenance, repair, manufacture, assembly and storage of aircraft.

The *Wright Flyer* outside the aircraft's makeshift hangar

The [Wright brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_brothers) stored and repaired their aircraft in a wooden hangar constructed in 1902 at [Kill Devil Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_Devil_Hills) in [North Carolina](21650.md) for their [glider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glider_aircraft). After completing design and construction of the *[Wright Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Flyer)* in [Ohio](22199.md), the brothers returned to Kill Devil Hills only to find their hangar damaged. They repaired the structure and constructed a new workshop while they waited for the *Flyer* to be shipped.



[Carl Richard Nyberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Richard_Nyberg)'s hangar for his *[Flugan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flugan)* (fly) from 1908, Täcka udden in [Lidingö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liding%C3%B6), [Sweden](5058739.md)

[Carl Richard Nyberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Richard_Nyberg) used a hangar to store his 1908 *[Flugan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flugan)* (fly) in the early 20th century and in 1909, [Louis Bleriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Bleriot) crash-landed on a northern French farm in [Les Baraques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Baraques?action=edit\&redlink=1) (between [Sangatte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangatte) and [Calais](85468.md)) and rolled his [monoplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoplane) into the farmer's cattle pen. Bleriot was in a race to be the first man to cross the [English Channel](9230.md) in a [heavier-than-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavier-than-air) aircraft, and he and set up his headquarters in the unused shed. In Britain, the earliest aircraft hangars were known as [aeroplane sheds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroplane_sheds?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the oldest survivors of these are at [Larkhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larkhill), Wiltshire. These were built in 1910 for the Bristol School of Flying and are now Grade II\* [Listed buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_buildings). British aviation pioneer [Alliott Verdon Roe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliott_Verdon_Roe) built one of the first aeroplane sheds in 1907 at [Brooklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklands), Surrey and full-size replicas of this and the 1908 Roe biplane are on display at [Brooklands Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklands_Museum).

As aviation became established in Britain before World War I, standard designs of hangar gradually appeared with military types too such as the [Bessonneau hangar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessonneau_hangar) and the side-opening aeroplane shed of 1913, both of which were soon adopted by the [Royal Flying Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Flying_Corps). Examples of the latter survive at [Farnborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnborough_Airfield), [Filton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filton) and [Montrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Montrose) airfields. During World War I, other standard designs included the RFC General Service Flight Shed and the Admiralty F-Type of 1916, the General Service Shed (featuring the characteristic [Belfast-truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_truss_bridge) roof and built-in various sizes) and the [Handley Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handley_Page) aeroplane shed (1918).

## Construction

### Steel construction

Sheds built for rigid airships survive at [Moffett Field, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangar_One_\(Mountain_View,_California\)); [Akron, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_Airdock); [Weeksville, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weeksville_Dirigible_Hangar); [Lakehurst, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangar_No._1,_Lakehurst_Naval_Air_Station); [Santa Cruz Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_Air_Force_Base) in Brazil; and [Cardington, Bedfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardington,_Bedfordshire). Steel rigid airship hangars are some of the largest in the world.

Hangar 1, Lakehurst, is located at [Naval Air Engineering Station Lakehurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Engineering_Station_Lakehurst) (formerly Naval Air Station Lakehurst), New Jersey. The structure was completed in 1921 and is typical of airship hangar designs of World War I. The site is best known for the [*Hindenburg* disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindenburg_disaster), when on May 6, 1937, the German airship [*Hindenburg*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LZ_129_Hindenburg) crashed and burned while landing. Hangar No.1 at Lakehurst was used to build and store the American [USS *Shenandoah*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Shenandoah_\(ZR-1\)). The hangar also provided service and storage for the airships USS [*Los Angeles*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Los_Angeles_\(ZR-3\)), [*Akron*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Akron_\(ZRS-4\)), [*Macon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Macon_\(ZRS-5\)), as well as the [*Graf Zeppelin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LZ_130_Graf_Zeppelin) and the *Hindenburg*.

The largest hangars ever built include the [Goodyear Airdock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_Airdock) measuring 1,175x325x211 feet[^cite_note-1] and [Hangar One (Mountain View, California)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangar_One_\(Mountain_View,_California\)) measuring 1,133 ft × 308 ft × 198 ft (345 m × 94 m × 60 m). The [Goodyear Airdock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_Airdock), is in Akron, Ohio and the structure was completed on November 25, 1929. The Airdock was used for the construction of the USS *Akron* and her sister ship, the USS *Macon*.

[Hangar One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangar_One_\(Mountain_View,_California\)) at [Moffett Federal Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moffett_Federal_Field) (formerly [Naval Air Station Moffett Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Moffett_Field)), is located in [Mountain View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_View,_CA), California. The structure was completed in 1931. It housed the USS *Macon*.

### Wood construction



Six [helium](13256.md)-filled blimps stored in one of the two hangars at the former US [Marine Corps Air Station Tustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Tustin)

The [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) established [more airship operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Navy_airships_during_World_War_II) during WWII. As part of this, ten "lighter-than-air" (LTA) bases across the United States were built as part of the coastal defence plan; a total of 17 hangars were built. Hangars at these bases are some of the world's largest freestanding timber structures.[^cite_note-2] Bases with wooden hangars included: the Naval Air Stations at [South Weymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_South_Weymouth), Massachusetts (1 hangar); Lakehurst, New Jersey (2); Weeksville, North Carolina (1); Glynco, Georgia (2); Richmond, Florida (3); Houma, Louisiana (1); Hitchcock, Texas (1); [Tustin (Santa Ana), California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tustin_air_base) (2); Moffett Field, California (2) and Tillamook, Oregon (2). Of the seventeen, only seven remain, [Moffett Federal Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moffett_Federal_Field), (former NAS Moffett Field), California (2); former [Tustin, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tustin_air_base) (former NAS Santa Ana and MCAS Tustin), California (2); [Tillamook Air Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tillamook_Air_Museum)/[Tillamook Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tillamook_Airport) (former NAS Tillamook), Oregon (1) and [Joint Base McGuire-Dix-Lakehurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Base_McGuire-Dix-Lakehurst)/Naval Support Activity Lakehurst (former NAS Lakehurst), New Jersey (2).[^cite_note-3]

### Fabric construction

Main article: [CargoLifter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CargoLifter)

A hangar for Cargolifter was built at [Brand-Briesen Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand-Briesen_Airfield) 1,180 ft (360 m) long, 705 ft (215 m) wide and 348 ft (106 m) high and is a free standing steel-dome "barrel-bowl" construction large enough to fit the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower) on its side. The company went into [insolvency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insolvency) and in June 2003, the facilities were sold off and the [airship hangar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airship_hangar) was converted to a 'tropical paradise'-themed indoor holiday resort called [Tropical Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Islands), which opened in 2004.

An alternative to the fixed hangar is a portable shelter that can be used for aircraft storage and maintenance. Portable [fabric structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabric_structure) can be built up to 215 ft (66 m) wide, 100 ft (30 m) high and any length. They are able to accommodate several aircraft and can be increased in size and even relocated when necessary.

## Structures and sizes

Hangars need special structures to be built. The width of the doors have to be large; this includes the aircraft entrance. The bigger the aircraft to be introduced, the more complex a structure is needed. According to the span of the hangar, sizes can be classified thus:&#x20;

| Size | Span (meters) |
| --- | --- |
| S | Less than 30 m |
| M | 30–60 m |
| L | 60–90 m |
| XL | 90–120 m |
| XXL | More than 120 m |

XXL hangars are built for the largest aircraft in the world like the [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380), [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) and the [Antonov 225](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-225_Mriya), and are the most complex to erect.[^cite_note-4]

## Regulation

Hangars are usually regulated by the building codes in the countries and jurisdictions and airports where they reside. In August 2014, the American FAA proposed legislation of how a hangar can be used on airfields that receive government funding. The definition of allowed activities included final assembly of aircraft.[^cite_note-5]

## Examples

### Airship hangars

[Airship hangars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airship_hangar) or airship sheds are generally larger than conventional aircraft hangars, particularly in height. Most early airships used [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) gas to provide them with sufficient buoyancy for flight, so their hangars had to provide protection from stray sparks to keep the gas from exploding. Hangars that held several airships were at risk from chain-reaction explosions. For this reason, most hangars for hydrogen-based airships were built to house only one or two such craft. During the "Golden Age" of airship travel from 1900, mooring masts and sheds were constructed to build and house airships. The British government built a shed in [Karachi](17123.md) for the [R101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R101), the Brazilian government built one in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), the [Hangar do Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangar_do_Zeppelin?action=edit\&redlink=1) for the German [Zeppelins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeppelin), and the U.S. government constructed [Moffett Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moffett_Field), [Mountain View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_View,_CA), California and [Lakehurst Naval Air Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Engineering_Station_Lakehurst), [Lakehurst, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakehurst,_New_Jersey).

### Hangars aboard ships



The helicopter hangar of an [*Akizuki*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akizuki-class_destroyer_\(2010\)) [destroyer](8779.md).

Many [warships](263960.md) carry aircraft and will often have hangars for storage and maintenance. Such hangars may be situated adjacent to the [flight deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_deck) on [cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser), [destroyers](8779.md) and [frigates](11464.md) or underneath the flight deck with [elevators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevator#Aircraft_elevators) to lift the aircraft on [aircraft carriers](2219.md) and [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship). On some vessels where space is short the hangar and flight deck share the same space, with the hangar stowing away for flight operations.

### Hangar homes

Main article: [Airpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airpark)



Hangar homes at [Independence State Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_State_Airport)

A hangar home is a residence that includes a hangar attached or integrated into the house, where the owner is able to park their privately owned aircraft. Hangar Homes are usually found in residential airparks.
