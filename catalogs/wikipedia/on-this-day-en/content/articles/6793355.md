**Ōita** (大分市, *Ōita-shi*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[oꜜː.i.ta, oː-, oː.i.taꜜ.ɕi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-1]) is the capital [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Japan) of [Ōita Prefecture](221919.md), located on the island of [Kyushu](16914.md), Japan. As of 30 November 2023, the city had an estimated population of 474,804 in 230,867 households, and a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 950 persons per km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-Ōita-hp-2] The total area of the city is 502.38 km<sup>2</sup> (193.97 sq mi).

## Geography

Ōita city is located in east-central Ōita Prefecture, facing Beppu Bay on the [Seto Inland Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto_Inland_Sea). The Ōno River flows from the south to the east, and the Ōita River flows from the west to the west, with the main urban center on the west side of the mouth of the Ōita River, with the Ōita Plain consisting of deltas and alluvial plains formed by the Ōita River and the Ōno River, and surrounding hills. The Takashima area in of the city is within the borders of the [Seto Inland Sea National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto_Inland_Sea_National_Park).

### Climate

Ōita has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (Köppen *Cfa*) characterized by warm summers and cool winters with light to no snowfall. The average annual temperature in Ōita is 15.6 °C. The average annual rainfall is 1663 mm with September as the wettest month. The temperatures are highest on average in August, at around 26.3 °C, and lowest in January, at around 5.1 °C.[^cite_note-3]

## History

The area of Ōita city was part of ancient [Bungo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungo_Province), and the location of the [Bungo Kokubun-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungo_Kokubun-ji) and presumably the Bungo *[kokufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokufu)* were located in this area. According to the [*Bungo no Kuni Fudoki*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fudoki), the name *Ōita* was given by [Emperor Keiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Keiko) when he visited this area in the late [Kofun period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofun_period); however, the main urban center was the port of Funai (府内) on the coast. During the [Kamakura period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura_period), the [Ōtomo clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ctomo_clan) was appointed *[shugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shugo)* of the province, and made Funai their *[jōkamachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dkamachi)*. By the [Sengoku period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengoku_period), the Ōtomo has conquered most of Kyushu and had embraced [Nanban culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanban_trade), with Funai becoming a stronghold of the [Kirishitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishitan) religion and western technology. However, the Ōtomo were destroyed by the [Shimazu clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimazu_clan) and after the establishment of the [Tokugawa shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate), their territories were divided, with Funai becoming the center of [Funai Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funai_Domain) ruled by the [Ogyū-Matsudaira clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsudaira_clan).

The town of Ōita was established on 1 May 1889 with the creation of the modern municipalities system, and was raised to city status in 1911. On 1 April 1997, it was designated as a [core city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_cities_of_Japan) with greater local autonomy.

On 1 January 2005, the town of [Notsuharu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notsuharu,_%C5%8Cita) (from [Ōita District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cita_District,_%C5%8Cita)) and the town of [Saganoseki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saganoseki,_%C5%8Cita) (from [Kitaamabe District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitaamabe_District,_%C5%8Cita)) were merged into Ōita.

## Government

Ōita has a [mayor-council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor-council) form of government with a directly elected mayor and a [unicameral](204304.md) city council of 44 members. Ōita contributes 13 members to the Ōita Prefectural Assembly. In terms of national politics, the city is divided between the [Ōita 1st district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cita_1st_district) and [Ōita 2nd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cita_2nd_district) of the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_of_Japan) of the [Diet of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_of_Japan).

During the 1960s and 1970s, an [industrial region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_region) was formed along the Beppu Gulf coast. Among the plants in the region were flagship plants of [Nippon Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Steel) and [Showa Denko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showa_Denko).

In the 1970s, [Toshiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiba) and [Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_\(company\)) built and expanded their plants in inland area. By then, the city emerged as a major production center of electronics products such as [LSIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit#SSI,_MSI,_LSI) and [digital cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_camera).

The downtown and shopping districts are located to the north of [Oita Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oita_Station). However, the area has been gradually declining because the main commercial areas have been dispersed due to the construction of big [shopping malls](235723.md) in the suburbs.

### Primary and secondary schools

Ōita has 54 public elementary schools, 25 public junior high schools, and two combined elementary/junior high schools operated by the city government. The city has 14 public high schools and one combined middle/high school operated by the Ōita Prefectural Board of Education, six private high schools and three private combined middle/high schools. The city also operates four and the prefecture operates one special education schools for the handicapped.
