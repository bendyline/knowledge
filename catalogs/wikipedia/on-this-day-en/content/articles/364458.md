The **chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff** (**CJCS**) is the presiding officer of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) (JCS). The chairman is the [highest-ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_defence) and [most senior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_seniority) military personnel in the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md)[^cite_note-CJCS-2] and the principal military advisor to the [president](24113.md), the [National Security Council](44026.md),[^cite_note-fuctions_of_JCS-3] the [Homeland Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Homeland_Security_Council),[^cite_note-fuctions_of_JCS-3] and the [secretary of defense](43998.md).[^cite_note-fuctions_of_JCS-3][^cite_note-goldwater-nichols_act-4] Although the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff outranks all other commissioned officers, the chairman is prohibited by law from having [operational command authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Command_Authority_\(United_States\)) over the armed forces; however, the chairman assists the president and the secretary of defense in exercising their command functions.[^cite_note-5]

The chairman convenes the meetings and coordinates the efforts of the Joint Chiefs, an advisory body within the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) comprising the chairman, the [vice chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chairman_of_the_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff), the [chief of staff of the Army](485507.md), the [commandant of the Marine Corps](7708.md), the [chief of naval operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Operations), the [chief of staff of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force), the [chief of space operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Space_Operations), and the [chief of the National Guard Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_National_Guard_Bureau).[^cite_note-fuctions_of_JCS-3] The post of a statutory and permanent Joint Chiefs of Staff chair was created by the 1949 amendments to the [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947). The 1986 [Goldwater–Nichols Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldwater%E2%80%93Nichols_Act) elevated the chairman from the [first among equals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primus_inter_pares) to becoming the "principal military advisor" to the president and the secretary of defense.

The [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff#Joint_Staff), managed by the [director of the Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_the_Joint_Staff) and consisting of military personnel from all the services, assists the chairman in fulfilling his duties to the president and secretary of defense, and functions as a conduit and collector of information between the chairman and the [combatant commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combatant_commander). The [National Military Command Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Military_Command_Center) (NMCC) is part of the Joint Staff operations directorate (J-3).

Although the office of Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff is considered very important and highly prestigious, the chairman, the vice chairman and the Joint Chiefs of Staff as a body do not have any command authority over combatant forces. The [Goldwater–Nichols Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldwater%E2%80%93Nichols_Act) places the operational [chain of command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_hierarchy) from the president to the secretary of defense directly to the commanders of the [unified combatant commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Combatant_Command).[^cite_note-6] However, the service chiefs do have authority over personnel assignments and oversight over resources and personnel allocated to the combatant commands within their respective services (derived from the service secretaries).

The chairman may also transmit communications to the combatant commanders from the president and secretary of defense[^cite_note-7] as well as allocate additional funding to the combatant commanders if necessary.[^cite_note-8] The chairman also performs all other functions prescribed under [10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 153](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/153) or allocates those duties and responsibilities to other officers in the joint staff.

## Organization and assistants



JCS chairman General [George Scratchley Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Scratchley_Brown) with Secretary of Defense [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld) during testimony before the [Senate Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Armed_Services) on January 15, 1976.

The principal deputy to the chairman is the [vice chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chairman_of_the_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) (VCJCS), another four-star general or admiral, who, among many duties, chairs the [Joint Requirements Oversight Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Requirements_Oversight_Council) (JROC).

The chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff is assisted by the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff), led by the [director of the Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_the_Joint_Staff), a three-star general or admiral. The [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff) is an organization composed of approximately equal numbers of officers contributed by the Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force who have been assigned to assist the chairman with the unified strategic direction, operation, and integration of the combatant land, naval, air, and space forces. The [National Military Command Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Military_Command_Center) (NMCC) is part of the Joint Staff operations directorate (J-3).

The chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff is also advised on enlisted personnel matters by the [senior enlisted advisor to the chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Enlisted_Advisor_to_the_Chairman), who serves as a communication conduit between the chairman and the senior enlisted advisors ([command sergeants major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_major#United_States_Army), [command master chief petty officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_Master_Chief_Petty_Officer), and [command chief master sergeants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_Chief_Master_Sergeant#Command_Chief_Master_Sergeant)) of the combatant commands.

Traditionally, the chairman serves as the final speaker of the [National Memorial Day Concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Memorial_Day_Concert) held on the day before [Memorial Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Day).

## Establishment of the CJCS position

The [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) (JCS) was established by the Joint Chiefs of Staff Act of 1942, which was signed into law on 1 July 1942. This act formalized the advisory body consisting of the senior military leaders of the Army, Navy, and later the Air Force, to assist the president and the [secretary of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_War) (later the [secretary of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_Defense)) with coordinating military strategy during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-JCS-9]

Before the establishment of the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff (CJCS), military leadership was more decentralized, with the service chiefs coordinating independently. The JCS existed as a body of senior military leaders, but no single officer held the position of chairman. Instead, leadership was shared, and the group advised the president and the secretary of defense on military matters.

[Fleet Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_admiral_\(United_States\)) [William D. Leahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_D._Leahy) served as Chief of Staff to the [Commander in Chief of the Army](485507.md) and [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Operations) from 20 July 1942, to 21 March 1949. In this role, he presided over meetings of the Joint Chiefs of Staff,[^cite_note-life1942100595-10] helping to unify military strategy during World War II. Leahy's office is considered a precursor to the position of CJCS, as it began to centralize military leadership and coordination.

The position of CJCS was formally established by an 10 August 1949 amendment[^cite_note-1949-Amend-11] to the [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947) ([50 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_50_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 44](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/44)),[^cite_note-Cornell50-44-12] which restructured the U.S. military after World War II. The first individual to hold the title of Chairman was General [Omar Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Bradley), who was appointed in 1949.

## Appointment and rank



General [Omar Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Bradley) is sworn in as the 1st chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff by Secretary of Defense [Louis A. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_A._Johnson) on August 16, 1949.



Admiral [Michael Mullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mullen) is sworn in as the 17th chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff by his predecessor, General [Peter Pace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pace) on October 1, 2007.

The chairman is nominated by the president for appointment from any of the regular components of the [armed forces](32212.md), and must be [confirmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent) via majority vote by the [Senate](24909346.md).[^cite_note-CJCS-2] The chairman and vice chairman may not be members of the same armed force service branch.[^cite_note-VJCS-13] However, the president may waive that restriction for a limited period of time in order to provide for the orderly transition of officers appointed to serve in those positions.[^cite_note-VJCS-13] The chairman serves a single four-year term of office[^cite_note-CJCS-2][^cite_note-NDAA2017-14] [at the pleasure of the president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_the_President_of_the_United_States),[^cite_note-CJCS-2] with reappointment to additional terms only possible during times of war or national emergency.[^cite_note-CJCS-2]

Historically, the chairman served two two-year terms, until the [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2017) amended the chairman's term of office to a single four-year term.[^cite_note-NDAA2017-14] By statute, the chairman is appointed as a [four-star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-star_rank) [general](2965165.md) or [admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(United_States\)) while holding office[^cite_note-CJCS-2] and assumes office on 1 October of odd-numbered years.[^cite_note-CJCS-2]

Although the first chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, [Omar Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Bradley), was eventually awarded a fifth star, the CJCS does not receive one by right, and Bradley's award was so that his subordinate, [General of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_of_the_Army_\(United_States\)) [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur), would not outrank him.[^cite_note-AP-15][^cite_note-Brassey-16] In the 1990s, there were proposals in [Department of Defense](7279897.md) academic circles to bestow on the chairman a five-star rank.[^cite_note-IFA-17][^cite_note-Jones-18][^cite_note-Owsley-19]

Previously, during the presidencies of [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman) and [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower), the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff position was rotated in accordance with the incumbent chairman's armed force service branch. As such, the incoming chairman would be from a different service branch. For example, in 1957, following the retirement of Admiral [Arthur Radford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_W._Radford) as Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, President Eisenhower nominated Air Force general [Nathan Twining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_F._Twining) as Radford's successor. When General Twining retired, Eisenhower nominated Army general [Lyman Lemnitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyman_Lemnitzer) to succeed Twining as chairman.[^cite_note-\:0-20]

In October 1962, President [Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) appointed Army General [Maxwell Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_D._Taylor) to succeed General Lemnitzer as Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. This decision — replacing an Army general with another Army general — broke the longstanding tradition of rotating the position between the Air Force, Navy, Marines, and Army. Tradition would have dictated that Kennedy appoint either [Air Force chief of staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force) General [Curtis LeMay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_LeMay), Chief of Naval Operations Admiral [George Anderson Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Whelan_Anderson_Jr.), or Commandant of the Marine Corps General [David Shoup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_M._Shoup) to the position. Following Maxwell's appointment, the tradition of rotating the chairmanship was discontinued.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-\:1-22][^cite_note-\:0-20]

## Pay

Effective 1 January 2025, according to the Monthly Rates of Basic Pay for commissioned officers, basic pay is limited to the rate of basic pay for level II of the Executive Schedule in effect during calendar year 2025, which is \$18,808.20 per month for officers at pay grades O-7 through O-10.[^cite_note-\:2-23] This includes officers serving as chairman or vice chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, chief of staff of the Army, chief of naval operations, chief of staff of the Air Force, commandant of the Marine Corps, chief of space operations, commandant of the Coast Guard, chief of the National Guard Bureau, or the commanders of the unified combatant commands.[^cite_note-\:2-23] In addition, according to [37 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_37_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 414](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/37/414), the CJCS receives an additional \$4,000 a year to cover expenses related to performing official duties.[^cite_note-37USC414-24]
