**Bangladeshis** ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): বাংলাদেশী[^cite_note-BdConst6Bn-60] [\[ˈbaŋladeʃi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Bengali)) are the [citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens) and nationals of [Bangladesh](3454.md), a South Asian country centred on the transnational [historical region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_region) of [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) along the [eponymous bay](47892.md).

[Bangladeshi citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_nationality_law) was formed in 1971, when the permanent residents of the former [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) were transformed into citizens of a new republic.[^cite_note-61] Bangladesh is the world's [eighth most populous nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population). The vast majority of Bangladeshis are ethnolinguistically [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis). The population of Bangladesh is concentrated in the fertile [Bengal delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_delta), which has been the centre of urban and agrarian civilizations for millennia. The country's highlands, including the [Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts) and parts of the [Sylhet Division](577594.md), are home to various tribal minorities.

[Bengali Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslims) are the predominant ethnoreligious group of Bangladesh, with a population of 150.36 million, which makes up 91.04% of the country's population as of 2022.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] The minority [Bengali Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Hindu) population made up approximately 7.95% of the population of the country according to the 2022 Census[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-census2011-66] Non-Bengali Muslims make up the largest immigrant community; while the [Tibeto-Burman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman) [Chakmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_people), who speak the Indo-Aryan [Chakma language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_language), are the largest indigenous ethnic group after Bengalis.[^cite_note-67] The [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic) [Santhals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhals) are the largest aboriginal community.

The [Bangladeshi diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_diaspora) is concentrated in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), North America and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Bangladeshi). A significant number of Non-Resident Bangladeshis (NRBs) have [dual citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_citizenship) in different countries.

## Terminology



[Bangladesh](3454.md) in Asia

After Independence of Bangladesh in 1971, Bangladeshis, as a nationality, have been referred to by various terms:

- **Bangladeshis**, the most widely used term to refer to the citizens of Bangladesh, comes from *Bangladesh* (meaning "Country of Bengal"), and can be traced to the early 20th century. Then, the term was used by Bengali patriotic songs like *Namo Namo Namo Bangladesh Momo*, by [Kazi Nazrul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazi_Nazrul_Islam), and *Aaji Bangladesher Hridoy*, by [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore).

  [^cite_note-68]

- **Bangalees**, an [exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exonym) for [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis), was used between 1972 and 1978 by the Constitution of Bangladesh for all citizens of Bangladesh, despite 2% of the population being indigenous and immigrant non-Bengalis. Under President [Ziaur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziaur_Rahman), the constitutional term was changed to *Bangladeshi*, as part of efforts to promote [Bangladeshi nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_nationalism).

  [^cite_note-Murshid_2001_165-69]

  &#x20;The term "Bangalee" is still used to denote people of [Bangladesh](3454.md) as a nation.

  [^cite_note-Citizenship_CoB-70]

None of these terms should be conflated with *[Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis)*, the name of the predominant ethnic group in the country who make up the bulk of all Bangladeshis.

## Demographics

The region of Bengal was settled by people of diverse origins, including [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples), [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_peoples), [Tibeto-Burman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman) and [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic) ancestry, with the most ancient settlements traced back to 4000 BCE.[^cite_note-71]

### Bengalis

Main article: [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis)



Bangladeshi artists performing in a dance show



Bangladeshi girls taking Selfie at [Pohela Falgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pohela_Falgun)

Approximately 99% of the Bangladeshis are [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis). [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal) was a prosperous [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot) for centuries. It witnessed a synthesis of Islamic, North Indian and indigenous [Bengali cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Bengal). Today, Bengalis enjoy strong cultural homogeneity with a common standardized language and a variety of [dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_dialects).

Over 91.04% of the population are [Bengali Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslim) (150.36 million) as of 2022. This makes Bangladesh the world's third largest [Muslim majority country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_by_country) after [Indonesia](14579.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md). Bengali Muslims also make up the world's second largest Muslim ethnic group after [Arab Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Muslims). Most Bangladeshi Muslims are member of the [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) branch of Islam. There are significant minorities of the [Shia](26961.md) and [Ahmadiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiya) branches. [Bengali Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Hindus) are the largest minority of Bangladesh, with a population between 13.1 million constituting 7.95% as per 2022 Census.[^cite_note-census2011-66] Bangladesh has the third largest Hindu population in the world after [India](14533.md) and [Nepal](171166.md). There are an estimated 400,000 [Bengali Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Christians) and 500,000 [Bengali Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Buddhists).

The Bengali population is concentrated in Bengal delta, the coastal areas of Chittagong Division and the river valleys of Sylhet-Division.

### Non-Bengali Muslims

Main articles: [Stranded Pakistanis in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranded_Pakistanis_in_Bangladesh) and [Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh](53544313.md)

An estimated 3 million Bangladeshi citizens are non-Bengali [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) immigrants from different parts of South Asia. They include affluent sections of the country's merchant and business class, particularly [Nizari Ismailism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizari_Ismailism) adherents.[^cite_note-78] They also include former [Stranded Pakistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranded_Pakistanis) and their descendants. Bangladesh's non-Bengali Muslims are usually fluent in both Bengali and [Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_language). Also there are over 1 million [Rohingya Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_Muslim) refugees living in Bangladesh who came here during the period of (2016–17) crisis.[^cite_note-79] On 28 September 2018, at the 73rd United Nations General Assembly, Bangladeshi Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina said there are 1.1–1.3 million Rohingya refugees now settled in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

### Tribes of the Chittagong Hill Tracts

Main articles: [Ethnic groups in the Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_the_Chittagong_Hill_Tracts) and [Jumma (people)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumma_\(people\))



[Chakma dancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_people) in a village in [Chittagong](380451.md) hill track.

In southeastern Bangladesh, the [Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts) frontier has a distinct history. It was an exclusive zone for Tibeto-Burman tribes in [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency) during the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj). Today, the area makes up 10% of Bangladesh's territory. It is home to several [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people) ethnic groups in the three hill districts of [Rangamati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangamati), [Bandarban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandarban) and [Khagrachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khagrachari). The three largest communities in the region have [a Raja as their tribal chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_monarchy_in_the_Chittagong_Hill_Tracts) who is recognized by the Government of Bangladesh.

- The [Chakma people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_people) are the largest tribe of the Chittagong Hill Tracts and the second largest indigenous ethnic group of Bangladesh after Bengalis. A [Tibeto-Burman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman) community, have been greatly influenced by [Bengali culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_culture), including in their native [Chakma language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_language), a branch of the [Bengali-Assamese languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali-Assamese_languages). Most Chakmas are concentrated in [Rangamati District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangamati_District). The community is headed by the [Chakma Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_Raja). The majority of Chakmas are [Therevada Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therevada_Buddhists), with a minority being [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu).

  [^cite_note-82]

- The [Marma people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marma_people) are second largest community in the Chittagong Hill Tracts. They have a [Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja) and are concentrated in the districts of [Bandarban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandarban) and [Khagrachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khagrachari). The Marmas are originally [Arakanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) people who moved to the territory in the 17th century in order to escape Burmese persecution.

  [^cite_note-83]

- The [Mro people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mru_people_\(Mrucha\)) are the third largest community in the region and have a [Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja). [Buddhism](3267529.md), [Christianity](5211.md) and [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animist) beliefs are among the chief faiths of the Mros. Their population is concentrated in [Bandarban District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandarban_District).

  [^cite_note-84]

  &#x20;Mros are originally related to the Chin people of Myanmar.

## Society

Main article: [Bangladeshi society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_society)

### Rural society

The basic [social unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_group) in a village is the family (*poribar* or *gushti*), generally consisting of a complete or incomplete [patrilineally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrilineal) extended household (*chula*) and residing in a homestead (*[bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari)*). The individual [nuclear family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_family) often is submerged in the larger unit and might be known as the house (*ghor*). Above the *bari* level, patrilineal kin ties are linked into sequentially larger groups based on real, fictional, or assumed relationships.[^cite_note-rs-109]

A significant unit larger than that of close kin is the voluntary religious and mutual benefit association known as "the society" (*shomaj* or *milat*). Among the functions of a *shomaj* might be the maintenance of a Mosque and support of a [mullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah). An informal council of *shomaj* elders (*matabdars* or *shordars*) settles disputes taking place in the village. Factional competition between the *motobdars* is a major dynamic of social and political interaction.[^cite_note-rs-109]

Groups of homes in a village are called *Paras*, and each *para* has its own name. Several *paras* constitute a *mauza*, the basic revenue and census survey unit. The traditional character of rural villages was changing in the latter half of the 20th century with the addition of brick structures of one or more stories scattered among the more common [thatched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatched) [bamboo](39029.md) huts.[^cite_note-rs-109]

Although farming has traditionally been ranked among the most desirable occupations, villagers in the 1980s began to encourage their children to leave the increasingly overcrowded countryside to seek more secure employment in the towns. Traditional sources of prestige, such as landholding, distinguished lineage, and religious piety were beginning to be replaced by modern education, higher income, and steadier work. These changes, however, did not prevent rural poverty from increasing greatly.

### Urban society



View of downtown Dhaka, the largest city in Bangladesh and one of the world's most populated cities

In 2015, 34% of Bangladeshis lived in cities.[^cite_note-110] [Dhaka](56656.md) is the largest city in Bangladesh and one of the world's most populous [megacities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacities). Other important cities include [Chittagong](380451.md), [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet), [Khulna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khulna), [Rajshahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajshahi), [Bogura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogra), [Jessore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessore), [Barisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisal), [Comilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comilla), [Narayanganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayanganj) and [Mymensingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mymensingh). Most urban centres are rural administrative towns. Urban centres grew in number and population during the 1980s as a result of an administrative decentralization program that featured the creation of [upazilas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upazila).[^cite_note-us-111]

## Identity

Main articles: [Bengali nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_nationalism) and [Bangladeshi nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_nationalism)

Bangladesh is noted for [cultural pluralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_pluralism) within a [Bengali Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslim) majority. Secularism has been an important contributor to the nation's society and ethos. The [Bengali language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) is a fundamental element of Bangladeshi identity. It is a secular language which evolved between the 7th and 10th centuries, with an indigenous alphabet, and unites people of different faiths and regions. The [Bengali language movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language_movement) sowed the seeds of East Pakistani nationalism, ultimately culminating in the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) in 1971. Since independence, the relationship between religion and the state has been controversial. Between 1972 and 1975, Bangladesh experienced [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) under a secular parliamentary system. [Military coups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_coups_in_Bangladesh) ushered a sixteen-year presidential regime, which restored the [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) and promoted moderate [Islamism](15012.md). In 1988, [Islam](6037917.md) was made the [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion). In 2010, the Bangladesh Supreme Court reaffirmed the principle of [separation of mosque and state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularity) in the constitution. The government generally respects freedom of religion and ensures protection for minorities.[^cite_note-state.gov-112] Another debate on national identity concerns attitudes towards the Chittagong Hill Tracts. A low-level insurgency took place in the region to demand constitutional autonomy against Bengali settlements. Despite a [peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts_Peace_Accord) in 1997, the Bangladeshi government is yet to implement many of its commitments to protect [adibashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adivasi) land rights. However, the deletion in 1977 of *Bangalee* as the nationality term for the country's citizens, in order to be inclusive of non-Bengali minorities, also reflects attempts to build a more cosmopolitan Bangladeshi society.

## Culture

See also: [Culture of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Bangladesh)

The culture of Bangladesh has evolved with influences from diverse social societies. Bangladesh's main religion is Islam, which has played a critical part in influencing the country's culture.

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Bangladesh)



The word *Wikipedia* written in the [Bengali script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_alphabet)

The official language of Bangladesh is [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), which is shared with the neighbouring Indian states of West Bengal, Assam, and Tripura. [Bengali dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_dialects) vary between different regions of Bangladesh but Standard Bengali is the most widely used.

According to the [Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue), there are 36 indigenous living languages, which include 17 [Sino-Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan), 10 [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European), 7 [Austro-Asiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Asiatic) and 2 [Dravidian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages).[^cite_note-ethnologue.com-113]

The oldest literary inscription in Bangladesh dates back to the 3rd century BCE. It was found at [Mahasthangarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahasthangarh) and is written in the [Brahmi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script). The language is [Magadhi Prakrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadhi_Prakrit).[^cite_note-114] The Bengali language developed from Magadhi Prakrit, and it's written from [Apabhramsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apabhramsa), between the 7th and 10th centuries. It once formed a single eastern [Indo-Aryan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_language) with [Assamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_language) and [Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language), but later became distinct. It became an official language of the [Sultanate of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bengal), where it was spoken as the main vernacular language. It absorbed vocabulary from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farsi) and [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit). Bengali is the [6th most spoken first language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_languages_by_total_number_of_speakers#Ethnologue_\(2025\)) in the world. The language was modernized during the [Bengali Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Renaissance) in the 19th century. It has influenced other languages in the region, including [Chakma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_language), [Rohingya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_language), Assamese, Odia and [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language). The indigenous [Bengali alphabets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_alphabet) descended from Brahmi serves as the Bengali script.

The [Bengali language movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language_movement) in East Pakistan was a key catalyst for forming Bangladeshi identity. It is commemorated by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as [International Mother Language Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mother_Language_Day), as part of worldwide efforts to preserve linguistic heritage.

Bangladesh is also home to number of minority indigenous languages, including [Santhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhali_language), [Garo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garo_language), [Marma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marma_language), [Manipuri language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuri_language) ([Meitei language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language)[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-Meitei-97]), Chakma and [Bisnupriya Manipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnupriya_Manipuri_language).

### Surnames

Main article: [Bengali name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_name)

Bangladeshi Muslims typically carry surnames that have [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) and [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) origins. Bangladeshi Hindus have Sanskritized Bengali surnames. Many Bangladeshi Christians have [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_name) surnames. Buddhists have a mixture of Bengali and Tibeto-Burman surnames.
