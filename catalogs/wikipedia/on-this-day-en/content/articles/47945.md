**Madhya Pradesh** ([/ˌmɑːdjə prəˈdɛʃ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-10] Hindi: [\[ˈmədʱjə pɾəˈdeːʃ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/66/MadhyaPradesh.ogg/MadhyaPradesh.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/66/MadhyaPradesh.ogg/MadhyaPradesh.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; lit. 'Central Province') is a [state](375986.md) in [central India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India). Its capital is [Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal). Other major cities include [Indore](365670.md), [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior), [Jabalpur](473219.md), [Chhindwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhindwara) and [Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagar,_Madhya_Pradesh). Madhya Pradesh is the [second largest Indian state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area) and the [fifth largest state by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population) with over 72 million residents. It borders the states of [Rajasthan](26291.md) to the northwest, [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) to the northeast, [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) to the east, [Maharashtra](20629.md) to the south and [Gujarat](53707.md) to the west.[^cite_note-11]

The area covered by the present-day Madhya Pradesh includes the area of the ancient [Avanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avanti_\(India\)) [Mahajanapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapada), whose capital [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain) (also known as Avantika) arose as a major city during the second wave of Indian urbanisation in the sixth century BCE. Subsequently, the region was ruled by the major dynasties of India. The [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy) dominated the majority of the 18th century. After the [Third Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Maratha_War) in the 19th century, the region was divided into several [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states) under the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) and incorporated into [Central Provinces and Berar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces_and_Berar) and the [Central India Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India_Agency). After India's independence, the Central Provinces and Berar was renamed as Madhya Pradesh with [Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur) as its capital. This state included the southern parts of the present-day Madhya Pradesh and northeastern portion of today's Maharashtra.&#x20;

Madhya Bharat with Gwalior as its capital

Madhya Bharat with Gwalior as its capital (1948)

Meanwhile Central India Agency was renamed as [Madhya Bharat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Bharat) which included parts of present day north western Madhya Pradesh and became a separate state with [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior) as its capital. In 1956, these states were [reorganised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act) and combined with the states of [Vindhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindhya_Pradesh) and [Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal_State_\(1949%E2%80%9356\)) to form the new Madhya Pradesh state. The Marathi-speaking [Vidarbha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidarbha) region was merged with the [Bombay State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_State). This state was the largest in India by area until 2000, when its southeastern Chhattisgarh region was designated a separate state.&#x20;

[Madhya Pradesh's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Madhya_Pradesh) is the [10th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP) in India, with a gross state domestic product (GSDP) of [₹](490783.md)9.17 trillion (US\$95 billion) and has the country's 26th highest per-capita income of ₹109,372 (US\$1272).[^cite_note-MOSPI-5] Madhya Pradesh [ranks 23rd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_territories_by_Human_Development_Index) among Indian states in [human development index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_development_index).[^cite_note-12] Rich in mineral resources, Madhya Pradesh has the largest reserves of [diamond](8082.md) and [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) in India. 25.14% of its area is under forest cover.[^cite_note-13] Madhya Pradesh has posted country’s highest agricultural growth rate which had averaged above 20 per cent over last four years. The state leads the country in production of pulses, oilseeds, soya bean, gram, pea, garlic, guava, and medicinal and aromatic herbs. Horticulture is being promoted as part of the state's Annual Action Plan.[^cite_note-14] Its tourism industry has seen considerable growth, with the state topping the National Tourism Awards in 2010–11.[^cite_note-15] In recent years, the state's GDP growth has been above the national average.[^cite_note-16] In 2019–20, state's GSDP was recorded at 9.07%.[^cite_note-17]

## Etymology

*Madhya Pradesh* name comes from the sanskrit words *madhya* (middle, central) and *pradeś* (state, province). It literally means "central province".

## History

Main article: [History of Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Madhya_Pradesh)

Isolated remains of *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus)* found in Hathnora in the [Narmada Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) indicate that Madhya Pradesh might have been inhabited in the [Middle Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Pleistocene) era.[^cite_note-18] Painted pottery, dated to the later [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) period has been found in the [Bhimbetka rock shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimbetka_rock_shelters).[^cite_note-Kenneth2000-19] [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) sites belonging to the [Kayatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayatha) culture (2100–1800 [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era)) and [Malwa culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa_culture) (1700–1500 BCE) have been discovered in the western part of the state.[^cite_note-Srivastava2008-20] Madhya Pradesh is also the world's ninth-most populous [subnational entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division).

The city of [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain) arose as a major centre in the region, during the second wave of Indian urbanisation in the sixth century BCE. It has served as the capital of the [Avanti kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avanti_\(India\)). Other kingdoms mentioned in ancient epics – [Malava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malava_Kingdom), [Karusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karusha_Kingdom), [Dasarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasarna_Kingdom) and [Nishada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishada_Kingdom) – have also been identified with parts of Madhya Pradesh.

[Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya) [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquered) northern [India](14533.md) around 320 BCE, establishing the [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Empire), which included all of modern-day Madhya Pradesh. [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) the greatest of Mauryan rulers, conquered it, bringing the region under firmer control. After the decline of the Maurya empire, the region was contested among the [Sakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka), the [Kushanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushana), the [Satavahanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty), and several local dynasties during the 1st to 3rd centuries CE. Heliodorus, the Greek Ambassador to the court of the Shunga King Bhagabhadra erected the [Heliodorus pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliodorus_pillar) near Vidisha.

[Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain) emerged as the predominant commercial centre of western India from the first century [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era), located on the trade routes between the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges) plain and India's [Arabian Sea](2747.md) ports. The [Satavahana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana) dynasty of the northern [Deccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) and the Saka dynasty of the [Western Satraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Satraps) fought for the control of Madhya Pradesh during the 1st to 3rd centuries CE.

The [Satavahana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty) King [Gautamiputra Satakarni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautamiputra_Satakarni) inflicted a crushing defeat upon the [Saka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka) rulers and conquered parts of [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) and [Gujarat](53707.md) in the 2nd century CE.[^cite_note-21]

Subsequently, the region was [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquered) by the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) in the 4th and 5th centuries, and their southern neighbours, the [Vakatakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vakataka). The rock-cut temples at [Bagh Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagh_Caves) in the Kukshi tehsil of the Dhar District show the presence of the Gupta dynasty in the region, supported by the testimony of a Badwani inscription dated to the year of 487 CE.[^cite_note-22] The attacks and the subsequent seize of [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior) by the [Hephthalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hephthalite) or [White Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Huns) brought about the collapse of the Gupta empire, which broke up into smaller states. The King [Yasodharman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasodharman) of [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) defeated the Huns in 528, ending their expansion. Later, [Harsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsha) (c. 590–647) ruled the northern parts of the [state](375986.md). Malwa was ruled by the south Indian [Rashtrakuta Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_Dynasty) from the late 8th century to the 10th century.[^cite_note-23] When the south Indian Emperor [Govinda III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govinda_III) of the [Rashtrakuta dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_dynasty) annexed Malwa, he set up the family of one of his subordinates there, who took the name of [Paramara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramara_dynasty).[^cite_note-24]

The Medieval period saw the rise of the [Rajput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajput) clans, including the [Paramaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramara_dynasty) of [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) and the [Chandelas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandela) of [Bundelkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundelkhand) along with the [Lodhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodhi_\(caste\)). The Chandellas built the majestic Hindu-Jain temples at [Khajuraho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khajuraho_Group_of_Monuments), which represent the culmination of Hindu temple architecture in Central India. The [Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjara-Pratihara_dynasty) also held sway in [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior) and western Madhya Pradesh at this time. It also left some monuments of architectural value in Gwalior. Southern parts of Madhya Pradesh like [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) were several times invaded by the south Indian [Western Chalukya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Chalukya_Empire) which imposed its rule on the Paramara kingdom of Malwa.[^cite_note-25] The Paramara King [Bhoja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhoja) (c. 1010–1060) was claimed to be a renowned [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath). The small Gond kingdoms emerged in the [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana_\(India\)) and [Mahakoshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakoshal) regions of the state.

In early 10th century [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior) and northern Madhya Pradesh came under the rule of [Kachchhapaghata dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachchhapaghata_dynasty), they belongs to the rajput clan. Gwalior was attacked and conquered by the [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) in the 12th century. Around 1500 Kachchhapaghata women along with the ladies of royal family committed jauhar at [Gwalior Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior_Fort). The Kachchhapaghata later shifted to [Amer](166537.md) (Jaipur). After the collapse of the Delhi Sultanate at the end of the 14th century, independent regional kingdoms re-emerged, including the [Tomara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomaras_of_Gwalior) kingdom of Gwalior and the Muslim Sultanate of [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa_Sultanate), with its capital at [Mandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandu,_Madhya_Pradesh).

The [Malwa Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa_Sultanate) was conquered by the [Sultanate of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Gujarat) in 1531. In the 1540s, most parts of the state fell to [Sher Shah Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Shah_Suri), and subsequently to the [Hindu king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_king) [Hemu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemu). Hemu, who had earlier served as the [General](148537.md) of the [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic) [Suri dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suri_dynasty), operated from the [Gwalior Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior_Fort) during 1553–56 and became the ruler of Delhi as a Vikramaditya king winning 22 battles continuously from Bengal to Gujarat and defeating Akbar's forces in the [Battle of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tughlaqabad) on 7 October 1556. However, he chose Delhi as his capital after his formal Coronation in [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior). After Hemu's defeat by [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) at the [Second Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Panipat) in 1556, Akbar seized Gwalior along with this most of Madhya Pradesh came under the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) rule. Gondwana and Mahakoshal remained under the control of Gond kings, who acknowledged Mughal [suzerainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerainty) but enjoyed virtual [autonomy](191145.md).

The Mughal control weakened considerably after the death of [Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor) [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb) in 1707. [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior) came under the rule of [Jats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jats) and [Bhim Singh Rana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhim_Singh_Rana) become ruler. However, he was defeated by the Scindias in 1735. Between 1720 and 1760, the [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathas) [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquered) most of Madhya Pradesh, resulting in the establishment of semi-autonomous states under the nominal control of the [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) of [Pune](164634.md): the [Holkars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Holkar) of [Indore](365670.md) ruled much of Malwa, [Pawars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawars) ruled [Dewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewas_State_\(Maratha_Confederacy\)) and [Dhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhar_State), the [Bhonsles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhonsle) of [Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur_kingdom) dominated Mahakoshal-Gondwana area, while the [Scindias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scindia) of [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior_State) controlled the vast northern parts of the state along with [Ajmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmer) and [Agra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra). [Later mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_mughals) accepted the surzenity of the [Scindia dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Scindia). The most notable Maratha rulers of the region were [Mahadji Scindia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahadji_Scindia), [Daulat Rao Scindia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulat_Rao_Sindhia), [Ahilyabai Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahilyabai_Holkar) and [Yashwantrao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashwantrao_Holkar). Besides these, there were several other [small states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Princely_states_of_Madhya_Pradesh), including [Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal_State), [Orchha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchha_State), and [Rewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewa_\(princely_state\)). The [Bhopal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal_state), which paid [tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute) to both the Marathas and the [Nizam of Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizam_of_Hyderabad), was founded by [Dost Mohammed Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dost_Mohammad_Khan,_Nawab_of_Bhopal), a former General in the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) army.

After the [Third Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Maratha_War), the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquered) the entire region. All the sovereign states in the region became [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India), governed by the [Central India Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India_Agency). The Mahakoshal region became a British province: the [Saugor and Nerbudda Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saugor_and_Nerbudda_Territories). In 1861, the British merged the [Nagpur Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur_Province) with the Saugor and Nerbudda Territories to form the [Central Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces).

During the [1857 uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857), rebellions happened in the northern parts of the state, led by leaders like [Tatya Tope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatya_Tope), [Rani Avantibai Lodhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avantibai). However, these were crushed by the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) and the princes loyal to them. The state witnessed a number of anti-British activities and protests during the [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement).[^cite_note-DwarkaPrasad1956-26] Several notable leaders such as [Chandra Shekhar Azad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandra_Shekhar_Azad), [B. R. Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar), [Shankar Dayal Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shankar_Dayal_Sharma), [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee), [Rajmata Vijaya Raje Scindia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijaya_Raje_Scindia), [Arjun Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjun_Singh_\(former_politician\)) and [Madhavrao Scindia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhavrao_Scindia) were born in what is now Madhya Pradesh.

After the independence of India, Madhya Pradesh was created in 1950 from the former British [Central Provinces and Berar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces_and_Berar) and the [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states) of [Makrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makrai) and [Chhattisgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarh_States_Agency), with [Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur) as the capital of the state. ## Geography

### Location in India

Madhya Pradesh literally means "Central Province", and is located in the geographic heart of India in between the latitude of 21.6°N–26.30°N and longitude of 74°9'E–82°48'E. The state straddles the [Narmada River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River), which runs east and west between the [Vindhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindhya_Range) and [Satpura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satpura_Range) ranges; these ranges and the Narmada are the traditional boundaries between the north and south of India. The highest point in Madhya Pradesh is [Dhupgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhupgarh), with an elevation of 1,350 m (4,429 ft).[^cite_note-28]

The state is bordered on the west by [Gujarat](53707.md), on the northwest by [Rajasthan](26291.md), on the northeast by [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md), on the east by [Chhattisgarh](47734.md), and on the south by [Maharashtra](20629.md).

### Climate

Madhya Pradesh also has three major seasons – Summer, Monsoon, and Winter. During summer (March–June), the temperature in the entire state ranges above 34.6 °C (94.3 °F) it has increased as it is all-time high in Madhya Pradesh. In general, the eastern parts of Madhya Pradesh are hotter than the western parts. The regions like Gwalior, Morena and Datia record temperatures of over 42 °C (108 °F) in May. The humidity is relatively very low and the region usually experiences frequent mild dust storms. The southwest Monsoon usually breaks out in mid-June and the entire state receives a major share of its rainfall between June and September. The south and south-east regions tend to experience a higher rainfall whereas the parts of the north-west receive less. Jabalpur, Mandla, Balaghat, Sidhi, and other extreme eastern parts receive more than 1,500 mm (60 in) of rainfall. The districts of western Madhya Pradesh receive less than 800 mm (30 in) of rainfall.[^cite_note-29]

The winter season starts in November. The temperature remains low in the northern parts of the state in comparison to the southern parts. The daily maximum temperature in most of the northern part of January remains between 15 and 18 °C (59 and 64 °F). The climate is generally dry and pleasant with a clear sky. The average rainfall is about 1,194 mm (50 in). The southeastern districts have the heaviest rainfall, some places receiving as much as 2,150 mm (84.6 in), while the western and northwestern districts receive 1,000 mm (39.4 in) or less.

### Ecology

According to the 2011 figures, the recorded forest area of the state is 94,689 km<sup>2</sup> (36,560 mi<sup>2</sup>) constituting 30.7% of the geographical area of the state.[^cite_note-FSI_2011-30] It constitutes 12.3% of the forest area of India. Legally this area has been classified into "Reserved Forest" (65.3%), "Protected Forest" (32.8%) and "Unclassified Forest" (0.2%). Per capita forest area is 2,400 m<sup>2</sup> (0.59 acres) as against the national average of 700 m<sup>2</sup> (0.17 acres). The forest cover is less dense in the northern and western parts of the state, which contain the major urban centres. Variability in climatic and [edaphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edaphic) conditions brings about significant difference in the forest types of the state. In January 2019 1.5 million volunteers in the state planted 66 million trees in 12 hours along the [Narmada River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River).[^cite_note-31]

The major types of soils found in the state are:

- Black soil, most predominantly in the [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) region, [Mahakoshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakoshal) and in southern [Bundelkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundelkhand)

- Red and yellow soil, in the [Baghelkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghelkhand) region

- Alluvial soil, in Northern Madhya Pradesh

- [Laterite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laterite) soil, in highland areas

- Mixed soil, in parts of the [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior) [Chambal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambal_Division) region.

The [Narmada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) is the longest river in Madhya Pradesh. It flows westward through a rift valley, with the Vindhya ranges sprawling along its northern bank and the Satpura range of mountains along the southern. Its tributaries include the Banjar, the [Tawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawa_River), the [Machna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machna?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Shakkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakkar_River), the [Denwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denwa) and the [Sonbhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonbhadra) rivers. The [Tapti River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapti_River) runs parallel to Narmada, and also flows through a rift valley. The Narmada–Tapti systems carry an enormous volume of water and provide drainage for almost a quarter of the land area of Madhya Pradesh. The Narmada river is considered very sacred and is worshipped throughout the region. It is the main source of water and acts as a lifeline to the state.

The [Vindhyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindhyas) form the southern boundary of the Ganges basin, with the western part of the Ganges basin draining into the [Yamuna](354286.md) and the eastern part directly into the Ganges itself. All the rivers, which drain into the Ganges, flow from south to north, with the [Chambal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambal_River), [Shipra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipra_River), [Kali Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Sindh_River), [Parbati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parbati_River_\(Madhya_Pradesh\)), [Kuno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuno_River), [Sind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_River), [Betwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betwa_River), [Dhasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhasan_River), [Ken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_River) and [Sunarrivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunar_River) being the main tributaries of the Yamuna. Shipra River is one of the most sacred rivers of Hinduism. It is the site of the [Simhastha Kumbh Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain_Simhastha), which is held every 12 years. Shipra is stretched across Indore, Ujjain and Dewas. The land drained by these rivers is agriculturally rich, with the natural vegetation largely consisting of grass and [dry deciduous forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_deciduous_forest) types, largely thorny. The eastern part of the Ganges basin consists of the [Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_River), the [Tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tons_River) and the [Rihand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihand) Rivers. Son, which arises in the Maikal hills around [Amarkantak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarkantak), is the largest tributary that goes into the Ganges on the south bank and that does not arise from the [Himalayas](37966.md). Son and its tributaries contribute the bulk of the monsoon flow into the Ganges, because the north bank tributaries are all snow fed. The forests in their basins are much richer than the thorn forests of the northwestern part of Madhya Pradesh.

After the formation of [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) State, the major portion of [Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi) basin now lies in Chhattisgarh. Presently, only 154 km<sup>2</sup> basin area of Hasdeo River in Anuppur District lies in Madhya Pradesh.

The [Satpuras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satpura), in the [Gawilgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gawilgarh) and [Mahadeo Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahadeo_Hills), also contain a watershed, which is south facing. The [Wainganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wainganga_River), the [Wardha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wardha_River), the Pench, the [Kanhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanhan_River) rivers, discharge an enormous volume of water into the [Godavari River](47988.md) system. The Godavari basin consists of [sub-tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-tropical), semi-moist forests, mainly in the valley of the Indrawati. There are many important multi-state irrigation projects in development, including the [Godavari River Basin Irrigation Projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godavari_River_Basin_Irrigation_Projects).

The population of Madhya Pradesh consists of a number of [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group), [tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe), [castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste) and communities. The [scheduled castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_castes) and the [scheduled tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_tribes) constitute a significant portion of the [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_\(human_biology\)) of the State at 15.6% and 21.1% respectively.

The main tribal groups in Madhya Pradesh are [Gondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondi_people), [Bhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhil), [Baiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiga_\(tribe\)), [Korku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korku_people), [Bharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharia_people), Halba, [Malto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malto_people) and [Sahariya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahariya). Tribal people form more than 50% of the population in the districts of [Mandla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandla_district), [Dhar](3227215.md), [Dindori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dindori_district), [Barwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barwani_district), [Jhabua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhabua_district) and [Alirajpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alirajpur_district). 90% of the people in Jhabua and Alirajpur have a tribal background.

In [Khargone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khargone), [Khandwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khandwa_district), [Burhanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burhanpur_district), [Betul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betul_district), [Chhindwara](2815567.md), [Seoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoni_district), [Anuppur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anuppur_district), [Umaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umaria_district), [Shahdol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahdol_district) and [Singrauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singrauli_district) districts 30–50% of the population is tribal. According to the 2011 census, the [tribal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adivasi) population in Madhya Pradesh was 15.34 million, constituting 21.1% of the total population. There were 46 recognised Scheduled Tribes and three of them have been identified as "Special Primitive Tribal Groups" in the State.[^cite_note-42]

Madhya Pradesh ranks 33rd on the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) with a value of 0.606 (2018).[^cite_note-UNDP_HDI_2011-43] The state's per-capita [gross state domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) (nominal GDP) is the 26th highest in the country (2018–19). According to the SDG India Index 2020–21 compiled by the [NITI Aayog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NITI_Aayog), Madhya Pradesh ranks 21st on sustainable development goals, 9th on gender equality, and 10th on clean water and sanitation.[^cite_note-FinalReport-44]

Madhya pradesh ranked ninth in the Fiscal Health Index (FHI) 2025, with a score of 42.2.[^cite_note-45]

The official language of the state is [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), which is spoken by over two-thirds of the population and is used for all government business.[^cite_note-langoff-4] In urban areas Standard Hindi is the main language, while [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) is spoken by Muslims. In rural areas, however, many niche languages are counted as Hindi dialects in the census despite not being mutually intelligible. In the west [Malvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvi_language) and [Nimadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimadi_language) are spoken in the [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) and [Nimar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimar) regions respectively, which are more closely related to the [Rajasthani languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthani_languages). In [Bundelkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundelkhand) in the north and [Baghelkhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagelkhand) in the east [Bundeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundeli_language) and [Bagheli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagheli_language) are spoken resp. which are similar to [Awadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadhi_language) and [Chhattisgarhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarhi_language). In the southeast Chhattisgarhi is dominant and [Powari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powari_language) is the main language of the southern part of the state. Most speakers of these languages consider them to be dialects of Hindi and so report their language as 'Hindi' on the census.[^cite_note-census2011-langreport-46]

[Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language) is another significant language. Due to [Maratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) rule over much of what is now Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh is home to the largest number of Marathis outside Maharashtra. Although large numbers of Marathis can be found in urban centres like Indore, the highest concentrations are in the southern areas of the state adjoining Maharashtra. Marathi is the most-spoken language in Burhanpur district, while it is a major minority language in the southern parts of Mahakoshal especially Betul, Chhindwara and Balaghat districts.[^cite_note-census2011-langreport-46]

There are several languages spoken by the Adivasis. The various [Bhil languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhil_languages) are Indo-Aryan languages spoken by 50 lakh Bhils of western Madhya Pradesh. Although many, especially in the eastern parts of their range, have adopted the regional languages as mother tongue, the languages are still strong in the far-western hills especially Barwani, Jhabua and Alirajpur districts where they are in the majority. [Bhili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhili_language), the [Bareli languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauri_Bareli_language) and [Bhilali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhilali_language) are the major varieties spoken in the state.

[Gondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondi_language) is the second-largest Adivasi language, spoken by 11 lakh Gonds in the state. A [Dravidian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages) related to [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language), it is mainly spoken in the southern Satpura highlands of Mahakoshal, where it is spoken with the regional languages. Some in the more remote valleys of the Satpuras speak a poorly-described Dravidian dialect called [Bharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharia_language). Smaller minorities of Gondi speakers can be found in Khandwa and Dewas districts in the west as well as Anuppur, Sidhi and Singrauli districts in the east. Elsewhere in the state, the Gonds have almost totally abandoned their original language.

[Korku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korku_language), a [Munda language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munda_languages), has over 400,000 speakers in the central highlands of the state. In the far-southeast of Burhanpur can be found some speakers of the language isolate [Nihali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihali_language) who live among the Korku. All speakers of tribal languages face significant pressure to switch to the dominant regional languages while their own tongues are considered 'backward' and 'rural'.[^cite_note-census2011-langreport-46]

The following languages are taught in schools in Madhya Pradesh under the Three Language Formula:[^cite_note-47]

First language: Any [Scheduled Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_with_official_status_in_India) or English

Second language: Hindi, Urdu or English

Third language: Another Scheduled Language (often Sanskrit) or Foreign Languages (like [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) or [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language))

Madhya Pradesh is noted for its classical and folk music. In 2023, UNESCO declared Gwalior as the [City of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior). Some of the noted [Hindustani classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_classical_music) [gharanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharana) in Madhya Pradesh include the [Gwalior gharana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior_gharana), the [Maihar gharana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maihar_gharana) and [Senia gharana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aashish_Khan). Gwalior is also known as the capital of Hindustani classical Music. Many of the medieval India's most noted sufi and mustic singers like [Muhammed Ghaus Gwaliori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ghawth), [Tansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tansen) and [Baiju Bawra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiju_Bawra), were born in Gwalior. Noted [Dhrupad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhrupad) exponents [Aminuddin Dagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aminuddin_Dagar) (Indore), [Gundecha Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gundecha_Brothers) (Ujjain) and [Uday Bhawalkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uday_Bhawalkar) (Ujjain) were also born in present-day Madhya Pradesh.[^cite_note-SimonMark2000-50] Renowned classical singer [Kumar Gandharva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumar_Gandharva) spent his life at Dewas. The birthplaces of noted [playback singers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playback_singer) [Kishore Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishore_Kumar) (Khandwa) and [Lata Mangeshkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lata_Mangeshkar) (Indore) and singer and composer Aadesh Shrivastava (Jabalpur) are also located in MP. The local styles of folk singing are Faga, Bhartahari, Sanja geet, [Bhopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopa), [Kalbelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalbelia), Bhat, Bhand, Vasdeva, Videsia, Kalgi Turra, Nirgunia, Alha, Pandwani Gayan and [Garba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garba_\(dance\)) Garbi Govalan.[^cite_note-51] Annual Tansen Music Festival is organised by the MP Tourism Board in Gwalior.&#x20;

The major folk dances of MP are [Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_\(dance\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), Karma, Saila, Matki, Gangaur, [Badhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badhai), Baredi, Naurata, Ahiri and Bhagoria.[^cite_note-52]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Madhya_Pradesh)



Matang was completely developed and manufactured by [Vehicle Factory Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_Factory_Jabalpur)

Madhya Pradesh's [gross state domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) (nominal GDP) for 2013–14 was ₹ 4,509 billion (approximately US\$ 72,726,000,000). The per-capita figure was US\$ 871.45 in 2013–14, the sixth-lowest in the country.[^cite_note-53] Between 1999 and 2008, the annualised growth rate of the state was very low: 3.5%.[^cite_note-lakshmi-54] Subsequently, the state's GDP growth rate has improved significantly, rising to 8% during 2010–11 and 12% during 2011–12.[^cite_note-TOI_2012_GDP_12pc-55]

Madhya Pradesh is also famous for honey production in district Morena.

The state has an [agrarian economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarian_economy).[^cite_note-TOI_2012_GDP_12pc-55] The major crops of Madhya Pradesh are wheat, soybean, gram, sugarcane, rice, maize, cotton, rapeseed, mustard and arhar.[^cite_note-IBEF_2011-56] [Minor Forest Produce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-timber_forest_product) (MFP), such as [tendu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diospyros_melanoxylon) leaves used to roll [beedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beedi), sal seed, teak seed, and lak also contribute to state's rural economy.



Woman harvesting wheat, Raisen district

Madhya Pradesh has 5 [Special Economic Zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Economic_Zones) (SEZs): 3 [IT/ITeS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology_in_India) (Indore, [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior)), 1 mineral-based ([Jabalpur](473219.md)) and 1 agro-based (Jabalpur). In October 2011, approval was given to 14 proposed SEZs, out of which 10 were IT/ITeS-based.[^cite_note-IBEF_2011-56] [Indore](365670.md) is the major commercial centre of the state. Because of the state's central location, a number of [consumer goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_goods) companies have established manufacturing bases in MP.[^cite_note-IBEF_2011-56]

The Industrial Belts of [Indore](365670.md)-[Dewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewas)-[Pithampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pithampur) and [Mandideep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandideep) (Bhopal) are the prominent Industrial Hubs of Madhya Pradesh. Numerous industrial units and factories are present there such as Mechanical and Industrial Parts (gears, cables), Chemical Production Units, Pharmaceutical Units, Food Processing & FMCG Industries, Textile Production, Food Grain Processing Units, Leather Industry and Sanitary Products etc.

Madhya Pradesh has natural white stone production in [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior), which is exported worldwide.

The state has the largest reserves of diamond and copper in India. Other major mineral reserves include those of coal, [coalbed methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalbed_methane), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese) and [dolomite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomite_\(mineral\)).[^cite_note-IBEF_2011-56]

Madhya Pradesh has six [Ordnance Factories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Factories), four of which are located at [Jabalpur](473219.md) ([Vehicle Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_Factory_Jabalpur), Grey Iron Foundry, [Gun Carriage Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_Carriage_Factory_Jabalpur), [Ordnance Factory Khamaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Factory_Khamaria)) and one each at Katni and Itarsi. The factories are run by the [Ordnance Factories Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Factories_Board), and manufacture a variety of products for the [Indian Armed Forces](7171338.md).

There are two major Security Press Units of [Security Printing and Minting Corporation of India Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Printing_and_Minting_Corporation_of_India) presently operating in the state. The first unit is situated in [Dewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewas). Also known as Bank Note Press (BNP). It is an industrial unit of SPMCIL, established in the year 1974 is wholly owned by [Government of India](553883.md), [Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(India\)) and [Department of Economic Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Economic_Affairs_\(India\)). BNP Dewas prints Indian Currency Notes (known as [Indian rupee](490783.md) or INR; Symbol: '₹') of denominations ₹50, ₹100, ₹500 and ₹2000 and is capable of printing Bank Notes of any denomination. There is also a specialised Security Ink Factory at BNP Dewas which supplies specialised inks for currency and other government approved documents.

The second unit is a Security Paper Mill was established in 1968 at [Hoshangabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoshangabad). It produces papers for banknotes and non–judicial stamps and further prints with new enhanced unit.

Madhya Pradesh won the 10th National Award for excellent work in Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005.

The state's tourism industry is growing, fuelled by wildlife tourism and a number of places of historical and religious significance. [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior), [Sanchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanchi) and [Khajuraho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khajuraho) are frequented by external tourists. Alongwith cities like [Bhedaghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhedaghat), [Jabalpur](473219.md), [Bhimbetka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimbetka), [Bhojpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpur,_Madhya_Pradesh), [Maheshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maheshwar), [Mandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandu,_Madhya_Pradesh), [Orchha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchha), [Pachmarhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachmarhi), [Kanha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanha_National_Park), [Amarkantak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarkantak) and [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain), Tumen Vindhyavasini temple ancient temple. The state has a total installed power generation capacity of 33352.64 MW as of 31 March 2026. The Madhya Pradesh Electric Board is located at [Jabalpur](473219.md). The [Rewa Ultra Mega Solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewa_Ultra_Mega_Solar) project is a photovoltaic solar park spread over an area of 1,590 acres (6.4 km<sup>2</sup>) in the Gurh tehsil of Rewa District of Madhya Pradesh.[^cite_note-58] The project was commissioned with 750 MW capacity.[^cite_note-59] 97% households have electricity access in the state.[^cite_note-60]

The Singrauli region on the eastern end of Madhya Pradesh is a major energy producer enclave for the country. The region has vast reserves of coal mines, which are excavated by Northern Coalfields Limited, a subsidiary of Coal India Limited, which is in turn used in local power plants of NTPC, Sasan Power and Hindalco. The area has more than 10,000 MW installed capacity for energy production.

The region of Malwa were selected to establish Wind Energy Production units by State Government. Constant wind flows in the region are suitable for harvesting wind energy. There are more than 100 wind mills on a series of hills 13 km (8.1 mi) from [Dewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewas), generating more than 30 megawatts of power. These were financed by a few private companies which sought a reliable power supply.

Bus and train services cover most of Madhya Pradesh. The 99,043-kilometre-long (61,542 mi) road network of the state includes 20 [national highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Highways_in_India).[^cite_note-IBEF_2011-56] A 4,948-kilometre-long (3,075 mi) rail network criss-crosses the state, with [Jabalpur](473219.md) serving as headquarters for the [West Central Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Central_Railway) Zone of the [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways). The [Central Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Railway_zone) and the [Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Railway_zone) also cover parts of the state. Most of the western Madhya Pradesh comes under [Ratlam Rail Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratlam_Rail_Division) of [Western Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Railways), including cities like Indore, Ujjain, Dewas, Mandsaur, Khandwa, [Neemuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neemuch) and Bairagarh in Bhopal. The state has a total of 20 major railway junctions. More than 455 trains transit through Madhya Pradesh daily. 220 trains transit through the State's capital Bhopal alone. North–South & East–West corridors cut across Madhya Pradesh, including the [New Delhi–Chennai main line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi%E2%80%93Chennai_main_line).

The [inter-state bus terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_State_Bus_Terminals) are located in [Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal), [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior), [Indore](365670.md) and [Jabalpur](473219.md). More than 2,000 buses are conducted daily from these four cities. The intra-city transit systems mostly consist of buses, private [autos](593765.md) and taxis. There is convenient access to major ports such as Kandla port and Jawaharlal Nehru port in the state[^cite_note-61] The state does not have a coastline, but is home to 5 functional ICD's (Inland/Dry Ports) which provide facilities to clear and load cargoes directly for dispatch within the state, they are located in Bhopal, Indore and Gwalior. Most of the sea trade happens through the [Kandla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandla) and [Jawaharlal Nehru Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru_Port) (Nhava Sheva) in the neighbouring states, which are well-connected to MP by road and rail networks.

[Devi Ahilyabai Holkar Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Ahilyabai_Holkar_Airport) in [Indore](365670.md) is the busiest airport in Madhya Pradesh, followed by [Raja Bhoj International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Bhoj_International_Airport) in the state capital of [Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhopal).

[Rajmata Vijayaraje Scindia Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior_Airport), [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior) is the largest and the oldest airport of Madhya Pradesh. It is one and only airport in central India having two parallel take off runways.

[Dumna Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumna_Airport) in [Jabalpur](473219.md) and [Khajuraho Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khajuraho_Airport) also have scheduled commercial passenger services. [Rewa](2815261.md) Airport is under construction with operations expected to start by 2025. Besides these, minor [airstrips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airstrip) are located at [Chhindwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhindwara), [Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhana_Airport), [Neemuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neemuch), [Ratlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratlam), [Mandsaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandsaur), [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain), [Khandwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khandwa_Airport), [Guna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guna,_India) and [Satna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satna_Airport).

## Government and politics

Main articles: [Government of Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Madhya_Pradesh) and [Legislative Assembly of Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Madhya_Pradesh)

See also: [List of Chief Ministers of Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chief_Ministers_of_Madhya_Pradesh) and [List of Governors of Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_Madhya_Pradesh)

Madhya Pradesh has a 230-seat state legislative assembly. The state also sends 40 members to the [Parliament of India](410238.md): 29 are elected to the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) (Lower House) and 11 to the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md) (Upper House). The constitutional head of the state is the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_Madhya_Pradesh), appointed by the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India). The executive powers lie with the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_\(India\)), who is the elected leader of the state legislature. The current governor is Mangubhai C. Patel, and the current chief minister is [Mohan Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohan_Yadav) of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP).

The political scenario of Madhya pradesh can be defines as below:

Post-Independence (1950s–1970s): The Indian National Congress (INC) held power for a significant period, with leaders like Ravishankar Shukla and Kailash Nath Katju as Chief Ministers. However, parties like Bharatiya Jana Sangh (BJS) – the precursor to BJP – started gaining ground in the 1960s.

Shifting Tides (1980s–1990s): This era saw a rise in regional parties and national alternatives. Janata Party (JP), Janata Dal (JD), and Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP) challenged the INC's dominance for a while.

Bi-polar Politics (2000s–present): Since the early 2000s, a two-party system has emerged. BJP and INC have become the main contenders, with occasional forays by smaller parties.

The dominant political parties in the state are the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP) and the [Indian National Congress](149333.md) (INC).

## Education

See also: [List of Engineering Colleges in Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Engineering_Colleges_in_Madhya_Pradesh)

According to the 2011 census, Madhya Pradesh had a literacy rate of 69.32%. According to the 2009–10 figures, the state had 105,592 primary schools, 6,352 high schools, and 5,161 higher secondary schools. The state has 208 engineering and architecture colleges, 208 management institutes, and 12 medical colleges.[^cite_note-IBEF_2011-56]

The state is home to some of the premier scientific educational and research institutions of India including [IIT Indore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIT_Indore), [IIM Indore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Management_Indore), [AIIMS Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Institute_of_Medical_Sciences), [NIT Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Azad_National_Institute_of_Technology), [IISER Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Science_Education_and_Research,_Bhopal), [SPA Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Planning_and_Architecture,_Bhopal), [IIIT Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Information_Technology,_Design_and_Manufacturing,_Jabalpur), [IIIT Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Information_Technology_and_Management,_Gwalior).

[Gajra Raja Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajra_Raja_Medical_College), [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior) is the first and the oldest medical college in Madhya Pradesh that was established in 1946.

Other premier institutes include [Indian Institute of Tourism and Travel Management (IITTM Gwalior)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Tourism_and_Travel_Management), [Indian Institute of Forest Management (IIFM) Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Forest_Management), [Institute of Hotel Management (IHM) Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Hotel_Management), [National Law Institute University (NLIU) Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Law_Institute_University), [Lakshmi Bai National Institute of Physical Education, (LNIPE) Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmibai_National_Institute_of_Physical_Education), [Jabalpur Engineering College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabalpur_Engineering_College), [Dharmashastra National Law University, Jabalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmashastra_National_Law_University,_Jabalpur) and [VIT University Bhopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vellore_Institute_of_Technology).

The state also has a [football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh_football_team) that participates in the [Santosh Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santosh_Trophy).

[MP United FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MP_United_FC_\(India\)) is an Indian football that played in the [2nd Division I-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-League_2).

On 6 December 2017, the Madhya Pradesh [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister) [Shivraj Singh Chouhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivraj_Singh_Chouhan) announced that players from the state would be given government jobs on winning medals in international events.

[Madhu Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhu_Yadav), the former Captain of the [India women's national field hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_women's_national_field_hockey_team), a 1982 [Asian Games](39205.md) gold medallist and an Arjuna Award recipient, is from [Jabalpur](473219.md).[^cite_note-84]
