**Baalbek**[^cite_note-5] ([/ˈbɑːlbɛk, ˈbeɪəlbɛk/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*BAHL-bek, BAY-əl-bek*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-6] [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بَعْلَبَكّ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Baʿlabakk*, [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Arabic): *Bʿalbak*; [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language): ܒܥܠܒܟ; [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_language): 𐤁𐤏𐤋𐤁𐤊, romanized: *bʿlbk*) is a city located east of the [Litani River](1920925.md) in [Lebanon](17771.md)'s [Beqaa Valley](2162385.md), about 67 km (42 mi) northeast of [Beirut](37428.md). It is the capital of [Baalbek-Hermel Governorate](53254600.md).[^cite_note-7] In 1998, the city had a population of 82,608.[^cite_note-\:11-8] Most of the population consists of [Shia Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'a_Islam_in_Lebanon), followed by [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam_in_Lebanon) and [Christians](4796145.md);[^cite_note-\:11-8] in 2017, there was also a large presence of [Syrian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_Syrian_civil_war).[^cite_note-9]

Baalbek's history dates back at least 11,000 years. After [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) conquered the city in 334 BCE, he renamed it **Heliopolis** ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ἡλιούπολις, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ancient_Greek): *Hēlioúpolis*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Sun City'; Syriac: ܐܝܠܝܘܦܘܠܝܣ, romanized: *ʾĪliyōp̄ōlīs*). The city flourished under Roman rule. However, it underwent transformations during the [Christianization period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography_of_the_Christianization_of_the_Roman_Empire) and the subsequent [rise of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam) following the [Arab conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Levant) in the [7th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_century_in_Lebanon). In later periods, the city was sacked by the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) and faced a series of earthquakes, resulting in a decline in importance during the [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Lebanon_under_Ottoman_rule) and modern periods.[^cite_note-\:102-10]

In the modern era, Baalbek is a tourist destination.[^cite_note-11] It is known for the ruins of the Roman temple complex, which includes the [Temple of Bacchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Bacchus) and the [Temple of Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_\(Baalbek\)), and was inscribed in 1984 as a [UNESCO World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_World_Heritage) site. Other tourist attractions are [the Great Umayyad Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Mosque_of_Baalbek), the [Baalbek International Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baalbeck_International_Festival), the mausoleum of Sit Khawla, and a [Roman quarry site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baalbek_Stones) named *Hajar al-Hibla*.[^cite_note-\:102-10] Baalbek's tourism sector has encountered challenges due to conflicts in Lebanon, particularly the [1975–1990 civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War), [spillover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_civil_war_spillover_in_Lebanon) from the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) between 2011 and 2017,[^cite_note-\:102-10][^cite_note-12] and the [Israel–Hezbollah conflict](75005425.md) since 2023.[^cite_note-13]

Baalbek is considered to be part of [Hezbollah](13919.md)'s heartland[^cite_note-14] and is known to be their political stronghold.[^cite_note-15] During the [Israeli occupation of Southern Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation_of_Southern_Lebanon_\(1982%E2%80%932000\)) (1982–2000), the group managed to overpower the Lebanese army in Baalbek and gain control of the city. The settlement was subsequently used as a base to recruit and train men for attacks against Israeli forces.[^cite_note-\:12-16][^cite_note-\:13-17][^cite_note-\:3-18] Hezbollah continues to hold significant political influence and popular support in Baalbek.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] In the [2022 Lebanese general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Lebanese_general_election) the [Amal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_Movement)-Hezbollah list won 9 out of 10 seats in the Baalbek-Hermel Governorate.[^cite_note-21]

Israel has conducted numerous airstrikes and raids against military and civilian targets in the Baalbek area in the past decades. For instance, in 2006 during the [Operation Sharp and Smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sharp_and_Smooth), Israeli commandos raided a hospital and bombed multiple houses, killing two Hezbollah fighters and at least eleven civilians.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-peda-23][^cite_note-time-24] In 2024, during the Israel–Hezbollah conflict, Israel sent [forced displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_displacement_during_the_Israel%E2%80%93Hezbollah_conflict) calls for the entire city.[^cite_note-\:4-25][^cite_note-\:5-26] Shortly after, Israeli airstrikes killed 19 people, including 8 women.[^cite_note-27]

## Etymology

A few kilometres from the swamp from which the [Litani](1920925.md) (the classical Leontes) and the [Asi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asi_River) (the upper [Orontes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontes_River)) flow, Baalbek may be the same as the *manbaa al-nahrayn* ("Source of the Two Rivers"), the abode of [El](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_\(god\)) in the [Ugaritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugaritic_religion) [Baal Cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baal_Cycle)[^cite_note-28] discovered in the 1920s and a separate serpent incantation.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESteiner2009-30]

Baalbek was called *Heliopolis* during the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), a [latinisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinization_of_names) of the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language) *Hēlioúpolis* (Ἡλιούπολις) used during the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period),[^cite_note-31] meaning "Sun City"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELendering2013-32] in reference to the [solar cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_worship) there. The name is attested under the [Seleucids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucids) and [Ptolemies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Egypt).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJidejian19755-33] However, [Ammianus Marcellinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus) notes that earlier [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language) names of [Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) towns continued to be used alongside the official [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language) ones imposed by the [Diadochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadochi), who were successors of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great).[^cite_note-34] In [Greek religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_religion), [Helios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helios) was both the [sun](26751.md) in the sky and its [personification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personification) as a [god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_god). The local [Semitic god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaanite_religion) [Baʿal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%CA%BFal) [Haddu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadad) was more often [equated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretatio_graeca) with [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus) or [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_\(mythology\)) or simply called the "Great God of Heliopolis",[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJidejian197557-35][^cite_note-37] but the name may refer to the Egyptians' association of [Baʿal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%CA%BFal) with their great god [Ra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJidejian19755-33][^cite_note-39] It was sometimes described as **Heliopolis in Syria** or **Coelesyria** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Heliopolis Syriaca* or *_Syriae_*) to distinguish it from [its namesake in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliopolis_\(Ancient_Egypt\)). In [Catholicism](606848.md), its [titular see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Catholic_titular_sees_\(B-K\)) is distinguished as **Heliopolis in Phoenicia**, from its former [Roman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) [Phoenice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenice_\(Roman_province\)). The importance of the solar cult is also attested in the name *Biḳāʿ al-ʿAzīz* borne by the plateau surrounding Baalbek, as it references an earlier solar deity named [Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aziz_\(disambiguation\)). In [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_Period) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) [antiquity](252905.md), it was known as *Heliopolis*. Some of the best-preserved Roman ruins in Lebanon are located here, including one of the largest temples of the Roman empire. The gods worshipped there ([Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_\(mythology\)), [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_\(mythology\)), and [Bacchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacchus)) were [equivalents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretatio_graeca) of the [Canaanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaanite_religion) deities [Hadad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadad), [Atargatis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atargatis). Local influences are seen in the planning and layout of the temples, which differ from classic Roman design. [^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40]

The name bʿlbk appears in the [Mishnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishnah), a second-century [rabbinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbinic) text, as a kind of [garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic), *shum ba'albeki* (שום בעלבכי).[^cite_note-41] It appears in two early 5th-century [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language) manuscripts, a c. 411[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10633_550\]-38] translation of [Eusebius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebius)'s *[Theophania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theophania_\(Eusebius\))*[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] and a c. 435[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBurkitt2004\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidvssdKNm9Hm4CpgPA51_51\]-44] [life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagiography) of [Rabbula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbula), [bishop of Edessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Edessa).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOverbeck1865\[httpsarchiveorgstreamsephraemisyrirab00overpage228mode2up_196\]-45][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10633_550\]-38] It was pronounced as **Baʿlabakk** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بَعْلَبَكّ) in [Classical Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Arabic).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArastu2014\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidMrxrBwAAQBAJpgPT616_616\]-46][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESteiner2009-30] In [Modern Standard Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Arabic), its [vowels are marked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_diacritics) as *Baʿlabak* (بَعْلَبَك)[^cite_note-alalc-47] or *Baʿlabekk*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913-48] It is *Bʿalbik* (بْعَلْبِك, is [\[ˈbʕalbik\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic)) in [Lebanese Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Arabic).[^cite_note-alalc-47]

The [etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) of Baalbek has been debated since the 18th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESteiner2009-30] [Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Bernard_Cook) took it to mean "[Baʿal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%CA%BFal) (Lord) of the [Beka](2162385.md)"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10633_550\]-38] and [Donne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bodham_Donne) as "City of the Sun".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''1878-49] [Lendering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jona_Lendering) asserts that it is probably a contraction of *Baʿal Nebeq* ("[Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%CA%BFal#Word) of the Source" of the [Litani River](1920925.md)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELendering2013-32] [Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_C._Steiner) proposes a [Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages) adaption of "[Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%CA%BFal#Word) Bacchus", from the classical temple complex.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESteiner2009-30]

Nineteenth-century [Biblical archaeologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_archaeology) proposed the association of Baalbek with the town of [Baalgad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baalgad) in the [Book of Joshua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Joshua); the town of [Baalath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baalath), one of [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon)'s cities in the [First Book of Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Book_of_Kings);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''New_Class._Dict.''1862-50] [Baal-hamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baal-hamon), where Solomon had a [vineyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyard);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3] and the "Plain of Aven" in [Book of Amos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Amos).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA468_468\]-51]

## History

### Prehistory

The hilltop of [Tell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_\(archaeology\)) Baalbek, part of a valley to the east of the northern [Beqaa Valley](2162385.md)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA453_453\]-52] ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *[Coele-Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coele-Syria)*),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1911-53] shows signs of almost continual habitation over the last 8–9000 years.[^cite_note-54] It was well-watered both from a stream running from the *Rās al-ʿAyn* [spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(water\)) southwest of the citadel[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA456_456\]-55] and, during the [spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(season\)), from numerous rills formed by meltwater from the [Anti-Lebanons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Lebanon_Mountains).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781036-56] [Macrobius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrobius) later credited the site's foundation to a colony of [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) or [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Empire) priests.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781036-56] The settlement's religious, commercial, and strategic importance was minor enough, however, that it is never mentioned in any known [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Empire) or [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) record,[^cite_note-57] unless under another name.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3] Its enviable position in a fertile valley, major watershed, and along the route from [Tyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Tyre) to [Palmyra](46709309.md) should have made it a wealthy and splendid site from an early age.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''New_Class._Dict.''1862-50] During the [Canaanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaanite_religion) period, the local temples were primarily devoted to the [Heliopolitan Triad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliopolitan_Triad): a male god ([Baʿal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%CA%BFal)), his consort ([Astarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astarte)), and their son ([Adon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammuz_\(god\))).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJidejian197547-58] The site of the present [Temple of Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Jupiter_\(Baalbek\)) was probably the focus of earlier worship, as its [altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar) was located at the hill's precise summit and the rest of the sanctuary raised to its level.

In [Islamic mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mythology), the temple complex was said to have been a palace of [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon)'s[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA470_470\]-59] which was put together by *[jinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinn)*[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVolney1787224-60][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781038-61][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA454_454\]-62] and given as a wedding gift to the [Queen of Sheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Sheba);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] its actual [Roman origin](#Antiquity) remained obscured by the citadel's [medieval fortifications](#Middle_Ages) as late as the 16th-century visit of the [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) prince [Radziwiłł](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miko%C5%82aj_Krzysztof_%22the_Orphan%22_Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''CT''2010-63][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERadziwiłł1601-64]

### Antiquity



Reconstruction of Ancient Roman temples in Heliopolis



Roman Heliopolis and its surroundings in the [2nd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_century_in_Lebanon) and the [3rd century.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_century_in_Lebanon)

After [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great)'s [conquest of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great's_conquest_of_Persia) in the 330s BC, Baalbek (under its Hellenic name [Heliopolis](#Name)) formed part of the [Diadochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadochi) kingdoms of [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Egypt) & [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Syria). It was annexed by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) during their eastern wars. The settlers of the [Roman colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_colony) **Colonia Julia Augusta Felix Heliopolitana** may have arrived as early as the time of [Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781036-56] but were more probably the [veterans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legionaries) of the [5th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legio_V_Alaudae) and [8th Legions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Legion) under [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''New_Class._Dict.''1862-50][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''191189-65][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10633_550\]-38] during which time it hosted a Roman garrison.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3] From 15 BC to AD 193, it formed part of the territory of [Berytus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berytus). It is mentioned in [Josephus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus),[^cite_note-66] [Pliny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder),[^cite_note-67] [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo),[^cite_note-68] and [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Ptolemy)[^cite_note-ptol-69] and on [coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_coinage) of nearly every [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roman_emperors) from [Nerva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerva) to [Gallienus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallienus).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3] The 1st-century Pliny did not number it among the [Decapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapolis), the "Ten Cities" of Coelesyria, while the 2nd-century Ptolemy did.[^cite_note-ptol-69] The population likely varied seasonally with market fairs and the schedules of the [Indian monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_monsoon) and [caravans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_\(travellers\)) to the coast and interior.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781037-70]



[Corinthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthian_order) [capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(column\)) [ornamenting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornament_\(art\)) the [columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column) of the [Temple of Bacchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Bacchus)

During [Classical Antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Antiquity), the city's [temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_temple) to [Baʿal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%CA%BFal) [Haddu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadad) [was conflated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretatio_graeca) first with the worship of the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_religion) [sun god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_god) [Helios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helios)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10633_550\]-38] and then with the Greek and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_religion) [sky god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_god) under the name "[Heliopolitan Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliopolitan_Zeus)" or "[Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliopolitan_Jupiter)". The present Temple of Jupiter presumably replaced an earlier one using the same foundation;[^cite_note-73] it was constructed during the mid-[1st century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_century_in_Lebanon) and probably completed around AD 60.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKropp_&_al.2011-76] His [idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idolatry) was a [beardless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouros) [golden](12240.md#Cultural_history) god in the pose of a [charioteer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot), with a [whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagellum) raised in his right hand and a [thunderbolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderbolt) and stalks of grain in his left;[^cite_note-79] its image appeared on local coinage and it was borne through the streets during several festivals throughout the year.[^cite_note-macrobe-77] [Macrobius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrobius) compared the rituals to those for [Diva Fortuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diva_Fortuna) at [Antium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antium) and says the bearers were the principal citizens of the town, who prepared for their role with abstinence, chastity, and shaved heads.[^cite_note-macrobe-77] In [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_sculpture) [statuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_sculpture) attested from [Byblos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byblos) in [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Phoenicia) and [Tortosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortosa) in [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Spain), he was encased in a pillarlike [term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_\(architecture\)) and surrounded (like the Greco-[Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Persian_religion) [Mithras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithras)) by busts representing the [sun, moon, and five known planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_astronomy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraves1990\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidClTmOEBNMhACpgPT40_40–41\]-80] In these statues, the bust of Mercury is made particularly prominent; a marble [stela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stela) at [Massilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massilia) in [Transalpine Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transalpine_Gaul) shows a similar arrangement but enlarges Mercury into a full figure.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraves1990\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidClTmOEBNMhACpgPT40_40–41\]-80] Local cults also revered the [Baetylia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baetylus), black conical stones considered sacred to [Baʿal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%CA%BFal).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781037-70] One of these was taken to Rome by the emperor [Elagabalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elagabalus), a former priest "of the sun" at nearby [Emesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emesa),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA471_471\]-81] who erected a temple for it on the [Palatine Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_Hill).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781037-70] Heliopolis was a noted [oracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle) and [pilgrimage](23763.md) site, whence the cult spread far afield, with inscriptions to the Heliopolitan god discovered in [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Athens), [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Pannonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia), [Venetia](43780.md), [Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaul), and near the [Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian's_Wall) in [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10635_552\]-78] The Roman temple complex grew up from the early part of the reign of [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) in the late 1st century BC until the [rise of Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_the_Roman_Empire) in the [4th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_century_in_Lebanon). (The 6th-century chronicles of [John Malalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Malalas) of [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch), which claimed Baalbek as a "[wonder of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonders_of_the_ancient_world)",[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA471_471\]-81] credited most of the complex to the 2nd-century [Antoninus Pius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoninus_Pius), but it is uncertain how reliable his account is on the point.)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''CT''2010-63] By that time, the complex housed three temples on Tell Baalbek: one to [Jupiter Heliopolitanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_Optimus_Maximus_Heliopolitanus) (Baʿal), one to [Venus Heliopolitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_Heliopolitana) (Ashtart), and a third to [Bacchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacchus). On a nearby hill, a fourth temple was dedicated to the third figure of the [Heliopolitan Triad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliopolitan_Triad), [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_\(mythology\)) (Adon or Seimios[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10637_554\]-82]). Ultimately, the site vied with [Praeneste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praeneste) in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Italy) as the two largest sanctuaries in the Western world.

The emperor [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) consulted the site's [oracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle) twice. The first time, he requested a written reply to his sealed and unopened question; he was favorably impressed by the god's blank reply as his own paper had been empty.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10635_552–553\]-83] He then inquired whether he would return alive from his wars against [Parthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia) and received in reply a [centurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centurion)'s [vine staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine_staff), broken to pieces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10636_553\]-84] In AD 193, [Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septimius_Severus) granted the city *[ius Italicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ius_Italicum)* rights.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] His wife [Julia Domna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Domna) and son [Caracalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracalla) toured [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Egypt) and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Syria) in AD 215; inscriptions in their honour at the site may date from that occasion; Julia was a Syrian native whose father had been an [Emesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emesa) priest "of the sun" like [Elagabalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elagabalus).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA471_471\]-81]

The town became a battleground upon the [rise of Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_the_Roman_Empire).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10637_554\]-82][^cite_note-89] Early Christian writers such as [Eusebius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea) (from nearby [Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima)) repeatedly execrated the practices of the local pagans in their worship of the Heliopolitan Venus. In AD 297, the actor [Gelasinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelasinus) converted in the middle of a scene mocking [baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism); his public profession of faith provoked the audience to drag him from the theater and [stone him to death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoning).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10637_554\]-82][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3] In the early 4th century, the deacon Cyril defaced many of the [idols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idolatry) in Heliopolis; he was killed and (allegedly) [cannibalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_cannibalism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10637_554\]-82] Around the same time, [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great), though not yet a Christian, demolished the goddess' temple, raised a basilica in its place, and outlawed the locals' ancient custom of prostituting women before marriage.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10637_554\]-82] [Bar Hebraeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Hebraeus) also credited him with ending the locals' continued practice of [polygamy](24475.md).[^cite_note-90] The enraged locals responded by raping and torturing Christian virgins.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10637_554\]-82] They reacted violently again under the freedom permitted to them by [Julian the Apostate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3] The city was so noted for its hostility to the Christians that [Alexandrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria) were banished to it as a special punishment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3] The Temple of Jupiter, already greatly damaged by earthquakes,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10639_556\]-91] was demolished under [Theodosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I) in 379 and replaced by another basilica (now lost), using stones scavenged from the pagan complex.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10638_555\]-92] The *[Easter Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Chronicle)* states he was also responsible for destroying all the lesser temples and shrines of the city.[^cite_note-93] Around the year 400, [Rabbula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbula), the future [bishop of Edessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Edessa), attempted to have himself martyred by disrupting the pagans of Baalbek but was only thrown down the temple stairs along with his companion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10638_555\]-92] It became the seat of its own bishop as well.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878176-3] Under the reign of [Justinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I), eight of the complex's [Corinthian columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthian_order) were disassembled and shipped to [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) for incorporation in the rebuilt [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia) sometime between 532 and 537. [Michael the Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_the_Syrian) claimed the golden idol of Heliopolitan Jupiter was still to be seen during the reign of [Justin II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_II) (560s & 570s),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10638_555\]-92] and, up to the time of its conquest by the Muslims, it was renowned for its palaces, monuments, and gardens.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878177-94]

### Middle Ages and early modernity



The ruins of the [Umayyad Mosque of Baalbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Mosque_of_Baalbek) c. 1900, prior to its reconstruction&#x20;



The probable remains of a medieval [mosque](19894.md) in front of some of the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Egypt) fortifications

Baalbek was occupied by the [Muslim army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_army) in AD 634 ([ah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH_\(era\)) 13),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10638_555\]-92] in 636,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA473_473\]-36] or under [Abu ʿUbaidah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_%CA%BFUbaidah) following the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) [defeat at Yarmouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Yarmouk) in 637 ([ah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH_\(era\)) 16), either peacefully and by agreement[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] or following a heroic defense and yielding 2,000 oz (57 kg) of gold, 4,000 oz (110 kg) of silver, 2000 silk vests, and 1000 swords.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878177-94] The ruined temple complex was fortified under the name **al-Qala'** (lit. "[The Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_\(surname\))")[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10638_555\]-92] but was sacked with great violence by the Damascene caliph [Marwan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwan_II) in 748, at which time it was dismantled and largely depopulated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878177-94] It formed part of the district of Damascus under the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_dynasty) and [Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_dynasty) before being conquered by [Fatimid Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Egypt) in 942.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] In the [mid-10th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_century_in_Lebanon), it was said to have "gates of palaces sculptured in marble and lofty columns also of marble" and that it was the most "stupendous" and "considerable" location in the whole of Syria.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA473_473\]-36] It was sacked and razed by the Byzantines under [John I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_I_Tzimiskes) in 974,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] raided by [Basil II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_II_the_Bulgarkiller) in 1000,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''CMH''1966\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksid9lHeh36S8ooCpgPT634_634\]-95] and occupied by [Salih ibn Mirdas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salih_ibn_Mirdas), [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) of [Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Aleppo), in 1025.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40]

In 1075, it was finally lost to the Fatimids on its conquest by [Tutush I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutush_I), [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Turks) emir of [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Damascus#Seljuq_emirs).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] It was briefly held by [Muslim ibn Quraysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_ibn_Quraysh), emir of [Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Aleppo), in 1083; after its recovery, it was ruled in the Seljuks' name by the eunuch [Gümüshtegin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCm%C3%BCshtegin) until he was deposed for conspiring against the usurper [Toghtekin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toghtekin) in 1110.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] Toghtekin then gave the town to his son [Buri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_al-Muluk_Buri). Upon Buri's succession to Damascus on his father's death in 1128, he granted the area to his son [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_al-Din_Muhammad).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] After Buri's murder, Muhammad successfully defended himself against the attacks of his brothers [Ismaʿil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shams-ul-Mulk_Isma'il) and [Mahmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shihab-ud-din_Mahmud?action=edit\&redlink=1) and gave Baalbek to his [vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizier) [Unur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu'in_ad-Din_Unur).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] In July 1139, [Zengi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imad_ad-Din_Zengi), atabeg of [Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atabegate_of_Aleppo?action=edit\&redlink=1) and stepfather of Mahmud, besieged Baalbek with 14 catapults. The outer city held until 10 October and the citadel until the 21st,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVenning_&_al.2015\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidubflCAAAQBAJpgPA109_109\]-96] when Unur surrendered upon a promise of safe passage.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1936\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA1225_1225\]-97] In December, Zengi negotiated with Muhammad, offering to trade Baalbek or [Homs](77533.md) for Damascus, but Unur convinced the atabeg to refuse.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVenning_&_al.2015\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidubflCAAAQBAJpgPA109_109\]-96] Zengi strengthened its fortifications and bestowed the territory on his lieutenant [Ayyub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najm_ad-Din_Ayyub), father of [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin). Upon Zengi's assassination in 1146, Ayyub surrendered the territory to Unur, who was acting as regent for Muhammad's son [Abaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujir_ad-Din_Abaq). It was granted to the eunuch [Ata al-Khadim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ata_al-Khadim?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] who also served as viceroy of Damascus.

In December 1151, it was raided by the garrison of [Banyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banias) as a reprisal for its role in a Turcoman raid on Banyas.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVenning_&_al.2015\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidubflCAAAQBAJpgPA138_138\]-98] Following Ata's murder, his nephew [Dahhak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahhak_ibn_Jandal?action=edit\&redlink=1), emir of the [Wadi al-Taym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_al-Taym), ruled Baalbek. He was forced to relinquish it to [Nur ad-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_ad-Din,_atabeg_of_Aleppo) in 1154[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] after Ayyub had successfully intrigued against Abaq from his estates near Baalbek. Ayyub then administered the area from Damascus on Nur ad-Din's behalf.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVenning_&_al.2015\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidubflCAAAQBAJpgPA141_141–142\]-99] In the mid-12th century, [Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi) mentioned Baalbek's two temples and the legend of their origin under Solomon;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA475_475–476\]-100] it was visited by the [Jewish](15624.md) traveler [Benjamin of Tudela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_of_Tudela) in 1170.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''CT''2010-63]

Baalbek's citadel served as a jail for [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) taken by the Zengids as [prisoners of war](25008.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlouf1999\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidqiyY1CKE9SICpgPA94_94\]-101] In 1171, these captives successfully overpowered their guards and took possession of the castle from its garrison. Muslims from the surrounding area gathered, however, and entered the castle through a secret passageway shown to them by a local. The Crusaders were then massacred.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlouf1999\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidqiyY1CKE9SICpgPA94_94\]-101]

Three major earthquakes occurred in the 12th century, in 1139, 1157, and 1170.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878177-94] The one in 1170 ruined Baalbek's walls and, though Nur ad-Din repaired them, his young heir [Ismaʿil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Salih_Ismail_al-Malik) was made to yield it to [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin) by a 4-month siege in 1174.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] Having taken control of Damascus on the invitation of its governor [Ibn al-Muqaddam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shams_al-Din_Muhammad_ibn_al-Muqaddam), Saladin rewarded him with the **emirate of Baalbek** following the [Ayyubid victory at the Horns of Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Horns_of_Hama) in 1175.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHumphreys1977\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidJfXl5kvabhoCpgPA52_52\]-102] [Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin_IV_of_Jerusalem), the young leper [king of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Jerusalem), came of age the next year, ending the Crusaders' treaty with Saladin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELock2013\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidAkCKZ9Hs4-QCpgPA63_63\]-103] His former regent, [Raymond of Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_III_of_Tripoli), raided the [Beqaa Valley](2162385.md) from the west in the summer, suffering a slight defeat at Ibn al-Muqaddam's hands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERunciman1987\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidQL88AAAAIAAJpgPA410_410\]-104] He was then joined by the main army, riding north under Baldwin and [Humphrey of Toron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_II_of_Toron);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERunciman1987\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidQL88AAAAIAAJpgPA410_410\]-104] they defeated Saladin's elder brother [Turan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turan_Shah) in August at [Ayn al-Jarr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayn_al-Jarr) and plundered Baalbek.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlouf1999\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidqiyY1CKE9SICpgPA94_94\]-101] Upon the deposition of [Turan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turan_Shah) for neglecting his duties in Damascus, however, he demanded his childhood home[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESato1997\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidMycQL-9_bqwCpgPA57_57\]-105] of Baalbek as compensation. Ibn al-Muqaddam did not consent and Saladin opted to invest the city in late 1178 to maintain peace within his own family.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaldwin1969\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidRfO1J6hjcdgCpgPA572_572\]-106] An attempt to pledge fealty to the Christians at [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) was ignored on behalf of an existing treaty with Saladin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKöhler2013\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidtYG0AQAAQBAJpgPA226_226\]-107] The siege was maintained peacefully through the snows of winter, with Saladin waiting for the "foolish" commander and his garrison of "ignorant scum" to come to terms.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyons_&_al.1982\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidkRF1F3wK26YCpgPA132_132–133\]-108] Sometime in spring, Ibn al-Muqaddam yielded and Saladin accepted his terms, granting him [Baʿrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%CA%BFrin), [Kafr Tab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafartab), and [al-Maʿarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ma%CA%BFarra).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyons_&_al.1982\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidkRF1F3wK26YCpgPA132_132–133\]-108][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESato1997\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidMycQL-9_bqwCpgPA58_58\]-109] The generosity quieted unrest among Saladin's vassals through the rest of his reign[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaldwin1969\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidRfO1J6hjcdgCpgPA572_572\]-106] but led his enemies to attempt to take advantage of his presumed weakness.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyons_&_al.1982\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidkRF1F3wK26YCpgPA132_132–133\]-108] He did not permit Turan Shah to retain Baalbek very long, though, instructing him to lead the Egyptian troops returning home in 1179 and appointing him to a sinecure in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Alexandria).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHumphreys1977\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidJfXl5kvabhoCpgPA52_52\]-102] Baalbek was then granted to his nephew [Farrukh Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrukh_Shah), whose family ruled it for the next half-century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHumphreys1977\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidJfXl5kvabhoCpgPA52_52\]-102] When Farrukh Shah died three years later, his son [Bahram Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahram_Shah) was only a child but he was permitted his inheritance and ruled til 1230.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] He was followed by [al-Ashraf Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ashraf_Musa,_Emir_of_Damascus), who was succeeded by his brother [as-Salih Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Salih_Ismail_\(Ayyubid_emir\)),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] who received it in 1237 as compensation for being deprived of [Damascus](8914.md) by their brother [al-Kamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Kamil).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVenning_&_al.2015\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidubflCAAAQBAJpgPA299_299\]-110] It was seized in 1246 after a year of assaults by [as-Salih Ayyub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Salih_Ayyub), who bestowed it upon [Saʿd al-Din al-Humaidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%CA%BFd_al-Din_al-Humaidi).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] When as-Salih Ayyub's successor [Turan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Muazzam_Turanshah) was murdered in 1250, [al-Nasir Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nasir_Yusuf), the [sultan of Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Aleppo), seized Damascus and demanded Baalbek's surrender. Instead, its emir did homage and agreed to regular payments of tribute.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40]

The [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Empire) general [Kitbuqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitbuqa) took Baalbek in 1260 and dismantled its fortifications. Later in the same year, however, [Qutuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutuz), the [sultan of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Egypt), defeated the Mongols and placed Baalbek under the rule of their emir in Damascus.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] Most of the city's still-extant fine mosque and fortress architecture dates to the reign of the sultan [Qalawun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalawun) in the 1280s. By the early 14th century, [Abulfeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abulfeda) the [Hamathite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama) was describing the city's "large and strong fortress".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA476_476\]-111] The revived settlement was again destroyed by a flood on 10 May 1318, when water from the east and northeast made holes 30 m (98 ft) wide in walls 4 m (13 ft) thick.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlouf1999\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidqiyY1CKE9SICpgPA96_96\]-112] 194 people were killed and 1500 houses, 131 shops, 44 orchards, 17 ovens, 11 mills, and 4 aqueducts were ruined, along with the town's mosque and 13 other religious and educational buildings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlouf1999\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidqiyY1CKE9SICpgPA96_96\]-112] In 1400, [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) pillaged the town,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEle_Strange1890xxiii-113] and there was further destruction from a 1459 earthquake.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114]

### Early modernity



Baalbek in 1757



Inside the historical [Hotel Palmyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Palmyra_\(Baalbek\)), which opened in 1878

In 1516, Baalbek was conquered with the [rest of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria) by the [Ottoman](22278.md) sultan [Selim the Grim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selim_the_Grim).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] In recognition of their prominence among the [Shiites](26961.md) of the [Beqaa Valley](2162385.md), the Ottomans awarded the [sanjak of Homs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_of_Homs) and local *[iltizam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iltizam)* concessions to Baalbek's [Harfush family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harfush_clan). Like the Hamadas, the Harfush emirs were involved on more than one occasion in the selection of Church officials and the running of local monasteries.\
&#x20;Tradition holds that many Christians quit the Baalbek region in the eighteenth century for the newer, more secure town of [Zahlé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahl%C3%A9) on account of the Harfushes' oppression and rapacity, but more critical studies have questioned this interpretation, pointing out that the Harfushes were closely allied to the Orthodox Ma'luf family of Zahlé (where indeed Mustafa Harfush took refuge some years later) and showing that depredations from various quarters as well as Zahlé's growing commercial attractiveness accounted for Baalbek's decline in the eighteenth century. What repression there was did not always target the Christian community per se. The Shiite 'Usayran family, for example, is also said to have left Baalbek in this period to avoid expropriation by the Harfushes, establishing itself as one of the premier commercial households of [Sidon](28366.md) and later even serving as consuls of Iran.[^cite_note-115]

From the 16th century, European [tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_tourism) began to visit the colossal and picturesque ruins.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10639_556\]-91][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878178-116][^cite_note-140] [Donne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bodham_Donne) hyperbolised "No ruins of antiquity have attracted more attention than those of Heliopolis, or been more frequently or accurately measured and described."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781037-70] Misunderstanding the temple of Bacchus as the "Temple of the Sun", they considered it the best-preserved [Roman temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_temple) in the world. The Englishman [Robert Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wood_\(antiquarian\))'s 1757 *Ruins of Balbec*[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1757-2] included carefully measured engravings that proved influential on British and Continental [Neoclassical architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture). For example, details of the Temple of Bacchus's ceiling inspired a bed[^cite_note-141] and [ceiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osterley_House) by [Robert Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Adam) and its portico inspired that of [St George's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George's,_Bloomsbury) in [Bloomsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomsbury).[^cite_note-142]

During the 18th century, the western approaches were covered with attractive groves of [walnut trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walnut_tree),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVolney1787224-60] but the town itself suffered badly during the [1759 earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East_earthquakes_of_1759), after which it was held by the [Metawali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metawali), who again feuded with other Lebanese tribes. Their power was broken by [Jezzar Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezzar_Pasha), the rebel governor of [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_Province_\(Ottoman_Empire\)), in the last half of the 18th century. All the same, Baalbek remained no destination for a traveller unaccompanied by an armed guard. Upon the pasha's death in 1804, chaos ensued until [Ibrahim Pasha of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Pasha_of_Egypt) occupied the area in 1831, after which it again passed into the hands of the Harfushes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] In 1835, the town's population was barely 200 people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAddison1838-133] In 1850, the Ottomans finally began direct administration of the area, making Baalbek a [kaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaza) under the [Damascus Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus_Eyalet) and its governor a [kaymakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymakam).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114]

### Excavations



[The largest stone at Baalbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_of_the_Pregnant_Woman#Second_monolith), uncovered in 2014

[Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Germany) [Wilhelm II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_II,_German_Emperor) of [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) and his wife passed through Baalbek on 1 November 1898,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10639_556\]-91] on their way to Jerusalem. He noted both the magnificence of the Roman remains and the drab condition of the modern settlement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10639_556\]-91] It was expected at the time that natural disasters, winter frosts, and the raiding of building materials by the city's residents would shortly ruin the remaining ruins.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA476_476\]-111] The archaeological team he dispatched began work within a month. Despite finding nothing they could date prior to Baalbek's [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Romans) occupation,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWiegand-143] [Otto Puchstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Puchstein) and his associates worked until 1904[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10639_556\]-91] and produced a meticulously researched and thoroughly illustrated series of volumes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWiegand-143] Later excavations under the Roman flagstones in the Great Court unearthed three [skeletons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletons) and a fragment of [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) pottery dated to the 6th–4th centuries BC. The [sherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherd) featured [cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform) letters.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJidejian197515-144]

In 1977, [Jean-Pierre Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Adam) made a brief study suggesting most of the large blocks could have been moved on rollers with [machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine) using [capstans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capstan_\(nautical\)) and [pulley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulley) blocks, a process which he theorised could use 512 workers to move a 557 tonnes (614 tons) block.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdam_&_al.199935-145][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdam1977-146] "Baalbek, with its colossal structures, is one of the finest examples of Imperial Roman architecture at its apogee", [UNESCO](21786641.md) reported in making Baalbek a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 1984.[^cite_note-147] When the committee inscribed the site, it expressed the wish that the protected area include the entire town within the Arab walls, as well as the southwestern extramural quarter between Bastan-al-Khan, the Roman site and the Mameluk mosque of Ras-al-Ain. Lebanon's representative gave assurances that the committee's wish would be honoured. Recent cleaning operations at the Temple of Jupiter discovered the deep trench at its edge, whose study pushed back the date of Tell Baalbek's settlement to the [PPNB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPNB) [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic). Finds included pottery [sherds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherd) including a spout dating to the early [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGenz2010-148] In the summer of 2014, a team from the [German Archaeological Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Archaeological_Institute) led by Jeanine Abdul Massih of the [Lebanese University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_University) discovered a sixth, much larger stone suggested to be the [world's largest ancient block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_largest_ancient_block#Monoliths). The stone was found underneath and next to the Stone of the Pregnant Woman ("Hajjar al-Hibla") and measures around 19.6 m × 6 m × 5.5 m (64 ft × 20 ft × 18 ft). It is estimated to weigh [^cite_note-\:9-149]1,650 tonnes (1,820 tons).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKehrer2014-150]

### Modern history



Baalbek Old souk

Baalbek was connected to the [DHP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut%E2%80%93Damascus_Railway), the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_French_Republic)-owned railway concession in [Ottoman Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria), on 19 June 1902.[^cite_note-back-151] It formed a station on the [standard-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge) line between [Riyaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyaq) to its south and [Aleppo](159244.md) (now in [Syria](7515849.md)) to its north.[^cite_note-riho-152] This Aleppo Railway connected to the [Beirut–Damascus Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut%E2%80%93Damascus_Railway) but—because that line was built to a 1.05-meter [gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_gauge)—all traffic had to be unloaded and reloaded at Riyaq.[^cite_note-riho-152] Just before the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), the population was still around 5000, about 2000 each of [Sunnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) and [Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiites) [Mutawalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims#Shia_Twelvers_\(Metouali\))[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] and 1000 [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church) and [Maronites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronites).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''191189-65] The [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_France) general [Georges Catroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Catroux) proclaimed the [independence of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Lebanon) in 1941 but colonial rule continued until 1943. Baalbek still has its railway station[^cite_note-riho-152] but service has been discontinued since the 1970s, originally owing to the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War).

In March 1974, [Musa al-Sadr](2432466.md) announced the launching of the "[Movement of the Deprived](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_Movement#Harakat_al-Mahrumin_/Movement_of_the_Deprived)" in front of a rally in Baalbek attended by 75,000 men. Its objective was to stand up for Lebanon's neglected [Shia](26961.md) community. In his speech he listed grievances with the Lebanese government, including Baalbek's lack of a secondary school, Shia communities receiving disproportionately less funds from the national budget, most of the homeless being Shia etc. The occasion was a religious one, and there was celebratory gunfire preceding it.[^cite_note-153] He also announced the setting up of military training camps to train villagers in southern Lebanon to protect their homes from Israeli attacks. These camps led to the creation of the [Amal Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_Militia).[^cite_note-154]

By the 2000s, Baalbek was frequently described as a stronghold of Hezbollah,[^cite_note-\:12-16][^cite_note-\:13-17][^cite_note-\:3-18] although it also has a large presence of Christians and Sunni Muslims.[^cite_note-\:6-155][^cite_note-\:9-149] Many of the city's mayors have been Shia, but since 2022, Baalbek has had a Sunni mayor.[^cite_note-\:9-149]

### Israeli–Lebanon conflict

In 1978, Israel invaded south Lebanon, forcing hundreds of thousands of Shia Muslims to flee.[^cite_note-156] As a result of the invasion, when [Hassan Nasrallah](313436.md) returned to Lebanon from his studies in [Najaf](200512.md), he was unable to return to his [hometown in the south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazouriyeh), and instead settled in Baalbek.[^cite_note-\:10-157] [Abbas al-Musawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_al-Musawi) also moved to Baalbek.[^cite_note-\:10-157]

In 1982, at the height of another [Israeli invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War), Amal split into two factions over [Nabih Berri](592775.md)'s acceptance of the American plan to evacuate Palestinians from West Beirut. A large number of dissidents, led by Amal's military commander [Hussein Musawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Musawi) moved to Baalbek.[^cite_note-158] Once established in the town the group, which was to evolve into Hezbollah, began to work with Iranian [Revolutionary Guards](1285472.md), veterans of the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War). The following year the Iranians established their headquarters in the Sheikh Abdullah barracks in Baalbek.[^cite_note-159] Ultimately there were between 1,500 and 2,000 Revolutionary Guards in Lebanon,[^cite_note-160] with outposts further south in the [Shia](26961.md) villages, such as [Jebchit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebchit).[^cite_note-161]

In 1984, Israel launched raids on Baalbek, killing 100 Lebanese civilians and wounding hundreds inside the city.[^cite_note-\:0-162] Israel also bombed a school in the [Wavel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavel_refugee_camp) Palestinian refugee camp next to the city, wounding 150 school children.[^cite_note-\:0-162]

On 24/25 June 1999, following elections in Israel and the new administration undecided, the [IAF](177619.md) launched two massive air raids across Lebanon. One of the targets was the [al Manar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Manar) radio station's offices in a four-storey building in Baalbek which was completely demolished. The attacks also hit Beirut's power stations and bridges on the roads to the south. An estimated \$52 million damage was caused. Eleven Lebanese were killed as well as two Israelis in [Kiryat Shmona](323369.md).[^cite_note-163]



A map of Israeli bombing during the [Second Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Lebanon_War). Baalbek was a major target, with more than 70 bombs dropped.

#### 2006 Lebanon War

During the 2006 Lebanon War, many Israeli bombs fell inside the historic Roman town, and some fell as close as 300 meters from the temple of Baalbek.[^cite_note-164] After the war, UNESCO stated that the cracks in the Roman temples had widened.[^cite_note-165] The damage was thought to be due to shockwaves created by the bombs.[^cite_note-\:1-166]

More than 250 houses and buildings were destroyed by Israel during the war.[^cite_note-\:6-155] Reporters investigating the aftermath found these to be civilian houses and didn't find any evidence of military use.[^cite_note-\:6-155]

On the evening of 1 August 2006,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHRW2007124-167] hundreds of [Israel Defense Forces (IDF)](39237.md) soldiers raided Baalbek and the Dar al-Hikma[^cite_note-timetimetime-168] or Hikmeh Hospital in Jamaliyeh[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHRW2007124-167] to its north ("[Operation Sharp and Smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sharp_and_Smooth)"). Their mission was to rescue two captured soldiers, [Ehud Goldwasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehud_Goldwasser) and [Eldad Regev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldad_Regev), who were abducted by Hezbollah on 12 July 2006. They were transported by helicopter[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHRW2007124-167] and supported by [Apache helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_helicopter) and [unmanned drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAV),[^cite_note-timetimetime-168][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHRW2007124-167] The [IDF](39237.md) was acting on information that Goldwasser and Regev were at the hospital. [al-Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic) and other sources claimed the IDF was attempting to capture senior Hezbollah officials, particularly Sheikh [Mohammad Yazbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Yazbek). The hospital had been empty for four days, the most unwell patients having been transferred and the rest sent home.[^cite_note-timetimetime-168]

On August 7, Israel killed 9 civilians in an attack on [Brital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brital), just south of Baalbek, and by the subsequent attack on the car leaving the scene for the hospital.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHRW2007137-169] A [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) investigation showed that none of those killed were combatants, it is likely none were members of Hezbollah, and none of the survivors had any knowledge of any military presence at place of the attack.[^cite_note-170]

On 14 August just before the ceasefire took effect, two Lebanese police and five Lebanese soldiers were killed by a drone strike while driving their van around the still-damaged road through Jamaliyeh.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHRW2007164–165-171]

## Geography

### Climate

Baalbek has a [mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*) with significant continental influences. It is located in one of the drier regions of the country, giving it an annual average of 450 millimetres or 18 inches of rainfall compared with 800 to 850 millimetres (31 to 33 in) in coastal areas, overwhelmingly concentrated in the months from November to April. Baalbek has hot rainless summers with cool (and occasionally snowy) winters. Autumn and spring are mild and fairly rainy.

## Economy

### Tourism

The Roman ruins have been the setting for the long running [Baalbek International Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baalbeck_International_Festival).

Baalbek's tourism sector suffered significantly due to the [Lebanese Civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) (1975–1990). After the civil war ended, tourism gradually saw a resurgence, including opera, orchestras.[^cite_note-\:8-174] But it was once again disrupted by Israeli bombings of Baalbek during the 2006 war.[^cite_note-\:8-174]

After the 2006 war, conservation work at Lebanon's historic sites began in October that year.[^cite_note-wapo-175] The ruins at Baalbek were not directly hit by Israeli bombing but the effects of blasts during the conflict toppled a block of stones at the Roman ruins and existing cracks in the temples of Jupiter and Bacchus were feared to have widened.[^cite_note-wapo-175] Frederique Husseini, director-general of Lebanon's Department of Antiquities, requested \$550,000 from Europeans to restore Baalbek's souk and another \$900,000 for repairs to other damaged structures.[^cite_note-wapo-175]

Starting in the early 2000s, Hezbollah organized permanent or temporary exhibitions called "Exposition of the Resistance" which commemorate what is considered to be Lebanese resistance to Israeli occupation. Spanning several hundred square meters, the exhibitions feature defused Israeli weapons, recreated scenes of war, and photos and videos of Lebanese people killed by Israel.[^cite_note-\:7-176][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaher2019119-177] Starting in 2009, Hezbollah setup an exhibition to commemorate the 2006 war.[^cite_note-\:7-176]

During the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), the UK [FCO](59653.md) designated areas close to the Syrian border, including Baalbek and the rest of Beqaa valley, as "red zone" – advising against all travel.[^cite_note-\:2-178] The US State Department made a similar designation. These designations, prompted by perceived proximity to Syria and clashes between [Sunnis and Alawites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_al-Tabbaneh%E2%80%93Jabal_Mohsen_conflict), discouraged international tourism.[^cite_note-\:2-178]

During [its 2024 invasion of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon), Israel bombed a restaurant frequented by tourists[^cite_note-\:9-149] and damaged the historic [Hotel Palmyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Palmyra_\(Baalbek\)).[^cite_note-179] In November, [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists) gave Baalbek enhanced protection to safeguard against [damage to the archaeological site during the invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_cultural_heritage_during_the_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon); it was one of 34 areas to receive this protection.[^cite_note-180]

## Ruins

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''191190-181]

&#x20;(Facing SW, with the Temple of Jupiter labelled "Temple of the Sun")

The Tell Baalbek temple complex, fortified as the town's citadel during the Middle Ages,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] was constructed from local stone, mostly white [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) and a rough white [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781038-61] Over the years, it has suffered from the region's numerous earthquakes, the [iconoclasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconoclasm) of Christian and Muslim lords,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781037-70] and the reuse of the temples' stone for fortification and other construction. The temples also suffered minor damage from the shockwaves generated by nearby Israeli bombings in the 2006 Lebanon war.[^cite_note-\:1-166] The nearby [Qubbat Duris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubbat_Duris), a 13th-century [Muslim shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubba) on the old road to Damascus, is built out of granite columns, apparently removed from Baalbek.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781038-61] Further, the jointed columns were once banded together with iron; many were gouged open[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA459_459\]-182] or toppled by the emirs of Damascus to get at the metal.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781038-61] As late as the 16th century, the Temple of Jupiter still held 27 standing columns[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThevet1554-120] out of an original 58;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10643_560\]-183] there were only nine before the [1759 earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East_earthquakes_of_1759)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1757-2] and six today.

The complex is located on a raised plaza erected 5 m (16 ft) over an earlier T-shaped base consisting of a podium, staircase, and foundation walls.[^cite_note-184] These walls were built from about 24 [monoliths](318257.md), at their lowest level weighing approximately 300 tonnes (330 tons) each. The tallest retaining wall, on the west, has a second course of monoliths containing the famous "[Three Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilithon)" ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Τρίλιθον, *[Trílithon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilithon)*):[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA456_456\]-55] a row of three stones, each over 19 m (62 ft) long, 4.3 m (14 ft) high, and 3.6 m (12 ft) broad, cut from [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone). They weigh approximately 800 tonnes (880 tons) each.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdam197752-185] A fourth, still larger stone is called the [Stone of the Pregnant Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_of_the_Pregnant_Woman): it lies unused in a nearby quarry 800 m (2,600 ft) from the town.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlouf1999139-186] Its weight is estimated at 1,000 tonnes (1,100 tons).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERuprechtsberger199915-187] A fifth, still larger stone weighing approximately 1,200 tonnes (1,300 tons)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERuprechtsberger199917-188] lies in the same quarry. This quarry was slightly higher than the temple complex,[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdam_&_al.199935-145][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHastings2004\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidyk1CKgPRKtACpgPA892_892\]-190] so no lifting was required to move the stones.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192]

The temple complex was entered from the east through the [Propylaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propylaea) (προπύλαιον, *propýlaion*) or Portico,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781037-70] consisting of a broad staircase rising 20 feet (6.1 m)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA465_465\]-193] to an arcade of 12 columns flanked by 2 towers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10639_556\]-91] Most of the columns have been toppled and the stairs were entirely dismantled for use in the nearby later wall,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA456_456\]-55][^cite_note-194] but a Latin inscription remains on several of their bases stating that Longinus, a lifeguard of the [1st Parthian Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Parthian_Legion), and Septimius, a freedman, gilded their capitals with [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze) in gratitude for the safety of [Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septimius_Severus)'s son [Antoninus Caracalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoninus_Caracalla) and empress [Julia Domna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Domna).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA466_466\]-195][^cite_note-196]

Immediately behind the Propylaeum is a hexagonal forecourt[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10639_556\]-91] reached through a threefold entrance[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878177-94] that was added in the mid-3rd century by the emperor [Philip the Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_the_Arab). Traces remain of the two series of columns which once encircled it, but its original function remains uncertain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10639_556\]-91] [Donne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bodham_Donne) reckoned it as the town's [forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_\(Roman\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781037-70] Badly preserved coins of the era led some to believe this was a sacred [cypress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress_tree) grove, but better specimens show that the coins displayed a single stalk of grain instead.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10641_558–559\]-197]

The rectangular Great Court to its west covers around 3 or 4 acres (1.2 or 1.6 ha)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878177-94] and included the main [altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar) for [burnt offering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrifice), with [mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_mosaic)-floored [lustration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lustration) basins to its north and south, a [subterranean chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souterrain),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10642_559\]-198] and three underground passageways 17 ft (5.2 m) wide by 30 ft (9.1 m) high, two of which run east and west and the third connecting them north and south, all bearing inscriptions suggesting their occupation by Roman soldiers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878177-94] These were surrounded by [Corinthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthian_order) [porticoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portico), one of which was never completed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10642_559\]-198] The columns' bases and capitals were of limestone; the shafts were monoliths of highly polished red [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Egypt) [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) 7.08 m (23.2 ft) high.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10642_559\]-198] Six remain standing, out of an original 128. Inscriptions attest that the court was once adorned by portraits of [Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius)'s daughter [Sabina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibia_Aurelia_Sabina), [Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septimius_Severus), [Gordian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordian_I), and [Velius Rufus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velius_Rufus?action=edit\&redlink=1), dedicated by the city's Roman colonists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10642_559\]-198] The [entablature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entablature) was richly decorated but is now mostly ruined.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10642_559\]-198] A westward-facing basilica was constructed over the altar during the reign of [Theodosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I); it was later altered to make it eastward-facing like most [Christian](5211.md) [churches](6325.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10638_555\]-92]



The Great Court of ancient Heliopolis's temple complex

The Temple of Jupiter—once wrongly credited to [Helios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helios)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10648_565\]-199]—lay at the western end of the Great Court, raised another 7 m (23 ft) on a 47.7 m × 87.75 m (156.5 ft × 287.9 ft) platform reached by a wide staircase.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10643_560\]-183] Under the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), it was also known as the "[Trilithon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilithon)" from the three massive stones in its foundation and, when taken together with the forecourt and Great Court, it is also known as the Great Temple.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''191190-181] The Temple of Jupiter proper was circled by a [peristyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peristyle) of 54 unfluted [Corinthian columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthian_column):[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA460_460\]-200] 10 in front and back and 19 along each side.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10643_560\]-183] The temple was ruined by earthquakes,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10639_556\]-91] destroyed and pillaged for stone under [Theodosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10638_555\]-92] and 8 columns were taken to [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) ([Istanbul](3391396.md)) under [Justinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) for incorporation into the [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia). Three fell during the late 18th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878177-94] 6 columns, however, remain standing along its south side with their entablature.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10643_560\]-183] Their capitals remain nearly perfect on the south side, while the [Beqaa](2162385.md)'s winter winds have worn the northern faces almost bare.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA462_462\]-201] The [architrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architrave) and [frieze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frieze) blocks weigh up to 60 tonnes (66 tons) each, and one corner block over 100 tonnes (110 tons), all of them raised to a height of 19 m (62.34 ft) above the ground.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoulton197416-202] Individual [Roman cranes](318378.md#Roman_Empire) were not capable of lifting stones this heavy. They may have simply been rolled into position along temporary earthen banks from the quarry.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA462_462\]-201] The [Julio-Claudian emperors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio-Claudian_emperors) enriched its sanctuary in turn. In the mid-1st century, [Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero) built the tower-altar opposite the temple. In the early 2nd century, [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) added the temple's forecourt, with [porticos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portico) of [pink granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) shipped from [Aswan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswan) at the southern end of [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Egypt).

The [Temple of Bacchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Bacchus)—once wrongly credited to Jupiter[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10647_564\]-203][^cite_note-205]—may have been completed under [Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septimius_Severus) in the 190s, as his coins are the first to show it beside the Temple of Jupiter. It is the best preserved of the sanctuary's structures, as the other rubble from its ruins protected it. It is enriched by some of the most refined reliefs and sculpture to survive from [antiquity](252905.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA459_459\]-182] The temple is surrounded by forty-two columns—8 along each end and 15 along each side[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA458_458\]-204]—nearly 20 m (66 ft) in height. These were probably erected in a rough state and then rounded, polished, and decorated in position.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA459_459\]-182][^cite_note-206] The entrance was preserved as late as [Pococke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Pococke)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPococke1745-126] and [Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wood_\(antiquarian\)),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1757-2] but the [keystone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_\(architecture\)) of the [lintel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lintel) had slid 2 ft (1 m) following the [1759 earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East_earthquakes_of_1759); a column of rough masonry was erected in the 1860s or '70s to support it.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA458_458\]-204] The [1759 earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East_earthquakes_of_1759) also damaged the area around the [soffit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soffit)'s famed inscription of an eagle,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878178-116] which was entirely covered by the keystone's supporting column. The area around the inscription of the eagle was greatly damaged by the [1759 earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East_earthquakes_of_1759).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878178-116] The interior of the temple is divided into a 98 ft (30 m) [nave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nave) and a 36 ft (11 m) [adytum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adytum) or sanctuary[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA458_458\]-204] on a platform raised 5 ft (2 m) above it and fronted by 13 steps.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA459_459\]-182] The screen between the two sections once held reliefs of [Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune_\(god\)), [Triton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triton_\(god\)), [Arion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arion) and his dolphin, and other marine figures[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaundrell1703-125] but these have been lost.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA459_459\]-182] The temple was used as a kind of [donjon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donjon) for the medieval Arab and Turkish fortifications,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] although its eastern steps were lost sometime after 1688.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878-207] Much of the portico was incorporated into a huge wall directly before its gate, but this was demolished in July 1870 by Barker on orders from [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria)'s governor [Rashid Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Pasha_\(governor_of_Syria\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA458_458\]-204] Two spiral staircases in columns on either side of the entrance lead to the roof.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878178-116]



*[The Gateway to the Great Temple at Baalbec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gateway_to_the_Great_Temple_at_Baalbec)* by [David Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Roberts_\(painter\)), 1841

The Temple of Venus—also known as the Circular Temple or [Nymphaeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphaeum)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA466_466\]-195]—was added under [Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septimius_Severus) in the early 3rd century but destroyed under [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great), who raised a basilica in its place.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878178-116] [Jessup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Jessup?action=edit\&redlink=1) considered it the "gem of Baalbek".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA466_466\]-195] It lies about 150 yd (140 m) from the southeast corner of the Temple of Bacchus.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA466_466\]-195] It was known in the 19th century as *El Barbara*[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA466_466\]-195] or *Barbarat el-Atikah* (St Barbara's), having been used as a [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church) church into the 18th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EB''1878178-116][^cite_note-208]

The ancient walls of Heliopolis had a circumference of a little less than 4 mi (6 km).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''DGRG''18781037-70] Much of the extant fortifications around the complex date to the 13th century[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECook1914\[httpdigiubuni-heidelbergdediglitcook1914bd10638_555\]-92] reconstruction undertaken by the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk) sultan [Qalawun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalawun) following the devastation of the earlier defenses by the [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Empire) army under [Kitbuqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitbuqa).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA543_543\]-40] This includes the great southeast tower.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] The earliest round of fortifications were two walls to the southwest of the Temples of Jupiter and Bacchus.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] The original southern gateway with two small towers was filled in and replaced by a new large tower flanked by curtains, probably under [Buri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_al-Muluk_Buri) or [Zengi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imad_ad-Din_Zengi).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] [Bahramshah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahramshah) replaced that era's southwest tower with one of his own in 1213 and built another in the northwest in 1224; the west tower was probably strengthened around the same time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] An inscription dates the [barbican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbican)-like strengthening of the southern entrance to around 1240.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] Qalawun relocated the two western curtains nearer to the western tower, which was rebuilt with great blocks of stone. The barbican was repaired and more turns added to its approach.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114] From around 1300, no alterations were made to the fortifications apart from repairs such as Sultan [Barkuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barkuk)'s restoration of the moat in preparation for Timur's arrival.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''EI''1913\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidzJU3AAAAIAAJpgPA544_544\]-114]

Material from the ruins is incorporated into a ruined mosque north of downtown[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA467_467\]-209] and probably also in the [Qubbat Duris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubbat_Duris) on the road to [Damascus](8914.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA467_467\]-209] In the 19th century, a "shell-topped canopy" from the ruins was used nearby as a [mihrab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihrab), propped up to show locals the direction of [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) for their [daily prayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJessup1881\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidmzdQAQAAIAAJpgPA467_467\]-209]

Under a white dome further towards town is the tomb of Khawla, daughter of [Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husayn_ibn_Ali) and granddaughter of Ali, who died in Baalbek while Husayn's family was being transported as prisoners to Damascus.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211]

## Ecclesiastical history

Heliopolis (in Phoenicia; not to be confused with the Egyptian bishopric [Heliopolis in Augustamnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliopolis_in_Augustamnica)) was a bishopric under Roman and Byzantine rule, but it disappeared due to the Islamic rule.

In 1701, Eastern Catholics ([Byzantine Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Rite)) established anew an [Eparchy of Baalbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Eparchy_of_Baalbek), which in 1964 was promoted to the present [Melkite Greek Catholic Archeparchy of Baalbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Archeparchy_of_Baalbek).

### Titular see

In the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church), the Ancient diocese was only nominally restored (no later than 1876) as [Titular archbishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_archbishopric) of Heliopolis (Latin) / Eliopoli (Curiate Italian), demoted in 1925 to Episcopal [Titular bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_bishopric), promoted back in 1932, with its name changed (avoiding Egyptian confusion) in 1933 to (non-Metropolitan) Titular archbishopric of **Heliopolis in Phoenicia**.

The title has not been assigned since 1965. It was held by:[^cite_note-212]

- Titular Archbishop: Luigi Poggi (1876.09.29 – death 1877.01.22) on emeritate (promoted) as former Bishop of [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Rimini) (Italy) (1871.10.27 – 1876.09.29)
