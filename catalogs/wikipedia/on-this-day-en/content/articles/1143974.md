# Aerial firefighting

"Airtanker" redirects here. For aerial fuel tankers, see [Aerial refueling](237949.md). For the British air-to-air refuelling project, see [AirTanker Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirTanker_Services).

**Aerial firefighting**, also known as **waterbombing**, is the use of [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft) and other aerial resources to [combat wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildfire_suppression). The types of aircraft used include [fixed-wing aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-wing_aircraft) and [helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopters). [Smokejumpers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smokejumper) and rappellers are also classified as aerial firefighters, delivered to the fire by parachute from a variety of fixed-wing [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft), or rappelling from [helicopters](8286923.md). Chemicals used to fight fires may include water, water enhancers such as [foams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-fighting_foam) and [gels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-retardant_gel), and specially formulated fire retardants such as [Phos-Chek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phos-Chek).[^cite_note-1]

## Terminology

The idea of fighting forest fires from the air dates back at least as far as [Friedrich Karl von Koenig-Warthausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Karl_von_Koenig-Warthausen)'s observations on seeing a blaze when overflying the [Santa Lucia Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Lucia_Range), California, in 1929.[^cite_note-Warthausen-2]<sup>: 142 </sup>

A wide variety of terminology has been used in the popular media for the aircraft (and methods) used in aerial firefighting. The terms **airtanker** or **air tanker** generally refer to fixed-wing aircraft based in the United States; "airtanker" is used in official documentation.[^cite_note-9th-3] The term "**waterbomber**" is used in some Canadian government documents for the same class of vehicles,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-redbook17-5] though it sometimes has a connotation of [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_aircraft).[^cite_note-6]

**Air attack** is an industry term used for the actual application of aerial resources, both fixed-wing and rotorcraft, on a fire. Within the industry, though, "air attack" may also refer to the supervisor in the air (usually in a fixed-wing aircraft) who supervises the process of attacking the wildfire from the air, including fixed-wing airtankers, helicopters, and any other aviation resources assigned to the fire. The Air Tactical Group Supervisor (ATGS), often called "air attack", is usually flying at an altitude above other resources assigned to the fire, often in a fixed-wing plane but occasionally (depending on assigned resources or the availability of qualified personnel) in a helicopter.

Depending on the size, location, and assessed potential of the wildfire, the "air attack" or ATGS person may be charged with initial attack (the first response of firefighting assets on fire suppression), or with extended attack, the ongoing response to and management of a major wildfire requiring additional resources including engines, ground crews, and other aviation personnel and aircraft needed to control the fire and establish control lines or [firelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firebreak) ahead of the wildfire.[^cite_note-redbook17-5]

## Equipment

A wide variety of helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft are used for aerial firefighting. In 2003, it was reported that "The [U.S. Forest Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Forest_Service) and [Bureau of Land Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Land_Management) own, lease, or contract for nearly 1,000 aircraft each fire season, with annual expenditures in excess of US\$250 million in recent years".[^cite_note-blueribbon1-7]

### Helicopters

Main article: [Helitack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helitack)



[State Emergency Service of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Emergency_Service_of_Ukraine) (DSNS) [Mil Mi-8MTV](957233.md) picking up water near [Nizhyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nezhin)

Helicopters may be fitted with tanks (helitankers) or they may carry buckets. Some helitankers, such as the Erickson AirCrane, are also outfitted with a front-mounted foam cannon. Buckets are usually filled by submerging or dipping them in lakes, rivers, reservoirs, or portable tanks. The most popular of the buckets is the flexible [Bambi Bucket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambi_bucket). Tanks can be filled on the ground (by water tenders or truck-mounted systems) or water can be siphoned from lakes, rivers, reservoirs, or a portable tank through a hanging snorkel. Popular firefighting helicopters include variants of the Bell UH-1H Super Huey, [Bell 204](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-1_Iroquois), [Bell 205](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_205), [Bell 212](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_212), [Boeing Vertol 107](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Vertol_107), [Boeing Vertol 234](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Vertol_234), [Sikorsky S-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_S-70) "Firehawk" and the [Sikorsky S-64 Aircrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-64_Skycrane) helitanker, which features a snorkel for filling from a natural or man-made water source while in hover. Currently the world's largest helicopter, the [Mil Mi-26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-26), uses a Bambi bucket.

*Airtankers* or *water bombers* are fixed-wing aircraft fitted with tanks that can be filled on the ground at an air tanker base or, in the case of [flying boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_boat) and [amphibious aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_aircraft), by skimming water from lakes, reservoirs, or large rivers without needing to land. Various aircraft have been used over the years for firefighting. In 1947, the [United States Air Force](32090.md) and [United States Forest Service](42652.md) experimented with military aircraft dropping water-filled bombs. The bombs were unsuccessful, and the use of internal water tanks was adopted instead.[^cite_note-8]

The [Mendocino Air Tanker Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendocino_Air_Tanker_Squad?action=edit\&redlink=1) formed by [Joseph Bolles Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bolles_Ely) in 1956 was the first such unit in the United States to drop water and retardant on fires. Based at the [Willows-Glenn County Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willows-Glenn_County_Airport) it soon led the way for other agencies to form similar squads.

Though World War II- and Korean War-era bombers were for a long time the mainstay of the aerial firefighting fleet,[^cite_note-9] newer purpose-built tankers have since come online. The smallest are the Single Engine Air Tankers (SEATs). These are agricultural sprayers that generally drop about 800 US gallons (3,000 L) of water or retardant. Examples include the [Air Tractor AT-802](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Tractor_AT-802), which can deliver around 800 gallons of water or fire retardant solution in each drop, and the Soviet [Antonov An-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-2) biplane. Both of these aircraft can be fitted with floats that scoop water from the surface of a body of water. Similar in configuration to the World War II–era [Consolidated PBY Catalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_PBY_Catalina), the [Canadair CL-215](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadair_CL-215) and its derivative the [CL-415](1898178.md) are designed and built specifically for firefighting. The [Croatian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Air_Force) uses six CL-415s as well as six AT 802s for firefighting purposes.

Medium-sized modified aircraft include the [Grumman S-2 Tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_S-2_Tracker) ([retrofitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrofit) with turboprop engines as the S-2T) as used by the [California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Department_of_Forestry_and_Fire_Protection) (CAL FIRE), as well as the [Conair Firecat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conair_Firecat) version developed and used by [Conair Group Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conair_Group_Inc.) of Canada, while the [Douglas DC-4](518813.md), the [Douglas DC-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-7), the [Lockheed C-130 Hercules](7697.md), the [Lockheed P-2 Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-2_Neptune), and the [Lockheed P-3 Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-3_Orion) – and its commercial equivalent, the [L-188 Electra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-188_Electra) – have been used as air tankers. Conair also converted a number of [Convair 580](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convair_580) and [Fokker F27 Friendship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_F27_Friendship) turboprop airliners to air tankers.

The largest aerial firefighter ever used is a [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) aerial firefighter, known as the [Global Supertanker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/747_Supertanker), that can carry 19,600 US gallons (74,200 L) fed by a pressurized drop system. The Supertanker was deployed operationally for the first time in 2009, fighting a fire in Spain.[^cite_note-10] The tanker made its first American operation on August 31, 2009, at the [Oak Glen Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2009_California_wildfires).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] It has since been replaced by a [Boeing 747-400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-400).[^cite_note-13] Another wide body jetliner that is currently being used as an air tanker is the modified [McDonnell Douglas DC-10-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_DC-10-30) operated by the [10 Tanker Air Carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_Tanker_Air_Carrier) company as the [DC-10 Air Tanker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-10_Air_Tanker).[^cite_note-14] It can carry up to 12,000 US gallons (45,400 L) of fire fighting retardant.

The Russian [Ministry of Emergency Situations](1568895.md) operates convertible-to-cargo [Ilyushin Il-76](229943.md) airtankers that have been operating with 11,000-US-gallon (41,600 L) tanking systems, and several [Beriev Be-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beriev_Be-200) jet powered [amphibian aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian_aircraft). The Be-200 can carry a maximum payload of about 12,000 litres (3,200 US gal) of water, making "scoops" in suitable stretches of water in 14 seconds.

Bombardier's [Dash 8 Q Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Dash_8) aircraft are the basis of new, next-generation air tankers. [Cascade Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Aerospace) has converted two pre-owned Q400s to act as part-time water bomber and part-time transport aircraft for France's [Sécurité Civile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9curit%C3%A9_Civile),[^cite_note-15] while Neptune Aviation is converting a pre-owned Q300 as a prototype to augment its [Lockheed P-2 Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-2_Neptune) aircraft. The Sécurité Civile also operates twelve Canadair CL-415 and nine [Conair Turbo Firecat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conair_Firecat) aircraft. Neptune Aviation also currently operates converted [British Aerospace 146](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_146) jetliners as air tankers.[^cite_note-16] The BAe 146 can carry up to 3,000 gallons of fire fighting retardant. Air Spray USA Ltd. of Chico, California has also converted the BAe 146 jetliner to the role of air tanker.[^cite_note-17] Another modern-era passenger aircraft that has now been converted for aerial firefighting missions in the U.S. is the [McDonnell Douglas MD-87](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_MD-80) jetliner operated by Erickson Aero Tanker.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] The MD-87 can carry up to 4,000 gallons of fire fighting retardant. [Coulson Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulson_Aviation) unveiled a [ Boeing 737-300 firefighting conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulson_737_Fireliner) in May 2017. Six aircraft have been purchased from [Southwest Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Airlines) for the RADS system conversion which was planned to enter service in December 2017. The 737 aircraft is smaller than the C-130Q which allows for a wider range of airfields to be utilized. Britt Coulson further stated the aircraft will be able to retain the current seat and galley configuration for tanker operations.[^cite_note-20] On 22 November 2018, the 737 was used for the first time to fight a fire near Newcastle, Australia.[^cite_note-21]

In July 2022, Airbus tested the aerial firefighting capacity of the [A400M](371265.md) using a roll-on/roll-off kit comprising a 20-tonne water tank and piping allowing the load to be expelled from the end of the cargo ramp.[^cite_note-22]

### Leadplanes&#x20;

The Lead Plane function directs the activities of the airtankers by both verbal target descriptions and by physically leading the airtankers on the drop run. The leadplane is typically referred to as a "Bird Dog" in Canada or "Supervision" aircraft in Australia. The [O-2 Skymaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O-2_Skymaster), Cessna 310 and [OV-10 Bronco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OV-10_Bronco) have been used as spotter and lead plane platforms. The [Ontario](22218.md) Ministry of Natural Resources has also used the [Cessna 337](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_337). The [Beechcraft Baron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_Baron) was long used as a leadplane or air attack ship, but most were retired in 2003; more common now is the [Beechcraft King Air](729142.md) and the [Twin Commander 690](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_Commander_500_family). A Cessna Citation 500 jet owned by Air Spray (1967) LTd. was used by the British Columbia Ministry of Forests beginning in 1995 and used for two fire seasons to lead the very fast Electra L188 air tanker to the fires. This was the first time a jet aircraft was used as a lead plane or "bird dog". The Department of Parks and Wildlife in Western Australia operates a fleet of nine [American Champion Scouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Champion_Scout) 8GCBC during the summer months as spotter aircraft and Air Attack platforms. The Provinces of Alberta and British Columbia and the Yukon Territories contract to supply Twin Commander 690 as bird dog aircraft for their air tanker fleets. Air Spray owns 9 Twin Commander 690 for use as bird dog aircraft.

### Fleet grounding

In the United States, most of these aircraft are privately owned and contracted to government agencies, and the [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Guard) and the [U.S. Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marines) also maintain fleets of firefighting aircraft. On May 10, 2004, The [U.S. Forest Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Forest_Service) (USFS) and the [Bureau of Land Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Land_Management) (BLM) announced that they were cancelling contracts with operators of 33 heavy airtankers. They cited liability concerns and an inability to safely manage the fleet after the wing failure and resulting [crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_airtanker_crashes) of a [C-130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130)A Hercules in [California](5407.md) and a [PB4Y-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PB4Y-2_Privateer) in [Colorado](5399.md) during the summer of 2002. Both aged aircraft broke up in flight due to catastrophic [fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_fatigue) cracks at the wing roots. After subsequent third-party examination and extensive testing of all USFS contracted heavy airtankers, three companies were awarded contracts and now maintain a combined fleet of 23 aircraft.

## Fire retardant



A [MAFFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_Airborne_FireFighting_System)-equipped [Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_National_Guard) [C-130 Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130_Hercules) drops [fire retardant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_retardant) on wildfires in southern California

See also: [Fire retardant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_retardant)

[Borate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borate) salts used in the past to fight wildfires have been found to sterilize the soil and be [toxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxin) to animals so are now prohibited.[^cite_note-24] Newer retardants use [ammonium sulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium_sulfate) or [ammonium polyphosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium_polyphosphate) with [attapulgite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attapulgite) [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) thickener or [diammonium phosphate](1722958.md) with a [guar gum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guar_gum) derivative thickener. Fire retardants often contain [wetting agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfactant), [preservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preservative) and [rust inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_inhibitor) and are colored red with [ferric oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferric_oxide) or [fugitive color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugitive_pigment) to mark where they have been dropped. Brand names of fire retardants for aerial application include Fortress and [Phos-Chek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phos-Chek).

Some water-dropping aircraft carry tanks of a guar gum derivative to thicken the water and reduce runoff.

## Tactics and capabilities



A helicopter dips its [bucket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambi_bucket) into a river to drop water on a wildfire in California.

Helicopters can hover over the fire and drop water or retardant. The S-64 Helitanker has [microprocessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microprocessor)-controlled doors on its tank. The doors are controlled based on the area to be covered and wind conditions. Fixed-wing aircraft must make a pass and drop water or retardant like a bomber. Spotter (Air Tactical Group Supervisor) aircraft often orbit the fire at a higher altitude to coordinate the efforts of the smoke jumper, helicopter, media, and retardant-dropping aircraft, while lead planes fly low-level ahead of the airtankers to mark the [trajectory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajectory) for the drop, and ensure overall safety for both ground-based and aerial firefighters.

Film showing Waterbombers from the [California Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Air_National_Guard) dropping substances used to fight fires

Water is not usually dropped directly on flames because its effect is short-lived. Fire retardants are not typically used to extinguish the fire, but instead are used to contain the fire, or slow it down to allow ground crews to contain it. Because of this, retardants are usually dropped in front of or around a moving fire, rather than directly on it, creating a [firebreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firebreak).

Aerial firefighting is most effectively used in conjunction with ground-based efforts, as aircraft are only one weapon in the firefighting arsenal. However, there have been cases of aircraft extinguishing fires long before ground crews were able to reach them.[^cite_note-25]

Some firefighting aircraft can refill their tanks in mid-flight, by flying down to skim the surface of large bodies of water. One example is the [Bombardier CL-415](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_CL-415). This is particularly useful in rural areas where flying back to an airbase for refills may take too much time. In 2002 an Ontario CL-415 crew was able to refill 100 times within a 4-hour mission, delivering 162,000 US gallons (613,240 L) or 1,350,000 pounds (612 t) of water on a fire near [Dryden, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dryden,_Ontario)[^cite_note-26] (June 1, 2002 Dryden fire # 10 Tanker #271 civil ident C-GOGE).

## Accidents and incidents

- June 27, 1969: a [North American B-25 Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_B-25_Mitchell), N9088Z SN 44-30733, operating as Tanker 8Z, crash landed on a sandbar after a multi engine failure shortly after takeoff in the Tanana River, near Fairbanks Alaska. All crew members survived with no injuries. The airplane was recovered in June 2013 and is now under restoration, flying under the name "Sandbar Mitchell".

  [^cite_note-27]

- May 26, 1977: a [Canadair CL-215](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadair_CL-215) aircraft crashed during a training session while doing its water-taking maneuver in Greece's [Eleusis Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleusis_Bay?action=edit\&redlink=1), killing all of its three crew members on board.
