The **Supreme Council of Kyrgyzstan**, also known as the ***Jogorku Kenesh*** ([Kyrgyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyz_language): Жогорку Кеңеш, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Kyrgyz): *Zhogorku Kengesh*, IPA: [\[t͡ɕoʁoɾqʊ́ kʰeŋéɕ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Kyrgyz)), is the [unicameral](204304.md) [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) of [Kyrgyzstan](170131.md). Before Kyrgyzstan's independence from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1991, it was known as the [Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet_of_the_Kirghiz_SSR) of the [Kirghiz Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirghiz_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).

The parliament has 90 seats,[^cite_note-2] with members elected for a five-year term by in 30 3-seat constituencies using [single non-transferable vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_non-transferable_vote).[^cite_note-3]

## History

During Soviet rule, it was known as the [Supreme Soviet of the Kirghiz SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet_of_the_Kirghiz_SSR).

From August 1991, when Kyrgyzstan gained independence from the Soviet Union, until October 2007, when the Constitution was changed in a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Kyrgyz_constitutional_referendum), the Supreme Council consisted of the **Legislative Assembly** (Мыйзам Чыгаруу Жыйыны, *Myizam Chygaruu Jyiyny*; lit. 'Upper House') and the **Assembly of People's Representatives** (Эл Өкүлдөр Жыйыны, *El Öküldör Jyiyny*; lit. 'Lower House') with 60 and 45 members, respectively. The members of both houses were elected to five-year terms. In the Assembly of People's Representatives all 45 members were elected in single-seat [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies); in the Legislative Assembly 45 members were elected in single-seat constituencies and 15 were elected through party lists.

Since October 2007, the Supreme Council is a unicameral legislature. Originally it consisted of 90 members, however when in 2010 President [Kurmanbek Bakiyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurmanbek_Bakiyev) was ousted during the [Kyrgyz Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyz_Revolution_of_2010), a new [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Kyrgyzstan) was adopted, that increased the number of members to 120. Parties are limited to 65 seats in order to prevent power concentration. A [vote on a new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Kyrgyz_constitutional_referendum) cut the number of seats in the parliament by 25%, thereby returning to 90 seats.[^cite_note-4]

Of those 90 seats, 54 were elected by [proportional representation](23298.md) in a single nationwide constituency, and 36 in single-seat districts.[^cite_note-5] To win seats, parties were required to pass a national electoral threshold of 5% of the votes cast (down from 7% in the October 2020 elections),[^cite_note-\:0-6] and receive at least 0.5% of the vote in each of the seven [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Kyrgyzstan).[^cite_note-7] The lists were open, with voters able to cast a single preferential vote. No one party was allowed to be given more than half of the proportional seats.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-IPU2-9] Party lists were required to have at least 30% of the candidates from each gender, and every fourth candidate had to be of a different gender. Each list was also required to have at least 15% of the candidates being from [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Kyrgyzstan#Ethnic_groups) and 15% of under 35 years old, as well as at least two candidates with disabilities.[^cite_note-IPU2-9][^cite_note-10]

## Electoral system

The 90 seats in the Supreme Council are elected by [single non-transferable vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_non-transferable_vote), with the country being divided into 30 three-member constituencies, with voters casting a single vote and the top three candidates being elected, though one seat per constituency is [ reserved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_political_positions) for the highest-polling woman and one for the highest-polling man. Candidates may run either as independents or as nominees of a political party.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### 2005 parliamentary election

Main articles: [2005 Kyrgyz parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Kyrgyz_parliamentary_election) and [Tulip Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulip_Revolution)

The 2005 Kyrgyz parliamentary elections were held in February and March 2005. More than 400 candidates ran for the new 75-member unicameral legislative assembly. There were two rounds of voting held on 27 February and 13 March. Six seats were won by opposition politicians. Most candidates were officially independent. International observers said the elections fell short of international standards for democratic elections in several important areas. Widespread protests over alleged rigging of the election by the government culminated in the [Tulip Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulip_Revolution) on 24 March. Revolutionaries overthrew President [Askar Akayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askar_Akayev).
