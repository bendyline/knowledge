**Philip Joseph Pierre**[^cite_note-\:3-5] (born 18 September[^cite_note-6] 1954)[^cite_note-\:4-7] is a [Saint Lucian](27208.md) politician currently serving as the [prime minister of Saint Lucia](466569.md) since 2021. Pierre serves as the [Minister for Finance, Economic Development and the Youth Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_of_Saint_Lucia).[^cite_note-8] He is the Leader of the [Saint Lucia Labour Party](1836043.md) since 18 June 2016. He has represented the [Castries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castries) East constituency in the [House of Assembly](4388564.md) since 1997.[^cite_note-Loop_20_June-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Pierre previously served as Minister for Tourism, Civil Aviation and International Financial Services from 1997 to 2000; Minister of Commerce, Tourism, Investment and Consumer Affairs from 2001 to 2006; [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Saint_Lucia) and Minister for Infrastructure, Port Services and Transport from 2011 to 2016; and as [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Saint_Lucia\)) from 2016 to 2021.

## Early life

Pierre's mother Evelyn was a schoolteacher, and his father Auguste[^cite_note-\:0-12] was a policeman.[^cite_note-\:1-13] He studied at [Saint Mary's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mary's_College_\(Saint_Lucia\)), then completed a [BA (hons)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BA_\(Hons\)) in accounts and a [Master of Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) from the [University of the West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_West_Indies). After graduation, he taught at Saint Mary's College[^cite_note-\:1-13] and worked as a trainee manager at J.Q. Charles Ltd. Pierre then entered the finance industry: he worked as an audit clerk at [Coopers & Lybrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coopers_&_Lybrand) and [Pannell Kerr Forster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannell_Kerr_Forster), and as a financial controller at Stanthur Co. Ltd.[^cite_note-\:0-12]

From 1985 to 1994, Pierre was the Director of the National Research and Development Corporation.[^cite_note-\:1-13] He was also Chief Executive of his own [management consultancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_consulting) firm, Philip J. Pierre Business Services Ltd.,[^cite_note-\:0-12] from 1990 to 1997.[^cite_note-\:1-13]

## Politics

Pierre joined the [Saint Lucia Labour Party](1836043.md) (SLP) in 1985, and served as the party treasurer from 1986 to 1992.[^cite_note-\:0-12] He contested the [1992 Saint Lucian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Saint_Lucian_general_election) for the first time in Castries East, but did not win.[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-\:2-4] After serving as the SLP chairman from 1992 to 1996,[^cite_note-\:0-12] Pierre ran again in [1997 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Saint_Lucian_general_election) and won. In the resulting SLP government led by [Kenny Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Anthony),[^cite_note-\:1-13] Pierre served as Minister for Tourism, Civil Aviation and International Financial Services from 1997 to 2000 and Minister for Commerce, Tourism, Investment and Consumer Affairs from 2001 to 2006.[^cite_note-\:0-12][^cite_note-14]

Pierre was re-elected to the [House of Assembly](4388564.md) from Castries East in the general elections of [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Saint_Lucian_general_election), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Saint_Lucian_general_election), and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Saint_Lucian_general_election).[^cite_note-\:2-4] In December 2011, he was sworn in as [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Saint_Lucia) and Minister for Infrastructure, Port Services, and Transportation.[^cite_note-15] Pierre retained his seat in the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Saint_Lucian_general_election),[^cite_note-\:2-4] but the SLP lost the election. Kenny Anthony resigned as party leader; Pierre was then elected as his successor on 18 June 2016. He also became the [Leader of the Parliamentary Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Saint_Lucia\)).[^cite_note-Loop_20_June-9][^cite_note-16]

Pierre is a member of the [Commonwealth Parliamentary Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Parliamentary_Association) (CPA). He also joined the Assembly of [Caribbean Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Community) Parliamentarians, attending its inaugural 1996 meeting in [Barbados](3455.md).[^cite_note-\:0-12]

Pierre led the SLP in the [2021 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Saint_Lucian_general_election), where the party won a majority of seats. He was sworn in as Prime Minister on 28 July 2021.[^cite_note-\:4-7][^cite_note-\:3-5]

Pierre obtained a second term as Prime Minister, after the victory of his party in the legislative elections of December 2025.[^cite_note-\:5-17]

In May 2026, after the [murder of Joy St. Omer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Joy_St._Omer) in [Castries District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castries_District) caused social outrage, Pierre condemned [violence against women](3634424.md) in the country and urged calm following calls to resume executions in Saint Lucia.[^cite_note-\:6-18][^cite_note-19] The country has had a [moratorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moratorium_\(law\)) on the [death penalty](5902.md) since 1995, when convicted rapist and murderer [Joseph Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Solomon_\(murderer\)) was hanged.[^cite_note-\:6-18] Pierre defended his government's strategy to combat crime and stated that resuming capital punishment requires thinking "very, very carefully."[^cite_note-\:6-18][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]
