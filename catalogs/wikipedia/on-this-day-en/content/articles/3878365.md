The **Cessna Citation II** models are light [corporate jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_jet) built by [Cessna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna) as part of the [Citation family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_family). Stretched from the [Citation I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_I), the Model 550 was announced in September 1976, first flew on January 31, 1977, and was certified in March 1978.  The **II/SP** is a single pilot version, the improved **S/II** first flew on February 14, 1984 and the **Citation Bravo**, a stretched S/II with new avionics and more powerful [P\&WC PW530](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PW500)A turbofans, first flew on April 25, 1995. The [United States Navy](20518076.md) adopted a version of the S/II as the **T-47A**.  Production ceased in 2006 after 1,184 of all variants were delivered.

## Design and development



A Citation II seen shortly after landing

The Citation II (Model 550) was developed to provide the same docile low-speed handling and good short-field performance as the preceding [Citation I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_I) while addressing a primary criticism of that aircraft — its relatively slow cruise speed of around 350 kn (650 km/h) at altitude.[^cite_note-aopa2011-2] The II stretches the Citation I fuselage by 1.14m (3 ft 9in), increasing [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) to ten (two pilots and eight passengers) and gross weight to 13,300 lb (6,000 kg). Wingspan was increased by 5.1 ft (1.6 m), fuel capacity was increased from 544 US gal (2,060 L) to 742 US gal (2,810 L), and more powerful, 2,500 lbf (11 kN) [Pratt & Whitney Canada JT15D-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_JT15D) engines were installed for a higher cruise speed of 385 kn (713 km/h) and a longer range of 1,159 nmi (2,146 km).[^cite_note-alnet550-3][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESzurovy199928–30-4] The cabin interior was also redesigned to increase headroom by 5 in (13 cm).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESzurovy199928-5]



Citation II/SP (model 551) front view

The stretched Citation was announced in September 1976, it first flew on January 31, 1977, and FAA certification was awarded in March 1978.[^cite_note-alnet550-3] The **II/SP** (Model 551) is the single pilot version, [type certificated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) to slightly less stringent [FAR Part 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAR_Part_23) standards, with a slightly reduced [maximum takeoff weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_takeoff_weight) (MTOW) at 12,500 lb (5,700 kg) and minor changes in cockpit equipment. As the II and II/SP are otherwise largely similar, the 800 lb (360 kg) reduction in MTOW of the II/SP often mandates operating with a reduced fuel load, shortening the aircraft's loaded range compared to the standard II. Both the II and II/SP require special training to be operated by a single pilot.[^cite_note-aopa2011-2] A total of 688 II and II/SP aircraft were delivered.[^cite_note-Cessna28apr2015-1]

### Citation S/II

The improved **Citation S/II** (Model S550) was announced in October 1983 and first flew on February 14, 1984, before certification in July. It gained a [supercritical airfoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercritical_airfoil) with swept wing roots, [aileron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileron) and [flap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flap_\(aeronautics\)) gap seals, and a [fluid deicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_protection_system#Fluid_deicing) system instead of the [pneumatic deicing boots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_protection_system#Pneumatic_deicing_boots) used on earlier Citations.[^cite_note-aopa2011-2][^cite_note-alnet550-3][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESzurovy199931-6] To further reduce drag, the fuselage and engine [nacelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacelle) pylons were redesigned, and nacelle fairings were added.[^cite_note-aopa2011-2][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESzurovy199931-6] Fuel capacity was increased by 120 US gal (450 L). The result of the improvements was a cruise speed of 403 kn (746 km/h)—exceeding 400 kn, felt to be an important marketing benchmark by Cessna—and a range of 1,378 nmi (2,552 km) with a 45-minute [fuel reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_reserve).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESzurovy199931–32-7] The improved 2,500 lbf (11 kN) JT15D-4B engines had higher temperature-rated components, allowing more thrust at higher altitudes.[^cite_note-aopa2011-2]

The S/II replaced the II from 1984, but some potential buyers objected to the sharp price increase from [US\$](18717338.md)2.6 million for the II to \$3.3M for the S/II, prompting Cessna to reintroduce the II[^cite_note-aopa2011-2] in late 1985; both were built until the Bravo was introduced.[^cite_note-alnet550-3] Deliveries of the S/II amounted to 160, including fifteen T-47A aircraft purchased by the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md).[^cite_note-Cessna28apr2015-1][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESzurovy199932-8] The S/II's higher performance coupled with its relatively low production total led to substantially higher demand on the used aircraft market compared to the standard II and II/SP.[^cite_note-aopa2011-2]

#### Government variants

The [US Customs & Border Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Customs_&_Border_Protection) purchased ten Citation IIs configured with [fire control radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_control_radar) (initially the [F-16](11642.md)'s [AN/APG-66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/APG-66)(V), later the [Selex ES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selex_ES) Vixen 500E system) and the WF-360TL imaging system.[^cite_note-9] These aircraft have been used effectively in Panama, Honduras, Colombia, Peru, Venezuela, Mexico and Aruba. The similar OT-47B aircraft are based on the [Cessna Citation V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Citation_V) airframe.



Several U.S. Navy T-47A radar systems trainers seen in 1989

The **T-47A** was a modified version of the Citation S/II (Model 552) for the U.S. Navy, featuring a 5 ft (1.5 m) wingspan reduction and hydraulically boosted [ailerons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aileron) for improved maneuverability, 2,900 lbf (13 kN) thrust JT15D-5 engines, a cockpit roof window for better pilot visibility during hard maneuvering, strengthened windshields for protection against [bird strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike) during high-speed low-altitude sorties, multiple radar consoles, and the [AN/APQ-167](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/APQ-159) radar system.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESzurovy199921-10][^cite_note-11] Intended to replace the [North American T-39D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Sabreliner) as a radar systems [trainer aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainer_aircraft), fifteen aircraft were purchased in 1984 to train naval [radar intercept officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_intercept_officer).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESzurovy199921-10]

All T-47A aircraft were operated with civil [aircraft registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_registration) numbers by Training Air Squadron [VT-86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VT-86) based at [Naval Air Station Pensacola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Pensacola), Florida. On July 20, 1993, ten of the fifteen aircraft were destroyed when a roofing contractor accidentally set fire to a hangar at [Forbes Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topeka_Regional_Airport) where the aircraft were being stored by Cessna. The five survivors were subsequently transferred to Cessna and other civil owners.[^cite_note-12]

### Citation Bravo

The **Citation Bravo** first flew on April 25, 1995, was granted certification in August 1996, and was first delivered in February 1997. It features new [P\&WC PW530](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PW500)A turbofans, modern [Honeywell Primus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeywell_Primus) EFIS avionics, a revised [Citation Ultra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Citation_Ultra) interior and a [trailing link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailing_link) main undercarriage.[^cite_note-alnet550-3] Production of the Bravo ceased in late 2006 after 336 had been delivered.[^cite_note-Cessna28apr2015-1]

Its more efficient PW530A generates 15% more thrust at takeoff and 23% more at altitude. It burns 1,100 lb (500 kg) of fuel in the first hour, dropping to 750–830 lb (340–380 kg) the second hour cruising at 360–365 kn (667–676 km/h) at FL410-430 and then 637 lb (289 kg) the third hour at 350 kn (650 km/h) and FL450. The engine overhaul every 4,000 hours cost \$1 million or \$275 at [power by the Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_by_the_Hour). In 2018, early 1997 models starts at \$800,000, up to \$1.7 million for 2006 planes. The Bravo was replaced by the better-but-more-expensive [Citation CJ3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Citation_CJ3). The competing [Beechjet 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechjet_400)A is roomier and faster but needs more fuel and more runway, while the compact [Learjet 31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learjet_31)A is faster but has less range. The faster and more expensive [Citation V Ultra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Citation_V) has a longer cabin but consumes more fuel.[^cite_note-BCA26feb2018-13]

### Upgrades

By December 2006, Clifford Development in Ohio had launched a program to re-engine Citation IIs with 3,000 lbf (13 kN) [Williams FJ44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_FJ44)-3 engines for \$1.9 million (\~\$2.84 million in 2024).[^cite_note-AIN11dec2006-14] Clifford expected a [STC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplemental_type_certificate) within 12 months, 21% faster long-range cruise, 29% longer range, 34% better single-engine climb rate and 20% better fuel efficiency.[^cite_note-AIN11dec2006-14] By May 2007, Sierra Industries in Texas was also developing a similar modification, as 900 Citations qualify for it, directly as a broker and [MRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maintenance,_repair_and_operations) provider, while Clifford should license its STC.[^cite_note-15]

In September 2008, the FAA granted a STC to Sierra Industries.[^cite_note-16] The Super S-II made its first flight on September 26.[^cite_note-17] The conversion cost \$1.9 million in 2009, resulting in a \$3.5-4.6 million value for a converted Citation II.[^cite_note-Flying14apr2009-18] Ceiling is increased from FL 410 to FL 430, reached directly in 25 min at max takeoff weight with a thrust increased from 2,500 to 2,820 lbf (11.1 to 12.5 kN) each.[^cite_note-Flying14apr2009-18] Dual-channel [FADEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_authority_digital_engine_control) allows a much lower residual thrust, eliminating the need for [thrust reversers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_reverser).[^cite_note-Flying14apr2009-18] Max fuel [payload](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payload) is bumped from 328 to 1,278 lb (149 to 580 kg) for the Citation II, and the S-II can carry 400 lb (180 kg) more than the initial 1,036 lb (470 kg).[^cite_note-Flying14apr2009-18]

Cruise speeds are faster by 45 to 400 kn (83 to 741 km/h) for the 550, and by 35 to 420 kn (65 to 778 km/h) for the Citation S-II.[^cite_note-Flying14apr2009-18] The converted 550 is 25% more fuel efficient than the JT15D-powered original at the same speed, and burns 775 lb (352 kg) of fuel per hour at 390 kn (720 km/h).[^cite_note-Flying14apr2009-18] The 550 Range is improved by 397 to 1,775 nmi (735 to 3,287 km), and by 461 to 2,300 nmi (854 to 4,260 km) for the S550.[^cite_note-Flying14apr2009-18] The re-engined S550 can reach 446 kn (826 km/h) at FL270.[^cite_note-19] Clifford and its partner Stevens Aviation could also update the flight deck with [Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins_Aerospace) ProLine 21 avionics and refurbish the cabin.[^cite_note-20] Clifford was touting a 14% faster optimum cruise speed, and a 32% lower fuel burn for the S550.[^cite_note-21] Sierra was announcing a 1,890 and 2,064 nmi (3,500 and 3,823 km) [IFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_flight_rules)/[VFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_flight_rules) range for the re-engined Super II; or a 2,340 and 2,610 nmi (4,330 and 4,830 km) IFR/VFR range for the re-engined Super S-II.[^cite_note-22] By June 2012, Sierra Industries had re-engined 59 various Citations with FJ44s, among avionics retrofit and airframe modifications.[^cite_note-23]

## Accidents and incidents

- On September 4, 2022, a Cessna 551 heading from [Jerez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerez_Airport), Spain, did not land at its intended destination, [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), Germany, but instead kept flying across Germany and the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) where it eventually [ran out of fuel and crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Baltic_Sea_Cessna_Citation_crash) in the sea off [Ventspils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventspils), Latvia. The pilot had reported problems with air conditioning and pressurization early in the flight but later stopped responding to communications. A possible cause of the crash is that loss of [cabin pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_pressurization) rendered everyone on board unconscious.

  [^cite_note-40]

- On May 22, 2025, a 1985 Cessna 551 heading from [Teterboro Airport](475231.md) in New Jersey to [Montgomery-Gibbs Executive Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery-Gibbs_Executive_Airport) in [San Diego](28504.md), California [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_San_Diego_plane_crash) into military housing in the Murphy Canyon neighbourhood of San Diego, causing extensive fires and six casualties.

  [^cite_note-41]

  &#x20;The NTSB preliminary report identified several potentially problematic factors prior to the crash including the pilot not discussing alternate airport weather conditions with the controller and the destination airport’s weather data system and runway lights being disabled. The plane was well below the minimum legal altitude at the last checkpoint it passed through.

  [^cite_note-42]

- On December 18, 2025, a Cessna 550 Citation II [crashed](81871726.md) while making an emergency landing at [Statesville Regional Airport](76607890.md) shortly after takeoff. The crash killed everyone on board: aircraft owner and semi-retired [NASCAR](21114.md) driver [Greg Biffle](1637067.md), his wife Cristina, their two children, and three others.

  [^cite_note-43]
