**Independence** is a condition of a [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation), [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country), or [state](23604120.md), in which residents and population, or some portion thereof, exercise [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-government), and usually [sovereignty](28791.md), over its territory. The opposite of independence is the status of a [dependent territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_territory) or [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony). The commemoration of the independence day of a country or nation celebrates when a country is free from all forms of [colonialism](7299.md); free to build a country or nation without any interference from other nations.

## Definition

Whether the attainment of independence is different from [revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution) has long been contested, and has often been debated over the question of violence as [legitimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legitimacy_\(family_law\)) means to achieving sovereignty.[^cite_note-2] In general, revolutions aim only to redistribute power with or without an element of emancipation, such as in [democratization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratization) *within* a state, which as such may remain unaltered. For example, the [Mexican Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Revolution) (1910) chiefly refers to a multi-factional conflict that eventually led to a [new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Mexico); it has rarely been used to refer to the [armed struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_War_of_Independence) (1821) against Spain. However, some [wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_independence) have been described as revolutions, such as the ones in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) (1783) and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution) (1949), while some revolutions that were specifically about a change in the political structure have resulted in breakaway states. [Mongolia](19271.md) and [Finland](10577.md), for example, gained their independence during the revolutions occurring in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_Revolution) (1911) and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) (1917) respectively. Causes for a country or province wishing to seek independence are many, but most can be summed up as a feeling of inequality compared to the dominant power. The means can extend from intended peaceful demonstrations as in the case of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) (1947), to a [violent war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_national_liberation) as in the case of [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War) (1962). In some cases, a country may also have declared independence, but may only be partially recognized by other countries; such as [Kosovo](17391.md) (2008), whose independence [Serbia](29265.md), from which Kosovo has seceded, has [not formally recognized](15808914.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

### Distinction between independence and autonomy

Main article: [Autonomy](191145.md)

*[Autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_entity)* refers to a kind of independence which has been granted by an overseeing [authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority) that itself still retains ultimate authority over that territory (see [Devolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution)). A [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) refers to an autonomous region that depends upon a larger government for its protection as an autonomous region.

## Right to independence

Main articles: [Secession § Right to secession](144732.md#Right_to_secession), [Sovereign state](23604120.md), and [Decolonization](302084.md)

During the 20th century wave of [decolonization](302084.md) colonies gained rights to independence through documents such as the 1960 [Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples](6075560.md), but this right remained mostly applicable only to unfree territorial entities, such as colonies.[^cite_note-The_Princeton_Encyclopedia_of_Self-Determination_1918-6] How much these rights apply to all people has been a crucial point of discussion. The rights to [nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality) and [self-determination](29269.md) allow clarification. The right of self-determination allows [self-governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance), as for example in the case of [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples), but is not a right of secession, except in extreme cases of oppression as a remedy from the oppression.[^cite_note-Shrinkhal_2021_pp._71–82-7] Therefore, the [right to secession](144732.md#Right_to_secession) is generally determined by the legislation of [sovereign states](23604120.md) and independence by the capacity to be a state.

## Declarations of independence

photograph of crowd during pro-independence demonstration

Public proclamation of the [Estonian Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_Declaration_of_Independence) in [Pärnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A4rnu), [Estonia](28222445.md) on 23 February 1918



[Ismail Kemal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Qemali) at the first anniversary of the [Assembly of Vlorë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Vlor%C3%AB) which proclaimed the [independence of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Albania) (28 November 1912)

Sometimes, a state wishing to achieve independence from a dominating power will issue a [declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_independence); the earliest surviving example is [Scotland](26994.md)'s [Declaration of Arbroath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Arbroath) in 1320, with the most recent examples being [Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawad)'s declaration of independence in 2012 and [Catalan declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_declaration_of_independence) in 2017. Declaring independence and attaining it, however, are quite different. A well-known successful example is the [U.S. Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_\(United_States\)) issued in 1776. The dates of established independence (or, less commonly, the commencement of revolution), are typically celebrated as a national [holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday) known as an [independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day).

## Historical overview

Historically, there have been four major periods of declaring independence:

- from the 1770s, beginning with the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) through the 1830s, when the last royalist bastions fell at the close of the [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence);

- the immediate aftermath of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) following the breakup of the [Ottoman](22278.md), [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) empires;

- 1945 to c. 1979, when seventy newly independent states emerged from the European [colonial empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_empire) like India, Algeria etc. and the collapse of the [Nazi German Reich](21212.md) and the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan);

  [^cite_note-8]

- and the early 1990s, following the breakup of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) and [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia).
