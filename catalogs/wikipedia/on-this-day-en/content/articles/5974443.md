An **arms embargo** is a restriction or a set of sanctions that applies either solely to weaponry or also to "[dual-use technology](1003982.md)." An arms embargo may serve one or more purposes:

- to signal disapproval of the behavior of a certain actor

- to maintain neutrality in an ongoing conflict

- as a peace mechanism that is part of a [peace process](24903.md) to resolve an armed conflict

  [^cite_note-UNSMIL_July19_peaceplan-1]

- to limit the ability of an actor to inflict violence on others

- to weaken a country's military capabilities before a [foreign intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_intervention)

## Historical examples

### Argentina

US President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) imposed an arms embargo on the [military government of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceso_de_Reorganizacion_Nacional) in 1977 in response to [human rights](13831.md) abuses.[^cite_note-carter-2]

An arms embargo was put in place, along with other economic sanctions by the [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community) (EEC), within a week of the [1982 invasion of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_invasion_of_the_Falkland_Islands) by [Argentina](18951905.md), two [British dependent territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_dependent_territories) in the [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic).[^cite_note-3] The European nations ended the embargo after the end of the ensuing [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War), and Argentina looked to Western European countries and Israel for arms supplies during the US embargo until it was lifted in 1989.[^cite_note-carter-2]

### Indonesia

The US government imposed an arms embargo against [Indonesia](14579.md) in 1999 because of human rights violations in [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor). The embargo was lifted in 2005.

### Iran

The [United States](3434750.md) imposed economic sanctions against [Iran](14653.md) following the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md) in 1979. However, to secure the release of American hostages, several senior [Reagan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_administration) officials secretly facilitated the sale of arms to Iran in the 1980s in a scandal called the [Iran–Contra affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Contra_affair).[^cite_note-4] In 1995, the US [expanded sanctions](20905961.md) to include firms dealing with the Iranian government.[^cite_note-5]

In March 2007, [UN Security Council Resolution 1747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1747) tightened the [sanctions imposed on Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_Iran) in connection with the [Iranian nuclear program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_nuclear_program). The sanctions were lifted on 16 January 2016.

In September 2020, US Secretary of State [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) announced the imposition of an arms embargo on the Iranian [Ministry of Defence and Armed Forces Logistics](18379483.md) and other entities involved in Iran's nuclear program, including the government of the disputed Venezuelan President [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md) for providing weapons to Iran.[^cite_note-6]

On 18 October 2020, Iran announced that the [United Nations](31769.md) conventional arms embargo imposed on the country in 2007 had expired. The embargo had barred Iran from purchasing arms, including tanks and fighter jets, from foreign nations. The embargo was lifted as per the conditions under [Iran's 2015 nuclear deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_nuclear_deal_framework) with world leaders, despite US objections.[^cite_note-dw\.com-7][^cite_note-8]

### Israel

Main article: [Arms embargoes on Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_embargoes_on_Israel)

During 2024, due to the [genocide in Gaza](75668427.md), several countries such as Italy, Japan, Spain, Canada, Colombia, the Netherlands and Belgium have ceased the sale of weapons to Israel.[^cite_note-UN_rights_body-9][^cite_note-The_UK’s_arms-10][^cite_note-11] Key US allies such as Britain and France are debating it.[^cite_note-Global_pressure_grows-12] However the United States and Germany as the major suppliers of Israel's arms imports keep supplying lethal weapons in spite of growing criticism of the mounting civilian casualties.[^cite_note-UN_rights_body-9][^cite_note-The_UK’s_arms-10] On May 20, 2025, Spain’s parliament has voted to enshrine in law an arms embargo on Israel that the prime minister, [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md), introduced to end “the genocide in Gaza”. During the vote to approve the decision, 178 members of parliament voted in favor, and 169 members of parliament from the right and far-Right voted against the proposal. The Spanish arms embargo would completely prevent the sale of weapons to Israel and the purchase of Israeli weapons by Spain.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

### People's Republic of China

The United States and the [European Union](9317.md) stopped exporting arms to [China](5405.md) after 1989 after the suppression of [protests in Tiananmen Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square_protests_of_1989). In 2004 and 2005, there was some debate in the EU over whether to lift the embargo.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Somalia

Main article: [United Nations arms embargo on Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_arms_embargo_on_Somalia)

On 1 December 2023 after 31 years the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) lifted the longest [arms embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_arms_embargo_on_Somalia) on the [Somali Armed Forces](27366.md) and the [Somali federal government](36817379.md), while continuing sanctions against the [Al-Shabaab](8726463.md) militant group.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

### South Africa

[UN Security Council Resolution 418](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_418) applied an arms embargo of [South Africa](17416221.md) in 1977 on dual-use items. The embargo was lifted by [Resolution 919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_919) in 1994.

### United States

To protest the [Vietnam War](32611.md), [Sweden](5058739.md) imposed an arms embargo on the United States in 1966.[^cite_note-23] That notably deprived [Navy SEALs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_SEALs) of the [Carl Gustav m/45 submachine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gustaf_m/45), which resulted in the creation of the [Smith & Wesson M76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_&_Wesson_M76).

### Yugoslavia

The [United Nations Security Council Resolution 713](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_713) applied arms embargo on Yugoslavia in 1991.[^cite_note-p144-24] Weakly enforced [security assurances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_assurance) and [deterrence](735661.md) contributed to the [Srebrenica massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre).[^cite_note-z377-25]

## List of current arms embargoes

The countries included in the list are under arms embargo of the [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN) or another international organization such as the [EU](9317.md) and the [OSCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSCE) and others) or a country. In some cases the arms embargo is supplemented by a general [trade embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_embargo), other [sanctions](411315.md) (financial), or [travel ban](521241.md) for specific persons. In some cases, the arms embargo applies to any entity residing or established in the country, but in others. it is partial with the recognized government's forces and international [peacekeepers](84617.md) being exempted from the embargo.
