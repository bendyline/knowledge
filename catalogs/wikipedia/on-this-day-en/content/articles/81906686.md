**Fanil Fanisovich Sarvarov** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Фанил Фанисович Сарваров; 11 March 1969 – 22 December 2025) was a Russian [lieutenant general](201916.md) who served as chief of the [Operational Training Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Operational_Directorate_of_the_General_Staff_of_the_Russian_Armed_Forces) of the [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff_of_the_Armed_Forces_of_the_Russian_Federation) of the [Russian Armed Forces](25709.md). Sarvarov was part of combat operations in the [East Prigorodny conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prigorodny_conflict) and of the [Second Chechen War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Chechen_War) in the late 1990s and early 2000s. He led the [Russian operations in the Syrian civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_intervention_in_the_Syrian_civil_war) between 2015 and 2016.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

Sarvarov was killed by a car bomb on Yasenevaya Street in Moscow on 22 December 2025.[^cite_note-3]

## Early life and education

Sarvarov was born in [Gremyachinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gremyachinsk), [Perm Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_Oblast), Russia, on 11 March 1969.[^cite_note-4] An ethnic [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars),[^cite_note-5] he graduated from the [Kazan Higher Tank Command Red Banner School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_Higher_Tank_Command_School) in 1990. He later graduated from the [Malinovsky Military Armored Forces Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malinovsky_Military_Armored_Forces_Academy) in 1999, and from the [Military Academy of the General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Academy_of_the_General_Staff_of_the_Armed_Forces_of_Russia) in 2008.[^cite_note-Blanc-6]

## Military career

Sarvarov began his career as a [military officer](36301328.md) in the [Russian Armed Forces](25709.md). He fought in the [East Prigorodny conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prigorodny_conflict) and the [Chechen wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechen%E2%80%93Russian_conflict) from 1992 to 2003. From 2015 to 2016, he helped command [Russia's intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_intervention_in_the_Syrian_civil_war) in the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md). He became the chief of the [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff_of_the_Armed_Forces_of_the_Russian_Federation)'s Operational Training Directorate in 2016, a role he held until his death in 2025.[^cite_note-Blanc-6][^cite_note-Smith-2025-7] He took part in the [Russo-Ukrainian war](42085878.md), overseeing the Russian military's [combat readiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_readiness) and training in Ukraine,[^cite_note-8] and was promoted to [lieutenant general](201916.md) on 2 May 2024 under presidential decree.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-Kelly-2025-10][^cite_note-11]

He was a recipient of several military awards, including the [Order of Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Courage_\(Russia\)), the [Medal of the Order "For Merit to the Fatherland"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_the_Order_%22For_Merit_to_the_Fatherland%22) 1st and 2nd classes, the [Order of Military Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Military_Merit_\(Russia\)), and the [Medal of Suvorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Suvorov).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

## Death

Sarvarov was killed in a [car bombing](155282.md) on 22 December 2025 at the age of 56. At 6\:55 am local time, a bomb placed under his [Kia Sorento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_Sorento) car exploded while he was driving out of a parking lot on Yaseneva Street, southern Moscow.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The explosion damaged seven nearby cars.[^cite_note-Kelly-2025-10] Sarvarov was injured in the explosion, receiving "multiple shrapnel injuries, closed fractures, leg injuries, and a fractured facial bone".[^cite_note-16] He later died in the hospital.[^cite_note-BBC_News-2025-17]

The [Russian Investigative Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_Committee_of_Russia) announced that it was investigating whether [Ukrainian intelligence agencies](709222.md) were involved in the killing.[^cite_note-Smith-2025-7] It was also investigating illegal [explosives trafficking](42210.md) that facilitated the bombing.[^cite_note-BBC_News-2025-17]
