**Pablo Emilio Escobar Gaviria** ([/ˈɛskəbɑːr/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*ESK-ə-bar*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), Spanish: [\[ˈpaβlo eskoˈβaɾ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish); 1 December 1949 – 2 December 1993) was a [Colombian drug lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_drug_lord), [narcoterrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcoterrorist), and politician who was the founder and leader of the [Medellín Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn_Cartel). Dubbed the "**King of Cocaine**", Escobar was one of the wealthiest conventional criminals in history, having amassed an estimated net worth of US\$30 billion by his death, while his [drug cartel](197015.md) monopolized the [cocaine](7701.md) trade into the US in the 1980s and early 1990s.[^cite_note-Macias-1][^cite_note-2]

Born in [Rionegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rionegro) and raised in [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn), Escobar studied briefly at the [Universidad Autónoma Latinoamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Aut%C3%B3noma_Latinoamericana) of Medellín but left without graduating; he instead began engaging in criminality, selling illegal cigarettes and fake lottery tickets, as well as participating in [motor vehicle theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_vehicle_theft) and more heinous crimes. In the early 1970s, he began to work for various drug smugglers, often [kidnapping](169376.md) and holding people for [ransom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransom). In 1976, Escobar founded the Medellín Cartel, which distributed powder cocaine, and established the first smuggling routes from Peru, Bolivia and Ecuador, through Colombia and eventually into the United States. Escobar's infiltration into the US created a [rapidly increasing demand for cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_boom) and by the 1980s it was estimated Escobar led monthly shipments of 70 to 80 tons into the US from Colombia. He quickly became one of the richest people in the world,[^cite_note-Macias-1][^cite_note-3] but constantly battled rival cartels domestically and abroad, leading to massacres and the murders of police officers, judges, locals, and prominent politicians.[^cite_note-colombialink.com-4]

In the [1982 Colombian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Colombian_parliamentary_election), Escobar was elected as an alternate member of the [Chamber of Representatives](645347.md) as part of the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Liberal_Party). Through this, he was responsible for community projects such as the construction of houses and football pitches, which gained him popularity among the locals of towns he frequented; however, Escobar's political ambitions were thwarted by the Colombian and US governments, who routinely [pushed for his arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_Bloc), with Escobar believed to have orchestrated the [Avianca Flight 203](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avianca_Flight_203) and [DAS Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAS_Building_bombing) bombings in retaliation. In 1991, Escobar surrendered to authorities, and was sentenced to five years' imprisonment on a host of charges, but struck a deal of no [extradition](239648.md) with Colombian president [César Gaviria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Gaviria), with the ability of being housed in his own, self-built prison, [La Catedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Catedral). In 1992, Escobar escaped and went into hiding when authorities attempted to move him to a more standard holding facility, leading to a nationwide manhunt.[^cite_note-DeathPablo-5] As a result, the Medellín Cartel crumbled, and in 1993, Escobar was killed in his hometown by the [Colombian National Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_National_Police), a day after his 44th birthday.[^cite_note-DEA-6]

Escobar's legacy remains controversial; while many denounce the heinous nature of his crimes, he was seen as a "[Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood)-like" figure for many in Colombia, as he provided amenities to the poor. His killing was mourned and his funeral attended by over 25,000 people.[^cite_note-7] Additionally, his private estate, [Hacienda Nápoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacienda_N%C3%A1poles), has been transformed into a theme park.[^cite_note-8] His life has also served as inspiration for or has been dramatized widely in film, television, and in music.

## Early life



The city of [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn), where Escobar grew up and began his criminal career

Pablo Emilio Escobar Gaviria was born on 1 December 1949 in [Rionegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rionegro), [Antioquia Department](185251.md). He belonged to the [Paisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paisa_\(region\)) ethnic subgroup. His family was of [Spanish](963152.md) origin, specifically from the [Basque Country](20514897.md), and also had [Italian](14532.md) roots.[^cite_note-9] He was the third of seven children and grew up in [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty), in the neighboring city of Medellín. His father was a small farmer and his mother was a teacher. Escobar dropped out of high school in 1966 shortly before his 17th birthday, before returning two years later with his cousin Gustavo Gaviria. At this time, the life they led on the streets of Medellín hardened them, and they were viewed as bullies in the eyes of teachers. The two dropped out of school after more than a year, but Escobar did not renounce getting an education.

## Criminal career

See also: [Illegal drug trade in Colombia](13005346.md), [Illegal drug trade in Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_drug_trade_in_Panama), and [Illegal drug trade in the Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_drug_trade_in_the_Bahamas)

### Early

Escobar started his criminal career with his gang by stealing tombstones, sandblasting their inscriptions, and reselling them. After dropping out of school, Escobar began to join gangs to steal cars.[^cite_note-10] Escobar soon became involved in violent crime, employing criminals to kidnap people who owed him money and demand ransoms, sometimes tearing up ransom notes even when Escobar had received the ransom. His most famous kidnapping victim was businessman [Diego Echavarria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Echavarr%C3%ADa_Misas), who was kidnapped and eventually killed in the summer of 1971. Escobar received a \$50,000 ransom from the Echavarria family; his gang became well known for this kidnapping.[^cite_note-11]

### Medellín Cartel



International drug routes

Escobar had been involved in organized crime for a decade when the cocaine trade began to spread in Colombia in the mid-1970s. Escobar's meteoric rise caught the attention of the [Colombian Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_Department_of_Security) (DAS), who arrested him in May 1976 on his return from drug trafficking in Ecuador. DAS agents found 39 kg of cocaine in the spare tire of Escobar's car. Escobar managed to change the first judge in the lawsuit and bribed the second judge, so he was released along with other prisoners. The following year, the agent who arrested Escobar was assassinated. Escobar continued to bribe and intimidate Colombian law enforcement agencies in the same fashion. His [carrot-and-stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrot_and_stick) strategy of bribing public officials and political candidates in Colombia, in addition to sending [hitmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_killing) to murder the ones who rejected his bribes, came to be known as "silver or lead", meaning "money or death".[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-about-13][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowden200140–42-14] The Medellín Cartel and the [Cali Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali_Cartel) both managed to bribe Colombian politicians, and campaigned for both the [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Conservative_Party) and [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Liberal_Party) parties.[^cite_note-\:0-15][^cite_note-16] Hence, Escobar and many other Colombian drug lords were pulling strings in every level of the Colombian government because many of the political candidates whom they backed financially were eventually elected.[^cite_note-\:0-15] Although the Medellín Cartel was only established in the early 1970s, it expanded after Escobar met several drug lords on a farm in April 1978, and by the end of 1978 they had transported some 19,000 kilograms of cocaine to the United States.[^cite_note-17]

### Rise to prominence



Powder cocaine was manufactured, packaged, and sold by Pablo Escobar and [his associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn_Cartel), and eventually distributed to the U.S. drug market.

Soon, the demand for cocaine greatly increased in the [United States](3434750.md), which led to Escobar organizing more smuggling shipments, routes, and distribution networks in [South Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Florida), [California](5407.md), [Puerto Rico](23041.md), and other parts of the country. He and cartel co-founder [Carlos Lehder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Lehder) worked together to develop a new trans-shipment point in the [Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamas), an island called [Norman's Cay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman's_Cay) about 350 km (220 mi) southeast of the Florida coast. Escobar and [Robert Vesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Vesco) purchased most of the land on the island, which included a 1-kilometre (3,300 ft) airstrip, a harbor, a hotel, houses, boats, and aircraft, and they built a refrigerated warehouse to store the cocaine. According to his brother, Escobar did not purchase Norman's Cay; it was instead a sole venture of Lehder's. From 1978 to 1982, this was used as a central smuggling route for the Medellín Cartel. With the enormous profits generated by this route, Escobar was soon able to purchase 20 square kilometres (7.7 sq mi) of land in Antioquia for several million dollars, on which he built the [Hacienda Nápoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacienda_N%C3%A1poles). The luxury house he created contained a [zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo), a lake, a sculpture garden, a private bullring, and other amenities for his family and the cartel.[^cite_note-18]

### Escobar at the height of his power

See also: [Avianca Flight 203](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avianca_Flight_203) and [DAS Building bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAS_Building_bombing)

At the height of his power, Escobar was involved in philanthropy in Colombia and paid handsomely for the staff of his cocaine lab. Escobar spent millions developing some of Medellín's poorest neighborhoods. He built housing complexes, parks, football stadiums, hospitals, schools, and churches.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Escobar also entered politics in the 1980s and participated in and supported the formation of the Liberal Party of Colombia. In 1982, he was elected to the Colombian Congress. Although only an alternate, he was automatically granted parliamentary immunity and the right to a diplomatic passport under Colombian law. At the same time, Escobar was gradually becoming a public figure, and because of his charitable work, he was known as "Paisa Robin Hood". He allegedly once, in an interview, said that his fortune came from a bicycle rental company he founded when he was 16 years old.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowden200148–57-21]



The Justice Minister [Rodrigo Lara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Lara_Bonilla) (center) and presidential candidate [Luis Carlos Galán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Carlos_Gal%C3%A1n) (left) were both assassinated on Escobar's orders.

In Congress, the new Minister of Justice, [Rodrigo Lara-Bonilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Lara_Bonilla), had become Escobar's opponent, accusing Escobar of criminal activity from the first day of Congress. Escobar's arrest in 1976 was investigated by Lara-Bonilla's subordinates. A few months later, Liberal leader [Luis Carlos Galán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Carlos_Gal%C3%A1n) expelled Escobar from the party. Although Escobar fought back, he announced his retirement from politics in January 1984. Three months later, Lara-Bonilla was murdered.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowden200163–67-22]

Escobar also maintained relations with the [Sandinista government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) in [Nicaragua](21362.md) and hired the [National Liberation Army (ELN)](822032.md) to carry out bombings and [assassinations](2963.md) against government targets.[^cite_note-\:1-23] It has also been alleged that he had good relations with [communist Cuba](5042481.md).[^cite_note-24]

The Colombian judiciary had been a target of Escobar throughout the mid-1980s. While bribing and murdering several judges, in the fall of 1985, the wanted Escobar requested the Colombian government to allow his conditional surrender without extradition to the United States. The proposal was initially rejected, and Escobar subsequently founded and implicitly supported the *Los Extraditable* Organization, which aims to fight the extradition policy. The *Los Extraditable* Organization was subsequently accused of attempting to prevent the Colombian Supreme Court from studying the constitutionality of Colombia's extradition treaty with the United States. It supported the far-left guerrilla movement that attacked the Colombian Judiciary Building in the [Palace of Justice siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Justice_siege) of 6 November 1985, in which 98 people died, including nearly half of the justices of the Supreme Court. In late 1986, Colombia's Supreme Court declared the previous extradition treaty illegal due to being signed by a presidential delegation, not the president. Escobar's victory over the judiciary was short-lived, with new president [Virgilio Barco Vargas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgilio_Barco_Vargas) having quickly renewed his agreement with the United States.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowden200182–85-26]

Escobar still held a grudge against Luis Carlos Galán for kicking him out of politics, so Galán was assassinated on 18 August 1989 on Escobar's orders. Escobar then planted a bomb on [Avianca Flight 203](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avianca_Flight_203) in an attempt to assassinate Galán's successor, [César Gaviria Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Gaviria), who missed the plane and survived. All 107 people were killed in the blast. Because two Americans were also killed in the bombing, the U.S. government began to intervene directly.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBowden200193–94-27][^cite_note-28]

#### La Catedral prison

Main article: [La Catedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Catedral)

After the assassination of Luis Carlos Galán, the administration of [César Gaviria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Gaviria) moved against Escobar and the drug cartels. Eventually, the government negotiated with Escobar and convinced him to surrender and cease all criminal activity in exchange for a reduced sentence and preferential treatment during his captivity. Declaring an end to a series of previous violent acts meant to pressure authorities and public opinion, Escobar surrendered to Colombian authorities in 1991. Before he gave himself up, the extradition of Colombian citizens to the United States had been prohibited by the newly approved [Colombian Constitution of 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1991). This act was controversial, as it was suspected that Escobar and other drug lords had influenced members of the Constituent Assembly in passing the law. Escobar was confined in what became his luxurious private prison, [La Catedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Catedral), which featured a football pitch, a giant dollhouse, a bar, a Jacuzzi, and a waterfall. Accounts of Escobar's continued criminal activities while in prison began to surface in the media, which prompted the government to attempt to move him to a more conventional jail on 22 July 1992. Escobar's influence allowed him to discover the plan in advance and make a successful escape, spending the remainder of his life evading the police.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## Death

Main article: [Death of Pablo Escobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Pablo_Escobar)

See also: [Los Pepes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Pepes) and [Search Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_Bloc)

Escobar faced threats from the Colombian police, the U.S. government and his rivals, the Cali Cartel. On 2 December 1993, a day after his 44th birthday, Escobar was found in a house in a middle-class residential area of Medellín by Colombian special forces, using technology provided by the United States which allowed them to trace Escobar's location after he made a call to his family. Police tried to arrest Escobar, but the situation quickly escalated to an exchange of gunfire. Escobar was shot and killed while trying to escape from the roof, along with a bodyguard who was also shot. He was hit by bullets in the torso and feet, and a bullet which struck him in the head, killing him. This sparked debate about whether he killed himself or whether he was shot and killed.[^cite_note-about-13]

## Aftermath of his death

Soon after Escobar's death and the subsequent fragmentation of the Medellín Cartel, the cocaine market became dominated by the rival Cali Cartel until the mid-1990s when its leaders were either killed or captured by the Colombian government. The [Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood) image that Escobar had cultivated maintained a lasting influence in Medellín. Many there, especially many of the city's poor whom Escobar had aided while he was alive, mourned his death, and over 25,000 people attended his funeral. Some of them consider him a saint and pray to him for receiving divine help. Escobar was buried at the Monte Sacro Cemetery.[^cite_note-31]

### Virginia Vallejo's testimony

See also: [Virginia Vallejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Vallejo), [Alberto Santofimio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Santofimio), [Alfonso López Michelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_L%C3%B3pez_Michelsen), [Ernesto Samper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Samper), and [Álvaro Uribe](243680.md)

On 4 July 2006, [Virginia Vallejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Vallejo), a television anchorwoman romantically involved with Escobar from 1983 to 1987, offered Attorney General [Mario Iguarán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Germ%C3%A1n_Iguar%C3%A1n_Arana) her testimony in the trial against former Senator [Alberto Santofimio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Santofimio), who was accused of [conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_conspiracies) in the 1989 assassination of presidential candidate Luis Carlos Galán. Iguarán acknowledged that, although Vallejo had contacted his office on 4 July, the judge had decided to close the trial on 9 July, several weeks before the prospective closing date. The action was seen as too late.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

On 18 July 2006, Vallejo was taken to the United States on a special flight of the [Drug Enforcement Administration](146720.md) (DEA) for "safety and security reasons" due to her cooperation in high-profile criminal cases.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] On 24 July, a video in which Vallejo had accused Santofimio of instigating Escobar to eliminate presidential candidate Galán was aired by [RCN Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCN_Television) of Colombia. The video was seen by 14 million people, and was instrumental for the reopened case of Galán's assassination. On 31 August 2011 Santofimio was sentenced to 24 years in prison for his role in the crime.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

#### Role in the Palace of Justice siege



Escobar funded the [M-19 communist guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_of_April_Movement) for the assault of the Colombian Palace of Justice.

Among Escobar's biographers, only Vallejo has given a detailed explanation of his role in the 1985 [Palace of Justice siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Justice_siege). She stated that Escobar had financed the operation, which was committed by [M-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_of_April_Movement); she blamed the army for the killings of more than 100 people, including 11 Supreme Court magistrates, M-19 members, and employees of the cafeteria. Her statements prompted the reopening of the case in 2008; Vallejo was asked to testify, and many of the events she had described in her book and testimonial were confirmed by Colombia's Commission of Truth.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] These events led to further investigation into the siege that resulted with the conviction of a high-ranking former colonel and a former general, later sentenced to 30 and 35 years in prison, respectively, for the [forced disappearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_disappearance) of the detained after the siege.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] Vallejo would subsequently testify in Galán's assassination.[^cite_note-42] In her book, *Amando a Pablo, odiando a Escobar* (*[Loving Pablo, Hating Escobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loving_Pablo,_Hating_Escobar)*), she had accused several politicians, including Colombian presidents [Alfonso López Michelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_L%C3%B3pez_Michelsen), [Ernesto Samper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Samper), and [Álvaro Uribe](243680.md) of having links to drug cartels.[^cite_note-43]

### Relatives

Escobar's widow (María Henao, now María Isabel Santos Caballero), son (Juan Pablo, now [Sebastián Marroquín Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Marroqu%C3%ADn)) and daughter (Manuela) fled Colombia in 1995 after failing to find a country that would grant them asylum.[^cite_note-BBCNovember1999-44] Despite Escobar's numerous and continual infidelities, Maria remained supportive of her husband. Members of the Cali Cartel even replayed their recordings of her conversations with Pablo for their wives to demonstrate how a woman should behave.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEscobar2014466-45] This attitude proved to be the reason the cartel did not kill her and her children after Pablo's death, although the group demanded and received millions of dollars in reparations for Escobar's war against them. Henao even successfully negotiated for her son's life by personally guaranteeing he would not seek revenge against the cartel or participate in the drug trade.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEscobar2014468–495-46]



[Sebastián Marroquín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Marroqu%C3%ADn) (born as Juan Pablo Escobar) is an outspoken critic of the violent deeds of his father.

After escaping first to [Mozambique](19301.md), then to [Brazil](3383.md), the family settled in [Argentina](18951905.md).[^cite_note-47] Living under her assumed name, Henao became a successful real estate entrepreneur until one of her business associates discovered her true identity, and Henao absconded with her earnings. Local media were alerted, and after being exposed as Escobar's widow, Henao was imprisoned for eighteen months while her finances were investigated. Ultimately, authorities were unable to link her funds to illegal activity, and she was released.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEscobar2014521–537-48] According to her son, Henao fell in love with Escobar "because of his naughty smile \[and] the way he looked at \[her]. \[He] was affectionate and sweet. A great lover. I fell in love with his desire to help people and his compassion for their hardship. We \[would] drive to places where he dreamed of building schools for the poor. From \[the] beginning, he was always a gentleman."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEscobar201468-49] María Victoria Henao de Escobar, with her new identity as María Isabel Santos Caballero, continues to live in [Buenos Aires](255919.md) with her son and daughter.[^cite_note-50] On 5 June 2018, the Argentine federal judge Nestor Barral accused her and her son, [Sebastián Marroquín Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Marroqu%C3%ADn), of money laundering with two Colombian drug traffickers.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] The judge ordered the seizing of assets for about \$1m each.[^cite_note-54]

Argentinian filmmaker Nicolas Entel's documentary *[Sins of My Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sins_of_My_Father_\(film\))* (2009) chronicles Marroquín's efforts to seek forgiveness, on behalf of his father, from the sons of Rodrigo Lara, Colombia's justice minister who was assassinated in 1984, as well as from the sons of Luis Carlos Galán, the presidential candidate who was assassinated in 1989. The film was shown at the 2010 [Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival) and premiered in the U.S. on [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) in October 2010.[^cite_note-CNNDecember2009-55] In 2014, Marroquín published *Pablo Escobar, My Father* under his birth name. The book provides a firsthand insight into details of his father's life and describes the fundamentally disintegrating effect of his death upon the family. Marroquín aimed to publish the book in hopes to resolve any inaccuracies regarding his father's excursions during the 1990s.[^cite_note-56]

Escobar's sister, Luz Maria Escobar, made multiple gestures in attempts to make amends for the drug baron's crimes. These include making public statements in the press, leaving letters on the graves of his victims, and, on the 20th anniversary of his death, organizing a public memorial for his victims.[^cite_note-57] Escobar's body was exhumed on 28 October 2006 at the request of some of his relatives in order to take a [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) sample to confirm the alleged paternity of an illegitimate child and remove all doubt about the identity of the body that had been buried next to his parents for 12 years.[^cite_note-58] A video of the exhumation was broadcast by [RCN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCN_Televisi%C3%B3n), angering Marroquín, who accused his uncle, [Roberto Escobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Escobar), and cousin, Nicolas Escobar, of being "merchants of death" by allowing the video to air.[^cite_note-59]

### Hacienda Nápoles

After Escobar's death, the ranch, zoo and citadel at [Hacienda Nápoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacienda_N%C3%A1poles) were given by the government to low-income families under a law called *Extinción de Dominio* (Domain Extinction). The property has been converted into a [theme park](137327.md) surrounded by four luxury hotels overlooking the zoo.[^cite_note-BBCJune2008-60]

### Hippos

Main article: [Hippopotamuses in Colombia](65074241.md)

Escobar kept four hippos in a private menagerie at Hacienda Nápoles. They were deemed too difficult to seize and move after Escobar's death, and hence left on the untended estate. By 2007, the animals had multiplied to 16 and had taken to roaming the area for food in the nearby [Magdalena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_River).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-baron-62] In 2009, two adults and one calf escaped the herd and, after attacking humans and killing cattle, one of the adults (called "Pepe") was killed by hunters under authorization of the local authorities.[^cite_note-baron-62] As of early 2014, 40 hippos have been reported to exist in Puerto Triunfo, Antioquia Department, from the original four belonging to Escobar.[^cite_note-63] As of 2016, without management, the population size is likely to more than double in the next decade.[^cite_note-64]

The [National Geographic Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Channel) produced a documentary about them titled *Cocaine Hippos*.[^cite_note-65] A report published in a Yale student magazine noted that local environmentalists are campaigning to protect the animals, although there is no clear plan for what will happen to them.[^cite_note-Zoo_Gone_Wild-66] In 2018, National Geographic published another article on the hippos which found disagreement among environmentalists on whether they were having a positive or negative impact but that conservationists and locals – particularly those in the tourism industry – were mostly in support of their continued presence.[^cite_note-67] By October 2021, the Colombian government had started a program of chemically sterilizing the animals.[^cite_note-68]

### Apartment demolition

On 22 February 2019, at 11\:53 AM local time, Medellín authorities demolished the six-story [Edificio Mónaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edificio_M%C3%B3naco?action=edit\&redlink=1) apartment complex in the [El Poblado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Poblado) neighborhood where, according to retired Colombian general [Rosso José Serrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosso_Jos%C3%A9_Serrano), Escobar planned some of his most brazen attacks. The building was initially built for Escobar's wife but was gutted by a Cali Cartel [car bomb](155282.md) in 1988 and had remained unoccupied ever since, becoming an attraction to foreign tourists seeking out Escobar's physical legacy. Mayor [Federico Gutierrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Gutierrez) had been pushing to raze the building and erect in its place a park honoring the thousands of cartel victims, including four presidential candidates and some 500 police officers. Colombian president [Ivan Duque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Duque) said the demolition "means that history is not going to be written in terms of the perpetrators, but by recognizing the victims", hoping the demolition would showcase that the city had evolved significantly and had more to offer than the legacy left by the cartels.[^cite_note-69]

## Personal life

### Family and relationships

In March 1976, the 26-year-old Escobar married María Victoria Henao, who was 15. The relationship was discouraged by the Henao family, who considered Escobar socially inferior; the pair eloped.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEscobar201474-70] They had two children: Juan Pablo (now [Sebastián Marroquín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Marroqu%C3%ADn)) and Manuela. In 2007, the journalist [Virginia Vallejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Vallejo) published her memoir *Amando a Pablo, odiando a Escobar* (*[Loving Pablo, Hating Escobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loving_Pablo,_Hating_Escobar)*), in which she describes her romantic relationship with Escobar and the links of her lover with several presidents, Caribbean dictators, and high-profile politicians.[^cite_note-71] Her book inspired the movie *[Loving Pablo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loving_Pablo)* (2017).[^cite_note-72] A drug distributor, [Griselda Blanco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griselda_Blanco#Personal_life), is also reported to have conducted a clandestine but passionate relationship with Escobar; several items in her diary link him with the nicknames "Coque de Mi Rey" (My Coke King) and "Polla Blanca" (White Cock).[^cite_note-73]

### Religious and political views

Escobar was a [Roman Catholic](606848.md) and was described by a [priest](8655005.md), Father Elkin, as a profoundly [devout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_devotions) man who was led astray by his ambitions. Escobar reportedly told Father Elkin "It's not important to be [repentant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repentance_in_Christianity#Catholicism), I am a believer." Escobar would attend confession; he even claimed to have experienced a vision of [Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_in_Christianity).[^cite_note-74]

Escobar is known to have stated that "they try to present me as an associate of the guerilla... hurts my personal dignity... I am a man of investments and therefore I cannot sympathise with the guerillas who fight against property". His alliance with the [M-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_of_April_Movement) largely stemmed from pragmatic mutual opposition to the [United States](3434750.md) rather than underlying political alignment.[^cite_note-75] He relied on a strategy of populism to build rapport with the Colombian poor.[^cite_note-76]

### Properties

After becoming wealthy, Escobar created or bought numerous residences and [safe houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_house), with the [Hacienda Nápoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacienda_N%C3%A1poles) gaining significant notoriety. The luxury house contained a colonial house, a sculpture park, and a complete zoo with animals from various continents, including [elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant), exotic birds, [giraffes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giraffe), and [hippopotamuses](37764.md). Escobar had also planned to construct a Greek-style [citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel) near it, and though construction of the citadel was started, it was never finished.[^cite_note-BBCJune2008-60]

Escobar owned a home in the US under his own name: a 6,500 square foot (604 m<sup>2</sup>), pink, waterfront mansion situated at 5860 North Bay Road in [Miami Beach, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Beach,_Florida). The four-bedroom estate, built in 1948 on [Biscayne Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscayne_Bay), was seized by the US federal government in the 1980s. Later, the dilapidated property was owned by Christian de Berdouare, proprietor of the Chicken Kitchen fast-food chain, who had bought it in 2014. De Berdouare would later hire a documentary film crew and professional treasure hunters to search the edifice before and after demolition, for anything related to Escobar or his cartel. They would find unusual holes in floors and walls, as well as a safe that was stolen from its hole in the marble flooring before it could be properly examined.[^cite_note-77]

Escobar owned a huge Caribbean getaway on Isla Grande, the largest of the cluster of the 27 [coral cluster islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_island) comprising [Islas del Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islas_del_Rosario), located about 35 km (22 mi) from [Cartagena](63927.md). The compound, now half-demolished and overtaken by vegetation and wild animals, featured a mansion, apartments, courtyards, a large swimming pool, a helicopter landing pad, reinforced windows, tiled floors, and a large but unfinished building to the side of the mansion.[^cite_note-78]
