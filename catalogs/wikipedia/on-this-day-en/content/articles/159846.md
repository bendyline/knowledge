**MS NOW** (formerly **MSNBC**) is an American [cable news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_news) channel owned by [Versant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versant). The channel primarily broadcasts rolling news coverage and [opinion journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_journalism). Its studios are in Versant's world headquarters at [229 West 43rd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/229_West_43rd_Street), [New York City](645042.md). It also maintains a bureau in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), co-located with that of former parent division [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News), while technical operations are handled from [CNBC](223441.md)'s studios in [Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Englewood_Cliffs,_New_Jersey).

Among cable news channels, the company is ranked second in ratings, well behind [Fox News](11121.md), but ahead of [CNN](62028.md). As of July 2026, *[The Rachel Maddow Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rachel_Maddow_Show)* on MS NOW was the 7th ranked news program by number of viewers; the top 6 were all on Fox News.[^cite_note-1]

The channel was established in 1996 as MSNBC, a joint venture between NBC News and [Microsoft](19001.md) (with its name being a [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) of "[MSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSN)" and "[NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC)") that encompassed the cable channel and the [web portal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_portal) MSNBC.com. Microsoft divested its stake in the television channel in 2005, followed by the website in 2012; the website was subsequently rebranded as [NBCNews.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCNews.com), and MSNBC.com was later relaunched to focus on editorial content from the network's personalities and programming.

It initially focused on rolling news coverage, including long-form reports, interactive programs, and stories contributed by the local news departments of NBC's affiliates. By the late 2000s, the network shifted to primarily [opinion journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_journalism) featuring liberal commentators such as [Keith Olbermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Olbermann), [Chris Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Matthews), [David Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gregory_\(journalist\)), [Ed Schultz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Schultz), and [Rachel Maddow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Maddow); in 2010, MSNBC surpassed CNN in primetime and overall viewership for the first time since 2001. In the mid-2010s, amid a decline in viewership, MSNBC concentrated on hard news coverage, and added programs incorporating NBC News personalities.

Under new leadership in the 2020s, MSNBC began reducing its reliance on NBC News personalities, and expanded its own newsgathering resources. In November 2025, ahead of the [corporate spin-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off) of Versant by [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast), MSNBC separated itself from [NBCUniversal Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCUniversal_Media_Group) and rebranded as "MS NOW" (a [backronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backronym) of "My Source \[for] News, Opinion, \[and the] World") to remove the overt use of NBC trademarks.

Programming is simulcast on [SiriusXM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SiriusXM)[^cite_note-2] and via [TuneIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TuneIn).[^cite_note-3] Content is also provided via a [YouTube](3524766.md) channel[^cite_note-4] and a [streaming service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_service) with live streaming of the channel.

### 1996–2007



MSNBC's logo used from 1996 until 2009. The "N" in the logo was changed from red to black in 2002. This variant was occasionally used after 2006 as an alternative logo in a horizontal form.

MSNBC was established in 1996 via a [joint venture](538782.md) between [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) and [Microsoft](19001.md) organized by [Tom Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Rogers_\(executive\)).[^cite_note-5] [James Kinsella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Kinsella_\(entrepreneur\)), a Microsoft executive, served as president of the online component, MSNBC.com.[^cite_note-6] Microsoft invested \$221 million for a 50% share of the cable channel.[^cite_note-7] The network launched on July 15, 1996. The first show was anchored by [Jodi Applegate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodi_Applegate) and included news, interviews, and [opinion pieces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_piece).[^cite_note-8] The first three news stories MSNBC covered were [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin) meeting then-[vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_president) [Al Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore) despite concerns over Yeltsin's health, an update on the [Bob Dole 1996 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dole_1996_presidential_campaign), and updated information about the [Khobar Towers bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khobar_Towers_bombing).[^cite_note-9]

During the day, news coverage continued with *The Contributors*, a show that featured [Ann Coulter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Coulter) and [Laura Ingraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Ingraham), as well as interactive programming coordinated by Applegate, [John Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gibson_\(media_host\)), and [John Seigenthaler Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Seigenthaler_Jr.) Stories were generally longer and more detailed than those of [CNN](62028.md). It also aired stories and breaking news coverage directly from NBC's network affiliates.[^cite_note-10] Its flagship prime time newscast was *[The News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_News_with_Brian_Williams)*, hosted by [Brian Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Williams).[^cite_note-11] and *[The Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Site)*, a show about the Internet and computers co-hosted by [Soledad O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soledad_O'Brien) and a "live" computer-generated character called Dev Null played by [Leo Laporte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Laporte).[^cite_note-12]

At launch, the network positioned itself as being integrated with the Internet, with the slogan "It's Time to Get Connected", anchors' [email addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email_address) and phone numbers being promoted regularly on-air,[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] and shows such as *Internight—*&#x61; primetime talk show featuring viewer questions submitted via phone and email, and *[The Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Site)*—a technology news program co-anchored by [Soledad O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soledad_O'Brien) and [Leo Laporte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Laporte) (the latter as a computer-animated character known as "Dev Null").[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Beginning in September 1996, MSNBC began to air a simulcast of the New York-based radio show *[Imus in the Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imus_in_the_Morning)* with [Don Imus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Imus).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

MSNBC was originally headquartered from studios in [Fort Lee, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lee,_New_Jersey). In November 1996, MSNBC moved to new studios in [Secaucus, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secaucus,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-20]

*[SportsCenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SportsCenter)* host [Keith Olbermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Olbermann) left [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) in an acrimonious split to join MSNBC in June 1997;[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] with *Internight* replaced by a new 8 p.m. show known as *The Big Show with Keith Olbermann*.[^cite_note-23] When the [Monica Lewinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Lewinsky) scandal broke in 1998, *The Big Show* morphed into *White House in Crisis*. Olbermann became frustrated as his show was consumed by the Lewinsky story. He left MSNBC and joined [FSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports_Networks).[^cite_note-24] Olbermann was then replaced by *Dateline NBC* host [John Hockenberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hockenberry), whose show *[Edgewise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgewise)* became popular.[^cite_note-25]

In September 1997, MSNBC laid off 20% of its staf&#x66;*,* and cancelled *The Site* due to low ratings and frequent pre-emptions for coverage of the deaths of [Princess Diana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Diana,_Princess_of_Wales) of [Mother Teresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Teresa).[^cite_note-26] That show was replaced by *Headliners and Legends*, a biography program that became a weekend staple on the network.[^cite_note-27]

A fictionalized version of MSNBC was featured in *[Deep Impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Impact_\(film\))* (1998).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

In 1999, *[Hardball with Chris Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardball_with_Chris_Matthews)* moved to MSNBC and replaced Hockenberry's 8PM show *Edgewise*. Prior to this, [Chris Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Matthews) hosted *Politics with Chris Matthews* on MSNBC predecessor [America's Talking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Talking). *Hardball* ran to March 2020.[^cite_note-30]

In January 2001, [Mike Barnicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Barnicle)'s show was launched, but it was canceled in June 2001 because of high production costs.[^cite_note-31]

Beginning in January 2001, [Mitch Albom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_Albom) hosted his own two-hour program *The Mitch Albom Show* as an experimental cross-network feature that lasted a few months. Because it was produced primarily for radio listeners, it did not fit the traditional, visually driven cable news format.[^cite_note-32]

In early 2001, Erik Sorenson was named president of MSNBC.[^cite_note-33] [Ashleigh Banfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Banfield) was hired as an anchor to attract a younger audience.[^cite_note-34] Banfield was present during the collapse of [7 World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_World_Trade_Center_\(1987%E2%80%932001\)) while covering the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md).[^cite_note-35] Her *Region in Conflict* program showcased exclusive interviews from [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-36]

MSNBC began calling itself "America's NewsChannel" and hired [Alan Keyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Keyes), [Phil Donahue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Donahue), [Pat Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Buchanan), and [Tucker Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson).[^cite_note-37] This branding makeover, however, was followed by declining ratings.[^cite_note-38]

*[The News with Brian Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_News_with_Brian_Williams)* was relocated to [CNBC](223441.md) in 2002.[^cite_note-39]

In December 2005, NBC Universal announced its acquisition of an additional 32% share of MSNBC from Microsoft.[^cite_note-40] The news website msnbc.com remained a separate joint venture between Microsoft and NBC until 2012.[^cite_note-buys-41]

Two years later, NBC exercised its option to purchase Microsoft's remaining 18% interest in MSNBC.

In June 2006, [Rick Kaplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Kaplan) resigned as president of MSNBC after holding the post for two years.[^cite_note-42] [Dan Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Abrams), a nine-year veteran of MSNBC and NBC News, was named general manager of MSNBC.[^cite_note-43] NBC News senior vice president [Phil Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Griffin_\(presenter\)) oversaw MSNBC while continuing to oversee *[Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_\(American_TV_program\))*, with Abrams reporting to Griffin. That month, Abrams announced the revamp of MSNBC's early-primetime and primetime schedule.[^cite_note-44]

In July 2006, *[Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_\(television_program\))* (formerly *The Situation with Tucker Carlson*) started airing at 4PM and 6PM ET, taking over Abrams' old timeslot, while [Rita Cosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Cosby)'s *Live & Direct* was canceled. Cosby was made the primary anchor for *MSNBC Investigates* at 10PM and 11PM ET, a new program that took over Cosby and Carlson's timeslots.[^cite_note-45]

In August 2006, [Keith Olbermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Olbermann) began critiquing and satirizing [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) media commentators such as [Bill O'Reilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_O'Reilly_\(political_commentator\)) and politicians such as [George W. Bush](3414021.md) during his *[Countdown With Keith Olbermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countdown_With_Keith_Olbermann)* program; this attracted liberal viewers and ratings of the show doubled.[^cite_note-46]

In September 2007, Abrams announced that he was leaving his general manager position so he could focus on his 9PM ET talk show, *Live With Dan Abrams*. Oversight of MSNBC was shifted to [Phil Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Griffin_\(presenter\)).[^cite_note-47]



MSNBC's studio in NYC



The MSNBC studio

In October 2007, MSNBC and NBC News began broadcasting from new studios at [30 Rockefeller Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_Rockefeller_Plaza) in New York City.[^cite_note-48] The extensive renovations of the associated studios allowed NBC to merge its entire news operation into one building. All MSNBC broadcasts and *[NBC Nightly News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Nightly_News)* originate from the new studios. MSNBC also announced new studios near the [Universal Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Studios,_Inc.) lot.[^cite_note-49] MSNBC's master control did not make the move to [30 Rockefeller Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_Rockefeller_Plaza). It remained in the old Secaucus headquarters until it completed its move to the NBC Universal Network Origination Center inside the CNBC Global Headquarters building in [Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Englewood_Cliffs,_New_Jersey) in 2007. In January 2009, [Major League Baseball](38776.md) began using the former MSNBC building in Secaucus for [MLB Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Network).[^cite_note-50]

### 2008–2015



MSNBC logo used from 2009 to 2015

From mid-2007 to mid-2008, MSNBC enjoyed a large increase in its [Nielsen ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_ratings).[^cite_note-Flak-51] Primetime viewings increased by 61%.[^cite_note-Flak-51]

[Tucker Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson)'s program was canceled in March 2008 and replaced at the 6PM slot with *Race for the White House*, hosted by [David Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gregory_\(journalist\)).[^cite_note-52] The show was renamed *[1600 Pennsylvania Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1600_Pennsylvania_Avenue_\(TV\))* in November, after the election. When Gregory became the permanent host for *[Meet the Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_the_Press)*, he stepped down as host of his MSNBC show, and [David Shuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Shuster) became the permanent host for the show.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

The death of [Tim Russert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Russert) in June 2008 led to a period of transition as NBC contemplated how he could be replaced.[^cite_note-fights-55][^cite_note-56]

In July 2008, former CNN producer [Phil Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Griffin_\(presenter\)) was announced as President of MSNBC, replacing [Dan Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Abrams). He worked with NBC for over 25 years, including working for MSNBC since its launch.[^cite_note-57]

During the [2008 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election), MSNBC's coverage was anchored by Olbermann, [Chris Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Matthews), and [David Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gregory_\(journalist\)).[^cite_note-fights-55] During the first three months of the presidential campaign, MSNBC's ratings grew by 158%.[^cite_note-Maddow-58] Olbermann and Matthews, however, were criticized for expressing left-leaning opinions on the channel. Both were later removed from anchor positions.[^cite_note-Incendiary-59] Audience viewership ahead of the 2008 elections more than doubled from the [2004 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election), and the channel topped CNN in ratings for the first time during the last three months of the campaign in the key 25–54 age demographic.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-Incendiary-59]

In September 2008, the channel hired [Rachel Maddow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Maddow) to host *[The Rachel Maddow Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rachel_Maddow_Show)* in the 9PM slot, replacing *Verdict with Dan Abrams*.[^cite_note-61] Reviews for the show were mostly positive.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-Maddow-58] The move to create a new program for the network was widely seen as a smart ratings move, where beforehand, the network lagged behind in coveted primetime ratings.[^cite_note-Maddow-58] The show began to regularly outperform CNN's *[Larry King Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_King_Live)*, and made the network competitive in the program's time slot for the first time in over a decade.[^cite_note-ontop-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

In March 2009, MSNBC finished in second place in primetime, ahead of CNN for the first time. [Phil Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Griffin_\(presenter\)) attributed this to the network's decision to go liberal with Olbermann and Maddow along with problems at CNN.[^cite_note-68]

On several dates in March 2009, [Ed Schultz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Schultz) served as a substitute host for [David Shuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Shuster) on *[1600 Pennsylvania Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1600_Pennsylvania_Avenue_\(TV\))*.[^cite_note-69] In April 2009, Schultz was given the 6PM ET slot with *[The Ed Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ed_Show)*, replacing *1600 Pennsylvania Avenue*, with Shuster being reassigned by the network as substitute host for Olbermann on *Countdown*.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

In June 2009, MSNBC launched a [1080i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1080i) [high-definition television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television) simulcast feed, MSNBC HD.[^cite_note-72]

*[Morning Meeting with Dylan Ratigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Meeting_with_Dylan_Ratigan)*, a news show hosted by [Dylan Ratigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Ratigan), debuted in June 2009 as part of changes to MSNBC's weekday daytime program schedule, along with a revamp of the channel's graphics and the launch of its high definition simulcast feed.[^cite_note-73] The show was relaunched as *[The Dylan Ratigan Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dylan_Ratigan_Show)* in January 2010. The change was made to make room for *The Daily Rundown* with Chuck Todd and Savannah Guthrie at 9AM, as part of MSNBC's commitment to straight news programming during the day.[^cite_note-74] The show focused on debate and discussion relating to politics and the economy. Ratigan often offered commentary on the subject matter and rebuttal to many of the guests who appear on the show.

In the first quarter of 2010, MSNBC beat CNN in primetime and overall ratings for the first time since 2001.[^cite_note-Tops-75] *[Morning Joe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Joe)*, *[The Ed Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ed_Show)*, *[Hardball with Chris Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardball_with_Chris_Matthews)*, *[Countdown with Keith Olbermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countdown_with_Keith_Olbermann)*, and *The Rachel Maddow Show* finished ahead of their time slot competitors at CNN.[^cite_note-Tops-75][^cite_note-Surges-76]

In the third quarter of 2010, MSNBC beat CNN in total day for the first time since the second quarter of 2001 in the key adult demographic.[^cite_note-Beats-77] MSNBC also beat CNN for the fourth consecutive quarter, among both primetime and total viewers, and became the only cable news network to have its key adult demographic viewership grow during the quarter, increasing by 4%. MSNBC also became the number-one cable news network in primetime among both African American and Hispanic viewers.[^cite_note-Beats-77]

In April 2010, MSNBC launched on [XM Satellite Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM_Satellite_Radio) and [Sirius Satellite Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirius_Satellite_Radio).[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] This is the second time MSNBC has been available on satellite radio. The channel was dropped from XM Radio in September 2006.[^cite_note-80]

[Lawrence O'Donnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_O'Donnell) began hosting a 10PM show on MSNBC called *[The Last Word with Lawrence O'Donnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Word_with_Lawrence_O'Donnell)*, which premiered in September 2010, replacing *Countdown*.[^cite_note-81]

In October 2010, MSNBC unveiled a \$2 million marketing campaign, "Lean Forward". Griffin considered the two-year campaign an effort to promote the channel as a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States) competitor to the [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States)-leaning [Fox News Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_News_Channel).[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-forward-83]

In January 2011, Olbermann announced his departure from MSNBC.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] MSNBC issued a statement that it had ended its contract with Olbermann, with no further explanation. Olbermann took his show to [Current TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_TV).[^cite_note-88]

In July 2012, Microsoft sold its stake in MSNBC.com to NBCUniversal for \$300 million; concurrently, the website was rebranded as [NBCNews.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCNews.com), while MSNBC.com was relaunched as a website for the channel itself.[^cite_note-buys-41] Concerns were raised by NBC News executives over potential confusion between the two properties due to their diverging editorial scopes, since MSNBC.com was largely a general-interest news website, while MSNBC pivoted towards political commentary.[^cite_note-89] In 2013, msnbc.com was relaunched as the website for the MSNBC channel.[^cite_note-90]

NBCUniversal News Group was created in July 2012, under chairwoman Pat Fili-Krushel composed of NBC News, [CNBC](223441.md), and MSNBC.[^cite_note-91]

In 2014, MSNBC's total ratings in the 25–54 demographic declined 20%, falling to third place behind CNN. Nevertheless, MSNBC retained its lead among the Hispanic and African-American demographics.[^cite_note-92]

In July 2014, msnbc.com launched *msnbc2*, a brand for several web-only series hosted by MSNBC personalities.[^cite_note-93] In December 2014, msnbc2 was renamed *[shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shift_\(MSNBC\))*, with a programming schedule that was less focused on politics and more tailored to a younger audience.[^cite_note-94] It was shut in 2015.

MSNBC logo used from 2015 to 2021

[Andrew Lack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lack_\(executive\)) became the chairman of NBC News and MSNBC in 2015. He imposed a mandate on the network to reduce its emphasis on opinion programming, and place a larger focus on creating closer ties between it and NBC News.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

In 2015, Griffin announced he was transitioning MSNBC from left-leaning, opinionated programming to hard news programming. Nearly all daytime opinionated news programs were replaced with more generic news programs. In September 2016, MSNBC launched *[The 11th Hour with Brian Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_11th_Hour_with_Brian_Williams),* a new 11 p.m. program that initially served as a daily recap of headlines relating to the [2016 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

In January 2017, MSNBC debuted *[For the Record with Greta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_the_Record_with_Greta)* at 6PM hosted by former Fox News Channel anchor [Greta Van Susteren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Van_Susteren).[^cite_note-104] The program aired for six months before being cancelled in late June 2017 due to a ratings flop.[^cite_note-105] The network's chief legal correspondent, Ari Melber was given a show, *[The Beat with Ari Melber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beat_with_Ari_Melber)*, at 6PM.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

In March 2017, MSNBC began to increase its use of the NBC News branding during daytime news programming, as part of an effort to emphasize MSNBC's relationship with the division.[^cite_note-108] Promotional campaigns including the slogan "This is who we are" were aired in March 2017, featuring network personalities explaining parallels between politics and activities such as sports.[^cite_note-109]

In May 2017, MSNBC introduced *[Deadline: White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline:_White_House)*, a late night program hosted by NBC political analyst and former White House communications director [Nicolle Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolle_Wallace).[^cite_note-110] That month, during the [first presidency of Donald Trump](52231341.md), MSNBC became the highest rated American cable news network in primetime for the first time, beating both Fox News and CNN as it covered allegations of [Russian interference in the 2016 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_interference_in_the_2016_United_States_elections).[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-Surges-76]

MSNBC also retired its on-air [news ticker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_ticker), citing a desire to reduce distractions and "\[put] our reporting more front and center".[^cite_note-112]

### 2021–2024



MSNBC logo used from 2021 to 2023

In December 2020, Phil Griffin stepped down from the network, and it was announced that NBC News' then-senior vice president of special programming [Rashida Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashida_Jones_\(television_executive\)) would succeeded him as president of MSNBC beginning in 2021; she became the first African-American woman to lead a U.S. cable news channel[^cite_note-\:1-116] Jones would aim to create a larger distinction between news and analysis programming to "create more opportunities for collaboration" across the network (with [Daniel Arnall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Arnall) and *The Last Word* EP Greg Kordick named senior vice presidents of news and analysis respectively), and announced plans to appoint a head of documentary acquisitions to oversee an expansion in long-form content.[^cite_note-\:1-116][^cite_note-117]

In March 2021, MSNBC introduced a refreshed logo and on-air imaging, including a rebranding of its *MSNBC Live* rolling news block as *[MSNBC Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSNBC_Reports)* (with each block carrying the anchor's name, patterned after the existing daytime show *[Andrea Mitchell Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Mitchell_Reports)*).[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] Under Jones, MSNBC began to scale back its integrations with NBC News, with some personalities and reporters leaving the network, or prioritizing contributions to NBC News' streaming channel [NBC News Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News_Now) instead.[^cite_note-Cross-120][^cite_note-121]

MSNBC gained a presence on NBCUniversal's [Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_\(streaming_service\)) streaming service by co-branding its streaming hub "The Choice" as "The Choice by MSNBC" in July 2021;[^cite_note-122] it included original news and opinion programs with personalities such as [Mehdi Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Hasan), [Zerlina Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zerlina_Maxwell), and [Sam Seder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Seder).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] In March 2022, it was replaced by an MSNBC hub, which included next-day, on-demand streaming of selected MSNBC programs, as well as specials and [documentaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSNBC_Documentaries).[^cite_note-126]

In November 2021, Brian Williams announced his departure from NBC News and MSNBC after 28 years, with his final edition of *The 11th Hour* scheduled for December 9; after a transitional period covered by guest hosts,[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] Stephanie Ruhle became the new host of *The 11th Hour* beginning March 2, with her existing hour of *[MSNBC Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_NOW_Reports)* replaced by a new fourth hour of *Morning Joe* in April 2022.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

As part of her new contract with NBCUniversal, Rachel Maddow took an extended hiatus from her program to focus on other film and [podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast) projects; she was replaced with rotating guest hosts. When she returned, beginning in May 2022, she only hosted the show on Monday nights and featured guest hosts throughout the rest of the week.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133] The guest hosts appeared under the *MSNBC Prime* banner until August 2022, when [Alex Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Wagner) became the permanent host in the timeslot with *[Alex Wagner Tonight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Wagner_Tonight)*.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

In October 2022, ahead of the [midterm elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_elections), *[The Last Word with Lawrence O'Donnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Word_with_Lawrence_O'Donnell)* was temporarily replaced on Friday evenings by *The Kornacki Countdown*, a weekly series hosted by MSNBC political analyst [Steve Kornacki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Kornacki).[^cite_note-136]

In November 2022, MSNBC parted ways with [Tiffany Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_Cross); her show *The Cross Connection*, was temporarily replaced by guest hosts under the *[MSNBC Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSNBC_Reports)* banner.[^cite_note-Cross-120]

In March 2023, MSNBC premiered the Sunday-morning program *[Inside with Jen Psaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_with_Jen_Psaki)*, hosted by former [White House press secretary](284101.md) [Jen Psaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jen_Psaki).[^cite_note-137] Between February and September 2023, the Monday edition of *[All in with Chris Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_in_with_Chris_Hayes)* also featured rotating guest hosts, with [Chris Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hayes) only hosting from Tuesdays to Fridays to accommodate Hayes' other projects. ### 2024–present



MSNBC logo used from 2023 to 2025

#### NBCU spinoff, personnel changes

On November 20, 2024, NBCUniversal announced its intent to spin off most of its cable networks, including MSNBC, as a new publicly traded company controlled by Comcast shareholders,[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] and later officially named [Versant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versant).[^cite_note-ComcastSpinoffName-145] Following the announcement, reports emerged of multiple unsolicited offers to buy MSNBC; CNN media analyst [Brian Stelter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Stelter) believed it was unlikely MSNBC would be sold, as NBCUniversal never declared any intent to divest properties when announcing the spin-off (with future CEO [Mark Lazarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Lazarus_\(businessman\)) contrarily suggesting that the spin-off planned to target further investments and acquisitions), a sale would incur taxes (the spin-off was structured to be tax-free), and that divesting the channel might not be seen as being in the best interest of shareholders.[^cite_note-146]

From January through April 2025, *The Rachel Maddow Show* returned to a weeknight schedule to cover the [first hundred days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_hundred_days_\(United_States\)) of the [second presidency of Donald Trump](78298108.md). During this time, *Alex Wagner Tonight* was placed on hiatus, and Wagner was placed on special assignment duty.[^cite_note-147]

In January 2025, Rashida Jones resigned as head of MSNBC.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] After acting as interim president, [Rebecca Kutler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Kutler) was appointed president of MSNBC in February 2025.[^cite_note-150] In preparation for the NBCU spin-off, Kutler began moving to expand the network's in-house newsgathering resources so that it could operate independently from NBC News.[^cite_note-\:2-151][^cite_note-\:3-152] On February 24, 2025, Kutler announced a revamp to MSNBC's primetime schedule, including the cancellation of its primetiime shows *The ReidOut* and *Alex Wagner Tonight* (with Joy Reid leaving the network effective immediately, and Wagner retained as a senior political correspondent), and a new show hosted by the panel of *The Weekend.[^cite_note-\:0-153][^cite_note-154]* Due to the planned closure of its studios in Miami, MSNBC also cancelled José Díaz-Balart and [Katie Phang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Phang)'s programs, with Díaz-Balart focusing on his roles at NBC News and [Noticias Telemundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noticias_Telemundo), and Phang later moving to the progressive podcast network [MeidasTouch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeidasTouch).[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-Shakeup-156]*[^cite_note-\:0-153]*[^cite_note-157]

Most of the staff that worked *The Rachel Maddow Show* and *Alex Wagner Tonight* were laid off and invited to reapply internally for new positions or receive [severance packages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_package). On that night's episode of *The Rachel Maddow Show*, Maddow criticized the changes and treatment of employees on-air, commenting that the release of Reid was "a bad mistake", and that the concurrent cancellations of multiple shows hosted by people of color (including Reid, Wagner, and Phang) was "unnerving".[^cite_note-158]

On May 5, MSNBC premiered *[The Weeknight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weeknight)* as its new 7 p.m. show, initially hosted by Alicia Menendez, Symone Sanders-Townsend, and Michael Steele.[^cite_note-\:3-152][^cite_note-\:4-159] In addition, *Inside with Jen Psaki* was renamed *[The Briefing with Jen Psaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Briefing_with_Jen_Psaki)* and expanded to a Tuesday–Friday schedule to replace *Alex Wagner Tonight*.[^cite_note-Primetime-160][^cite_note-Shakeup-156][^cite_note-\:3-152] *The Weekend* was also relaunched with a new panel consisting of [Jonathan Capehart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Capehart), Jacqueline Alemany, and new senior Washington correspondent [Eugene Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Daniels),[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162] while a new evening edition known as *The Weekend: Primetime* would feature [Ayman Mohyeldin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_Mohyeldin), [Catherine Rampell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Rampell), [Antonia Hylton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Hylton), and [Elise Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Jordan).[^cite_note-Primetime-160]

In April 2025, Steve Kornacki left MSNBC, focusing on a role as chief data analyst for NBC News and [NBC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sports).[^cite_note-163] Over mid-2025, MSNBC also hired [Ken Dilanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Dilanian), [Vaughn Hillyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaughn_Hillyard), David Noriega, [Brandy Zadrozny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandy_Zadrozny), and [Jacob Soboroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Soboroff), with Soboroff named senior national and political correspondent.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-Split-165]

#### Rebranding as MS NOW

In August 2025, it was announced that as part of the Versant spin-off, MSNBC would rebrand as MS NOW later in the year to signify its separation from NBC News.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] The name is a [backronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backronym) of "My Source \[for] News, Opinion, \[and the] World"; a name that contained "MS", reflecting original part-owner Microsoft, was chosen to maintain [brand awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_awareness), as "MS" had also been used as a shorthand name for the channel.[^cite_note-soon-169] The network also planned to relocate from NBC Studios to Versant's interim headquarters at the former New York Times Building on [229 West 43rd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/229_West_43rd_Street).[^cite_note-170]

In September 2025, MSNBC reached an agreement with [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News) to use its resources for international news coverage. MSNBC began to separate its editorial operations from NBC News in October 2025, and its use of NBC News correspondents was gradually reduced over the course of the month.[^cite_note-Split-165][^cite_note-mixes-171] Versant also reached agreements with Comcast sister company [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News) to provide international news coverage,[^cite_note-Reporting-172] and [AccuWeather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AccuWeather) to provide weather data and forecasts for both MS NOW and CNBC, and allow its meteorologists to appear as guests during weather events. MSNBC also hired former CBS News reporter David Parkinson to serve as senior weather and election data analyst, and Moses Small from [KGTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGTV)/San Diego as a climate reporter.[^cite_note-173]

A \$20 million advertising campaign entitled "[We the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preamble_to_the_United_States_Constitution)" and featuring the tagline "Same mission. New name." was launched in November 2025, coinciding with the [2025 United States elections](69577044.md). It included two television commercials featuring vignettes of American life and MS NOW personalities, accompanied by readings of the [U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Constitution) by Rachel Maddow and [Maya Angelou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Angelou)'s 1996 reading of her poem "The Human Family" to the United Nations.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176] The "We The People" branding was later adopted by other facets of the network; beginning with its coverage of the [2026 United States elections](70357296.md), MS NOW branded its election coverage as *We the People (year)*, replacing the *Decision* branding that was inherited from NBC News.[^cite_note-177]

The rebranding took effect on November 15, 2025.[^cite_note-soon-169][^cite_note-mixes-171][^cite_note-178] Alongside the rebrand, MS NOW also introduced its new New York City studios at 229 West 43rd Street; the facility consists of three main studios, including the main Studio 9A (which carries over visual elements from MSNBC's former Studio 3A at NBC Studios) and 9C, and a three-sided [LED volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-set_virtual_production) (Studio 9B). The new studios were constructed in three months, and used existing broadcasting infrastructure that had been installed by previous tenants such as [BuzzFeed News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BuzzFeed_News). Master control and other technical functions are handled from CNBC's headquarters in [Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Englewood_Cliffs,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-mixes-171]

With the rebranding of the channel as MS NOW, its website was moved to ms.now,[^cite_note-179] and MS NOW programs were dropped from [Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_\(streaming_service\)); in December 2025, the network announced plans to launch its own OTT subscription offering in the future, which would focus on being a "digital hub for progressives" and offer access to live [virtual events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_event) featuring its personalities.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

In February 2026, MS NOW announced a partnership with [Crooked Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crooked_Media), in which the channel will air *Crooked on MS NOW*—a weekly program featuring highlights from its podcasts (such as *[Pod Save America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pod_Save_America)*)—on Saturday nights beginning February 28.[^cite_note-182]

On June 15, 2026, MS NOW began a revamp of its daytime schedule, with Stephanie Ruhle returning to daytime to host the new 9-11 a.m. block *Money, Power, Politics*, Alicia Menendez moving from *The Weeknight* to host the new 12–2 p.m. show *On the Line*, and *Katy Tur Reports* being rebranded as *The Moment*. Meanwhile, Morning Joe reverted back to three hours from 6-9 a.m. with Jonathan Lemire being co-host of the third hour, Luke Russert joined *The Weeknight* to succeed Menendez, Ali Velshi moved to *The 11th Hour* to succeed Ruhle, and his existing weekend show *Velshi* was replaced by *Connect with Jacob Soboroff*. Chris Jansing remained with the network as its chief political correspondent, while Ana Cabrera departed the network. MS NOW also planned to launch a new 11 a.m. program anchored by chief national correspondent [Peter Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Alexander_\(journalist\)), which later premiered on August 10 as *State of Play*.[^cite_note-183] Kutler stated that these changes were part of an effort to strengthen MS NOW's daytime lineup while maintaining its larger focus on hard news coverage.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185]

On June 26, 2026, MS NOW cancelled *The Weekend: Primetime* (with its final episode airing that weekend), and announced that weekend anchor Alex Witt would depart the network after nearly 30 years; Witt was succeeded by former *The Weekend: Primetime* panelist [Antonia Hylton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Hylton), with *[Alex Witt Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Witt_Reports)* replaced by *The Assignment with Antonia Hylton* beginning on September 1&#x32;*.*[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187] Kutler stated that MS NOW was moving away from live evening programming on weekends to prioritize its upcoming direct-to-consumer offering, and that the daypart will focus primarily on television versions of MS NOW podcasts (such as *The Best People with Nicolle Wallace*) and other acquired programming—citing the Crooked Media partnership as an example of this strategy. Kutler stated that despite the cuts, MS NOW will still air around 20 hours of live weekend programming per-week, and would still air live breaking news coverage when warranted.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189]

On September 9, 2026, MS NOW launched its direct-to-consumer subscription service, branded as the "MS NOW membership"; alongside live streaming of the network and digital-exclusive shows (such *Rachel Maddow: In Conversation With…,* *Political Potluck* with [Claire McCaskill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claire_McCaskill), *Off the Charts* with Ali Velshi, and *The Very Last Word* with Lawrence O’Donnell), the service includes a "community hub" featuring forum discussions with network personalities and columnists, live streams of MS NOW's in-person events and interactive virtual events, and a partnership with [Calm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calm_\(company\)).[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191]

## Demographics

A study by [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) in 2014 found that MSNBC's audience was more moderate than that of [BuzzFeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BuzzFeed), *[Politico](9172097.md)*, *[The Washington Post](102226.md)*, and *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, but slightly more liberal than [CNN](62028.md)'s audience.[^cite_note-192]

A survey by [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) in 2019 showed that among Americans who named MSNBC as their main source for political news, 74% are ages 50 or older, with 44% ages 65 or older. 95% of those who named MSNBC as their main political news source identify as [Democrats](5043544.md); among the eight most commonly named main sources for political and election news by US adults, MSNBC and Fox News have the most partisan audiences.[^cite_note-193]

## Carriage



MSNBC's former New Jersey headquarters studio, now the home of [MLB Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Network)

Before 2010, MSNBC was not available to [Verizon FiOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verizon_FiOS) and [AT\&T U-verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_U-verse) television subscribers in the portions of New York, northern [New Jersey](21648.md), and [Connecticut](6466.md) that overlapped [Cablevision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cablevision)'s service area. One of several reasons for this was an exclusive carriage agreement between MSNBC and Cablevision that prohibited competing wired providers from carrying MSNBC. In 2009, Verizon filed a formal "program-access complaint" with the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) and petitioned for termination of the deal. In support of Verizon, Connecticut Attorney General [Richard Blumenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Blumenthal) argued that the arrangement could be illegal.[^cite_note-194] After entering into a new contract, FiOS added MSNBC in New York City and New Jersey in February 2010.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197]

## International availability



The monitors of the MSNBC newsroom are tuned into various global channels.

[MSNBC Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSNBC_Canada), a joint venture between [Rogers Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Communications), [Shaw Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Communications), and MSNBC (each holding a 33.33% interest), was in operation from September 2001 to 2004. The channel faced logistics difficulty due to requirements by the [Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Radio-television_and_Telecommunications_Commission) that channels carry a certain percentage of [Canadian content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_content). As a result, it was replaced with a direct feed from MSNBC.[^cite_note-198]

In Australia, MSNBC launched November 2019 on the [Fetch TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetch_TV) online PayTV network, on channel 171.[^cite_note-199] As in Canada, this is a direct US feed of MS NOW, without any delay. Although Fetch TV has no MS NOW catch-up channel/service, it also offers MS NOW programs on a reverse EPG which allows any shows from the previous 24 hours to be selected and watched. The channel was added to subscription-television outlet [Foxtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxtel) and its streaming service [Flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_\(streaming_service\)) in April 2023, as part of a negotiated programming agreement between the [Foxtel Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxtel_Group) and NBCUniversal.[^cite_note-200] It was also added to [Binge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binge_\(streaming_service\)), another of Foxtel's streaming services, in October 2023.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202]

In Asia and Europe, MS NOW is not shown on a dedicated channel.&#x20;

In Turkey, NTV-MSNBC was a joint venture with [NTV Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTV_Turkey); it shut down in December 2014.[^cite_note-203]

In 2007, MSNBC expanded to South Africa, reaching a deal to become the "exclusive news provider" to Southern Africa’s Free2View TV satellite service.[^cite_note-204]

As of 2024, MSNBC's evening programming features progressive hosts.[^cite_note-Cross-120] In November 2007, a *New York Times* article stated that MSNBC's primetime lineup was tilting more to the left.[^cite_note-Nods-205] Since then, some commentators have argued that MSNBC has a bias towards [liberal politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_politics) and the [Democratic Party](5043544.md). *[Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Post)* media analyst [Howard Kurtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Kurtz) said in 2008 that the channel's evening lineup "has clearly gravitated to the left in recent years and often seems to regard itself as the antithesis of Fox News."[^cite_note-Flak-51] In 2011, *[Politico](9172097.md)* referred to MSNBC as "left-leaning",[^cite_note-206] and Steve Kornacki of *[Salon.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon.com)* stated that, "MSNBC's prime-time lineup is now awash in [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_progressivism) politics."[^cite_note-207] Regarding changes in the channel's evening programming, senior vice president of NBC News [Phil Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Griffin_\(presenter\)) said that "it happened naturally. There isn't a dogma we're putting through. There is a 'Go for it.'"[^cite_note-Nods-205]

In the February 2008 issue of *[Men's Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_Journal)* magazine, an MSNBC interviewee quoted a senior executive as saying that liberal commentator Keith Olbermann "runs MSNBC" and that "because of his success, he's in charge" of the channel.[^cite_note-208] In 2007, *The New York Times* called Olbermann MSNBC's "most recognizable face".[^cite_note-Nods-205] In September 2008, MSNBC stated that Olbermann and [Chris Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Matthews) would no longer anchor live political events, with [David Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gregory_\(journalist\)) assuming that role. MSNBC cited the growing criticism that they were "too opinionated to be seen as neutral in the heat of the presidential campaign".[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-Incendiary-59] Olbermann's show *Countdown* continued to run before and after the presidential and vice presidential debates, and both Matthews and Olbermann joined Gregory on the channel's election night coverage.[^cite_note-210]

In November 2009, just before the release of [Sarah Palin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Palin)'s book *[Going Rogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_Rogue)*, MSNBC's [Dylan Ratigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Ratigan) used [photoshopped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoshopping) pictures of Palin on the channel's *[Morning Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Meeting)* program. Ratigan apologized a few days later.[^cite_note-211]

In October 2010, MSNBC began using the tagline "Lean Forward". Some media outlets, including msnbc.com, claimed that the network was now embracing its politically progressive identity.[^cite_note-forward-83][^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213]

In January 2012, MSNBC used Rachel Maddow, Chris Matthews, and other network commentators during its coverage of the [Iowa Republican caucuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Republican_caucuses,_2012). Nando Di Fino of the [Mediaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediaite) website said MSNBC was "giving up on the straight news coverage, and instead \[appearing] to be aiming to create some controversy".[^cite_note-214]

In November 2012, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* called MSNBC "The Anti-Fox" and quoted former President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) as saying, "Boy, it really has become our version of Fox."[^cite_note-ground-215] Citing data from the [A.C. Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.C._Nielsen) TV ratings service, the article noted that while the Fox News Channel had a larger overall viewership than MSNBC, the two networks were separated by only around 300,000 viewers among the 25–54 age bracket most attractive to advertisers.[^cite_note-ground-215]

In the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center)'s 2013 "State of the News Media" report, MSNBC was found to be the most opinionated news network, with 85% of the content being commentary or opinions and the remaining 15% being factual reporting. The report also stated that in 2012, MSNBC spent only \$240 million on news production compared to CNN's \$682 million and the Fox News Channel's \$820 million.[^cite_note-216]

In October 2019, [American socialist](28903916.md) magazine *[Jacobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobin_\(magazine\))* argued that "MSNBC embodies the politics and sensibility of Trump-era liberalism.", but argued that MSNBC "wasn't always liberal".[^cite_note-217]

Writing for the [Poynter Institute for Media Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poynter_Institute_for_Media_Studies) in February 2021, senior media writer Tom Jones argued that the primary distinction between MSNBC and Fox News is not left bias vs. right bias, but rather that much of the content on Fox News, especially during its primetime programs, is not based in truth.[^cite_note-218]

Under NBC News head [Cesar Conde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesar_Conde), the division had made moves to incorporate more diverse viewpoints—including from conservative perspectives—in its output outside of MSNBC (including *Meet the Press*) to counter concerns from NBC's affiliate body that MSNBC's partisan content reflected upon the division as a whole. These moves coincided with MSNBC's own changes in leadership, which resulted in a gradual increase in opinion programming, and as a result, a gradual decrease in talent sharing with the remainder of NBC News.[^cite_note-Cross-120]

#### 2008 United States presidential election

During the 2008 election, MSNBC was accused of bias in favor of [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama)[^cite_note-219] and bias against primary challenger [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton).[^cite_note-220] During the conventions, Matthews and Olbermann had a bitter argument on the air.[^cite_note-221] As a result, [David Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gregory_\(journalist\)) took over as the only news anchor of debates and election night.[^cite_note-222]

In the fall of 2008, MSNBC had a new channel slogan called "The Power of Change", which put emphasis on Obama's "change" rhetoric.[^cite_note-223]

#### Obama era

Some Democratic Party supporters, including former Pennsylvania governor [Ed Rendell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Rendell) and Bill Clinton advisor [Lanny Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanny_Davis),[^cite_note-224] criticized MSNBC during and after the [2008 Democratic Party presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries) as covering [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) more favorably than [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton).[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226] In January 2011, Rendell became an on-air contributor to MSNBC.[^cite_note-227]

A study done by the [Project for Excellence in Journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_for_Excellence_in_Journalism) showed that MSNBC had less negative coverage of Obama with 14% of stories versus 29% in the press overall and more negative stories about Republican presidential candidate [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) with 73% of its coverage versus 57% in the press overall.[^cite_note-228] MSNBC's on-air slogan during the week of the 2008 presidential election, "The Power of Change", was criticized for being too similar to Obama's campaign slogan of "Hope and Change".[^cite_note-229] After the election, conservative talk show host [John Ziegler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ziegler_\(talk_show_host\)) made a documentary entitled *Media Malpractice... How Obama Got Elected*, which was very critical of the media's role, especially MSNBC's, in the election. While promoting the documentary, he had an on-air dispute with MSNBC news anchor [Contessa Brewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contessa_Brewer) about how the media, especially MSNBC, had portrayed Sarah Palin.[^cite_note-230]

During MSNBC's coverage of the Potomac primary, MSNBC's Chris Matthews said, "I have to tell you, you know, it's part of reporting this case, this election, the feeling most people get when they hear Barack Obama's speech. My, I felt this thrill going up my leg. I mean, I don't have that too often." This led conservative media to assert that both he and MSNBC were biased toward Obama.[^cite_note-231]

#### *Rise of the New Right* documentary

In June 2010, the MSNBC documentary *Rise of the New Right* aired. It featured interviews with right-wing figures, including [Dick Armey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Armey), the former House majority leader, [Orly Taitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orly_Taitz), a leading figure in the "[birther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birther)" movement, and [conspiracy theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theorist) radio host [Alex Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Jones). The documentary also showed the [Michigan Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Militia)'s [survival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivalism) training camp and hit the campaign trail with Kentucky senatorial candidate [Rand Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul).[^cite_note-232]

The documentary angered [Tea Party movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_movement) figures and others on the right. After the documentary aired, [FreedomWorks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreedomWorks), chaired by Armey, called for a boycott of [Dawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_\(brand\)) and [Procter & Gamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procter_&_Gamble), which advertised during *Hardball with Chris Matthews*.[^cite_note-233]

#### Romney coverage during 2012 election

A study by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) found that MSNBC's coverage of [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney) during the final week of the [2012 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election) of 68% negative with no positive stories in the sample was far more negative than the overall press, and even more negative than it had been during October 1 to 28, when 5% was positive, and 57% was negative. However, coverage of Barack Obama improved in the final week before the presidential election. From 1 to October 28, 33% of stories were positive and 13% negative. During the campaign's final week, 51% of MSNBC's stories were positive, while there were no negative stories about Obama in the sample.[^cite_note-234]

#### Trump era

MSNBC received significant criticism and accusations of a liberal bias from [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and [his supporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_supporters). In social media posts, Trump has frequently referred to MSNBC as "MSDNC", (accusing the network of acting as an arm to the [Democratic National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Committee)), classified the network as part of the "fake news media", and claimed the network's viewership ratings were in decline. Trump has also directly criticized MSNBC shows and hosts as well, including *Morning Joe*, where, in 2017, Trump referred to host [Mika Brzezinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mika_Brzezinski) as "low I.Q. Crazy Mika" and [Joe Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Scarborough) as "Psycho Joe" in a tweet.[^cite_note-235] In May 2025, Trump accused MSNBC of committing a "Major Campaign Violation" after statements critical of Trump's [Liberation Day tariffs](79617151.md) were made by hosts on the network.[^cite_note-236] In August 2025, Trump referred to *[Deadline: White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline:_White_House)* host [Nicolle Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolle_Wallace) as a "loser", and predicted that she would be fired from the network.[^cite_note-237]

### Allegations of conservative bias

Others have argued that MSNBC has a bias against progressive politics. [Phil Donahue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Donahue)'s show was canceled in 2003 due to his opposition to the [Iraq War](5043324.md), and Donahue later commented that the management of MSNBC required that "we have two conservative (guests) for every liberal. I was counted as two liberals."[^cite_note-238]

[Cenk Uygur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenk_Uygur), after his departure from MSNBC in 2011, said that MSNBC management had told him "people in Washington" were "concerned about \[his] tone",[^cite_note-239] and that he "didn't want to work in a place that didn't challenge power".[^cite_note-240]

MSNBC anchors tend to be [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) or [centrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrism_in_the_United_States) and wealthy.[^cite_note-people-241]

In 2000, host [Joe Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Scarborough) received a 95 rating from the [American Conservative Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Conservative_Union) and supported [anti-abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-abortion) policies when he was a [U.S. representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._representative), while host [Stephanie Ruhle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Ruhle), a former [hedge fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund) manager, declared, "I don't have any political ideals that I'm tied to." Former host [Chris Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Matthews) identifies as a "liberal" but voted for [George W. Bush](3414021.md) in 2000, while current host [Nicolle Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolle_Wallace), a registered [Republican](32070.md), worked for both Bush and [Sarah Palin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Palin).[^cite_note-people-241]

### Lack of diversity of views

Maria Bustillos noted in 2019 that "MSNBC's bland, evenhanded respectability is buttressed with a careful performance of diversity both 'ideological' and [demographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic)", and that "the network actively discourages consideration of its anchors' personal convictions" in favor of "interchangeable 'television personalities'" who are "compressed into the network's identity and subservient to it".[^cite_note-people-241] It has been argued that MSNBC, like other [cable networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_cable_news), "is simply not incentivized to be informative", and instead turns its "viewers into partisan junkies who don't change the channel because they need a fix that tells them they're right about everything (and that the other side is wrong)."[^cite_note-242] In 2014, Jason Linkins claimed that MSNBC prefers "the incessant production of insidery ideations" over "the service of the public trust in an honest and equitable way".[^cite_note-243]

### Melissa Harris-Perry's comments on Romney's black grandchild

Political commentator [Melissa Harris-Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Harris-Perry) and her guest panel, in a look back on the 2013 segment of [her show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Harris-Perry_\(TV_program\)), featured a picture of former Republican presidential candidate [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney) and his extended family. Romney was holding on his knee his adopted grandchild, Kieran Romney, an African-American. Harris-Perry and her guests, including actress Pia Glenn and comedian [Dean Obeidallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Obeidallah), joked about coming up with captions for the photo. Glenn sang out, "[One of these things is not like the others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_of_these_things_is_not_like_the_others), one of these things just isn't the same." Obeidallah said, "It sums up the diversity of the Republican Party and the \[Republican National Committee], where they have the whole convention and they find the one black person." Afterwards, Harris-Perry gave an on-air apology as well as apologized in a series of [tweets](9988187.md).[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245]

### Coverage of the 2020 Democratic primary

In February 2019, NBC ran a story about presidential candidate [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md) claiming that her campaign was benefiting from Russian [state media](20803507.md), stating that she had received twice as many mentions on [RT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT_\(TV_network\)), [Sputnik News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik_News) and [Russia Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_Insider) compared to expected front-runners [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) and [Joe Biden](145422.md).[^cite_note-246]

In March 2019, [Yashar Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashar_Ali), a contributor for *[HuffPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost)* accused [Dafna Linzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafna_Linzer), a managing editor at MSNBC, of ceding editorial control to the [Democratic National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Committee). Ali, who planned to announce the locations of the DNC debates in advance of MSNBC, received a call attempting to dissuade him with the phrase "let them make a few phone calls", referring to party leaders.[^cite_note-247]

The Democratic Party presidential candidate [Andrew Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Yang) and his supporters were critical of MSNBC's coverage of [his campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Yang_2020_presidential_campaign) and the speaking time allocated to Yang at a November 2019 primary debate hosted by MSNBC.[^cite_note-248]

In December 2019, *[In These Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_These_Times_\(publication\))* analyzed coverage of the 2020 Democratic Party presidential primary by MSNBC between August and September 2019.[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250] They said that "MSNBC talked about Biden twice as often as [Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Warren) and three times as often as Sanders", and that Sanders was the candidate spoken of negatively the most frequently of the three."[^cite_note-251]

[Chris Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Matthews) compared Sanders to [George McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McGovern) in terms of electability on February 3 and criticized Sanders for adopting the "democratic socialist" label on February 7.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253] In reference to Sanders' praise of some aspects of [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro)'s [Cuba](5042481.md), Matthews said on air during *Hardball*, "I believe if Castro and the [Reds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communists) had won the Cold War there would have been executions in Central Park, and I might have been one of the ones executed". He then questioned what Sanders meant when he used the term 'socialism'.[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255] The following week, [Chuck Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Todd) criticized the rhetoric of Sanders supporters by quoting a [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) article which compared them to Nazi [brown shirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_shirts).[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257] Commenting on the [2020 Nevada Democratic caucuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nevada_Democratic_caucuses), Matthews invoked ["the fall of France" to the Nazis in 1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France) as a metaphor for Sanders' apparent victory in the state.[^cite_note-258] These analogies were criticized by the Sanders campaign and other commentators, who noted that members of Sanders' family had been murdered in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust). Matthews later issued an on-air apology to Sanders and his supporters.[^cite_note-259] On March 2, 2020, Matthews announced that he would depart MSNBC effective immediately.[^cite_note-\:5-113]

[Jason Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Johnson_\(professor\)), an MSNBC contributor, was temporarily suspended in February 2020 after backlash over his accusing Bernie Sanders supporters of alienating minorities and saying of African-American Sanders staffers and surrogates, "I don't care how many people from the island of misfit black girls that you throw out to defend you on a regular basis."[^cite_note-260] He was reinstated in July 2020.[^cite_note-261]

### Coverage of Israeli–Palestinian conflict

[Ayman Mohyeldin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_Mohyeldin) has been alleged to have an anti-Israel bias in his reporting.[^cite_note-262] In 2015, Mohyeldin was reporting live from [Gaza](55387.md), where he claimed to have witnessed an unarmed Palestinian man being shot by Israeli police at the [Damascus Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus_Gate); the man turned out to be wielding a large knife.[^cite_note-263]

Following the outbreak of the [Gaza war](74998519.md), it was reported by *[Semafor](72046207.md)* that a number of Muslim hosts (including [Mehdi Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Hasan), Mohyeldin, and [Ali Velshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Velshi)) were sidelined from coverage.[^cite_note-264]

## Suspensions and dismissals of hosts

### Michael Savage

[Michael Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Savage) briefly hosted a weekend talk show in 2003. That July, Savage responded to a prank caller on his show by calling him a "pig" and a "sodomite", and telling him he "should get AIDS and die". Savage's show was canceled and he was dismissed from MSNBC shortly thereafter.[^cite_note-265]

### Don Imus

[Don Imus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Imus)' radio show *[Imus in the Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imus_in_the_Morning)* was simulcast on MSNBC for over ten years. In 2007, he described members of the [Rutgers Scarlet Knights women's basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_Scarlet_Knights_women's_basketball) team as "some nappy-headed hoes". The remark sparked outrage. The comments sparked outrage, as many considered them to be racist and sexist. After sponsors began to withdraw advertisements from the show, MSNBC canceled the simulcast. Both Imus and NBC News apologized to the Rutgers Basketball team for the remarks.[^cite_note-266]

### Brief suspension of Joe Scarborough due to political contributions

In November 2010, [Joe Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Scarborough), host of *Morning Joe*, was briefly suspended after he donated \$4,000 to Republican candidates in Florida. [^cite_note-267]

### Suspension and departure of Keith Olbermann

In November 2010, Keith Olbermann, the host of the highly rated news/opinion show *[Countdown with Keith Olbermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countdown_with_Keith_Olbermann)*, was suspended without pay for having contributed \$2,400 (the maximum personal donation limit) to each of three [Democratic Party](5043544.md) candidates ahead of the [2010 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_elections).[^cite_note-268] NBC News policy prohibited contributions to political campaigns unless NBC News had given its prior permission. A few days later, he was reinstated.[^cite_note-269]

In January 2011, Olbermann left MSNBC.[^cite_note-270] Before reading a [James Thurber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Thurber) short story called "The Scottie Who Knew Too Much", Olbermann thanked viewers, producers, and technical staff for his show's eight-year success. However, he did not thank or even mention Griffin or NBC News president [Steve Capus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Capus). Olbermann did not disclose the reason for his departure, and a statement from MSNBC would only reveal that the two parties had ended the commentator's four-year contract. Many liberal bloggers and commentators blamed cable provider [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast) for Olbermann's sudden departure, accusing the company of silencing the host for political purposes just days after acquiring [NBCUniversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCUniversal) on January 18.[^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272] Statements from MSNBC and Comcast denied this allegation. *[Daily Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Beast)* media critic [Howard Kurtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Kurtz), former MSNBC anchor [David Shuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Shuster), and an anonymous NBC executive[^cite_note-273] said that Olbermann's past suspension and subsequent conflicts with network management was a more likely precipitating factor in *Countdown'*&#x73; cancellation.

The following month, Olbermann relaunched his show on [Current TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_TV). He also started a blog called FOKNewsChannel.com, a commentary site parodying Fox News, which included written commentaries and articles based on former *Countdown* segments.[^cite_note-274]

### Martin Bashir

In November 2013, [Martin Bashir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Bashir) criticized [Sarah Palin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Palin) for equating the [federal debt](322221.md) to slavery, leading to his resignation.[^cite_note-275] Bashir referred to the cruel and barbaric punishment of slaves as described by [slave overseer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) [Thomas Thistlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Thistlewood), specifically a punishment called "[Derby's dose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby's_dose)", which forced slaves to defecate or urinate into the mouth of another slave. Bashir then said, "When Mrs. Palin invokes slavery, she doesn't just prove her rank ignorance. She confirms if anyone truly qualified for a dose of discipline from Thomas Thistlewood, she would be the outstanding candidate."[^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277]

### Alec Baldwin

[Alec Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Baldwin)'s 2013 show *[Up Late with Alec Baldwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_Late_with_Alec_Baldwin)* was suspended after five episodes because of a [homophobic slur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobic_slur) Baldwin made to a photographer in New York City.[^cite_note-278]

## Racism allegations

In May 2014, during the show "Way Too Early", a segment was done about the historical background of [Cinco De Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinco_De_Mayo) and featured Louis Burgdorf dancing around the set in a sombrero, shaking maracas and drinking tequila, which host [Thomas Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Roberts_\(television_journalist\)) referred to as "go-go juice". The segment was criticized for its mocking of Mexican heritage and use of false stereotypes.[^cite_note-279] MSNBC apologized the next day, saying there was no intention to be disrespectful and that while the props were planned, Roberts and Burgdorf acted the way they did on their own. The two hosts also apologized, although they partially deflected the blame back at their producers for allowing the segment.[^cite_note-280]

In January 2020, as MSNBC reported on the death of [Kobe Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe_Bryant), [Alison Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Morris) quickly called Bryant's former team, the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers), what sounded like "the Los Angeles [Niggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigger)", immediately self-correcting to "Los Angeles Lakers".[^cite_note-Denies-281] Morris claimed to have verbally stumbled and said "Los Angeles Nakers", claiming she had stuttered, [combining the words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoonerism) "Knicks" and "Lakers" into "Nakers", rather than the racial slur.[^cite_note-282] Many people accused her of being racist, which Morris quickly denied.[^cite_note-283][^cite_note-284][^cite_note-Denies-281] A petition to have Morris fired was signed by over 184,000 people.[^cite_note-285][^cite_note-286]
