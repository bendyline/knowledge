**Pakistan Air Force Base, Nur Khan** (abbreviated **PAF Base Nur Khan**) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **NRK**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **OPRN**) is a Pakistani military facility located in the town of [Chaklala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaklala) at [Islamabad–Rawalpindi metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad%E2%80%93Rawalpindi). The former [Benazir Bhutto International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Bhutto_International_Airport) is integrated into the airbase's infrastructure.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

The base is named after [Air Marshal Nur Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_Khan) (1923–2011), former [Commander-in-Chief of the Pakistan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Air_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) during the [1965 India-Pakistan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_India-Pakistan_War). The airbase is known for serving as the hub of PAF's air mobility operations and plays a key role in logistics, VIP transport, and strategic operations.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## History



PAF Base Nur Khan

The base was originally operated by the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) as **RAF Chaklala** and, during the Second World War, parachute training flights were conducted. [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJefford200130-7]

&#x20;\
&#x20;In 1947, it was transferred to the then [Royal Pakistan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Pakistan_Air_Force) (RPAF). Afterwards, it began use as a transport hub and forward base, with the PAF's fleet of various transport aircraft operating from it. By 1958, the base was equipped with five brick and steel hangars and accommodation for 1200 personnel. It had storage for 50,000 gallons of fuel.[^cite_note-MEAFS-8]

In 1979, the base was home to both [No. 15 Squadron PAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._15_Squadron_PAF) and [No. 26 Squadron PAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._26_Squadron_PAF) with the [North American F-86F Sabres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_F-86_Sabre) and [No. 6 Squadron PAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._6_Squadron_PAF) with the [Lockheed C-130B/E Hercules](7697.md) & [Lockheed L-100 Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-100_Hercules).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewishSweetmanWheelerGunston1984195-9]

After the [Pakistan earthquake of 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_earthquake_of_2005), 300 U.S. troops as well as U.S. aircraft were deployed to Chaklala to aid in relief efforts. According to an anonymous 2013 source, the U.S. had been allowed permanent military presence at Chaklala since late 2001 for handling logistics efforts and other movements in relation to the war in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-10]

In 2009 the PAF's first of four [Il-78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il-78) [aerial refuelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_refuelling) tanker aircraft was delivered to PAF Base Chaklala and the No. 10 MRTT (Multi Role Tanker Transport) squadron was established there.[^cite_note-warnes1-11]

The name of the base was changed in 2012 from PAF Base Chaklala to PAF Base Nur Khan in remembrance of its first Base Commander in 1947, [Air Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Marshal) [Nur Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_Khan). Nur Khan was also the second Pakistani chief of the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) and a veteran of several conflicts fought by Pakistan.[^cite_note-12]

Five PAF [F-16s](11642.md) escorted US [Vice-president](32759.md) [JD Vance](53396477.md)'s [C-32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_C-32) to Nur Khan base in Islamabad on 11 April 2026 for negotiations following the [2026 Iran war ceasefire](82888027.md).[^cite_note-13]

### Airstrikes

During the [2025 India–Pakistan conflict](79896622.md), the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) carried out [precision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition) [cruise missile](6590.md) airstrikes on several key [military targets in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pakistan_Air_Force_bases) which included technical infrastructure, command and control centres, radar sites and weapon storage areas.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-nyt_clashesunfolded-15]

On 10 May, the Indian Air Force carried out airstrikes on the Nur Khan Airbase, targeting runways and military infrastructure.[^cite_note-NYTimes_nurkhan-16][^cite_note-\:0-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] According to Indian defense officials, the operation was intended as a "calibrated response" to cross-border attacks. According to an eye-witness and two Pakistani security officials, the attack on Nur Khan airbase saw at least two missile strikes as well as drone attacks. Pakistan’s military stated that its air defense systems intercepted most of the incoming missiles, and no critical assets were lost. However, according to a Reuters citing an official who visited the base the next day, the barrage hit two roofs and hit the hangar of a refueling plane.[^cite_note-reuters_pulledback-20][^cite_note-nyt_clashesunfolded-15] According to William Goodhind, a geospatial analyst at Contested Ground, two mobile control centers at the Nur Khan airbase were damaged. Further *[The Washington Post](102226.md)*, also reported videos recorded from a parking lot nearby which "showed smoke billowing from the damaged area".[^cite_note-21] According to *[Air Forces Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Forces_Monthly)*, the missiles struck the operations room of the 35th Composite Transport Wing, destroying two fuel trucks and collaterally damaging one Lockheed C-130 Hercules of the [No. 6 Squadron PAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._6_Squadron_PAF) at the aforementioned hangar.[^cite_note-22]

A ceasefire was proposed after these airstrikes.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Within hours of India's missile-drone strikes on several Pakistani airbases, especially Nur Khan,[^cite_note-reuters_pulledback-20][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] reportedly prompted US intervention for a ceasefire agreement. The base is a strategic asset for PAF as "one of the central transport hubs" and "home to the [air refueling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_refueling) capability" ([No. 10 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pakistan_Air_Force_squadrons#Aerial_Refueling_Squadrons)). The facility is also just over a mile from the headquarters of [Strategic Plans Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Plans_Division) responsible for [Pakistan's nuclear arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_weapons_of_mass_destruction) which includes over 170 warheads.[^cite_note-NYTimes_nurkhan-16][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-reuters_pulledback-20] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* also cited a former American official who stated, "Pakistan's deepest fear is of its nuclear command authority being decapitated" and that the Indian missile strikes were interpreted to be a warning for India's capability to do the same.[^cite_note-NYTimes_nurkhan-16][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] According to Christopher Clary, an associate professor at the [University at Albany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Albany), "So, an attack on the facility may have been perceived as more dangerous than India intended – and the two sides should not conclude that it is possible to have a conflict without it going nuclear".[^cite_note-reuters_pulledback-20] On 14 May, Reuters reported that Indian strikes on Nur Khan airbase had alarmed US officials, due to the base's proximity to Pakistan's Strategic Plans Division.[^cite_note-reuters_pulledback-20]

The Afghan [Taliban](30635.md) were reported by Indian sources to have launched drone strikes against PAF Nur Khan on 2 March 2026.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

## Location

The base has surrounding facilities including the Frontier Works Organization Headquarters, [Chaklala Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Railways) and the Joint Services Headquarters (JSHQ). Two housing schemes Askaris VIII and IX are also located alongside Nur Khan road that extends to the main entrance of the base from Airport Road. The [Centre for Aerospace and Security Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Aerospace_and_Security_Studies), a research think tank founded by the Pakistan Air Force, is located next to Nur Khan Base.
