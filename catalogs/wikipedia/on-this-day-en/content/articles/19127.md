**Mali**,[^cite_note-14] officially the **Republic of Mali**,[^cite_note-15] is a [landlocked country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country) in [West Africa](67393.md). It is the [eighth-largest country in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_area) and [the 23rd largest country in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area), with an area of 1,240,192 square kilometres (478,841 sq mi).[^cite_note-bbc_com-9] It is bordered to the north by [Algeria](358.md), to the east by [Niger](21373.md), to the northwest by [Mauritania](18778516.md), to the south by [Burkina Faso](3470.md) and [Ivory Coast](57744.md), and to the west by [Guinea](12176.md) and [Senegal](27278.md). The population is about 25.20 million,[^cite_note-populationest-10] 47.19% of which are estimated to be under age 15 in 2024.[^cite_note-16] Its capital and largest city is [Bamako](56606.md). French was the official language until 2022, when it was replaced by 13 [African languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Africa), with [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language) being the first language of the majority of the population.[^cite_note-p6-4]

Mali's northern borders reach deep into the middle of the [Sahara Desert](325363.md). Southern Mali, where the majority of inhabitants live, is in the [Sudanian savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanian_savanna) and has the [Niger](21437.md) and [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal_River) rivers running through it. The economy centres on agriculture and mining with its most prominent natural resources including gold (which represents 80% of its exports) and cotton. Mali is one of the poorest and [least developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_countries) in the world.[^cite_note-ITA-17][^cite_note-UNCTAD-18]

Mali was part of three successive powerful and wealthy West African empires that controlled [trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade): the [Ghana Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire), the [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire), and the [Songhai Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_Empire). At its peak in 1300, the Mali Empire was the wealthiest country in Africa[^cite_note-19] with Emperor [Mansa Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansa_Musa) believed to be one of the wealthiest individuals in history.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Besides being a hub of trade and mining, medieval Mali was a centre of [Islam](6037917.md), culture and knowledge, with [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu) becoming a renowned place of education. The Songhai Empire absorbed the Mali Empire in 1468, followed by a [Saadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_Sultanate) army [which defeated the Songhai in 1591](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_invasion_of_the_Songhai_Empire).

In the late 19th century, during the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa), France seized control of Mali, making it a part of [French Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Sudan); as the Sudanese Republic, [a brief federation with Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Federation) was formed, achieving independence in 1960. After Senegal's withdrawal, the Republic of Mali was established. After a long period of one-party rule, a [coup in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) led to a new constitution and the establishment of Mali as a democratic, multi-party state. From this, Mali experienced economic growth and increased civil liberties, though this began to reverse after 2012.[^cite_note-23] Since independence, there have been four rebellions involving [Tuareg people](103054.md), which occurred from [1962 to 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(1962%E2%80%931964\)), [1990 to 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(1990%E2%80%931995\)), [2007 to 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(2007%E2%80%932009\)), and [since 2012](36856978.md).[^cite_note-24]

In January 2012, an [armed conflict broke out in northern Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(2012\)), in which [Tuareg rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad) took control of territory in the north, and in April declared the [secession](144732.md) of a new state, [Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawad).[^cite_note-25] The conflict was complicated by [a military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) in March 2012[^cite_note-telegraph.co.uk-26] and later fighting between Tuareg and other rebel factions. In response to territorial gains, the French military launched [Operation Serval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Serval) in January 2013.[^cite_note-27] A month later, Malian and French forces partially recaptured the north, though the conflict continued.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28]

[Ibrahim Boubacar Keïta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Boubacar_Ke%C3%AFta) was [elected president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Malian_presidential_election) in 2013, but allegations of fraud during the [2020 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Malian_parliamentary_election) led to [mass protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Malian_protests). Keïta was removed in [a coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) led by [Assimi Goïta](65000306.md), who established himself as military ruler of Mali after [another coup in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] In 2025, all political parties were dissolved, and Goïta was granted a five-year presidential term, renewable without elections.[^cite_note-29] The [al-Qaeda](1921.md) affiliate [JNIM](53401088.md) imposed [a fuel blockade](81764054.md) on major cities, causing economic disruption[^cite_note-ISW-100925-30] and in 2026 launched a [joint offensive](83033878.md) with [Tuareg separatists](78996050.md).[^cite_note-AP-26042026-31]

## Etymology

See also: [Mali Empire § Etymology of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire#Etymology_of_Mali)

The name *Mali* is taken from the name of the [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire). Originally meaning "[hippopotamus](37764.md)", the clanic symbol of the [Keita Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keita_Dynasty), it eventually came to mean "the place where the king lives".[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Fourteenth-century [Maghrebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebi_Arabic) traveller [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) reported that the capital of the empire was called Mali.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] One [Mandinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_people) tradition tells that the legendary first emperor [Sundiata Keita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundiata_Keita) changed himself into a hippopotamus upon his death in the [Sankarani River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankarani_River) and that it was possible to find villages in the area of this river called "old Mali". A study of Malian proverbs notes that in old Mali, there is a village called Malikoma, which means "New Mali", and that *Mali* could have formerly been the name of a city.[^cite_note-\:0-36]

Another theory suggests that *Mali* is a [Fulani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_language) pronunciation of the name of the [Mande peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mand%C3%A9_peoples).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] It is suggested that a sound shift led to the change, whereby in Fulani the alveolar segment /nd/ shifts to /l/ and the terminal vowel denasalizes and raises, leading "Manden" to shift to /mali/.[^cite_note-\:0-36]

### Prehistory

The pages above are from [Timbuktu Manuscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu_Manuscripts) written in Sudani script (a form of [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic)) from the [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire) showing established knowledge of astronomy and mathematics. Today there are close to a million of these manuscripts found in [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu) alone.

Rock art in the [Sahara](325363.md) suggests that northern Mali has been inhabited since 10,000 BC, when the Sahara was fertile and rich in wildlife. Early ceramics have been discovered at the central Malian site of [Ounjougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ounjougou) dating to about 9,400 BC, and are believed to represent an instance of the independent invention of pottery in the region.[^cite_note-39] Farming took place by 5000 BC, and iron was used by around 500 BC. In the first millennium BC, early cities and towns were created by [Mandé peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mand%C3%A9_peoples) related to the [Soninke people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_people), along the middle [Niger River](21437.md), including [Dia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dia,_Mali) which began from around 900 BC, and reached its peak around 600 BC,[^cite_note-Arazi-40] and [Djenné-Djenno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djenn%C3%A9-Djenno), which lasted from around 300 BC to 900 AD. Through approximately 6th century BC and 4th century BC, the lucrative [trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade) in pack animals, gold, salt and slaves had begun, facilitating the rise of West Africa's great empires.

### Rise of Islam

There are a few references to Mali in early Islamic literature. Among these are references to "Pene" and "Malal" in the work of [al-Bakri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bakri) in 1068,[^cite_note-41] the story of the conversion of an early ruler, known to [Ibn Khaldun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Khaldun) (by 1397) as Barmandana,[^cite_note-42] and a few geographical details in the work of [al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Idrisi).[^cite_note-43]

The have Keita dynasty have been claimed to have claimed descent from a man called "Lawalo", whom Niane claim was one of the sons of [Bilal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilal_ibn_Ribah), the faithful *[muezzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muezzin)* of [Islam](6037917.md)'s prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad).[^cite_note-sundiata-44] The original epos, however, tends to portray Sundiata as a powerful sorcerer and hunter, rather than a devout Muslim.[^cite_note-Fage-45] Religious life in the Mali Empire was divided between animist and Islamic belief systems, but the boundary was blurry and porous, if existent at all.[^cite_note-Badru-46][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFauvelle202267-47] The northern parts of the empire towards [Sahara desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_desert) tended to be more Islamicised, while [traditional African religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions) dominated in the south.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFauvelle202266-48]

&#x20;Mali was once part of three famed West African empires which controlled trans-Saharan trade in gold, salt, other precious commodities, and slaves majorly during the 14th century reign of [Mansa Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansa_Musa).[^cite_note-p1-49] These [Sahelian kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahelian_kingdoms) had neither rigid geopolitical boundaries nor rigid ethnic identities.[^cite_note-p1-49] The earliest of these empires was the [Ghana Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire), which was dominated by the [Soninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_people), a [Mande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mande_languages)-speaking people.[^cite_note-p1-49] The empire expanded throughout West Africa from the 8th century until 1078, when it was conquered by the [Almoravids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravid_dynasty).[^cite_note-p2-50]



[Griots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griot) of [Sambala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambala), king of Médina ([Fula people](102383.md), Mali), 1890. Photo by [Joannès Barbier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joann%C3%A8s_Barbier).

The [Battle of Kirina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kirina) in 1235 culminated in a victory for the [Mandinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_people) under the command of the exiled prince [Sundiata Keita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundiata_Keita), which led to the downfall of the [Sosso Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosso_Empire).

### Mali Empire

The [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire) formed on the upper [Niger River](21437.md) and reached its height of power in the 14th century.[^cite_note-p2-50] Following the death of Sundiata Keita, in c. 1255, the kings or emperors of Mali were referred to by the title *[mansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansa_\(title\))*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEImperatoImperato2008201-51] Largely due to its decentralised nature, the empire covered a larger area for a longer period of time than any other West African state before or since. [Joseph Ki-Zerbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Ki-Zerbo) states that the farther from the capital, the more decentralised the *mansa*'s power became.[^cite_note-52]

[Djenné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djenn%C3%A9) and [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu) were centers of both trade and Islamic learning.[^cite_note-p2-50] In the late 14th century, the Songhai gradually gained independence from the Mali Empire and expanded, ultimately subsuming the entire eastern portion of the Mali Empire.[^cite_note-p2-50] The Songhai Empire's eventual collapse was largely the result of the [Moroccan invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_invasion_of_the_Songhai_Empire) of 1591 under the command of [Judar Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judar_Pasha).[^cite_note-p2-50] The fall of the Songhai Empire marked the end of the region's role as a trading crossroads.[^cite_note-p2-50] Following the [establishment of sea routes by the European powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_exploration_of_Africa), the trans-Saharan trade routes lost significance.[^cite_note-p2-50] At that time, the Mali Empire's abundance in wealth expanded its commercial assets of salt and gold. Most of the empire consisted of autonomous kingdoms of communities who recognised the Mansa's ultimate authority and paid tribute.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECissoko198359-53] [Leo Africanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Africanus) made note of their professors and regarded Malians as "the most civilised, most intelligent, and most highly regarded of the blacks."[^cite_note-54]



Map of the [Pashalik of Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashalik_of_Timbuktu) (yellow-striped) as part of the [Saadi dynasty of Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_dynasty) (outlined black) within the [Songhai Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_Empire) (outlined red), c. 1591

Some of the worst famines in the region's recorded history occurred in the 17th and 18th centuries. According to [John Iliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Iliffe_\(historian\)), "The worst crises were in the 1680s, when famine extended from the [Senegambian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegambia) coast to the Upper Nile and 'many sold themselves for slaves, only to get a sustenance', and especially in 1738–1756, when West Africa's greatest recorded subsistence crisis, due to drought and locusts, reportedly killed half the population of Timbuktu."[^cite_note-55]

Mali fell under the control of France during the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa) in the late 19th century.[^cite_note-p2-50] By 1905, most of the area was under firm French control as a part of [French Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Sudan).[^cite_note-p2-50] In November 1915 a large [anti-French uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volta-Bani_War) broke out among the tribes.[^cite_note-56] French colonial troops destroyed over 100 villages, and the last resistance was suppressed by September 1916.[^cite_note-57] On 24 November 1958, French Sudan (which changed its name to the Sudanese Republic) became an autonomous republic within the [French Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Community).[^cite_note-Independent_Mali-58] In January 1959, Mali and [Senegal](27278.md) united to become the [Mali Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Federation).[^cite_note-Independent_Mali-58]

The Mali Federation gained independence from France on 20 June 1960.[^cite_note-p2-50] Senegal withdrew from the federation in August 1960, which allowed the Sudanese Republic to become the independent Republic of Mali on 22 September 1960, the country's [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_independence_days).[^cite_note-59]

### Independence and Keïta regime

[Modibo Keïta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modibo_Ke%C3%AFta) was elected the first president.[^cite_note-p2-50] He quickly established a one-party state, adopted an socialist orientation with close ties to the East, and nationalised large parts of the economy.[^cite_note-p2-50][^cite_note-60]

The only legal political party became the [Sudanese Union – African Democratic Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Union_%E2%80%93_African_Democratic_Rally) (*Union Soudanaise – Rassemblement Démocratique Africain,* US-RDA). Those who tried to create a political opposition were arrested and sentenced.[^cite_note-61] Prior to independence, Keïta and the US-RDA campaigned against colonial and authoritarian rule, advocating for a democratic system in its place.[^cite_note-62] This, however, did not happen and rather it became a [Marxist-Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) state. Keïta adapted the Marxist-Leninist ideology for [African socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_socialism), moving past the development of capitalism and instead opting for immediate strict control over foreign investments and involvement in the economy.[^cite_note-63]

The government introduced a national civil service programme, *service civique rural*, to help the development of Mali as a socialist and self-sustaining independent country.[^cite_note-64] The service targeted men in rural Mali aged 18–21 and in the early 1960s—when almost 60% of Mali's population was under age 25—had assembled around 40,000 young men.[^cite_note-65] The national civil service outlined a 2-year programme of education and training to prepare the men for the handling of their own duties in their communities as peasant farmers. As part of the programme, men were taught methods of modern agriculture while working on state owned farms, and they took lessons in literacy and were taught the socialist values that the Malian government expected from them.[^cite_note-66] The programme saw the use of young people as labourers for the state which led to significant desertions and a declining number recruited. Some people saw comparisons between the national civil service and forced labour under colonial rule. A 1962 report from [The International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization) deems the national service scheme unlawful as it did not follow the Forced Labour Convention that had made it illegal in 1957.[^cite_note-67] Another report from 1962 states that the Malian government had submitted only 1 of 11 annual reports to the Committee of Experts on the Application of Conventions and Recommendations regarding labour conventions that had been signed and ratified by Mali previously.[^cite_note-68]

At the time of independence, 93% were illiterate in French, 90% illiterate in Arabic, and very few people were given a Western education.[^cite_note-\:3-69][^cite_note-70] Those who did receive a Western education were part of the elites after independence. France used education in Mali to create an elite loyal to France and for the exploitation of people and resources.[^cite_note-71] In 1964 the government passed the Educational Reform Law which introduced 9 years of mandatory education across Mali.[^cite_note-\:6-72] By 1964 the number of school-age Malians in education had more than tripled the pre-independence numbers with 24% in school and the number of people in secondary education had increased from 434 students in 1962-1963 to 1,325 by 1966-1967.[^cite_note-\:6-72] Keïta had support from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in improving education, founding the Higher Administrative School of Bamako, the first institution of higher education in Mali, and created education centres for agriculture and medicine.[^cite_note-73] Education was used to promote socialist ideology and was adapted to fit the national aims of Keïta's economic and political policies.[^cite_note-74] The education system was changed to focus on African history, culture, and needs rather than that of France. Teaching materials focused on African socialism were introduced in schools.[^cite_note-75] Despite the increases in the number of people in education, numbers were still restricted due to the lack of teachers and resources.[^cite_note-76]

In 1962 Keïta created the [Bank of the Republic of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_the_Republic_of_Haiti) which was responsible for minting its own currency, the [Malian franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malian_franc). Keïta believed that Mali having its own currency was a sign of sovereignty, stating in a speech "political power is always and necessarily accompanied by the sovereign right of minting money, that monetary power is inseparable from national sovereignty".[^cite_note-77] It was revealed that the Malian franc would be equal to the [CFA franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFA_franc), the currency used in the Franc zone.[^cite_note-78] The Franc zone was created by France for continued monetary influence over former French colonies. Nationalising the banking industry gave the Malian government more control, reducing economic dependence and influence from foreign countries including France. Protestors of the Malian franc were arrested and sent to prison.[^cite_note-\:7-79] Throughout the 1960s, inflation increased, the economy declined, and so the Malian franc in 1967 was devalued by 50% against the CFA franc to prevent further inflation rates and further economic decline. In December 1967, Keïta announced an agreement between Mali and France that would later lead to Mali's re-entry into the [West African Economic and Monetary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Economic_and_Monetary_Union) in 1984.[^cite_note-80]

Opposition and critics of the regime were met with violence and imprisonment. Political opponents of the government were arrested, and some were sent to [Kidal](1463133.md), a desert town in northern Mali, and imprisoned there.[^cite_note-81] [Fily Dabo Sissoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fily_Dabo_Sissoko), who founded the [Sudanese Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Regroupment_Party) as a political opponent of the US-RDA, was imprisoned in Kidal and died there, supposedly under the orders of Keïta, though this is not confirmed.[^cite_note-\:7-79]

Keïta oversaw the nationalisation of various sectors of the economy to help the development of Mali as a socialist state.[^cite_note-82] To further control the economy and the development of industrialisation, Keïta created state-owned enterprises. These enterprises spanned across different industries including textiles, food processing, tobacco, cotton, and radio manufacturing. On 19 November 1968, following progressive economic decline, the Keïta regime was overthrown in a bloodless military coup led by [Moussa Traoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Traor%C3%A9),[^cite_note-p3-83] a day which is now commemorated as [Liberation Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Day).[^cite_note-84]

### Traoré regime, 1968-1991

The subsequent military-led regime, with Traoré as president, attempted to reform the economy. His efforts were frustrated by political turmoil and a devastating [drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droughts_in_the_Sahel) from 1968 to 1974,[^cite_note-p3-83] in which famine killed thousands of people.[^cite_note-85] The Traoré regime faced student unrest beginning in the late 1970s and three coup attempts. The Traoré regime repressed all dissenters until the late 1980s.[^cite_note-p3-83]



WWI Commemorative Monument to the "Armée Noire"

Opposition to the corrupt and dictatorial regime grew during the 1980s. During this time strict programs, imposed to satisfy demands of the International Monetary Fund, brought increased hardship upon the people, while elites close to the government supposedly lived in growing wealth. The government continued to attempt economic reforms, and the populace became increasingly dissatisfied.[^cite_note-p3-83] The late 1980s saw limited political liberalisation far from a full-fledged democratic system.[^cite_note-p3-83]

In 1990, cohesive opposition movements began to emerge and was complicated by the turbulent rise of ethnic violence in the north following the return of many [Tuaregs](103054.md) who had migrated to [Algeria](358.md) and [Libya](17633.md) during the drought.[^cite_note-p3-83] Peaceful student protests in January 1991 were brutally suppressed, with mass arrests and torture of leaders and participants.[^cite_note-nonviolent-86] Scattered acts of rioting and vandalism of public buildings followed, but most actions by the dissidents remained nonviolent.[^cite_note-nonviolent-86]

In March 1991, mass pro-democracy rallies and a nationwide strike was held in both urban and rural communities, which became known as *les évenements* ("the events") or the March Revolution. In response to mass demonstrations organized by university students that were later joined by trade unionists and others, soldiers opened fire indiscriminately on the nonviolent demonstrators. Riots broke out briefly following the shootings. Barricades as well as roadblocks were erected, and Traoré declared a state of emergency and imposed a nightly curfew. Despite an estimated loss of 300 lives over the course of four days, nonviolent protesters continued to return to Bamako each day demanding the resignation of Traoré and the implementation of democratic policies.[^cite_note-nesbitt-87]

By 26 March, the growing refusal of soldiers to fire into the largely nonviolent protesting crowds turned into a full-scale tumult. Military soldiers clashed with peaceful protesters, resulting in the massacre of dozens under the orders of Traoré. That afternoon, Lieutenant Colonel [Amadou Toumani Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadou_Toumani_Tour%C3%A9) announced on the radio that he had arrested Traoré. He and three associates were tried, convicted and received the death sentence for their role in this massacre. The date is now a national holiday in Mali.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] The coup is remembered as the [March Revolution of 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-90]

### Multi-party democracy

In 1992, a multi-party system was established, with a transitional government was formed and a national congress of civil and political groups met to draft a [democratic constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Constitution_of_Mali) to be approved by a national referendum.[^cite_note-nesbitt-87][^cite_note-p3-83] In 1992, [Alpha Oumar Konaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Oumar_Konar%C3%A9) won Mali's first democratic, multi-party presidential election, and he was re-elected in 1997. Touré was elected in 2002.[^cite_note-p4-91] During this democratic period Mali was regarded as one of the most politically and socially stable countries in Africa.[^cite_note-92]

[Slavery persists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_contemporary_Africa) in Mali today with as many as 200,000 people held in direct servitude to a master.[^cite_note-slaves-93] In the [Tuareg Rebellion of 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(2012\)), ex-slaves were a vulnerable population with reports of some slaves being recaptured by their former masters.[^cite_note-94]

### Northern Mali conflict

Main article: [Mali War](36856978.md)

See also: [International Criminal Court investigation in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Mali)



Tuareg separatist rebels in Mali, January 2012

In January 2012 a Tuareg rebellion began in northern Mali, led by the [National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad) (MNLA).[^cite_note-95] In March, military officer [Amadou Sanogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadou_Sanogo) seized power in [a coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), citing Touré's failures in quelling the rebellion and leading to sanctions and an embargo by the Economic Community of West African States ([ECOWAS](36848.md)).[^cite_note-96] The MNLA quickly took control of the north, declaring its independence as [Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawad).[^cite_note-97] Islamist groups, including [Ansar Dine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_Dine) and [Al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Islamic_Maghreb), who had helped the MNLA defeat the government, turned on the Tuareg and took control of the north[^cite_note-98] with the goal of implementing [sharia](28840.md) in Mali.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

On 11 January 2013, the [French Armed Forces](10724.md) [intervened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Serval) at the request of the interim government of President [Dioncounda Traoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioncounda_Traor%C3%A9). On 30 January, the coordinated advance of the French and Malian troops claimed to have retaken the last remaining Islamist stronghold of Kidal, which was also the last of three northern provincial capitals.[^cite_note-kidal_retaken-101] On 2 February, French President [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Hollande) joined Traoré in a public appearance in recently recaptured Timbuktu.[^cite_note-BBC-Hollande_Timbuktu-102]



Map showing the fullest extent of rebel-held territory in January 2013

In August 2013, [Ibrahim Boubacar Keita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Boubacar_Keita) was elected president of Mali in the second round of [the election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Malian_presidential_election).[^cite_note-103]

### Conflict in central Mali

In [Mopti Region](1064867.md), conflict has escalated since 2015 between agricultural communities like the [Dogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogon_people) and the [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_people), and the pastoral [Fula people](102383.md).[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-\:4-105] Historically, the two sides had fought over access to land and water, factors exacerbated by climate change as the Fula move into new areas.[^cite_note-106] The Dogon and the Bambara communitie accused the Fula of working with armed [Islamists](566821.md) linked to al-Qaeda.[^cite_note-\:4-105] While some Fula have joined Islamist groups, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) reports that the links had been exaggerated.[^cite_note-\:4-105]

The conflict has seen the creation and growth of Dogon and Bambara militias. The government is suspected of supporting some of these groups under the guise of being proxies in the war against Islamists in the northern Mali conflict.[^cite_note-\:5-107] The government denies this.[^cite_note-\:5-107] One such militia is the Dogon group [Dan Na Ambassagou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Na_Ambassagou), created in 2016.[^cite_note-\:4-105]



[Modibo Sidibe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modibo_Sidibe) voting in Bamako, 2018 Malian presidential election

In the [2018 Malian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Malian_presidential_election),[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] no candidate received more than 50% of the vote in the first round. A runoff was held on 12 August 2018 between the top two candidates, incumbent president [Ibrahim Boubacar Keïta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Boubacar_Ke%C3%AFta) of the [Rally for Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_for_Mali) and [Soumaïla Cissé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souma%C3%AFla_Ciss%C3%A9) of the [Union for the Republic and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Republic_and_Democracy), and Keïta was re-elected with 67% of the vote.[^cite_note-110]

In September 2018, the [Centre for Humanitarian Dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Humanitarian_Dialogue) negotiated a unilateral ceasefire with Dan Na Ambassagou "in the context of the conflict which opposes the group to other community armed groups in central Mali".[^cite_note-111] The group was blamed for the [24 March 2019 massacre of 160 Fula villagers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2019_attacks_against_Fulani_herders);[^cite_note-112] the group denied the attack, but Keïta ordered the group to disband.[^cite_note-113] The UN Special Adviser on the Prevention of Genocide, [Adama Dieng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adama_Dieng), warned of a growing ethnic resentments in the conflict.[^cite_note-114] By 2020, more than 600,000 people had been displaced by the conflict.[^cite_note-115] The United Nations reported that the number of children killed in the conflict in the first six months of 2019 was twice as many for the entire year of 2018. Many of the children have been killed in intercommunal attacks attributed to ethnic militias, with the majority of attacks occurring around [Mopti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mopti). It is reported that around 900 schools have closed down and that armed militias are recruiting children.[^cite_note-116]

In October 2019, two jihadist attacks in Boulikessi and [Mondoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondoro) killed more than 25 Mali soldiers near the border with Burkina Faso.[^cite_note-117] Keïta declared "no military coup will prevail in Mali", saying he does not think it "is on the agenda at all and cannot worry us".[^cite_note-118] On 1 November 2019, the [IS-GS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_in_the_Greater_Sahara) militants killed at least 50 soldiers in the [2019 Indelimane attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indelimane_attack) in the [Ménaka Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9naka_Region).[^cite_note-119] In February 2020, Human Rights Watch documented atrocities against civilians in Central Mali and said that at least 456 civilians were killed, while hundreds were injured from January 2019 until November.[^cite_note-120]

### 2020s coups and Assimi Goïta junta

Main articles: [2020 Malian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and [2021 Malian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)

See also: [List of terrorist attacks in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_terrorist_attacks_in_Mali)



Members of the [National Committee for the Salvation of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Committee_for_the_Salvation_of_the_People), September 2020

Popular unrest began on 5 June 2020 following irregularities in the March and April parliamentary elections, including outrage against the kidnapping of opposition leader [Soumaïla Cissé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souma%C3%AFla_Ciss%C3%A9).[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] Between 11 and 23 deaths followed protests that took place from 10 to 13 June.[^cite_note-cnews-123] In July Keïta dissolved the constitutional court.

Members of the military led by Colonel [Assimi Goïta](65000306.md) and Colonel-Major [Ismaël Wagué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isma%C3%ABl_Wagu%C3%A9) in [Kati](1386780.md), [Koulikoro Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koulikoro_Region), began a mutiny on 18 August 2020.[^cite_note-cnews-123] Keïta and Prime Minister [Boubou Cissé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boubou_Ciss%C3%A9) were arrested, and shortly after midnight Keïta announced his resignation, saying he did not want to see any bloodshed.[^cite_note-cnews-123] Wagué announced the formation of the [National Committee for the Salvation of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Committee_for_the_Salvation_of_the_People) (CNSP) and promised elections in the future. A curfew was begun, and the streets of Bamako were quiet.[^cite_note-cnews-123] ECOWAS condemned the coup and demanded that Keïta be reinstated as president.[^cite_note-neighbours-124]

In September 2020, the CNSP agreed to an 18-month political transition to civilian rule. Shortly after, [Bah N'daw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah_Ndaw) was named interim president by a group of 17 electors, with Goïta being appointed vice president. The government was inaugurated on 25 September. In January 2021 the transitional government announced that the CNSP had been disbanded.[^cite_note-125] Tensions between the civilian transitional government and the military ran high after the handover of power. The tensions came to a head on 24 May 2021 after a cabinet reshuffle, where two leaders of the 2020 military coup—[Sadio Camara](67924702.md) and [Modibo Kone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modibo_Kone)—were replaced by N'daw's administration.[^cite_note-126] On 7 June Goïta was sworn into office as interim president.[^cite_note-127]



Military situation in Mali. For a detailed map, see [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template\:Mali_War_detailed_map).

In 2022 and 2023, the [Islamic State in the Greater Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_in_the_Greater_Sahara) saw major gains in the Mali War, occupying large swaths of territory in southeastern Mali. [Ansongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansongo) and [Tidermène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiderm%C3%A8ne) were captured.[^cite_note-bbc_africatoday-128] By mid-2023, the militant group had doubled the amount of territory it controlled since the overthrow of the previous government and establishment of the junta.[^cite_note-npr-129]

On 10 January 2022, Mali announced the closure of its borders and recalled several ambassadors to ECOWAS countries in response to sanctions placed on Mali for deferring elections for four years.[^cite_note-130] On 4 February, France's ambassador was expelled.[^cite_note-131] According to [Human Rights Watch](46530.md), Malian troops and suspected Russian mercenaries from the [Wagner Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group) executed around 300 civilian men in central Mali in March 2022. France had started withdrawing French troops from Mali in February 2022, commencing the end of [Operation Barkhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barkhane).[^cite_note-132] On 2 May, the military government announced breaking its defence accords concluded in 2013 with France, constituting an additional step in the deterioration of Malian–French relations.[^cite_note-133] France considered the announcement as illegitimate.[^cite_note-134] A UN panel reported that in the first three months of 2022, 543 civilians were killed and 269 wounded, warning the 2015 peace agreement between the government and pro-independence groups was threatened by a potential risk of confrontation for the first time in five years. The need for humanitarian assistance also increased.[^cite_note-135]

In June 2023, Mali removed French as an official language with the approval of a new constitution by 97% of voters in [a referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Malian_constitutional_referendum) conducted by the junta.[^cite_note-136] In July 2024, [CSP-DPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Framework_for_the_Defense_of_the_People_of_Azawad) rebels and JNIM militants killed dozens of Russian mercenaries and Malian government forces during the [Battle of Tinzaouaten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tinzaouaten_\(2024\)).[^cite_note-137] On 17 September 2024, al-Qaeda linked JNIM militants [attacked several locations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bamako_attacks) across Bamako, killing at least 77 people and injuring 255 others.[^cite_note-138]

In 2025 JNIM began a strategy to blockade the government-controlled cities from foreign fuel imports and to cut them off from each other.[^cite_note-AfricaCenter-139] Mali depends on foreign fuel imports, receiving 95% of its fuel from Senegal or Ivory Coast.[^cite_note-AfricaCenter-139][^cite_note-140] JNIM attacks cut off connections to Mauritania and Senegal, and they were followed by similar attacks in the south near Ivory Coast and Guinea.[^cite_note-AfricaCenter-139] JNIM imposed a blockade against cities in southern Mali, including Bamako, after the government had stopped fuel sales in rural areas.[^cite_note-ISW-100925-30] This increased fuel prices in the capital by 500% and led to economic disruption, lines at gas stations, school closures, and cancellations of flights from the [Bamako International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako_International_Airport).[^cite_note-AJ-102925-141] In early September the Malian truckers' union stopped operations for two weeks due to the dangerous conditions along roads in southern Mali, leaving 1,000 fuel trucks waiting in Ivory Coast.[^cite_note-AfricaCenter-139] In October the U.S. Embassy advised American citizens to leave the country immediately because of increasing instability, and to do so by plane, because of "terrorist attacks along national highways".[^cite_note-AJ-102925-141] In April 2026, [widespread unrest was reported from various locations across the country](83033878.md), apparently sponsored by a diverse range of rebel groups.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-AP-26042026-31]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Mali)



Satellite image of Mali

Mali is a landlocked country in [West Africa](67393.md). It lies between latitudes [10°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_parallel_north) and [25°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_parallel_north) and longitudes [13°W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_meridian_west) and [5°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_meridian_east). Mali borders [Algeria](358.md) to [the north-northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria%E2%80%93Mali_border), [Niger](21373.md) to [the east](62289405.md), [Burkina Faso](3470.md) to [the southeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkina_Faso%E2%80%93Mali_border), [Ivory Coast](57744.md) to [the south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_Coast%E2%80%93Mali_border), [Guinea](12176.md) to [the south-west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea%E2%80%93Mali_border), and [Senegal](27278.md) to [the west](62957126.md) and [Mauritania](18778516.md) to [the north-west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali%E2%80%93Mauritania_border).[^cite_note-144]



Landscape in [Hombori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hombori)



Mali map of Köppen climate classification

At 1,240,192 square kilometres (478,841 sq mi),[^cite_note-CIA-2021-Mali-145] Mali is the [24th-largest country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_outlying_territories_by_total_area) in the world and the [eighth-largest country in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_area).[^cite_note-146] Most of the country lies in the southern Sahara Desert, which produces an extremely hot, dust-laden [Sudanian savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanian_savanna) zone.[^cite_note-geography-147] Mali is mostly flat, rising to rolling northern plains covered by sand. The [Adrar des Ifoghas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_des_Ifoghas) massif lies in the northeast.

Mali lies in the [torrid zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_zone#Torrid_Zone) and is among the hottest countries in the world. The [thermal equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_equator) (which matches the hottest spots year-round on the planet based on the mean daily annual temperature) crosses the country.[^cite_note-geography-147] Most of Mali receives negligible rainfall, and droughts are very frequent.[^cite_note-geography-147] Late April to early October is the rainy season in the south. During this time, flooding of the Niger River is common, creating the [Inner Niger Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Niger_Delta).[^cite_note-geography-147] The vast northern desert has a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BWh*) with long, extremely hot summers and scarce rainfall which decreases northwards. The central area has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) (Köppen climate classification *BSh*) with very high temperatures year-round, a long, intense dry season and a brief, irregular rainy season. The southern areas have a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate) (Köppen climate classification *Aw*).

Mali has considerable natural resources, with gold, uranium, phosphates, kaolinite, salt and limestone being most widely exploited. Mali is estimated to have in excess of 17,400 tonnes of uranium (measured + indicated + inferred).[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] In 2012, additional uranium deposits were identified in the north.[^cite_note-150] Mali faces numerous environmental challenges, including [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification), [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), [soil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion), and inadequate supplies of [potable water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potable_water).[^cite_note-geography-147]

### Biodiversity

See also: [Wildlife of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Mali)

Five terrestrial ecoregions lie within Mali's borders: [Sahelian Acacia savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahelian_Acacia_savanna), [West Sudanian savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sudanian_savanna), [Inner Niger Delta flooded savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Niger_Delta_flooded_savanna), [South Saharan steppe and woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Saharan_steppe_and_woodlands), and [West Saharan montane xeric woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Saharan_montane_xeric_woodlands).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-151] The country had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 7.16/10, ranking it 51st globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-152]

## Politics and government

Main article: [Politics of Mali](19131.md)

### Government



[Assimi Goita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assimi_Goita), interim president of Mali since the [2021 Malian coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)

Mali is led by [transitional President](430390.md) [Assimi Goïta](65000306.md), who is assisted by [Prime Minister](889867.md) [Abdoulaye Maïga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdoulaye_Ma%C3%AFga_\(officer\)),[^cite_note-153] and the [National Transitional Council](83059416.md) that replaced the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Mali\)) following the 2020 coup. However, in practice, Goïta and a military junta consisting of four other officers, the [National Committee for the Salvation of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Committee_for_the_Salvation_of_the_People), have complete control over the political system.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] The [1992 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Constitution_of_Mali)[^cite_note-UF-Sahara-154] established a Mali as a secular [constitutional republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_republic) with a separation of powers between executive, legislative, and judicial branches, and identifies eight state institutions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_25-155][^cite_note-UF-Sahara-154] These included the presidency; the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_Mali); the National Assembly; the Supreme, Constitutional, and High Courts; the High Council of Territorial Units; and the Economic, Social, and Cultural Council.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_25-155] The 1992 constitution was replaced by the junta with a new constitution on 22 July 2023.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28]

The president, who is elected by universal suffrage for a five-year term that is renewable once,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_30-156] is the head of state that serves as the guardian of the constitution,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_29-157] the commander-in-chief of the armed forces,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_44-158] and appoints the prime minister and other government ministers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_38-159] The prime minister is the head of government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_55-160] The National Assembly of Mali was a unicameral legislature[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_59-161] consisting of deputies elected by universal suffrage to a term of five years.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_61-162] It convened twice a year,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_65-163] or in extraordinary sessions called by the prime minister or the parliamentary majority.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_66-164] The transitional government pushed back the timetable for a new election, initially to be held in February 2022, to February 2024.[^cite_note-New_Election-165] In exchange for the government's commitment to a 2024 election, [ECOWAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) agreed to lift sanctions on the country.[^cite_note-ECOWAS_sanctions_lifted-166] In July 2025 a law granted Goïta a five-year term that is renewable indefinitely, and there are no immediate plans to hold elections.[^cite_note-ITA-17]

The constitution provides for an independent judiciary, tasked with the application of the laws of the republic and the protection of civil liberties,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_81-167] which are guaranteed by both the 1992 and 2023 constitutions.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] Mali's highest courts are the Supreme Court, which has both judicial and administrative powers,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Article_83-168] and a separate Constitutional Court that provides judicial review of legislative acts and serves as an election arbiter.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConstitution_of_Mali_(1992)Articles_86,_87-169] Various lower courts exist, though village chiefs and elders resolve most local disputes in rural areas.[^cite_note-p15-170] In practice, many of the freedoms that were gained during the development of democracy after 1991, and were relatively well respected, have been suppressed during the 2020s military junta.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] There is no real separation of powers, with the junta being dominant. Freedom of association, expression, and assembly are undermined by the transitional government, and members of the judiciary and civil service have been dismissed for criticizing the junta.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28]

According to [International IDEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Democracy_and_Electoral_Assistance)'s Global State of Democracy Indices and Democracy Tracker, Mali performs in the low range on overall democratic measures, with particular weaknesses in political representation and rule of law.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist) [Democracy Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index)* classified Mali as an "authoritarian regime" in 2024, ranking it 139 out of the 167 countries that were evaluated.[^cite_note-174] [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) describes Mali as "not free" in 2026.[^cite_note-175] The [Fragile States Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragile_States_Index) ranked Mali as the 14th most fragile country in the world, out of 179 countries, in 2024.[^cite_note-176]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Mali)



Assimi Goïta with Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) at the [Russia–Africa Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Russia%E2%80%93Africa_Summit) in St. Petersburg, July 2023

Until 2012, foreign policy orientation had become increasingly pragmatic and pro-Western over time.[^cite_note-p17-177] Starting from the 1990s, Mali's successive governments worked with the [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF) to obtain debt relief, as well as to end the country's political and economic isolation.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] After the institution of a democratic form of government, relations with the West in general and [with the United States](15993198.md) in particular have improved significantly.[^cite_note-p17-177] Mali has a longstanding relationship with France.[^cite_note-p17-177] Mali was a member of the in [African Union](10634933.md) until its suspension over the 2012 coup d'état.[^cite_note-p17-177][^cite_note-178]

After the coups in 2020 and 2021, Mali took a more belligerent stance towards the UN, ECOWAS, and France.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] At the request of the government, France withdrew its troops from Mali in 2022, and the UN followed in 2023.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] The [Alliance of Sahel States](74840743.md) was formed in 2023 between Mali, Niger, and Burkina Faso after they [experienced similar coups in the early 2020s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_Belt).[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] It is anti-French, anti-[neocolonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neocolonialism) and anti-ECOWAS, demonstrated with acts including the removal of French as an official language in all three states.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] Mali ended its membership in ECOWAS in 2025. The country expanded its relationship with Russia, acquiring military equipment and assistance.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28]

Working to control and resolve regional conflicts—such as in Ivory Coast, Liberia, and Sierra Leone—is one of its major foreign policy goals.[^cite_note-p17-177] Mali feels threatened by the potential for the spillover of conflicts in neighboring states, and relations with those neighbors are often uneasy.[^cite_note-p17-177] General insecurity along borders in the north, including cross-border banditry and terrorism, remain troubling issues in regional relations.[^cite_note-p17-177]

### Military



Malian soldiers at the 2013 [Bastille Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastille_Day) parade in France

Mali has 41,000 troops in its military and paramilitary forces. These include the [Malian Armed Forces](19135.md) which consist of an army and air force, numbering 19,000 and 2,000 personnel, respectively, as of 2025. The Gendarmerie includes eight paramilitary companies and an air transport group, while the National Guard has six camel cavalry companies and a special forces anti-terrorism unit. The military is under-equipped and has a limited capability for the manufacturing and maintenance of equipment.[^cite_note-IISS-2025-184]

Russia's [Africa Corps](78298390.md) has between 1,500[^cite_note-IISS-2025-184] and 3,500 troops across Mali.[^cite_note-185]

### Territorial units

A map of former regions of Mali

Regions of Mali since 2023

The administrative structure is organized on four levels: 19 [regions](690325.md), 159 [cercles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercles_of_Mali), 466 [arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Mali), and 819 [communes](16112417.md) or 12,712 villages, along with the capital district of Bamako.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] Each region has a governor[^cite_note-pictures-186] and is represented on the High Council of Territorial Units by indirectly elected national councilors.[^cite_note-UF-Sahara-154] Many rural areas lack any state presence, with the administrative structure being distributed unevenly. This is because of the size of the country and the persistent insecurity.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28]

Mali has been implementing decentralization since 1993. There were eight regions and the district of Bamako from 1996,[^cite_note-UF-Sahara-154] which became ten regions in 2006.[^cite_note-187] Since 2023, Mali has added nine regions to its administrative structure, bringing the total to 19 regions plus the district of Bamako. This initiative continues the decentralization efforts that began with the creation of the Taoudénit and Ménaka regions in 2016.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Mali](19132.md)



A market scene in [Djenné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djenn%C3%A9)



[Kalabougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalabougou) potters



Cotton processing at CMDT

As of 2024, the country had a [nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) of [US\$](18717338.md)26.78 billion[^cite_note-193] and a [PPP-adjusted GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)) of [\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_dollar)81.45 billion.[^cite_note-194] Mali remains one of the poorest and [least developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_countries) in the world and has a large informal economy. It has heavily relied on assistance from foreign donors, which have reduced their activities during the instability of the 2020s. Since then the transitional government has used regional financial markets to finance its deficit.[^cite_note-ITA-17][^cite_note-UNCTAD-18] Most Malians (95%) have informal jobs,[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] with 80% of the population involved in farming, fishing, or raising livestock.[^cite_note-ITA-17] The agriculture sector is the largest, accounting for a third of the GDP.[^cite_note-ITA-17] Mali's export economy is dominated by gold mining, which represented 80% of the country's exports in 2024. Though cotton is a distant second as an export commodity, one-fifth of the population works in that industry.[^cite_note-ITA-17] Ivory Coast, Senegal, and China are Mali's top import partners, while South Africa, Switzerland, and Australia are top export partners.[^cite_note-ITA-17]

Economic development has been undermined by instability, limited infrastructure and electricity, and government corruption. There has been an economic recovery since the [COVID-19 pandemic in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Mali) and the ECOWAS sanctions in 2022.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28] As a member of the [West African Economic and Monetary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Economic_and_Monetary_Union), Mali uses the CFA franc that is issued by the [Central Bank of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_West_African_States). As a result, it experiences monetary stability and low inflation. However, businesses have struggled to get access to financing in recent years.[^cite_note-ITA-17] Mali is also connected with the French government by agreement since the creation of the bank in 1962.[^cite_note-195]



GDP per capita development of Mali up to 2018

Mali underwent economic reform beginning in 1988 by signing agreements with the [World Bank](36752.md) and the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md).[^cite_note-state-196] During 1988 to 1996, the government largely reformed public enterprises.[^cite_note-state-196] Between 1992 and 1995, Mali implemented an economic adjustment programme that resulted in economic growth and a reduction in financial imbalances. The programme increased social and economic conditions and led to Mali joining the [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) in 1995.[^cite_note-197] Mali is a member of the [Organization for the Harmonization of Business Law in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_the_Harmonization_of_Business_Law_in_Africa) ([OHADA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OHADA)).[^cite_note-ohada.com-198] Mali was ranked 135th out of 139 in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200]

### Agriculture

Mali's key industry is agriculture. Cotton is the largest crop export and is exported west throughout Senegal and Ivory Coast.[^cite_note-Goldenhope-201][^cite_note-marshall-202] During 2002, 620,000 tons of cotton were produced, but cotton prices declined significantly in 2003.[^cite_note-Goldenhope-201][^cite_note-marshall-202] In addition to cotton, Mali produces rice, millet, corn, vegetables, tobacco, and tree crops.[^cite_note-state-196] Agriculture is undermined by erratic rainfall, flooding, low usage of agricultural equipment, and a volatile security situation.[^cite_note-ITA-A-203]

Eighty percent of workers are employed in agriculture, and 15% are employed in the service sector.[^cite_note-marshall-202] Seasonal variations lead to regular temporary unemployment of agricultural workers.[^cite_note-204] Around 127,000 hectares of agricultural land are managed by the state-owned Niger River Authority (Office du Niger).[^cite_note-ITA-A-203]

### Mining



Melted gold at [Sadiola Gold Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiola_Gold_Mine), [Kayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayes_Region) (Source: [Iamgold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iamgold))

Gold is mined in the southern region,[^cite_note-Goldenhope-201] and Mali was the second largest producer of gold in Africa as of 2024.[^cite_note-ITA-M-205] In 2015, the country produced 41 metric tonnes of gold.[^cite_note-206] The emergence of gold as its leading export product since 1999 has helped mitigate some of the negative impact of the cotton and Ivory Coast crises.[^cite_note-207] Other natural resources include kaolin, salt, phosphate, and limestone.[^cite_note-state-196]

In 1991, with the assistance of the [International Development Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Development_Association), Mali relaxed the enforcement of mining codes which led to renewed foreign interest and investment in the mining industry.[^cite_note-208] In 2023, Mali adopted a mining code that raises the requirement of state and local investor ownership of mining companies to 35% from the previous 20%, seeking to increase revenue. The code has led to disputes with established mining companies.[^cite_note-ITA-M-205]

### Energy

See also: [List of power stations in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Mali)

Electricity and water are maintained by the Energie du Mali (EDM).[^cite_note-state-196] Outside the cities, the Malian Agency for Rural Electrification allows private companies to generate and distribute electricity to villages. EDM is heavily financed by the government and regional multinational banks. As of 2023, the majority (66%) of electricity was generated by diesel generators, followed by [hydroelectric](381399.md) generation. Solar represented 2.7% of generation capacity.[^cite_note-ITA-E-209] In the cities, 55% of the population have access to EDM.[^cite_note-210] Overall, the national accessibility to electricity is 40%, and it is just 20% in rural areas.[^cite_note-ITA-17] Powers cuts are common,[^cite_note-ITA-E-209] and as of 2024 Bamako and other areas experienced blackouts that lasted up to 18 hours.[^cite_note-BTI-2026-28]

### Transportation

Main article: [Transport in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Mali)

Mali has 89,000 kilometres (55,000 mi) of roads, which are in poor condition. The most significant highway is the [Trans-Sahelian Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Sahelian_Highway), which links three major cities—Bamako, [Sikasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikasso), and [Kayes](1079700.md)—with [Dakar](44251.md) and [N'Djamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Djamena). The section from Bamako to the Senegalese border is called the Northern Bamako-Dakar Corridor, and is Mali's most important roadway, with the majority of its imports and exports passing on it. Domestically, the Northern Bamako-Dakar Corridor uses Malian National Routes 1 and 3. Another major highway is the Trans-Saharan Road Corridor, which uses National Routes 6, 16, and 17 to connect Bamako to Ségou, Mopti, Sévaré, and Gao; as well as to [Algiers](1644.md) and [Lagos](85232.md) via Niger.[^cite_note-WorldBank2024-211]

The [Dakar–Bamako Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar%E2%80%93Niger_Railway) runs parallel to part of the Northern Bamako-Dakar Corridor.[^cite_note-WorldBank2024-211] The Malian section has 641 kilometres (398 mi) of track. The railway was managed by foreign companies until they ceased operations in 2010. In 2016, Mali and Senegal formed the joint company *Dakar Bamako Ferroviaire* to revive the line.[^cite_note-212] Passenger service between Kayes and Bamako was restarted in June 2023 after five years of renovation.[^cite_note-213] There are also approximately 29 airports, of which 8 have paved runways.[^cite_note-214] Urban areas are known for their large quantity of green and white taxi cabs. A significant sum of the population is dependent on public transportation.

In 2021 the population was an estimated 21.9 million,[^cite_note-UN_WPP-215][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-216] which grew from 7.7 million in 1982.[^cite_note-217] The population is predominantly rural (69.7% in 2022),[^cite_note-2022census-7] and 5%–10% of Malians are nomadic.[^cite_note-p6-4] More than 90% live in southern Mali, especially in Bamako,[^cite_note-p6-4] which has over 4 million residents.[^cite_note-2022census-7]

In 2024, about 47% were 14 years old or younger, 50% were 15–64 years old, and 3% were 65 and older.[^cite_note-CIA-2021-Mali-145] The median age was 16.4 years.[^cite_note-CIA-2021-Mali-145] The [birth rate](515898.md) in 2024 was 40 births per 1,000, and the [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) in 2024 was 5.35 children per woman.[^cite_note-CIA-2021-Mali-145] The [death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_rate) in 2024 was 8.1 deaths per 1,000.[^cite_note-CIA-2021-Mali-145] [Life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) at birth was 63.2 years total (60.9 for males and 65.6 for females).[^cite_note-CIA-2021-Mali-145] Mali has one of the [world's highest rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_infant_mortality_rate) of [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality),[^cite_note-p6-4] with 57.4 deaths per 1,000 live births in 2024.[^cite_note-CIA-2021-Mali-145]

### Ethnic groups



A [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_people) wedding in Mali, observed by a tourist



[Fulani](102383.md) children in Mali



The [Tuareg](103054.md) are nomadic inhabitants of northern Mali.

Mali's population encompasses several [sub-Saharan](27067.md) ethnic groups. The [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_people) are by far the largest single ethnic group, making up over a third of the population, particularly concentrated around Bamako.[^cite_note-\:8-191] According to the 2022 general population and housing census, the largest ethnic groups are the Bambara (35.9%), Fulani (Peuhl) (12.8%), [Senufo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senufo_people)/[Manianka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_people) (9.4%), Malinke (8.4%), Sarakole/[Soninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_people)/Marka (8.2%), [Dogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogon_people) (6.1%), [Sonrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_people) (4.8%), Tuareg (3.9%), [Bobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobo_people)/[Bwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bwa_people) (2.2%), Bozo (1.9%), Arabs (1.7%), with smaller ethnic groups making up the rest.[^cite_note-2022census-7] While some ethnicities, like the Bambara and Fula, are spread throughout southern Mali, others have particular concentrations. The Malinke and Soninke live in the west. The [Bandiagara Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandiagara_Escarpment) is dominated by the Dogon, while the Songhai mainly inhabit the Niger River valley.[^cite_note-\:8-191] The Sahara is dominated by the nomadic Arab and [Tuareg](103054.md) tribes. In Mali and in Niger, the Moors are also known as [Azawagh Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawagh_Arabs), named after the Azawagh region of the Sahara. They speak mainly [Hassaniya Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassaniya_Arabic), one of the regional [varieties of Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Arabic).[^cite_note-219]

In the far north, there is a division within the Tuareg between the free Tuareg and the darker-skinned Bella, who formed the historical slave caste of the region. An estimated 800,000 people in Mali are descended from slaves.[^cite_note-slaves-93] Slavery has persisted in Mali for centuries.[^cite_note-220] The Arabic population kept slaves well into the 20th century, until slavery was suppressed by French authorities around the mid-20th century. There still persist certain hereditary servitude relationships,[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222] and according to some estimates approximately 200,000 Malians are enslaved.[^cite_note-223]

Some mixed European/African descendants of Muslims of Spanish, as well as French, Irish, Italian and Portuguese origin, live in Mali, where they are known as the [Arma people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arma_people) (1% of the population).[^cite_note-224]

Although Mali has enjoyed reasonably good inter-ethnic relationships based on a long history of coexistence, some hereditary servitude and bondage relationship exist, as well as ethnic tension between settled Songhai and nomadic Tuaregs of the north.[^cite_note-p6-4] Due to a backlash against the northern population after independence, Mali is now in a situation where both groups complain about discrimination on the part of the other group.[^cite_note-225] This conflict also plays a role in the continuing northern Mali conflict where there is a tension between both Tuaregs and the Malian government, and the Tuaregs and Jihadist militias who are trying to establish sharia.[^cite_note-226]

There is a small [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Mali) community in Mali.[^cite_note-227]

Malians speak a variety of languages, the most widely being [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language) spoken as mother tongue by nearly half the population. In January 2022 the government announced Bambara its official language;[^cite_note-2022endfrench-230] French was dropped as an official language, becoming instead a [working language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_language).[^cite_note-Lingua_2023-2] At the same time, the 13 national languages,[^cite_note-LangNat-3] namely Bambara, [Bobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobo_language), [Bozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozo_language), [Dogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogon_languages), [Fula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_language), [Hassaniya Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassaniya_Arabic), [Kassonke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassonke_language), [Maninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maninka_language), [Minyanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minyanka_language), [Senufo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senufo_languages), the [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhay_languages), [Soninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_language) and [Tamasheq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamasheq_language), became official languages.[^cite_note-Lingua_2023-2] The *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca)* in Mali is mainly Bambara, which about 80 percent of the population can communicate in.[^cite_note-p6-4] Over 40 other [African languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_languages) are spoken by the various ethnic groups of Mali.[^cite_note-p6-4]

According to the 2022 census, the languages spoken natively were Bambara by 49.9%,[^cite_note-censusculture-228] Fula (8.2%), [Mandinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_language) (7.1%), Soninké (6.5%), Dogon (6.0%), Songhai (4.6%), [Minianka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minianka_language) (4.2%), [Tamasheq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamasheq_language) (3.9%), Sénoufo (2.8%), Bobo (1.9%), [Tieyaxo Bozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozo_language) (1.4%), [Kassonké](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassonke_language) (1.1%), [Maure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassaniya_Arabic) or Hassaniya Arabic (0.9%), [Dafing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marka_language) (0.3%), [Samogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samogo_languages) (0.5%), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) (other dialects) (0.4%), other Malian languages (0.6%), French (0.1%), other African languages (0.1%), and other non-African languages (0.1%).[^cite_note-2022census-7]

An entrance to the Djinguereber mosque

Islam was introduced to West Africa in the 11th century and remains the predominant religion in much of the region. The 2022 census reported that 96.4% of Malians are [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Mali) (mostly [non-denominational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslim) and [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni)[^cite_note-pew-232]), approximately 2.3% are Christian (about 1.4% [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Mali) and 0.8% [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism)), and the remaining 0.7% adhere to [traditional African religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions) such as the [Dogon religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogon_religion).[^cite_note-IRFR-233][^cite_note-2022census-7] [Atheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) and [agnosticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnosticism) are believed to be rare among Malians, most of whom practise their religion daily,[^cite_note-p7-234] though the census found 0.5% of Malians identified with no religion.[^cite_note-2022census-7]

The constitution establishes a [secular state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_state) and provides for [freedom of religion](46006.md), and the government largely respects this right.[^cite_note-p7-234]

Islam as historically practised in Mali has been malleable and adapted to local conditions; relations between Muslims and practitioners of minority religious faiths have generally been amicable.[^cite_note-p7-234] After the 2012 imposition of [sharia](28840.md) rule in northern parts of the country, however, Mali came to be listed high (number 7) in the Christian persecution index published by [Open Doors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Doors), which described the persecution in the north as severe.[^cite_note-DeutscheWelle-235][^cite_note-OpenDoor-236]

### Education



High school students in [Kati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kati)

Public [education in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Mali) is in principle provided free of charge and is compulsory for nine years between the ages of seven and sixteen.[^cite_note-p7-234] The system encompasses six years of primary education beginning at age 7, followed by six years of secondary education.[^cite_note-p7-234] Primary school enrollment rate is low, in large part because families are unable to cover the cost of uniforms, books, supplies, and other fees required to attend.[^cite_note-p7-234]

In 2017, the primary school enrollment rate was 61% (65% of males and 58% of females).[^cite_note-237] In the late 1990s, the secondary school enrollment rate was 15% (20% of males and 10% of females).[^cite_note-p7-234] The education system is plagued by a lack of schools in rural areas, as well as shortages of teachers and materials.[^cite_note-p7-234]

Estimates of literacy rates range from 27–30 to 46.4%, with literacy rates significantly lower among women than men.[^cite_note-p7-234] The [University of Bamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bamako), which includes four constituent universities, is the largest university and enrolls approximately 60,000 undergraduate and graduate students.[^cite_note-238]

### Health

Main article: [Health in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Mali)

Mali faces numerous health challenges related to poverty, malnutrition, and inadequate hygiene and sanitation.[^cite_note-p7-234] Health and development indicators rank among the worst in the world.[^cite_note-p7-234] Life expectancy at birth is estimated to be 63.2 years in 2024.[^cite_note-CIA-2021-Mali-145] In 2000, 62–65% of the population was estimated to have access to safe drinking water and only 69% to sanitation services of some kind.[^cite_note-p7-234] In 2001, the general government expenditures on health totaled about US\$4 per capita at an average exchange rate.[^cite_note-p8-239]

Efforts have been made to improve nutrition and reduce associated health problems by encouraging women to make nutritious versions of local recipes. For example, the [International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crops_Research_Institute_for_the_Semi-Arid_Tropics) and the [Aga Khan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aga_Khan_Foundation) trained women's groups to make *equinut*, a healthy and nutritional version of the traditional recipe *di-dèguè* (comprising peanut paste, honey and millet or rice flour). The aim was to boost nutrition and livelihoods by producing a product that women could make and sell, and which would be accepted by the local community because of its local heritage.[^cite_note-240]



Village in the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) region

Medical facilities are very limited, and medicines are in short supply.[^cite_note-p8-239] [Malaria](20423.md) and other arthropod-borne diseases are prevalent, as are a number of infectious diseases such as [cholera](7591.md) and [tuberculosis](30653.md).[^cite_note-p8-239] The population suffers from a high rate of child malnutrition and a low rate of immunity.[^cite_note-p8-239] An estimated 1.9% of the adult and children population was afflicted with HIV/AIDS that year, among the lowest rates in [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md).[^cite_note-p8-239] An estimated 85%–91% of Mali's girls and women have had [female genital mutilation](11408.md) (2006 and 2001 data).[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242]

In 2024, approximately 7.1 million people, including over 3.8 million children, require urgent humanitarian assistance due to escalating conflict and climate crises. [UNICEF](19698110.md) is amplifying its efforts to provide essential services like health, education, and protection, while appealing for \$133.5 million to address these needs. The situation is dire, with over 522,000 children lacking access to education and millions at risk of malnutrition amid underfunded humanitarian responses.[^cite_note-243]

### Gender inequality

In 2017, Mali ranked 157th out of 160 countries in the gender inequality index as reported by the [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme).[^cite_note-244] The constitution states that it protects women's rights, however many laws exist that discriminate against women.[^cite_note-\:02-245] Provisions in the laws limit women's decision-making power after marriage, in which the husband becomes superior to his wife.[^cite_note-\:02-245] Women are blamed for not maintaining the appearance of their husbands and are also blamed for the actions of their children if they misbehave, which encourages the cultural attitude that women are inferior to men.[^cite_note-\:02-245] The lack of participation of women in politics is due to the idea that politics is associated with men and that women should avoid this sector.[^cite_note-\:02-245] Education is also an area in which boys dominate, since it is seen as a better investment for the parents.[^cite_note-\:02-245] As traditional values and practices have contributed to gender inequality, conflict and lawlessness have also influenced the growing gap in gender through gender-based violence.[^cite_note-\:2-246]

Religion, the patriarchal norms, and gender-based violence are major negative factors shaping the lives of women.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Patriarchal norms cause major gender inequalities and lead to male domination within the household.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Girls learn household activities like chores, cooking, and childcare at a young age and are expected to take the main responsibility of household chores throughout their life. This hampers women's ability to enter the formal workforce and leads to a lack of education of girls.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Gender-based violence happens both on a national and a family level. At the national level, in 2012 the conflict in the north increased cases of kidnappings and rapes.[^cite_note-\:2-246] The conflict also reduced women's access to resources, economy, and opportunities.[^cite_note-\:2-246] At the household level, women face gender-based violence through domestic violence, forced marriages, and marital rape.[^cite_note-\:02-245] The Demographic Health Survey for Mali in 2013 stated that 76% of women and 54% of men believed physical harm towards women was acceptable if the women burnt food, argued back, went out without notifying her husband, or refused sexual relations with her husband.[^cite_note-\:2-246] In 2024, officials approved a bill criminalising [intimate relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_Mali) between same-sex couples.[^cite_note-248]



Malian women in Djenné

After adjusting the entrance requirements and access to education, girls have lower enrollment rates and less access to formal education.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Drop-out rates for girls are 15% higher than that of boys because they have a higher responsibility at home and most parents refuse to allow all their children to go to school, so boys tend to become educated.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Technical and vocational education has less female participation and isinadequately distributed throughout the country, with the training centers focused in the urban cities.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Higher education for girls consist of short programs because early marriages prevent most girls from pursuing a longer term education program like those in science.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Although women do not have the same access to education, in recent decades women have been entering and representing themselves in decision-making positions in the public administration sector, which has led to the promotion of women's rights in the political sphere..[^cite_note-\:1-247] Out of 147 members of Parliament, 15 were women in 2010.[^cite_note-\:1-247]

Legislation at the international and national levels have been implemented over the decades to help promote women's rights.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Mali signed the [Beijing Platform for Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Platform_for_Action), which suggests that women should participate in decision-making, and the convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women, which is the foundation to women's rights promotion.[^cite_note-\:1-247] The constitution dictates equality to all citizens and discrimination is prohibited.[^cite_note-\:1-247] The Poverty Reduction Strategy Programme and the Growth and Poverty Reduction Strategy Programme seek to improve the well-being of the citizens, and changes to governance and gender in the country.[^cite_note-\:1-247] The Ministry for Advancement of Women, Children and the Family was created specifically for women and children so that their basics rights and needs get met under the law.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Although there exists legislation and policy for gender equality, the institutionalization of the National Gender Policy of Mali is necessary to support the importance of women's rights.[^cite_note-\:1-247] Strengthening and the support of girls' and women's access to education and training is recommended to improve gender equality.[^cite_note-\:1-247]

## Culture



[Konoguel Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konoguel_Mosque?action=edit\&redlink=1) tower

The varied everyday [culture of Malians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Mali) reflects the country's ethnic and geographic diversity.[^cite_note-p13-249] Most Malians wear flowing, colorful robes called [boubous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boubou_\(clothing\)) that are typical of West Africa. Malians frequently participate in traditional festivals, dances, and ceremonies.[^cite_note-p13-249]

### Music



Mali Dogon dance

[Malian musical traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Mali) are derived from the [griots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griot), who are known as "Keepers of Memories".[^cite_note-250] Malian music is diverse and has several different genres. Some influences are [kora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kora_\(instrument\)) virtuoso musician [Toumani Diabaté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toumani_Diabat%C3%A9), the [ngoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngoni_\(instrument\)) with [Bassekou Kouyate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassekou_Kouyate) the virtuoso of the electric [jeli ngoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngoni_\(instrument\)), the late roots and blues guitarist [Ali Farka Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Farka_Tour%C3%A9), the [Tuareg](103054.md) band [Tinariwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinariwen), [Khaira Arby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaira_Arby), and several [Afro-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro_pop_music) artists such as [Salif Keita](734004.md), the duo [Amadou et Mariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadou_et_Mariam), [Oumou Sangare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oumou_Sangare), [Fatoumata Diawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatoumata_Diawara), [Rokia Traore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokia_Traore), and [Habib Koité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habib_Koit%C3%A9). Dance also plays a large role;[^cite_note-embassy-251] parties are common events among friends, and traditional mask dances are performed at ceremonial events.[^cite_note-embassy-251]

### Literature

Though Mali's literature is less famous than its music,[^cite_note-p29-252] Mali has always been one of Africa's liveliest intellectual centers.[^cite_note-p128-253] Literary tradition is passed mainly by word of mouth, with *jalis* reciting or singing histories and stories known by heart.[^cite_note-p128-253][^cite_note-p28-254] [Amadou Hampâté Bâ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadou_Hamp%C3%A2t%C3%A9_B%C3%A2), Mali's best-known historian, spent much of his life writing these oral traditions down for the world to remember.[^cite_note-p28-254]

The best-known novel by a Malian writer is [Yambo Ouologuem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yambo_Ouologuem)'s *Le devoir de violence*, which won the 1968 [Prix Renaudot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Renaudot) but whose legacy was marred by accusations of plagiarism.[^cite_note-p128-253][^cite_note-p28-254] Other well-known writers include Baba Traoré, Modibo Sounkalo Keita, [Massa Makan Diabaté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massa_Makan_Diabat%C3%A9), [Moussa Konaté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Konat%C3%A9_\(writer\)), and [Fily Dabo Sissoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fily_Dabo_Sissoko).[^cite_note-p128-253][^cite_note-p28-254]

### Sport



Malian children playing football in a [Dogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogon_people) village

The [most popular sport in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Mali) is association football,[^cite_note-p151-255][^cite_note-p55-256] which became more prominent after Mali hosted the [2002 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_African_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-p151-255][^cite_note-p320-257] Most towns and cities have regular games;[^cite_note-p320-257] the most popular teams nationally are [Djoliba AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djoliba_AC), [Stade Malien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Malien), and [Real Bamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Bamako), all based in the capital.[^cite_note-p55-256] Informal games are often played by youths using a bundle of rags as a ball.[^cite_note-p55-256]

Basketball is another major sport;[^cite_note-p55-256][^cite_note-258] the [Mali women's national basketball team](13561581.md), led by [Hamchetou Maiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamchetou_Maiga), competed at the 2008 [Beijing Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-259] [Traditional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutte_Traditionnelle) (*la lutte*) is also somewhat common, though popularity has declined in recent years.[^cite_note-p320-257] The game [wari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oware), a [mancala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mancala) variant, is a common pastime.[^cite_note-p55-256] Mali featured a men's national team in [beach volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_volleyball) that competed at the [2018–2020 CAVB Beach Volleyball Continental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932020_CAVB_Beach_Volleyball_Continental_Cup).[^cite_note-260]

### Cuisine



Malian tea

Rice and [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet) are the staples of [Malian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malian_cuisine), which is heavily based on cereal grains.[^cite_note-p30-261][^cite_note-p146-262] Grains are generally prepared with sauces made from edible leaves, such as [spinach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinach) or [baobab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adansonia_digitata), with tomato peanut sauce, and may be accompanied by pieces of grilled meat (typically chicken, [mutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutton), beef, or goat).[^cite_note-p30-261][^cite_note-p146-262] Malian cuisine varies regionally.[^cite_note-p30-261][^cite_note-p146-262] Other popular dishes include [fufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fufu), [jollof rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jollof_rice), and [maafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maafe).
