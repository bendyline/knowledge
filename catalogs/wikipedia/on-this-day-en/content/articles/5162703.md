**Tetramethylammonium hydroxide** (**TMAH** or **TMAOH**) is a [quaternary ammonium salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary_ammonium_salt) with molecular formula N(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>OH<sup>−</sup>, abbreviated NMe<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>OH<sup>−</sup>, where Me stands for the [methyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methyl) group, –CH<sub>3</sub>. It is commonly encountered in the form of concentrated solutions in water or [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol). TMAH in solid state and its aqueous solutions are all colorless, but may be yellowish if impure. Although TMAH has virtually no odor when pure, samples often have a strong fishy smell due to presence of [trimethylamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trimethylamine), which is a common impurity. TMAH has several diverse industrial and research applications.

TMAH is most commonly encountered as an aqueous solution, in concentrations of \~2–25%, and less frequently as solutions in [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol). These solutions are identified by CAS number [75-59-2](https://commonchemistry.cas.org/detail?cas_rn=75-59-2). Several hydrates such as N(CH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>4</sub>OH·xH<sub>2</sub>O. have been crystallized.[^cite_note-3] These salts contain well separated Me<sub>4</sub>N<sup>+</sup> cations and [hydroxide anions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxide). The hydroxide groups are linked by hydrogen bonds to the [water of crystallization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_of_crystallization). Anhydrous TMAH has not been isolated.

### Properties

TMAH is a very [strong base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_base).[^cite_note-10]

## Uses

TMAH is used industrially for [anisotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropic) [etching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etching_\(microfabrication\)) of [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon).[^cite_note-Thong-11] It is used as a basic solvent in the [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photographic_processing) of acidic [photoresists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoresist) in the [photolithography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photolithography) process, and is highly effective in stripping photoresists. TMAH has some [phase transfer catalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transfer_catalyst) properties.&#x20;

TMAH is used as a [surfactant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfactant) in the synthesis of [ferrofluids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrofluid) and to inhibit [nanoparticle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoparticle) aggregation.

TMAH is one of the most common reagents used in [thermochemolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermochemolysis?action=edit\&redlink=1), an analytical technique involving both [pyrolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrolysis) and chemical [derivatization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivatization) of [analytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analyte).[^cite_note-12]

### Wet anisotropic etching

TMAH belongs to the family of [quaternary ammonium hydroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary_ammonium_salt) (QAH) solutions and is commonly used to [anisotropically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropic) [etch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_etching) [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon). TMAH is preferred over [sodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_hydroxide) or potassium hydroxide in applications that are sensitive to metal ion contamination.[^cite_note-13] Typical etching temperatures are between 70 and 90 °C and typical concentrations are 5–25 wt.% TMAH in water. For etching the [(100) face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_index) of silicon, the etching rates generally increase with temperature and decrease with TMAH concentration. Surface roughness of etched (100) silicon decreases with increasing TMAH concentration, and smooth surfaces can be obtained with 20% TMAH solutions. Etch rates are typically in the 0.1–1 [micrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrometre) per minute range.

Common masking materials for long etches in TMAH include [silicon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_dioxide) ([LPCVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LPCVD) and thermal) and [silicon nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride). Silicon nitride has a negligible etch rate in TMAH. The etch rate for silicon dioxide in TMAH varies with the quality of the film, but is generally on the order of 0.1 nm/minute.[^cite_note-Thong-11]

## Toxicology

Main article: [Tetramethylammonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetramethylammonium)

The tetramethylammonium cation[^cite_note-14] affects nerves and muscles, causing difficulties in breathing, muscular paralysis and possibly death.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-Yang2022-16] It is structurally related to [acetylcholine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetylcholine), an important [neurotransmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotransmitter) at both the [neuromuscular junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromuscular_junction) and [autonomic ganglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomic_ganglia). When it acts as an [agonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agonist), this structural similarity is reflected in its mechanism of toxicity – it binds to and activates the [nicotinic acetylcholine receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicotinic_acetylcholine_receptor), although they may become desensitized in continued presence of the agonist. The action of tetramethylammonium is most pronounced in autonomic ganglia, so it is traditionally classified as a ganglion-stimulant drug.[^cite_note-17]

The ganglionic effects may have contributed to deaths following accidental industrial exposure. "[Chemical burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_burn)" induced by this strong base are also severe. There is evidence that poisoning and even death can occur through skin-contact with low concentration solutions of TMAH.[^cite_note-Yang2022-16][^cite_note-18]
