**Narendra Modi Stadium** is an international [cricket](25675557.md) stadium in [Motera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motera), [Ahmedabad](206337.md), Gujarat, India. With a total capacity of 132,000,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-BCCI23cap-3][^cite_note-battle-1][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-renames-2] it is the [world's largest cricket stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cricket_grounds_by_capacity) and the [largest overall stadium in the world by capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stadiums_by_capacity).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] It is owned by the [Gujarat Cricket Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Cricket_Association), and hosts both domestic and international cricket matches.[^cite_note-Seating-12][^cite_note-\:0-20][^cite_note-21] The stadium has hosted several high-profile matches, such as the [2023 Cricket World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup_final) and the [2026 Men's T20 World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Men's_T20_World_Cup_final).[^cite_note-\:1-22] The stadium will also be used for the [2030 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2030_Commonwealth_Games). Situated near the [Sabarmati River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_River), the stadium is named after the [prime minister of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_India), [Narendra Modi](444222.md).&#x20;

It replaced [Sardar Patel Gujarat Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Patel_Gujarat_Stadium), which hosted domestic and international cricket in the city until its demolition in 2015, including the [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Cricket_World_Cup), [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Cricket_World_Cup) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup) [Cricket World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup).[^cite_note-\:3-23][^cite_note-\:8-24][^cite_note-25] In 2014, it was decided that a new stadium should be built on the same plot.[^cite_note-oza-26] The new stadium, originally named **Motera Stadium**, was designed by [Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_\(company\)) and built by [Larsen and Toubro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larsen_and_Toubro). It took five years to build, at an estimated cost of [₹](490783.md)800 [crore](121420.md) (US\$83 million). After completion, the new arena replaced the [Melbourne Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Ground) as the world's largest cricket stadium. The stadium has four dressing rooms, 11 centre pitches, and two practice grounds. The practice grounds can also serve as venues for domestic matches.[^cite_note-\:3-23][^cite_note-S-8][^cite_note-\:7-27][^cite_note-\:11-28]

On 24 February 2020, the new stadium first opened to the public by hosting the [Namaste Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namaste_Trump) event, which saw an approximate attendance of 125,000 people.[^cite_note-NamasteTrumpsources-29] One year later, on 24 February 2021, the stadium was officially inaugurated by Indian president [Ram Nath Kovind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Nath_Kovind) and renamed "Narendra Modi Stadium" in honour of Modi who had previously served as the Chief Minister of Gujarat (2001–2014) and President of the Gujarat Cricket Association (2009–2014).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-\:6-31][^cite_note-32] This ceremony took place just hours before the venue hosted its first-ever international cricket match—a [day-night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day/night_cricket) pink-ball Test between India and England.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-\:7-27][^cite_note-34]

On 29 September 2022, the opening ceremony of the [2022 National Games of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Games_of_India) was held in the stadium.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

## Planning and construction

### Conception

The idea to build a new stadium was proposed by [Narendra Modi](444222.md) himself who was the president of the [Gujarat Cricket Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Cricket_Association) and the Chief Minister of Gujarat at the same time.[^cite_note-oza-26][^cite_note-\:11-28] Shortly before Modi moved to Delhi after becoming the [prime minister of India](24452.md), there were discussions about adding minor upgrades to the stadium and further developing areas of the structure at the pavilion end. Modi asked officials to build a new, larger stadium instead of pursuing minor renovation work.[^cite_note-Gujarat_soon_to_have_the_largest_stadium2-37]



US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and Indian prime minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) at the Narendra Modi Stadium, 24 February 2020.

In October 2015, the stadium was demolished for reconstruction, though some media referred to it as a renovation. The total cost of reconstruction was estimated to be [₹](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rupee)700 [crores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_numbering_system) (₹7 billion (US\$73 million)).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] However, the final cost was reported at ₹800 crore (US\$83 million). The redevelopment, originally planned to be completed in 2019, finished in February 2020.

### Bids

After starting demolition work at the end of 2015, the GCA issued a [request for tender](36571608.md) on 1 January 2016 in [The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India) and [The Indian Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Indian_Express). Nine bidders showed interest and purchased the tender documents, out of which three submitted technical and financial bids on time; they were the [Shapoorji Pallonji Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapoorji_Pallonji_Group), Nagarjuna Construction Company, and [Larsen & Toubro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larsen_&_Toubro) (L\&T). A Tender Commercial Committee (TCC) of nine experts was formed to evaluate tenders. Additionally, STUP Consultants, a civil [engineering consultancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_consulting) firm based in [Mumbai](19189.md), was appointed as the project management consultant to evaluate proposals and technical details of each bid working with the TCC.[^cite_note-Motera_Stadium_-_GCA2-40]

Each of the three bidders presented their designs, models, and technical details of their concepts and designs. Due to the sheer size and complexity of the project, the bidders were evaluated on many parameters. These included efficiency, resources, project length, and ease of implementation, among others. The bidders were ranked and weighted based on the parameters.

### Building work

L\&T took over the construction work of the stadium in December 2016.[^cite_note-41] On 16 January 2017, the Gujarat Cricket Association oversaw the project, which formally began on the same day. The stadium was planned to be finished in 2 years, and the reconstruction project was estimated to cost around ₹700 crore (\$93 million).[^cite_note-42] Final completion of the stadium was finished in February 2020, and it hosted an [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_cricket_team)-[India](407754.md) day-night test match in 2021.[^cite_note-43]

Mumbai-based commercial kitchen consultant "Span Asia" was hired to collaborate with [Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_\(company\)) and L\&T on food and beverage-related areas such as concession counters, main stadium kitchens, player kitchens, VIP/VVIP boxes, corporate boxes, press & media boxes, pantries, the GCA Club, and related areas.

## Service

In 2020, the event "[Namaste Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namaste_Trump)" was organized at the stadium for the visit of US president [Donald J. Trump](4848272.md). 125,000 attendees were recorded.[^cite_note-NamasteTrumpsources-29] Before it became clear that the complex would continue to use the name "Sardar Patel Sports Enclave" and that only the stadium would receive a new name, Congress first criticized the decision.[^cite_note-44]

Prime Minister Modi inaugurated the [2022 National Games of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Games_of_India) in the arena.

The stadium later became the home ground of IPL team [Gujarat Titans](70008752.md) and hosted the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Indian_Premier_League_final), [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Indian_Premier_League_final), and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indian_Premier_League_final) IPL Finals.[^cite_note-\:92-45][^cite_note-\:0-20][^cite_note-\:102-46]

The stadium also hosted five matches of the [2023 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup), including the opening and [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup_final) games, as well as high-profile matches pitting India against Pakistan and Australia against England.[^cite_note-\:1-22]

British [rock](25423.md) band [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay) played two shows at the stadium on 25 and 26 January 2025, as part of their [Music of the Spheres World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_Spheres_World_Tour).[^cite_note-47]

## Design and facilities



The Stadium during the 2023 Cricket World Cup final between India and Australia

The stadium is built on 63 acres of land and has four entry points, with a metro line at one of the entry points. It has 76 corporate boxes that can hold 25 persons each,[^cite_note-business-standard.com2-48] a 55-room clubhouse, an Olympic size swimming pool,[^cite_note-business-standard.com2-48][^cite_note-49] and four dressing rooms. A food court and hospitality area are available in each spectator stand. The stadium also includes an indoor cricket academy and practice pitches, with dormitories for 40 athletes.[^cite_note-\:11-28]

The stands generally follow a two-tier structure; however, there is a three-tier main pavilion.[^cite_note-S-8] The two-tier structure is supported from beneath by numerous Y-shaped columns, which eliminate the need for pillars and thus give spectators an unobstructed view of the entire field from all parts of the stadium. An [LED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-emitting_diode) light system lines the stadium roof's inside edge. This eliminates the need for [floodlights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodlight) and helps reduce shadows on the pitch. The LED lights are installed on an antibacterial, fireproof canopy with [PTFE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytetrafluoroethylene) membrane that covers 30 metres (98 ft) out of 55 metres (180 ft) width of the seating area. The roof was installed by [Walter P. Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_P_Moore), and was specifically designed to be lightweight and separate from the seating bowls in order to improve earthquake resistance.[^cite_note-50]

The ground has 11 center pitches. The playing field has sensors to detect the need for water for the grass, which can automatically activate 67 fully automated underground pop-up sprinklers. The ground surface is made of [Bermuda grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda-grass). The stadium has an extensive drainage system.[^cite_note-S-8]

The stadium premises have many facilities, including an indoor [cricket](25675557.md) practice area, [badminton](3956.md), [tennis](29773.md), and [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\)) courts, a [table tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_tennis) area, a 3D projector theatre, three practice grounds, and 50 rooms.[^cite_note-World's_largest_stadium_to_host_POTUS2-51][^cite_note-S-8] The parking lot can accommodate 3,000 cars and 10,000 two-wheelers. A ramp leading to the stadium is designed to facilitate the movement of around 60,000 people simultaneously. The arena has been designed in such a way that the upper stands can remain empty (such as during smaller events) while maintaining a sufficient crowd atmosphere. However, a game between [Royal Challengers Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Challengers_Bangalore) and [Rajasthan Royals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan_Royals) in the [IPL 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indian_Premier_League) saw a near houseful crowd of over 1 [lakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakh).[^cite_note-52]

There was a plan that the stadium would be connected to the nearest metro station by a skywalk bridge in order to decrease road congestion. Construction was planned to be completed after September 2020. As of November 2023, the skywalk is yet to be constructed.[^cite_note-Motera_to_get_skywalk_by_20202-53]

### 75 years of Friendship through Cricket

Main article: [75 Years of Friendship through Cricket Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75_Years_of_Friendship_through_Cricket_Event)

See also: [Australia–India relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93India_relations)

On 9 March 2023, Narendra Modi Stadium hosted the [75 Years of Friendship through Cricket Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75_Years_of_Friendship_through_Cricket_Event), which was attended by Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) and visiting Australian prime minister [Anthony Albanese](556745.md). The event was held in conjunction with the fourth Test match of the [Border-Gavaskar Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border%E2%80%93Gavaskar_Trophy) between India and Australia.[^cite_note-55]

The event began with the two prime ministers singing the [national anthems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthems) of their respective countries and shaking hands with the players of both teams. They then visited a gallery showcasing glimpses of 75 years of cricket friendship between the two countries.[^cite_note-56] After the gallery visit, the two prime ministers took a lap of honor around the stadium in a specially designed golf cart. They were greeted by loud cheers from the crowd, who waved [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Flag) and [Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_flag) flags. The event concluded with the two prime ministers [tossing the coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toss_\(cricket\)) to start the [Test match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_match_\(cricket\)).[^cite_note-57]

The [75 Years of Friendship through Cricket Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75_Years_of_Friendship_through_Cricket_Event) was a celebration of the strong ties between India and Australia, which have been forged over many years through the shared love of cricket. The event was also an opportunity to promote sports diplomacy and to encourage people from both countries to come together and celebrate their friendship.[^cite_note-58]

## Domestic cricket

The stadium is the home ground of the [Gujarat cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_cricket_team), the [Gujarat women's cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_women's_cricket_team), and the IPL franchise [Gujarat Titans](70008752.md).[^cite_note-85] Other than the main ground inside the stadium, it also has two grounds known as "Narendra Modi Stadium A Ground" and "Narendra Modi Stadium B Ground", which are located near the stadium on the eastern side. They host domestic cricket matches for tournaments such as the [Ranji Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranji_Trophy).[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] The stadium hosted the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Indian_Premier_League_final) and [2023 IPL finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Indian_Premier_League_final). In 2022, the home team Gujarat Titans prevailed; in 2023, the [Chennai Super Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_Super_Kings) won their fifth title.[^cite_note-\:92-45][^cite_note-89] The [2022 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Vijay_Hazare_Trophy) for the [Vijay Hazare Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Hazare_Trophy) was held inside the arena. [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_cricket_team) scored 248/9 in first innings; [Saurashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurashtra_cricket_team) chased down it in 46.3 overs. Maharashtra's [Ruturaj Gaikwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruturaj_Gaikwad) and Saurashtra's [Sheldon Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Jackson_\(cricketer\)) scored hundreds.[^cite_note-90]

## Notable achievements

- On 24 February 2021, the arena hosted its first [Day/night Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day-night_Test), with India facing off against England.

  [^cite_note-\:43-91]

- In 2022, the arena hosted the [IPL final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Indian_Premier_League_final) between the [Gujarat Titans](70008752.md) and the [Rajasthan Royals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan_Royals). The Titans won the IPL in their inaugural season.

  [^cite_note-92]

  &#x20;This event was attended by 104,859 people, the highest attendance in the history of the stadium and cricket in general.

  [^cite_note-\:8-24]

- In the 2023 [IPL final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Indian_Premier_League) between the Gujarat Titans and [Chennai Super Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_Super_Kings) (CSK), [MS Dhoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Dhoni) (playing in his 250th match) captained CSK to their fifth IPL victory, tying the [Mumbai Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Indians) for the record of most IPL trophies.

  [^cite_note-\:92-45]

- In the [2026 Men's T20 World Cup](69284234.md), [India](407754.md) defeated New Zealand in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Men's_T20_World_Cup_final) held here, thus becoming the only team to win back-to-back T20 World Cups (2024 and 2026), and a record three titles in the T20 World Cup.

- In the [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indian_Premier_League_final) and [2026](80074642.md) IPL finals, [Royal Challengers Bengaluru](15861688.md) defeated [Punjab Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Kings) and Gujarat Titans respectively to win their first two titles. RCB thus retained the title successfully.
