**Spaniards**,[^cite_note-a-1] or **Spanish people**, are an [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity) and nation indigenous to [Spain](26667.md). [Genetically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) and [ethnolinguistically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnolinguistic_group), Spaniards belong to the broader [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe) and [Western European](33800.md) populations, exhibiting a high degree of continuity with other [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages)-derived ethnic groups in the region. Spain is also home to a diverse array of [national and regional identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_and_regional_identity_in_Spain), shaped by its complex [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Spain). These include various [languages and dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Spain), many of which are direct descendants of [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), the language imposed during [Roman rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania). Among them, [Castilian](26825.md) is the most widely spoken and the only official language across the entire country.

Commonly spoken regional languages include, most notably, the sole surviving indigenous language of [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberia), [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language), as well as other Latin-descended [Romance languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages) like Spanish itself, [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language) and [Galician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_language). Many populations outside Spain have ancestors who [emigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_diaspora) from Spain and share elements of Hispanic cultures. The most notable of these comprise [Hispanic America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America).

The [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) conquered Iberia during the 2nd and 1st centuries BC. [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania), the name given to Iberia by the Romans as a province of the Empire, underwent a process of linguistic and cultural [Romanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_history_of_the_Roman_military#Conquest_of_the_Iberian_peninsula_\(218-19_BC\)), and as such, the majority of local languages in Spain today, with the exception of [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language), evolved out of [Vulgar Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulgar_Latin) which was introduced by the [ancient Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Romans). At the [end of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire), the Germanic tribal confederations migrated from Central Europe, invaded the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) and established relatively independent realms in its western provinces, including the [Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebi), [Alans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alans), and [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals). Eventually, the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths) would forcibly integrate all remaining independent territories in the peninsula, including the [Byzantine province of Spania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spania), into the [Visigothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom), which more or less unified politically, ecclesiastically, and legally all the former Roman provinces or successor kingdoms of what was then documented as Hispania.

In the early eighth century, the Visigothic Kingdom was conquered by the [Umayyad Islamic Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) that arrived to the peninsula in the year 711. The [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) rule in the Iberian Peninsula, termed [al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus), soon became autonomous from Baghdad. The handful of small Christian pockets in the north left out of Muslim rule, along the presence of the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire) near the Pyrenean range, would eventually lead to the emergence of the Christian kingdoms of [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Le%C3%B3n), [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile), [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aragon), [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal), and [Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Navarre). Along seven centuries, an intermittent southwards expansion of the latter kingdoms (known in historiography as the *[Reconquista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista)*) took place, culminating with the Christian seizure of the last Muslim polity (the [Nasrid Kingdom of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada)) in 1492, the same year [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) arrived in the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World). During the centuries after the [Reconquista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista), the Christian kings of Spain persecuted and expelled ethnic and religious minorities such as [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Spain) and [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Spain) through the [Spanish Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Inquisition).[^cite_note-38]

A process of political conglomeration among the Christian kingdoms also ensued, and the late 15th-century saw the dynastic union of Castile and Aragon under the [Catholic Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Monarchs), generally considered the point of emergence of Spain as a unified country. The [Conquest of Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Navarre) occurred in 1512. There was also a period called [Iberian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Union), the [dynastic union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynastic_union) of the [Kingdom of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal) and the [Spanish Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Crown); during which, both countries were ruled by the [Spanish Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Habsburg) kings between 1580 and 1640.

In the early modern period, Spain had [one of the largest empires in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire), which was also one of the first global empires, leaving a large cultural and linguistic legacy that includes over 570 million [Hispanophones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanophone),[^cite_note-39] making Spanish the world's [second-most spoken native language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_languages_by_number_of_native_speakers), after [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese). During the [Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Golden_Age) there were also many advancements in the arts, with the rise of renowned painters such as [Diego Velázquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez). The most famous Spanish literary work, *[Don Quixote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Quixote)*, was also published during the Golden Age of the Spanish Empire.

The population of Spain has become more diverse due to immigration of the late 20th and early 21st centuries. From 2000 to 2010, Spain had among the highest per capita immigration rates in the world and the second-highest absolute net migration in the world (after the [United States](3434750.md)).[^cite_note-epp.eurostat.ec.europa.eu-40] The diverse regional and cultural populations mainly include the [Castilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilians), [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragonese_people), [Catalans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_people), [Andalusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusian_people), [Valencians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_people), [Balearics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_people), [Canarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_people), [Basques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_people), and [Galicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_people) (among others).

## History

### Early populations



[Lady of Elche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_of_Elche), a piece of [Iberian sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_sculpture) from the 4th century BC



A young [Hispano-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania) nobleman from the 1st century BC



Marble bust of Roman Emperor [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan), born in Roman [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania) (in [Italica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italica) near modern-day [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville))

The earliest modern humans inhabiting the region of Spain are believed to have been [Paleolithic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_modern_humans), who may have arrived in the Iberian Peninsula as early as 35,000–40,000 years ago. The [Iberians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberians) are believed to have arrived or emerged in the region as a culture between the [4th millennium BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_millennium_BC) and the 3rd millennium BC, settling initially along the Mediterranean coast.[^cite_note-41]

Then [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) settled in Spain during the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age_Europe). Some of those tribes in north-central Spain, who had cultural contact with the Iberians, are called [Celtiberians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtiberians). In addition, a group known as the [Tartessians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartessos) and later [Turdetanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turdetani) inhabited southwestern Spain. They are believed to have developed a separate culture influenced by [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia). The seafaring [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians),[^cite_note-42] [Greeks](42056.md), and [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginian_Republic) successively settled trading colonies along the Mediterranean coast over a period of several centuries. Interaction took place with Indigenous peoples. The [Second Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Punic_War) between the Carthaginians and [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) was fought mainly in what is now Spain and Portugal.[^cite_note-43]

The [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) conquered Iberia during the 2nd and 1st centuries BC, and established a series of [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin)-speaking provinces in the region. As a result of [Roman colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_history_of_the_Roman_military#Conquest_of_the_Iberian_peninsula_\(218-19_BC\)), the majority of local languages, with the exception of [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language), stem from the [Vulgar Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulgar_Latin) that was spoken in [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania) (Roman Iberia). A new group of [Romance languages of the Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Iberia) including [Spanish](26825.md), which eventually became the main language in Spain evolved from Roman expansion. Hispania emerged as an important part of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and produced notable historical figures such as [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan), [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian), [Seneca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seneca_the_Younger), [Martial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial), [Theodosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I), and [Quintilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintilian).

The [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals) and [Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebi), with [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples) [Alans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alans) under King [Respendial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respendial), arrived in the peninsula in 409 AD. Part of the Vandals with the remaining Alans, now under [Geiseric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geiseric), removed to North Africa after a few conflicts with another Germanic tribe, the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths#Visigothic_Kingdom). The latter were established in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) and supported Roman campaigns against the Vandals and Alans in 415–19 AD.

The Visigoths became the dominant power in Iberia and reigned for three centuries. They were highly [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_\(cultural\)) in the eastern Empire and already Christians, so they became fully integrated into the late Iberian-Roman culture.

The [Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebi) were another Germanic tribe in the west of the peninsula; some sources said that they became established as *federates* of the Roman Empire in the old Northwestern Roman province of [Gallaecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallaecia) (roughly, present-day [northern Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region,_Portugal) and [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\))). But they were largely independent and raided neighboring provinces to expand their political control over ever-larger portions of the southwest after the Vandals and Alans left. They created a totally independent [Suebic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebic_Kingdom_of_Galicia). In 447 AD they converted to Roman Catholicism under King [Rechila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rechila).

After being checked and reduced in 456 AD by the Visigoths, the Suebic Kingdom survived to 585 AD. It was decimated as an independent political unit by the Visigoths, after having been involved in the internal affairs of their kingdom.

### Middle Ages

After two centuries of domination by the [Visigothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom), the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) was [invaded by a Muslim force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_conquest_of_Hispania) under [Tariq Bin Ziyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_ibn_Ziyad) in 711. This army consisted mainly of ethnic [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) from the Ghomara tribe, who were reinforced by [Arabs](2185.md) from [Syria](7515849.md) once the conquest was complete. Only a remote mountainous area in the far north retained independence, eventually developing as the Christian [Kingdom of Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Asturias).

Muslim Iberia became part of the Umayyad Caliphate and would be known as [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus). The Berbers of Al Andalus revolted as early as 740 AD, halting Arab expansion across the [Pyrenee Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrenee_Mountains) into France. Upon the collapse of the [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad) in [Damascus](8914.md), Spain was seized by [Yusuf al Fihri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_ibn_Abd_al-Rahman_al-Fihri). The exiled Umayyad Prince [Abd al-Rahman I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Rahman_I) next seized power, establishing himself as Emir of [Cordoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain). [Abd al Rahman III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al_Rahman_III?action=edit\&redlink=1), his grandson, proclaimed a [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate) in 929, marking the beginning of the [Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age) of [Al Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus). This policy was the effective power of Iberia and the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb); it competed with the [Shiite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiite) rulers of [Tunis](57659.md) and frequently raided the small Christian kingdoms in the North.

The [Caliphate of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba) effectively collapsed during a ruinous civil war between 1009 and 1013; it was not finally abolished until 1031, when Al-Andalus broke into a number of mostly independent mini-states and principalities called *[taifas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifas).* These were generally too weak to defend themselves against repeated raids and demands for tribute from the Christian states to the north and west, which were known to the Muslims as "the Galician nations". These had expanded from their initial strongholds in Galicia, Asturias, Cantabria, the Basque country, and the Carolingian Marca Hispanica to become the Kingdoms of Navarre, León, Portugal, Castile and Aragon, and the County of Barcelona. Eventually they began to conquer territory, and the Taifa kings asked for help from the Almoravids, Muslim Berber rulers of the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb). But the [Almoravids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravids) went on to conquer and annex all the Taifa kingdoms.

In 1086 the Almoravid ruler of Morocco, [Yusuf ibn Tashfin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_ibn_Tashfin), was invited by the Muslim princes in Iberia to defend them against [Alfonso VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_VI), King of Castile and León. In that year, Tashfin crossed the straits to Algeciras and inflicted defeat on the Christian army at the [Battle of Sagrajas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sagrajas). By 1094, Yusuf ibn Tashfin had removed all Muslim princes in Iberia and had annexed their states, except for the one at Zaragoza. He also regained [Valencia](63861.md) from the Christians. About this time a massive process of conversion to [Islam](6037917.md) took place, and Muslims comprised the majority of the population in Spain by the end of the 11th century.

The Almoravids were succeeded by the [Almohads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohads), another [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) dynasty, after the victory of Abu Yusuf Ya'qub al-Mansur over the Castilian Alfonso VIII at the [Battle of Alarcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Alarcos) in 1195. In 1212 a coalition of Christian kings under the leadership of the Castilian Alfonso VIII defeated the Almohads at the Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa. But the Almohads continued to rule Al-Andalus for another decade, though with much reduced power and prestige. The civil wars following the death of Abu Ya'qub Yusuf II rapidly led to the re-establishment of taifas. The taifas, newly independent but weakened, were quickly conquered by the kingdoms of Portugal, Castile, and Aragon. After the fall of Murcia (1243) and the Algarve (1249), only the [Emirate of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada) survived as a Muslim state, tributary of Castile until 1492.



Iberian Kingdoms in 1400

In 1469 the marriage of Ferdinand of Aragon and Isabella of Castile signaled a joining of forces to attack and conquer the Emirate of Granada. The King and Queen convinced the [Pope](23056.md) to declare their war a [crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades). The Christians were successful and finally, in January 1492, after a long siege, the Moorish sultan [Muhammad XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_XI_of_Granada) surrendered the fortress palace, the renowned [Alhambra](30543.md).

Spain [conquered the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Canary_Islands) between 1402 and 1496. Their indigenous Berber population, the [Guanches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanches), were gradually absorbed by intermarrying with Spanish settlers.

Spanish conquest of the Iberian part of Navarre was begun by Ferdinand II of Aragon and completed by Charles V. The series of military campaigns extended from 1512 to 1524, while the war lasted until 1528 in the Navarre to the north of the Pyrenees. Between 1568 and 1571, Charles V armies fought and defeated a general insurrection of the Muslims of the mountains of Granada. Charles V then ordered the expulsion of up to 80,000 people from Granada and their dispersal throughout Spain.

The union of the Christian kingdoms of Castile and Aragon as well as the conquest of [Granada](38264.md), Navarre and the Canary Islands led to the formation of the Spanish state as known today. This allowed for the development of a Spanish identity based on the Spanish language and a local form of Catholicism. This gradually developed in a territory that remained culturally, linguistically and religiously very diverse.

A majority of Jews were forcibly [converted to Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Converso) during the 14th and 15th centuries and those remaining were expelled from Spain in 1492. The open practice of Islam by Spain's significant [Mudejar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudejar) population was similarly outlawed. Furthermore, between 1609 and 1614, hundreds of thousands of [Moriscos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morisco)— (Muslims who had been baptized Catholic) were [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Moriscos) by royal decree.[^cite_note-44] Although initial estimates of the number of Moriscos expelled such as those of Henri Lapeyre reach 300,000 Moriscos (or 4 percent of the total Spanish population), the extent and severity of the initial expulsions have been challenged by some modern historians. Nevertheless, the eastern region of Valencia, where ethnic tensions were highest, was particularly affected by the expulsion, suffering economic collapse and depopulation of much of its territory.

Spain's Islamic legacy has endured, and among many others, accounts for two of the eight [masterpieces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masterpieces) of [Islamic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture) from around the world: the [Alhambra of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra_of_Granada) and the [Cordoba Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordoba_Mosque);[^cite_note-45] the [Palmeral of Elche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmeral_of_Elche) [^cite_note-46] is listed as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) due to its uniqueness.[^cite_note-47]

Those who avoided expulsion or who managed to return to Spain merged into the dominant culture.[^cite_note-Susan_M_2008-48] The last mass prosecution against Moriscos for [crypto-Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto-Islam) practices took place in Granada in 1727, with most of those convicted receiving relatively light sentences. By the end of the 18th century, Indigenous Islam and Morisco identity were considered to have been extinguished in Spain.[^cite_note-49]

### Colonialism and emigration



Spanish and Portuguese empires in 1790

In the 16th century, following the military conquest of most of the new continent, perhaps 240,000 Spaniards entered American ports. They were joined by 450,000 in the next century.[^cite_note-Axtell_12–18-50] It is estimated that during the colonial period (1492–1832), a total of 1.86 million Spaniards settled in the Americas and a further 3.5 million immigrated during the post-colonial era (1850–1950); the estimate is 250,000 in the 16th century, and most during the 18th century as immigration was encouraged by the new Bourbon Dynasty. After the conquest of [Mexico](3966054.md) and [Peru](170691.md) these two regions became the principal destinations of Spanish colonial settlers in the 16th century.[^cite_note-Migration_to_Latin_America-51] In the period 1850–1950, 3.5 million Spanish left for [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Americas), particularly [Argentina](18951905.md), [Uruguay](31843.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Chile](5489.md), [Venezuela](32374.md), and [Cuba](5042481.md).[^cite_note-Diaspora-52] From 1840 to 1890, as many as 40,000 Canary Islanders emigrated to [Venezuela](32374.md).[^cite_note-53] 94,000 Spaniards chose to go to [Algeria](292341.md) in the last years of the 19th century, and 250,000 Spaniards lived in [Morocco](19291.md) at the beginning of the 20th century.[^cite_note-Diaspora-52]

By the end of the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), some 500,000 Spanish Republican [refugees](45547.md) had crossed the border into France.[^cite_note-54] From 1961 to 1974, at the height of the [guest worker](384142.md) in Western Europe, about 100,000 Spaniards emigrated each year.[^cite_note-Diaspora-52] Spain has formally apologized to expelled Jews, and, since 2015, the State offers the opportunity to reclaim Spanish citizenship. By 2019, over 132,000 [Sephardic Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jewish) descendants had reclaimed Spanish citizenship.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

The population of Spain has become more diverse due to immigration of the late 20th and early 21st centuries. From 2000 to 2010, Spain had among the highest per capita immigration rates in the world and the second-highest absolute net migration in the world (after the [United States](3434750.md)).[^cite_note-epp.eurostat.ec.europa.eu-40] Immigrants now make up about 10% of the population. But Spain's prolonged economic crisis between 2008 and 2015 reduced economic opportunities, and both immigration rates and the total number of foreigners in the country declined. By the end of this period, Spain was becoming a net emigrant country.

### Romani minority

Main article: [Romani people in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Spain)

Spain is home to one of the largest communities of [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) (commonly known by the English [exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exonym) "gypsies", Spanish: *gitanos*). The Spanish Roma, which belong to the Iberian Kale subgroup (*[calé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people#Romani_subgroups)*), are a formerly-nomadic community, which spread across Western Asia, North Africa, and Europe, first reaching Spain in the 15th century.

Data on ethnicity is not collected in Spain, although the Government's statistical agency CIS estimated in 2007 that the number of *Gitanos* present in Spain is probably around one million.[^cite_note-Diagnostico_Social_de_la_Comunidad_Gitana_en_Espana_-_CIS-74] Most Spanish Roma live in the autonomous community of Andalusia, where they have traditionally enjoyed a higher degree of integration than in the rest of the country. A number of Spanish Calé also live in Southern France, especially in the region of [Perpignan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan).

### Modern immigration

Main article: [Immigration to Spain](10499603.md)

Due to recent immigration, the population of Spain has become increasingly diverse. From 2000 to 2010, Spain had among the world's highest per capita immigration rates and the second highest absolute net migration in the world (after the [United States](3434750.md));[^cite_note-epp.eurostat.ec.europa.eu-40] immigrants now make up about 10% of the population. Since 2000, Spain has absorbed more than three million immigrants, with thousands arriving each year.[^cite_note-75] In 2008, Spain's immigrant population topped 4.5 million.[^cite_note-76] These immigrants came mainly from [Europe](9239.md), [Central America](6121.md), [South America](26769.md), [Asia](689.md), [North Africa](21714.md), and [West Africa](67393.md).[^cite_note-77]

Main article: [Languages of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Spain)

Languages spoken in Spain include [Spanish](26825.md) (*castellano* or *español*) (74%), [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language) (*català*, called *[valencià](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language)*, in the [Valencian Community](20514168.md)) (17%), [Galician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_language) (*galego*) (7%), and [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language) (*euskara*) (2%).[^cite_note-CIA-78] Other languages with a lower level of official recognition are [Asturian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturian_language) (*asturianu*), [Aranese Gascon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranese_language) (*aranés*), [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragonese_language) (*aragonés*), and [Leonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonese_language), each with their own various dialects. Spanish is the official state language, although the other languages are co-official in a number of autonomous communities.

Peninsular Spanish is typically classified in northern and southern dialects; among the southern ones [Andalusian Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusian_Spanish) is particularly important. The Canary Islands have a [distinct dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_Spanish) of Spanish which is close to [Caribbean Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Spanish). The Spanish language is a [Romance language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages) and is one of the aspects (including laws and general "ways of life") that causes Spaniards to be labelled a [Romanic people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance-speaking_Europe). Spanish has a significant [Arabic influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_influence_on_the_Spanish_language) in vocabulary; between the 8th and 12th centuries, Arabic was the dominant language in [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus)[^cite_note-79] and some 4,000 words are of Arabic origin, including nouns, verbs and adjectives.[^cite_note-80] It also has influences from other Romance languages such as [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), [Italian](14708.md), [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language), [Galician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_language) or [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language). Traditionally, the [Basque language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language) has been considered a key influence on Spanish, though nowadays this is questioned. Other changes are borrowings from English and other Germanic languages, although English influence is stronger in the Americas than in Spain.

The number of speakers of [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_given_to_the_Spanish_language) as a mother tongue is roughly 35.6 million, while the vast majority of other groups in Spain such as the [Galicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_people), [Catalans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_people), and [Basques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_people) also speak Spanish as a first or second language, which boosts the number of Spanish speakers to the overwhelming majority of Spain's population of 46 million.

Spanish was exported to the Americas due to over three centuries of Spanish colonial rule starting with the arrival of [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) to [Santo Domingo](57638.md) in 1492. Spanish is spoken natively by over 400 million people and spans across most countries of the Americas; from the Southwestern United States in North America down to [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego), the southernmost region of South America in [Chile](5489.md) and [Argentina](18951905.md). A variety of the language, known as [Judaeo-Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaeo-Spanish) or Ladino (or [Haketia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haketia) in Morocco), is still spoken by descendants of Sephardic Jews who fled Spain and Portugal following a [decree of expulsion of practising Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra_Decree) in 1492. Also, a Spanish [creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language) known as [Chabacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabacano_language), which developed by the mixing of Spanish and native [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) and [Cebuano languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) during Spain's rule of the country through [Mexico](3966054.md) from 1565 to 1898, is spoken in the [Philippines](23440.md) (by roughly 1 million people).[^cite_note-81]

## Religion

Main article: [Religion in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Spain)

[Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Spain) is by far the largest denomination present in [Spain](26667.md),[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] although its share of the population has been decreasing for decades. According to a study by the Spanish Centre for Sociological Research in 2013 about 71% of Spaniards self-identified as [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholics), 2% other faith, and about 25% identified as atheists or declared they had [no religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_Spain). Survey data for 2019 show Catholics down to 69%, 2.8% "other faith" and 27% atheist-agnostic-non-believers.[^cite_note-CIA-78]

[Spanish Chilean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Chilean) of Chile and [Spanish Uruguayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Uruguayan) of Uruguay could be included by percentage (each at above 40%) instead of numeral size.
