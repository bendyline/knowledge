The **Sioni Cathedral of the Dormition** ([Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language): სიონის ღვთისმშობლის მიძინების ტაძარი) is a [Georgian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Orthodox) [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral) located in [Tbilisi](42298.md), the capital of [Georgia](48768.md). Following a medieval Georgian tradition of naming churches after specific places in the [Holy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land), the Sioni Cathedral bears the name of [Mount Zion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Zion) at [Jerusalem](16043.md). It is commonly referred to as the "Tbilisi Sioni" to distinguish it from other churches in Georgia with the same name.

The Tbilisi Sioni Cathedral is situated in the historic Sionis Kucha (Sioni Street) in downtown Tbilisi, with its eastern façade fronting the right bank of the [Kura River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura_\(Caspian_Sea\)). The cathedral was initially built in the 6th and 7th centuries. Since then, it has been destroyed by foreign invaders and reconstructed several times. The current structure is based on a 13th-century version, with some modifications made between the 17th and 19th centuries. The Sioni Cathedral served as the main Georgian Orthodox Cathedral and the seat of the [Catholicos-Patriarch of All Georgia](435047.md) until the [Holy Trinity Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Cathedral,_Tbilisi) was consecrated in 2004.

## History



Sioni Cathedral as seen from the right side of the [Kura River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura_\(Caspian_Sea\)), 1870s

According to medieval Georgian annals, the construction of the original church on this site was initiated by King [Vakhtang Gorgasali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vakhtang_I_of_Iberia) in the 5th century. A hundred years later, [Guaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaram_I_of_Iberia), the prince of [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Iberia) ([Kartli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartli)), in c. 575 began building a new structure, which was completed by his successor [Adarnase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adarnase,_Eristavt_Eristavi) in circa 639. According to legend, both princes were buried in this church, but no trace of their graves has been found. This early church was destroyed by [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests), and was subsequently built *de novo*.

The cathedral was completely rebuilt by King [David the Builder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_IV_of_Georgia) in 1112. The basic elements of the existing structure date from this period. It was heavily damaged in 1226, when its [dome was ruined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Thousand_Martyrs_of_Tbilisi) on the order of [Jalal al-Din Mangburni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalal_al-Din_Mangburni). It was subsequently repaired, but damaged again by [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) in 1386 and repaired by King [Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Georgia). It was again damaged during the Persian invasions in 1522 and in the 17th century.



19th-century sectional drawing showing Grigory Gagarin's frescoes

In 1657, the [Metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) of Tbilisi, Elise Saginashvili (died 1670), substantially restored the [cupola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupola), vaults, added the southern chapel, paintings and decorations, which is written on the southern annex, but the structure was again devastated in 1668, this time by [earthquake](10106.md). The regent of Kartli, *[batonishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batonishvili)* (prince) [Vakhtang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vakhtang_VI), carried out restorations of the cupola and stone plates of the cathedral walls in 1710, as it is carved on the northern wall. Thus, the current tufa facing of the church comes from that period. However, the church was again damaged by the invasion of the Persians in 1795.

The cathedral's interior took on a different look between 1850 and 1860, when the Russian artist and general [Knyaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knyaz) [Grigory Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Gagarin) (1810–1893) composed an interesting series of the murals, though a number of medieval [frescoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresco) were lost in the process. A portion of the murals on the western wall were executed by the Georgian artist [Levan Tsutskiridze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levan_Tsutskiridze) in the 1980s.

The stone [iconostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconostasis) dates to the 1850s, and was also created according G. Gagarin's design. It replaced the wooden iconostasis burned during the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Empire) invasion in 1795. To the left of the [altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar) is the venerated [Grapevine cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapevine_cross) which, according to tradition, was forged by [Saint Nino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nino), a [Cappadocian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappadocia) woman who preached Christianity in the Caucasus in the early 4th century. King [Vakhtang III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vakhtang_III_of_Georgia) gave the [reliquary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliquary) itself in the early 14th century.

The Sioni Cathedral was where the Russian Imperial manifesto on the annexation of Georgia was first published. On April 12, 1802, the Russian commander-in-chief in Georgia, General [Karl von Knorring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_von_Knorring), assembled the Georgian nobles in the cathedral, which was then surrounded by Russian troops. The nobles were forced to take an oath to the Russian Imperial crown; any who disagreed were taken into custody.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

Sioni Cathedral remained functional through [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) times, and was partially renovated between 1980 and 1983.

## Architecture



Eastern facade of the church with three faceted apsids

The Sioni Cathedral is a typical example of medieval Georgian [church architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_architecture) of an inscribed [cross-in-square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-in-square) design with projecting polygonal [apses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apse) in the east façade. Main entrance is in the west side. The dome with the [tholobate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tholobate) is supported by the altar wall and two freely standing pillars (a more advanced design that appeared after the 11th century). Transition from the pillars to the tholobate is via pointed arches. In addition, the pillars are connected to the northern and southern walls by helm-shaped arches (probably 16th century). The yellow [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff) from which the cathedral was built comes from [Bolnisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolnisi), a town southwest of Tbilisi. The façades are simple, with few decorations, although there are [bas-relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas-relief) carvings of a cross and a chained lion on the western side and an angel and saints on the north. All sixteen windows have carved ornamental frames. Shallow carvings indicate late decoration.

North of the cathedral, within the courtyard, is a freestanding three-story bell tower dating from the 1425 reconstruction by King [Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Georgia). Largely destroyed by the Persian shah [Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_Mohammad_Khan_Qajar) in 1795, it was restored to its present condition in 1939.

Across the street stands another three-story bell tower; one of the earliest examples of Russian [Neoclassical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) in the region.[^cite_note-4] Complete in 1812, the bell tower was commissioned under [Pavel Tsitsianov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Tsitsianov) using money awarded in recognition of [his conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ganja_\(1804\)) of [Ganja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganja_Khanate) for the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).[^cite_note-5]



View of the ceiling

The murals, painted by [G. Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Gagarin), and iconostasis, also designed by him in 1850s-60s, differed from traditional Georgian tradition.
