**Najaf**[^cite_note-3] is the capital city of the eponymous [Najaf Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najaf_Governorate) in central [Iraq](7515928.md), about 160 km (99 mi) south of [Baghdad](4492.md). Its estimated population in 2024 was about 1.41 million people.[^cite_note-citypopulation.de-2] It is widely considered amongst the holiest cities of [Shia Islam](26961.md) and one of its spiritual capitals, as well as the center of Shia political power in Iraq.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

It is the [burial place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Ali_Shrine) of [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad)'s son in law and cousin, [‘Alī ibn Abī Tālib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E2%80%98Al%C4%AB_ibn_Ab%C4%AB_T%C4%81lib), and thus a major pilgrimage destination for Shia Muslims. The largest cemetery in the world ([Wadi-us-Salaam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi-us-Salaam)) and the oldest Shi'a Islamic seminary in the world ([Hawza of Najaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawza_Najaf)) are located in Najaf.

## Etymology

According to [Ibn Manzur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Manzur), the word, "najaf" (نجف), literally means a high and rectangular place around which water is accumulated, although the water does not go above its level.[^cite_note-9] [Al-Shaykh al-Saduq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shaykh_al-Saduq) appeals to a hadith from [Ja'far al-Sadiq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ja'far_al-Sadiq), claiming that "Najaf" comes from the phrase, "nay jaff" which means "the nay sea has dried".[^cite_note-10]

"Najaf" is usually accompanied by the adjective "al-Ashraf" (the dignified). According to the author of *al-Hawza al-'ilmiyya fi l-Najaf al-ashraf*, this is because 'Ali, one of the most dignified persons, is buried in the city.

Al-Ghari or al-Ghariyyan, Hadd al-'Adhra', al-Hiwar, al-Judi, Wadi l-Salam, al-Zahr, Zahr al-Kufa (behind Kufa), al-Rabwa, Baniqiya, and Mashhad are other names for this land.[^cite_note-11]

## History

The area of An-Najaf is located 30 km (19 mi) south of the ancient city of [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon), and 400 km (248 mi) north of the ancient city of [Ur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ur). The city itself was founded in 791 AD, by the [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid) [Caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph) [Harūn ar-Rashīd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harun_al-Rashid), as a shrine to ‘Alī bin Abī Ṭālib.[^cite_note-12]

### Prehistoric and ancient times



View of the city of Najaf, ca. 1914

Archaeological discoveries show the existence of a people in the present-day Najaf dating back to the 1st century BC. Najaf possesses one of the largest burial grounds in the vicinity for Christians. Discoveries in the following centuries point toward the city's multicultural and diverse religious history. Mohammed al-Mayali, director of Inspectorate Effects of the province of Najaf, states, "the excavation on the graves, which we have been working on for years, confirm that "Najaf" contains the largest Christian cemetery in Iraq, with a cemetery area of 1416 acres. We have found indications of Christianity on the graves through representations of crosses and stones with Christ-like engravings. There are also relics that date back to the [Sassanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid) period. Also discovered in the excavation was proof of a thriving glass industry. Pots were decorated with the cross, as well as Hebrew writings, indicating a community of religious coexistence."

[Wadi-us-Salaam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi-us-Salaam) in Najaf was once a holy cemetery for [Jews](25955086.md) and was then called Baniqia, which could be the first recorded name for the area.[^cite_note-13] [^cite_note-14]

The name Baniqia also was found in some texts which state that Abraham once visited and stayed in this village before continuing his journey from Mesopotamia to Arabia.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

In [Islam](6037917.md), the city is considered to have started with Ali who instructed that his burial place should remain a secret, as he had many enemies and he feared that his body might be subjected to some indignity. According to legend, the body of Ali was placed on a [camel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel) which was driven from [Kufah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kufa). The camel stopped a few miles west of the city where the body was secretly buried.[^cite_note-17] No tomb was raised and nobody knew of the burial place except for a few trusted people. It is narrated that more than a hundred years later the Abbasid Caliph, [Harun al-Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harun_al-Rashid), went [deer hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer_hunting) outside Kufah, and the [deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer) sought sanctuary at a place where the hounds would not pursue it. On inquiry as to why the place was a sanctuary, he was told that it was the burial place of ‘Ali. Harūn ar-Rashīd ordered a mausoleum to be built on the spot and in due course the town of Najaf grew around the mausoleum.[^cite_note-18]

### Medieval and modern history

In early 14th century, Sheikh [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) visited the burial site of [Ali ibn Abi Talib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_ibn_Abi_Talib) during his travels in Iraq after his pilgrimage to Mecca. During this period, Najaf was called Meshhed Ali. As Translated by [Samuel Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Lee_\(linguist\)), Ibn Battuta in his Arabic *[Rihla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihla)* relates:[^cite_note-19]

> We next proceeded to the city of Meshhed Ali where the grave of Ali ibn Abu Talib is thought to be. It is a handsome place and well peopled. There is no governor here, except a sort of tribune. The inhabitants consist chiefly of rich and brave merchants. About the gardens are plastered walls adorned with paintings, and within them are carpets, couches and lamps of gold and silver. Within the city is a large treasury kept by the tribune, which arises from the votive offerings arrived from different parts: for when anyone happens to be ill, or suffer under any infirmity, he will make a vow, and thence receive relief. The garden is also famous for its miracles; and hence it is believed that the grave of Ali is there. \
> &#x20;Of these miracles, the "night of revival" is one: for, on the 17th day of the month Rejeb, cripples come from different parts of Fars, Room, Khorasaan, Irak, and other places, assemble in companies from twenty to thirty in number. They are placed over the grave soon after sun-set. People then, some praying, some reciting the Quran, and others prostrating themselves, wait expecting their recovery and rising, when about the night, they all get up sound and right. This is a matter well known among them: I heard it from a creditable person, but was not present at one of those nights. I saw, however, several such afflicted persons, who had not yet received, but were looking forward for the advantages of this "night of revival".

In the 16th century, Najaf was conquered by the [Ottomans](22278.md), and became part of [Ottoman Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Iraq). The [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) of Iran maintained continuous interest to this Shia site. During the [Ottoman–Safavid War (1623–1639)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Safavid_War_\(1623%E2%80%931639\)), they were twice able to capture the city, but lost it again to the Ottomans in 1638.

Under the rule of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), Najaf experienced severe difficulties as the result of repeated raids by Arab desert tribes and the Persian army with acute water shortages causing lack of a reliable water supply. The number of inhabited houses in the city had plummeted from 3,000 to just 30 by the start of the 16th century.

When the Portuguese traveller [Pedro Texeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Texeira) passed through Najaf in 1604, he found the city in ruins, inhabited by little more than 500 people.[^cite_note-20] This was largely the result of a change in the course of the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) river eastwards in the direction of [Hilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilla), leaving Najaf and [Kufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kufa) high and dry, leading to the destruction of the local formerly rich agriculture, demise of the palm groves and orchards, followed by the salinization of the underground water due to evaporation.

During the 18th century, the scholarly life of Najaf came to be dominated by [Farsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farsi)-speaking ‘[Ulema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulema)’ ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عُلُمَاء, Scholars) from [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-21]

The water shortages were finally resolved in 1803 when the Euphrates made its way to the city once again. The shift in the river's flow was the product of a century-long effort by the Ottomans to shift the flow of the river, so as to deprive marsh-dwelling tribes like the [Khaza'il](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Khaza'il) of the watery environment that allowed them to evade state control. These long-term efforts rendered successful the construction of the Hindiyya Canal in 1793, which further shifted the flow of the Euphrates. These hydrological shifts were to have religious implications. Most notable was the consolidation and spread of Shi'ism. As the shrine city of Najaf gained access to water again, its notables and holy men began to wield considerable power in the area.[^cite_note-22] In 1811, the last [city wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_wall) was rebuilt.[^cite_note-transformation-23]

### Modern period



[Imam Ali Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Ali_Shrine) in 1932.

The Ottomans were expelled in an uprising in 1915, following which the city fell under the rule of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire). The sheikhs of Najaf including Sayyed Mahdi Al-Awadi rebelled in 1918, killing the British governor of the city, and cutting off grain supplies to the [Anazzah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anazzah), a tribe allied with the British. In retaliation the British [besieged the city and cut off its water supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Najaf_\(1918\)). The rebellion was put down and the rule of the sheikhs was forcibly ended. A great number of the Shi‘i ‘Ulema’ were expelled into [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia), where they set the foundations for the rise of the city of [Qom](638742.md) as the center of the Shi‘ite learning and authority, in lieu of Najaf. Najaf lost its religious primacy to Qom, and was not to regain it until the 21st century, during the establishment of a [Shī‘ī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'a)-majority government in Iraq after 2003.

In the 20th century, much of the Old City was rebuilt in a series of modernization initiatives. Beginning in the 1950s, many historic buildings and monuments, including those adjoining the shrine, were demolished for the construction of Sadeq, Zainulabidin, Rasool and Tousi streets. In 1958, the city wall was torn down and replaced with a [ring road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road). In the 1980s, the entire area between the shrine and the city's western edge was demolished, and the residents resettled outside the city, in what locals perceived as a government reprisal for the [Shia uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979%E2%80%931980_Shia_uprising_in_Iraq) under the leadership of [Muhammad Baqir al-Sadr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Baqir_al-Sadr), who was based in the neighborhood.[^cite_note-transformation-23]

During the [2003 U.S. invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_U.S._invasion_of_Iraq), Najaf was a key target of the invading [United States Armed Forces](32212.md). The city was encircled during heavy fighting on 26 March 2003 and was captured on 3 April 2003 ([Battle of Najaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Najaf_\(2003\))). The clerical authorities of the Shī‘ī enclave of [Sadr City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadr_City) in [Baghdad](4492.md), which claimed autonomy in April 2003, after the [fall of Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baghdad_\(2003\)), claimed to be taking their orders from senior clerics in Najaf.

On 4 April 2004, the [Mahdi Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdi_Army) attacked the Spanish-Salvadoran-ALARNG base (Camp Golf, later renamed Camp Baker) in An Najaf, part of a coordinated uprising across central and southern Iraq in an apparent attempt to seize control of the country ahead of the 30 June 2004 handover of power to a new Iraqi government. This uprising led to the American troops arriving in the city in the wake of the Spanish withdrawal. In August 2004, heavy fighting broke out again between U.S. forces and Al-Sadr's Mahdi Army.[^cite_note-24] The battle lasted three weeks and ended when senior Iraqi cleric Grand Ayatollah [Ali al-Sistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_al-Sistani) negotiated an end to the battle.[^cite_note-25]

In 2012, Najaf was named the Cultural Centre of the Arab World.[^cite_note-26] On 6 March 2021, [Pope Francis](1687680.md) visited Najaf during his historic [papal visit to Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis's_visit_to_Iraq) and held an interfaith dialogue with al-Sistani, where he expressed a message of peaceful coexistence between Muslims and Christians in the country.[^cite_note-27] During the ISIS war, Najaf Shiites launched solidarity initiative with Sunni areas affected by ISIS.[^cite_note-28] Many Iraqis including religious minorities such as Christians from the north, as well as Lebanese Shia have found safe haven in the city.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## Geography

### Climate

Najaf has a [hot arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_arid_climate), *BWh* in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), with long, very hot summers and mild winters. The average annual temperature is 23.6 °C (74.5 °F). Annual rainfall averages 93 millimetres or 3.7 inches.

## Religious significance

Further information: [Iraq in the Qur’an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_in_the_Qur%E2%80%99an)

An-Najaf is considered sacred by Shi'a Muslims. An-Najaf is renowned as the site of the burial place of Muhammad's son-in-law and cousin, [‘Alī ibn Abī Tālib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E2%80%98Al%C4%AB_ibn_Ab%C4%AB_T%C4%81lib). The city is now a center of [pilgrimage](23763.md) throughout the Shi'ite [Islamic](6037917.md) world. It is estimated that only [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) and [Medina](36636.md) receive more Muslim pilgrims. As the burial site of one of Shi'a Islam's most important figure,[^cite_note-Never_Again!-34] the [Imam Ali Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Ali_Shrine) is considered by Shiites as the third holiest Islamic site.[^cite_note-Never_Again!-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

The Imam ‘Ali Mosque is housed in a grand structure with a gold gilded dome and many precious objects in the walls. Nearby is the [Wadi-us-Salaam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi-us-Salaam) cemetery, the largest in the world.[^cite_note-FattahCaso2009-43] It contains the tombs of several prophets and many of the devout from around the world aspire to be buried there, to be raised from the dead with Imām ‘Alī on [Judgement Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Judgement). Over the centuries, numerous hospices, schools, libraries and convents were built around the shrine to make the city the center of Shīʻa learning and theology.

The An-Najaf seminary, or [Hawza Najaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawza_Najaf), is one of the most important teaching centres in the Islamic world. [Ayatollah Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayatollah_Khomeini) lectured there from 1965 to 1978.[^cite_note-Ring-44] Many of the leading figures of the new Islamic movement that emerged in Iraq, Iran and Lebanon in the 1970s had studied at Najaf.[^cite_note-45] As of 2014, it was estimated to have about 13,000 students.[^cite_note-monitor-enter-2014-46]

## Economy

The fall of the Ba'athist regime ended restrictions on Shi'ite pilgrimage, which led to a pilgrimage boom in Najaf and increased demand for facilities and infrastructure.[^cite_note-transformation-23][^cite_note-\:0-47] In 2006, the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Maliki_I_Government) sponsored reconstruction of the previously demolished western area of the city as the City of Pilgrims project.[^cite_note-transformation-23] Najaf, alongside [Karbala](210957.md), is considered a thriving pilgrimage destination for Shia Muslims and the pilgrimage industry in the city boomed after the end of Saddam Hussein's rule.[^cite_note-\:0-47] However, due to the [U.S. sanctions on Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._sanctions_on_Iran), the number of Iranian pilgrims dropped significantly.[^cite_note-48]

Since the end of the [2003–2011 war](5043324.md), numerous projects have been proposed for Najaf. The city has become a model for development in Iraq. Najaf have been experiencing economic boom, along with political events.[^cite_note-\:1-49] In 2008, over 50% of about licensed 200 investment projects totaling \$8 billion were under construction in [Najaf Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najaf_Governorate), with most development coming in the housing and tourism sectors.[^cite_note-50] Najaf has been described as a strategically important city with a stable investment environment and available skilled manpower.[^cite_note-51] The city has experienced an increase in investment.[^cite_note-52] Saudi Arabian firm ACWA Power is in process to construct a 1,000 MW solar power plant in Najaf.[^cite_note-53]

&#x20;The government proposed to build 15 housing complexes in Najaf, with a cost of \$7 billion as a part of 240 projects.[^cite_note-\:2-54] It also includes development of two industrial parks.[^cite_note-\:2-54] In 2024, prime minister [Mohammed Shia' Al Sudani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Shia'_Al_Sudani) announced several projects in the city, during his visit to Najaf.[^cite_note-\:3-55] The projects proposed by Al-Sudani included the Holy shrine's carpet-washing factory project will cost 3.46 billion.[^cite_note-\:3-55] A cement bag factory covering an area of 75,000 square meters with a production capacity of 240 million bags per year, is being constructed in Najaf.[^cite_note-\:1-49] Another project is a glass production factory, which will have a net profit of 32% in the first year of operation because the Najaf desert region has high-quality raw materials for this factory.[^cite_note-\:1-49]

## Culture

### Religious tourism

Najaf being home to the [Sanctuary of Imam Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Ali_Shrine) (considered the patriarch and the first of the [Twelve Imams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Imams)) is the destination of large numbers of Shi’i religious pilgrims annually hailing from around the world.

### Sports

The city of Najaf is home to [Al-Najaf SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Najaf_SC) and [Naft Al-Wasat SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naft_Al-Wasat_SC) that play in the premier [Iraq Stars League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Stars_League), and the second tier [Iraqi Premier Division League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Premier_Division_League), respectively.

Najaf has two football stadiums, the [An-Najaf Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-Najaf_Stadium) (also referred to as the old stadium) with a capacity of 12,000 spectators, and the newer [Al-Najaf International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Najaf_International_Stadium) with a capacity of 30,000. The latter stadium is the home of the Stars League club Al-Najaf as well as Naft Al-Wasat, whilst the former is the home stadium of the neighbouring [Al-Kufa SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Kufa_SC).

### Najaf Seminary

Main article: [Hawza Najaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawza_Najaf)

The Hawza of Najaf has been a centre of traditional Shia Islamic education since the 11th century CE. It was possibly established in the 9th century CE, making it the oldest Shi’i seminary among those still active, and many prominent Shi’i Islamic scholars have studied in the Najaf Seminary, including [Murtadha al-Ansari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtadha_al-Ansari), and [Ali al-Sistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_al-Sistani).

## Infrastructure



Najaf Airport, 2013

[Najaf International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Najaf_International_Airport) is an important logistical hub that plays a pivotal role in facilitating transportation, particularly for religious tourism. Annually, it oversees the transit of over 3 million passengers, predominantly pilgrims visiting the holy sites in Najaf. It is situated in the eastern part of Najaf, approximately 6 kilometres from the city center.

In February 2024, the Iraqi National Investment Commission (NIC) unveiled a project to construct an inter-city [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) connecting the cities of Najaf and [Karbala](210957.md). Once finished, it is set to accommodate up to 25,000 passengers per hour.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

The construction of a road between Iraq and Saudi Arabia around the region of Najaf is about to complete.[^cite_note-58]
