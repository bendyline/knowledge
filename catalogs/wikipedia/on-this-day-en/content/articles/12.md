**Anarchism** is a [far-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left_politics) [political philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_philosophy) and [movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_movement) that seeks to abolish all institutions that perpetuate [authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority), [coercion](6512.md), or [hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_hierarchy), primarily targeting the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)), [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism), and [organised religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organised_religion). Anarchism advocates for the replacement of these hierarchical [social structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_structures) with [stateless societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stateless_society) consisting of [horizontal networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism#In_anarchism) of [voluntary associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_associations) that operate based on the principles of [free association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_association_of_producers) and [reciprocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocity_\(social_psychology\)). Anarchism is described as being part of the [libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian) wing of the [socialist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_movement), broadly known as [libertarian socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_socialism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1992641Cohn20096LevyAdams2018104-1][^cite_note-nb1-5]

Although traces of anarchist ideas are found all throughout [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History), modern anarchism emerged from the [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment). During the latter half of the 19th and the first decades of the 20th century, the anarchist movement flourished in most parts of the world and had a significant role in [workers' struggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement) for their immediate interests and for [emancipation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation). Various anarchist schools of thought formed during this period. Anarchists have taken part in [several revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_revolutions_and_rebellions), most notably in the [Paris Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Commune), the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War) and the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), whose conclusion marked the end of the [classical era of anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_era_of_anarchism). In the last decades of the 20th and into the 21st century, the anarchist movement has been resurgent, growing in popularity and influence within [anti-capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-capitalist), [anti-war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-war) and [anti-globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-globalisation) movements.

Anarchists employ a range of approaches to social change, often categorised as revolutionary or evolutionary, though the two frequently overlap and are reflected in a [diversity of tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity_of_tactics). Evolutionary approaches generally favour gradual and often [nonviolent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonviolence) change, while revolutionary approaches seek to destroy oppressive states and institutions. Many facets of [human civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_civilisation) have been influenced by anarchist theory, critique, and [praxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxis_\(process\)).

## Etymology, terminology, and definition

Main article: [Definition of anarchism and libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definition_of_anarchism_and_libertarianism)

See also: [Glossary of anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_anarchism)



[Pierre-Joseph Proudhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Joseph_Proudhon) – the first political philosopher to call himself an anarchist

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKahn2000-6]

[^cite_note-Merriman-7]

[^cite_note-Leier-8]

The [etymological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) origin of *anarchism* is from the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) *anarkhia* (ἀναρχία), meaning "without a ruler", composed of the [prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefix) *an-* ("without") and the word *arkhos* ("leader" or "ruler"). The [suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffix) *[-ism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-ism)* denotes the ideological current that favours anarchy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBates2017128Long2013217-9] *Anarchism* appears in English from 1642 as *anarchisme* and *anarchy* from 1539; early English usages emphasised a sense of disorder.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMerriam-Webster2019"Anarchism"''Oxford_English_Dictionary''2005"Anarchism"Sylvan2007260-10] Various factions within the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) labelled their opponents as *anarchists*, although few such accused shared many views with later anarchists. Many revolutionaries of the 19th century such as [William Godwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Godwin) (1756–1836) and [Wilhelm Weitling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Weitling) (1808–1871) would contribute to the anarchist doctrines of the next generation but did not use *anarchist* or *anarchism* in describing themselves or their beliefs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJoll196427–37-11][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarlson197222–23-12]

The first [political philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_philosophers) to call himself an *anarchist* (French: *anarchiste*) and invest the word with a positive meaning was [Pierre-Joseph Proudhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Joseph_Proudhon) (1809–1865).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKahn2000-6][^cite_note-Merriman-7][^cite_note-Leier-8] The anarchist movement appeared between Proudhon and the [Saint-Imier Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Imier_Congress) (1872), with historians arguing for both.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaker202340-43-13] Since the 1890s and beginning in France,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENettlau1996162-14] *[libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism)* has often been used as a synonym for anarchism;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuérin1970"The_Basic_Ideas_of_Anarchism"-15] its use as a synonym is still common around the world.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWard200462Goodway20064Skirda2002183Fernández20099-16] But in the United States, some usages of *libertarianism* refer to [individualistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualistic) [free-market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-market) philosophy only, and [free-market anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-market_anarchism) in particular is termed *libertarian anarchism*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorris200261-17]

While the term *libertarian* has been largely synonymous with anarchism,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1992641Cohn20096-18] its meaning has more recently been diluted by wider adoption from ideologically disparate groups,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1992641-19] including both the [New Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Left) and [libertarian Marxists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Marxists), who do not associate themselves with [authoritarian socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarian_socialists) or a [vanguard party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguard_party), and extreme [cultural liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_liberals), who are primarily concerned with [civil liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_liberties).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1992641-19] Additionally, some anarchists use *[libertarian socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_socialist)*[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1992641Cohn20096LevyAdams2018104-1] to avoid anarchism's negative connotations and emphasise its connections with [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1992641-19] *Anarchism* is broadly used to describe the [anti-authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-authoritarian) wing of the [socialist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_movement).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyAdams2018104-20][^cite_note-nb1-5] Anarchism is contrasted to socialist forms which are [state-oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_socialism) or from above.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOsgood18891-21] Scholars of anarchism generally highlight anarchism's socialist credentials[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENewman200515-22] and criticise attempts at creating dichotomies between the two.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorris201564-23] Some scholars describe anarchism as having many influences from [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1992641-19] and being both liberal and socialist but more so.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalter200244-24] Many scholars reject that [anarcho-capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-capitalism) is anarchist, saying that it is a misunderstanding of anarchist principles.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1992564–565Jennings1993143GayGay199915Morris200813Johnson2008169Franks2013393–394-25][^cite_note-27]

While [opposition to the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-statism) is central to anarchist thought, defining *anarchism* is not an easy task for scholars, as there is much discussion among scholars and anarchists on the matter, and various currents perceive anarchism slightly differently.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELong2013217-28][^cite_note-31] Major definitional elements include the will for a non-coercive society, the rejection of the state apparatus, the belief that [human nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_nature) allows humans to exist in or progress toward such a non-coercive society, and a suggestion on how to act to pursue the ideal of anarchy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcLaughlin200725–26-32]

## History

Main article: [History of anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_anarchism)

### Pre-modern era



[Zeno of Citium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeno_of_Citium) (c. 334 – c. 262 BC), whose *[Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_\(Zeno\))* inspired [Peter Kropotkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kropotkin)

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall199370-33]

The most notable precursors to anarchism in the ancient world were in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China#Ancient_China) and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece). In China, [philosophical anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_anarchism) (the discussion on the legitimacy of the state) was delineated by [Taoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) philosophers [Zhuang Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_Zhou) and [Laozi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laozi).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoutinho2016Marshall199354-34] Alongside [Stoicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoicism), Taoism has been said to have had "significant anticipations" of anarchism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESylvan2007257-35]

Anarchic attitudes were also articulated by [tragedians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragedy) and [philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) in Greece. [Aeschylus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeschylus) and [Sophocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophocles) used the myth of [Antigone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigone) to illustrate the conflict between laws imposed by the state and personal [autonomy](191145.md). [Socrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates) questioned [Athenian](1216.md) authorities constantly and insisted on the right of individual [freedom of conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_conscience). [Cynics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynicism_\(philosophy\)) dismissed human law (*[nomos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomos_\(sociology\))*) and associated authorities while trying to live according to nature (*[physis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physis)*). [Stoics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoics) were supportive of a society based on unofficial and friendly relations among its citizens without the presence of a state.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall19934,_66–73-36]

In the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) (224-651 CE), [Mazdak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazdak) called for an [egalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarian) society and the [abolition of monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_monarchy), only to be soon executed by [Emperor Kavad I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavad_I).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall199386-37] In [Basra](57580.md), religious sects preached against the state.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrone20003,_21–25-38] In [medieval Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), there was no anarchistic activity except some ascetic religious movements. Various European religious sects developed anti-state and libertarian tendencies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENettlau19968-39] These, and other [Muslim](19541.md) movements, later gave birth to [religious anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_religion).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall199386-37]

Renewed interest in antiquity during the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) and in private judgement during the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) restored elements of anti-authoritarian [secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism) in Europe, particularly in France.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993108-40] [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) challenges to intellectual authority (secular and religious) and the [revolutions of the 1790s and 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_revolutions_and_rebellions) all spurred the ideological development of what became the era of classical anarchism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyAdams2018307-41]

### Modern era



The [Paris Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Commune), one of the earliest experiments of anarchism

During the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), partisan groups such as the [Enragés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrag%C3%A9s) and the *[sans-culottes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sans-culottes)* saw a turning point in the fermentation of anti-state and federalist sentiments.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall19934-42] The first anarchist currents developed throughout the 19th century: [William Godwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Godwin) espoused [philosophical anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_anarchism) [in England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_the_United_Kingdom), morally delegitimising the state, while [Max Stirner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Stirner)'s thinking paved the way to [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualist_anarchism) and [Pierre-Joseph Proudhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Joseph_Proudhon)'s theory of [mutualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutualism_\(economic_theory\)) found fertile soil [in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_France).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall19934–5-43] By the late 1870s, various anarchist schools of thought had become well-defined and a wave of then-unprecedented [globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalisation) occurred from 1880 to 1914.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevy201110–15-44] This era of [classical anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_anarchism) lasted until the end of the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) and is considered the golden age of anarchism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall19934–5-43]



Mikhail Bakunin opposed the Marxist aim of [dictatorship of the proletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship_of_the_proletariat) and allied himself with the federalists in the First International before his expulsion by the Marxists.

Drawing from mutualism, [Mikhail Bakunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Bakunin) founded [collectivist anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivist_anarchism) and entered the [International Workingmen's Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workingmen's_Association), a class [worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) [union](17626.md) later known as the First International that formed in 1864 to unite diverse revolutionary currents. The International became a significant political force, with [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx) being a leading figure and a member of its General Council. Bakunin's faction (the [Jura Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_Federation)) and Proudhon's followers (the mutualists) opposed [state socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_socialism), advocating political [abstentionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstentionism) and small property holdings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDodson2002312Thomas1985187ChaliandBlin2007116-45] After bitter disputes, the Bakuninists were expelled from the International by the [Marxists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxists) at the [1872 Hague Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1872_Hague_Congress).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraham2019334–336Marshall199324-46] Anarchists were treated similarly in the [Second International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_International), being ultimately expelled in 1896.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevy201112-47] Bakunin predicted that if revolutionaries gained power by Marx's terms, they would end up the [new tyrants of workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship_of_the_proletariat). In response to their expulsion from the First International, anarchists formed the [St. Imier International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Imier_International). Under the influence of [Peter Kropotkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kropotkin), a Russian philosopher and scientist, [anarcho-communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-communism) overlapped with collectivism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall19935-48] Anarcho-communists, who drew inspiration from the 1871 [Paris Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Commune), advocated for free federation and for the distribution of goods [according to one's needs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_each_according_to_his_ability,_to_each_according_to_his_needs).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraham2005xii-49]

During this time, a minority of anarchists adopted tactics of revolutionary [political violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_violence), known as [propaganda of the deed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_of_the_deed).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993633–636-50] The dismemberment of the French socialist movement into many groups and the execution and exile of many [Communards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communards) to [penal colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_colony) following the suppression of the Paris Commune favoured individualist political expression and acts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2004-51] Even though many anarchists distanced themselves from these terrorist acts, infamy came upon the movement and attempts were made to prevent [anarchists immigrating to the US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_the_United_States), including the *[Immigration Act of 1903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Act_of_1903)*, also called the Anarchist Exclusion Act.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993633–636LutzUlmschneider201946-52] [Illegalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegalism) was another strategy which some anarchists adopted during this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBantman2019374-53]

By the turn of the 20th century, the terrorist movement had died down, giving way to [anarchist communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_communism) and [syndicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicalism), while anarchism had spread all over the world.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoya2015327-54][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevy201116-55] In [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_China), small groups of students imported the [humanistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism) pro-science version of anarcho-communism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993519–521-56] [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Japan) was a hotspot for [rebellious](513246.md) youth from [East Asian](19605700.md) countries, who moved to the Japanese capital to study.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDirlik1991133Ramnath2019681–682-57] In [Latin America](18524.md), [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Argentina) was a stronghold for [anarcho-syndicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-syndicalism), where it became the most prominent left-wing ideology.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevy201123Laursen2019157Marshall1993504–508-58] Anarchists were involved in the [Strandzha Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strandzha_Commune) and [Krusevo Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krusevo_Republic) established in [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(region\)) in [Ilinden–Preobrazhenie Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilinden%E2%80%93Preobrazhenie_Uprising) of 1903, and in the [Mexican Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Revolution) of 1910. The [revolutionary wave of 1917–23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1917%E2%80%931923) saw varying degrees of active participation by anarchists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993470-59]



[Nestor Makhno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestor_Makhno) seen with members of the anarchist [Revolutionary Insurgent Army of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Insurgent_Army_of_Ukraine)

Despite concerns, [anarchists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Russia) enthusiastically participated in the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution) in opposition to the [White movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement), especially in the [Makhnovshchina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makhnovshchina). Seeing the victories of the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) in the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) and the resulting [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), many workers and activists turned to [Communist parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_parties), which grew at the expense of anarchism and other socialist movements. In France and the United States, members of major syndicalist movements such as the [General Confederation of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Confederation_of_Labour_\(France\)) and the [Industrial Workers of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Workers_of_the_World) left their organisations and joined the [Communist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_International).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENomad196688-60] However, anarchists met harsh suppression after the [Bolshevik government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik_government) had stabilised, including during the [Kronstadt rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronstadt_rebellion).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvrich2006204-61] Several anarchists from Petrograd and Moscow fled to Ukraine, before the Bolsheviks crushed the [anarchist movement there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Ukraine) too.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvrich2006204-61] With the anarchists being repressed in Russia, two new antithetical currents emerged, namely [platformism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platformism) and the [anarchist synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_synthesis). The former sought to create a coherent group that would push for revolution while the latter were against anything that would resemble a political party.

In the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) of 1936–39, anarchists and syndicalists ([CNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederaci%C3%B3n_Nacional_del_Trabajo) and [FAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federaci%C3%B3n_Anarquista_Ib%C3%A9rica)) once again allied themselves with various currents of leftists. A long tradition of [Spanish anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_anarchism) led to anarchists playing a pivotal role in the war, and particularly in the [Spanish Revolution of 1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Revolution_of_1936). In response to the army [rebellion](513246.md), an [anarchist-inspired movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Spain) of peasants and workers, supported by armed militias, took control of [Barcelona](4443.md) and of large areas of rural Spain, where they [collectivised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivised) the land.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBolloten19841107-62] The [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) provided some limited assistance at the beginning of the war, but the result was a bitter fight between communists and other leftists in a series of events known as the [May Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Days), as [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) asserted Soviet control of the [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_faction_\(Spanish_Civil_War\)) government, ending in another defeat of anarchists at the hands of the communists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993xi,_466-63]

#### Post-WWII



This [anarchist symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_symbolism), commonly known as the [circle-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle-A), represents the core principles of anarchism, including individual freedom and anti-authoritarianism

[^cite_note-64]

By the end of [World War II](32927.md), the anarchist movement had been severely weakened.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993xi-65] The 1960s witnessed a revival of anarchism, likely caused by a perceived failure of [Marxism–Leninism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) and tensions built by the [Cold War](325329.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993539-66] During this time, anarchism found a presence in other movements critical towards both capitalism and the state such as the [anti-nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-nuclear_movement), [environmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_movement), and [peace movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_movement), the [counterculture of the 1960s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterculture_of_the_1960s), and the [New Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Left).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993xi,_539-67] It also saw a transition from its previous revolutionary nature to provocative [anti-capitalist reformism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-capitalist_reform).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevy20115-68] Anarchism became associated with [punk subculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_subculture) as exemplified by bands such as [Crass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crass) and the [Sex Pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_Pistols).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993493–494-69] The established [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist) tendencies of [anarcha-feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcha-feminism) returned with vigour during the [second wave of feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_wave_of_feminism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993556–557-70] [Black anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_anarchism) began to take form at this time and influenced anarchism's move from a [Eurocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocentric) demographic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2015680-71] This coincided with its failure to gain traction in Northern Europe and its unprecedented height in Latin America.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarmon201170-72]



[Black bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_bloc) organising during the [Battle of Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Seattle)

Around the turn of the 21st century, anarchism grew in popularity and influence within anti-capitalist, [anti-war](39766716.md) and [anti-globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-globalisation) movements.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERupert200666-73] Interest in the anarchist movement developed alongside momentum in the anti-globalisation movement,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvren20111-74] whose leading activist networks were anarchist in orientation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvren20112-75] Anarchists became known for their involvement in protests against the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) (WTO), the [Group of Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Eight) and the [World Economic Forum](172185.md). During the [protests](266137.md), *ad hoc* [leaderless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaderless_resistance) anonymous cadres known as [black blocs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_bloc) engaged in rioting, [property destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_destruction) and [violent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence) confrontations with the police. Other organisational tactics pioneered at this time include [affinity groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affinity_group), [security culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_culture) and the use of decentralised technologies such as the Internet. A significant event of this period was the confrontations at the [1999 Seattle WTO conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Seattle_WTO_conference).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERupert200666-73] As the movement shaped 21st century radicalism, wider embrace of anarchist principles signalled a revival of interest.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvren20112-75] Contemporary news coverage of black bloc demonstrations emphasises violence committed by anarchists.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

While having revolutionary aspirations, many contemporary forms of anarchism are not confrontational. Instead, they are trying to build an alternative way of [social organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_organisation) (following the theories of [dual power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_power)), based on [mutual interdependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_aid) and voluntary cooperation, for instance in groups such as [Food Not Bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_Not_Bombs) and in [self-managed social centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-managed_social_centers).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoneywell2021-79]

Anarchism's publicity has also led more scholars in fields such as [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology) and [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian) to engage with the anarchist movement, although contemporary anarchism favours actions over [academic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy) theory.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2010110Evren20111AngelbeckGrier2012549-80] Anarchist ideas have been influential in the development of the [Zapatistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapatista_Army_of_National_Liberation) in [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Mexico) and the Democratic Federation of Northern Syria, more commonly known as [Rojava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria), a *de facto* [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_autonomous_zone) in northern [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Syria).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERamnath2019691-81]

## Schools of thought&#x20;

Anarchist schools of thought have been generally grouped into two main historical traditions, [social anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_anarchism) and [individualist anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualist_anarchism), owing to their different origins, [values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_\(ethics_and_social_sciences\)) and evolution.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcLeanMcMillan2003"Anarchism"Ostergaard200314"Anarchism"-82] The individualist current emphasises [negative liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_liberty) in opposing restraints upon the free individual, while the social current emphasises [positive liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_liberty) in aiming to achieve the free potential of society through equality and [social ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_ownership).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarrisonBoyd2003251-83] In a chronological sense, anarchism can be segmented by the classical currents of the late 19th century and the post-classical currents ([anarcha-feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcha-feminism), [green anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_anarchism), and [post-anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-anarchism)) developed thereafter.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyAdams20189-84]



Black bloc protesters parading anarcho-communism imagery such as the motto "[No War but the Class War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_War_but_the_Class_War)"

Anarchism's emphasis on [anti-capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-capitalism), [egalitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarianism), and for the extension of community and [individuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual) sets it apart from [anarcho-capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-capitalism) and other types of [economic libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_libertarian).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1992564–565Jennings1993143GayGay199915Morris200813Johnson2008169Franks2013393–394-25] Much of its [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_economics) and [legal philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_law) reflect [anti-authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-authoritarian), [anti-statist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-statist), [libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian), and [radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_politics) interpretations of left-wing and [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist) politics[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuérin197012-2] such as [collectivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivist_anarchism), [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_communism), [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualist_anarchism), [mutualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutualism_\(economic_theory\)), and [syndicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-syndicalism), among other [libertarian socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_socialist) economic theories.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuérin197035"Critique_of_authoritarian_socialism"-85] Anarchism is placed on the [far-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left_politics) of the political spectrum, though many reject state authority from [right-wing](56522.md) principles, such as anarcho-capitalists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrooks1994\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidgHyUj9HFYBICpgPP15_xi\]-86][^cite_note-\:1-87][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBackes2023a12-88][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKrüusselmannWeggemans2023a58-89]

As anarchism does not offer a fixed body of [doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine) from a single particular [worldview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldview),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall199314–17-90] many anarchist types and traditions exist and varieties of anarchy diverge widely.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESylvan2007262-91] One reaction against [sectarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sectarianism) within the anarchist milieu was [anarchism without adjectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_without_adjectives), a call for [toleration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toleration) and unity among anarchists first adopted by [Fernando Tarrida del Mármol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Tarrida_del_M%C3%A1rmol) in 1889 in response to the bitter debates of anarchist theory at the time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvrich19966-92] Despite separation, the various anarchist schools of thought are not seen as distinct entities but rather as tendencies that intermingle and are connected through a set of shared principles such as autonomy, [mutual aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_aid), [anti-authoritarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-authoritarianism) and [decentralisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralisation).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall19931–6AngelbeckGrier2012551-93]

Beyond the specific factions of anarchist political movements which constitute political anarchism lies [philosophical anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_anarchism), which holds that the state lacks [moral legitimacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legitimacy_\(political\)), without necessarily accepting the imperative of revolution to eliminate it.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEgoumenides20142-94] A component especially of individualist anarchism,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOstergaard200312Gabardi1986300–302-95] philosophical anarchism may tolerate the existence of a [minimal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_state) but claims that citizens have no [moral obligation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_obligation) to obey government when it conflicts with individual [autonomy](191145.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlosko20054-96] Philosophical currents as diverse as [Objectivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objectivism) and [Kantianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantianism) have provided arguments drawn on in favour of philosophical anarchism, including [Wolff's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Paul_Wolff) defence of anarchism against formal methods for legitimating it.[^cite_note-sa-97] Anarchism pays significant attention to moral arguments since ethics have a central role in anarchist philosophy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFranks2019549-98] Belief in political [nihilism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihilism) has been espoused by anarchists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalter200252-99]

### Classical



[Pierre-Joseph Proudhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Joseph_Proudhon) is the primary proponent of [mutualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutualism_\(economic_theory\)) and influenced many future [individualist anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualist_anarchism) and [social anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_anarchism) thinkers.

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilbur2019216–218-100]

Main articles: [Individualist anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualist_anarchism), [Mutualism (economic theory)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutualism_\(economic_theory\)), [Collectivist anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivist_anarchism), [Anarchist communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_communism), and [Anarcho-syndicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-syndicalism)

Inceptive currents among classical anarchist currents were [mutualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutualism_\(economic_theory\)) and [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualist_anarchism). They were followed by the major currents of social anarchism ([collectivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivist_anarchism), [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_communism) and [syndicalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-syndicalism)). They differ on [organisational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisational) and economic aspects of their ideal society.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyAdams20182-101]

Mutualism is an 18th-century economic theory that was developed into anarchist theory by Pierre-Joseph Proudhon. Its aims include "abolishing the state",[^cite_note-\:0-102] [reciprocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocity_\(cultural_anthropology\)), [free association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_association_\(Marxism_and_anarchism\)), voluntary contract, federation and [monetary reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_reform) of both credit and currency that would be regulated by a bank of the people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilbur2019213–218-103] Mutualism has been retrospectively characterised as ideologically situated between individualist and collectivist forms of anarchism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvrich19966Miller199111-104] In *[What Is Property?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Is_Property%3F)* (1840), Proudhon first characterised his goal as a "third form of society, the synthesis of communism and property."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPierson2013187-105] Collectivist anarchism is a [revolutionary socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_socialist) form of anarchism[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorris199376-106] commonly associated with [Mikhail Bakunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Bakunin).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShannon2019101-107] Collectivist anarchists advocate [collective ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_ownership) of the [means of production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means_of_production) which is theorised to be achieved through violent revolution[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvrich19963–4-108] and that workers be paid according to time worked, rather than goods being distributed according to need as in communism. Collectivist anarchism arose alongside [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism) but rejected the [dictatorship of the proletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship_of_the_proletariat) despite the stated Marxist goal of a collectivist [stateless society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stateless_society).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeywood2017146–147Bakunin1990-109]

Anarcho-communism is a theory of anarchism that advocates a [communist society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_society) with [common ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ownership) of the means of production,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMayne1999131-110] held by a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism) network of [voluntary associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_association),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993327-111] with production and consumption based on the guiding principle "[From each according to his ability, to each according to his need](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_each_according_to_his_ability,_to_each_according_to_his_need)."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993327Turcato2019237–238-112] Anarcho-communism developed from radical socialist currents after the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraham2005-113] but was first formulated as such in the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Italy) section of the [First International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_International).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPernicone2009111–113-114] It was later expanded upon in the theoretical work of [Peter Kropotkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kropotkin),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcato2019239–244-115] whose specific style would go onto become the dominating view of anarchists by the late 19th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevy20116-116] Anarcho-syndicalism is a branch of anarchism that views [labour syndicates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_syndicate) as a potential force for revolutionary social change, replacing capitalism and the state with a new society democratically self-managed by workers. The basic principles of anarcho-syndicalism are [direct action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_action), workers' [solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity) and [workers' self-management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_self-management).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVan_der_Walt2019249-117]

Individualist anarchism is a set of several traditions of thought within the anarchist movement that emphasise the [individual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual) and their [will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_\(philosophy\)) over any kinds of external determinants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERyley2019225-118] Early influences on individualist forms of anarchism include [William Godwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Godwin), [Max Stirner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Stirner), and [Henry David Thoreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_David_Thoreau). Through many countries, individualist anarchism attracted a small yet diverse following of Bohemian artists and intellectuals[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993440-119] as well as young anarchist outlaws in what became known as [illegalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegalism) and [individual reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_reclamation).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEImrie1994Parry198715-120]

### Post-classical and contemporary

Main article: [Contemporary anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_anarchism)



Lawrence Jarach (left) and [John Zerzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Zerzan) (right) are two prominent contemporary anarchist authors, with Zerzan being a prominent voice within [anarcho-primitivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-primitivism) and Jarach a notable advocate of [post-left anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-left_anarchy).

Anarchism has continued to generate many philosophies and movements, at times eclectic, drawing upon various sources and [combining disparate concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretic) to create new philosophical approaches.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2007303-121]The anti-capitalist tradition of classical anarchism has remained prominent in contemporary currents.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams20184-122]

Various anarchist groups, tendencies, and schools of thought exist today, making it difficult to describe the contemporary anarchist movement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFranks2013385–386-123] While theorists and activists have established "relatively stable constellations of anarchist principles", there is no consensus on which principles are core and commentators describe multiple *anarchisms*, rather than a singular *anarchism*, in which common principles are shared between schools of anarchism while each group prioritises those principles differently. [Gender equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_equality) can be a common principle, although it ranks as a higher priority to anarcha-feminists than anarcho-communists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFranks2013386-124]

Anarchists are generally committed against coercive authority in all forms, namely "all centralized and [hierarchical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchical_organization) forms of government (e.g., monarchy, representative democracy, state socialism, etc.), economic class systems (e.g., capitalism, [Bolshevism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevism), [feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism), [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery), etc.), autocratic religions (e.g., [fundamentalist Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_fundamentalism), [Roman Catholicism](606848.md), etc.), [patriarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchy), [heterosexism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterosexism), [white supremacy](33178.md), and [imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism)."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJun2009507–508-125] Anarchist schools disagree on the methods by which these forms should be opposed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJun2009507-126]

## Tactics

Anarchists' tactics take various forms but in general serve two major goals, namely, to first oppose [the Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Establishment) and secondly to promote anarchist ethics and reflect an anarchist vision of society, illustrating the [unity of means and ends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumental_and_intrinsic_value).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019107–108-127] A broad categorisation can be made between aims to destroy oppressive states and institutions by revolutionary means on one hand and aims to change society through evolutionary means on the other.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams20184–5-128] Evolutionary tactics embrace [nonviolence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonviolence) and take a gradual approach to anarchist aims, although there is significant overlap between the two.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinna2019125-129]

Anarchist tactics have shifted during the course of the last century. Anarchists during the early 20th century focused more on strikes and militancy while contemporary anarchists use a [broader array of approaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity_of_tactics).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019112-130]

### Classical era



The relationship between [anarchism and violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_violence) is a controversial subject among anarchists as shown by anarchist [Leon Czolgosz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Czolgosz) [assassinating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_William_McKinley) [William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McKinley).

During the classical era, anarchists had a militant tendency. Not only did they confront state armed forces, as in Spain and Ukraine, but some of them also employed terrorism as [propaganda of the deed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_of_the_deed). Assassination attempts were carried out against [heads of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), some of which were successful. Anarchists also took part in revolutions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019112–113-131] Many anarchists, especially the [Galleanists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleanists), believed that these attempts would be the impetus for a revolution against capitalism and the state.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENorris20207–8-132] Many of these attacks were done by individual assailants and the majority took place in the late 1870s, the early 1880s and the 1890s, with some still occurring in the early 1900s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevy201113Nesser201262-133] Their decrease in prevalence was the result of further judicial power and of targeting and cataloguing by state institutions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarmon201155-134]

Anarchist perspectives towards violence have always been controversial.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarter1978320-135] [Anarcho-pacifists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-pacifism) advocate for non-violence means to achieve their stateless, nonviolent ends.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFiala2017section_3.1-136] Other anarchist groups advocate [direct action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_action), a tactic which can include acts of [sabotage](29462.md) or [terrorism](30636.md). This attitude was quite prominent a century ago when seeing the state as a [tyrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrant) and some anarchists believing that they had every right to oppose its oppression by any means possible.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinna2019116–117-137] [Emma Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Goldman) and [Errico Malatesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errico_Malatesta), who were proponents of limited use of violence, stated that violence is merely a reaction to state violence as a [necessary evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_evil).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarter1978320–325-138]

Anarchists took an active role in strike actions, although they tended to be antipathetic to formal [syndicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicalism), seeing it as [reformist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformist). They saw it as a part of the movement which sought to overthrow the state and capitalism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019113-139] Anarchists also reinforced their propaganda [within the arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_the_arts), some of whom practised [naturism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturism) and [nudism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nudism). Those anarchists also built communities which were based on friendship and were involved in the news media.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019114-140]

[Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx), considered to be one of the principal founders of [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism), criticised anarchism as the movement of the "[petty bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petty_bourgeois)", i.e. of formerly self-employed craftsmen and artisans who had been ruined by capitalistic industrialisation or even war and then driven to factories; even so, they refused to subject themselves to factory discipline, party leadership, and State control, were prone to violence when frustrated, and advocated seizing factories only to break down mass production and return to craftsmanship.[^cite_note-141] [Friedrich Engels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Engels), who is considered Marxism's other principal founder, criticised anarchism's anti-authoritarianism as inherently counter-revolutionary because in his view a revolution is by itself authoritarian.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETucker1978-142] A [Socialist Workers Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Workers_Party_\(UK\)) pamphlet by [John Molyneux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Molyneux_\(academic\)), *Anarchism: A Marxist Criticism* argues that "anarchism cannot win", believing that it lacks the ability to properly implement its ideas.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDodds2011-143] Another Marxist criticism of anarchism is that it has a utopian character because all individuals should have anarchist views and values. According to this Marxist view, that a social idea would follow directly from this human ideal and out of the free will of every individual formed its essence. Marxists state that this contradiction was responsible for their inability to act. In the anarchist vision, the conflict between liberty and equality was resolved through coexistence and intertwining.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaárFalinaJanowskiKopeček2016488-144]

### Revolutionary and insurrectionary



[Internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Freedom_Battalion) in the [IRPGF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRPGF) battalion with text on revolution



[EZLN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EZLN) soldiers next to a captured bulldozer

In the current era, [Italian anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Italy) [Alfredo Bonanno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Bonanno), a proponent of [insurrectionary anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurrectionary_anarchism), has reinstated the debate on violence by rejecting the nonviolence tactic adopted since the late 19th century by Kropotkin and other prominent anarchists afterwards. Both Bonanno and the French group [The Invisible Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Invisible_Committee) advocate for small, informal affiliation groups, where each member is responsible for their own actions but works together to bring down oppression using [sabotage](29462.md) and other violent means against state, capitalism, and other enemies. [Members of The Invisible Committee were arrested in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnac_Nine) on various charges, terrorism included.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinna2019134–135-145]

Overall, contemporary anarchists are much less violent and militant than their ideological ancestors. They mostly engage in confronting the police during demonstrations and riots, especially in countries such as [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Canada), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Greece), and [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_in_Mexico). Militant black bloc protest groups are known for clashing with the police;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019115-146] however, anarchists not only clash with state operators, they also engage in the struggle against [fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism), [racists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism), and [other bigots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bigot), taking [anti-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-fascist) action and mobilising to prevent hate rallies from happening.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019117-147]

### Evolutionary



[Food not Bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_not_Bombs) poster with a [circle A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_A) with text reading "Free Soup for the Revolution"

Anarchists commonly employ [direct action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_action). This can take the form of disrupting and protesting against unjust hierarchy, or the form of self-managing their lives through the creation of counter-institutions such as [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intentional_community) and non-hierarchical collectives.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams20184–5-128] Decision-making is often handled in an anti-authoritarian way, with everyone having [equal say in each decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_decision-making), an approach known as [horizontalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontalidad).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019109–117-148] Contemporary-era anarchists have been engaging with various [grassroots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassroots) movements that are more or less based on horizontalism, although not explicitly anarchist, respecting personal autonomy and participating in mass activism such as strikes and demonstrations. In contrast with the "big-A Anarchism" of the classical era, the newly coined term "small-a anarchism" signals their tendency not to base their thoughts and actions on classical-era anarchism or to refer to classical anarchists such as Peter Kropotkin and Pierre-Joseph Proudhon to justify their opinions. Those anarchists would rather base their thought and praxis on their own experience, which they will later theorise.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinna2019145–149-149]

The concept of [prefigurative politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefigurative_politics) is enacted by many contemporary anarchist groups, striving to embody the principles, organisation and tactics of the changed social structure they hope to bring about. As part of this the decision-making process of small anarchist affinity groups plays a significant tactical role.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019109,_119-150] Anarchists have employed various methods to build a rough consensus among members of their group without the need of a leader or a leading group. One way is for an individual from the group to play the role of [facilitator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facilitator) to help achieve a [consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making) without taking part in the discussion themselves or promoting a specific point. Minorities usually accept rough consensus, except when they feel the proposal contradicts anarchist ethics, goals and values. Anarchists usually form small groups (5–20 individuals) to enhance autonomy and friendships among their members. These kinds of groups more often than not interconnect with each other, forming larger networks. Anarchists still support and participate in strikes, especially [wildcat strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildcat_strike_action) as these are leaderless strikes not organised centrally by a syndicate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019119–121-151]

As in the past, newspapers and journals are used, and anarchists have gone online to spread their message. Anarchists have found it easier to create websites because of distributional and other difficulties, hosting electronic libraries and other portals.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019118–119-152] Anarchists were also involved in developing various software that are available for free. The way these [hacktivists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacktivism) work to develop and distribute resembles the anarchist ideals, especially when it comes to preserving users' privacy from [state surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_surveillance).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019120–121-153]

Anarchists organise themselves to [squat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatting) and reclaim [public spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_space). During important events such as protests and when spaces are being occupied, they are often called [Temporary Autonomous Zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Autonomous_Zone) (TAZ), spaces where art, poetry, and [surrealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealism) are blended to display the anarchist ideal.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinna2019139Mattern2019596Williams20185–6-154] As seen by anarchists, squatting is a way to regain urban space from the capitalist market, serving pragmatical needs and also being an exemplary direct action.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinna2012250Williams2019119-155] Acquiring space enables anarchists to experiment with their ideas and build social bonds.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2019122-156] Adding up these tactics while having in mind that not all anarchists share the same attitudes towards them, along with various forms of protesting at highly symbolic events, make up a [carnivalesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnivalesque) atmosphere that is part of contemporary anarchist vividity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorland200437–38-157]

## Key issues

As anarchism is a philosophy that embodies many diverse attitudes, tendencies, and schools of thought, disagreement over questions of values, ideology, and [tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactic_\(method\)) is common. Its diversity has led to widely different uses of identical terms among different anarchist traditions which has created a number of [definitional concerns in anarchist theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definitional_concerns_in_anarchist_theory). The compatibility of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_capitalism),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993565Honderich199531Meltzer200050Goodway20064Newman201053-158] [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_nationalism), and [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_religion) with anarchism is widely disputed, and anarchism enjoys complex relationships with ideologies such as [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism), [collectivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivist_anarchism), [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism), and [trade unionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_unionism). Anarchists may be motivated by [humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism), [divine authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God), [enlightened self-interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlightened_self-interest), [veganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veganarchism), or any number of alternative ethical doctrines. Phenomena such as [civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilisation), technology (e.g. within anarcho-primitivism), and the [democratic process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_statist_democracy) may be sharply criticised within some anarchist tendencies and simultaneously lauded in others.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDe_George200531–32-159]

### The state



Anarchist protesters in [Boston](24437894.md) opposing state-waged war

Objection to the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)) and its institutions is a *[sine qua non](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine_qua_non)* of anarchism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarter197114Jun201929–30-160] The most minimal form of anarchist objection to the state is simply that political and social life would be better in a stateless society. However, many anarchists go further and consider the state to be a tool of domination, believing it to be not only suboptimal, but illegitimate regardless of its political tendencies. For instance, instead of people being able to control their own lives, anarchists often accuse states of taking decisions only involving a small elite. Another anarchist argument against states is that the people constituting a government, even the most altruistic among officials, will unavoidably seek to gain more power, leading to corruption. Anarchists consider the idea that the state is the collective will of the people to be an unachievable fiction due to the fact that the [ruling class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruling_class) is distinct from the rest of society.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJun201932–38-161]

One of the earliest criticisms is that anarchism defies and fails to understand the biological inclination to authority.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFerguson1886-162] [Joseph Raz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Raz) states that the acceptance of authority implies the belief that following their instructions will afford more success.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGans199237-163] Raz believes that this argument is true in following both authorities' successful and mistaken instruction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGans199238-164] Anarchists reject this criticism because challenging or disobeying authority does not entail the disappearance of its advantages by acknowledging authority such as doctors or lawyers as reliable, nor does it involve a complete surrender of independent judgement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGans199234,_38-165] Anarchist perception of human nature, rejection of the state, and commitment to social revolution has been criticised by academics as naive, overly simplistic, and unrealistic, respectively.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrinn2020206-166] Classical anarchism has been criticised for relying too heavily on the belief that the abolition of the state will lead to human cooperation prospering.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELucy2020162-167]

Specific anarchist attitudes towards the state vary. [Robert Paul Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Paul_Wolff) believed that the tension between authority and autonomy would mean the state could never be legitimate. Bakunin saw the state as meaning "coercion, domination by means of coercion, camouflaged if possible but unceremonious and overt if need be." [A. John Simmons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._John_Simmons) and [Leslie Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Green_\(philosopher\)), who leaned toward philosophical anarchism, believed that the state could be legitimate if it is governed by consensus, although they saw this as highly unlikely.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWendt20202Ashwood2018727-168] Beliefs on how to abolish the state also differ.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAshwood2018735-169]

A counterpoint of anarchism is the assertion that humans cannot [self-govern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance) and so a state is necessary for human survival; this critique was supported by Philosopher [Bertrand Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Russell), stating that "\[p]eace and war, [tariffs](55551.md), regulations of [sanitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation) conditions and the sale of noxious [drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug), the preservation of a just system of distribution: these, among others, are functions which could hardly be performed in a community in which there was no central government."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKrimermanPerry1966494-170] Another common criticism of anarchism is that it fits a world of [isolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolationism) in which only the small enough entities can be self-governing; a response would be that major anarchist thinkers advocated anarchist federalism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWard200478-171]

In [Anarchy, State, and Utopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy,_State,_and_Utopia), the philosopher [Robert Nozick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Nozick) argued that a "[night-watchman state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night-watchman_state)", or minarchy, would emerge from anarchy through the process of an [invisible hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisible_hand), in which people would exercise their liberty and buy protection from protection agencies, evolving into a minimal state.[^cite_note-Nozick_1974-172] Anarchists reject these criticisms by arguing that humans in a [state of nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_nature) would not just be in a state of war. [Anarcho-primitivists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-primitivism) in particular argue that humans were better off in a state of nature in small tribes living close to the land, while anarchists in general argue that the negatives of state organisation, such as hierarchies, monopolies and inequality, outweigh the benefits.[^cite_note-173] Philosophy lecturer [Andrew G. Fiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_G._Fiala?action=edit\&redlink=1) composed a list of common arguments against anarchism which includes critiques such as that anarchism is innately related to violence and destruction, not only in the pragmatic world, such as at protests, but in the world of ethics as well. Secondly, anarchism is evaluated as unfeasible or utopian since the state cannot be defeated practically. He says that this line of arguments most often calls for political action within the system to reform it. The third argument is that anarchism is self-contradictory as a ruling theory that has no ruling theory. Anarchism also calls for collective action while endorsing the autonomy of the individual, hence no collective action can be taken. Lastly, Fiala mentions a critique towards philosophical anarchism of being ineffective (all talk and thoughts) and in the meantime capitalism and bourgeois class remains strong.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFiala2017"4._Objections_and_Replies"-174]

### Gender, sexuality, and free love

Main article: [Anarchism and issues related to love and sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_issues_related_to_love_and_sex)

As [gender](38076.md) and [sexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexuality) carry along them dynamics of hierarchy, many anarchists address, analyse, and oppose the suppression of one's autonomy imposed by gender roles.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENicholas2019603-175]



Collection of [anarcha-feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcha-feminist) protests, symbols, and flags

Sexuality was not often discussed by classical anarchists but the few that did felt that an anarchist society would lead to sexuality naturally developing.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELucy2020162-167] Sexual violence was a concern for anarchists such as [Benjamin Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Tucker), who opposed [age-of-consent laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age-of-consent_laws), believing they would benefit predatory men.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELucy2020178-176] A historical current that arose and flourished during 1890 and 1920 within anarchism was [free love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_love). In contemporary anarchism, this current survives as a tendency to support [polyamory](24591.md), [relationship anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relationship_anarchy), and [queer anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_anarchism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENicholas2019611JeppesenNazar2012175–176-177] Free love advocates were against marriage, which they saw as a way of men imposing authority over women, largely because marriage law greatly favoured the power of men. The notion of free love was much broader and included a critique of the established order that limited women's sexual freedom and pleasure.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJeppesenNazar2012175–176-178] Those free love movements contributed to the establishment of communal houses, where large groups of travellers, anarchists and other activists slept in beds together.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJeppesenNazar2012177-179] Free love had roots both in Europe and the United States; however, some anarchists struggled with the jealousy that arose from free love.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJeppesenNazar2012175–177-180] Anarchist feminists were advocates of free love, against marriage, and [pro-choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion-rights_movement) (using a contemporary term), and had a similar agenda. Anarchist and non-anarchist feminists differed on [suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage) but were supportive of one another.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinna2019166–167-181]

During the second half of the 20th century, anarchism intermingled with the [second wave of feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_wave_of_feminism), radicalising some currents of the feminist movement and being influenced as well. By the latest decades of the 20th century, anarchists and feminists were advocating for the rights and autonomy of women, LGBT people, and other marginalised groups, with some feminist thinkers suggesting a fusion of the two currents.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENicholas2019609–611-182] With the [third wave of feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_wave_of_feminism), sexual identity and [compulsory heterosexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_heterosexuality) became a subject of study for anarchists, yielding a [post-structuralist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-structuralist) critique of [sexual normality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteronormativity).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENicholas2019610–611-183] Some anarchists distanced themselves from this line of thinking, suggesting that it leaned towards an individualism that was dropping the cause of social liberation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENicholas2019616–617-184]

### Education

Main article: [Anarchism and education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_education)

|  | Anarchist education | State education |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concept | Education as self-mastery | Education as service |
| Management | Community based | State run |
| Methods | Practice-based learning | Vocational training |
| Aims | Being a critical member of society | Being a productive member of society |

The interest of anarchists in education stretches back to the first emergence of classical anarchism. Anarchists consider proper education, one which sets the foundations of the future autonomy of the individual and the society, to be an act of [mutual aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_aid_\(organization_theory\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinna201983–85-186] Anarchist writers such as William Godwin (*[Political Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_Justice)*) and Max Stirner ("[The False Principle of Our Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_False_Principle_of_Our_Education)") attacked both [state education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school) and private education as another means by which the ruling class replicate their privileges.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuissa2019514,_521Kinna201983–86Marshall1993222-187]

In 1901, [Catalan](6822.md) anarchist and free thinker [Francisco Ferrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Ferrer) established the [Escuela Moderna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escuela_Moderna) in Barcelona as an opposition to the established education system which was dictated largely by the Catholic Church.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuissa2019511–512-188] Ferrer's approach was secular, rejecting both state and church involvement in the educational process while giving pupils large amounts of autonomy in planning their work and attendance. Ferrer aimed to educate the working class and explicitly sought to foster [class consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_consciousness) among students. The school closed after constant harassment by the state and Ferrer was later arrested. Nonetheless, his ideas formed the inspiration for a series of [modern schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_School_\(United_States\)) around the world.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuissa2019511–514-189] [Christian anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_anarchism) [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy), who published the essay *Education and Culture*, also established a similar school with its founding principle being that "for education to be effective it had to be free."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuissa2019517–518-190] In a similar token, [A. S. Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._S._Neill) founded what became the [Summerhill School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summerhill_School) in 1921, also declaring being free from coercion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuissa2019518–519-191]

Anarchist education is based largely on the idea that a child's right to develop freely and without manipulation ought to be respected and that rationality would lead children to morally good conclusions; however, there has been little consensus among anarchist figures as to what constitutes [manipulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipulation_\(psychology\)). Ferrer believed that moral indoctrination was necessary and explicitly taught pupils that equality, liberty and [social justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice) were not possible under capitalism, along with other critiques of government and nationalism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvrich19803–33Suissa2019519–522-192]

Late 20th century and contemporary anarchist writers ([Paul Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Goodman), [Herbert Read](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Read), and [Colin Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Ward)) intensified and expanded the anarchist critique of [state education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school), largely focusing on the need for a system that focuses on children's creativity rather than on their ability to attain a career or participate in [consumerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumerism) as part of a [consumer society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_society).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinna201989–96-193] Contemporary anarchists such as Ward claim that state education serves to perpetuate [socioeconomic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWard197339–48-194]

While few anarchist education institutions have survived to the modern-day, major tenets of anarchist schools, among them respect for [child autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_rights_movement) and relying on reasoning rather than indoctrination as a teaching method, have spread among mainstream educational institutions. Judith Suissa names three schools as explicitly anarchists' schools, namely the Free Skool Santa Cruz in the United States which is part of a wider American-Canadian network of schools, the Self-Managed Learning College in [Brighton, England](49796.md), and the Paideia School in Spain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuissa2019523–526-195]

### The arts

Main article: [Anarchism and the arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_the_arts)



*Les chataigniers a Osny* (1888) by anarchist painter [Camille Pissarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Pissarro) is a notable example of blending anarchism and the arts.

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAntliff199899-196]

The connection between anarchism and art was quite profound during the classical era of anarchism, especially among artistic currents that were developing during that era such as futurists, surrealists and others.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMattern2019592-197] In literature, anarchism was mostly associated with the [New Apocalyptics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Apocalyptics) and the [neo-romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-romanticism) movement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGifford2019577-198] In music, anarchism has been associated with music scenes such as [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_subculture).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall1993493–494Dunn2012EvrenKinnaRouselle2013-199] Anarchists such as [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy) and [Herbert Read](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Read) stated that the border between the artist and the non-artist, what separates art from a daily act, is a construct produced by the alienation caused by capitalism and it prevents humans from living a joyful life.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMattern2019592–593-200]

Other anarchists advocated for or used art as a means to achieve anarchist ends.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMattern2019593-201] In his book *Breaking the Spell: A History of Anarchist Filmmakers, Videotape Guerrillas, and Digital Ninjas*, Chris Robé claims that "anarchist-inflected practices have increasingly structured movement-based video activism."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobé201744-202] Throughout the 20th century, many prominent anarchists ([Peter Kropotkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kropotkin), [Emma Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Goldman), [Gustav Landauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Landauer) and [Camillo Berneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Berneri)) and publications such as *[Anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy_\(magazine\))* wrote about matters pertaining to the arts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerDirlikRosemontAugustyn20191-203]

Three overlapping properties made art useful to anarchists. It could depict a critique of existing society and hierarchies, serve as a prefigurative tool to reflect the anarchist ideal society and even turn into a means of direct action such as in protests. As it appeals to both emotion and reason, art could appeal to the whole human and have a powerful effect.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMattern2019593–596-204] The 19th-century [neo-impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-impressionist) movement had an ecological aesthetic and offered an example of an anarchist perception of the road towards socialism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAntliff199878-205] In *Les chataigniers a Osny* by anarchist painter [Camille Pissarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Pissarro), the blending of aesthetic and social harmony is prefiguring an ideal anarchistic agrarian community.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAntliff199899-196]
