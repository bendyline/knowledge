**Salim** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): سالم)[^cite_note-3] is a [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) town in the northern [West Bank](33209.md), located six kilometers east of [Nablus](233369.md) and is a part of the [Nablus Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Governorate). According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS), Salim had a population of 6,266 inhabitants in 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1]

## Location

Salim is located 6.63 kilometers (4.12 mi) east of [Nablus](233369.md). It is bordered by [Beit Dajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Dajan,_Nablus) to the east, [Deir al Hatab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_al_Hatab) to the north and west, Beit Dajan and [Beit Furik](14896389.md) to the south.[^cite_note-4]

## History

See also: [Shechem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shechem)

The village is ancient with foundations of houses.[^cite_note-5] The village has been populated in [Early Bronze I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age#Near_East_timeline), [Iron Age II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age#Near_East_timeline), [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) and [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states)/[Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) eras.[^cite_note-6] In 1882, traces of ruins, [cisterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern), a ruined tank, and a cemetery of rock-cut tombs were noted.[^cite_note-7]

Salim dates back to the [Middle Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Bronze_Age). It was near the ancient [Canaanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan) and later [Israelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelite) town of [Shechem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shechem).[^cite_note-8]

Salem was large and ancient [Samaritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans) village.[^cite_note-\:1-9] According to Samaritan tradition, Salim was founded by the biblical figure of [Jared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_\(biblical_figure\)) son of [Mahalalel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahalalel), and this is where 4th-century [High Priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan_High_Priest) [Baba Rabba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baba_Rabba) built his sixth synagogue.[^cite_note-BenZvi76-10] Samaritan texts refer to the place as *"Shalem Rabbta"*,[^cite_note-BenZvi76-10] and mention that Samaritan High Priests live there.[^cite_note-11]

Salim is also mentioned in the Samaritan *Continuatio of the Samaritan Chronicle of Abu l-Fath*.[^cite_note-\:0-12] The text mentions an event during the [Fourth Fitna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Fitna) (811–819) when a rebel named Abu 'Uf, from the [Judham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Judham) tribe, reached Salem and was killed there during battles between Muslim factions.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

### Ottoman era

In 1517, Salim was incorporated into the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) with the rest of Palestine. In 1596, it appeared in Ottoman [tax registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter) as being in the *[Nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* of Jabal Qubal of the *[Liwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_\(Arabic\))* of [Nablus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Sanjak). It had a population of 42 households, all [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). The villagers paid a fixed tax-rate of 33,3% on agricultural products, including wheat, barley, summer crops, olives, and goats or beehives, and for a press for olives or grapes; a total of 10,432 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e).[^cite_note-Hutteroth132-13]

In 1838, [Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) noted Salim as a village in the same area as the villages [Azmut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azmut) and [Deir al-Hatab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_al-Hatab),[^cite_note-14] all were part of the *El-Beitawy* district, east of Nablus.[^cite_note-15]

In May, 1870, [Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) came to the village, after walking through fields of olives, [figs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figs) and [almond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond) trees. He found a village with a maximum of 200 people, in ancient houses. A dozen [cisterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern) in the village were dry, so the women had to fetch water from a stream, called Ain Salim, about 1 kilometre north-northwest of the village.[^cite_note-16]

In 1882, the [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)* described Salim as a small village, but evidently ancient, surrounded by olive-trees and with two springs to the north.[^cite_note-17]

### British Mandate era

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), Salem had a population of 423, all Muslims,[^cite_note-Census1922-18] while in the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine), Salim, including El Hamra, had 100 occupied houses and a population of 490, again all Muslim.[^cite_note-19]

In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) Salim had a population of 660, all Muslims,[^cite_note-1945p19-20] with 10,293 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of land, according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-21] Of this, 229 dunams were plantations and irrigable land, 5,158 used for cereals,[^cite_note-22] while 24 dunams were built-up land.[^cite_note-23]

### Jordanian era

During the 1948 war the area was held by units from the Iraqi Army.[^cite_note-24] In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War) Salim came under [Jordanian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank).

The Jordanian census of 1961 found 888 inhabitants.[^cite_note-25]

### Post-1967

Since the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Salim has been under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md).

After the [1995 accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord) 27% of the village land is defined to be [Area B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves) land, while the remaining 73% is in [Area C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_C_\(West_Bank\)).[^cite_note-26]
