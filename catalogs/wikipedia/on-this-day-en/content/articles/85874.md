**Seine-Maritime** (French pronunciation: [\[sɛn maʁitim\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/51/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-Seine-Maritime.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-Seine-Maritime.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/51/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-Seine-Maritime.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-Seine-Maritime.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [department of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_France) in the [Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_\(administrative_region\)) [region](58846.md) of northern France. It is situated on the northern coast of France, at the mouth of the [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine), and includes the cities of [Rouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouen) and [Le Havre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Havre). Until 1955 it was named **Seine-Inférieure**. It had a population of 1,260,964 in 2023.[^cite_note-pophist-3]

## History

The department of Seine-Inférieure was created from part of the [old province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France) of [Normandy](21724.md) during the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) on 4 March 1790 by the application of a law of 22 December 1789.

After the victory at [Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo) of the coalition armies, the department was occupied by the [British Army](4887.md) from June 1815 to November 1818.

In [Rouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouen), [Elbeuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbeuf) and [Bolbec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolbec), the number of textile factories increased. Metallurgy and naval construction were other industries in the department.

After President [Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III)'s [coup d'état in 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_of_1851), Seine-Inférieure was one of several departments to be placed under a state of emergency (in French, literally, *[state of siege](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tat%20de%20si%C3%A8ge%20\(France\)#Constitution_du_14_janvier_1852))* [^cite_note-4] because fears of significant republican resistance to the new government.

In 1942, during the [German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_military_administration_in_occupied_France_during_World_War_II) of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), two [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) raids, the [Bruneval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Biting) and [Dieppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieppe_raid), took place in towns of the [English Channel](9230.md) coast.

In 1955, the department's name was officially renamed to Seine-Maritime.

## Geography

The department can be split into three main areas:[^cite_note-5]

- The [Seine valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine_valley). The Seine flows through the provincial capital [Rouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouen).

- The chalk plateau [Pays de Caux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pays_de_Caux), with its abrupt coastline (the [Alabaster Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabaster_Coast)).

- The Norman [Pays de Bray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pays_de_Bray), with its hills and [bocage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocage) landscape.

### Administration

The département was created in 1790 as Seine-Inférieure, one of five departements that replaced the former [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_France) of [Normandy](21724.md). In 1800 five [arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements) were created within the département, namely [Rouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouen), [Le Havre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Havre), [Dieppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieppe,_Seine-Maritime), [Neufchatel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neufch%C3%A2tel-en-Bray) and [Yvetot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvetot), although the latter two were disbanded in 1926. On 18 January 1955 the name of the département was changed to Seine-Maritime, in order to provide a more positive-sounding name and in-keeping with changes made in a number of other French departements.

## Transport

In 1843 the railway from [Paris](22989.md) reached the region. The département is connected to the adjacent [Eure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eure) department via the [Tancarville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tancarville_Bridge) and [Pont de Normandie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pont_de_Normandie) bridge crossings of the [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine).

## Culture

*[Madame Bovary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Bovary)* by [Gustave Flaubert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Flaubert) is set in Seine Maritime.

The novel *La Place* by [Annie Ernaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Ernaux) largely takes place in Seine-Maritime and describes events and changes that take place in relation to French society in the 20th century especially in relation to the rural population.

The first story of the long-running series *[Valérian and Laureline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val%C3%A9rian_and_Laureline)* is set in Seine-Maritime, with the character Laureline originating from the area.

[Cauchois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchois_dialect) is the dialect of the Pays de Caux, and is one of the most vibrant forms of the [Norman language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_language) beyond [Cotentinais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotentinais).
