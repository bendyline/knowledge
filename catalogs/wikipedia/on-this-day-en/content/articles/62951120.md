# Egypt–Sudan border

| --- | --- |



Map of the [22nd parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circles_of_latitude_between_the_20th_parallel_north_and_the_25th_parallel_north#22nd_parallel_north) which forms most of the Egypt-Sudan border



Map of the Egypt-Sudan border

The **[Egypt](8087628.md)–[Sudan](27421.md) border** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الحدود السودانية المصرية) is 1,276 km (793 mi) in length and runs from the [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) with [Libya](17633.md) in the west to the [Red Sea](26191.md) in the east.[^cite_note-1] The eastern section of the border is subject to a [territorial dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_dispute) between the two states.

## Description

The border starts in the west at the tripoint with Libya on [Gabal El Uweinat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabal_El_Uweinat) and then proceeds eastwards along the [22nd parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_parallel_north) to [Lake Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nasser).[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] The border then briefly veers northwards, creating an area known as the '[Wadi Halfa Salient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Halfa_Salient)', before resuming its course along the 22nd parallel out to the Red Sea just south of Cape Elba (Ras Hadarba).[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] Sudan maintains that the border diverges about 183 kilometres (114 mi) east of the salient, shifting south so as to leave [Bir Tawil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Tawil) in Egypt, and then north-east so as to include the [Halaib Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halaib_Triangle) within Sudan. The boundary traverses a thinly populated region of the [Sahara Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_Desert) and [Libyan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Desert) known traditionally as [Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia), with the main population centers being around the [river Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Nile) in the vicinity of [Wadi Halfa](1126657.md).

## History



Map showing the disputed Halayib Triangle, with Bir Tawil to the immediate west

[Egypt](8087628.md) and [Sudan](27421.md) are both ancient civilizations, with a long history of human settlement and complex societies dating back thousands of years, with the region of northern Sudan, known as [Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia), particularly intertwined with the history of [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt); both sharing cultural and political connections along the [Nile River](21244.md) valley throughout antiquity. Both countries have numerous archaeological sites showcasing their ancient past, including pyramids, temples, and burial grounds to 4000 BC. The northern region of Sudan was known as Kush in ancient times and had a powerful kingdom that interacted significantly with Egypt, sometimes even ruling parts of it. Following the [Battle of Actium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Actium) and [Battle of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Alexandria_\(30_BC\)) in 30 BC, [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) gained control of Egypt, which became a major grain producer for the empire led by [Octavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) who became the Roman Emperor with the title *Augustus*. The primary power in Sudan during the Roman period was the [Kingdom of Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush), which resisted Roman expansion. The Roman military campaigns into northern Sudan did not establish permanent control over the region due to the conflict between Rome and Nubia. Egypt, though nominally part of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), had acquired a large degree of autonomy under [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_of_Egypt) following the [Second Egyptian-Ottoman War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Egyptian-Ottoman_War) of 1839–1841.[^cite_note-IBS18-3] In 1882, the [British occupied Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_War), effectively establishing a [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Egypt_under_the_British) (formally declared only in 1914).[^cite_note-IBS18-3][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] Egypt's traditional claim to Sudan was maintained, and following a war against [Mahdist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdist_State) forces in the 1890s, the British [conquered Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Omdurman) and created [Anglo-Egyptian Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan) in January 1899 as a condominium state, divided from Egypt along the 22nd parallel.[^cite_note-Henderson-4][^cite_note-IBS18-3][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] The Wadi Halfa salient was added to Sudan on 26 March 1899 in order place a rail terminus from [Khartoum](17320.md) under Sudanese control.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2] A further agreement of 1902 created an 'administrative boundary' in the east in order to facilitate the administration of various nomadic peoples, thereby creating the [Bir Tawil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Tawil) region (to Egypt) and the [Halayib triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halaib_Triangle) (to Anglo-Egyptian Sudan).[^cite_note-IBS18-3][^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2]

Egypt gained full independence in 1922, and in 1956, the Anglo-Egyptian condominium was terminated with Sudan becoming independent. [South Sudan](32350676.md) declared independence from Sudan in 2011, following a [referendum](13265409.md) after 55 years of Sudan gaining independence from Britain and Egypt.[^cite_note-IBS18-3] At that point the 1902 agreement remained in force, however in 1958, Egypt re-asserted the 1899 boundary, a move protested by Sudan.[^cite_note-Brownlie,_I.-2][^cite_note-IBS18-3] After a brief show of force Egyptian forces withdrew from the region, and the dispute thereafter lay dormant. In 1959, Egypt and Sudan signed a treaty that paved the way for Egypt to create the Aswan Dam, which had the knock-on effect of flooding much of the Wadi Halfa salient under [Lake Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nasser).[^cite_note-IBS18-3]

The Halayib dispute flared up again in 1992 when it looked as if oil might be discovered off its coast. Egypt moved troops into the area, effectively establishing Egyptian control, despite the protestations of Sudan.[^cite_note-5] Thereafter, then Sudanese President [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md) reiterated the Sudanese claim of sovereignty over Halayib in a 2010 speech in [Port Sudan](213572.md), saying "Halayeb is Sudanese and will always be Sudanese."[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] At present the 1899 border is *de facto* in effect, leaving Bir Tawil in the unique position of being the only non-polar piece of land [not claimed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_nullius) by any country on Earth.
