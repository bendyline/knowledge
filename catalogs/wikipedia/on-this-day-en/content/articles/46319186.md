**98943 Torifune** ([provisional designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_planet_provisional_designation) **2001 CC<sub>21</sub>**) is an [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_asteroid)-type [near-Earth asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_asteroid) with a diameter of about 450 metres (1,500 feet). It was discovered by the [Lincoln Near-Earth Asteroid Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Near-Earth_Asteroid_Research) (LINEAR) survey at [Socorro, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socorro,_New_Mexico), United States on 3 February 2001. It was a [flyby](48936466.md) target of [JAXA](716184.md)'s *[Hayabusa2](10915042.md)* spacecraft as a part of its extended mission, which approached as close as 744 m (0.462 mi) from the center of the asteroid on 5 July 2026.[^cite_note-PDC-Yoshikawa2023-9][^cite_note-\:1-10]

Observations by *[Hayabusa2](10915042.md)* have shown that Torifune is a [contact binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_binary_\(small_Solar_System_body\)) asteroid.[^cite_note-11] Telescope observations from Earth had previously indicated that Torifune is an elongated [S-type asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-type_asteroid) with a rocky, [weathered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weathering) surface composed mostly of the [silicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicate) minerals [pyroxene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroxene) and [olivine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivine). It rotates with a [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_period) of 5.02 hours and has a small [axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt) of around 5° with respect to Earth's orbit.

## Discovery and naming

This asteroid was discovered on 3 February 2001 by the [Lincoln Near-Earth Asteroid Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Near-Earth_Asteroid_Research) project near [Socorro, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socorro,_New_Mexico), United States.[^cite_note-MPC-object-1] After several days of further observations by other observatories, the [Minor Planet Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Planet_Center) (MPC) published the asteroid's discovery on 9 February 2001 and gave it the [provisional designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_designation) 2001 CC<sub>21</sub>.[^cite_note-MPEC-2001-C19-2] The MPC gave the permanent [minor planet number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_planet_number) of 98943 for the asteroid on 24 February 2005.[^cite_note-MPC53713-12]

After selecting the asteroid as a target for *[Hayabusa2](10915042.md)*'s extended mission, [JAXA](716184.md) held a public naming campaign for the asteroid from December 2023 to May 2024.[^cite_note-13] A committee of *Hayabusa2* team members and their children reviewed the 60 names submitted to the campaign and ultimately chose "Torifune".[^cite_note-jaxa-torifune-name-14] The LINEAR team on behalf of the *Hayabusa2* team proposed the name to the [International Astronomical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union)'s Working Group for Small Body Nomenclature, which approved and announced the name on 23 September 2024.[^cite_note-jaxa-torifune-name-14][^cite_note-WGSBN-V4-13-15] "Torifune" is an abbreviation of [Ame-no-torifune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamiumi#Birth_of_the_kami), the name of a Japanese god and his ship, "which can travel safely at high speed like a bird and steady as a rock."[^cite_note-WGSBN-V4-13-15]

## Orbit and observation

Torifune is a [near-Earth asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_asteroid) following an elliptical orbit around the Sun, with a [semi-major axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-major_axis) of 1.03 [astronomical units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit) (AU), an [orbital period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_period) of 1.05 years, an [orbital eccentricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_eccentricity) 0.22, and an [inclination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_inclination) of 4.8°.[^cite_note-jpldata-3] Torifune is classified as an [Apollo asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_asteroid) because it crosses the orbit of [Earth](9228.md) with a semi-major axis outside Earth's orbit (>1 AU).[^cite_note-Hirabayashi2026-4][^cite_note-Popescu2025-7] At [perihelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perihelion) of 0.81 AU from the Sun, Torifune can come close to [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus).[^cite_note-MPC-object-1][^cite_note-jpldata-3] Due to the [Yarkovsky effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkovsky_effect), the orbit of Torifune is gradually drifting with a [transverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial,_transverse,_normal) acceleration (A<sub>2</sub>) of (1.3±0.6)×10<sup>−14</sup> AU/day<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-jpldata-3][^cite_note-Hirabayashi2026-4]

The asteroid's orbital period around the Sun is similar to that of Earth's, which allows it to hover near Earth for longer periods of time (giving it a long [synodic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synodic_period)).[^cite_note-Wright2025-8] Based on its [minimum orbit intersection distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_orbit_intersection_distance), Torifune can come as close as 0.083 AU (12.4 million km; 7.7 million mi) to Earth and as close as 0.080 AU (12.0 million km; 7.4 million mi) from Venus.[^cite_note-MPC-object-1] During its long-lasting close approaches to Earth, Torifune becomes brighter than [apparent magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_magnitude) 18, which makes it to observable to small- and medium-class telescopes on Earth.[^cite_note-Popescu2025-7] When viewed in a frame rotating with Earth's orbit, Torifune appears to move in an [epicyclic path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicycle) with a slowly drifting center.[^cite_note-Wright2025-8]

Torifune has been observed for over 13 [oppositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_\(astronomy\)), with an [observation arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation_arc) spanning more than 40 years as of 2026.[^cite_note-MPC-object-1] Torifune has been identified in two [precovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precovery) observations, which were both taken by the [Siding Spring Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siding_Spring_Observatory) on 10 November 1982.[^cite_note-MPC-object-1][^cite_note-Wright2025-8] The extensive number of observations provide a highly accurate [determination of Torifune's orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_determination),[^cite_note-Wright2025-8] which allows for the prediction of [stellar occultations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_occultation), where Torifune passes in front of a background star.[^cite_note-Arimatsu2024-16] As of 2026, only one occultation by Torifune has been observed; it was observed on 5 March 2024 by only one person in [Japan](15573.md), with 19 others reporting no detection.[^cite_note-Hirabayashi2026-4]

## Exploration

Animation of *Hayabusa2*'s trajectory from 2014 to 2031, with Torifune's orbit shown in cyan.\
&#x20; *[Hayabusa2](10915042.md)* **·**   [Ryugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/162173_Ryugu) **·**   [Earth](9228.md) **·**   Torifune **·**   [1998 KY26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_KY26)

In September 2020, a mission extension for JAXA's *Hayabusa2* asteroid [sample return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_return) probe was selected to do additional [flybys](48936466.md) of two near-Earth asteroids: Torifune in July 2026 and a rendezvous with [1998 KY26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_KY26) in July 2031.[^cite_note-Hirabayashi2021-17][^cite_note-PDC-Yoshikawa2023-9] *Hayabusa2* flew by Torifune at an estimated distance of 744 m (0.462 mi) and a very high relative speed of 5 km/s (3.1 mi/s), posing a challenge for the spacecraft's navigation and tracking capabilities during the encounter.[^cite_note-NHK-18][^cite_note-\:1-10]

## Physical characteristics

### Size, shape, and rotation

Telescopic observations have shown that Torifune is smaller than 1 km (1,000 m; 3,300 ft) in diameter, with various studies finding diameter measurements between 300 and 500 m (980 and 1,640 ft) (most likely around 450 m or 1,480 ft).[^cite_note-Hirabayashi2026-4] There is a large uncertainty in Torifune's diameter because it is calculated from an uncertain [absolute magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_magnitude#Small_Solar_System_bodies_\(H\)) of 18.78±0.14.[^cite_note-Hirabayashi2026-4][^cite_note-Wright2025-8] As of August 2026, the latest published measurement of Torifune's diameter is 540 m (1,770 ft). This diameter was derived by measurements from *Hayabusa2*.[^cite_note-\:0-19]

Torifune shows periodic variations in brightness as it rotates, which indicates it has an elongated shape.[^cite_note-Popescu2025-7] First determined in 2002, [photometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photometry_\(astronomy\)) measurements of Torifune's [light curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_curve) show that it has a [rotation period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_period) of about 5.02 hours.[^cite_note-Popescu2025-7][^cite_note-lcdb-20] Observations of long-term changes in Torifune's lightcurve suggest that it has a [prograde rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrograde_and_prograde_motion) with a low [axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt) of 5°±3° with respect to the [ecliptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecliptic).[^cite_note-Popescu2025-7] In visible light, the brightness of Torifune varies by up to 0.96 [magnitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitude_\(astronomy\)) during one rotation period, which suggests its longest equatorial diameter is roughly 2.44 times as long as its shortest equatorial diameter (equatorial axes ratio *b*/*a*=0.41).[^cite_note-Popescu2025-7] An approximate shape model derived from Torifune's lightcurve suggests it has dimensions of 840+160\
−120 × 320+100\
−80 × 340+60\
−60 m.[^cite_note-Popescu2025-7] The inferred elongation of Torifune's shape allows for the possibility that it might be a [contact binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_binary_\(small_Solar_System_body\)) object.[^cite_note-Fornasier2024-21] On the other hand, near-infrared observations by NEOWISE show smaller variations in brightness, suggesting a less elongated shape with dimensions of 591 m × 461 m × 392 m (1,939 ft × 1,512 ft × 1,286 ft) or an equatorial axes ratio of *b*/*a*=0.804±0.060.[^cite_note-Wright2025-8] Observations by *Hayabusa2* resulted in measurements for its longest and shortest axis being 840 m × 340 m (2,760 ft × 1,120 ft).[^cite_note-\:0-19]

In addition to light curve photometry, Torifune's elongated shape has been inferred from observations of [stellar occultations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_occultation).[^cite_note-Hirabayashi2026-4][^cite_note-Arimatsu2024-16] Only one occultation has been observed, with a single detection giving a [chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chord_\(astronomy\)) length of 449 m (1,473 ft).[^cite_note-Fornasier2024-21] Non-detections by other observers provided additional constraints to Torifune's location and shape, showing that it must be highly elongated with an equatorial axes ratio of *b*/*a*=0.37±0.09.[^cite_note-Arimatsu2024-16]

### Surface albedo and composition



Visible and near-infrared spectrum of Torifune (blue) compared to the Sq (black), [S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-type_asteroid) (green), [Q](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-type_asteroid) (cyan), and [L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-type_asteroid) (yellow) [asteroid spectral types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid_spectral_type)

Torifune's diameter and brightness in visible light can be used to derive its [geometric albedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_albedo),[^cite_note-Fornasier2024-21]<sup>: 4–5 </sup> though its uncertain absolute magnitude leads to uncertainties in albedo.[^cite_note-Wright2025-8]<sup>: 11 </sup> [Spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy) observations in the near-infrared show that it is a stony [S-complex asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-type_asteroid), with its spectrum best matching with the Sq subtype.[^cite_note-Micas2025-22]<sup>: 2 </sup>[^cite_note-Geem2023-23] Torifune was previously thought to be a [calcium–aluminium-rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium%E2%80%93aluminium-rich_inclusion) [L-type asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-type_asteroid) when it was observed spectroscopically for the first time in 2004.[^cite_note-Fornasier2024-21]<sup>: 1 </sup>[^cite_note-Geem2023-23]

Torifune's spectral features show a surface composed mainly of the [silicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicate) minerals [pyroxene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroxene) and [olivine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivine) (roughly 40% and 60%, respectively).[^cite_note-Popescu2025-7]<sup>: 12 </sup> The surface's mineralogy resembles that of [L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_chondrite) and [LL chondrites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LL_chondrite), and it shows signs of moderate [space weathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weathering).[^cite_note-Micas2025-22]<sup>: 3 </sup>[^cite_note-Popescu2025-7]<sup>: 14 </sup> Torifune's spectral features do not vary periodically, suggesting that its surface is broadly homogeneous.[^cite_note-Micas2025-22]<sup>: 4 </sup>
