# Health zone (Democratic Republic of the Congo)

A **health zone** (French: *zone de santé*, often abbreviated ZdS or ZS) is the basic operational unit of the public health system in the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) (DRC).&#x20;

Health zones deliver primary care, coordinate public health activities, and manage referral services for a defined population. They are overseen by a management team led by a *Médecin chef de zone* (zonal medical officer) and typically comprise a network of health centres linked to a general referral hospital.[^cite_note-WB2019-1][^cite_note-Normes2019-2]

## Overview

Under national norms, a health zone is a geographically delimited health district contained within a territory or commune. It is designed to ensure continuous, comprehensive primary health care and referral services for its population.[^cite_note-Normes2019-2] Zonal coverage targets vary by context; standards indicate at least around 100,000 inhabitants in rural areas and around 200,000 in urban areas,[^cite_note-Normes2019-2] while other planning documents describe typical coverage of about 100,000–150,000 inhabitants per zone.[^cite_note-WB2019-1]

As of the late 2010s and early 2020s, the number of health zones nationwide has been reported as 516–519, reflecting periodic administrative updates and dataset revisions.[^cite_note-WB2019-1][^cite_note-HDX519-3][^cite_note-BMC2025-4]

## Organisation

Each zone comprises two interdependent service levels:[^cite_note-Normes2019-2]

- Primary level: a network of *centres de santé* (health centres), each serving a [catchment area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catchment_area) known as an *aire de santé* (health area). Health areas generally cover about 5,000–10,000 people.

  [^cite_note-WB2019-1]

- Referral level: one *Hôpital général de référence* (general referral hospital, HGR), which provides the Complementary Package of Activities (*Paquet complementaire d'activites*, PCA) and clinical support to health centres that deliver the Minimum Package of Activities (*Paquet minimum d'activites*, PMA).

  [^cite_note-Normes2019-2]

The zone's management structures typically include the *Bureau central de la zone de santé* (BCZS) — the central office housing the zonal management team, *Équipe cadre de la zone* (ECZ) — which plans, supervises, and evaluates services across facilities and coordinates with communities.[^cite_note-Normes2019-2] At the community level, *Comités de développement de l'aire de santé* (CODESA) support governance and participation within health areas.[^cite_note-5]

## Governance

A health zone is led by a zonal medical officer (*Médecin chef de zone*), a physician who heads the management team and is responsible for planning, quality assurance, supervision, and liaison with the provincial health division.[^cite_note-WB2019-1][^cite_note-BMC2025-4] The Ministry of Public Health sets policies and standards at the central level, while provincial health divisions coordinate implementation across zones.[^cite_note-WB2019-1]

## Coverage and infrastructure

According to a World Bank review (2019), the DRC had 516 health zones subdivided into 8,504 health areas, each with a health centre. Of the zones, 393 had government-run general referral hospitals; the remainder relied on faith-based or private facilities to serve as the referral hospital for the zone.[^cite_note-WB2019-1] More recent sources describe 519 zones across the country,[^cite_note-HDX519-3][^cite_note-BMC2025-4] indicating incremental changes in counts due to administrative updates and mapping refinements.

## Functions

Health zones are intended to:

- implement primary health care (including maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health; infectious disease control; and health promotion);

- organise referral and counter-referral between health centres and the HGR;

- plan, supervise, and evaluate service delivery; and

- engage communities through local health committees.

  [^cite_note-Normes2019-2]
