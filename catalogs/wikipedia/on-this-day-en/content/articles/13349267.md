**Aleksandar Vučić**[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-4] (born 5 March 1970) is a Serbian politician who served as [President of Serbia](774382.md) from 2017 to 2026.[^cite_note-5] A founding member of the [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md) (SNS), he previously served as President of the SNS from 2012 to 2023,[^cite_note-6] [First Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Serbia) from 2012 to 2014, and [Prime Minister of Serbia](606320.md) from 2014 to 2017.

Born in [Belgrade](55904.md), Vučić graduated as a lawyer from the [Faculty of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Belgrade_Faculty_of_Law) of [University of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Belgrade). Vučić began his political career in 1993, as a member of the [far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics_in_Serbia) [Serbian Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Radical_Party) (SRS) in the [National Assembly of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Serbia). In 1995, he became the secretary-general of SRS. He was appointed [minister of information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_and_Telecommunications_\(Serbia\)) in 1998 in the government of [Mirko Marjanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirko_Marjanovi%C4%87). During his tenure as minister, which lasted until the [overthrow of Slobodan Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overthrow_of_Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87) in 2000, Vučić introduced restrictive measures against journalists and banned foreign TV networks.[^cite_note-Independent-7][^cite_note-8] After 2000, he was one of the most prominent figures in the Serbian opposition. Together with [Tomislav Nikolić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomislav_Nikoli%C4%87), Vučić left SRS and co-founded SNS in 2008, initially serving as its deputy president. SNS became the largest party in the [2012 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Serbian_parliamentary_election) and SNS soon formed a government with the [Socialist Party of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Serbia). Vučić was appointed first deputy prime minister and elected president of SNS.

Despite not being prime minister, Vučić held the most influence and power due to being the leader of the largest party in the government.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] He was one of the crucial figures in [cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo%E2%80%93Serbia_relations) and European Union (EU)-mediated [dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade-Pristina_negotiations) between the governments of [Kosovo](17391.md) and Serbia, advocating the implementation of the [Brussels Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Agreement_\(2013\)) on the normalization of their relations. Vučić became prime minister in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Serbian_parliamentary_election), leading to the establishment of a [dominant-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant-party_system).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] He continued the [accession process](10038148.md) to the EU by [privatizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatizing) state businesses and [liberalizing the economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalization).[^cite_note-15] EU opened first [chapters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquis_communautaire) during the accession conference with the Serbian delegation led by Vučić in 2015. In [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Serbian_presidential_election), Vučić was elected president of Serbia. He was re-elected in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Serbian_general_election). In 2026, he resigned as President to run as a prime ministerial candidate in the [2026 Serbian parliamentary election](75196857.md).

During his tenure as president, Vučić initiated [Open Balkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Balkan), an economic zone of Balkan countries intended to guarantee "[four freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market)", and signed an [agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_and_Serbia_Economic_Normalization_agreements_\(2020\)) in September 2020 to normalize economic relations with Kosovo. A [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist) politician, Vučić supports the accession of Serbia to the EU and also wants to retain good relations with Russia and China. Critics have described Vučić's rule as an [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism), [autocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocratic) or [illiberal democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiberal_democracy) regime, citing [curtailed press freedom](#The_media) and a decline in [civil liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_liberties).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-JovanovicSrdjanMladenov-18][^cite_note-19] Supporters of Vučić's administration highlight economic growth and the pursuit of pragmatic and balanced policies.[^cite_note-The_Man_Who_Remade_Serbia-20]

## Early life and education

Vučić was born on 5 March 1970[^cite_note-istinomer-21] in [Belgrade](55904.md) to Anđelko Vučić and Angelina Milovanov. He has a younger brother, [Andrej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrej_Vu%C4%8Di%C4%87).[^cite_note-22]

His paternal ancestors came from Čipuljić, near [Bugojno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugojno), in [Central Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bosnia). They were expelled by the [Croatian fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_State_of_Croatia) ([Ustaše](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usta%C5%A1e)) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia) and settled near Belgrade, where his father was born.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPoreklo-23] According to Vučić, his paternal grandfather Anđelko and tens of other close relatives were [killed by the Ustaše](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_of_Serbs_in_the_Independent_State_of_Croatia).[^cite_note-Novosti-289-571605-24] His mother was born in [Bečej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be%C4%8Dej) in [Vojvodina](176801.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPoreklo-23] Both of his parents were economics graduates. His father worked as an economist, and his mother as a journalist.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPoreklo-23]

Vučić was raised in the [Blokovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blokovi) neighborhood of [New Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPoreklo-23] where he attended the Branko Radičević Elementary School, and later a [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_\(school\)) in [Zemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun). He graduated from the [University of Belgrade Faculty of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Belgrade_Faculty_of_Law) in 1994.

He learned English in [Brighton](49796.md), England, and worked as a merchant in London for more than a year.[^cite_note-25] After returning to [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia), he worked as a journalist in [Pale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale,_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), [SR Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SR_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). There, he interviewed politician [Radovan Karadžić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radovan_Karad%C5%BEi%C4%87) and once played chess with general [Ratko Mladić](22742941.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIsailović6_September_2010-26] As a youngster, Vučić was a fan of [Red Star Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_Belgrade), often attending their matches,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIsailović6_September_2010-26] including the one played between [Dinamo Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNK_Dinamo_Zagreb) and Red Star on 13 May 1990, which [turned into a huge riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinamo%E2%80%93Red_Star_riot).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndex.hr13_May_2015-27] The homes of his relatives were destroyed in the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War).[^cite_note-Novosti-289-571605-24]

## Political career

Vučić joined the [Serbian Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Radical_Party) (SRS) in 1993, a far-right party whose core ideology is based on [Serbian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_nationalism) and the goal of creating a [Greater Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Serbia),[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-sense-29] and was elected to the [National Assembly](619753.md) following the [1993 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Serbian_parliamentary_election). During the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War), Vučić visited [Army of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska) positions during the [siege of Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sarajevo).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Photographs and video footage from the period have been the subject of controversy, including recordings from the [Pale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale,_Republika_Srpska) area showing him carrying what critics identify as a [rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rifle);[^cite_note-32] Vučić has disputed these interpretations, at one point claiming the object was an umbrella and later a [camera tripod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camera_tripod),[^cite_note-33] or that the photos and videos have been AI manipulated.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Since 2025, he has also faced allegations linking him to the [Sarajevo Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarajevo_Safari),[^cite_note-36] involving claims that foreign visitors paid to shoot at civilians during the siege.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Vučić has denied any involvement.[^cite_note-39]

After his party won the local elections in Zemun in 1996, he became the director of [Pinki Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinki_Hall),[^cite_note-40] which was his first employment.

### Minister of Information (1998–2000)

See also: [Propaganda during the Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_during_the_Yugoslav_Wars)

In March 1998, Vučić was appointed [Minister of Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_\(Serbia\)) in the government of [Mirko Marjanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirko_Marjanovi%C4%87).[^cite_note-41] Scholars described Vučić as the crucial figure in the shaping of turn-of-the century media policies in Serbia.[^cite_note-JovanovicMladenov-42] Following rising resentment against [Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87), Vučić introduced fines for journalists who criticized the government and banned foreign TV networks.[^cite_note-De_Launey-43] He recalled in 2014 that he was wrong and had changed, stating "I was not ashamed to confess all my political mistakes".[^cite_note-44]

During this period, Serbian media was accused for broadcasting Serbian nationalist [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_during_the_Yugoslav_Wars), which demonized ethnic minorities and legitimized Serb atrocities against them.[^cite_note-45] In 1998, the government adopted Europe's most restrictive media law by the end of the 20th century, which created a special misdemeanor court to try violations. It had the ability to impose heavy fines and to confiscate property if they were not immediately paid.[^cite_note-Brosse-46][^cite_note-JovanovicMladenov-42] Serbian media were under severe repression of the state, and that foreign media had been seen as "foreign elements" and "spies".[^cite_note-JovanovicMladenov-42] [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) reported that five independent newspaper editors were charged with disseminating misinformation because they referred to [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Albanians) who had died in Kosovo as "people" rather than "terrorists".[^cite_note-HRW-47] The government crackdown on independent media intensified when [NATO](21133.md) forces were threatening intervention in Kosovo in late September and early October 1998. The government also maintained direct control of state radio and television, which provided news for the majority of the population.[^cite_note-HRW-47] After the [NATO bombing of Yugoslavia](322473.md) began in March 1999, Vučić called for a meeting of all Belgrade's editors. Print media were ordered to submit all copies to the Ministry for approval and they were allowed to publish only official statements and information taken from media outlets, which either are controlled by the state or practice radical self-censorship.[^cite_note-JovanovicMladenov-42] Vučić also ordered all NATO countries journalists to leave the country.[^cite_note-JovanovicMladenov-42]

### Radical Party to Progressive Party



Vučić and Tomislav Nikolić on the founding congress of the [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md), Belgrade, 2008

[Tomislav Nikolić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomislav_Nikoli%C4%87), deputy leader of the Radical Party and de facto interim leader due to absence of [Vojislav Šešelj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojislav_%C5%A0e%C5%A1elj), resigned on 6 September 2008 because of disagreement with Šešelj over the party's support for Serbia's EU membership. With some other well-known Radical Party members he formed a new parliamentary club called "*Napred Srbijo!*" (*Forward Serbia!*). On 12 September 2008, Nikolić and his group were officially ejected from the Radical Party on the session of SRS leadership. Vučić, as secretary-general was called to attend this session, but he did not appear. Tomislav Nikolić announced he would form his own party and called Vučić to join. Vučić, one of the most popular figures among SRS supporters, resigned from Radical Party on 14 September 2008.[^cite_note-48] The next day, Vučić announced his temporary withdrawal from politics.[^cite_note-49]



Vučić and U.S. secretary of defense [Leon Panetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Panetta) in Washington, D.C. in 2012

On 6 October 2008, Vučić confirmed in a TV interview that he was to join the newly formed Nikolić's [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md) (SNS) and that he would be the Deputy President of the party. He then seemed to change his positions. In 2010 he made statements such as a "horrible crime was committed in Srebrenica", saying he felt "ashamed" of the Serbs who did it. "I do not hide that I have changed... I am proud of that." he told AFP in an interview in 2012. "I was wrong, I thought I was doing the best for my country, but I saw the results and we failed, We need to admit that."[^cite_note-50]

### Minister of Defence and First Deputy Prime Minister (2012–2014)

Vučić briefly served as [minister of defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_\(Serbia\)) and first deputy prime minister from July 2012 to August 2013, when he stepped down from his position of defence minister in a [cabinet reshuffle](845103.md). Although the prime minister, [Ivica Dačić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivica_Da%C4%8Di%C4%87) Deba, held formal power as head of government, many analysts thought that Vučić had the most influence in government as head of the largest party in the governing coalition and parliament.[^cite_note-De_Launey-43]

### Prime Minister (2014–2017)

#### 2014 parliamentary election

Main article: [2014 Serbian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Serbian_parliamentary_election)

As a result of the 2014 parliamentary election, Vučić's Serbian Progressive Party won 158 out of 250 seats in Parliament and formed a ruling coalition with the [Socialist Party of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Serbia). Vučić was elected Prime Minister of Serbia.

#### 2016 parliamentary election

Main article: [2016 Serbian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Serbian_parliamentary_election)

At a party conference of his ruling Serbian Progressive Party, Vučić announced early general elections, citing that: 'He wants to ensure that the country has stable rule that its current political direction will continue – including its attempt to secure membership of the EU.'[^cite_note-51] On 4 March 2016, Serbian president, Tomislav Nikolić, dissolved the parliament, scheduling early elections for 24 April.[^cite_note-52] The ruling coalition around Vučić's SNS obtained 48.25% of the vote.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] Vučić's ruling SNS retained majority in the parliament, despite winning less seats than in 2014 parliamentary election. The coalition around SNS won 131 seats, 98 of which belong to SNS.[^cite_note-55]

### 2017 presidential election

Main article: [2017 Serbian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Serbian_presidential_election)



Vučić with [Prime Minister of India](24452.md), [Narendra Modi](444222.md) in 2026

Vučić announced his candidacy in the presidential election on 14 February 2017, despite earlier statements that he would not run.[^cite_note-56] According to the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Serbia), Serbia is a [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic) in which the [presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_\(government_title\)) is largely ceremonial with no significant executive power.[^cite_note-57]

After initial speculations that the incumbent president, Tomislav Nikolić, would also run, he backed Vučić and his ruling SNS party. Vučić won the election in the first round, having obtained 56.01 percent of the vote. The independent candidate, [Saša Janković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C5%A1a_Jankovi%C4%87) was second with 16.63 percent, ahead of satirical politician [Luka Maksimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubi%C5%A1a_Preleta%C4%8Devi%C4%87) and former minister of foreign affairs [Vuk Jeremić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuk_Jeremi%C4%87).[^cite_note-58]

A public opinion survey, carried out by [CeSID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CeSID), showed that significant proportions of Vučić supporters are composed of [pensioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensioner) (41%) and that a large majority of them (63%) hold secondary education degrees, while 21% don't even have a high school degree.[^cite_note-CeSID-59]

### President (2017–2026)

250

[President of Israel](153049.md), [Isaac Herzog](4645588.md), Aleksandar Vučić, and Idit Ohel, the mother of the abductee to Gaza, [Alon Ohel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alon_Ohel), on 11 September 2024

The election result sparked [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Serbian_protests) around Serbia. Thousands of protesters accused Vučić of leading the country towards authoritarianism. Protesters organized the rallies through social media, insisted that they are not linked to any party or politician, and demanded a total overhaul of what they call "corrupt political, business and media systems that serve an elite led by Mr Vučić".[^cite_note-60] Vučić said that the protests were organized by his political opponents who, he said, expected "the dictator would bring the police into the streets."[^cite_note-61]

Vučić was sworn in as President of Serbia on 31 May, in front of [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Serbia).[^cite_note-62] He promised to continue with reforms and said Serbia will remain on a European path. Vučić also said Serbia will maintain military neutrality and continue to build partnerships with both [NATO](21133.md) and Russia.[^cite_note-63]

After becoming president, Vučić disbanded the traditional police security service responsible for president's protection, and replaced it with members of the [Cobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Police_Battalion_Cobra), military police unit which contrary to the law, protected him while he served as the prime minister from 2014 to 2017.[^cite_note-64]

During late 2018 and early 2019, thousands of Serbs [took to the streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_protests_\(2018%E2%80%932020\)) to protest the presidency of Vučić. The protesters accused Vučić and the SNS of corruption and stated that Vučić is trying to cement himself as an autocrat, which he denied.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] In 2019, [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House)'s report downgraded Serbia's [status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_in_the_World) from *Free* to *Partly Free* due to the deterioration in the conduct of elections, continued attempts by the government and allied media outlets to undermine independent journalists through legal harassment and smear campaigns, and Vučić's accumulation of executive powers that conflict with his constitutional role.[^cite_note-67]



Vučić participated in the India–AI Impact Summit 2026 in [New Delhi](51585.md), where he met with [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md), Narendra Modi, [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md), and other world leaders.

After Vučić's announcement of the reintroduction of [lockdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns) in July 2020 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Serbia), thousands of people [protested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_COVID-19_protests_and_riots), accusing the government of missteps in handling of the pandemic, including the premature lifting of restrictions and downplaying the risk to hold the [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Serbian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-NYT2020-68][^cite_note-69] Some analysts said that they had not witnessed [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality) which occurred during the protest since the Slobodan Milošević's regime.[^cite_note-NYT2020-68]

According to [Amnesty International](18947898.md)'s annual report for 2021, Vučić's mandate is characterized by human rights violations, restrictions on freedom of expression and campaigns of harassment against the opposition figures, journalists and media outlets.[^cite_note-70]

Vučić participated in the [2022 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Serbian_general_election) as the presidential candidate of the Serbian Progressive Party. He won 58% of the popular vote in the first round, and secured his second mandate as president of Serbia.[^cite_note-71] Vučić announced the formation of the [People's Movement for the State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Movement_for_the_State) in March 2023.[^cite_note-72]

Following the [Belgrade school shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_school_shooting) and [Mladenovac and Smederevo shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mladenovac_and_Smederevo_shootings) in May 2023, large-scale [anti-government protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Serbian_protests) took place against Vučić's rule. As a result, Vučić promised that early [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Serbian_parliamentary_election) would be held before the end of the year.[^cite_note-73]

On 1 November 2024, the concrete [canopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canopy_\(architecture\)) of the [main railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_railway_station) in Novi Sad [collapsed](78262640.md) onto the busy pavement below, killing 16 people.[^cite_note-74] The collapse spawned a series of [mass protests in Novi Sad](78597389.md), which then spread throughout Serbia,[^cite_note-75] fueled by dissatisfaction with other issues including government corruption and media censorship.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] Vučić dismissed the protesters as "terrorists".[^cite_note-78]

In June 2026, Vučić announced he would resign in a matter of weeks after a year and a half of student-led protests against the government.[^cite_note-79] On 5 September 2026, SNS unanimously nominated Vučić, the president of Serbia, as candidate for Prime Minister and head of the [United Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_Must_Not_Stop) electoral list prior to the [elections](75196857.md) scheduled for late October 2026.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] *[Euractiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euractiv)* reported that the campaign is likely to become not only a referendum on Vučić’s rule, but also a test of Serbia's political direction and its increasingly strained relationship with the EU.[^cite_note-83] Vučić resigned on 27 September 2026, and [Ana Brnabić](54310335.md) subsequently became acting President.[^cite_note-l861-84][^cite_note-TC1-85]

## Policies

### Economy

Further information: [Economy of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Serbia)



Vučić with [Chancellor of Germany](20890626.md), [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz), [Belgrade](55904.md), on 19 July 2024.

After his election as prime minister in 2014, Vučić promoted austerity-based economic policies, whose aim was to reduce Serbia's budget deficit. Vučić's policy of fiscal consolidation was primarily aimed at cuts in the public sector. One of the measures was the reduction of pensions and salaries in the public sector as well as a ban on further employment in the public sector.[^cite_note-86] On 23 February 2015, Vučić's government has concluded a three-year stand-by arrangement with the [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF) worth €1.2 billion as a precautionary measure to secure the country's long term fiscal stability.[^cite_note-87] The IMF has praised the reforms as has the EU[^cite_note-B92.net-88][^cite_note-89] calling them one of the most successful programmes the IMF has ever had. The GDP of Serbia has surpassed the pre crisis of 2008 levels as have the salaries.[^cite_note-90] The economic prospects are good with GDP growth rising above 3% and the debt to GDP ratio falling below 68%[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-B92.net-88]

### Corruption and organized crime

Further information: [Corruption in Serbia](42552633.md), [Crime in Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_Serbia), and [Serbian mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_mafia)

Vučić has pledged to tackle corruption and [organized crime](22625.md) in Serbia.[^cite_note-Vasovic-92] He also vowed to investigate controversial [privatizations](24661.md) and ties between [tycoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycoon) and former government members.[^cite_note-De_Launey-43][^cite_note-Pekusic-93]



Vučić with [President of Azerbaijan](938372.md), [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) on 21 December 2022

On the other hand, data from the [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International) showed that a significant increase in perceived corruption was seen exactly from 2012, when Vučić came into power.[^cite_note-JovanovicCorruption-94] According to research conducted by the *Centre for Investigative Journalism*, the battle against corruption in practice comes down to media announcements and arrests in front of cameras. "They are followed by a large number of criminal charges, significantly fewer indictments, and even fewer convictions".[^cite_note-JovanovicCorruption-94][^cite_note-95]

In May 2023, the *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* published an article by journalist Robert F. Worth which described alleged ties between Vučić and organized crime in Serbia, particularly its leader Veljko Belivuk. It detailed crimes committed by the Belivuk criminal gang and court transcripts in which Belivuk stated he had met Vučić, worked directly under him and provided services such as intimidating political rivals.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] Vučić called the allegations "lies", saying that the article was ordered by the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) months prior to send him a message and that it was written in Belgrade, accusations which Worth rejected.[^cite_note-98]

### EU and Immigration policy

Further information: [Accession of Serbia to the European Union](10038148.md)



Vučić with European Commission Vice-President [Josep Borrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Borrell), 17 February 2024

During the 2015 – 2016 [European migrant crisis](46415102.md), Vučić strongly aligned himself with the policies of the German Chancellor, [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel), and publicly praised German migration policy. Vučić also stated that Serbia will cooperate with the EU in solving the migrant stream going from the Middle East to EU member countries through the Balkan route, and that Serbia will be ready to take some portion of the migrants. "Serbia will receive a certain number of migrants. This makes us more European than some member states. We don't build fences," Vučić wrote on Twitter, while criticizing the migrant policies of some EU member countries.[^cite_note-99]

### EU and lithium

During 2021, a [series of mass protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Serbian_environmental_protests) erupted in Serbia against the construction of a [lithium](17561.md) mine in Western Serbia by the [Rio Tinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Tinto_\(corporation\)) corporation.[^cite_note-100] In January 2022, the Serbian government revoked licences for the [Jadar project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadar_mine).[^cite_note-101] In January 2024, Vučić stated that the government wants to hold further talks with Rio Tinto and that there should be more public discussion over whether the project should go ahead. If completed, the project could supply 90% of Europe's current lithium needs and help to make Rio Tinto a leading lithium producer.[^cite_note-102]

On 19 July 2024, following EU pressure,[^cite_note-103] Vučić, German Chancellor [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz), and European Union energy chief [Maroš Šefčovič](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maro%C5%A1_%C5%A0ef%C4%8Dovi%C4%8D) met and signed an agreement regarding the European Union's access to "critical raw materials" mined in Serbia, representing a further step towards facilitating the Jadar mining project.[^cite_note-104] The project resulted in [renewed environmental protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Serbian_environmental_protests) in 2024.[^cite_note-105] One week later, Serbian Minister of Mining and Energy [Dubravka Đedović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubravka_%C4%90edovi%C4%87) signed a memorandum with Maroš Šefčovič, Vice-President and "overseer" of the [European Green Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Deal), agreeing on the basis of critical raw materials, battery value chains, and [electric vehicles](279350.md) related to the mining project."[^cite_note-106]

### Policy towards Kosovo

Further information: [Political status of Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Kosovo) and [Kosovo–Serbia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo%E2%80%93Serbia_relations)

Until the new coalition government was formed in 2012, during the time he served as the secretary general of the [Serbian Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Radical_Party), the largest opposition party at the time, as well as during his position of the vice president of then newly formed Serbian Progressive Party, in 2008, Vučić was highly critical towards [Koštunica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojislav_Ko%C5%A1tunica) and [Cvetković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirko_Cvetkovi%C4%87)'s administrations, and offered a "reversal" of the agreements made by [Borko Stefanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borko_Stefanovi%C4%87) and the other officials during the negotiation process. Upon forming the government, Vucic stated that his government "cannot pretend that that \[the former administration] was some different state which made the deals".

Vučić was one of the key political figures in the negotiation process on Serbia's bid for EU accession, traveling to [Brussels](3708.md) for talks with the EU's Foreign Affairs High Commissioner, [Baroness Ashton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Ashton), as well as to [North Mitrovica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Mitrovica) to discuss the details of a political settlement between on behalf of the Government of Serbia and Kosovo administration.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] During his visit to [North Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kosovo), to garner support for the Brussels-brokered deal, he urged Kosovo Serbs to "leave the past and think about the future".[^cite_note-Pekusic-93]



Vučić (left), [Donald Trump](4848272.md), President of the United States (middle), and [Avdullah Hoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avdullah_Hoti), [Prime Minister of Kosovo](1260764.md) (right), signing the [2020 Kosovo and Serbia economic agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_and_Serbia_economic_normalization_agreements) in the [White House](33057.md)

In 2017, Vučić criticized the EU for "hypocrisy and double standards over its very different attitude towards [separatist](286527.md) crises in [Kosovo](17391.md) and [Catalonia](6822.md)".[^cite_note-109] In September 2018 in a speech to Kosovo Serbs he stated: "[Slobodan Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87) was a great Serbian leader, he had the best intentions, but our results were far worse."[^cite_note-110] Journalists report that Vučić advocates the [partition of Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Kosovo), in what he refers to as "ethnic demarcation with Albanians".[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

On 27 May 2019, during a special session of the Serbian parliament on Kosovo, Vučić said: "We need to recognize that we have been defeated... We lost the territory."[^cite_note-SekuVaso-115] while also criticizing the 'unprincipled attitude of [great powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power)' and "no one reacting to announcements for the formation of a [Greater Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Albania)".[^cite_note-116] He stated that Serbia no longer controlled Kosovo and that a compromise was needed on the issue through a future referendum in the country.[^cite_note-SekuVaso-115] Vučić has close links to the [Serb List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serb_List_\(Kosovo\)) and he invited Kosovo Serbs to vote for them in the elections.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

On 20 January 2020, Serbia and Kosovo agreed to restore flights between their capitals for the first time in more than two decades.[^cite_note-flight1-119][^cite_note-flight2-120] The deal came after months of diplomatic talks by [Richard Grenell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Grenell), the [United States ambassador to Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_the_United_States_to_Germany), who was named special envoy for [Serbia-Kosovo relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo%E2%80%93Serbia_relations) by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) the year before.[^cite_note-flight1-119] Vučić welcomed the flights agreement and tweeted his thanks to American diplomats.[^cite_note-flight3-121]

On 4 September 2020 Serbia and Kosovo signed an [agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_and_Serbia_Economic_Normalization_agreements_\(2020\)) at the [White House](33057.md) in Washington D.C., in the presence of US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md). In addition to the economic agreement, Serbia agreed to move its Israeli embassy to [Jerusalem](16043.md) from [Tel Aviv](31453.md) starting in June 2021 and Israel and Kosovo agreed to mutually [recognise](15808914.md) each other.[^cite_note-122]

### Open Balkan



[Open Balkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Balkan) Summit

Main article: [Open Balkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Balkan)

On 10 October 2019, together with [Edi Rama](277805.md), [Prime Minister of Albania](259136.md), and [Zoran Zaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoran_Zaev), [Prime Minister of North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_North_Macedonia), Vučić signed the so-called [Mini Schengen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Schengen_Zone) (now known as Open Balkan) deal on regional economic cooperation, including on the [free movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_market) of goods, capital, services, and labor between their three countries, while they await progress on EU enlargement.[^cite_note-123] A month later, the leaders presented a set of proposals to achieve the "[four freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market)" and the first steps towards them, including the possibility to the [open border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_border) area.[^cite_note-124] In December, the three leaders also met with [Milo Đukanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo_%C4%90ukanovi%C4%87), [President of Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Montenegro), opening the possibility for the country to join the zone.[^cite_note-125] Following the 2020 Kosovo and Serbia economic agreement the two sides pledged to join the Mini Schengen Zone.[^cite_note-126]

### Relations with Croatia

Further information: [Croatia–Serbia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia%E2%80%93Serbia_relations)

In 2007 Vučić stated that the [Democratic League of Croats in Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_League_of_Croats_in_Vojvodina) is a branch of the [Croatian Democratic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Democratic_Union).[^cite_note-127] In 2008, with the establishment of the [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md), Vučić said that the goal of a [Greater Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Serbia) taking Croatian territory up to the proposed [Virovitica-Karlovac-Karlobag line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virovitica-Karlovac-Karlobag_line) "is unrealistic and silly".[^cite_note-128] The Croatian newspaper *[Jutarnji list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutarnji_list)* claimed in a reportage that none of his family members had been killed during World War II, upon which he replied that these were "brutal lies and attacks on his family".[^cite_note-Novosti-289-571605-24]

During 2015 and 2016, relations between Croatia and Serbia were further affected by to the ongoing migrant crisis, when Croatia decided to close its border with Serbia. In September 2015, Croatia barred all cargo traffic from Serbia,[^cite_note-129] due to the migrant influx coming from Serbia in a move which further eroded the fragile relations between the two countries. In response to these actions, Vučić announced that counter measures will be enacted if an agreement with Croatia is not reached.[^cite_note-130] The dispute was eventually resolved through the mediation of the [EU Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Commission), yet the relations between the two neighboring countries remain fragile.

On 31 March 2016, Vojislav Seselj, leader of the Serbian Radical Party, was acquitted of War Crime charges in the Hague Tribunal for Former Yugoslavia. The verdict has caused controversy in Croatia. Vučić distanced himself from Šešelj and his policy, but stated that the verdict should not be used as a tool for political pressure on Serbia.

On 7 April 2016, Croatia refused to endorse the EU Commission opinion to open Chapter 23, a part of Serbia's EU accession negotiations, thus effectively blocking Serbia' EU integration process. Serbia accused Croatia of obstructing its EU membership, and Vučić said that his government was: *"Stunned by Croatia's decision not to support Serbia's European path."*[^cite_note-131] Croatia has not agreed for Serbia to open negotiations of Chapter 23. On 14 April 2016, the EU Commission rejected Croatian arguments in its dispute with Serbia.[^cite_note-132]

On 23 May 2023, Vučić accused Croatia of attempting to topple the government in Serbia.[^cite_note-133]

### Relations with Russia

Further information: [Russia–Serbia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Serbia_relations)



President Vučić with Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), Belgrade

Vučić has maintained traditional good relations between Serbia and Russia, and his government refused to enact sanctions on Russia, following the start of the [Russo-Ukrainian war](42085878.md). Vučić has repeatedly announced that Serbia will remain committed to its European integration, but also maintain historic relations with Russia. "We have proven our sincere and friendly attitude to Russia by being one of the European countries that refused to impose sanctions on Russia," Vučić said after meeting with Russian prime minister [Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev). "Serbia will continue pursuing this policy in the future."[^cite_note-134]

During Vučić's mandate, Serbia has continued to expand its economic ties with Russia, especially by increasing Serbian exports to Russia. In early 2016, after a meeting with the Russian deputy prime minister [Dmitry Rogozin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Rogozin), Vučić announced the possibility of Serbia boosting its military cooperation with Russia by purchasing Russian missile systems.[^cite_note-135]

In December 2017, Vučić made an official visit to the Russian Federation for the first time as the president of Serbia.[^cite_note-RussiaDEC17-136] He expressed his gratefulness to Russia for protecting Serbian national interests, and stated that: "Serbia will never impose sanctions on the Russian Federation (in relation to the [international sanctions during the Russo-Ukrainian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War))".[^cite_note-RussiaDEC17-136] During his visit, he focused on strengthening cooperation in the field of military industry and energy.[^cite_note-RussiaDEC17-136]

On 25 February 2022, Vučić said Serbia would not impose sanctions against Russia during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

On 24 February 2024, Croatian foreign minister [Gordan Grlić-Radman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordan_Grli%C4%87-Radman) described Vučić as a Russian "satellite" in the Balkans during an interview on [N1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_\(TV_channel\)), adding that Vucic must decide whether to side with Russia or the European Union "because it is impossible and uncomfortable sitting on two chairs at the same time." Vučić denied the allegation, saying that Grlić-Radman "brutally interferes in the internal affairs of Serbia, but as usual he lies and insults the Serbian people and threatens its citizens." The [Serbian foreign ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Serbia\)) issued a note of protest saying that it expected Croatian officials to "refrain from statements that represent interference in the internal affairs of Serbia and will lead a policy of reconciliation and good-neighborly relations between the two states."[^cite_note-139]



Vučić with Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), on 2 September 2025

On 24 February 2025, Vučić took responsibility and apologised after Serbia voted in favour of a [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) resolution calling Russia an aggressor state over its invasion of Ukraine. Vučić attributed Serbia's vote to his being "tired" and having "too many things (to deal with)", adding that Serbia was originally slated to abstain.[^cite_note-140]

On 11 June 2025, Vučić made his first visit to Ukraine since the Russian invasion to attend a summit in [Odesa](55545.md) hosted by Ukrainian president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md). During the visit, Vučić said he supported Ukraine's territorial integrity.[^cite_note-141]

### Relations with Israel

Further information: [Israel–Serbia relations](20081805.md)



President Aleksandar Vučić with Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) during the [2018 Moscow Victory Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade)

Following the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), Vučić condemned the "horrific attacks", adding that "the Jewish people have endured a history of suffering and Israel deserves to live in peace and security".[^cite_note-142] Media reports have suggested Serbia is a major [supplier of weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_military_aid_to_Israel_during_the_Gaza_war) and ammunition to Israel during the [Gaza war](74998519.md), with at least two major shipments sent during the conflict.[^cite_note-143] Serbia's state-owned arms manufacturer, [Yugoimport SDPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoimport_SDPR), exported €42.3 million worth of arms and ammunition to Israel during 2024, mostly [155 mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/155_mm_caliber) artillery shells.[^cite_note-144] In September 2024, Israeli President [Isaac Herzog](4645588.md) visited Sebia, meeting with Vučić in Belgrade.[^cite_note-145]

In June 2025, due to the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War), Vučić announced halt on exporting arms to Israel. This came after Iran warned that providing weapons to Israel would be considered an "act of aggression" against Iran.[^cite_note-146]

In April 2026, *BIRN* revealed that Serbia was planning to open a drone factory in cooperation with Israeli company [Elbit Systems](1341669.md), a company which was criticised for taking part in the Gaza war. Vučić announced the opening of the factory in March, without mentioning the name of the company.[^cite_note-147] The factory was opened in September 2026.[^cite_note-148]

### Relations with the United States

Aleksandar Vučić and [Marco Rubio](5502549.md), [New York City](645042.md), [New York](8210131.md), 23 September 2025

In July 2017 Vučić visited the United States and met with U.S. vice president [Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence), where they discussed U.S. support for Serbia's efforts to join the European Union, the need for continued reforms, and further progress in normalizing the relationship with Kosovo.[^cite_note-149] Referencing the proposed [land swap arrangement between Serbia and Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo%E2%80%93Serbia_land_swap), U.S. national security advisor [John Bolton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Bolton) has said that the United States would not oppose a territorial exchange between Kosovo and Serbia to resolve their long-running dispute. ### Relations with China

Further information: [China–Serbia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Serbia_relations)

Vučić has sought closer cooperation with China.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] He has met with Chinese president [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) in 2016,[^cite_note-153] 2018,[^cite_note-154] and 2019.[^cite_note-155] After meeting with Chinese [Minister of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) [Wang Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Yi_\(politician\)), he secured Chinese help in combating the [COVID-19 pandemic in Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Serbia) through delivery of [PPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment) and [CoronaVac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoronaVac) vaccine doses,[^cite_note-156] which has contributed to Serbia leading COVID-19 vaccination rates in Europe.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] In October 2023, he signed a free-trade deal with China.[^cite_note-The_Man_Who_Remade_Serbia-20] During an interview with [China Global Television Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Global_Television_Network) in February 2024, Vucic said, "Taiwan is China — and it's up to you what, when and how you're going to do it — full stop".[^cite_note-159]

of freedom of expression.[^cite_note-2014_Report-164] Report said there was a growing trend of self-censorship which combined with undue influence on editorial policies.[^cite_note-2014_Report-164] Reports published in 2016 and 2018 stated that no progress was made to improve conditions for the full exercise of freedom of expression.[^cite_note-2016_Report-165][^cite_note-2018_Report-166] In July 2016, the ruling party organized an exhibition of government-critical press articles and social media posts, labeled as 'lies', saying that they wanted to document wrongful attacks and to prove there is no official censorship.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] In 2017, Freedom House reported that Serbia posted one of the largest single-year declines in press freedom among all the countries and territories. Also, they emphasized that Vučić had sought to squeeze critical media out of the market and discredit the few journalists with the funds and fortitude to keep working.[^cite_note-170] Some commentators have described that Vučić built a [cult of personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_of_personality), with the significant role of mass media.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174][^cite_note-balkaneu-175][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-RFE-177]



Vučić with journalists during [European People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party) Congress in Helsinki (2018)

Observers described that during the campaign for the [2017 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Serbian_presidential_election), Vučić had ten times more airtime on national broadcasters than all other candidates combined and mainstream media under Vučić's control have been demonizing most of the opposition presidential candidates, without giving them the opportunity to respond.[^cite_note-AP-178][^cite_note-RWB-179] Organizations that observed the elections emphasized that the presence of Vučić in newspaper and the electronic media during the presidential campaign was disproportionate, adding that media have lost their critical role and that they have become a means of political propaganda.[^cite_note-Final_Report-180] The OSCE Report explains that general reluctance of media to report critically on or to challenge the governing authorities, significantly reduced the amount of impartial information available to voters.[^cite_note-OSCE-181] They also mentioned that the government used public resources to support Vučić.[^cite_note-OSCE-181] Amnesty International and [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) reported harassment and physical assaults on journalists during the presidential inauguration ceremony, after Vučić won the elections.[^cite_note-Amnesty-162][^cite_note-182]

> Within five years of President Aleksandar Vučić in effect governing the country, Serbia has become a place where practicing journalism is neither safe nor supported by the state. The number of attacks on media is on the rise, including death threats, and inflammatory rhetoric targeting journalists is increasingly coming from the governing officials.

— [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md)[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

In 2018, [International Research & Exchanges Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Research_&_Exchanges_Board) described the situation in the media in Serbia as the worst in recent history, and that [Media Sustainability Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_Sustainability_Index) dropped because the most polarized media in almost 20 years, an increase in [fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news) and editorial pressure on media.[^cite_note-IREX-185] They also pointed out that the judiciary responds promptly only in cases in which the media allegedly violates the rights of authorities and ruling parties.[^cite_note-IREX-185] The increased government control of the media comes as Serbian journalists face more political pressure and intimidation, in 2018 *the Independent Association of Serbian Journalists* recorded the highest number of attacks against journalists in decade.[^cite_note-186] According to Serbian investigative journalism portal *Crime and Corruption Reporting Network*, more than 700 fake news were published on the front pages of pro-government tabloids during 2018.[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-KRIK-188] Many of them were about alleged attacks on Vućić and attempts of [coups](25914154.md), as well as messages of support to him by [Vladimir Putin](32817.md).[^cite_note-KRIK-188] The bestselling newspaper in Serbia is the pro-government tabloid *[Informer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informer_\(newspaper\))*, which most often presents Vučić as a powerful person under constant attack, and also has [anti-European](10037.md) content and [pro-war rhetoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-war_rhetoric).[^cite_note-JovanovicSrdjanMladenov-18][^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190] After Vučić was hospitalized for [cardiovascular problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease) in November 2019, his associates and pro-regime media accused the journalists of worsening the president's health by asking questions about alleged corruption by government ministers.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] The [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) warned that the investigative outlet was target of [smear campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smear_campaign) from the state after they caught Vučić's son with members of crime groups, while the [Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_Crime_and_Corruption_Reporting_Project) reported that Vučić "pledges to fight the lies".[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194] In early November 2021, seven [US Congressman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Congressman) have accused Vučić of deepening corruption and putting pressure on the media.[^cite_note-195]

#### Internet surveillance

Since Vučić's party came to power, Serbia has seen a surge of [internet trolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_trolls) and pages on social networks praising the government and attacking its critics, free media and the opposition in general.[^cite_note-Breitbart-196] That includes a handful of dedicated employees running fake accounts, but also the Facebook page associated with a Serbian franchise of the far-right [Breitbart News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breitbart_News) website.[^cite_note-trolls-197][^cite_note-Breitbart-196] On 26 March 2020, Twitter announced that they had shut down a network of 8,500 [spam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_spam) accounts that worked in concert to write 43 million tweets praising president Vučić and his party, boosting Vučić-aligned content to increase its visibility and popularity, and attacking his political opponents.[^cite_note-Stanford-198]

## Criticism and controversies

### Public profile



Vučić at the EPP Congress Madrid (2015)

Some commentators have compared Vučić to other strongmen in European politics and accused him of being an autocrat.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200]

### Greater Serbia

Until 2008, Vučić publicly supported the [Greater Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Serbia) ideology, which he testified was envisaged as extending to a western border running along the [Virovitica–Karlovac–Karlobag line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virovitica%E2%80%93Karlovac%E2%80%93Karlobag_line).[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202] In 1995, during the [Croatian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_War_of_Independence), Vučić said in [Glina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glina,_Croatia) (which was at the time [controlled by Serb rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Serbian_Krajina)) that 'Serbian Krajina' and Glina would never be Croatian, [Banovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banovina_\(region\)) would never be returned to Croatia, and that if [Serbian Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Radical_Party) had won elections, Serbs would have lived in Greater Serbia.[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-index.hr-204] In another speech from the early 2000s, Vučić called [Karlobag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlobag), [Ogulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogulin), [Karlovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovac) and [Virovitica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virovitica) "Serbian towns", stated that "they \[SRS' critics] rejoice that Ustaše (referring to Croats) have occupied Serbian lands and want to convince us Serbian radicals that it wasn't Serbian, that we were saying nonsenses. (...) We want what's ours, Serbian."[^cite_note-index.hr-204] After split from the Serbian Radical Party and creation of the [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md), Vučić said he no longer supports the Greater Serbia ideology.[^cite_note-205]

On 1 September 2020, Montenegrin President [Milo Đukanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo_%C4%90ukanovi%C4%87) accused Vučić and [Belgrade-based media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_Serbia) of interfering in the internal politics of [Montenegro](20760.md), as well of allegedly trying to revive a "Greater Serbia policy".[^cite_note-206]

### Srebrenica massacre and Ratko Mladić



Vučić attending the commemoration of the 20th anniversary of the [Srebrenica massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre), 11 July 2015

Only a few days after more than 8,000 Muslim [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks) were killed by the [Army of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska) (VRS) and paramilitary groups from Serbia in the [Srebrenica massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre), Vučić said on 20 July 1995 in the [National Assembly](619753.md) in a comment on the [NATO bombing campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Deliberate_Force) against the VRS positions that "for every Serb killed, we will kill 100 Muslims".[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208][^cite_note-Independent-7][^cite_note-209] In 2015, he said that his statement from 1995 was "taken out of context" and "that was not the essence of that sentence."[^cite_note-210]

Before leaving the Radical Party of Vojislav Šešelj, Vučić openly and publicly celebrated and called for the protection of [Ratko Mladić](22742941.md), a military officer [convicted](32307769.md) of committing war crimes, crimes against humanity, and genocide. In 2007, while Mladić was still at large in Serbia, Vučić distributed posters with the declaration "Safe House for General Mladić". During a parliament session he stated that the Serbian Parliament will always protect and be a safe house for the general and that any house in Serbia that bears the last name of Vučić will protect and shelter Mladić.[^cite_note-pressonline.rs-211]

In the same year, Vučić organized a street protest during which street signs with the name of the assassinated pro-western Serbian PM were replaced with Ratko Mladić Boulevard street signs.[^cite_note-pressonline.rs-211] This vandalism has become a frequent activity of Serbian ultra-right groups on the anniversary of the [Zoran Đinđić assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoran_%C4%90in%C4%91i%C4%87_assassination).[^cite_note-212]

Vučić also participated in protests against the arrests of war criminals convicted later, including [Veselin Šljivančanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veselin_%C5%A0ljivan%C4%8Danin), [Radovan Karadžić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radovan_Karad%C5%BEi%C4%87), and [Vojislav Šešelj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojislav_%C5%A0e%C5%A1elj), who was president of his party.[^cite_note-Sljivancanin-213][^cite_note-Karadzic-214][^cite_note-215]

In 2024, Vučić condemned the [UN General Assembly](31957.md)'s decision to adopt an annual commemoration of the [Srebrenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre) massacre.[^cite_note-216]

### Račak massacre

In December 2019, Vučić generated controversy for referring to the 1999 [Račak massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C4%8Dak_massacre) of 45 Albanian civilians as "fabricated", and accusing the former head of the OSCE Kosovo Verification Mission [William G. Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_G._Walker) of faking the evidence for the killings.[^cite_note-\:0-217][^cite_note-218] His remarks came after the conviction of Kosovo Serb MP [Ivan Todosijevic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Todosijevi%C4%87) by the Pristina Basic Court of incitement to ethnic, racial or religious intolerance for claiming that the massacre was staged.[^cite_note-\:0-217]

The statement was condemned by Kosovo president [Hashim Thaçi](3626980.md), as well as prime minister [Ramush Haradinaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramush_Haradinaj), and Minister for Foreign affairs, [Behgjet Pacolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behgjet_Pacolli).[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220] The statement was also condemned by the European Union.[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222] Lawyer Tome Gashi filed a criminal complaint to Kosovo's Special Prosecution, alleging Vučić had "intentionally incited and spread hatred, division and intolerance between ethnic groups, Serbs and Albanians in Kosovo".[^cite_note-\:0-217][^cite_note-223] Upon the 25th anniversary of the massacre in 2024, Kosovo president [Vjosa Osmani](61979540.md) again criticized Vučić for denying the killings,[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225] with Prime Minister [Albin Kurti](4774301.md) criticizing Vučić for not apologizing for the massacre, either as Milosevic's minister of information or as the current president of Serbia.[^cite_note-226]

### Slavko Ćuruvija

It was during Vučić's term as the Minister of Information that [Slavko Ćuruvija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavko_%C4%86uruvija), a prominent journalist who reported on the [Kosovo War](16760.md), was murdered in a state-sponsored assassination.[^cite_note-227][^cite_note-228] In 1999, before the assassination took place, Vučić gave a front page interview to the tabloid *Argument* in which he stated "I will wreak vengeance upon Slavko Ćuruvija for all the lies published in *[Dnevni telegraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnevni_telegraf)*" (Ćuruvija's paper).[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231] In 2014, Vučić apologized to the Ćuruvija family for having waited so long to bring the perpetrators to justice, and thanked everyone who was involved in solving the case for their work.[^cite_note-232] [Branka Prpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branka_Prpa), Ćuruvija's [common-law spouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common-law_marriage), said Vučić participated in the murder and that he is the creator of the practice of persecution of journalists.[^cite_note-233]

### Remarks on journalists

At an SNS gathering in [Bor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bor,_Serbia) on 2 March 2025, Vučić referred to a female reporter from the state broadcaster [RTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio-televizija_Srbije) who covered the [Serbian anti-corruption protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_anti-corruption_protests) since 2024 as an "imbecile". Vučić later apologised for the remarks, which were condemned by RTS.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235]

## Personal life



[Tamara Đukanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_Vu%C4%8Di%C4%87), who married Vučić in 2013

On 27 July 1997, Vučić married Ksenija Janković, a journalist at Radio Index and *Srpska reč*. The couple had two children before divorcing: Danilo (born 1 December 1998)[^cite_note-236] and Milica.[^cite_note-237] Janković died on 29 January 2022.[^cite_note-238] At some points, Vučić married [Tamara Đukanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_%C4%90ukanovi%C4%87), a diplomat with the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Serbia\)).[^cite_note-239] On 9 June 2017, a week after Vučić took the presidential office, his wife gave birth to a son.[^cite_note-240]

Apart from Serbian, he is fluent in Russian and English, with a basic knowledge of French, and is studying German.[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242] He learned Russian in a Belgrade high school and every second day of the week took a Russian language course to improve his knowledge.[^cite_note-243]

During the opposition period, he has frequently appeared in popular TV shows.[^cite_note-vreme-biografija-244] In 2006, Vučić became the winner of the first season of the Serbian version of *[The Pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pyramid_\(Croatian_TV_series\))*, a talk show with a competitive element broadcast on [Pink TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_TV_\(Serbia\)).[^cite_note-245] He was the first politician who participated in the humanitarian dance contest *Plesom do snova* in 2009 and the first politician to guest-star on a [late-night talk show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late-night_talk_show) *[Veče sa Ivanom Ivanovićem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ve%C4%8De_sa_Ivanom_Ivanovi%C4%87em)* in 2010.[^cite_note-vreme-biografija-244][^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247] He also was a guest judge in one episode of the third season of *[Zvezde Granda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvezde_Granda)*.[^cite_note-vreme-biografija-244][^cite_note-248]

On 15 November 2019, he was hospitalized at a military hospital in Belgrade due to apparent "cardiovascular issues". Three days later, it was reported that he was released. Some, including his media advisor and the deputy mayor of Belgrade, have said that his health problems were in part due to pressure from journalists. Vučić explicitly denied this at a media conference shortly after his hospital stay, stating that these health issues were chronic in nature.[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250]

In July 2020, Vučić became a student at Belgrade's College of Sports and Health, with the goal to become a basketball trainer for juniors after he ended his political career.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252] Some Serbian journalists have reported that a mandatory condition for entering the college was active participation in sports for three years, which was removed from the official website shortly after Vučić's enrollment.[^cite_note-253]
