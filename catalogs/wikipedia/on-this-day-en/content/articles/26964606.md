**Austria**,[^cite_note-18] formally the **Republic of Austria**,[^cite_note-19] is a [landlocked country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country) in [Central Europe](5188.md), lying in the [Eastern Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Alps).[^cite_note-formal_name-20] It is a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) of nine [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_states_of_Austria), of which the capital [Vienna](55866.md) is the [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_in_Austria) and state. Austria is bordered by [Germany](11867.md) to the northwest, the [Czech Republic](5321.md) to the north, [Slovakia](26830.md) to the northeast, [Hungary](13275.md) to the east, [Slovenia](27338.md) and [Italy](14532.md) to the south, and [Switzerland](26748.md) and [Liechtenstein](17810.md) to the west. The country occupies an area of 83,879 km<sup>2</sup> (32,386 sq mi) and has [a population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrians) of about 9.2 million.[^cite_note-statistik-population-13]

The area of today's Austria has been inhabited since at least the [Paleolithic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic). Around 400 BC, it was inhabited by the [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) and then annexed by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in the late 1st century BC. [Christianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization) in the region began in the 4th and 5th centuries, during the late [Roman period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire), followed by the arrival of numerous [Germanic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_tribes) during the [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period).[^cite_note-21]

Austria as a state emerged from the remnants of the [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_Pannonia) and [Hungarian March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_March) at the end of the [first millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_millennium), first as a [frontier march](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Austria) of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). It then developed into a [Duchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Austria) in 1156, and was made an [Archduchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduchy_of_Austria) in 1453. Being the heartland of the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) since the late 13th century, Austria was a major imperial power in Central Europe for centuries and, from the 16th century, Vienna also served as the Holy Roman Empire's administrative capital.[^cite_note-22] The Holy Roman Empire was [dissolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Holy_Roman_Empire) in 1806, two years after Austria had established [its own empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire), which became a [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power) and one of the largest states in Europe. The empire's defeat in wars and the loss of territories in the 1860s paved the way for the [establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Compromise_of_1867) of [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) in 1867.[^cite_note-23]

After the [assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand) in 1914, Emperor [Franz Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I_of_Austria) declared war on [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia), which rapidly escalated into [World War I](4764461.md). The empire's defeat and subsequent collapse led to the proclamation of the [Republic of German-Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_German-Austria) in 1918 and the [First Austrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Austrian_Republic) in 1919. During the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period), anti-parliamentarian sentiments culminated in the [formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Civil_War) of [an Austrofascist dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_State_of_Austria) under [Engelbert Dollfuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelbert_Dollfuss) in 1934. A year before the outbreak of [World War II](32927.md), Austria [was annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss) into [Nazi Germany](21212.md) by [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md), and it became [a sub-national division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_within_Nazi_Germany). After [its liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_offensive) in 1945 and a decade of [Allied occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Austria), the country [regained its sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_State_Treaty) and declared [its perpetual neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Neutrality) in 1955.

Austria is a [semi-presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential)[^cite_note-system-10] representative democracy with a popularly elected [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Austria) as head of state and a [chancellor](60582.md) as head of government and chief executive. Austria has the 13th highest [nominal GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)_per_capita) with high standards of living. The country has been a member of the [United Nations](31769.md) since 1955[^cite_note-24] and of the [European Union](9317.md) since 1995.[^cite_note-CIA-25] It hosts the [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md) (OSCE) and the [Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_the_Petroleum_Exporting_Countries) (OPEC) and is a founding member of the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development) (OECD) and [Interpol](14338129.md).[^cite_note-OECD-26] It also signed the [Schengen Agreement](179592.md) in 1995,[^cite_note-Schengen-27] and adopted the [euro](9472.md) currency in 1999.[^cite_note-28]

## Etymology

Main article: [Name of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Austria)

The native name for Austria, *Österreich*, derives from the [Old High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_High_German) *Ostarrîchi*, which meant "eastern realm", can be also translated as "eastern empire" or "eastern kingdom", and which first appeared in the "Ostarrîchi document" of 996.[^cite_note-University_of_Klagenfurt-29][^cite_note-30] This word is probably a translation of [Medieval Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Latin) *[Marchia orientalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marchia_orientalis_\(Austria\))* into a local (Bavarian) dialect.

Austria was a prefecture of Bavaria created in 976. The word "Austria" is a Latinisation of the German name and was first recorded in the 12th century.[^cite_note-31] At the time, the Danube basin of Austria ([Upper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Austria) and [Lower Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Austria)) was the easternmost extent of Bavaria.

## History

Main article: [History of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Austria)

### Prehistory and antiquity



[Venus of Willendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_of_Willendorf), 28,000 to 25,000 BC, at the [Museum of Natural History Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturhistorisches_Museum)

During the pre-Roman era, the region of what is now Austria was settled by various [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celt) tribes and had served as the core of the [Hallstatt culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallstatt_culture) by the 6th century BC.[^cite_note-32] The city of [Hallstatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallstatt) has the oldest archaeological evidence of the Celts in Europe.[^cite_note-33]

The Celtic [Kingdom of Noricum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Noricum) that included most of modern Austria and parts of modern Slovenia was conquered by the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in 16 BC and made a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) called [Noricum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noricum) which lasted until 476.[^cite_note-34] The regions of today's Austria which were not located within the province of Noricum were divided between the Roman provinces of [Pannonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia), which encompassed parts of eastern Austria, and [Raetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raetia), which encompassed the areas of present-day [Vorarlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorarlberg) and [Tyrol](148994.md).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

Present-day Petronell-[Carnuntum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnuntum) in eastern Austria was an important army camp turned capital city in what became known as the [Pannonia Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia_Superior). Carnuntum was home to 50,000 people for nearly 400 years.[^cite_note-Carnuntum_Tourism-37]



The [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in the time of [Hadrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrian) (ruled 117–138 AD), with the [imperial province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_province) of **[Noricum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noricum)** covering most of today's Austria, and **[Raetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raetia)** and **[Pannonia Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia_Superior)** the west and eastern parts of today's Austria

### Middle Ages

After the [fall of the Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Roman_Empire), the area was first invaded by the Germanic [Rugii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugii) which made this region part of their "[Rugiland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugiland)".[^cite_note-38] In 487, most of modern Austria was conquered by [Odoacer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoacer), a [barbarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbarian_kingdoms) soldier and statesman from the [Middle Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Danube), which incorporated most of today's Austria in his [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(476-493\)). By 493, it was conquered by the [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths) which created their own kingdom, the [Ostrogothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom).[^cite_note-39] Following the Kingdom's fall the area was invaded by the [Alemanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni), [Baiuvarii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiuvarii), [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs), and [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Avars).[^cite_note-johnson_19-40][^cite_note-41]

After [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne), king of the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks), defeated the Avars in the late eighth century, he established [frontier marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_\(territory\)#Holy_Roman_Empire) that became centres of [Bavarian](3764.md) settlement and Christianisation, expanding German influence in the [Danube Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Valley) and the [Alps](981.md).[^cite_note-johnson_19-40] In 976, [Emperor Otto II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Otto_II) appointed [Leopold I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_I,_Margrave_of_Austria) of the [House of Babenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Babenberg) as margrave of the *[marchia Orientalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_Austria)*, a territory within the [German kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_kingdom) that would form the core of modern Austria.[^cite_note-johnson_20-42]

The earliest written record of the name Austria dates to 996, where it appears as *[Ostarrîchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostarr%C3%AEchi)* in reference to the territory of the Babenberg March.[^cite_note-johnson_20-42] In 1156, the [Privilegium Minus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privilegium_Minus) elevated Austria to the status of a duchy. In 1192, the Babenbergs also acquired the Duchy of [Styria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styria_\(duchy\)). With the death of [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II_of_Austria) in 1246, the line of the Babenbergs was extinguished.[^cite_note-johnson_21-43]

As a result, [Ottokar II of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottokar_II_of_Bohemia) effectively assumed control of the duchies of Austria, Styria, and [Carinthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthia_\(duchy\)).[^cite_note-johnson_21-43] His reign came to an end with his defeat at [Dürnkrut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCrnkrut) at the hands of [Rudolph I of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolph_I_of_Germany) in 1278.[^cite_note-44] Thereafter, until World War I, Austria's history was largely that of its ruling dynasty, the [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburgs).



Habsburg (orange), Luxembourg (violet) and Wittelsbach (green) dominions within the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), 14th century

In the 14th and 15th centuries, the Habsburgs began to expand [their domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) by acquiring additional provinces around the Duchy of Austria. In 1438, Duke [Albert V of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_V_of_Austria) was chosen as the successor to his father-in-law, [Emperor Sigismund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Sigismund). Although Albert himself only reigned for a year, henceforth every emperor of the Holy Roman Empire was a Habsburg, with only one exception.

The Habsburgs also began to accumulate territory far from the hereditary lands. In 1477, Archduke [Maximilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), only son of Emperor [Frederick III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), married the heiress Maria of [Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Burgundy), thus acquiring most of the Netherlands for the family.[^cite_note-johnson_25-45][^cite_note-brook_11-46] In 1496, his son [Philip the Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_the_Fair) married [Joanna the Mad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_the_Mad), the heiress of [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) and [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon), thus acquiring Spain and its Italian, African, Asian, and [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World) appendages for the Habsburgs.[^cite_note-johnson_25-45][^cite_note-brook_11-46]

In 1526, following the [Battle of Mohács](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moh%C3%A1cs), [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia) and the part of Hungary not occupied by the Ottomans came under Austrian rule.[^cite_note-47] [Ottoman expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Hungarian_Wars) into Hungary led to [frequent conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Habsburg_wars) between the two empires, particularly evident in the [Long War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_War_\(Ottoman_wars\)) of 1593 to 1606. The Turks made incursions into [Styria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styria) nearly 20 times,[^cite_note-48] of which some are cited as "burning, pillaging, and taking thousands of slaves".[^cite_note-49] In late September 1529, [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent) launched the first [siege of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Vienna_\(1529\)), which unsuccessfully ended, according to Ottoman historians, with the snowfalls of an early beginning winter.

### 17th and 18th centuries



The [Battle of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vienna) in 1683 broke the advance of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) into Europe.

During the long reign of [Leopold I, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) following the successful [defence of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vienna) against the Turks in 1683, under the command of the King of Poland [John III Sobieski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_III_Sobieski),[^cite_note-50] the [Great Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Turkish_War) resulted in most of Hungary being controlled by Austria. This arrangement was formalized in the [Treaty of Karlowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Karlowitz) in 1699.

[Charles VI, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) relinquished many of the gains the empire made in the previous years. He enjoyed the imminent extinction of the [House of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg). Charles VI was willing to offer concrete advantages in territory and authority in exchange for recognition of the [Pragmatic Sanction of 1713](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatic_Sanction_of_1713). Therefore, his daughter [Maria Theresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa) was recognized as his heir. With the rise of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia), the [Austria–Prussia rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria%E2%80%93Prussia_rivalry) began in Germany. Austria participated, together with Prussia and Russia, in the first and the third of the three [Partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) in 1772 and 1795 respectively.

### 19th century

See also: [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) and [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary)



The [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) met in 1814–15. The objective of the Congress was to settle the many issues arising from the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars), the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), and the dissolution of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire).

Austria engaged in war with [Revolutionary France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_France), as part of the First and Second Coalitions, and subsequently in the Napoleonic Wars. From 1792 to 1801, the Austrians had suffered 754,700 casualties.[^cite_note-51] The Hapsburg rulers of Austria and the Holy Roman Empire suffered successive defeats at the hands of [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) Bonaparte. To protect their status as an imperial dynasty after Napoleon proclaimed the French Empire, the [Empire of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) was founded in 1804,[^cite_note-52] and to prevent Napoleon from claiming their previous Imperial throne, the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) was abolished 1806. In 1814, Austria was part of the coalition of Allied forces that invaded France and brought to an end the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars).

The Austrian Empire emerged from the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) in 1815 as one of the continent's four dominant powers and a recognized [great power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_power). The same year, the [German Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Confederation) (*Deutscher Bund*) was founded under the presidency of Austria. Because of unsolved social, political, and national conflicts, the German lands were shaken by the [1848 revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1848_revolutions) aiming to create a unified Germany.[^cite_note-johnson_36-53]



Map of the [German Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Confederation) with its 39-member sovereign states

The possibilities for a unified Germany were: a [Greater Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleindeutschland_and_Gro%C3%9Fdeutschland) or a [Greater Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Greater_Austria) or a German Confederation without Austria at all. In 1864, Austria and Prussia fought together against Denmark and secured the independence from Denmark of the duchies of [Schleswig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schleswig) and [Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holstein). As they could not agree on how the two duchies should be administered, though they fought the [Austro-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Prussian_War) in 1866. Defeated by Prussia in the [Battle of Königgrätz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_K%C3%B6niggr%C3%A4tz),[^cite_note-johnson_36-53] Austria had to leave the German Confederation and no longer took part in German politics.[^cite_note-johnson_55-54][^cite_note-55] As Austria's Habsburg monarchy was not willing to join its German-speaking territory to the [German Empire of 1871](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany), and relinquish its non-German-speaking territories, the crown of the newly formed empire was offered to the Prussian King [Friedrich Wilhelm IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_IV).

To settle issues lingering from the suppressed [Hungarian Revolution of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1848), the [Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Compromise_of_1867), the *Ausgleich*, provided for a dual sovereignty, the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) and the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), under [Franz Joseph I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I_of_Austria).[^cite_note-56] The Austrian-Hungarian rule of this diverse empire included various groups, including Germans, Hungarians, Croats, Czechs, Poles, [Rusyns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyns), Serbs, Slovaks, Slovenes, and Ukrainians, as well as large Italian and Romanian communities.

As a result, ruling Austria-Hungary became increasingly difficult in an age of emerging nationalist movements, requiring considerable reliance on an expanded secret police. Yet, the government of Austria tried its best to be accommodating in some respects: for example, the *Reichsgesetzblatt*, publishing the laws and ordinances of [Cisleithania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisleithania), was issued in eight languages; and all national groups were entitled to schools in their own language and to the use of their mother tongue at state offices.



An ethnic map of [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), 1910

Many Austrian Germans of all different social circles such as [Georg Ritter von Schönerer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Ritter_von_Sch%C3%B6nerer) promoted strong [pan-Germanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nationalism_in_Austria) in the hope of reinforcing an ethnic German identity amongst Austrian Germans and the annexation of Austria to Germany (*[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss)*).[^cite_note-DPSO-57] Some Austrians such as [Karl Lueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Lueger) also used pan-Germanism as a form of populism to further their own political goals. Although Bismarck's policies excluded Austria and the German Austrians from Germany, many Austrian pan-Germans idolized him and wore blue cornflowers, known to be the favourite flower of German Emperor [William I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I,_German_Emperor), in their buttonholes, along with cockades in the German national colours (black, red, and yellow), although they were both temporarily banned in Austrian schools, as a way to show discontent towards the multi-ethnic empire.[^cite_note-58]

Austria's exclusion from Germany caused many Austrians a problem with their national identity and prompted the Social Democratic Leader [Otto Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Bauer) to state that it was "the conflict between our Austrian and German character".[^cite_note-59] The Austro-Hungarian Empire caused ethnic tension between the German Austrians and the other ethnic groups. Many Austrians, especially those involved with the pan-German movements, desired a reinforcement of an ethnic German identity and hoped that the empire would collapse, which would allow an annexation of Austria by Germany.[^cite_note-60]

Many Austrian pan-German nationalists protested passionately against minister-president [Kasimir Count Badeni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_Kasimir_Felix_Badeni)'s language decree of 1897, which made German and Czech co-official languages in Bohemia and required new government officials to be fluent in both languages. This meant in practice that the civil service would almost exclusively hire Czechs because most middle-class Czechs spoke German but not the other way around. The support of [ultramontane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultramontanism) Catholic politicians and clergy for this reform triggered the launch of the *[Away from Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_from_Rome)* movement, which was initiated by supporters of Schönerer and called on "German" Christians to leave the Roman Catholic Church.[^cite_note-61]

### Early 20th century

See also: [Republic of German-Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_German-Austria) and [First Austrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Austrian_Republic)

As the [Second Constitutional Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Constitutional_Era) began in the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), Austria-Hungary took the opportunity to annex [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_in_Austria-Hungary) in 1908.[^cite_note-62] The [assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria) of Archduke [Franz Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria) in [Sarajevo](26786.md) in 1914 by Bosnian Serb [Gavrilo Princip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavrilo_Princip)[^cite_note-63] was used by leading Austrian politicians and generals to persuade the emperor to declare war on Serbia, thereby risking and prompting the outbreak of World War I, which eventually led to the dissolution of the Austro-Hungarian Empire. Over one million Austro-Hungarian soldiers died in World War I.[^cite_note-64]



German-speaking provinces claimed by [German-Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-Austria) in 1918. The border of the subsequent Second Republic of Austria is outlined in red.

On 21 October 1918, the elected German members of the *Reichsrat* (parliament of Imperial Austria) met in Vienna as the Provisional National Assembly for German Austria (*Provisorische Nationalversammlung für Deutschösterreich*). On 30 October the assembly founded the [Republic of German-Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_German-Austria) by appointing a government, called *Staatsrat*. This new government was invited by the Emperor to take part in the decision on the planned armistice with Italy but refrained from this business.[^cite_note-65]

This left the responsibility for the end of the war, on 3 November 1918, solely to the emperor and his government. On 11 November, the emperor, advised by ministers of the old and the new governments, declared he would not take part in state business any more; on 12 November, German-Austria, by law, declared itself to be a democratic republic and part of the new German republic. The constitution, renaming the *Staatsrat* as *Bundesregierung* (federal government) and *Nationalversammlung* as *Nationalrat* (national council) was passed on 10 November 1920.[^cite_note-66]

The [Treaty of Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain) of 1919 (for Hungary the [Treaty of Trianon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Trianon) of 1920) confirmed and consolidated the new order of Central Europe which to a great extent had been established in November 1918, creating new states and altering others. The German-speaking parts of Austria which had been part of Austria-Hungary were reduced to a rump state named the Republic of German-Austria (German: *Republik Deutschösterreich*), though excluding the predominantly German-speaking [South Tyrol](169611.md).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] The desire for the annexation of Austria to Germany was a popular opinion shared by all social circles in both Austria and Germany.[^cite_note-70] On 12 November, German-Austria was declared a republic, and named Social Democrat [Karl Renner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Renner) as provisional chancellor. On the same day it drafted a provisional constitution that stated that "German-Austria is a democratic republic" (Article 1) and "German-Austria is an integral part of the German Reich" (Article 2).[^cite_note-71] The Treaty of Saint Germain and the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) explicitly forbade union between Austria and Germany.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] The treaties also forced German-Austria to rename itself as "Republic of Austria" which consequently led to the first [Austrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Austrian_Republic).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

Over three million German-speaking Austrians found themselves living outside the new Austrian Republic as minorities in the newly formed or enlarged states of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), [Yugoslavia](34244.md), [Hungary](13275.md), and Italy.[^cite_note-76] These included the provinces of [South Tyrol](169611.md), and [German Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Bohemia). The status of German Bohemia and [Sudetenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudetenland) later played a role in World War II.[^cite_note-a1-77]

The border between Austria and the [Kingdom of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) was settled with the [Carinthian Plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthian_Plebiscite) in October 1920 and allocated the major part of the territory of the former Austro-Hungarian Crownland of [Carinthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthia) to Austria. This set the border on the [Karawanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karawanks) mountain range, with many Slovenes remaining in Austria.

### Interwar period and World War II

See also: [First Austrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Austrian_Republic), [Federal State of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_State_of_Austria), [Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss), [Austria within Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_within_Nazi_Germany), and [History of the Jews in Austria § The Holocaust in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Austria#The_Holocaust_in_Austria)

After the war, inflation began to devalue the Krone, which was still Austria's currency. In the autumn of 1922, Austria was granted an international loan supervised by the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations).[^cite_note-78] The purpose of the loan was to avert bankruptcy, stabilise the currency, and improve Austria's general economic condition. The loan meant that Austria passed from an independent state to the control exercised by the League of Nations. In 1925, the *[Austrian schilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_schilling)* was introduced, replacing the Krone at a rate of 10,000\:1. Later, it was nicknamed the "Alpine dollar" due to its stability. From 1925 to 1929 the economy enjoyed a short high before nearly crashing after [Black Tuesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_crash_of_1929).

The [First Austrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Austrian_Republic) lasted until 1933, when Chancellor [Engelbert Dollfuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelbert_Dollfuss), using what he called ["self-switch-off of Parliament"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selbstausschaltung_des_Parlaments), established an autocratic regime tending towards [Italian fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fascism).[^cite_note-johnson_104-79][^cite_note-brook_269-80] The two big parties at this time, the Social Democrats and the Conservatives, had paramilitary armies;[^cite_note-Brook-Shepherd_261-81] the Social Democrats' *[Republikanischer Schutzbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republikanischer_Schutzbund)* was now declared illegal, but was still operative[^cite_note-Brook-Shepherd_261-81] as the 12–15 February 1934 [Austrian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Civil_War) broke out.[^cite_note-johnson_104-79][^cite_note-brook_269-80][^cite_note-johnson_107-82]

In February 1934, several members of the *Schutzbund* were executed,[^cite_note-83] the Social Democratic party was outlawed, and many of its members were imprisoned or emigrated.[^cite_note-johnson_107-82] On 1 May 1934, the [Austrofascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrofascism) imposed a new constitution ("Maiverfassung") which cemented Dollfuss's power, but on 25 July he was assassinated in an [Austrian Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Nazi) [coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_Putsch).[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]



[Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) speaking at [Heldenplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heldenplatz), Vienna, 1938

His successor [Kurt Schuschnigg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Schuschnigg) acknowledged the fact that Austria was a "German state" and he also believed that Austrians were "better Germans" but he wished that Austria would remain independent.[^cite_note-86] He announced a [referendum](51582.md) on 9 March 1938, to be held on 13 March, concerning Austria's independence from Germany.

#### Nazi rule

On 12 March 1938, Austrian Nazis took over the government, while German troops occupied the country, which prevented Schuschnigg's referendum from taking place.[^cite_note-johnson_112-87] On 13 March 1938, the *[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss)* (lit. 'joining'  or 'connection') of Austria was officially declared. Two days later, Austrian-born [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) announced what he called the "reunification" of his home country with the "rest of the [German Reich](21212.md)" on Vienna's [Heldenplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heldenplatz). He established a referendum which confirmed the union with Germany in April 1938.

Parliamentary elections were held in Germany (including recently annexed Austria) on 10 April 1938. They were the final elections to the Reichstag during Nazi rule, and they took the form of a single-question referendum asking whether voters approved of a single Nazi-party list for the 813-member Reichstag, as well as the recent annexation of Austria (the *Anschluss*). Jews, Roma and Sinti were not allowed to vote.[^cite_note-88] Turnout in the election was officially 99.5%, with 98.9% voting "yes". In the case of Austria, Adolf Hitler's native soil, 99.71% of an electorate of 4,484,475 officially went to the ballots, with a positive tally of 99.73 percent.[^cite_note-test-89] Although most Austrians favoured the *Anschluss*, in certain parts of Austria, the German soldiers were not always welcomed with flowers and joy, especially in Vienna, which had Austria's largest Jewish population.[^cite_note-90] Nevertheless, despite the propaganda and the manipulation and rigging which surrounded the ballot box result, there was massive genuine support for Hitler for fulfilling the *Anschluss*,[^cite_note-91] since many Germans from both Austria and Germany saw it as completing the long overdue unification of all Germans into one state.[^cite_note-92]



Austria in 1941 when it was known as the *[Ostmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostmark_\(Austria\))*

On 13 March 1938, Austria was annexed by the [Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Reich) and ceased to exist as an independent country (the *Anschluss*). The [Aryanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryanization_\(Nazism\)) of the wealth of Jewish Austrians started immediately in mid-March, with a so-called "wild" (i.e. extra-legal) phase, but it was soon structured legally and bureaucratically so the assets which Jewish citizens possessed could be stripped from them. At that time, [Adolf Eichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Eichmann), who grew up in Austria, was transferred to Vienna and ordered to persecute the Jews. During the November pogrom in 1938 ("Reichskristallnacht"), Jews and Jewish institutions such as synagogues were subjected to violent attacks in Vienna, Klagenfurt, Linz, Graz, Salzburg, Innsbruck and several cities in Lower Austria.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] [Otto von Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Habsburg), a vehement opponent of the Nazis, the last Crown Prince of Austria-Hungary, an honorary citizen of hundreds of places in Austria and partly envisaged by Schuschnigg as a monarchical option, was in Belgium at the time. He spoke out against the Anschluss and was then wanted by the Nazi regime and his property would have been expropriated and he would have been shot immediately if he were caught.[^cite_note-98] In 1938, the Nazis renamed Austria the "[Ostmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostmark_\(Austria\))",[^cite_note-johnson_112-87] a name which it had until 1942, when it was renamed the "Alpine and Danubian Gaue" (Alpen-und Donau-Reichsgaue).[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

Though Austrians made up only 8% of the population of the Third Reich,[^cite_note-dav_art_nazi_past-101] some of the most prominent Nazis were native Austrians, including [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md), [Ernst Kaltenbrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Kaltenbrunner), [Arthur Seyss-Inquart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Seyss-Inquart), [Franz Stangl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Stangl), [Alois Brunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Brunner), [Friedrich Rainer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Rainer), and [Odilo Globocnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odilo_Globocnik),[^cite_note-102] as were over 13% of the members of the [SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel) and 40% of the staff at the Nazi [extermination camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extermination_camp).[^cite_note-dav_art_nazi_past-101] In the [Reichsgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_under_National_Socialism), besides the main camp [KZ-Mauthausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauthausen_concentration_camp), there were numerous sub-camps in all provinces where Jews and other prisoners were killed, tortured and exploited.[^cite_note-103] At this time, because the territory was outside the operational radius of Allied aircraft, the armaments industry was greatly expanded through the forced labour of concentration camp prisoners, this was especially the case with regard to the manufacture of fighter planes, tanks and missiles.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

Most of the [resistance groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_resistance) were soon crushed by the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo). While the plans of the group around [Karl Burian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Burian) to blow up the Gestapo's headquarters in Vienna were uncovered,[^cite_note-107] the important group around the later executed priest [Heinrich Maier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Maier) managed to contact the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II). This so-called Maier-Messner group was able to send the Allies information about armaments factories where [V-1 flying bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb), [V-2 rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket), [Tiger tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_I), and aircraft ([Messerschmitt Bf 109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt_Bf_109), [Messerschmitt Me 163 Komet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt_Me_163_Komet), etc.) were manufactured, information which was important to the success of [Operation Crossbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Crossbow) and [Operation Hydra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Hydra_\(1943\)), both of which were preliminary missions before the launch of [Operation Overlord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Overlord). This resistance group, which was in contact with the American secret service ([OSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Strategic_Services)), soon provided information about mass executions and [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps) such as [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp). The group's aim was to cause Nazi Germany to lose the war as quickly as possible and re-establish an independent Austria.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]



The liberation of [Mauthausen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauthausen_concentration_camp), 1945

### Allied occupation

Vienna fell on 13 April 1945, during the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [Vienna offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_offensive), just before the total collapse of the Third Reich. The invading Allied powers, in particular the Americans, planned for the supposed "Alpine Fortress Operation" of a national redoubt, that was largely to have taken place on Austrian soil in the mountains of the [Eastern Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Alps). However, it never materialised because of the rapid collapse of the Reich.

[Karl Renner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Renner) and [Adolf Schärf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Sch%C3%A4rf) ([Socialist Party of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Austria)), Leopold Kunschak ([Christian Social Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Party_\(Austria\))), and [Johann Koplenig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Koplenig) ([Communist Party of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Austria)) declared Austria's secession from the Third Reich by the Declaration of Independence on 27 April 1945 and set up a [provisional government](598010.md) in Vienna under state Chancellor Renner the same day, with the approval of the victorious [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) and backed by [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin).[^cite_note-johnson_135-111] (The date is officially named the birthday of the second republic.) At the end of April, most of western and southern Austria were still under Nazi rule. On 1 May 1945, the [Federal Constitutional Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Constitutional_Law) of 1920, which had been terminated by dictator Dollfuss on 1 May 1934, was declared valid again. The total number of [Austrian military deaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_casualties#endnote_Austria) from 1939 to 1945 was 260,000.[^cite_note-112] The total number of Jewish [Austrian Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Austria) victims was 65,000.[^cite_note-113] About 140,000 Jewish Austrians had fled from the country in 1938–39. Thousands of Austrians had taken part in serious Nazi crimes (hundreds of thousands of people died in the [Mauthausen-Gusen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauthausen_concentration_camp) alone), a fact which was officially acknowledged by Chancellor [Franz Vranitzky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Vranitzky) in 1992.



The [United Nations Office in Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_in_Vienna)

[Allied-occupied Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Austria) was after [World War II](32927.md) divided into [military occupation](816214.md) zones. Austria was governed by the [Allied Commission for Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Commission_for_Austria).[^cite_note-114] As stipulated in the [Moscow Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Declaration) of 1943 a subtle difference was seen in the treatment of Austria by the Allies.[^cite_note-johnson_135-111]

The Austrian government, consisting of Social Democrats, Conservatives, and Communists resided in [Vienna](55866.md), which was surrounded by the Soviet zone. This Austrian government was recognised by the [allies of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) in October 1945 despite concerns that [Karl Renner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Renner) could be Stalin's puppet.[^cite_note-115] On 26 July 1946 the Austrian Parliament passed its first [nationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) law and approximately 70 mining and manufacturing companies were seized by the Austrian state. The Ministry of Property Protection and Economic Planning (*Ministerium für Vermögenssicherung und Wirtschaftsplanung*) was responsible for directing the nationalized industries under the directorship of Minister [Peter Krauland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Krauland) (party ÖVP).[^cite_note-116]

### Independence

On 15 May 1955, after talks which lasted for years and were influenced by the [Cold War](325329.md) Austria regained full independence by concluding the [Austrian State Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_State_Treaty) with the allies of World War II. On 26 October 1955, all occupation troops had left and Austria declared its *permanent neutrality* by an act of parliament.[^cite_note-johnson_153-117] This day is now Austria's [National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Day), a public holiday.[^cite_note-118]

The status of [Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrol) was a lingering problem between Austria and [Italy](14532.md). To this day, there are 20 different squares in Austrian cities called "Südtiroler Platz" (South Tyrolean Square) in memory of the supposed loss of the Austrian territories. Terrorist acts by the [South Tyrolean independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tyrolean_independence_movement) have been documented in the 1950s and 1960s. A great degree of autonomy was granted to Tyrol by the Italian national government.



Austria joined the [European Union](9317.md) in 1995 and signed the [Lisbon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Treaty) in 2007.

The political system of the [Second Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Austrian_Republic) is based on the constitution of 1920 and 1929, which was reintroduced in 1945. The system came to be characterised by *[Proporz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proporz)*, whereby most posts of political importance were split proportionately between members of the [Social Democratic Party of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Austria) (SPÖ) and the [Austrian People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_People's_Party) (ÖVP).[^cite_note-119] Interest group "chambers" with mandatory membership (e.g. for workers, business people, farmers) grew to considerable importance and were usually consulted in the legislative process, so hardly any legislation was passed that did not reflect widespread consensus.[^cite_note-120]

Since 1945, governing via a single-party government has occurred twice: 1966–1970 (ÖVP) and 1970–1983 (SPÖ). During all other legislative periods, either a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition) of SPÖ and ÖVP or a "small coalition" (one of these two and a smaller party) ruled the country.

[Kurt Waldheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Waldheim), the former secretary-general of the [United Nations](31769.md), was elected President of Austria from 1986 to 1992. He had been a Wehrmacht officer in the Second World War and was [accused of war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_%E2%80%93_the_Nazis'_first_victim#Waldheim_Affair).[^cite_note-121]

Following a [referendum in 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Austrian_European_Union_membership_referendum), at which consent reached a majority of two-thirds, the country became a member of the [European Union](9317.md) on 1 January 1995.[^cite_note-122]

The major parties SPÖ and ÖVP have contrary opinions about the future status of Austria's military nonalignment: While the SPÖ in public supports a neutral role,[^cite_note-123] the ÖVP argues for stronger integration into the EU's security policy;[^cite_note-124] even a future NATO membership is not ruled out by some ÖVP politicians (ex. Werner Fasslabend (ÖVP) in 1997).[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] In reality, Austria is taking part in the EU's [Common Foreign and Security Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy), participates in [peacekeeping](84617.md) and peace creating tasks, and has become a member of [NATO](21133.md)'s "[Partnership for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_for_Peace)"; the constitution has been amended accordingly.[^cite_note-127] Since [Liechtenstein](17810.md) joined the [Schengen Area](16565139.md) in 2011, none of Austria's neighbouring countries performs border controls towards it anymore.[^cite_note-128]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Austria](67542.md)



A topographic map of Austria showing cities with over 100,000 inhabitants



A [glacial](12463.md) region in winter, close to the valley [Ötztal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96tztal) in Tyrolia. The highest peak is the [Wildspitze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildspitze) (3,768 metres (12,362 ft)), the second highest mountain in Austria.

Austria is a largely mountainous country because of its location in the [Alps](981.md).[^cite_note-BritannicaAlps-129] The [Central Eastern Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Eastern_Alps), [Northern Limestone Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Limestone_Alps), and [Southern Limestone Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Limestone_Alps) are all partly in Austria. Of the total area of Austria (83,871 km<sup>2</sup> or 32,383 sq mi), only about a quarter can be considered low-lying, and only 32% of the country is below 500 metres (1,640 ft). The Alps of western Austria give way somewhat into low lands and plains in the eastern part of the country.

Austria lies between latitudes [46°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/46th_parallel_north) and [49° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north), and longitudes [9°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_meridian_east) and [18° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_meridian_east).

It can be divided into five areas, the biggest being the [Eastern Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Alps), which constitute 62% of the nation's total area. The Austrian foothills at the base of the Alps and the [Carpathians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains) account for about 12% and the foothills in the east and areas surrounding the periphery of the Pannoni low country amount to about 12% of the total landmass. The second greater mountain area (much lower than the Alps) is situated in the north. Known as the Austrian [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) [plateau](538005.md), it is located in the central area of the Bohemian Mass and accounts for 10% of Austria. The Austrian portion of the [Vienna basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_basin) makes up the remaining 4%.[^cite_note-130]

In Austria, [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover) is about 47% of the total land area, equivalent to 3,899,150 hectares (ha) of forest in 2020, up from 3,775,670 hectares (ha) in 1990. In 2020, naturally regenerating forest covered 2,227,500 hectares (ha) and planted forest covered 1,671,500 hectares (ha). Of the naturally regenerating forest 2% was reported to be [primary forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_forest) (consisting of native tree species with no clearly visible indications of human activity) and about 23% of the forest area was found within protected areas. For the year 2015, 18% of the forest area was reported to be under [public ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership), 82% [private ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property) and 0% with ownership listed as other or unknown.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

[Phytogeographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytogeography), Austria belongs to the Central European province of the [Circumboreal Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumboreal_Region) within the [Boreal Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boreal_Kingdom). According to the [WWF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature), the territory of Austria can be subdivided into four ecoregions: the [Central European mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_mixed_forests), [Pannonian mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_mixed_forests), [Alps conifer and mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alps_conifer_and_mixed_forests), and [Western European broadleaf forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_broadleaf_forests).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-133] Austria had a 2018 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 3.55/10, ranking it 149th globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-134]

### Climate



[Köppen-Geiger climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) map for Austria

[^cite_note-135]

The greater part of Austria lies in the cool/temperate [climate zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_zone), where humid westerly winds predominate. With nearly three-quarters of the country dominated by the Alps, the [alpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate) is predominant. In the east—in the [Pannonian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Plain) and along the [Danube valley](21209639.md)—the climate shows continental features with less rain than the alpine areas. Although Austria is cold in the winter (−10 to 0 °C), summer temperatures can be relatively high,[^cite_note-Climate-136] with average temperatures in the mid-20s and a highest temperature of 40.5 °C (105 °F) in August 2013.[^cite_note-ZAMG-137]

According to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_Classification) Austria has the following climate types: [humid subtropical (Cfa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate), [oceanic (Cfb)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), [cool/warm-summer humid continental (Dfb)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate), [subarctic/subalpine (Dfc)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate), [tundra/alpine (ET)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra_climate), and [ice-cap (EF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cap_climate). Austria may experience very cold, severe winters, but most of the time they are only around as cold as those in somewhat comparable climate zones, for example, Southern Scandinavia or Eastern Europe. As well, at higher altitudes, summers are usually considerably cooler than in the valleys/lower altitudes. The subarctic and tundra climates seen around the Alps are much warmer in winter than what is normal elsewhere due in part to the Oceanic influence on this part of Europe.[^cite_note-ZAMG-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

[Climate change in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Austria) has already caused temperature rises of almost 2 °C since 1880, and temperatures are expected to increase further while [heat waves](293270.md) become more common. Extreme precipitation events have become more frequent, and associated [floods](50482.md) and [landslides](37208.md) could threaten Austria's electricity supply security.[^cite_note-140] Austria's mountainous regions are highly sensitive to climate change and are experiencing reduced snowfall, earlier [snowmelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmelt) and [glacier](12463.md) loss.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

The [Austrian Parliament Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Parliament_Building) in Vienna

The [Parliament of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Austria) is located in Vienna, the country's capital and most populous city. Austria became a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation), [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) through the [Federal Constitutional Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Constitutional_Law) of 1920. The political system of the Second Republic with its [nine states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_states_of_Austria) is based on the constitution of 1920, amended in 1929, which was re-enacted on 1 May 1945.[^cite_note-143]

The [president of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Austria) is the head of state. The president is directly elected by popular majority vote, with a run-off between the top-scoring candidates if necessary. The [chancellor of Austria](60582.md) is head of the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Austria). The chancellor is selected by the president and tasked with forming a government based on the partisan composition of the lower house of parliament.

The government can be removed from office by either a presidential decree or by [vote of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_of_no_confidence) in the lower chamber of parliament, the [*Nationalrat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_Austria). Voting for the president and for the parliament used to be compulsory in Austria. The compulsion was abolished in steps from 1982 to 2004.[^cite_note-144]

Austria's parliament consists of two chambers. The composition of the Nationalrat (183 seats) is determined every five years (or whenever the Nationalrat has been dissolved by the federal president on a motion by the federal chancellor, or by the Nationalrat itself) by a general election in which every citizen over the age of 16 has the [right to vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage). The voting age was lowered from 18 to 16 in 2007.[^cite_note-145]

While there is a general [threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) of 4% of the vote for all parties in federal elections (*Nationalratswahlen*) to participate in the proportional allocation of seats, there remains the possibility of being elected to a seat directly in one of the 43 regional electoral districts (*[Direktmandat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direktmandat)*).

The Nationalrat is the dominant chamber in the legislative process in Austria. However, the upper house of parliament, the [*Bundesrat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Council_of_Austria), has a limited right of veto (the Nationalrat can—in almost all cases—ultimately pass the respective bill by voting a second time; this is referred to as a *Beharrungsbeschluss*, lit. "vote of persistence"). A constitutional convention, called the *Österreich -Konvent*[^cite_note-146] was convened on 30 June 2003 to consider reforms to the constitution, but failed to produce a proposal that would command a two-thirds majority in the Nationalrat, the margin necessary for constitutional amendments or reforms.

While the [bicameral Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) and the Government constitute the legislative and executive branches, respectively, the courts are the third branch of Austrian state powers. The [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_\(Austria\)) exerts considerable influence on the political system because of its power to invalidate legislation and ordinances that are not in compliance with the Constitution. Since 1995, the [European Court of Justice](87187.md) may overrule Austrian decisions in all matters defined in the laws of the [European Union](9317.md). Austria also implements the decisions of the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights), since the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights) is part of the Austrian constitution.

### Since 2006



The Federal Chancellery on [Ballhausplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballhausplatz)

After the general election held [in October 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Austrian_legislative_election), the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Austria) (SPÖ) emerged as the strongest party, and the [Austrian People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_People's_Party) (ÖVP) came in second, having lost about 8 [Percentage points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percentage_point) of its previous polling.[^cite_note-Election2002-147][^cite_note-Election2006-148] Political realities prohibited any of the two major parties from forming a coalition with smaller parties. In January 2007 the ÖVP and SPÖ formed a grand coalition with [Alfred Gusenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Gusenbauer) (SPÖ) as Chancellor. [This coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusenbauer_government) broke up on 7 July 2008.[^cite_note-149]

The election [in September 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Austrian_legislative_election) further weakened both major parties (SPÖ 29.3 % and ÖVP 26.0 %). They formed a coalition with [Werner Faymann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Faymann) (SPÖ) as Chancellor ([First Faymann government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Faymann_government)). The [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greens_%E2%80%93_The_Green_Alternative) came in third with 11% of the vote. The FPÖ and the deceased [Jörg Haider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B6rg_Haider)'s new party [Alliance for the Future of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_the_Future_of_Austria), both on the political right, were strengthened during the election but taken together received less than 20% of the vote. On 11 October 2008, Jörg Haider died in a car accident.[^cite_note-150]

In the [legislative elections of 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Austrian_legislative_election), the Social Democratic Party received 27% of the vote and 52 seats; the People's Party 24% and 47 seats, thus controlling together the majority of the seats. The Freedom Party received 40 seats and 21% of the votes, while the Greens received 12% and 24 seats. Two new parties, Stronach and the NEOS, received less than 10% of the vote, and 11 and nine seats respectively.[^cite_note-151]

On 17 May 2016, [Christian Kern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Kern) from Social Democrats (SPÖ) was sworn in as the new chancellor. He continued governing in a "grand coalition" with the conservative People's Party (ÖVP). He took the office after the former chancellor, also from SPÖ, [Werner Faymann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Faymann)'s resignation.[^cite_note-152]

On 26 January 2017, [Alexander Van der Bellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Van_der_Bellen) was sworn in as the mostly ceremonial – but symbolically significant – role of Austrian president.[^cite_note-153]

After the Grand Coalition broke in Spring 2017 a snap election was proclaimed for [October 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Austrian_legislative_election). The Austrian People's Party (ÖVP) with its new young leader [Sebastian Kurz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Kurz) emerged as the largest party in the National Council, winning 31.5% of votes and 62 of the 183 seats. The Social Democratic Party (SPÖ) finished second with 52 seats and 26.9% votes, slightly ahead of the Freedom Party of Austria (FPÖ), which received 51 seats and 26 percent. NEOS finished fourth with 10 seats (5.3% of votes), and PILZ (which split from the Green Party at the start of the campaign) entered parliament for the first time and came in fifth place with 8 seats and 4.4% The Green Party failed with 3.8% to cross the 4% threshold and was ejected from parliament, losing all of its 24 seats.[^cite_note-154] The ÖVP decided to form a coalition with the FPÖ. The new government between the centre-right wing and the right-wing populist party under the new chancellor Sebastian Kurz was sworn in on 18 December 2017,[^cite_note-155] but the coalition government later collapsed in the wake of the ["Ibiza" corruption scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza_affair)[^cite_note-156] and [new elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Austrian_legislative_election) were called for 29 September 2019. The elections led to another landslide victory (37.5 percent) of the Austrian People's Party (ÖVP) which formed a coalition government with the reinvigorated (13.9 percent) Greens, which was sworn in with Kurz as chancellor on 7 January 2020.[^cite_note-157]

On 11 October 2021, Chancellor Sebastian Kurz resigned following pressure from a corruption scandal. He was succeeded by Foreign Minister Alexander Schallenberg of the ÖVP. However, Schallenberg stepped down after less than two months, and Karl Nehammer was sworn in as chancellor on 6 December 2021, making him Austria's third conservative leader in two months. The ÖVP and the Greens continued to govern together.[^cite_note-158]

Following the [election on 29 September 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Austrian_legislative_election), coalition negotiations between the ÖVP, SPÖ, and [NEOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEOS_\(Austria\)) collapsed, leading to Nehammer's resignation. Schallenberg stepped in as interim chancellor for the second time. Attempts to form an ÖVP-FPÖ coalition also failed. Eventually, the ÖVP, SPÖ, and NEOS reached an agreement, forming a coalition government with [Christian Stocker](78148335.md) (ÖVP) as chancellor ([Stocker government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stocker_government)).[^cite_note-159]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Austria)



The [European Parliament](9581.md): Austria is one of the 27 EU members.

The 1955 [Austrian State Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_State_Treaty) ended the occupation of Austria following World War II and recognised Austria as an independent and sovereign state. On 26 October 1955, the [Federal Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Assembly_of_Austria) passed a constitutional article in which "Austria declares of her own free will her perpetual neutrality". The second section of this law stated that "in all future times Austria will not join any military alliances and will not permit the establishment of any foreign military bases on her territory". Since then, Austria has shaped its foreign policy on the basis of neutrality, but rather different from the neutrality of Switzerland.[^cite_note-162]

Austria began to reassess its definition of [neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_country) following the fall of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), granting overflight rights for the UN-sanctioned action against [Iraq](7515928.md) in 1991, and since 1995, it has developed participation in the EU's Common Foreign and Security Policy. Also in 1995, it joined NATO's [Partnership for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_for_Peace) (although it was careful to do so only after Russia joined) and subsequently participated in peacekeeping missions in Bosnia. Meanwhile, the only part of the Constitutional Law on Neutrality of 1955 still fully valid is not to allow foreign military bases in Austria.[^cite_note-163] Austria signed the UN's [Nuclear Weapon Ban Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons),[^cite_note-164] which was opposed by all NATO members.[^cite_note-165]

### Military

Main article: [Austrian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Armed_Forces)



Headquarters of the [Federal Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Austria\)) in [Rossauer Kaserne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rossauer_Kaserne), [Vienna](55866.md)

The manpower of the Austrian Armed Forces (Austrian German: *Bundesheer*) mainly relies on [conscription](5735.md).[^cite_note-166] All males who have reached the age of eighteen and are found fit have to serve a six months compulsory [military service](938728.md), followed by an eight-year reserve obligation. Both males and females at the age of sixteen are eligible for voluntary service.[^cite_note-CIA-25] [Conscientious objection](83003.md) is legally acceptable and those who claim this right are obliged to serve an institutionalised nine months [civilian service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zivildienst_in_Austria) instead. Since 1998, women volunteers have been allowed to become professional soldiers.

The main sectors of the Bundesheer are Joint Forces (Streitkräfteführungskommando, SKFüKdo) which consist of Land Forces (Landstreitkräfte), Air Forces (Luftstreitkräfte), International Missions (Internationale Einsätze) and Special Forces (Spezialeinsatzkräfte), next to Joint Mission Support Command (Kommando Einsatzunterstützung; KdoEU) and Joint Command Support Centre (Führungsunterstützungszentrum; FüUZ). Austria is a [landlocked country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country) and has no navy.



As a neutral country, Austria's defence focuses on peacekeeping expertise and missions by the [United Nations](31769.md), as in this picture of [UNIFIL](787766.md).

In 2012, Austria's defence expenditures corresponded to approximately 0.8% of its GDP. The Army currently has about 26,000[^cite_note-167] soldiers, of whom about 12,000 are conscripts. As head of state, the [Austrian president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Austria) is nominally the commander-in-chief of the Armed Forces. Command of the Austrian Armed Forces is exercised by the minister of defence, as of May 2020: [Klaudia Tanner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaudia_Tanner).

Since the end of the [Cold War](325329.md), and more importantly the removal of the former heavily guarded "[Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain)" separating Austria and its [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) neighbours ([Hungary](13275.md) and former [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia)), the Austrian military has been assisting Austrian border guards in trying to prevent border crossings by [illegal immigrants](7124412.md). This assistance came to an end when Hungary and [Slovakia](26830.md) joined the EU [Schengen Area](16565139.md) in 2008, for all intents and purposes abolishing "internal" border controls between treaty states. Some politicians have called for a prolongation of this mission, but the legality of this is heavily disputed. In accordance with the Austrian constitution, armed forces may only be deployed in a limited number of cases, mainly to defend the country and aid in cases of national emergency, such as in the wake of [natural disasters](58921.md).[^cite_note-168] They may only exceptionally be used as auxiliary police forces.

Within its [self-declared status of permanent neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Neutrality), Austria has a tradition of engaging in UN-led peacekeeping and other humanitarian missions. The [Austrian Forces Disaster Relief Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Forces_Disaster_Relief_Unit) (AFDRU), in particular, an all-volunteer unit with close ties to civilian specialists (e.g. rescue dog handlers) enjoys a reputation as a quick (standard deployment time is 10 hours) and efficient [SAR](172599.md) unit. Currently, larger contingents of Austrian forces are deployed in [Bosnia](3463.md) and [Kosovo](17391.md). According to the 2024 [Global Peace Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index), Austria is the 3rd most peaceful country in the world.[^cite_note-169]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Austria)



[Kitzbühel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitzb%C3%BChel), a famous winter tourist destination in Austria

Austria consistently ranks high in terms of [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)_per_capita),[^cite_note-autogenerated1-170] due to its highly industrialised economy, and well-developed [social market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_market_economy). Until the 1980s, many of Austria's largest industry firms were nationalised; in recent years, however, [privatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation) has reduced state holdings to a level comparable to other European economies. [Labour movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement) are particularly influential, exercising a large influence on labour politics and decisions related to the expansion of the economy. Next to a highly developed industry, international tourism is the most important part of the [economy of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Austria).

Germany has historically been the main trading partner of Austria, making it vulnerable to rapid changes in the [German economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_economy). Since Austria became a member state of the [European Union](9317.md), it has gained closer ties to other EU economies. Membership of the EU has drawn an influx of foreign investors attracted by Austria's access to the single European market and proximity to the aspiring economies of the European Union. Growth in GDP reached 3.3% in 2006.[^cite_note-171] At least 67% of Austria's imports come from other European Union member states.[^cite_note-172]



Austria is part of a monetary union, the [eurozone](184391.md) (dark blue), and of the [EU single market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Market_\(European_Union\)).

The [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) led to the [Hypo Alpe-Adria-Bank International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypo_Alpe-Adria-Bank_International) to be purchased in December 2009 by the government for 1 euro owing to credit difficulties, thus wiping out the €1.63bn of [BayernLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BayernLB). As of February 2014, the HGAA situation was unresolved,[^cite_note-173] causing Chancellor [Werner Faymann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Faymann) to warn that its failure would be comparable to the 1931 [Creditanstalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creditanstalt) event.[^cite_note-174]

Austria indicated on 16 November 2010 that it would withhold the December instalment of its contribution to the EU bailout of Greece, citing the material worsening of the Greek debt situation and the apparent inability of Greece to collect the level of tax receipts it had previously promised.[^cite_note-175]

Since the [fall of communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_communism), [Austrian companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Austrian_companies) have been quite active players and consolidators in Eastern Europe. Between 1995 and 2010, 4,868 [mergers and acquisitions](20769.md) with a total known value of 163 billion EUR with the involvement of Austrian firms have been announced.[^cite_note-176] The largest transactions with involvement of Austrian companies[^cite_note-177] have been: the acquisition of [Bank Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Austria) by [HypoVereinsbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HypoVereinsbank) for 7.8 billion EUR in 2000, the acquisition of [Porsche Holding Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche) by [Volkswagen Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group) for 3.6 billion EUR in 2009,[^cite_note-178] and the acquisition of [Banca Comercială Română](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banca_Comercial%C4%83_Rom%C3%A2n%C4%83) by [Erste Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erste_Group) for 3.7 billion EUR in 2005.[^cite_note-179]

[Tourism in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Austria) accounts for almost 9% of its gross domestic product.[^cite_note-STAT-180] In 2007, Austria ranked 9th worldwide in international tourism receipts, with 18.9 billion US\$.[^cite_note-UNTWO-181] In [international tourist arrivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tourism_rankings), Austria ranked 12th with 20.8 million tourists.[^cite_note-UNTWO-181]

### Infrastructure and natural resources

Main articles: [Transport in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Austria), [Energy in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Austria), and [Renewable energy in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Austria)



The [Kölnbrein Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6lnbrein_Dam) in [Carinthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthia_\(state\))

In 1972, the country began construction of a [nuclear power plant](207347.md) to produce electricity at [Zwentendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zwentendorf) on the River [Danube](21209639.md), following a unanimous vote in parliament. However, in 1978 a [referendum](51582.md) voted approximately 50.5% against nuclear power, 49.5% for,[^cite_note-182] and parliament subsequently unanimously passed a law forbidding the use of nuclear power to generate electricity although the nuclear power plant had already finished.

Austria currently produces more than half of its electricity by [hydropower](14073.md).[^cite_note-RES-183] Together with other [renewable energy](25784.md) sources such as [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), [solar power](13690575.md), and [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass), the electricity supply from renewable energy amounts to 62.89 percent.[^cite_note-Renewables-184]

Compared to most European countries, Austria is ecologically well endowed. Its [biocapacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biocapacity) (or biological [natural capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_capital)) is more than double of the world average: In 2016 Austria had 3.8 global hectares[^cite_note-GFN-185] of biocapacity per person within its territory, compared to the world average of 1.6 global hectares per person. By contrast, in 2016 they used 6.0 global hectares of biocapacity which amounts to Austria's [ecological footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_footprint) of consumption. This means that Austrians use about 60% more biocapacity than Austria contains. As a result, Austria is running a biocapacity deficit.[^cite_note-GFN-185]

[Rail transport in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Austria) is primarily provided by the national carrier [Austrian Federal Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Federal_Railways) (*Österreichische Bundesbahnen*, ÖBB). It operates most [commuter rail systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn#Austria) and long-distance trains.

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Austria)



Population density in Austria by [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Austria)

Austria's population was estimated to be 9,220,882 in April 2026 by [Statistik Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistik_Austria). The population of the capital, Vienna, exceeds 2 million, representing about a quarter of the country's population.[^cite_note-\:5-186] It is known for its cultural offerings and high standard of living.

[Vienna](55866.md) is the country's largest city. [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz) is second in size, with 305,314 inhabitants, followed by [Linz](55856.md) (213,557), [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg) (157,659), [Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innsbruck) (132,499), and [Klagenfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klagenfurt) (105,256). All other cities have fewer than 100,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-\:5-186]

According to Statistik Austria, at the beginning of 2024, there were 1.8 million [foreign-born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_born) residents in Austria, corresponding to 22.3% of the total population. There are more than 620,100 descendants of foreign-born immigrants.[^cite_note-\:1-187]

Largest group of foreign born population are from [Bosnian-Croatian-Montenegrin-Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian-Croatian-Montenegrin-Serbian) (BCMS) speaking countries (about 5.1% of Austria's total population), mainly [Bosnia-Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia-Herzegovina) which by it self is only surpassed by the second largest group, people born in Germany, followed by people born in [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_Austria) and [Romania](25445.md).

There are a group of legally recognized indigenous non-German speaking minorities, like [Carinthian Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthian_Slovenes), or the approximately 25,000 [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Austria).[^cite_note-188]

The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) (TFR) in 2023 was estimated at 1.32 children born per woman,[^cite_note-189] below the replacement rate of 2.1, it remains considerably below the high of 4.83 children born per woman in 1873.[^cite_note-190] In 2015, 42.1% of births were to unmarried women.[^cite_note-191] Austria had [the 14th oldest population in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_median_age#CIA_figures) in 2020, with the average age of 44.5 years.[^cite_note-cia.age-192]

The [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) in 2024 was estimated at 82.3 years, placing Austria above the EU average of 81.7 years.[^cite_note-193]

In 2024, Statistik Austria estimated that the population will grow to 10,179,834 people by 2080, primarily driven by immigration.[^cite_note-194]

### Language

Main article: [Languages of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Austria)



The school version of the 43rd edition of the *[Österreichisches Wörterbuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96sterreichisches_W%C3%B6rterbuch)* for the [Austrian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_German), 2018

The official language of Austria is [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), as specified by article 8 of [its constitution of 1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Constitutional_Law).[^cite_note-195] The written form of German in Austria and in the Italian [South Tyrol](169611.md) region is [Austrian German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_German), or simply Austrian, a variety of [Standard High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_High_German); it has been standardized in Austria since 1951, when the [Ministry of Education, Science and Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education,_Science_and_Research) published the *[Österreichisches Wörterbuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96sterreichisches_W%C3%B6rterbuch)*. This standard form, however, is used primarily in education and for government publications, announcements and websites; the *de facto* common spoken languages of Austria are not the Austrian German as taught in schools but rather the [Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language) and [Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German) dialects. These are two mutually intelligible [Upper German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_German) local languages or collections of dialects that are understood, with varying degrees of difficulty, by each other as well as by speakers of non-Austrian German dialects. Taken as a collective whole, [German languages or dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_dialects) are thus spoken natively by 88.6% of the population, which includes the 2.5% German-born citizens who reside in Austria, followed by Turkish (2.28%), Serbian (2.21%), Croatian (1.63%), English (0.73%), Hungarian (0.51%), Bosnian (0.43%), Polish (0.35%), Albanian (0.35%), Slovenian (0.31%), Czech (0.22%), Arabic (0.22%) and Romanian (0.21%).[^cite_note-Language-196]

The Austrian state of [Carinthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthia_\(state\)) is home to a significant indigenous [Slovene-speaking minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthian_Slovenes), while in the easternmost state, [Burgenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgenland) (formerly part of the Hungarian portion of [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary)), there are significant [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language)- and [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language)-speaking minorities. [Burgenland Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgenland_Croatian), Hungarian, and [Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenian_language) are also recognized as [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) beside German in parts of Carinthia and Burgenland.[^cite_note-demokratiewebstatt.at-3][^cite_note-Regional_Languages_of_Austria-4]



Bilingual sign of [Oberwart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberwart) (in [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language) *Felsőőr*) in [Burgenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgenland)

According to census information published by [Statistik Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistik_Austria),[^cite_note-Language-196] there were a total of 710,926 foreign nationals living in Austria in 2001. Of these, the largest by far were 283,334 foreign nationals from the [former Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) (of whom 135,336 spoke Serbian; 105,487 Croatian; 31,591 Bosnian — i.e., 272,414 Austrian resident native speakers in total, plus 6,902 Slovenian and 4,018 [Macedonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_language) speakers).

### Ethnic groups

Further information: [Austrian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_nationalism)



The birthplaces of foreign-born naturalised residents of Austria

The predominant historic [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity) of Austrians is [German speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples),[^cite_note-197] which is closely related to people from neighbouring countries [Germany](11867.md), [Liechtenstein](17810.md), and [German-speaking Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-speaking_Switzerland).[^cite_note-198] That said Austrian ethnicity has considerable convergence with non-German speaking populations to the east and south of Austria, particularly in Vienna, through its position and role in Eastern Europe today and as the capital of the former multi-ethnic [Habsburg Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Empire).[^cite_note-s036-199]

This is reflected in the officially recognized non-German speaking indigneous populations of Austria. An estimated 13,000 to 40,000 [Slovenes](246311.md) in the Austrian state [Carinthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthia_\(state\)) (the [Carinthian Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthian_Slovenes)) as well as [Croats](69801.md) (about 30,000)[^cite_note-200] and [Hungarians](17106358.md) in Burgenland were recognised as a minority and have had special rights following the Austrian State Treaty (*Staatsvertrag*) of 1955.[^cite_note-johnson_153-117] Since 1994 the [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) and [Sinti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinti) have been an officially recognised ethnic minority in Austria.

Austria's people who are of non-Austrian descent, many come from surrounding countries, especially from the former [East Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) nations. [Guest workers](384142.md) *(Gastarbeiter)* and their descendants, as well as refugees from the [Yugoslav wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_wars) and other conflicts, also form an important [minority group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group) in Austria.&#x20;

The largest single immigrant group in Austria are Germans, only collectively surpassed by people from ex-[Yugoslavian](34244.md) countries. The largest group of people born in ex-Yugoslavian countries are from [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md). The next biggest group are [people born in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_Austria).[^cite_note-201] People from Turkey are closely followed by [people from Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_in_Austria).[^cite_note-202] Serbs form one of the largest ethnic groups in Austria, numbering around 300,000 people.[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205] Historically, Serbian immigrants moved to Austria during the time of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire), when [Vojvodina](176801.md) was under Imperial control. Following [World War II](32927.md) the number of Serbs expanded again, and today the community is very large. The Austrian Serbian Society was founded in 1936. Today, Serbs in Austria are mainly found in [Vienna](55866.md), [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg), and [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz).

Interior of [St. Stephen's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_Cathedral,_Vienna) in [Vienna](55866.md), a [Catholic Christian church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Austria)

Austria was historically a strongly Catholic country as the centre of the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy), which championed Catholicism.[^cite_note-Zulehner_2004_1-207] Although in the 16th century many Austrians converted to [other denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) ([Lutheranism](23371382.md), in particular) as the Protestant [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) (begun in 1517) spread across Europe, the Habsburgs enacted measures of [Counter-Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation) as early as 1527 and harshly repressed Austrian evangelicalism; only a minority of Austrians remained Protestant.[^cite_note-Zulehner_2004_1-207] At least since the 1970s, a few decades after the fall of the Habsburg monarchy and the transformation of Austria into a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) republic, there has been a continuous decline of Christianity (with the exception of Orthodox churches) and a proliferation of other religions, a process which has been particularly pronounced in [Vienna](55866.md), with its large foreign and immigrant populations.[^cite_note-208]

In 2001, about 74% of Austria's population were registered as Catholic,[^cite_note-Volkszählung-209] while about 5% considered themselves [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant).[^cite_note-Volkszählung-209] Austrian Christians, both Catholic and Protestant,[^cite_note-210] are obliged to pay a mandatory membership fee (calculated by income – about 1%) to their churches; this payment is called the *Kirchenbeitrag* ("ecclesiastical contribution").

From the second half of the 20th century, the number of adherents and churchgoers has declined. Data for 2023 list 4,638,000 members of the Catholic Church, or about 50% of the total Austrian population, yet Sunday [church attendance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_attendance) was only 347,000, or 3.7% of the total Austrian population.[^cite_note-211] Additionally, the Lutheran church recorded a loss of 74,421 adherents between 2001 and 2016.

The 2001 census reported that about 12% of the population declared themselves [without a religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion);[^cite_note-Volkszählung-209] according to ecclesiastical information, this share had grown to 20% by 2015[^cite_note-Kista-212] and further increased to 22.4% (1,997,700 people) in 2021.[^cite_note-Religion2021-206] Of the remaining population, about 340,000 were registered as members of various Muslim communities in 2001, originating chiefly from [Turkey](11125639.md), [Bosnia-Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia-Herzegovina), and [Kosovo](17391.md);[^cite_note-Volkszählung-209] the number of Muslims doubled in the fifteen years to 2016, to 700,000,[^cite_note-213] and reached 745,600 in 2021.[^cite_note-Religion2021-206] In 2021, another 436,700 residents of Austria (mostly [Serbs](59512.md)) were members of [Eastern Orthodox Churches](10186.md), 26,600 were [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Austria), 10,100 were [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Austria), about 21,800 were active [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses), and 5,400 were [Jews](25955086.md).[^cite_note-Religion2021-206][^cite_note-214]

According to the [Eurobarometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobarometer) 2010,[^cite_note-215]

- 44% of Austrian citizens "believe there is a God";

- 38% "believe there is some sort of spirit or life force"; and

- 12% "do not believe there is any sort of spirit, God, or life force".

### Education

Main article: [Education in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Austria)



[Stiftsgymnasium Melk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stiftsgymnasium_Melk) is the oldest Austrian school.

Education in Austria is federally governed by the [Ministry of Education, Science and Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education,_Science_and_Research), with administration handled by individual states. School attendance is mandatory for nine years, typically from ages six to fifteen. Kindergarten is available for children aged three to six, with compulsory attendance starting at five. While part-time daycare is free nationwide for this age group, [Vienna](55866.md) and [Burgenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgenland) offer free all-day care. By 2018, about 90% of children between three and five attended early childhood education.[^cite_note-\:2-216][^cite_note-\:3-217][^cite_note-\:4-218][^cite_note-219]



Zollergasse in Vienna

[Volksschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksschule) or [Grundschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Germany) starts at age six and lasts for four years, with an average class size of 18 pupils. Secondary education is divided into different tracks: [Mittelschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittelschule), which focuses on vocational training, and Allgemeinbildende Höhere Schule, an academic secondary school that prepares students for university through the [Matura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura) examination. At age 10, students, parents, and teachers decide the most suitable path based on the child's abilities and interests. Austria is also known for its dual education system, which integrates theoretical learning with practical training; in 2021, 34.1% of secondary level II students participated in this system.[^cite_note-\:2-216][^cite_note-\:3-217][^cite_note-\:4-218]

Austria allocated 4.9% of its GDP to education in 2021, with spending per student averaging US\$16,734. German proficiency is essential in public schools, and children's language skills are assessed using the MIKA-D test upon admission. Students who require additional support receive targeted language assistance to facilitate integration into the educational system.[^cite_note-\:2-216][^cite_note-\:3-217][^cite_note-\:4-218]

### Health

Main article: [Health in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Austria)

See also: [Healthcare in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Austria)

Life Expectancy in Austria over time

Life expectancy in Austria over time

Even though Austria has a 0.9 health index and a [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) of 81 years,[^cite_note-220] the country still faces numerous problems when it comes to health, one example being that 2 in 5 Austrians have a [chronic condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_condition). Cancer is a big problem in the country, as about 21,500 people died of this condition in 2019, having lung cancer as the primary cause of cancer deaths, probably linked to several risk factors in the country's population, as it is estimated that 40% of deaths in the country are caused by smoking, dietary risks, alcohol, low physical activity, and air pollution. One of the most costly health services in the EU is located in Austria. In 2019, health spending per capita ranked third in the EU. Health-related [out-of-pocket expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-of-pocket_expense) are higher than the EU average.[^cite_note-\:0-221]

#### Medical personnel

With 5.2 physicians per 1,000 inhabitants, Austria has among the highest physician density in [OECD](33853117.md) countries. Overall, the country has 271 hospitals with a total of 45,596 physicians (data from 2017), about 54% of which work (also or primarily) in hospitals. Although Austria has the second highest physician rate in the [EU](9317.md), a large share of physicians is tropical to retirement age (55 years and older), and may thus be at a higher risk of developing severe conditions in a specimen of [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Austria) infection.

The number of nurses in Austria has been subject to debate in recent years with regard to definitions of qualifications and their interpretation in cross-country comparisons. A new mandatory health professional registry was set up in 2018. However, due to the elapsing of the COVID-19 pandemic in early 2020, compulsory registration has been suspended. This implies that professional activities in long-term superintendency are moreover possible without registration until the end of the pandemic by late spring 2022 (Transition without the pandemic is still to be defined).[^cite_note-222]

### Music

Main article: [Music of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Austria)



[Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart)

Austria's past as a European power and its cultural environment generated a broad contribution to various forms of art, most notably among them music.[^cite_note-b1-223] Austria was the birthplace of many [famous composers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Austria) such as [Joseph Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haydn),[^cite_note-jh-224] [Michael Haydn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Haydn),[^cite_note-225] [Franz Liszt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Liszt),[^cite_note-226] [Franz Schubert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Schubert),[^cite_note-227] [Anton Bruckner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Bruckner),[^cite_note-228] [Johann Strauss Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Strauss_Sr.), and [Johann Strauss Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Strauss_Jr.), as well as members of the [Second Viennese School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Viennese_School) such as [Arnold Schoenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schoenberg),[^cite_note-229] [Anton Webern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Webern),[^cite_note-230] and [Alban Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alban_Berg). [Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart)[^cite_note-231] was born in [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg), then an independent Church Principality of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), which later became part of Austria, and much of Mozart's career was spent in Vienna.[^cite_note-bv-232]

Vienna was for a long time an important centre of musical innovation. 18th- and 19th-century composers were drawn to the city due to the patronage of the Habsburgs, and made Vienna the European capital of classical music. During the [Baroque period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_period), Slavic and Hungarian folk forms influenced Austrian music.[^cite_note-bv-232]



The [Vienna State Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_State_Opera)

Vienna's status began its rise as a cultural centre in the early 16th century and was focused around instruments, including the [lute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lute). [Ludwig van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven) spent the better part of his life in Vienna. Austria's current [national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthem), attributed to Mozart, was chosen after [World War II](32927.md) to replace the [traditional Austrian anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sei_gesegnet_ohne_Ende) by Joseph Haydn.[^cite_note-bv-232][^cite_note-jh-224]

Austrian [Herbert von Karajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_von_Karajan) was principal conductor of the [Berlin Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Philharmonic) for 35 years. He is generally regarded as one of the greatest conductors of the 20th century, and he was a dominant figure in European classical music from the 1960s until his death.[^cite_note-NYT_obit-233]

[Kurt Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_G%C3%B6del) as a student in 1925

Austria was the cradle of numerous scientists with international reputation. Among them are [Ludwig Boltzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Boltzmann), [Ernst Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Mach), [Victor Franz Hess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Franz_Hess), and [Christian Doppler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Doppler), prominent scientists in the 19th century. In the 20th century, contributions by [Lise Meitner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lise_Meitner), [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schr%C3%B6dinger), and [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli) to nuclear research and [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics) were key to these areas' development during the 1920s and 1930s. Prominent present-day [quantum physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics) are [Anton Zeilinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Zeilinger) and [Peter Zoller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Zoller) renown for important developments in [quantum optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_optics) and [quantum information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information).

In addition to [physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), Austria was the birthplace of two of the most noteworthy philosophers of the 20th century, [Ludwig Wittgenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Wittgenstein) and [Karl Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Popper). In addition to them, biologists [Gregor Mendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Mendel) and [Konrad Lorenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Lorenz) as well as mathematician [Kurt Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_G%C3%B6del) and engineers such as [Ferdinand Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Porsche) and [Siegfried Marcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegfried_Marcus) were Austrians. [Bertha von Suttner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertha_von_Suttner) became the first woman to be awarded the [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md), and the first Austrian laureate.

A focus of Austrian science has always been medicine and psychology, starting in [medieval times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_times) with [Paracelsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracelsus). Eminent physicians like [Theodore Billroth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Billroth), [Clemens von Pirquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemens_von_Pirquet), and [Anton von Eiselsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Eiselsberg) have built upon the achievements of the 19th-century Vienna School of Medicine. Austria was home to [Sigmund Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Freud), founder of [psychoanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoanalysis), [Alfred Adler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Adler), founder of [Individual psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_psychology), psychologists [Paul Watzlawick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Watzlawick) and [Hans Asperger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Asperger), and psychiatrist [Viktor Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl). Austria was ranked 17th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2024,[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236] but it declined to the 19th rank according to GII 2025.[^cite_note-237]

The [Austrian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_School) of Economics, which is prominent as one of the main competitive directions for economic theory, is related to Austrian economists [Carl Menger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Menger), [Joseph Schumpeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Schumpeter), [Eugen von Böhm-Bawerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_von_B%C3%B6hm-Bawerk), [Ludwig von Mises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_von_Mises), and [Friedrich Hayek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hayek). Other noteworthy Austrian-born émigrés include the econimic anthropologist [Karl Paul Polanyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Paul_Polanyi), management thinker [Peter Drucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Drucker), sociologist [Paul Felix Lazarsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Felix_Lazarsfeld), and scientist [Sir Gustav Nossal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Nossal).

Austria's cuisine is derived from that of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire). Austrian cuisine is rooted in the long-standing traditions of the imperial court kitchen ("Hofküche"), which evolved over centuries within the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy). It is distinguished by its refined and well-balanced preparation of beef and pork, as well as by a wide variety of vegetable-based dishes. There is also the "Mehlspeisen" tradition of bakeries, which created particular delicacies such as Sachertorte, "Krapfen" which are doughnuts usually filled with apricot jam or custard, and "Strudel" such as "[Apfelstrudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apfelstrudel)" filled with apple, "Topfenstrudel" filled with a type of cheese curd called "topfen", and "[Millirahmstrudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millirahmstrudel)" (milk-cream strudel).

In addition to native regional traditions, the cuisine has been influenced by [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_cuisine), [Czech](5321.md), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine), [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_cuisine), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_cuisine), [Balkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan), and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_cuisine) cuisines, from which both dishes and methods of food preparation have often been borrowed. The Austrian cuisine is therefore one of the most multicultural and transcultural in Europe.

Typical Austrian dishes include [Wiener Schnitzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Schnitzel), Schweinsbraten, [Kaiserschmarren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiserschmarren), [Knödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kn%C3%B6del), [Sachertorte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachertorte), and [Tafelspitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafelspitz). There are also Kärntner Kasnudeln, which are pockets of dough filled with Topfen, potatoes, herbs and peppermint which are boiled and served with a butter sauce. Kasnudeln are traditionally served with a salad. [Eierschwammerl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantharellus) dishes are also popular. The sugar block dispenser [Pez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pez) was invented in Austria, as well as [Mannerschnitten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manner_\(confectionary\)). Austria is also famous for its [Mozartkugeln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozartkugel) and its coffee tradition. With over 8 kg per year it has the sixth highest per capita coffee consumption worldwide.[^cite_note-238]

Beer is sold in 0.2-litre (a *Pfiff*), 0.3-litre (a *Seidel*, *kleines Bier* or *Glas Bier*) and 0.5-litre (a *Krügerl* or *großes Bier* or *Halbe*) measures. At festivals one litre *Maß* and two-litre *Doppelmaß* in the [Bavarian](3764.md) style are also dispensed. The most popular types of beer are [lager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lager) (known as *Märzen* in Austria), naturally cloudy *Zwicklbier* and [wheat beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_beer). At holidays like Christmas and Easter [bock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bock) beer is also available.

The most important wine-producing areas are in [Lower Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Austria), [Burgenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgenland), [Styria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styria), and Vienna. The [Grüner Veltliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%BCner_Veltliner) grape provides some of Austria's most notable white wines[^cite_note-239] and [Zweigelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zweigelt) is the most widely planted red wine grape.[^cite_note-240]

In [Upper Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Austria), Lower Austria, Styria, and [Carinthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carinthia_\(state\)), *Most*, a type of [cider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cider) or [perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry), is widely produced.

A [Schnapps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnapps) of typically up to 60% alcohol or fruit [brandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandy) is drunk, which in Austria is made from a variety of fruits, for example [apricots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apricot) and [rowanberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowan). The produce of small private schnapps [distilleries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distillery), of which there are about 20,000 in Austria, is known as *Selbstgebrannter* or *Hausbrand*.

Local soft drinks such as [Almdudler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almdudler) are very popular around the country as an alternative to alcoholic beverages. Another popular drink is the so-called "Spezi", a mix between Coca-Cola and the original formula of [Orange Fanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanta) or the more locally renowned [Frucade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frucade). [Red Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull), the highest-selling energy drink in the world, was introduced by [Dietrich Mateschitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietrich_Mateschitz), an Austrian entrepreneur.

[Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innsbruck) hosted the [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Winter_Olympics) and [1976 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Winter_Olympics), as well as the [2012 Winter Youth Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Winter_Youth_Olympics), the first in history.

Due to the mountainous terrain, [alpine skiing](39576.md) is a prominent sport in Austria and is extremely valuable in the promotion and economic growth of the country.[^cite_note-241] Similar sports such as [snowboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowboarding) or [ski-jumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski-jumping) are also widely popular. Austrian athletes such as [Annemarie Moser-Pröll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annemarie_Moser-Pr%C3%B6ll), [Franz Klammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Klammer), [Hermann Maier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Maier), [Toni Sailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Sailer), [Benjamin Raich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Raich), [Marlies Schild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlies_Schild), and [Marcel Hirscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Hirscher) are widely regarded as some of the greatest alpine skiers of all time, [Armin Kogler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armin_Kogler), [Andreas Felder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Felder), [Ernst Vettori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Vettori), [Andreas Goldberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Goldberger), [Andreas Widhölzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Widh%C3%B6lzl), [Thomas Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Morgenstern), and [Gregor Schlierenzauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Schlierenzauer) as some of the greatest ski jumpers of all time. [Bobsleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobsleigh), [luge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luge), and [skeleton](159776.md) are also popular events with a permanent track located in [Igls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igls_bobsleigh,_luge,_and_skeleton_track), which hosted bobsleigh and luge competitions for the [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Winter_Olympics) and [1976 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Winter_Olympics) held in [Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innsbruck). The [first Winter Youth Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Winter_Youth_Olympics) in 2012 were held in Innsbruck as well.[^cite_note-242]



Ski racer [Franz Klammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Klammer) won a gold medal at the [1976 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Winter_Olympics) in [Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innsbruck).

Football in Austria is governed by the [Austrian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Football_Association).[^cite_note-Football-243] Austria was among the most successful football playing nations on the European continent, placing 4th at the [1934 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), 3rd at the [1954 FIFA World Cup](60523.md) and 7th at the [1978 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup). However, Austrian football has not been internationally successful since the mid 20th century. Austria co-hosted the [2008 UEFA European Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_UEFA_European_Football_Championship) with Switzerland. The national Austrian football league is the [Austrian Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Bundesliga), which includes teams such as record-champions [SK Rapid Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Rapid_Wien), [FK Austria Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Austria_Wien), [Red Bull Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Red_Bull_Salzburg), and [Sturm Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturm_Graz).

Besides football, Austria also has professional national leagues for most major team sports, including the [Austrian Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Hockey_League) for [ice hockey](14790.md), [Österreichische Basketball Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96sterreichische_Basketball_Bundesliga) for basketball and the [Austrian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Football_League) for American football. Horseback riding is also popular; the famed [Spanish Riding School of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Riding_School_of_Vienna) is located in Vienna.

[Niki Lauda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niki_Lauda) was a [Formula One](10854.md) driver who was three times F1 World Champion, winning in 1975, 1977 and 1984. He is currently the only driver to have been champion for both Ferrari and McLaren, the sport's two most successful constructors. Other known Austrian F1 drivers include [Gerhard Berger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Berger) and [Jochen Rindt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jochen_Rindt). Austria also hosts F1 races ([Austrian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Grand_Prix)); now held at the [Red Bull Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Ring), in the past also at the [Österreichring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96sterreichring) and the [Zeltweg Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeltweg_Airfield).

[Thomas Muster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Muster) is a former [tennis](29773.md) player and was one of the world's leading [clay court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_court) players in the 1990s. He won the [1995 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_French_Open) and in 1996 was ranked [number 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_players) in the [ATP ranking](13480983.md). [2020 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_\(tennis\)) winner [Dominic Thiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Thiem) is also another prominent tennis player having been as high as world number 3 and also been in the finals of the [French Open](147724.md) and [Australian Open](97851.md). Other well known Austrian tennis players include [Horst Skoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horst_Skoff) and [Jürgen Melzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Melzer).

Sport played a significant role in developing national consciousness and boosting national self-confidence in the early years of the Second Republic after World War II, through events such as the [Tour of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_of_Austria) cycle race and through sporting successes such as the national football team's run to third at the 1954 World Cup and the performances of Toni Sailer and the rest of the "Kitzbühel Miracle Team" in the 1950s.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245]
