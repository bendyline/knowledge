**Operation Aspides**, also known as **European Union Naval Force (EUNAVFOR) Aspides,** is an EU [military operation](666301.md) in response to [Houthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement) [attacks](75207766.md) with [international shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_shipping) in the [Red Sea](26191.md).[^cite_note-3] Named after the Greek word for shields ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Ασπίδες), the EU describes Operation Aspides as a "purely defensive" mission to increase maritime surveillance in the region, provide escort to merchant vessels, and defend against strikes.[^cite_note-4] The mission began in February 2024 and includes two or three ships deployed at any given time.[^cite_note-USNI-03092026-5]

## Background

See also: [List of United Nations Security Council resolutions concerning Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_Nations_Security_Council_resolutions_concerning_Yemen)

Since the start of the [Israel–Gaza war](74998519.md), the [Houthi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement) has [engaged with and boarded certain ships](75207766.md) passing through the [Red Sea](26191.md), as a response to the Israeli attacks in [Gaza](12047.md) and the rest of Palestine since October.[^cite_note-6]

Since the beginning of the conflict up to February 2024, at least four EU country-flagged ships have been struck by the Houthis.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## Mission

On 8 February 2024, EU member states took a decision in the [Council of the European Union](5866.md) to start Operation Aspides, which was to begin on 19 February and last one year, with its base of operations being in Greece and with a [Hellenic Navy](425896.md) officer being in charge of operations.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

According to the [European External Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_External_Action_Service), the purpose of the operation is to protect merchant vessels against strikes, to accompany them and to reinforce maritime situational awareness in the region.[^cite_note-eeas-13] It thus has a "purely defensive" mandate, something which has also been underlined by EU officials, in contrast to the US-led [Operation Prosperity Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Prosperity_Guardian).[^cite_note-euronews1902-14] The mission is instructed to coordinate closely with [Operation Atalanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Atalanta), another EU-led naval mission in the wider region.[^cite_note-councilaspidespress-1]

The EUNAVFOR ASPIDES Area of Operations – AOO, according with its mandate, comprise the Baab al-Mandab Straits and the Strait of Hormuz, as well as international waters in the Red Sea, the Gulf of Aden, the Arabian Sea, the Gulf of Oman and the Persian Gulf.[^cite_note-eeasabout-15]

On 14 February 2025, Council of the EU decided to prolong the mandate of the operation until 28 February 2026.[^cite_note-16] On that day, however, the [Israeli–U.S. war with Iran](82533582.md) began, and the mission remained on high alert.[^cite_note-17]

On 9 March 2026, French president [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) announced that France is sending additional ships to the region to set up an escort mission for the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) with other states, due to the [crisis](82550763.md), in the framework of EUNAVFOR Operation Aspides.[^cite_note-USNI-03092026-5][^cite_note-18]

## Force structure

### Operation Headquarter

EUNAVFOR Aspides is led by the Operation Commander, Rear Admiral Vasileios Gryparis [HN](425896.md). He was appointed as Operation Commander on 8 February 2024 and promoted to Rear Admiral on 2 March 2024.[^cite_note-eeasabout-15]

The operational headquarters of EUNAVFOR ASPIDES is the Hellenic European Union Operational Headquarters (EL EU OHQ) in [Larissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larissa), Greece.[^cite_note-hqhellenic-19][^cite_note-councilaspidespress-1] The European External Action Service has indicated that 130 staff officers will be based in the operational headquarters.[^cite_note-eeas-13]

On 22 February 2024, the [Government of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sweden) announced that Sweden would be sending some military personnel to participate in Operation Aspides. Sweden will initially send four staff officers with the possibility to increase the number to ten.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] On 8 March, Finland made a decision to participate in the operation by sending a maximum of five soldiers with staff duties, in addition to dispatching up to two soldiers with similar responsibilities to Operation Prosperity Guardian.[^cite_note-22] On 28 March, the government of Estonia announced that one member of the [Estonian Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_Defence_Forces) will participate in the operation.[^cite_note-23] Similarly, the [Latvian National Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_National_Armed_Forces) have also been greenlighted to participate in the operation.[^cite_note-24]

#### Force Command

The Force Commander (FCdr) exercises command and control of all military forces in the area of operation. He and his staff are operating from a flagship in the Red Sea. Since 2 February 2025, the Force Commander is Commodore Michail Pantouvakis (HEL Navy), aboard Italian Destroyer&#x20;*&#x20;FEDERICO MARTINENGO*. The previous Force Commander till 2 November 2024 was the Commodore Konstantinos Pitykakis (HEL Navy) serving aboard Italian destroyer *Caio Duilio*. The post was first held by Italian Rear Admiral Stefano Costantino, based on the Italian destroyer *Caio Duilio*, and from 29 April on the Italian frigate *Virginio Fasan*.[^cite_note-FlagshipVirginioFasan-25][^cite_note-councilaspidespress-1][^cite_note-nn-hydra-26] From 15 June 2024, Netherlands Commodore George Pastoor took over the role, serving from on board the Netherlands joint support ship *Karel Doorman* till 8 August 2024.[^cite_note-NLCommand-27] From 8 August 2024 Rear Admiral Massimo Bonu took over the role of the Force Commander till 2 November 2024.

## Timeline of events

On 27 February 2024, the German frigate *Hessen* engaged and destroyed two Houthi drones.[^cite_note-55]

On 2 March, the Greek frigate *Hydra* passed the Suez Canal to join the operation in the Red Sea.[^cite_note-nn-hydra-26] On the same day, the [Italian destroyer *Caio Duilio*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_destroyer_Caio_Duilio) shot down a Houthi missile over the Red Sea. The missile was within 4 miles (6.4 km) of the destroyer when it was shot down.[^cite_note-56] On 12 March, the [Italian Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Italy\)) reported that the *Caio Duilio* had shot down two Houthi drones in self-defense.[^cite_note-57]

On 13 March, the Greek frigate *Hydra* shot down two Houthi drones.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

On 12 April, Belgian frigate [*Louise-Marie*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_frigate_Louise-Marie) failed technical operational tests in the Mediterranean Sea while en route to the area of operations, delaying her deployment indefinitely. The test failures reportedly included a case where a [RIM-7 Sea Sparrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-7_Sea_Sparrow) missile got "stuck" in its launch tube.[^cite_note-defensenews.com-42][^cite_note-marineschepen.nl-43]

On 20 March, a French Navy helicopter deployed in the area of the Gulf of Aden, the Strait of Bab el-Mandeb and the Red Sea to fight against the Houthi rebels shot down a drone, the first time this had happened.[^cite_note-60]

On 21 March, a German Navy [Sea Lynx Mk88A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Lynx) helicopter engaged and destroyed an uncrewed surface vessel (USV) when it approached a civilian convoy under tow.[^cite_note-german_heli_attack-39] On the same day, the French frigate [*Alsace*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_frigate_Alsace) shot down three Houthi [ballistic missiles](38025.md).[^cite_note-61]

As of 26 March, the French had fired 22 [Aster missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aster_missiles).[^cite_note-cap1-62]

On 6 April, the German frigate *Hessen* intercepted a missile launched from Houthi-controlled territory.[^cite_note-63]

On 25 April, the Greek frigate *Hydra* fired shots at two drones as part of its mission in the Gulf of Aden. According to reports, the incident occurred while the frigate was deployed to protect a merchant vessel in the maritime route. The Greek frigate reportedly shot at two unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) using its 127 mm cannon: one drone was shot down, and the other altered course away from the merchant vessel.[^cite_note-64]

On 27 April, the issues affecting the deployment of Belgian frigate *Louise-Marie* were resolved, and the ship set course to the area of operations in the Red Sea.[^cite_note-LouiseMarieRepaired-44]

On 8 May, the [Netherlands Ministry of Defence](9473113.md) announced *Karel Doorman* was suffering from technical issues with one of its weapons systems, delaying the deployment into the Red Sea. Independent reports confirmed the issues were with the [Goalkeeper CIWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goalkeeper_CIWS), which was deemed fully operational upon departure from the Netherlands on 21 April. After departing [Crete Naval Base](49049283.md) on 2 May, the vessel was spotted there again on 4 May, where it awaited repairs until setting sail for the area of operations on 10 May.[^cite_note-DoormanDelay1-53][^cite_note-DoormanDelay2-52][^cite_note-DoormanDelay3-54]

On 13 June, the merchant vessel *Verbena* was struck by a Houthi missile attack. A heavily wounded crewmember was evacuated to *Karel Doorman* for medical treatment.[^cite_note-65]

On 15 June, Force Command transitioned from Italian Rear Admiral Stefano Costantino to Dutch Commodore George Pastoor, serving the role from aboard *Karel Doorman*.[^cite_note-NLCommand-27]

On 7 July, the Greek frigate *Psara* repelled a Houthi-launched drone strike of four drones, downing two of them.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

On 20 July, the Italian destroyer *Andrea Doria* joined the area of operations, replacing the departing Italian frigate *Virginio Fasan*[^cite_note-NewItalianShip-28]

On 8 August, command transitioned from Dutch Commodore George Pastoor aboard *Karel Doorman*, departing the area, to Italian Commodore Massimo Bonu aboard Italian destroyer *Andrea Doria*.[^cite_note-KarelDoormanLeaves-29]

On 21 August, the entire crew of the tanker [*Sounion*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacks_on_the_Sounion) was rescued while the ship was drifting about 77 nautical miles (143 km; 89 mi) to the west of [Al Hudaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hudaydah) port, using the French frigate [*Chevalier Paul*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_frigate_Chevalier_Paul) to evacuate the crew to nearby Djibouti on 22 August 2024.[^cite_note-Maritime_Executive-31]

While engaged in rescue operations, *Chevalier Paul* sighted an approaching Houthi explosive boat and successfully engaged and destroyed it with the frigate's [20 mm Narwhal guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nexter_Narwhal).[^cite_note-68]
