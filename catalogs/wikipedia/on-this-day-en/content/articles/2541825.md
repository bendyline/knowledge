[Flag of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Russia)



[Saint Basil's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Basil's_Cathedral) on [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square) in Moscow is often considered as a cultural symbol of Russia.

**Russophilia** is the identification or solidarity with, appreciation of, or support for the [country](25391.md), [people](59510.md), [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), and [history of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Russia). One who espouses Russophilia is called a **Russophile**. Its [antonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposite_\(semantics\)) is [Russophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russophobia).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] In the 19th century, Russophilia was often linked to variants of [pan-Slavism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Slavism), since the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) was the only Slavic sovereign state during and after the [Springtime of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springtime_of_Nations).

In politics, the term has been used to describe political actors who support closer relations with the [Russian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Russian_Federation) or support its policies. Particularly in the [post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states), Russophile politicians may also support maintaining or increasing [Russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification) policies, such as [Alexander Lukashenko](156589.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

### Armenia

Further information: [Russia–Armenia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Armenia_relations) and [List of pro-Russian political parties § Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_pro-Russian_political_parties#Armenia)

The [Armenian Revolutionary Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Revolutionary_Federation), [Republican Party of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Armenia), and [Prosperous Armenia](11199781.md) are the main pro-Russian political parties in [Armenia](10918072.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

### Belarus

[Belarus](3457.md) has close political and economic ties with Russia, both being part of the [Union State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_State), the [Collective Security Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Security_Treaty_Organization), the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md), and the [Eurasian Economic Union](27290106.md). Following the [2020–2021 Belarusian protests](64399515.md) and the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), many observers have described Belarus as a Russian [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) or a [satellite state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_state).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

### China

The [People's Republic of China](5405.md) under the [leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_secretaryship_of_Xi_Jinping) of General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) has supported the Russian Federation closely following [international sanctions after Russia invaded Ukraine](70323386.md).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] China had close ties with the Soviet Union prior to the [Sino-Soviet split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Soviet_split), owing to ideological kinship between the two communist states.[^cite_note-17] Previous anti-Russian sentiment in China has greatly downgraded, due to perceived common anti-Western sentiment among Russian and Chinese nationalists.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] [Ethnic Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_China) are one of the 56 ethnic groups officially recognized by the People's Republic of China.[^cite_note-20]

According to a 2019 survey by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), 71% of Russians have a favourable view of China.[^cite_note-21] A [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov) survey conducted in the same year found that 71% of the Chinese think Russia has a positive effect on world affairs.[^cite_note-22] During the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, many [social media](5897742.md) users in China showed sympathy for Russian narratives due in part to distrust of [US foreign policy](7564.md).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] According to a Carter Center China Focus survey in April 2022, approximately 75% of respondents agreed that supporting Russia in the war in Ukraine was in China's best interest.[^cite_note-25] On the other hand, a [Genron NPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genron_NPO) poll published seven months later found that 50.6% of Chinese respondents expressed some level of opposition to Russia’s wartime actions, compared with the 39.5% who expressed that its wartime actions were "not wrong".[^cite_note-26]

### Finland

Further information: [Russia–Finland relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Finland_relations)

The [Communist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Finland) in [Finland](10577.md) during the [Cold War](325329.md) inclined towards pro-Soviet tendencies, of which the [Taistoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taistoism) movement was especially pro-Soviet.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Into the 2020s, Russophilic sentiment in Finland has persisted in various forms. In 2023, former Social Democrat representative Mikko Elo, together with Mauno Saari, founded the Russophilic organization *Naapuriseura* ('Neighbour Society'), which promotes closer ties and cultural exchange with Russia.[^cite_note-29]

The modern Finnish political landscape has also seen some pro-Russian political parties. The [Power Belongs to the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_Belongs_to_the_People) (VKK) party was notable for being the only political party in Finland with a strong, openly pro-Russian platform in 2022. VKK opposed economic sanctions imposed on Russia and expressed support for the Russian invasion of Ukraine.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] However, the party has since dissasociated from the connections to Russia after Ano Turtiainen was replaced by Antti Asikainen.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Nevertheless, other political entities have also since echoed pro-Russian positions to varying degrees. The [Truth Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_Party_\(Finland\)) in Finland, had also refused to condemn Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine and advocates for stronger bilateral relations with Russia.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] And some who have promoted pro-Russian sentiment have also been found in the [Freedom Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Alliance_\(Finland\)) party of Finland, as a former [National Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coalition_Party) member who argued that Ukraine should be Russian territory was allowed to be among the candidates of the Freedom Alliance party in the [2025 Finnish municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Finnish_municipal_elections).[^cite_note-36] Pro-Russian viewpoints have also been advocated by the Freedom Alliance member Armando Meman, who has been seen on Russian national television.[^cite_note-37]

Certain individual political activists in Finland have also been prominent for their support of Russia. [Johan Bäckman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_B%C3%A4ckman) is widely recognized for his pro-Russian views and has actively recruited Finnish volunteers to participate in the conflict in Ukraine on Russia’s side, even for a time joining the VKK party, led by [Ano Turtiainen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ano_Turtiainen).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Similarly to Bäckman, another Finnish influencer [Janus Putkonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janus_Putkonen) is known for pro-Russian rhetoric, and maintains the extremely pro-Russian Finnish language [MV-media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV-media) website from Russian occupied [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas), which is known for sharing pro-Kremlin rhetoric.[^cite_note-40] Some members of the [Finns Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finns_Party) also held pro-Russian views in the past.[^cite_note-41]

### Germany



Members of the "[Reichsbürger movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsb%C3%BCrger_movement)" protesting in [Berlin](3354.md), featuring flags of the [Ribbon of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon_of_Saint_George), of the [Donetsk People's Republic](42425747.md), and of the [Federal State of New Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiya_\(confederation\)), 2014

German philosopher [Friedrich Nietzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Nietzsche) described Russia as "the only power that has durability in it, which can wait, which can still produce something... the antithesis of that pitiable European petty-state politics and nervousness, with which the foundation of the German *Reich* has entered its crucial phase..." in his 1895 book *[The Antichrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Antichrist_\(book\))*.[^cite_note-42] [Many members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AfD_pro-Russia_movement) of the [far-right](221220.md) political party [Alternative for Germany (AfD)](38708229.md) have expressed pro-Russian or pro-[Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin) sentiments on various issues.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-\:0-44] The left-populist party [Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahra_Wagenknecht_Alliance) has expressed pro-Russian sentiments and opinions.[^cite_note-\:0-44]

### India

A poll conducted in summer 2022 shows that Indians most frequently named Russia their most trusted partner, with 43% naming Russia as such compared to 27% who named the US.[^cite_note-45]

### Indonesia

Some Indonesians have positively compared support for Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) in the [Russo-Ukrainian war](42085878.md) to support for former president [Suharto](217513.md) in the [Indonesian invasion of East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_invasion_of_East_Timor).[^cite_note-46] Russophiles are also found among the [political left](18499.md), who support Russia due to the inaugural Indonesian president, [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno)'s closeness to the Soviet Union. Pro-Russian sentiment is especially strong among members of the governing [Indonesian Democratic Party of Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Democratic_Party_of_Struggle), led by Sukarno's daughter [Megawati Sukarnoputri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawati_Sukarnoputri), who publicly criticized Ukraine and President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md).[^cite_note-47]

### Romania

Further information: [Accusations of Russian interference in the 2024 Romanian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accusations_of_Russian_interference_in_the_2024_Romanian_presidential_election)

Traditionally, relations between [Romania](25445.md) and Russia were shaped by the political system applied in both countries. Relations were cordial prior to the 19th century, and Russia helped Romania [achieving its independence from the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_War_of_Independence), the royal families of both countries later being allied. Relations developed after the [Second World War](32927.md), when Romania fell under the communist umbrella led by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), becoming a [satellite state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_state) of the USSR. However, after [Ceaușescu's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceau%C8%99escu) rise to power in 1965, relations became strained; Romania became the first country to free itself fully from the Soviet Union, and relations were mostly only cordial, as Ceaușescu promoted [his own view of communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_Theses), inspired by the Chinese and North Korean systems, rather than the Soviet vision.

After the fall of the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc), Romania became an ally of the [United States](3434750.md), joining both [NATO](21133.md) and the [European Union](9317.md), which faced criticism from Moscow. Romania's strategic position in NATO was seen as undesirable by Russia. As nationalist movements grew in Romania during the early 2020s, parties such as the [Alliance for the Union of Romanians](64666536.md) or [S.O.S. Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.O.S._Romania), parties seeking closer ties with Russia, rose to power, inadvertently dragging Romania back into a potential Russian influence zone.[^cite_note-48]

Revelations of [Russian interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accusations_of_Russian_interference_in_the_2024_Romanian_presidential_election) in the [2024 Romanian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_presidential_election) strained bilateral relations, with numerous large protests erupting across Romania after pro-Russian far-right candidate [Călin Georgescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83lin_Georgescu) won the first round of elections through supposedly corrupt means, such as falsifying his budget for the electoral campaign, alongside Russian state-sponsored [troll farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_web_brigades) and [hackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker) artificially increasing his support on social media, particularly [TikTok](56822861.md).[^cite_note-49] Due to those accusations, the [Constitutional Court of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Romania) annulled the election, while Romania's [Permanent Electoral Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Electoral_Authority_\(Romania\)) barred Georgescu from running at next year's repeated election.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Subsequently, [George Simion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Simion) replaced Georgescu as a candidate in the [2025 Romanian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Romanian_presidential_election); however, despite securing a victory in the first round, he was eventually defeated in the runoff on 18 May by his pro-European and pro-Western opponent, [Nicușor Dan](35543359.md).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

### Serbia

Further information: [Russia–Serbia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Serbia_relations)

Serbia has historically been regarded as one of the most pro-Russian countries not only in Europe but also globally, with sympathies towards Russia remaining widespread to this day. Many Serbs consider Russia to be one of their country's closest allies, particularly due to the countries' shared (or similar) Slavic heritage, culture, language, and [Eastern Orthodox Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) faith.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] According to the [European Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council_on_Foreign_Relations)' 2021 opinion poll, 54% of Serbians considered Russia as an ally. In comparison, 11% perceived the [European Union](9317.md) as an ally, and only 6% regarded the [United States](3434750.md) in the same manner.[^cite_note-58]

In 2017, the inhabitants of the Serbian village of Adžinci renamed their village [Putinovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putinovo), in honor of Vladimir Putin.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] Following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), [People's Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics_in_Serbia#People's_Patrol), a [far-right](221220.md) group, organized pro-Russian rallies in [Belgrade](55904.md), which were attended by 4,000 people.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

Public opinion surveys have shown that, even after many years into the Russian full-scale invasion and occupation of Ukraine, Serbian citizens' support for Russia has remained, on average, higher than in any other European country.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] Results of opinion polls that were conducted by the [International Republican Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Republican_Institute) across [Western Balkan](4829.md) countries in the summer of 2025 found that 52% of Serbian citizens hold "very favorable" views of Russia, while Russian President Vladimir Putin is viewed "very favorably" by 50% of Serbian citizens.[^cite_note-66] Similarly, the results of WeBalkans' opinion polls, which were conducted during the same time, found that Russia is considered as "trustworthy" by 59% of Serbian citizens, which was significantly higher than Serbian citizens' trust in any other country or international institution, including [European Union](9317.md), [United States](3434750.md), [United Kingdom](31717.md), and [NATO](21133.md), which were regarded as "trustworthy" only by 38%, 17%, 13%, and 5% of Serbian citizens respectively.[^cite_note-67]

### Ukraine

Main article: [Derussification in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derussification_in_Ukraine)

See also: [Galician Russophilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_Russophilia)



Map showing the [Russian Federation](25391.md) in dark red with Russian-occupied territories in Europe in light red



Pro-Russian rally in [Donetsk](562951.md), eastern Ukraine, April 2014

Following [Ukrainian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine) in 1991, in the [1991 Ukrainian independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), 92% (including 55% of [ethnic Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Ukraine)) voted for independence from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union),[^cite_note-68] but some Ukrainians, mostly in the east and south of the country, voted to see a more Russophile attitude of the [government](1372585.md), ranging from closer economic partnership to full national union.[^cite_note-69] Russia and Ukraine had especially close economic ties, and the Russophilic [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties_in_Ukraine), the [Party of Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Regions), became the largest party in the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md) in the [2006 Ukrainian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Ukrainian_parliamentary_election), receiving 33% of the votes. It would remain a dominant force in Ukrainian politics until the 2014 [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity). Following the 2014 Russian military intervention in Ukraine, the overall attitude of Ukrainians towards Russia and Russians has become much more negative,[^cite_note-70] with most Ukrainians favoring [NATO](21133.md)[^cite_note-71] and [European Union](9317.md) [membership](36830143.md).[^cite_note-72] Their views on Russia would further deteriorate following the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.

A survey by the [Kyiv International Institute of Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_International_Institute_of_Sociology) in 2016 found that 67% of Ukrainians had a positive attitude to Russians, but that only 8% had a positive attitude to the Russian government.[^cite_note-73] According to a February 2021 poll of the country's population, 41% of Ukrainians had a "good" attitude towards Russians (42% negatively), while in general, 54% of Russians had a positive attitude towards Ukraine.[^cite_note-74] However, this sentiment among Ukrainians collapsed following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022. In a poll in August 2022, 81% of Ukrainians expressed negative views towards Russia, 14% have neutral attitudes, and only 3% have positive ones.[^cite_note-75]

This change following the invasion in 2022 is also reflected in political attitudes. According to two polls conducted by the [Kyiv International Institute of Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_International_Institute_of_Sociology) in February and May 2022, Russophilic attitudes in Ukraine plunged in just three months, with positive attitudes towards Russia falling from 53% to 4% in [the East](13065940.md), and from 45% to just 1% in [the South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ukraine).[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] Conversely, support for [Ukrainian membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine%E2%80%93NATO_relations) in NATO skyrocketed, from 36% to 69% in the country's east, and from 48% to 81% in the south.[^cite_note-78] From a poll in April 2022, 90% of surveyed Ukrainians support stripping deputies from pro-Russian parties of their mandates, and 86% support banning the activities of these parties entirely.[^cite_note-79] As a result, the [Carnegie Endowment for International Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Endowment_for_International_Peace) announced that regardless of how the war ends, pro-Russian parties and sentiments in Ukraine are "firmly in the past".[^cite_note-80]

Besides politics, there is also increasing support for the removal of symbols of [Russian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_culture) in Ukraine, including monuments[^cite_note-81] and streets[^cite_note-82] named after notable Russians,[^cite_note-83] along with limiting [Russian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_literature) and [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_music).[^cite_note-84] Massive [decommunization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization) campaigns coupled with intensive [derussification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derussification_in_Ukraine) have been carried out since 2014, most notably the toppling of several statues of [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin) (termed Leninfall) and the renaming of many places with [Soviet-associated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) names.[^cite_note-85] Since the invasion, Ukrainian cities [demolished monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_of_monuments_to_Alexander_Pushkin_in_Ukraine) to Russian writer [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin) across the country,[^cite_note-86] and there are also hundreds of [renamed placenames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ukrainian_placenames_affected_by_derussification) due to their affiliation with Russia.[^cite_note-87]

### United Kingdom

In a 2023 interview conducted by the Ukrainian [Rada TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rada_TV), former Prime Minister of the United Kingdom [Boris Johnson](19065069.md) admitted he had a favorable view of Russian language, civilisation and culture, despite his criticism of Russia's war on Ukraine.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

[MP](3776360.md) for [Clacton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clacton_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and [Reform UK](59757202.md) leader [Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage) has made multiple comments praising Russian president Vladimir Putin[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] and pushing pro-Russian views[^cite_note-92] since the beginning of the Russo-Ukrainian war in 2014 as well as after the Russian invasion in 2022.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Along with Farage, [Your Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Your_Party_\(UK\)) leader [Jeremy Corbyn](415036.md) is also noted for not sharing the dislike of Russia promoted by mainstream politicians[^cite_note-95] and he was called "UK's Russophile-in-chief" in 2018 for his dissenting stance on the [poisoning of Sergei and Yulia Skripal](56823699.md).[^cite_note-96]

### United States

From the end of [World War II](32927.md) to the end of the [Cold War](325329.md) and also several decades afterward, the [Republican Party](32070.md) was considered to have more negative attitudes towards Russia than the [Democratic Party](5043544.md), with the former overwhelmingly perceiving Russia as one of the biggest (if not the biggest) US adversaries and threats to US's friends and allies across the world, as well as a threat to US interests on international level, which it pursued by supporting US's enemies.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] However, the perception of Russia among Republican Party members began to shift gradually from negative to positive in the 2010s, with an increasing number of Republicans and their supporters expressing positive views on Russia.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] A 2017 poll highlighted that around 32% of respondents had favorable views of Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md).[^cite_note-103] Following the Russian invasion of Ukraine, these numbers surged.[^cite_note-104] A [YouGov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouGov) poll found nearly 62% of Republicans preferred [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) over [Joe Biden](145422.md), noting that the former was a stronger leader than the latter.[^cite_note-105] Many notable Republican politicians and conservative public figures, including US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md), US Vice President [JD Vance](53396477.md), Trump's Senior Counselor and media executive [Steve Bannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bannon), politician and commentator [Pat Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Buchanan), Kentucky Senator [Rand Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul), Georgia Representative [Marjorie Taylor Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Taylor_Greene), Florida Representative [Matt Gaetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Gaetz), political commentator and live streamer [Nick Fuentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Fuentes), social media influencer [Jackson Hinkle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Hinkle), television presenter [Tucker Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson), political commentator and journalist [Megyn Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megyn_Kelly), political activist and author [Candace Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candace_Owens), and [Turning Point USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_Point_USA)'s founder [Charlie Kirk](53245776.md), have expressed support for Russia or Putin in the war against Ukraine.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

### Vietnam

Favorable perceptions of Russia in [Vietnam](202354.md) have 83% of Vietnamese people viewing Russia's influence positively in 2017.[^cite_note-108] This stems from historic [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) support for [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam) and the [Viet Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Cong) during the [Vietnam War](32611.md), as well as support for Vietnam since 1975 by both the Soviet Union and Russia.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-109]
