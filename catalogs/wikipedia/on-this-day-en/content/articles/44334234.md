# Fossil fuel subsidies



Fossil fuel subsidies per capita



Fossil fuel subsidies as share of GDP

**Fossil fuel subsidies** are [energy subsidies](16174922.md) on [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel). Under a narrow definition, fossil fuel subsidies totalled around \$725 billion (0.6% of global [GDP](12594.md))[^cite_note-\:0-1] to \$900 billion in 2024.[^cite_note-\:1-2] Under a more expansive definition, they totalled \$6.7 trillion (5.8% of GDP).[^cite_note-\:0-1] Most subsidy is for [oil](23195.md) and [natural gas](22131.md).[^cite_note-\:1-2] Subsidies are mainly on [consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_\(economics\)),[^cite_note-3] such as a lower [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) on natural gas for [residential heating](57169.md); or [subsidies](146719.md) on [production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_\(economics\)), such as [tax breaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_break) on [exploration for oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon_exploration). Or they may be free or cheap [negative externalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_Externalities); such as [air pollution](10934212.md) or [climate change](5042951.md) due to burning [gasoline](23639.md), [diesel](81761.md) and [jet fuel](1205435.md). Some fossil fuel subsidies are via [electricity generation](9540.md), such as subsidies for [coal-fired power stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal-fired_power_station).

Eliminating fossil fuel subsidies would reduce the [health risks of air pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_risks_of_air_pollution),[^cite_note-4] and would greatly reduce global [carbon emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_emissions) thus helping to [limit climate change](2119179.md).[^cite_note-5] As of 2021, policy researchers estimate that substantially more money is spent on fossil fuel subsidies than on environmentally harmful [agricultural subsidies](171866.md) or environmentally harmful [water subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_subsidies).[^cite_note-6] The [International Energy Agency](285292.md) says: "High fossil fuel prices hit the poor hardest, but subsidies are rarely well-targeted to protect vulnerable groups and tend to benefit better-off segments of the population."[^cite_note-fossil-fuels-consumption-subsidies-2022-7]



Fossil-fuel subsidies

Despite the [G20](2206608.md) countries having pledged to phase-out inefficient fossil fuel subsidies,[^cite_note-8] they continue because of voter demand,[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-Martinez-Alvarez-Hazlett-Mahdavi-Ross-2022-10] or for [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security).[^cite_note-FT20220225-11] However the [International Institute for Sustainable Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Sustainable_Development) says that, done carefully, phasing-out fossil fuel subsidies would increase energy security.[^cite_note-12]

## Definition

Fossil fuel subsidies have been described as "any government action that lowers the cost of fossil fuel energy production, raises the price received by energy producers, or lowers the price paid by energy consumers."[^cite_note-13] Including [negative externalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_Externalities) such as [health costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_costs) results in a much larger total.[^cite_note-14] Thus by the IMF definition they are far larger than by the OECD and [International Energy Agency](285292.md) (IEA) definitions.[^cite_note-iisd_presentation_fossil_fuel_subsidies-15]

Subsidies for electricity and heat may be taken into account, depending on the share produced by fossil fuels.[^cite_note-iisd_presentation_fossil_fuel_subsidies-15] Sometimes there are disputes about what definition to use: for example the UK government said in 2021 that it uses the IEA definition and does not subsidize fossil fuels,[^cite_note-16] but others said the same year that under the OECD definition it does.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Measurement

Subsidies may be estimated by adding up direct subsidies from government, comparing prices in a country to world [market prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_prices), and sometimes attempting to include the cost of damage to human health and the climate.[^cite_note-19] Setting fossil fuel prices that reflect their true cost would cut global CO<sub>2</sub> emissions by 10% by 2030, according to the IPCC in 2023.[^cite_note-20] The [International Institute for Sustainable Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Sustainable_Development) say that [G7 countries](1196634.md) should reveal their subsidies every year under [Sustainable Development Goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals) (SDG) indicator 12.c.1 (fossil fuel subsidies).[^cite_note-21]

The fiscal cost of government support for fossil fuels was 1.1 trillion USD in 2023. Most (90%) of which is related to the consumption of fossil fuels. The fiscal cost of support for residential users was 189 billion USD in 2023, while for manufacturing and other industries it was 103.8 billion USD. The [OECD](33853117.md) said that "Most of this support lacked systematic targeting towards those in greatest need, raising both equity and efficiency concerns." Economic incentives to decarbonise from fuel taxes, carbon taxes, [emissions trading systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissions_trading_system) (ETSs) and price-reducing support mechanisms - summarised in the net Effective Carbon Rate (Net ECR) - averaged EUR 14.0/tCO2e in 2023. The share of GHG emissions covered by a positive Net ECR was 42%; 27% of GHG emissions are covered by explicit carbon prices (carbon taxes or ETSs).[^cite_note-fossil-fuel-support-22]<sup>: 4 </sup>

The [OECD](33853117.md) said that "The high fiscal cost of government support for fossil fuels and low Net ECR highlight the challenges of staying on track with net zero commitments in the face of economic and geopolitical pressures. Reforms should focus on better targeting those most in need and phasing out inefficient support for fossil fuels as soon as possible to enable the release of much-needed resources for the net zero transition and help accelerate innovation for energy efficiency. Given the high costs of inaction, governments should reaffirm and implement their SDG commitment to phase out and reform inefficient support to fossil fuels to align fiscal policy with climate goals."[^cite_note-fossil-fuel-support-22]<sup>: 4 </sup>

## Effects

Subsidies on consumption reduce the price of energy for end consumers, for example the cost of gasoline for car drivers in Iran. This may win votes at elections and some people in government say it helps poorer citizens.

The consensus among economists is that the rich get most *absolute benefit* from fossil fuel subsidies,[^cite_note-why-are-energy-subsidy-reforms-so-unpopular-23] for example the poorest people do not usually own cars. But removing the subsidies may hit poor people via indirect price increases such as food prices, so they get a lot of benefit relative to their total income.[^cite_note-why-are-energy-subsidy-reforms-so-unpopular-23] Producers, such as oil companies, say that increasing taxes on them would cause unemployment and reduce national [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security).[^cite_note-24]

### Health effects

Subsidies are estimated to cause hundreds of thousands of deaths from [air pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_effects_of_air_pollution) each year.[^cite_note-25]

### Economic effects

See also: [Subsidy § Economic effects](146719.md#Economic_effects)

Fossil fuel subsidies are a negative [carbon price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_price)[^cite_note-fossil-fuel-subsidies-26] and use government money that could be spent on other things.[^cite_note-energy-subsidies-27] The [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) says that by encouraging excess energy use they can make countries more vulnerable to variation in international energy prices.[^cite_note-energy-subsidies-27] However some governments say that the subsidies are necessary to shield citizens from such variation.[^cite_note-FT20220225-11] According to the [International Energy Agency](285292.md) (IEA) phasing out fossil fuel subsidies would benefit [energy markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_market), [climate change mitigation](2119179.md) and [government budgets](4360909.md).[^cite_note-28]

### Environmental effects

See also: [Environmental impact of the petroleum industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_the_petroleum_industry), [Health and environmental impact of the coal industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_and_environmental_impact_of_the_coal_industry), [Environmental impact of electricity generation § Fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_electricity_generation#Fossil_fuels), [Externality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externality), and [Subsidy § Environmental externalities](146719.md#Environmental_externalities)

Subsidies [affect the environment](146719.md#Environmental_externalities) and removing them would save the [carbon budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_budget)[^cite_note-fossil-fuel-subsidies-26] and help [limit climate change](2119179.md).[^cite_note-29]

## Phase-out

See also: [Fossil fuel phase-out § Phase-out of fossil fuel subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_phase-out#Phase-out_of_fossil_fuel_subsidies)

Many economists recommend replacing consumption subsidies with direct payments targeted at poor people or households.[^cite_note-why-are-energy-subsidy-reforms-so-unpopular-23][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The best way to use the money saved will likely require country specific studies.[^cite_note-33] However phase-out is politically difficult.[^cite_note-Martinez-Alvarez-Hazlett-Mahdavi-Ross-2022-10]

## History

[Tax breaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_break) for [oil and gas exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon_exploration) have been in place since at least the early 20th century.[^cite_note-34]

## Subsidies by fuel

In 2023, the OECD estimated that coal subsidies amounted to 27.7 billion USD,[^cite_note-fossil-fuel-support-22]<sup>: 10 </sup> oil to 400 billion USD,[^cite_note-fossil-fuel-support-22]<sup>: 9 </sup> and gas to 343 billion USD.[^cite_note-fossil-fuel-support-22]<sup>: 11 </sup>

## Subsidies by country

See also: [Gasoline and diesel usage and pricing § Countries with subsidised gasoline](8032596.md#Countries_with_subsidised_gasoline)

The [International Energy Agency](285292.md) estimates that governments subsidised consumption of fossil fuels by US \$1 trillion in 2022.[^cite_note-fossil-fuels-consumption-subsidies-2022-7] At [their meeting in September 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_G-20_Pittsburgh_summit) the [G-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-20_major_economies) countries committed to "rationalize and phase out over the medium term inefficient fossil fuel subsidies that encourage wasteful consumption".[^cite_note-35] Many say that all fossil fuel subsidies are inefficient.[^cite_note-36]

The 2010s saw many other countries reducing energy subsidies, for instance in July 2014 Ghana abolished all diesel and gasoline subsidies, whilst in the same month Egypt raised diesel prices 63% as part of a raft of reforms intended to remove subsidies within 5 years.[^cite_note-37]

In Sept, 2021, the [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF) produced a working paper with estimates for the subsidies caused by the gap between the efficient price of fossil fuels and user prices.[^cite_note-Guardian2-38][^cite_note-IMF1-39] "Underpricing for local air pollution costs is the largest contributor to global fossil fuel subsidies, accounting for 42 percent, followed by global warming costs (29 percent), other local externalities such as congestion and road accidents (15 percent), explicit subsidies (8 percent) and foregone consumption tax revenue (6 percent)."[^cite_note-IMF1-39] Globally, fossil fuel subsidies were \$5.9 trillion which amounts to 6.8% of GDP in 2020 and are expected to rise to 7.4% in 2025.[^cite_note-40]

### Canada

The Canadian federal government offers subsidies for fossil fuel exploration and production and Export Development Canada regularly provides financing to oil and gas companies.[^cite_note-42] Canada committed to phasing out inefficient fossil fuel subsidies by 2025,[^cite_note-43] but that didn’t happen.[^cite_note-44] The government reviews itself.[^cite_note-45] Canadian provincial governments also offer subsidies for the consumption of fossil fuels. For example, Saskatchewan offers a fuel tax exemption for farmers and a sales tax exemption for natural gas used for heating.[^cite_note-46]

### China

Although the [15th Five-Year Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Five-Year_Plan) supports clean energy it also includes ‘strengthening the clean and efficient utilisation of fossil energy’.[^cite_note-47] The [energy policy of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy_of_China) emphasises [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security), and as of 2026 [coal-fired power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_power_in_China) are still being built.[^cite_note-48] The [OECD](33853117.md) details various fossil fuel subsidies.[^cite_note-49] The [Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air](68658532.md) suggests that capacity payments to coal-fired power plants should be reformed to reward flexibility and system value.[^cite_note-50]

### Iran

See also: [Economy of Iran](64704.md), [Energy in Iran](10580575.md), and [2007 Gasoline Rationing Plan in Iran § Fuel smuggling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Gasoline_Rationing_Plan_in_Iran#Fuel_smuggling)

Contrary to the subsidy reform plan's objectives, under [President Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Rouhani) the volume of [Iranian subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_subsidies) given to its citizens on fossil fuel increased 42% in 2019 to over 15% of [Iran's GDP](64704.md) and 16% of total global energy subsidies. This has made [Iran](14653.md) the world's largest subsidizer of energy prices.[^cite_note-52] This situation is leading to [highly wasteful consumption patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_subsidy_reform_plan#Consumption_patterns), [large budget deficits](64702.md#Budget), [price distortions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distortion_\(economics\)) in its [entire economy](64704.md), [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_Iran) and [very lucrative (multi-billion dollars) contraband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Iran#Smuggling) (because of [price differentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Gas_Rationing_Plan_in_Iran#Prices_in_neighboring_countries)) with neighbouring countries each year by rogue elements within the [Iranian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_government) supporting the status-quo.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

### Libya

Libya had the highest subsidy by percent GDP in 2020 at 17.5%.[^cite_note-55]

### Russia

See also: [Energy policy of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy_of_Russia)

Russia holds the world's largest natural gas reserves (27% of total), the second-largest coal reserves, and the eighth-largest oil reserves.[^cite_note-GrantLanteigneOverland2010-56] Russia is the world's third-largest energy subsidizer as of 2015.[^cite_note-57] The country subsidizes electricity and natural gas as well as oil extraction. Approximately 60% of the subsidies go to natural gas, with the remainder spent on electricity (including under-pricing of gas delivered to power stations).[^cite_note-GrantLanteigneOverland2010-56] For oil extraction the government gives tax exemptions and duty reductions amounting to about 22 billion dollars a year. Some of the tax exemptions and duty reductions also apply to natural gas extraction, though the majority is allocated for oil.[^cite_note-58] The large subsidies of Russia are costly and it is recommended in order to help the economy that Russia lowers its domestic subsidies.[^cite_note-59] However, the potential elimination of energy subsidies in Russia carries the risk of social unrest that makes Russian authorities reluctant to remove them.[^cite_note-researchgate.net-60]

### Saudi Arabia

Most energy subsidies in Saudi Arabia are implicit in nature. This is due to the fact domestic oil prices are generally below global market prices but above domestic production costs, leading to forgone revenue but not direct subsidy costs. Contrary to the estimates above, a recent paper posits that the incremental electricity subsidy in Saudi Arabia has been eliminated as a result of the 2018 domestic energy price reforms.[^cite_note-61]

### Turkey

This section is an excerpt from [Energy in Turkey § Fossil fuel subsidies and taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Turkey#Fossil_fuel_subsidies_and_taxes).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Energy_in_Turkey\&action=edit)]

In the 21st century, Turkey's fossil fuel subsidies are around 0.2% of GDP,[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-Energy_in_Turkey_BloombergG20-63]<sup>: 29 </sup> including at least US\$14 billion (US\$169 per person) between January 2020 and September 2021.[^cite_note-64] If unpaid damages (such as health damage from air pollution) are included road fuel subsidy is estimated at over 400 dollars per person per year and for all fossil fuels over one thousand dollars.[^cite_note-65] Data on finance for fossil fuels by state-owned banks and [export credit agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export_credit_agencies) is not public.[^cite_note-Energy_in_Turkey_BloombergG20-63]<sup>: 32 </sup> The energy minister Fatih Dönmez supports coal[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] and most [energy subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_subsidies#Turkey) are for coal,[^cite_note-Energy_in_Turkey_OECD-68] which the [OECD](33853117.md) has strongly criticised.[^cite_note-69] Capacity mechanism payments to [coal-fired power stations in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_coal_fired_power_stations_in_Turkey) in 2019 totalled [₺](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_lira)720 million (US\$130 million) compared to [₺](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_lira)542 million (US\$96 million) to gas-fired [power stations in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Turkey).[^cite_note-70] In 2022 these payments totalled over US\$200 million.[^cite_note-71] As of 2020, the tax per unit energy on gasoline was higher than diesel,[^cite_note-72] despite diesel cars on average emitting more lung damaging [NOx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOx) (nitrogen oxide).[^cite_note-73]

### United States

Estimates vary considerably.[^cite_note-74] The [Cato Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Institute) says that fossil fuel subsidies are negligible,[^cite_note-75] but the [Center for American Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_American_Progress) says direct subsidies are \$29 billion a year.[^cite_note-76] Because the [value of a statistical life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_of_a_statistical_life) is assumed to be zero the [health impact of burning coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_and_environmental_impact_of_the_coal_industry) and [health damage from oil products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_and_environmental_impact_of_the_petroleum_industry) via [air pollution in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution_in_the_United_States) are not estimated.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] As of 2026 the [Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) is developing guidance to estimate the [Value of Mortality Risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_of_Mortality_Risk).[^cite_note-79]

### Venezuela

As of 2026 gasoline is subsidized.[^cite_note-80]
