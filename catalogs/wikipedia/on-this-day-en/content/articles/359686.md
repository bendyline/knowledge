**Mandaluyong** ([/məndɑːˈlujɒŋ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/fd/LL-Q33298\_%28fil%29-Sy.Paul-Mandaluyong.wav/LL-Q33298\_%28fil%29-Sy.Paul-Mandaluyong.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/fd/LL-Q33298_%28fil%29-Sy.Paul-Mandaluyong.wav/LL-Q33298_%28fil%29-Sy.Paul-Mandaluyong.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*mən-dah-LOO-yong*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); Tagalog pronunciation: [\[mɐndɐˈlujoŋ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tagalog)), officially the **City of Mandaluyong** ([Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Lungsod ng Mandaluyong*, Tagalog pronunciation: [\[luŋˈsod nɐŋ mɐndɐˈlujoŋ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tagalog)), is a [highly urbanized city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Legal_classification) in the [National Capital Region](72340.md) of the Philippines. According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 465,902 people.[^cite_note-5]

Located directly east of [Manila](184334.md), Mandaluyong was originally the barrio of **San Felipe Neri** under the jurisdiction of [Santa Ana de Sapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_Manila). It became a separate municipality within the province of [Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_\(province\)) (later Manila) in 1841 and was annexed to the province of [Rizal](232458.md) in 1901, briefly serving as the latter's provincial capital in 1904. The municipality was officially renamed Mandaluyong in 1931 during the [American colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)) and became part of the [City of Greater Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_Manila) from 1942 to 1945 and finally [Metro Manila](72340.md) upon the region's establishment in 1975. It was granted cityhood in 1994, becoming the first municipality in Metro Manila to be converted into a city since the region's establishment.

Mandaluyong is home to part of [Ortigas Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Center), one of Metro Manila's principal central business districts, which it shares with [Pasig](359702.md). It is also the site of the headquarters of the [Asian Development Bank](2512.md), [Banco de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_de_Oro), and [San Miguel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_Corporation). The city is bordered by [Manila](184334.md), [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Metro_Manila), [Quezon City](284223.md), [Pasig](359702.md), [Taguig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig), and [Makati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makati), and is the [fourth-smallest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_Philippines#List) in the Philippines by land area, covering 11.26 km<sup>2</sup> (4.35 sq mi).

## Etymology

There are different stories on the origin of the name *Mandaluyong*.

One tells of how the place was abundant with a kind of tree called *luyong*, now more commonly known as *anahaw* (*[Saribus rotundifolius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saribus_rotundifolius)*),[^cite_note-ERDB-6] from which canes and furniture were made.

Another claims that the Spaniards named the place based on the report of a navigator named Acapulco, who saw the rolling hills frequently being lashed at by *daluyong* (“big waves from the sea”).[^cite_note-mandabook-7] This seems to confirm traditional pre-Hispanic stories that giant waves from the sea would meet the adjoining hills of the vast lowland, referred to as *salpukan ng alon*. Felix dela Huerta, a [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) historian, observed that the rolling topography of this land resembled giant waves of the sea.

As with the etymological legends of many Philippine places, when the foreigners asked what the place was called, the locals answered with the description "*madaluyong*" ("undulating"), later transcribed by Spanish writers as "Mandaluyong," with the addition of an “n”.

Another version of the name is based on a legend that a [Maharlika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharlika) named Luyong fell in love with Manda, the lovely daughter of a [barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) chieftain. The chieftain did not like Luyong and forbade him Manda's hand. Luyong overcame this objection by winning a series of tribal contests, as was the custom at the time. The couple settled thereafter in a place which was later called “Mandaluyong" – a term made up of [joining their names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau).[^cite_note-RidiculousEtymology-8][^cite_note-esquire-9]

## History

### Early history

Natives of Mandaluyong trace their roots to Emperor Soledan (also known as "Anka Widyaya" of the Great Majapahit Empire) and Empress Sasaban of the [Kingdom of Sapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namayan), whose son Prince Balagtas ruled as sovereign of the kingdom in about the year 1300.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

More than a century later, in about the year 1470, it expanded and was called the "[Kingdom of Namayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namayan)" with "Lakan Takhan" as sovereign. The vast Kingdom comprised what are now [Quiapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiapo,_Manila), [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel,_Manila), [Sta, Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mesa,_Manila), [Paco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paco,_Manila), [Pandacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandacan), [Malate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malate,_Manila) and [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_Manila) in [Manila](184334.md), and Mandaluyong, [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Metro_Manila), [Makati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makati), [Pasay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasay), [Pateros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pateros), [Taguig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig) and [Parañaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para%C3%B1aque).[^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-11]

### Spanish colonial era

#### Foundation

Mandaluyong was first known as a barrio of [Santa Ana de Sapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_Manila), which was part of the District of [Paco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paco,_Manila), [Province of Tondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_\(province\)) (later known as the Province of Manila). It was named **San Felipe Neri** by the Spaniards in honor of the [patron saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Philip_Neri) of [Rome](25458.md). It was separated civilly from Santa Ana de Sapa in 1841.

On September 15, 1863, San Felipe Neri established its own parish. Under the administration of the Congregation “Dulcísimo Nombre de Jesús” (lit. transl. Sweet Name of Jesus), it constructed its own church, convent and school. The Parish of San Felipe Neri played a significant role as a relay station for propagating the Katipunan during the 1896–1898 Revolution.

#### Original barrios

In 1829, the then-barrio of Mandaloyon had 10 barangays, each named after saints: San Pedro Bautista, San Roque, San Pascual, San Antonio, San Francisco, San Pedro Apostol, San Miguel, San Rafael, San Pedro Alcantara, and San Isidro. By 1840, there were a total of 14 barangays, and in 1845, when Mandaluyong became a separate pueblo, there were 17 barangays.[^cite_note-Vicencio1987-12]<sup>: 19–23, 27d </sup> This would increase to 28 barangays in 1864, until the early 1880s where the practice of naming barangays after saints were dropped and the number of barangays (which was changed to barrios) was reduced to ten: Poblacion, Buhangin, Licod Hacienda, Hagdanbato, Pasobancal, Barranca, Jolo, Punta, Santo Nino, and Zaniga.[^cite_note-Vicencio1987-12]<sup>: 35–36, 43–44 </sup>

During the American colonial era, the first recorded census in 1903 listed five barrios: Poblacion, Barangka, Hagdang Bato, Namayan, and Hulo.[^cite_note-Patricio1976-13] From these five evolved 22 sub-barrios.

#### The Philippine revolution

Mandaluyong was significant in the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) of 1896 as the baluarte (territory) of the Katipunan or "Makabuhay" group, with seventeen branches.[^cite_note-Mandaluyong.gov.ph-14]

On August 29, 1896, [Andres Bonifacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andres_Bonifacio), together with [Emilio Jacinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Jacinto) and other members of the [Katipunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katipunan) went into the house of Romualdo Vicencio at Sitio Balakbak (now Villa San Miguel) to prepare for the upcoming revolution against Spanish authority. In this site, Bonifacio read the last manifestation of the Katipunan before they transferred in Hagdan Bato, in the house of Felix Sanchez. This event is also known as the "29 De Agosto" and "Pinagtipunan" in which it is already named in two streets near the historic Barangay Hagdan Bato Itaas. It was in Barangay Hagdang Bato on August 28, 1896, where Andres Bonifacio issued a proclamation setting Saturday, August 29, 1896, as the date of the attack on Manila.

On August 30, 1896, after the successfully revolution in San Felipe Neri, the Katipuneros went to San Juan del Monte and attacked the El Polvorin (gunpowder depot) in order to amass more weapons to use against the Spaniards. This event is popularly known as the [Battle of San Juan del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Juan_del_Monte). It was also in this town that the revolutionary paper, *La Republika*, was established on September 15, 1896.

### American colonial era

On June 11, 1901, San Felipe Neri was incorporated into the newly established province of [Rizal](232458.md). During the [American Occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)), it was raised to a first-class municipality with five barrios, namely: Poblacion, Barangka, Hagdang Bato, Namayan and Hulo. By virtue of Act No. 942 dated November 6, 1903, it was consolidated with the municipality of [San Juan del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Metro_Manila) and became the seat of the municipal government.[^cite_note-15] For several months in 1904, San Felipe Neri became the capital of Rizal.[^cite_note-16] San Juan del Monte was later separated from San Felipe Neri to regain its independent municipality status on March 27, 1907.[^cite_note-Act-1625-17]

San Felipe Neri was renamed to its present name of Mandaluyong on November 6, 1931, by virtue of Act No. 3836.[^cite_note-18] Many government infrastructures are established during the American Period, including the Correctional Institute for Women, Welfareville Compound, The Boy's Town, and the [National Center for Mental Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Mental_Health).

### Japanese occupation era



Map of the [City of Greater Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_Manila), showing Mandaluyong's territory that reached up to [Marikina River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marikina_River) at the east.

From 1942 to 1945, during [World War II](32927.md), Mandaluyong formed part of the [City of Greater Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_Manila), along with Manila, Quezon City, and other nearby towns of Rizal.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-eo58-20] Also during the war, Mandaluyong lost many of her people; among them were Catholic priests and civilians. Destruction was felt all over, but with the timely arrival of the American Liberation Forces and the Philippine Commonwealth troops on February 9, 1945, the municipality was saved from further damages. That day became a red calendar day for Mandaluyong marking its liberation from the Japanese Imperial forces by the Allies.

### Philippine independence



Dambana ng Ala-ala Alay sa mga Dakilang Anak ng Mandaluyong (lit. transl. Shrine of Remembrance Offering to the Great Sons of Mandaluyong), a memorial dedicated to the gallant locals of Mandaluyong



Liberation Monument at Liberation Park, Pag-asa, Mandaluyong

After World War II, Mandaluyong began to become progressive and dramatically increase the economy. Many infrastructures, companies, and other businesses were developed in 1950s–1960s and as the result, making Mandaluyong recognized as the most developed municipality in the province of Rizal.[^cite_note-Mandaluyong.gov.ph-14]

In 1967, actor and Mandaluyong native [Pancho Magalona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancho_Magalona) ran for [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Rizal) of [Rizal](232458.md) under the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Philippines\)), but was defeated by incumbent [Nacionalista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacionalista_Party) governor [Isidro Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidro_Rodriguez_\(politician\)) from Montalban (now [Rodriguez](335181.md)).[^cite_note-21]

In May 1970, the Mandaluyong-based textile firm Continental Manufacturing Corporation of Rufino Deeunhong and his son Dewey Dee began exporting Vonnel yarn to both the [Middle East](19323.md) and [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) with the cooperation of [Mitsubishi Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Corporation) (owner of the Vonnel brand), which was credited as the first time a Philippine-manufactured good was exported to the Middle East.[^cite_note-22]

#### The martial law era

Main article: [Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos)

##### Ambush of Juan Ponce Enrile

Main article: [1972 ambush of Juan Ponce Enrile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_ambush_of_Juan_Ponce_Enrile)

Mandaluyong played a small part in Ferdinand Marcos' efforts to rationalize his [declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_No._1081) of [Martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_Law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos) because the alleged [1972 ambush of Juan Ponce Enrile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_ambush_of_Juan_Ponce_Enrile) took place in Wack Wack Village, an exclusive neighborhood in Mandaluyong, in the hours immediately preceding its implementation.[^cite_note-23] Because the alleged ambush took place in an exclusive subdivision, no independent eyewitnesses of the event have come forward, and witnesses of the immediate aftermath are few.[^cite_note-PhoenixLopez-24] This has lent credence to accounts which say that the ambush was faked, and that the site was selected specifically because it was easy to stage the incident there.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-PDItrueorfalse-26][^cite_note-27] The 14-year period which followed that night is remembered for the Marcos administration's record of [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship),[^cite_note-McCoy199909202-28][^cite_note-Abinales\&Amoroso20052-29] particularly targeting political opponents, student activists, journalists, religious workers, farmers, and others who fought against the [Marcos dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship).[^cite_note-Rappler7Youth-30]

##### Industrial growth and government takeover efforts

A prominent figure in Philippine business before Martial Law was Mandaluyong-based industrialist Domingo M. Guevara Sr., whose success began when he created Radiowealth, a brand of affordable Philippine-made appliances including radios and televisions.[^cite_note-31] This eventually became the Mandaluyong-based Guevara Enterprises which dominated the fields of electronics, communications, agriculture and industrial development, transportation, and manufacturing in the Philippines, whose headquarters was on Libertad (now D.M. Guevara).[^cite_note-DMGStory-32] National Artist [Nick Joaquin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Joaquin) noted that Guevara's projects - which included the manufacturing the "Sakbayan," which was the ever first Philippine-made car - brought the Philippine economy to the verge of Newly Industrialized Country status in the years before Martial Law.[^cite_note-Pelaez-Marfori-33] However, the growth of Guevara's businesses stopped when he refused to surrender control of his businesses to President Ferdinand Marcos during Martial Law,[^cite_note-DMGStory-32] and Marcos retaliated by making it difficult for Guevara to do business.[^cite_note-DMGStory-32][^cite_note-Pelaez-Marfori-33]

##### Separation from Rizal province

Main article: [Metro Manila](72340.md)

On November 7, 1975, Mandaluyong was formally included in newly established Metropolitan Manila by virtue of Presidential Decree No. 824[^cite_note-34] signed by President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos).

### Cityhood

Main article: [Cities of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines)

By virtue of the 1987 Constitution, Mandaluyong and the then-municipality of San Juan were represented in Congress by a single congressman.

[San Juan–Mandaluyong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_district_of_San_Juan%E2%80%93Mandaluyong) Representative [Ronaldo Zamora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_Zamora) sponsored a House Bill which eventually became Republic Act No. 7675[^cite_note-35] otherwise known as "An Act Converting the Municipality of Mandaluyong into a Highly Urbanized City to be known as the City of Mandaluyong." President [Fidel V. Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_V._Ramos) signed R.A. No. 7675 into law on February 9, 1994 (the 49th anniversary of its liberation from the Japanese), which was ratified through a plebiscite on April 10, 1994, making Mandaluyong the fifth city in [Metro Manila](72340.md). Mandaluyong became a [lone district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaluyong's_at-large_congressional_district) with its own representative in Congress. Prior to the enactment of the assailed statute, the municipalities of Mandaluyong and San Juan belonged to only one legislative district.

### Contemporary

Mandaluyong today is composed of 27 barangays divided into two political districts mainly by Boni Avenue and G. Aglipay Street.

In 2002, Mandaluyong was recognized as "The Tiger City of the Philippines" because of the dramatic improvement in the city's economy.[^cite_note-36]

## Geography



Political map of Mandaluyong

Mandaluyong lies on a heart-shaped[^cite_note-37] 21.26 square kilometers (8.21 sq mi)[^cite_note-arearef1-38][^cite_note-arearef2-39] of land, 7 kilometers (4.3 mi) southeast of [Manila](184334.md) and 8 kilometers (5.0 mi) west of [Pasig](359702.md). To the south lies [Makati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makati) across [Pasig River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River), To the southeast lies [Taguig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig) also across [Pasig River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River), to the northwest, [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Metro_Manila), and to the northeast, [Quezon City](284223.md). Thus, Mandaluyong is located at the center of Metro Manila.

### Climate

Mandaluyong's climate is classified as tropical. In winter, there is much less rainfall in Mandaluyong than in summer. This climate is considered to be Aw according to the Köppen-Geiger climate classification. The temperature here averages 27.2 °C (81.0 °F). In a year, the average rainfall is 2,093 mm (82.4 in). Precipitation is the lowest in February, with an average of 8 mm (0.31 in). With an average of 448 mm (17.6 in), the most precipitation falls in August. At an average temperature of 29.2 °C (84.6 °F), May is the hottest month of the year. January has the lowest average temperature of the year. It is 25.5 °C (77.9 °F). Between the driest and wettest months, the difference in precipitation is 440 mm (17 in). During the year, the average temperatures vary by 3.7 °C (38.7 °F).

## Economy

Mandaluyong has the fourth-highest per capita GDP of the country at [₱](227672.md)629,421 ([US\$](18717338.md)11,700).[^cite_note-GDP-57]

The city is home to a number of shopping centers, entertainment hubs, commercial establishments, high-rise offices, residential condominiums and hotels. The city is one of the important business and financial areas in the metropolis.[^cite_note-58]

### Commercial activities

Like other cities in [Metro Manila](72340.md), Mandaluyong has its own share of commercial strips and a central business district. The former commercial area, consisting mostly of banks, offices and service establishments, stretch along public transport routes thereby serving both local consumers and passers-by from the neighboring localities. Major commercial strips of the city include the stretch of [Boni Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boni_Avenue), [Shaw Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Boulevard), Libertad-Sierra Madre area, Kalentong, San Francisco, part of Felix Martinez Lorenzo or F. Martinez Ave, Sgt. Bumatay towards Barangka Drive and Pinatubo towards [EDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA). Mandaluyong's central business district is concentrated on the EDSA-Shaw-Pioneer area; it includes the [Greenfield District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenfield_District) development.[^cite_note-59]

### Industrial activities



[TV5 Media Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV5_Media_Center)

Industrial activities are mostly concentrated within the [Shaw Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Boulevard)-[Pioneer area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Street) and along the [Pasig River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River). Although prominent in the manufacture of foods, medicines and laboratory equipment, these industries are gradually declining in number, opting to relocate in newly developed industrial zones outside Metropolitan Manila. In the [Pasig River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River) area, particularly in Barangays Namayan and Mabini–J. Rizal, areas formerly industrial are now the sites for residential subdivisions and townhouses. In the EDSA-Shaw-Pioneer area, the transformation is toward a more economically profitable and globally competitive commercial activity. And since December 2013, Mandaluyong is the home of one of the largest television networks in the country [TV5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV5_\(Philippine_TV_network\)) and one of the largest pay TV operator [Cignal TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cignal_TV) (both owned by the [PLDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLDT)-backed firm [MediaQuest Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MediaQuest_Holdings) located at the [TV5 Media Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV5_Media_Center) which also serving as the master playout facility of [CNN Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_Philippines)' successor [RPTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPTV_\(TV_channel\))), along with both the master playout facility of [Solar Entertainment Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Entertainment_Corporation) and the relay playout facility of RPTV (located at the Worldwide Corporate Center which also housed the main offices of [companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vista_Land) owned by real estate magnate, former Senator, and [All TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_TV)'s owner [Manny Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manny_Villar) and formerly the [Nine Media Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Media_Corporation)-owned news channel CNN Philippines), and several radio stations associated to the Vera Group ([Mellow 94.7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DWLL), [Magic 89.9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DWTM), [99.5 Play FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DWRT-FM), [All Radio 103.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DWKX), [DWBL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DWBL-AM) and the now-defunct [DWSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DWSS-AM), all are located at the Paragon Plaza).[^cite_note-69]

### Shopping centers



SM Megamall

These super-regional supermalls each have over a hundred local and international stores and are anchored by at least one department store and supermarket or hypermarket. They are the largest malls in Metro Manila which feature not just stores but also such attractions as movie theaters, rides, skating rinks, bowling alleys and other recreational facilities. Each provides thousands of automobile parking spaces and are located mostly near [rail stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Manila_LRT_and_MRT_stations) and established [business districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_district) within the metropolis. These malls serve not only the Metro Manila and Greater Manila Area residents, but also local and foreign tourists. Among the malls in the city are [Shangri-La Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangri-La_Plaza_Mall), [SM Megamall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_Megamall), [The Podium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Podium), [St. Francis Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francis_Square), Starmall EDSA-Shaw, SM Cherry Shaw, The Marketplace Shopping Mall (the site of the Mandaluyong Public Market), Shaw Center Mall, four strip malls at the [Greenfield District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenfield_District), and pocket malls at residential condominiums.

The city is also home to [Puregold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puregold) Shaw, the supermarket chain's inaugural branch that opened in 1998.[^cite_note-LucioAndSusanCo-70]

## Transportation



[Shaw Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Boulevard), one of the major thoroughfares in the city

The city is provided with good access roads to and from adjacent cities in [Metro Manila](72340.md) through main roads such as the [Epifanio de los Santos Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA) (EDSA), [Ortigas Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Avenue) and [Shaw Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Boulevard).[^cite_note-Transportation-71]

### Land



Boni-Pioneer Underpass

Mandaluyong is primarily served by a public road network consisting of 80.93 km (50.29 mi) concrete and asphalt roads. With a total road density of 7.19 km (4.47 mi) per 1 ha (0.010 km<sup>2</sup>) of land, the city is considered to be over-served with roads.[^cite_note-Transportation-71]

The city is mainly served by EDSA, Metro Manila's main thoroughfare. Considered as the heart of the metropolis, main roads such as Ortigas Avenue and Shaw Boulevard provide inter-city linkages, while [Boni Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boni_Avenue) and F. Martinez Street serve as alternate routes in the city.[^cite_note-Transportation-71] Other major roads in Mandaluyong include the Boni-Pioneer Underpass, a 280-meter-long (920 ft) tunnel underneath EDSA connecting Boni Avenue on its western-end and [Pioneer Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Street) on the east. and [Julia Vargas Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Vargas_Avenue) in Ortigas Center. [Jeepneys](884965.md) are one of the most common modes of public transportation for commuters in the city. Aside from jeepneys, tricycles and pedicabs are also one of the important modes of public transportation in Mandaluyong, especially on alleys around the city.

### Water



Hulo station of the [Pasig River Ferry Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River_Ferry_Service).

The presence of the [Pasig River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River) stretching along the south border of Mandaluyong provides an alternative route and mode of public transportation mainly for cargo freight of industries along the river, and for commuters seeking for a faster and more direct route to and from the cities of [Pasig](359702.md) and [Manila](184334.md).[^cite_note-Transportation-71] The [Pasig River Ferry Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasig_River_Ferry_Service) has one station in the city, located at Hulo.

### Railway



[MRT Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRT_Line_3_\(Metro_Manila\)) train leaving [Shaw Boulevard station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Boulevard_station)

The city is also served by rail via the [Manila Metro Rail Transit System Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Metro_Rail_Transit_System_Line_3) (MRT-3), located along EDSA. The city is served by the three MRT-3 stations of [Ortigas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_station), [Shaw Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Boulevard_station), and [Boni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boni_station).

[Philippine National Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Railways) once served Mandaluyong through its defunct [Santa Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mesa)–[Antipolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipolo) branch until the bridge that carried it across the [San Juan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_River_\(Metro_Manila\)) collapsed in 1982.

## Healthcare

See also: [List of hospitals in Metro Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Metro_Manila)



The [National Center for Mental Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Mental_Health)

Mandaluyong has several private and public hospitals & health center, namely the privately owned Dr. Victor R. Potenciano Medical Center along EDSA and Unciano General Hospital, and the government hospital Mandaluyong City Medical Center. The city is also home to the [National Center for Mental Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Mental_Health). Many residents, specifically the middle-to-upper class medical clientele, visit the nearby [The Medical City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Medical_City) in [Ortigas Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Center), [Pasig](359702.md).

In 2007, the Mandaluyong city government, together with [non-governmental organization](46539.md) Rehabilitation and Empowerment of Adults and Children (REACH) Foundation, established a [community-based rehabilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community-based_rehabilitation) program called Project Therapy, Education, and Assimilation of Children with Handicap (TEACH), that caters to children with special needs coming from indigent families. Services given by Project TEACH include free [occupational therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_therapy), [physical therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_therapy), [speech therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech%E2%80%93language_pathology) and [special education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_education) classes.[^cite_note-72]

St. Benilde Gymnasium in La Salle Green Hills

Four well-known educational institutions in the city are the [Arellano University – Plaridel Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arellano_University), [Don Bosco Technical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bosco_Technical_College), [José Rizal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Rizal_University) and [Rizal Technological University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Technological_University).

A good number of city officials of Mandaluyong are alumni of Don Bosco,[^cite_note-73] including incumbent Mayor, [Benjamin Abalos Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Abalos_Jr.) (HS '79);[^cite_note-74] former Vice Mayor, Renato Santa Maria (HS '65);[^cite_note-75] City Councilors Edward Bartolome (HS '96),[^cite_note-76] Noel Bernardo (HS '79),[^cite_note-77] and Jonathan Abalos (HS '85).[^cite_note-78] Other notable alumni include rapper [Francis Magalona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Magalona) (HS '81);[^cite_note-79] and actor [Ricky Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Davao) (HS '78).[^cite_note-80] Meanwhile, the alumni of JRU that includes President [Ramon Magsaysay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Magsaysay), [Roderick Paulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderick_Paulate), and [Armand Fabella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Fabella). Other colleges in the city include the Our Lady of Guadalupe Colleges (specializing in Medicine and Nursing), [STI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STI_College) and AMA (both specializing in Computer Technology education, both located on [Shaw Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Boulevard)), NAMEI Polytechnic Institute (specializing in Marine Sciences), and the International Baptist College.

The city is also home to [Lourdes School of Mandaluyong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lourdes_School_of_Mandaluyong) (est. 1911), a [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan)-Marian all-boys school, located in the [Ortigas Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Center) district managed by the [OFM Capuchins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OFM_Capuchins); [La Salle Green Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Salle_Green_Hills) (est. 1959), a private co-educational school, managed by the [De La Salle Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_La_Salle_Brothers), located along Ortigas Avenue; and [Saint Pedro Poveda College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pedro_Poveda_College) (est. 1960), another all-girls institution, offering pre-school, grade school, high school, and college education. Although the official school address is Quezon City, part of the lot Poveda's campus stands on is under Mandaluyong.

[Mandaluyong High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaluyong_High_School) (est. 1977) is the oldest public high school in the city. City of Mandaluyong Science High School (est. 1996) is a public science high school on E. Pantaleon Street. The city has 18 public schools, including primary and secondary schools, all under the supervision of [Department of Education](5464548.md)'s Schools Division Office (SDO) of Mandaluyong. The Mataas Na Paaralang Neptali A. Gonzales, named after Mandaluyong native and former Senator [Neptali Gonzales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptali_Gonzales), is the largest school in Mandaluyong and the only school in the city with the [STEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science,_technology,_engineering,_and_mathematics) high school program.
