**Biała Podlaska** (Polish: [\[ˈbjawa pɔdˈlaska\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/02/Pl-Bia%C5%82a_Podlaska.ogg/Pl-Bia%C5%82a_Podlaska.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/02/Pl-Bia%C5%82a_Podlaska.ogg/Pl-Bia%C5%82a_Podlaska.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Alba Ducalis*) is a city in the [Lublin Voivodeship](344869.md) in eastern [Poland](22936.md) with 56,498 inhabitants as of December 2021.[^cite_note-population-3][^cite_note-TERYT-5] It is the capital of [Biała County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82a_County,_Lublin_Voivodeship), although the city is not part of the county (it constitutes a separate city county). The city lies on the [Krzna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzna) river.

First recorded in the [medieval period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_period), Biała Podlaska is a former residential city of the once influential magnate [Radziwiłł family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82_family), whose landmarks include a Palace and Park ensemble and [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) and [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) churches. It is the location of one of the oldest [high schools](554992.md) in Poland, whose student was the most prolific Polish 19th-century writer [Józef Ignacy Kraszewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Ignacy_Kraszewski), the birthplace of [virtuoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtuoso) violinist [George Bridgetower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bridgetower), a former pioneering centre of [faience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faience) manufacturing in Poland, and a former [aircraft manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podlaska_Wytw%C3%B3rnia_Samolot%C3%B3w) centre. It was a place of Nazi German-perpetrated atrocities against Jews, Poles and Italians during the [German occupation of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Poland) in [World War II](32927.md) with over 4,000 victims. In 1975–1998, it was the capital of the [Biała Podlaska Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82a_Podlaska_Voivodeship).

## History

### Early history

The first historical document mentioning Biała Podlaska dates to 1481. In the beginning, Biała Podlaska belonged to the Illnicz family. The founder of the city may have been Piotr Janowicz, nicknamed "Biały" (Polish for "white"), who was the [hetman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetman) of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania). Biała Podlaska was administratively part of the [Podlaskie Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podlaskie_Voivodeship_\(1513%E2%80%931795\)), and then the [Brest Litovsk Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest_Litovsk_Voivodeship) in the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania) (then in [union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_union) with [Poland](22936.md)).[^cite_note-bialapodlaska.pl-6]

In 1569, Biała Podlaska changed ownership; the new owners were the [Radziwiłł family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82_family). Under their rule, Biała Podlaska had been growing for two and a half centuries. In 1622, [Aleksander Ludwik Radziwiłł](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksander_Ludwik_Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82) built the fortress and the castle. In 1628, Krzysztof Ciborowicz Wilski established Bialska Academy as a regional center of education (since 1633 it was a branch of the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University), then called Kraków Academy). It was one of the best schools in Poland in that time (about 1650–1700). During this time, many churches were erected, as was a hospital.



Castle tower and the regional museum at the [Radziwiłł](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82_family) park in Biała Podlaska

The prosperity period ended with the [Swedish invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deluge_\(history\)) in 1655. Then Biała Podlaska was attacked by [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) and [Rákóczi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_II_R%C3%A1k%C3%B3czi) armies. The town was significantly destroyed; however, thanks to [Michał Kazimierz Radziwiłł](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha%C5%82_Kazimierz_Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82) and his wife [Katarzyna Sobieska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katarzyna_Sobieska), it was rebuilt. In 1670, Michał Kazimierz Radziwiłł gives Biała Podlaska town rights and the coat of arms, which depicts [archangel Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_\(archangel\)) standing on a dragon.[^cite_note-bialapodlaska.pl-6]

In 1720, [Anna Katarzyna Radziwiłłowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Katarzyna_Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82owa) began building the tower and the gate - both buildings exist to this day and are the most interesting remains of the castle and palace. In the 18th century, the city and the fortress were times destroyed many times in warfare and rebuilt. The last heir, [Dominik Hieronim Radziwiłł](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominik_Hieronim_Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82), died on 11 November 1813 in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire), as a colonel of the Polish army. The palace fell into ruin and was pulled down in 1883.[^cite_note-bialapodlaska.pl-6]

In 1738, on the initiative of [Anna Katarzyna Radziwiłłowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Katarzyna_Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82owa), a [faience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faience) factory was established in Biała Podlaska, marking the beginning of continuous faience production in Poland.[^cite_note-7] In 1751, a theater was founded that drew foreign artists to Biała Podlaska.[^cite_note-lppe-8]

### Late modern period

After the [Third Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Partition_of_Poland) in 1795, Biała Podlaska temporarily came under the rule of Austrian [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) (Austrian partition of Poland). Following the [Austro-Polish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Polish_War) of 1809, temporarily recovered by Poles, Biała Podlaska was part of the Polish [Duchy of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Warsaw). In 1815, the town became part of [Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland) within the [Russian Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Partition) of Poland. In the 1820s, [Józef Ignacy Kraszewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Ignacy_Kraszewski), the most prolific Polish writer of the 19th century, attended the local school.[^cite_note-lppe-8] He wrote his first work and edited the school newspaper in Biała Podlaska, and later, he featured the city in his several of his novels.[^cite_note-lppe-8] In the 1880s, another prominent Polish writer, [Stefan Żeromski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_%C5%BBeromski), visited the city several times.[^cite_note-lppe-8] At the end of the 19th century, Biała Podlaska was a large [garrison town](1033489.md) of the [Imperial Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army). Near the intersection of Brzeska Street and Aleje Tysiclecia Avenue is a cemetery for soldiers killed during [World War I](4764461.md).



County office in 1937

In 1918, following World War I, Poland regained independence and the town was reunited with Poland. During the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) in the interwar period, Biała Podlaska was growing fast. The town was the seat of the [Podlaska Wytwórnia Samolotów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podlaska_Wytw%C3%B3rnia_Samolot%C3%B3w) (PWS), an important aircraft factory.

There was also a garrison of the 34th infantry regiment of the [Polish Armed Forces](40153898.md). The regiment, formed in 1919, fought successfully in the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Soviet_War), and also fought against Germans and Soviets during the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), which started [World War II](32927.md) in 1939. During the Polish–Soviet War, on 1 August 1920, Russians invaded the city, but were quickly repelled by the Poles, and later on they invaded again, before the town was eventually liberated by the Polish [1st Legions Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Legions_Infantry_Division_\(Poland\)) on 17 August 1920.[^cite_note-9] The last commander of the regiment, lieutenant colonel Wacław Budrewicz, was taken prisoner of war by the Soviets and murdered by them in the 1940 [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre), in which also many Polish teachers and policemen from the city, and alumni of local schools were murdered.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

### World War II



Memorial at the site of public executions of Poles carried out by the Germans in November 1943

World War II halted the town's development because of [Nazi and Soviet repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland). The Germans captured Biała Podlaska on 13 September 1939, but withdrew on 26 September to allow the [Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) to station in the town. But, on 10 October 1939, in accordance with the terms of the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact), the Soviets departed and the town was reoccupied by the Germans. By that time, the Soviets had already completely plundered the PWS aircraft factory, so that nothing but empty buildings remained.

Poles [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Poles_by_Nazi_Germany) by the Germans in 1939–1940 from various places in German-annexed western Poland were deported to the area, while their previous homes were handed over to [German](152735.md) colonists as part of the *[Lebensraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensraum)* policy.[^cite_note-12] In March and July 1940, the Germans imprisoned dozens of [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) in the local prison, and then massacred them in the nearby Grabarka forest.[^cite_note-mw-13] Local [starost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starost) (head of county administration) Mieczysław Lutman was murdered by the Russians in the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre) in 1940.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Over 40 Polish teachers were arrested in the town on 24 June 1940, imprisoned in Lublin and then deported to [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps), as part of the *[AB-Aktion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_AB-Aktion_in_Poland)*.[^cite_note-16] On 5 July 1940 the Germans carried out another massacre of Poles in Grabarka, whom they previously imprisoned in the town.[^cite_note-mw-13] Further massacres of Poles were carried out by the Germans throughout the war. Despite such circumstances, the [Polish resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II) was active in the city.



Cemetery of 406 Italian prisoners killed by the Germans in 1943–1944

After Germany attacked the Soviets in [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), a [German prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) was set up near Biała Podlaska for Soviet and Polish POWs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorek200411-17] In 1942, they were replaced by some 600 [French POWs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_prisoners_of_war_in_World_War_II) brought from [Rawa Ruska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawa_Ruska), who were soon relocated to a camp in nearby [Hola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hola,_Bia%C5%82a_Podlaska_County).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorek200411-17] In 1943, after [Italian surrender to the Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Cassibile), [Italian Military Internees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Military_Internees) were brought to the city and placed in a local subcamp of the [Stalag 366](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_366?action=edit\&redlink=1) POW camp.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorek200412-18] The Germans subjected the Italians to mass starvation, epidemics, harassment and beatings, and over 400 Italians died in the camp.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorek200414–16-19] Poles provided food to starving Italians, despite the threat of being shot on sight by German guards, for both Italians and Poles for such attempts, with confirmed cases of such killings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorek200415-20] There was also a [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) subcamp for some 200–300 Italian POWs in the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorek200413-21]

On 12 April 1944, the [14th Waffen Grenadier Division of the SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Waffen_Grenadier_Division_of_the_SS_\(1st_Galician\)), composed of Ukrainian nationalists collaborating with Germany, [pacified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacification_actions_in_German-occupied_Poland) the present-day district of [Sielczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sielczyk), burning twelve houses and murdering nine Polish men and women.[^cite_note-22]

In 1944 the town was recaptured by Polish and Soviet troops and restored to Poland, although with a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-installed communist regime, which remained in power until the [Fall of Communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Communism) in 1989.

### Recent history

In the postwar period and after the fall of communism, Biała Podlaska has developed as a more modern city. It retains many of the historic features in the central Polish old town of the city. From 1975 to 1999 Biała Podlaska was a capital of the voivodeship, later it again became a city county, as before 1975.

### History of the Jewish community

The first mention of Jewish settlement in Biała Podlaska dates from 1621, when 30 Jewish families were granted rights of residence there. By 1841, there were 2,200 Jews of a total population of 3,588 in the town. In 1897, the number was 6,549 out of 13,090 inhabitants. In the 19th century, the [chasidic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasidic_Judaism) movement established strong roots in Biała Podlaska.

A descendant of the Yid Hakodosh of [Przysucha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przysucha) formed the [Biala chasidic court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biala_\(Hasidic_dynasty\)), which survives to this day with communities in [London](17867.md), and cities in the United States and Israel. The chasidim of [Kotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Mendel_of_Kotzk) also had a large presence in Biała Podlaska, some of whom later became [Gerrer chasidim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ger_\(Hasidic_dynasty\)). In [sovereign Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) by 1931, the Jews constituted 64.7% of the total population, or 6,923 out of 10,697 citizens. Four [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish) newspapers were published locally between the two world wars.

The Germans captured Biała Podlaska on 13 September 1939 during the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) but withdrew on 26 September. On 10 October 1939, the Soviets handed the town back to the Germans when [the line of demarcation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division_of_Polish_territories_during_World_War_II) was finally set up. Around 600 Jews escaped the town during the Soviet departure. The Germans formed a [Judenrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judenrat) in November 1939, which set up a public kitchen and a Jewish infirmary.[^cite_note-no-copyvio-23] By the end of the year, the Nazis began to impose discriminatory restrictions against the Jews. On 1 December 1939, they decreed that all Jews had to wear a yellow [Star of David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_of_David). Jews were ordered to move into an open-type ghetto along the Grabanów, Janowa, Prosta and Przechodnia streets, and a [Jewish Ghetto Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Ghetto_Police) was established. At the end of 1939, some 2,000 to 3,000 more Jews were brought there as a result of deportations from [Suwałki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwa%C5%82ki) and [Serock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serock). The overcrowding and poor sanitations resulted in a [typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhus) epidemic in Biała Podlaska in early 1940, causing many deaths.[^cite_note-no-copyvio-23] The Germans imprisoned some Poles in the local prison and then deported them to concentration camps for [helping and rescuing Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_of_Jews_by_Poles_during_the_Holocaust).[^cite_note-24]

Several hundred more Jews were brought in from as far as [Kraków](16815.md) and [Mława](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C5%82awa) during German "resettlement" actions conducted in 1940 and 1941. The men were sent to new labour camps in the Wola district at an airfield, the train station, and elsewhere. Hundreds were forced to pave roads, drain ditches, construct sewage lines and build barracks. Many Jewish women worked in the Nazi farms. In March 1942 the ghetto had 8,400 inmates.[^cite_note-no-copyvio-23]

#### The Holocaust



Biała Podlaska Ghetto liquidation action conducted in 1942

After the launch of [Operation Reinhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Reinhard) on 6 June 1942– the code name for the most deadly phase of [the Holocaust in occupied Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_occupied_Poland) – the Jews were told to prepare for "resettlement". Only workers from the forced labour camps possessing labour permits were to be exempt from the deportation. On 10 June 1942, some 3,000 Jews, including women with children, were assembled in the synagogue courtyard. Many Jews fled to the forests. The assembled Jews were led by the German police to the train station. The next day the prisoners were packed into [deportation trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_train) and sent to [Sobibór extermination camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobib%C3%B3r_extermination_camp). All were gassed.[^cite_note-no-copyvio-23][^cite_note-25]

In September 1942, some 3,000 Jews from the neighbouring towns of [Janów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan%C3%B3w,_Lublin_County) and [Konstantynów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantyn%C3%B3w,_Lublin_Voivodeship) were brought into Biała Podlaska Ghetto. The overcrowding became desperate. The Jews sensed that the ghetto was slated for liquidation. Many escaped to the forests; others prepared hiding places in the basements.[^cite_note-no-copyvio-23] On 6 October 1942, the Germans deported about 1,200 Jews from the local labour camps to [Międzyrzec Podlaski Ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C4%99dzyrzec_Podlaski_Ghetto). The subsequent "deportation actions" conducted by the Nazi German [Reserve Police Battalion 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Police_Battalion_101), augmented by the Ukrainian [Trawnikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trawnikis), lasted throughout October and November 1942. In total, some 10,800 Jews from around Biała Podlaska and its county were murdered at the [Treblinka extermination camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treblinka_extermination_camp), 125 kilometres (78 mi) away, or massacred on the spot during roundups.[^cite_note-Robertson-26]

[Chil Rajchman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chil_Rajchman), a *[Sonderkommando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonderkommando)* who survived the Treblinka revolt and the war, later testified that he witnessed a transport of 6,000 Jews from Biała Podlaska arrive at Treblinka. When the sealed doors were opened, 90 percent of prisoners – men, women, and children – were already dead inside. Their bodies were thrown into smouldering mass grave at the "Lazaret".[^cite_note-Kopówka-27]

The remaining Jews of Biała Podlaska were sent to a transit point at the Międzyrzec Podlaski Ghetto for deportations to death camps.[^cite_note-Robertson-26] In July 1943 the transit ghetto in [Międzyrzec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C4%99dzyrzec) was liquidated. All its inmates were deported to [Majdanek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majdanek) and [Treblinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treblinka), where they were murdered in the [gas chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chamber).[^cite_note-Robertson-26] The Nazis left a small group of 300 Jewish slave labourers in Biała Podlaska to clean up the decaying ghetto area. In May 1944, the surviving workers were murdered at Majdanek. Biała Podlaska was captured by the Red Army on 26 July 1944. Only 300 of the Jews who had lived in Biała Podlaska are known to have survived the Holocaust. Most of them left Poland after the war.[^cite_note-no-copyvio-23]

#### Remembrance

The parts of the city which were originally the Jewish "quarter" still exist.[^cite_note-28] The Jewish community is commemorated by a memorial erected at the site of the Jewish cemetery, which was destroyed by the Nazis.[^cite_note-Hały-29] Another memorial was recently erected by Jewish survivors from the town who now live in the USA. Two former private prayer houses of the Jewish community are still in existence.[^cite_note-30] The cemetery otherwise stands as a reminder of the hole that was ripped out of Biała Podlaska life by the Holocaust and loss of so many Jews. Apart from [Israel](9282173.md), [Melbourne](17306237.md) in Australia has the largest number of Jewish survivors from Biała Podlaska - all now very aged.

Among the local art galleries are the *Galeria Podlaska*,[^cite_note-32] *Galeria Ulica Krzywa* (Crooked Street Gallery),[^cite_note-33] *Bialskie Centrum Kultury* (the Biała Podlaska Cultural Center), and *Galeria Autorska Jakusza Maksymiuka* (Janusz Maksymiuk's Gallery).

There are two popular cinemas in Biała Podlaska including *Novekino Merkury* - 1 hall, 282 seats, digital cinema 3D, as well as *Cinema 3D* - multiplex situated in *Rywal* Shopping Center; 4 halls; digital cinema 3D, 4K (Ultra HD). Plays are staged in the auditorium of the Pope John II State School of Higher Education in Biała Podlaska and in amphitheater in Radziwiłł park.

There are also several cultural centers in the city including Bialskie Centrum Kultury, *Scena*, *Piast*, and *Eureka*.

## Transport



Main railway station

The city is a major transport hub: [national road 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_road_2_\(Poland\)), which is also the [european route E30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E30), two [voivodeship roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeship_road) (DW811, DW812) and national railway line 2, pass through the town.

Biała Podlaska has its own bus lines. The organizer of the communication is the Management of Urban Transport (Polish: ZKM - Zarząd Komunikacji Miejskiej). Buses operate 8 lines marked with letters from "A" to "H" (frequency approx. 30 min). On the basis of an agreement between the neighboring villages, buses carry courses outside the administrative boundaries of the city.

The [Biała Podlaska Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82a_Podlaska_Airport) has one of the longest runways in Poland. The airport was used for military purposes, however since 2020 the airport is closed.[^cite_note-airport-37]
