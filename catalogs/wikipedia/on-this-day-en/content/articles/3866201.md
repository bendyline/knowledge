**Radio France Internationale**, usually referred to as **RFI**, is the [state-owned](20803507.md) international radio news network of [France](5843419.md). With 59.5 million listeners in 2022, it is one of the most-listened-to international radio stations in the world, along with [Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle), the [BBC World Service](37627.md) and [Voice of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_America).[^cite_note-Who_are_we-1][^cite_note-auto-2]

RFI broadcasts 24 hours per day around the world in French and in 16 other languages in FM, shortwave, medium wave, satellite and on its website. It is a channel of the state company [France Médias Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_M%C3%A9dias_Monde). The majority of shortwave transmissions are in French and [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_language) but also includes some hours of [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language), [Fulfulde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulfulde_language) and [Mandinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_language). RFI broadcasts to over 150 countries on 5 continents.[^cite_note-auto-2] Africa is the largest part of radio listeners, representing 60% of the total audience in 2010.[^cite_note-3] In the [Paris region](1716427.md), RFI comprises between 150,000 and 200,000 listeners. Its digital platforms attract an average of 24.6 million visits a month (2022 average) while 31.1 million followers stay connected via Facebook, Twitter and Instagram and Youtube.[^cite_note-Who_are_we-1]

In 2020, the audience was of 58.1 million listeners (up to 11.6 million compared to 2019, +25%), breaking down into 29.8 million in French-speaking Africa, 11 million in non-French speaking Africa, 2 million in the Maghreb region, 1.3 million in Europe, 13 million in the Americas and 1 million in Asia.[^cite_note-4]

## History



Logo of RFI from 1996 until June 2013.

RFI was created in 1975 as part of [Radio France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_France) by the [Government of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_France), and replaced the [Poste Colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poste_Colonial?action=edit\&redlink=1) (created in 1931), [Paris-Mondial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Mondial) (1937), [Radio Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Paris) (1939), a private station which was commandeered by the Germans during the [occupation of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_military_administration_in_occupied_France_during_World_War_II), and the Voice of France which was operated by the [Vichy regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France) from 1941 to 1944, [RTF Radio Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTF_Radio_Paris?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1945) and [ORTF Radio Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ORTF_Radio_Paris?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1965).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] In 1986 the [French Parliament](1009776.md) changed the law to allow RFI to operate independently of Radio France.[^cite_note-8]

RFI operates under the auspices and primary budget of the [Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs](250002.md).[^cite_note-9] It broadcasts primarily in French, but also in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language), [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_language), [Spanish](26825.md), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Language), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), [Vietnamese](32511.md), [Cambodian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_language) and as of 2015, [Manding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manding_languages).[^cite_note-10]

Until 2012, RFI owned [Monte Carlo Doualiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Doualiya) (formerly Radio Monte Carlo Middle East), which produces [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) programmes in Paris, and airs them from a transmitter in Cyprus to audiences across the Middle East and North Africa.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Incidents

On 17 September 2002, [Togolese](30138.md) President [Gnassingbé Eyadéma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnassingb%C3%A9_Eyad%C3%A9ma) tried to stop the broadcasting of an interview with one of his opponents, [Agbéyomé Kodjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agb%C3%A9yom%C3%A9_Kodjo), by phoning directly to the [Elysée Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elys%C3%A9e_Palace). The interview was not [censored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship) by [Jean-Paul Cluzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Paul_Cluzel), RFI's CEO at the time, due to the coordinated intervention of the journalists' trade unions. However, a report raising questions regarding the [French secret services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direction_G%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale_de_la_S%C3%A9curit%C3%A9_Ext%C3%A9rieure) responsibilities in the 1995 death of judge [Bernard Borrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Borrel?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Djibouti](17207794.md), which was broadcast on 17 May 2005, was later removed from RFI's website for undisclosed reasons, possibly due to the intervention of Djiboutian President [Ismail Omar Guelleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Omar_Guelleh).[^cite_note-13]

On 21 October 2003, [Jean Hélène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_H%C3%A9l%C3%A8ne) was reporting for RFI during the [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Ivorian_Civil_War) in [Ivory Coast](57744.md) when he was killed in [Abidjan](21371767.md) by police sergeant Théodore Séry Dago.

On 2 November 2013, RFI reporting team [Ghislaine Dupont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghislaine_Dupont) and [Claude Verlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Verlon) were murdered while covering the [Mali](19127.md) elections. The [United Nations](31769.md) set their death date to commemorate the [International Day to End Impunity for Crimes Against Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Day_to_End_Impunity_for_Crimes_Against_Journalists) each year.[^cite_note-rsf-14]

In November 2020, RFI mistakenly published numerous obituaries of famous people on its own web site, as well as sending them to related web sites, after moving draft stories to a new system.[^cite_note-15]

The government of [Niger](21373.md) suspended two state-owned media outlets ([France 24](1263244.md) and RFI) following the [2023 Nigerien coup d'état](74454838.md).[^cite_note-16]

## Podcasts

RFI offers a daily podcast in simple [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) named *Journal en français facile*.[^cite_note-17] There are also several other podcasts including the weekly *[Afrique Presse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrique_Presse?action=edit\&redlink=1)*,[^cite_note-18] which is hosted by Assane Diop and discusses the most important news in Africa that week.
