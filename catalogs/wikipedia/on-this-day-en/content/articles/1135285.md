**Football Club des Girondins de Bordeaux** (French pronunciation: [\[ʒiʁɔ̃dɛ̃ də bɔʁdo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)), commonly referred to as **Girondins de Bordeaux** ([Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language): *Girondins de Bordèu*) or simply **Bordeaux**, is a French [football](10568.md) club based in the city of [Bordeaux](4097.md) in [Gironde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gironde_\(department\)), [Nouvelle-Aquitaine](45092904.md). They compete in the [ Regional 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_1), the sixth tier of [football in France](1963109.md), after an administrative double relegation in 2024 and 2026.[^cite_note-4]

Bordeaux was founded on 1 October 1881 as a [multi-sports club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_club) and it is one of the most successful football clubs in France. The club has won six [Division 1/Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) titles, the last in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Ligue_1). Bordeaux have also won four [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) titles, three [Coupe de la Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_la_Ligue) titles, and three [Trophée des champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troph%C3%A9e_des_champions) titles. In international competitions Bordeaux has reached the [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_cup) final in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_UEFA_Cup) and the [Latin Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Cup) final in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Latin_Cup). From the year of its inception to 2015 the club's stadium was the [Stade Chaban-Delmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Chaban-Delmas), after which Bordeaux's home ground was moved to the [Stade Atlantique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Atlantique).[^cite_note-Girondins-5][^cite_note-Profile-6]

## History

### Beginnings

The club took its name *[Girondins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girondins)* from the demonym for people from the [department](38523.md) of [Gironde](64654.md), and was founded on 1 October 1881 as a [gymnastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnastics) and [shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_sports) [club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_club). The club, chaired by André Chavois, later added sports such as [rowing](25736.md), [equestrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrianism), and [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_\(sport\)), among others. It was not until 1910 when football was officially introduced to the club following strong urging from several members within the club, most notably club president Raymond Brard, though it was only available on a [trial](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/trial) basis. The experiment with football lasted only a year before returning almost a decade later in 1919. The club contested its first official match in 1920 defeating Section Burdigalienne 12–0.[^cite_note-LesGirondins-7]

Bordeaux achieved professional status in football on 2 July 1936, partly due to the club's merger with fellow *[Bordelais](4097.md)* outfit Girondins Guyenne Sport, which resulted in the club that exists today. Bordeaux's rise to professionalism came about alongside the [French Football Federation](682002.md)'s plea to increase professionalism in [French football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_football), which prior to 1932, had been non-existent. The club was inserted into the [second division of French football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2) and made its debut appearance during the 1937–38 season. The club's first manager was Spaniard [Benito Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_D%C3%ADaz). Diaz brought fellow Spanish players Santiago Urtizberea and Jaime Mancisidor to the team with the latter serving as [captain](633462.md). The club's most prominent [Frenchmen](5843419.md) on the team were homegrown [attacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striker_\(association_football\)) Henri Arnaudeau and [goalkeeper](5348091.md) [André Gérard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_G%C3%A9rard). Bordeaux played its first official match on 23 May 1937 defeating [Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes)-based FC [Scionzier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scionzier) 2–1 at the [Stade de Colombes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Olympique_Yves-du-Manoir). The club's first ever league match was contested on 22 August losing away to [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse_FC_\(1937\)) 3–2. Bordeaux recorded its first league win against [Nîmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%AEmes_Olympique). Unfortunately for the club, the team finished 6th in the Southern region of the division. Bordeaux's disappointing finish inserted the club into the relegation playoff portion of the league where the team finished a respectable 3rd. A year later, Bordeaux moved into a new home, the [Stade Chaban-Delmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Chaban-Delmas), which had previously been known as, simply Parc Lescure. The facility was built specifically for the [1938 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup) and, following the competition's completion, was designated to Bordeaux. The club had formerly played its home matches at the Stade Galin, which today is used as a [training ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_ground).[^cite_note-LesGirondins-7]

### Success and stability



Trophy of the centenary tournament of Girondins de Bordeaux

On 15 October 1940, Bordeaux merged with local club AS Port and took on one of the club's most prestigious traditions, the [scapular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scapular). Bordeaux ASP, which the club was now known, adorned the scapular during its run to the [1941 edition of the Coupe de France final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France_Final_1941). The match, played in [occupied France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_France_during_World_War_II) at the Stade Municipal in [Saint-Ouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Ouen,_Seine-Saint-Denis), saw Bordeaux defeat [SC Fives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Fives) 2–0 with Urtizberea netting both goals. The [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) triumph was the club's first major honour. Following the [liberation of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_France), Bordeaux returned to league play and earned promotion to the [first division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) following its 2nd-place finish during the 1948–49 season. After the season, André Gérard, now manager of the club, signed [Dutchman](21148.md) [Bertus de Harder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertus_de_Harder). Led by the three-headed monster of De Harder, [Édouard Kargu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Kargu), and [Camille Libar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Libar), Bordeaux captured its first-ever league championship, in just the club's first season in the first division, winning by six points over second place [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille_OSC). The league success led to Bordeaux being selected to participate in the second edition of the [Latin Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Cup). In the competition, Bordeaux reached the final drawing 3–3 with [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Portugal) outfit [Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.L._Benfica). The draw forced a second match with Benfica claiming victory following an [extra time](633242.md) goal after over two hours and 25 minutes of play.[^cite_note-LesGirondins-7]

Bordeaux maintained its title-winning aspirations finishing runners-up to [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OGC_Nice) two seasons after winning its first title. The club also performed well in cup competitions reaching the Coupe de France final in [1952](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Coupe_de_France_Final) and [1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_Coupe_de_France_Final). In 1952, Bordeaux suffered defeat to the team it finished runner-up to the same year, Nice, following a thrilling match in which eight goals were scored with five of them coming in the first 40 minutes. Bordeaux drew the match at 3–3 following a 55th-minute goal from Henri Baillot, but Nice countered minutes later with two goals in a span of four minutes to go up 5–3, which was the final result. In 1955, Bordeaux were trounced 5–2 by Lille who went up 4–0 within 35 minutes. The resulting struggles in the cup competitions led to struggles domestically with the club suffering relegation in the 1955–56 season. The club returned to the first division for the 1959–60 season, but failed to make an impact falling back to [Division 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2) after finishing last in the standings with 21 points.[^cite_note-LesGirondins-7]

Bordeaux returned to its former self in the 1960s under new manager and former player [Salvador Artigas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Artigas). Under the helm of Artigas, Bordeaux returned to the first division and finished in a respectable fourth place for the 1962–63 season. The following season, Bordeaux [returned to the Coupe de France final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Coupe_de_France_Final) where the club faced off against [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais). Bordeaux, once again, were defeated 2–0 courtesy of two goals from the [Argentine](18951905.md) [Nestor Combin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestor_Combin). The club's runner-up finish resulted in the team qualifying for the [1964–65 Inter-Cities Fairs Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964%E2%80%9365_Inter-Cities_Fairs_Cup). The appearance was brief with the club losing 4–3 on aggregate to German club [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund). Four seasons later, Bordeaux again reached the final of the Coupe de France, the club's seventh appearance overall. The team faced [Saint-Étienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Saint-%C3%89tienne) and, again failed to match the achievement reached in 1941 losing 2–1. The following season, Bordeaux earned another appearance in the final, but again, failed to win the trophy losing 2–0 to [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille). The team suffered an extreme decline during the 1970s, despite the arrival of [Alain Giresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Giresse). The club played under seven different managers during the decade and consistently finished at the bottom half of the table. In 1979, the club was sold to the influential and ambitious real estate [mogul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnate) [Claude Bez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bez), who positioned himself as president of the club. In the summer of 1983, Girondins de Bordeaux organised a centenary tournament; Bordeaux won a 2–0 victory over [Barcelona](68187.md) in the semi-finals of this tournament, and in the final, the club was defeated by [VfB Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfB_Stuttgart).[^cite_note-LesGirondins-7][^cite_note-8]

### Return to prominence in the 1980s



Bordeaux's home kit of their victorious 1984–85 [Division 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) season



[Alain Giresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Giresse), influential Bordeaux player in the '70s and '80s and the club's all-time top scorer

Under the helm of [Claude Bez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bez), who injected millions into the club, Bordeaux flourished winning three league championships, two [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) titles, and also performed well in [European](32332.md) competitions, most notably reaching the European Cup semi-final in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_European_Cup). During Bez's run presiding over the team, he recruited several [French internationals](250197.md) such as [Bernard Lacombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lacombe), [Jean Tigana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Tigana), [René Girard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Girard_\(footballer\)), [Jean-Christophe Thouvenel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Christophe_Thouvenel), and [Thierry Tusseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Tusseau). Bez also brought in established manager [Aimé Jacquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aim%C3%A9_Jacquet). Led by 1970s mainstays Giresse and [Gernot Rohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gernot_Rohr), Bordeaux captured its first league championship since 1950 in the [1983–84 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_French_Division_1) finishing equal on points with [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC), however, due to having a better goal difference, Bordeaux were declared champions. The [next season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_French_Division_1), Bordeaux again won the league claiming the title by four points over second place [Nantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Nantes). In Europe, Bordeaux played in the [1984–85 European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_European_Cup) and reached the semi-finals, defeating Spanish club [Athletic Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao), Romanian club [Dinamo București](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dinamo_Bucure%C8%99ti), and Soviet outfit [Dnipro Dnipropetrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro_Dnipropetrovsk) before losing to Italian club [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC). In the Coupe de France, Bordeaux finally achieved cup glory defeating Marseille 2–1 in the [1986 edition of the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France_Final_1986) with Tigana and Giresse recording both goals. The Coupe de France trophy was the club's first since 1941 after eight agonising attempts in finals. The [following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Coupe_de_France_Final) the club responded by winning the trophy again; in a re-match with Marseille, Bordeaux won its second consecutive cup courtesy of goals from [Philippe Fargeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Fargeon) and [Zlatko Vujović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatko_Vujovi%C4%87). Bordeaux then capped off the [1986–87 Division 1 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_French_Division_1) by winning its fourth league title and achieving the [double](88184.md) as well.

Bordeaux ended the decade with a 13th-place finish in their [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_French_Division_1) league campaign.

### Rising from the ashes in the 1990s

Due to administrative problems, the club was relegated just two years thereafter. In [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_French_Division_2), however, *Les Girondins* won that year's Division 2 title, thus being elevated to the top tier of French football. In the emergence of young and exciting players such as playmaker [Zinedine Zidane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane), striker [Christophe Dugarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_Dugarry) and left back [Bixente Lizarazu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bixente_Lizarazu), the club ascended even higher to win the [UEFA Intertoto Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Intertoto_Cup) in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_UEFA_Intertoto_Cup). With this talented trio, the club defeated [FC Rotor Volgograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Rotor_Volgograd) (the 1995 [King's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Cup_\(Thailand\)) Winner), [Real Betis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Betis), [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.C._Milan) and [Slavia Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Slavia_Prague) in the second, third, quarter- and semi-finals respectively to reach the [1996 UEFA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_UEFA_Cup_final) where they were beaten by [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayern_Munich) 5–1 on aggregate. Bordeaux witnessed further glory only three years later, winning their fifth French league title in the [1998–99 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_French_Division_1) with winger [Sylvain Wiltord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvain_Wiltord) winning the [Golden Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ligue_1_top_scorers) of that season with 22 goals.[^cite_note-Bordeaux-9]

### Into the 2000s

During the 1999–2000 season, the club played in the new [UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_UEFA_Champions_League) for the first time. In two seasons time Bordeaux won another piece of silverware, beating [Lorient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Lorient) 3–0 in the [2002 Coupe de la Ligue final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Coupe_de_la_Ligue_Final). *Le club au scapulaire* then two seasons later defeated [Club Brugge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Brugge_KV) 4–1 on aggregate in the fourth round to reach the [2004 UEFA Cup quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_UEFA_Cup), where the club fell to [eventual winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_UEFA_Cup_Final) [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_CF).[^cite_note-Bordeaux-9] Bordeaux got to another final in 2007 where there were eventually victorious in winning the [Coupe de la Ligue of that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Coupe_de_la_Ligue_Final). Bordeaux then achieved further honours in winning the Ligue 1 and [Coupe de la Ligue titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Coupe_de_la_Ligue_Final) of the 2008–09 French footballing season thus achieving the first ever double in the club's history.[^cite_note-10] In 2013, Bordeaux won the Coupe de France defeating Evian 3–2 in the final.[^cite_note-11] In the 2013–14 Ligue 1 season, Bordeaux finished 7th in the table.[^cite_note-12] In 2015, Bordeaux appointed [Willy Sagnol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Sagnol) but in 2016 Sagnol was terminated after only winning one match in the first eight games of the season and was replaced by [Ulrich Rame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_Rame).[^cite_note-13] On 27 May 2016, Rame was replaced by [Jocelyn Gourvennec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jocelyn_Gourvennec).[^cite_note-14] On 20 January 2018, Gourvennec was fired and was replaced by [Gus Poyet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Poyet). Poyet guided Bordeaux to a 6th-placed finish at the end of the season.[^cite_note-15]

In July 2018, General American Capital Partners's CEO Joseph DaGrosa pursued the purchase of the French professional football team for €70 million after 19 years of [M6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M6_Group)'s ownership.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

On 18 August 2018, Poyet was suspended by Bordeaux after labelling the situation as "embarrassing" when [Gaëtan Laborde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga%C3%ABtan_Laborde) was sold to [Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier_HSC) without his knowledge or consent. On 5 September 2018, [Ricardo Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Gomes) was appointed as "general manager" — he did not possess the necessary coaching badges to be officially appointed the first-team coach.

The purchase of the club by General American Capital Partners would be completed in November 2018, before they sold their stake to majority shareholder King Street Capital in December 2019.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### COVID-19 financial crisis

On 23 April 2021, the club cited a decreased revenue due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). There was also the loss of income of [Mediapro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediapro), the TV rights holder who went bankrupt after missed payments the year prior. The club was placed in [administration](8824684.md) when the American owners, King Street stated they would no longer support the club financially.[^cite_note-20] On 22 June 2021, Girondins de Bordeaux announced that [Gérard López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_L%C3%B3pez) acquired the club.[^cite_note-21]

For the first time since the 1990–91 season, Bordeaux finished last in the [2021–22 Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Ligue_1) season and were relegated to the [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2). That was when the club were administratively relegated as a consequence of financial difficulties.[^cite_note-22] On 14 June 2022, the [DNCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direction_Nationale_du_Contr%C3%B4le_de_Gestion) administratively relegated Bordeaux to the [Ligue 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_3) due to financial issues. The club confirmed it would appeal the decision, citing it as 'brutal'.[^cite_note-23] On 27 July 2022, Bordeaux won its appeal and was officially reinstated in Ligue 2 for the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Ligue_2).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Financial administration

#### Administrative relegation to Championnat National 2

At the end of the 2023–24 season, Bordeaux was again administratively relegated to the Championnat National. Although the club had initially appealed the decision, it later withdrew the appeal.[^cite_note-26]

On 25 July 2024, it was announced that the club informed the [French Football Federation](682002.md) (FFF) that they would officially give up their status as a professional football club, with current player contracts being terminated and the team's training centre closing indefinitely. In a press release, the club stated that they would still be promoting their youth academies, and hoping they could play in the Championnat National next season and get back to Ligue 1 with "sound finances and a renewed ambition".[^cite_note-27] Then, on 1 August 2024, due to bankruptcy, Bordeaux was forcibly relegated to the [Championnat National 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Championnat_National_1) and put into financial administration by the French football controlling body, the National Directorate of Management Control/DNCG ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Direction Nationale du Contrôle de Gestion*).[^cite_note-28]

#### Relegation to regional

On 30 June 2026, reports confirmed that Bordeaux had been relegated to the fifth tier of French football following its ongoing financial collapse, after failing to cover a €9 million shortfall.[^cite_note-29] The club remained in financial difficulty amid uncertainty over investment and restructuring, including talks with British firm Sparta Capital Management, led by investor [James Bord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bord),[^cite_note-30] over a potential takeover and funding package.[^cite_note-31] On 15 July 2026, the FFF Appeals Commission upheld the DNCG's decision to exclude Bordeaux from the national championships after Sparta Capital Management failed to raise the required €10 million. As a result, the club faced relegation to [Régional 1](52169824.md), the sixth tier, and the possibility of compulsory liquidation.[^cite_note-32] However, on 31 July 2026, Sparta Capital Management secured the required funding before the deadline to complete its takeover of the club. Bordeaux subsequently sought reinstatement to the fourth-tier Championnat National 2 through an appeal to the [CNOSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Olympic_and_Sports_Committee), while owner Gérard Lopez sold the club to Sparta Capital Management for a symbolic 1€.[^cite_note-33] On 14 August 2026, the CNOSF recommended maintaining Bordeaux's exclusion from national competitions and relegation to Régional 1 or lower. The final decision would rest with the FFF Executive Committee. Bordeaux cited €10.6 million in [escrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escrow), an additional US\$10 million from new investors, and commitments to repay €20 million in debts, warning that exclusion could lead to liquidation.[^cite_note-34] A week later, on 21 August, the Paris Administrative Court (*Tribunal administratif de Paris*) upheld the DNCG's decision to exclude Bordeaux from national competitions, which means that Bordeaux competes in Régional 1, the sixth tier of French football, for the 2026–27 season.[^cite_note-35] On 3 September, the FFF upheld Bordeaux's relegation to Régional 1, rejecting the club's request for reinstatement in National 2 following its exclusion from national competitions over financial concerns.[^cite_note-36]

On September 5, 2026, few days after the FFF confirmed the exclusion from national competitions of the Girondins, the new owners Park Bench and Sparta Capital abandoned the agreement with Gérard Lopez.[^cite_note-37]

## Rivalries

Bordeaux has two main rivalries, firstly the [Derby de la Garonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_de_la_Garonne) with [Toulouse FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse_FC), so named because Bordeaux and [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) are the two major clubs that play in cities in south-western France, both of which are on the river [Garonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garonne). The consistency and competitiveness of the rivalry developed following Toulouse's return to Ligue 1 after being administratively relegated to the Championnat National in 2001. Les Girondins also contest the [Derby de l'Atlantique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_de_l'Atlantique?action=edit\&redlink=1) with their other main rival [FC Nantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Nantes), with the name stemming from the two cities' proximity to the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md). The history of this rivalry transcends over 50 years and 90 derby games have been played between the two clubs altogether.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Bordeaux also held a 44-year-old record against another big rival, Marseille. From October 1977 to January 2022, Marseille did not win away at Bordeaux's home ground.[^cite_note-41]

### Coaching history

In its history, Bordeaux has had 46 coaches. The first was the [Spaniard](26667.md) [Benito Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_D%C3%ADaz). Díaz was the first Bordeaux coach to achieve an honour when, in 1941, the club won the Coupe de France. The first Bordeaux coach to win the league was [André Gérard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_G%C3%A9rard). Gérard led the team to the league crown in 1950. He also has the honour of being the club's longest-serving coach having spent a decade with the club from 1947 to 1957. Gérard is followed by [Aimé Jacquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aim%C3%A9_Jacquet) who spent nine seasons with the club in the 1980s. Under Jacquet, Bordeaux won three league titles and two Coupe de France titles.

## FC Girondins de Bordeaux in European football

FC Girondins de Bordeaux first competitive European match was in the [1968–69 European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968%E2%80%9369_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup), beating [1. FC Köln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1._FC_K%C3%B6ln) 2–1 before ultimately losing 2–4 on aggregate. Since then, the club has participated in 30 UEFA competitions, its peak being the co-champions of the [1995 UEFA Intertoto Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_UEFA_Intertoto_Cup) and the final game of the [1995–96 UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_UEFA_Cup).
