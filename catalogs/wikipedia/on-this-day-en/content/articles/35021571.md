The **Libyan Army**[^cite_note-LibObs_Libyan_Army-2] (الجيش الليبي) is the ground force composed of a number of separate regular and irregular military formations in [Libya](17633.md), which were under the command of the internationally recognised [Government of National Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) (GNA), now the [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)) (GNU).

Since December 2015 the groups of the Libyan Army has been nominally subordinated to the internationally-recognised [Government of National Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) (GNA) based in [Tripoli](31232.md). Due to the instability in the country in [2011 civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2011\)) and the outbreak of a [new conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War) in 2014, the Libyan ground forces remain structurally divided, with components constituting the [Tobruk](405871.md)-based [Libyan National Army](35457665.md) (LNA) under the command of [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md).[^cite_note-3] The forces loyal to the GNA have been fighting against various other factions in Libya, including the [Islamic State](9087364.md).[^cite_note-Reuters-4][^cite_note-5] Some efforts have been made to create a truly national army, but most of the forces under the Tripoli government's command consist of various militia groups, such as the [Tripoli Protection Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli_Protection_Force), and local factions from cities like [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata) and [Zintan](26063692.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-fr-7]

## Background

Since the outbreak of the [Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_civil_war_\(2011\)) in 2011, the unified pre-war [Gaddafi's armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya) dissolved. The pre-war **[Libyan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Army_\(1951%E2%80%932011\))** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): رئاسة الأركان العامة للجيش الليبي) no longer exists.

The [Libyan Armed Forces](35021184.md) dissolved in the course of the first Libyan civil war from 2011, and after the [second civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_civil_war_\(2014%E2%80%932020\)), armed groups in Libya are generally divided between the [Government of National Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) (GNA) based in [Tripoli](31232.md), which has a collection of militias, and [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md)'s "[Libyan National Army](35457665.md)" in and around [Benghazi](204114.md).[^cite_note-8]

GNA forces have been fighting against various other factions in Libya, including the [Islamic State](9087364.md).[^cite_note-Reuters-4][^cite_note-9] Most of the forces under the Tripoli government's command consist of various militia groups, such as the [Tripoli Protection Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli_Protection_Force), and local factions from cities like [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata) and [Zintan](26063692.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-fr-7]

## Factions

Libya is divided between the GNA in the west; the LNA in the east; and other factions. The [Libyan National Army](35457665.md) (LNA) evolved following the fall of Muammar Gaddafi. In 2014, the LNA came under the control of [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md) and the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)), whose geographical location is in the eastern Libyan city of [Tobruk](405871.md).

In 2017, there was no truly unified army or [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Air_Force) under the [Presidential Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Council_\(Libya\))'s command, and only the [Libyan Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Navy) was fully operating under the GNA's control.[^cite_note-ly-11] The Tripoli government aimed to integrate many different militia groups into a regular command structure,[^cite_note-Reuters-4][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-pro-13] and created a [Presidential Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Presidential_Guard). Prime Minister Sarraj hold the role of supreme commander of the army.[^cite_note-joint-14] The Libyan Army is commanded by the GNA Defense Ministry, which was initially led by Colonel [Al-Mahdi Al-Barghathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mahdi_Al-Barghathi) from 2016[^cite_note-oppose-15] until he was removed in July 2018, at which point GNA Prime Minister [Fayez al-Sarraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayez_al-Sarraj) took on the role of defense minister.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-fr-7] The Chief of the General Staff was Major General [Abdel Rahman al-Taweel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Rahman_al-Taweel),[^cite_note-ly-11] from September 2017 until his removal in February 2019, being replaced by Lieutenant General [Mohammed al-Shareef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_al-Shareef?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-17]

### 2017–2018

Since the establishment of the Government of National Accord in 2016 [clashes continued to occur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9319_West_Libya_clashes) between different factions in Tripoli nominally loyal to the new UN-backed unity government, leaving hundreds dead. [Khalifa al-Ghawil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_al-Ghawil) proclaimed the creation of a new government consisting of the former [General National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_National_Congress).[^cite_note-18] Elements of the Presidential Guard defected to the rebels and took over key buildings in the capital.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-libyanexpress.com-20] Pro-GNA forces eventually were able to defeat the GNC coup attempt.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Around mid-2017, militias allied to the GNA fully secured the capital. In August 2018 [fighting broke out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tripoli_\(2018\)) between different groups in Tripoli that were all nominally subordinated to the GNA's defense ministry, forcing Prime Minister Sarraj to call in other militias from different towns outside the capital. A unit called the 7th Brigade had rebelled, leading to its dissolution.[^cite_note-fr-7][^cite_note-23]

### 2019

On 6 April 2019, a joint operations room was formed in response to [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md)'s [attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Western_Libya_offensive) on Tripoli to coordinate their military forces. It is led by Western military zone commander [Osama al-Juwaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_al-Juwaili) and includes the heads of the Tripoli and Central military zones, the Counter-Terrorism Force, and representatives from the Presidential Guard and Military Intelligence Bureau.[^cite_note-joint-14]

In response to a common interest in defending Tripoli against the LNA, the armed militias that in mid 2019 composed the armed forces of the GNA coordinated with one another mainly by agreement among armed group commanders rather than by the official command structure. The militias remained mostly autonomous in decision-making while formally being integrated into the GNA chain of command. Lacher Wolfram, writing in a *Security Assessment in North Africa* publication, described this as "bottom-up integration" and a "remarkable development" that "could potentially serve as a starting point for the creation of properly integrated forces ... \[with] loyalty to a unified command structure".[^cite_note-SANA_WhoWhom_Tripoli-24]

### Military zones

On 1 June 2017, the GNA announced the creation of seven military zones throughout Libya. They include Tripoli, [Benghazi](204114.md), [Tobruk](405871.md), [Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabha,_Libya) (Southern), [Kufra](3337477.md), Central (from [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata) to [Zuwetina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuwetina)), and Western (west of Tripoli to [Jebel Nafusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Nafusa)) . The commanders of each zone were responsible for training and preparation of the forces in their area and answered to the Libyan army chief of staff. Not all of the territories accounted for were under the GNA's control at the time.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-zone-26][^cite_note-27]

## Allies

One of the GNA's main allies is Turkey. Turkey had deployed weapons and equipment to GNA troops even before the Government of National Accord (GNA) requested Turkish military support in December 2019.[^cite_note-56] Turkey's engagement for the GNA is linked to its broader strategic interests in the Eastern Mediterranean: in November, Turkey and GNA leader [Fayez al-Sarraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayez_al-Sarraj) signed a defense cooperation deal. At the same time GNA and Turkey agreed one on maritime boundaries in the Eastern Mediterranean, where Turkey is locked in a dispute with regional rivals [Greece](12108.md), Cyprus, Egypt and Israel over access to sea regions rich in natural gas.
