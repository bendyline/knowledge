### January

- 11: The [Japan Sumo Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Sumo_Association) announces that 2,955 envelopes of prize money (called *[kenshō-kin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#kensh%C5%8D-kin)*) will be distributed to winning wrestlers at the upcoming January grand sumo tournament, setting an all-time record.

  &#x20;On the first day of the tournament–244 prize money envelopes are distributed, establishing a single-day record.

  [^cite_note-5]

  &#x20;Among the sponsor banners paraded during the tournament are those promoting the future [Major League Baseball](38776.md) Tokyo Series games, featuring photos of [Shohei Ohtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shohei_Ohtani) and other Japanese baseball players.

  [^cite_note-6]

- 12: *[Maegashira](985879.md#Maegashira)* competitors [Rōga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C5%8Dga_Tokiyoshi) and [Hokutofuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokutofuji_Daiki) withdraw at the start of the January tournament. Roga suffered a torn thigh muscle a week earlier and is reported to be considering entry into the tournament while it is in progress, while Hokutofuji has spine and knee issues that are expected to take until the end of the month to heal.

  [^cite_note-7]

- 16: *[Yokozuna](985879.md#Yokozuna)* [Terunofuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terunofuji_Haruo) withdraws on the fifth day of the January tournament after suffering a first-day loss to [Wakatakakage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakatakakage_Atsushi) and conceding a [gold star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinboshi) victory to [Tobizaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobizaru_Masaya) on Day 4. It is his 13th [absence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#ky%C5%ABj%C5%8D) in 21 tournaments at sumo's highest rank.

  [^cite_note-8]

  &#x20;Later, it is rumored that Terunofuji has decided to retire.

  [^cite_note-9]

- 17: The Sumo Association officially announces Terunofuji's retirement.

  [^cite_note-kyodo170125-10]

  &#x20;His retirement would leave the sumo [rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banzuke) without a *yokozuna* for the first time since the [March 1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_in_sumo) tournament.

  [^cite_note-11]

  [^cite_note-12]

  &#x20;He will remain with the Sumo Association coaching at [Isegahama stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isegahama_stable_\(2007\)) under the [ring name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikona) of Terunofuji, which his status as a former *yokozuna* [allows him to do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikishi#Post-retirement_career).

  [^cite_note-kyodo170125-10]

- 18: [Rōga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C5%8Dga_Tokiyoshi) withdraws for a second time after re-entering the January tournament on Day 5 and suffering two consecutive losses.

- 19: The Sumo Association announces that it will hold a sumo exhibition in the [Accor Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accor_Arena) of [Paris](22989.md) in June 2026, following the announcement of a 2025 London tour. The Sumo Association will be returning to Paris for the third time in its history.

  On Day 8 of the January tournament, *maegashira* [Kitanowaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitanowaka_Daisuke) loses his match after getting his right foot trapped on the [straw bales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#tawara) when he retreated from an attack by [Takerufuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takerufuji_Mikiya). - 26: *Ōzeki* [Hōshōryū](65166031.md), the nephew of the 68th *[yokozuna](985879.md#Yokozuna)* [Asashōryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asash%C5%8Dry%C5%AB_Akinori), stages a late comeback on the final day of the January 2025 tournament to win his second [Emperor's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%ABsh%C5%8D) and, as a result, is set to become the sport's 74th *yokozuna*. [Hōshōryū](65166031.md) entered Day 15 tied with *maegashira* [Ōhō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ch%C5%8D_K%C5%8Dnosuke) and one win behind *maegashira* and tournament leader [Kinbōzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinb%C5%8Dzan_Haruki). Hōshōryū first needed help from Ōhō, who defeated Kinbōzan in their regularly scheduled final day contest, to remain in contention. With a [playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#kettei-sen) between Kinbōzan and Ōhō assured, Hōshōryū joined the playoff by defeating struggling *ōzeki* [Kotozakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotozakura_Masakatsu_II). In the [three-way playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#tomoesen), a wrestler needs to win two consecutive matches to claim victory. Kinbōzan and Hōshōryū drew first, and Hōshōryū powered himself to victory by force-out. In the second match Ōhō denied Hōshōryū his favorite grip from the outset and after a stalemate, Hōshōryū sent Ōhō down to the *[dohyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dohy%C5%8D)* to clinch the tournament win. Hōshōryū's victory ensures that the lack of a *yokozuna* following the retirement of [Terunofuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terunofuji_Haruo) will be brief, as the [Yokozuna Deliberation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokozuna_Deliberation_Council) will meet on 27 January and is expected to formally recommend Hōshōryū's promotion. Two days after the meeting, the full board of the Sumo Association is expected to give their final approval. The two runner-ups in the top division receive their first [special prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansh%C5%8D_\(sumo\)) for their efforts, with Kinbōzan receiving the *Kantō-shō* (Fighting Spirit prize) and Ōhō receiving the *Ginō-shō* (Technique prize). A second Fighting Spirit prize is given to former *ōzeki* [Kirishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishima_Tetsuo), who remained in contention for the championship until he was defeated on Day 14. Meanwhile Kotozakura, the other *yokozuna* candidate, suffers a losing record and will need to win eight matches at the next tournament in March to [hold on to his *ōzeki* rank](985879.md#Demotion_from_%C5%8Dzeki).

- 10: As [YouTube](3524766.md) channels of *[heya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heya_\(sumo\))* increased in popularity, the Sumo Association publishes official YouTube management guidelines. - 23: *Sekiwake* [Aonishiki](79019015.md) (12–3) becomes the first Ukrainian to take home the Emperor's Cup with his playoff victory at the November 2025 tournament. One day earlier, Aonishiki secured his 11th win of the meet with his third straight victory over *yokozuna* [Hōshōryū](65166031.md). He sat in a three-way tie for the lead with both [Hōshōryū](65166031.md) and *yokozuna* [Ōnosato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cnosato_Daiki), with the Sumo Association's Judging Department already scheduling an emergency meeting to discuss whether Aonishiki should be recommended for promotion to *ōzeki*. Day 15 begins with the surprise injury withdrawal of Ōnosato (11–4) due to a dislocated shoulder, which allows Hōshōryū (12–3) to win their scheduled contest [by default](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#fusensh%C5%8D). Aonishiki ensures a [playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_sumo_terms#kettei-sen) against Hōshōryū by defeating *ōzeki* [Kotozakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotozakura_Masakatsu_II) (8–7) with the [winning technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite) of *uchimuso* (inner thigh propping twist down). In the deciding match, Aonishiki wins by *okurinage* (rear throw down) to remain undefeated versus Hōshōryū in four career contests. Aonishiki becomes the second-fastest to reach their maiden victory in the top division, doing so in 14 tournaments. At 21 years and 8 months of age, he is the fourth youngest to win the *makuuchi* title in modern sumo history, while [Ukraine](31750.md) becomes the seventh country in history to have produced a sumo champion in the top division. At the end of the tournament, it was announced that Aonishiki's performance of 34 wins over three tournaments–the last two at *san'yaku* ranks–was good enough for the Judging Department to recommend Aonishiki's promotion to *ōzeki*, which will be discussed at a Sumo Association meeting on 26 November. Aonishiki took home his first Outstanding Performance prize and his third Technique prize, tying Ōnosato by collecting a [special prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansh%C5%8D_\(sumo\)) in each of his first five tournaments in the top division. Former *ōzeki* [Kirishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishima_Tetsuo), seeking to return to *san'yaku* status after having just been demoted, finishes with 11 wins and his third Fighting Spirit prize. Another former *ōzeki*, [Takayasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takayasu_Akira), will hold on to his *san'yaku* ranking following his eighth tournament victory. [Yoshinofuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshinofuji_Naoya), who lost to Takayasu on Day 15, finishes with nine wins. The former Kusano also collected his first career [gold star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinboshi) during the tournament, and wins his second Technique Prize. The last of the special prizes goes to [Ichiyamamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichiyamamoto_Daiki) (11–4), who collects his second Fighting Spirit prize. Despite pulling out on the last day, *yokozuna* [Ōnosato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cnosato_Daiki) finishes 2025 with 71 victories, the most in the top division, along with two playoff wins.
