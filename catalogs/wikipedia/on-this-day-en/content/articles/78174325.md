**LGBTQ people** are individuals who are [lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian), [gay](5171128.md), [bisexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexual), [transgender](19904525.md), or [queer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer). Other individuals are often included in the group, such as [questioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questioning_\(sexuality_and_gender\)), [intersex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex), [asexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asexuality), [aromantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanticism), and [agender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agender),[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] denoted under variants of the initialism "[LGBTQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_\(term\))". The group is generally conceived as broadly encompassing all individuals who are part of a [sexual or gender minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_and_gender_minorities).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Variants of the abbreviation, including LGBTQ+, LGBTQIA, and LGBTQIA+, are also used.[^cite_note-6]

## Scope and terminology

Main articles: [*LGBTQ* (term)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_\(term\)) and [List of LGBTQ acronyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_LGBTQ_acronyms)

LGBTQ people express a broad array of sexual and gender minority identities. The alternative umbrella *gender, sexual, and romantic minorities* is sometimes used for this group.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Groups that blend into the larger LGBTQ population include:

- People with a [sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation) that is [non-heterosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-heterosexual), including [lesbians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbians), [gay men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_men), [bisexual people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexuality), and [asexual people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asexuality)

- People who are [transgender](19904525.md) or [non-binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binary)

- People who are [aromantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromantic)

- People who are [intersex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex)

- [Queer people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_people), sometimes used as a synonym for LGBTQ people generally, sometimes as a specific identity

Common variations of the initialism include ***LGBT***, ***LGBT+***, ***LGBTQ+***, ***LGBTQIA+***, and ***QUILTBAG***.

## Culture



Cologne Germany Gay Pride Parade (2014)

Main article: [LGBTQ culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_culture)

See also: [Outline of LGBTQ topics § Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_LGBTQ_topics#Culture)

LGBTQ culture varies widely by geography and the identity of the participants. Elements common to cultures of gay, lesbian, bisexual, transgender, and [intersex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex) people include:

- [Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_\(LGBTQ_culture\)) movements, including [pride parades](58168.md)

- Events such as the [Gay Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_Games) and [Southern Decadence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Decadence)

- [LGBTQ media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_media) and works by LGBTQ artists, including the [queer art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_art) movement

- [LGBTQ-owned businesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ-owned_business), particularly those that cater specifically to the LGBTQ community

Not all LGBTQ people identify with LGBTQ culture; this may be due to geographic distance, unawareness of the subculture's existence, fear of [social stigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stigma) or a preference for remaining unidentified with sexuality- or gender-based subcultures or communities. The [Queercore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queercore) and [Gay Shame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_Shame) movements critique what they see as the commercialization and self-imposed "ghettoization" of LGBTQ culture.[^cite_note-dupleissis-19][^cite_note-gayshamesf.org-20]

## History

Main article: [LGBTQ history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_history)



Statue of [Alexander Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Wood_\(merchant\)), [Toronto, Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto,_Canada)

The history of LGBTQ people dates back to the first recorded instances of same-sex love and diverse gender identities and sexualities in cultures around the world.[^cite_note-\:0-21] Male and female homoerotic relations, as well as transgender expression and gender variance, have been documented globally across both premodern and modern eras, often shaped by locally specific frameworks rather than fixed identities.[^cite_note-Janes_2024_252–272-22][^cite_note-Janes_2024_252–272-22][^cite_note-23] In many cultures, however, this history has involved sustained marginalization and persecution—intensified by colonial legal codes, religious prohibitions, and the pathologizing frameworks of modern medical science—such that these histories have only in recent decades been systematically pursued and interwoven into more mainstream historical narratives.[^cite_note-\:0-21]

In 1994, the annual observance of [LGBT History Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_History_Month) began in the United States, and it has since been picked up in other countries. This observance involves highlighting the history of the people, [LGBTQ rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights) and related [civil rights](37071.md) movements. It is observed during October in the United States, to include [National Coming Out Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coming_Out_Day) on October 11.[^cite_note-glsen-24] In the United Kingdom it has been observed during February since 2005: [Section 28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_28), which had prohibited local authorities from "promoting" homosexuality was repealed in England and Wales in 2003, while the same legislation (named Section 2a in the Scottish legislation) was repealed by the [Scottish parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_parliament) in 2000.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] A celebrated achievement in LGBTQ history occurred when [Queen Beatrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Beatrix) signed a law making Netherlands the [first country to legalize same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_Netherlands) in 2001,[^cite_note-27] and another when Ireland became [the first country to legalise same-sex marriage by popular vote in 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland).

## Rights

Main article: [LGBTQ rights by country or territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_by_country_or_territory)

See also: [Outline of LGBTQ topics § Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_LGBTQ_topics#Rights)

The legal rights held by LGBTQ people vary greatly by country or jurisdiction—ranging from the legal recognition of [same-sex marriage](92656.md) to the [death penalty for homosexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_for_homosexuality).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] An example of such protections are legal prohibitions against [incitement to hatred and violence against LGBTQ people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-LGBTQ_rhetoric).[^cite_note-30]

Laws that affect LGBTQ people include:

- legal recognition of [same-sex marriage](92656.md)

- laws concerning [same-sex parenting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_parenting), including [same-sex adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_adoption)

- [anti-discrimination laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-discrimination_laws) in employment, housing, education, public accommodations

- [hate crime laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_crime_laws) imposing enhanced criminal penalties for prejudice-motivated [violence against LGBTQ people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_against_LGBTQ_people)

- [bathroom bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathroom_bill) affecting access to sex-segregated facilities by transgender people

- [sodomy laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy_laws) that penalize consensual same-sex sexual activity

- laws concerning access to [gender-affirming surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender-affirming_surgery) and [gender-affirming hormone replacement therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender-affirming_hormone_replacement_therapy)

- [legal recognition and accommodation of the affirmed gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_status_of_transgender_people)

Even in jurisdictions with strong protections for LGBTQ rights, they may still be subject to [discrimination against LGBTQ people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_LGBTQ_people).

## Discrimination

Main article: [Discrimination against LGBTQ people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_LGBTQ_people)

See also: [Outline of LGBTQ topics § Anti-LGBTQ topics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_LGBTQ_topics#Anti-LGBTQ_topics)

Discrimination against LGBTQ people can manifest in legal, institutional, and social forms.[^cite_note-Ramos-31]<sup>: 1 </sup> This includes [discrimination directed specifically at lesbians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_lesbians), [at homosexuals more broadly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_homosexuals), [at gay men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_gay_men), [at bisexuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_bisexuals), [at transgender people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_transgender_people), [at asexual people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_asexual_people), [at intersex people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_intersex_people), and [at non-binary people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination_against_non-binary_people).

[Opposition to LGBTQ rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_LGBTQ_rights) exists worldwide. While laws are "a necessary foundation to achieve equality ... protections under the law are not sufficient to eliminate prejudice", and "social equality is not synonymous with equality under the law", according to [Ilan Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilan_Meyer).[^cite_note-32] According to a study by the [European Parliament](9581.md)'s internal policy body in 2012: "To resolve the vast majority of problems faced by LGBTI people, individuals, society, organisations and authorities must stop regarding their differences as factors which require differential treatment. While this seems self evident, such shifts in attitude cannot be achieved through one-off, short term action, nor through legislation alone. ... in some areas a change of views of some groups may simply not be possible."[^cite_note-33]

Some countries practice [censorship of LGBTQ issues](62482718.md).[^cite_note-34]

Social divides exist over the social acceptance of LGBTQ people, including [societal attitudes toward homosexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Societal_attitudes_toward_homosexuality).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

## Movements

Main article: [LGBTQ movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_movements)

LGBTQ movements are [social movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_movements) that advocate for the inclusion, recognition, and rights of LGBTQ people. These movements work to secure legal rights, or enact broader social changes aimed at advancing equality and inclusion.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] In addition, LGBTQ movements and communities work to advance [LGBTQ culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_culture).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

## Health

Main articles: [LGBTQ health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_health), [Mental health of LGBTQ people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health_of_LGBTQ_people), and [LGBTQ psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_psychology)

LGBTQ people may face disparities in access to care, targeted public health interventions, and the impact of stigma on physical and mental well-being.[^cite_note-Ramos-31]<sup>: 2 </sup> The [psychology of LGBTQ people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_psychology) covers aspects such as identity development including the [coming out](71537.md) process, parenting and family practices and support for LGBTQ individuals.
