The **LG Twins** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): LG 트윈스) are a South Korean professional baseball team based in [Seoul, South Korea](19159283.md). They are a member of the [KBO League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KBO_League). The Twins play their home games at [Jamsil Baseball Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamsil_Baseball_Stadium), which they share with their rivals, the [Doosan Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doosan_Bears).[^cite_note-1]

## History



[Jamsil Baseball Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamsil_Baseball_Stadium), home of the LG Twins

The club was established in 1982 as MBC Chungyong, owned by the [Munhwa Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munhwa_Broadcasting_Corporation). In the first part of the 1982 season, the team played at [Dongdaemun Baseball Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongdaemun_Baseball_Stadium); in the second part of the season, they moved to their current home, [Jamsil Baseball Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamsil_Baseball_Stadium).

The Chungyong were initially led by player-manager [Baek In-chun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baek_In-chun), who had spent 19 seasons in Japan's [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball). Aged 38, Baek led the league in hitting in 1982, with a record-setting .412 batting average.[^cite_note-KoreaTimes-2] As manager, Baek brought the Japanese "[small ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_ball_\(baseball\))" technique to his team, focusing on sacrifice bunts, stolen bases, and sacrifice flies.[^cite_note-KoreaTimes-2] Although the team finished above .500 in 1982, Baek was dismissed by the team after the season.

In [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_in_baseball), the franchise was acquired by the [LG Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG_Corporation), which renamed the team the LG Twins. The following year, the Twins won their first [Korean Series](9325146.md) title with Baek In-chun as their manager. In 1994, they won their second championship. The Korean Series MVP was [Kim Yong-soo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Yong-soo) in both Series, and his jersey number 41 was later retired. However, after their Korean Series appearance in 2002, the team experienced an unsuccessful streak and did not make the postseason for eleven years. They earned the second seed in the regular season in 2013, qualifying and competing in the playoffs until ultimately being eliminated by the [Doosan Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doosan_Bears). Between 2014 and 2022, the Twins made six postseason appearances, but failed to return to the Korean Series. In [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_KBO_League_season), they reached their first Korean Series since 2002 by virtue of winning the regular season title.[^cite_note-3] In [the finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Korean_Series), the Twins, led by captain and Korean Series MVP [Oh Ji-hwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Ji-hwan), defeated the [KT Wiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KT_Wiz) 4–1 in five games, ending the club's 29-year championship drought.[^cite_note-4]

## In popular culture

The team featured prominently in the [tvN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVN_\(South_Korea\)) drama *[Reply 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reply_1994)*, where the team's 1994 head coach is a main character. In the drama, the team is referenced under a different name, "Seoul Twins" ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 서울 쌍둥이), due to trademark issues with the [LG Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG_Corporation).

In 2023, [TVING](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVING) released the original documentary *Our Game: LG Twins*, directed by Lee Hyun-hee, covering behind-the-scenes stories inside and outside the stadium. It received praise for its unique documentary and became the best original documentary in TVING's history in terms of paid subscriptions.[^cite_note-6]
