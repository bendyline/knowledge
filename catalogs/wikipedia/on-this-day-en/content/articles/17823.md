**Politics of Lithuania** takes place in a framework of a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) [semi-presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system) [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic),[^cite_note-Lina-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Draft-3][^cite_note-Dual-4] whereby the [president of Lithuania](273654.md) is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and the [prime minister of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Lithuania) is the [head of government](37570.md), and of a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system).

[Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the [president](273654.md) and the Government, which is headed by the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Lithuania). [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in both the Government and the [unicameral](204304.md) [Seimas](571739.md) (Lithuanian [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament)). [Judicial power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_power) is vested in judges appointed by the president of Lithuania and is independent of executive and legislature power. The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) consists of the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_the_Republic_of_Lithuania), the [Supreme Court](4913027.md), and the [Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeal) as well as the separate [administrative courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_court). The [Constitution of the Republic of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Lithuania) established these powers upon its approval on 25 October 1992. Being a multi-party system, the government of Lithuania is not dominated by any single [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party), rather it consists of numerous parties that must work with each other to form [coalition governments](6036.md). The [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md) rated Lithuania a "[flawed democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index#Definitions)" in 2024.[^cite_note-democracy-index-5]

## History

Since Lithuania [restored independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_the_Re-Establishment_of_the_State_of_Lithuania) on 11 March 1990, it has kept democratic traditions. Drawing from the interwar experiences, politicians made many different proposals that ranged from strong [parliamentarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) to a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) with [checks and balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers#Checks_and_balances) similar to the United States. Through compromise, a [semi-presidential system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system) was settled.[^cite_note-Lina-1] In a [referendum](51582.md) on 25 October 1992, the first general vote of the people since their declared independence, 56.75% of the total number of voters supported the [new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Lithuania).[^cite_note-6]

All major political parties declared their support for Lithuania's membership in [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty_Organization) and the [European Union](9317.md) (EU). Lithuania joined NATO on 29 March 2004.[^cite_note-7] Lithuania joined the EU on 1 May 2004 and [Schengen Area](16565139.md) on 21 December 2007 and [Eurozone](184391.md) on 1 January 2015.[^cite_note-8]

Since 1991, Lithuanian voters have shifted from right to left and back again, swinging between the Conservatives, led by [Vytautas Landsbergis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vytautas_Landsbergis), and the (formerly [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Lithuania)) [Democratic Labour Party of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Labour_Party_of_Lithuania), led by president [Algirdas Brazauskas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algirdas_Brazauskas). During this period, the prime minister was [Gediminas Vagnorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gediminas_Vagnorius).

[Valdas Adamkus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdas_Adamkus) was the president since 1998. His proposed prime minister was [Rolandas Paksas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolandas_Paksas), whose government got off to a rocky start and collapsed within seven months. The alternation between left and right was broken in the October 2000 elections when the [Liberal Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Union_of_Lithuania) and [New Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Union_\(Social_Liberals\)) parties won the most votes and were able to form a [centrist](39619035.md) ruling coalition with minor partners. President Adamkus played a key role in bringing the new centrist parties together. [Artūras Paulauskas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art%C5%ABras_Paulauskas), the leader of the centre-left New Union (also known as the social-liberal party), became the Chairman of the Seimas. In July 2001, the [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left) New Union party forged an alliance with the [Social Democratic Party of Lithuania](425771.md) and formed a new cabinet under former president Algirdas Brazauskas. On 11 April 2006, Artūras Paulauskas was removed from his position and [Viktoras Muntianas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktoras_Muntianas) was elected Chairman of the Seimas.[^cite_note-9]

The cabinet of Algirdas Brazauskas resigned on 31 May 2006, as President Valdas Adamkus expressed no confidence in two of the Ministers, formerly party colleagues of Brazauskas, over ethical principles. Brazauskas decided not to remain in office as acting prime minister, and announced that he was finally retiring from politics.[^cite_note-10] Even so, he led the ruling Social Democratic Party of Lithuania for one more year, until 19 May 2007, when he passed the reins to [Gediminas Kirkilas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gediminas_Kirkilas). On 27 November 2008, [Andrius Kubilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrius_Kubilius) of conservative Homeland Union was appointed as a prime minister.[^cite_note-11] In December 2012 Andrius Kubilius was succeeded by [Algirdas Butkevičius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algirdas_Butkevi%C4%8Dius) after his Social Democratic Party became the biggest party in parliamentary [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Lithuanian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-12]

In 2016, [The Peasant and Green's Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Peasant_and_Green_Union) (LGPU) won parliamentary [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Lithuanian_parliamentary_election). It secured 54 seats in the 141-member parliament ([Seimas](571739.md)), making a previously small centrist agrarian party the biggest in parliament. The conservative [Homeland Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Union) won 30 seats. The ruling [Social Democrats](425771.md), led by Lithuania's Prime Minister [Algirdas Butkevciu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algirdas_Butkevi%C4%8Dius), lost heavily and secured just 17 seats.[^cite_note-13] On 22 November 2016, [Saulius Skvernelis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saulius_Skvernelis) of the Lithuanian Peasants and Greens Union, became new prime minister.[^cite_note-14]

In October 2020, conservative opposition [Homeland Union-Lithuanian Christian Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Union) (TS-LKD) won parliamentary [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Lithuanian_parliamentary_election) with 50 seats. Prime Minister Saulius Skvernelis' Union of Farmers and Greens came a distant second with just 32 seats.[^cite_note-15] In November 2020, [Ingrida Šimonytė](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrida_%C5%A0imonyt%C4%97) became new prime minister, after forming a centre-right coalition government of her TS-LKD and two liberal parties.[^cite_note-16]

## Government

Main article: [Government of the Republic of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_Lithuania)

Government in Lithuania is made up of three branches originally envisioned by [enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) [Baron de Montesquieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Secondat,_baron_de_Montesquieu): [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_branch), [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_branch), and [judicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_branch). Each branch is separate and is set up to do [checks and balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers#Checks_and_balances) on each other branch.

### Executive branch

The executive branch of the Lithuanian government consists of a president, a prime minister, and the president's Council of Ministers. It is in charge of running the government.

#### President



[Presidential Palace of the Republic of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace,_Vilnius) in [Daukanto Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daukanto_Square)

The [president of Lithuania](273654.md) is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) of the country, [elected directly](652563.md) for a five-year term and can serve maximum of two terms consecutively. Presidential elections take place in a modified version of the [two-round system](25932.md). If half of voters participate, a candidate must win a majority of the total valid vote in order to win election in the first round. If fewer than half of voters participate, a candidate can win outright with a plurality and at least one third of the total vote. If the first round does not produce a president, a runoff is held between the top two finishers in the first round, with a plurality sufficient to win.

The president, with the approval of the [Seimas](571739.md), is first responsible of appointing the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Lithuania). Upon the prime minister's nomination, the president also appoints, under the recommendation of the prime minister, the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)) (13 [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\))), as well as a number of other top civil servants and the judges for all courts. The president also serves as the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief), oversees foreign and security policy, addresses political problems of foreign and domestic affairs, proclaims states of emergency, considers the laws adopted by the Seimas, and performs other duties specified in the Constitution.[^cite_note-Lithuania-17] Lithuanian presidents have somewhat greater power than their counterparts in Estonia and Latvia, but have more influence in foreign policy than domestic policy.[^cite_note-18]

Former president [Rolandas Paksas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolandas_Paksas), who had defeated Adamkus in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_presidential_election,_2002%E2%80%932003), was [impeached](15328.md) in April 2004 for leaking classified information.[^cite_note-19]

[Dalia Grybauskaitė](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalia_Grybauskait%C4%97), the first female president, served as the president of Lithuania since July 2009 until 2019, winning a [reelection bid in 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Lithuanian_presidential_election). Grybauskaitė succeeded [Valdas Adamkus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdas_Adamkus) who had served a total of two non-consecutive terms.[^cite_note-20]

In 2019, [Gitanas Nauseda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitanas_Nauseda) won Lithuania's presidential runoff [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Lithuanian_presidential_election) after his opponent [Ingrida Šimonytė](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrida_%C5%A0imonyt%C4%97) conceded.[^cite_note-21]

#### Prime minister



[Government of Lithuania](16769373.md) building

The [prime minister of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Lithuania) is the [head of government](37570.md) of the country, appointed by the president and approved by the Seimas. The prime minister, within 15 days of being appointed, is responsible for choosing ministers for the president to approve to each of the 13 ministries. In general, the prime minister is in charge of the affairs of the country, maintains homeland security, carries out laws and resolutions of the Seimas and decrees of the president, maintains diplomatic relations with foreign countries and international organizations, and performs other duties specified in the Constitution.[^cite_note-Lithuania-17] In practice, the prime minister is mostly responsible for domestic policy, while the president mostly handles foreign policy.

#### Council of Ministers

Similar to the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)) of other nations, the Council of Ministers consists of 13 ministers chosen by the prime minister and appointed by the president. Each minister is responsible for their own ministry of the Lithuanian government and must provide reports when directed.[^cite_note-Government-22] When the prime minister resigns or dies, the position is to be filled as soon as possible and the new leader will appoint a new government.

### Legislative branch



[Seimas Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seimas_Palace) in [Vilnius](32597.md)

The parliament ([Seimas](571739.md)) has 141 members that are elected for a 4-year term. About half of the members are elected in [single-member districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_voting_system) (71), and the other half (70) are elected in the nationwide vote using [proportional representation](23298.md) by party lists. A party [must receive at least 5% of the national vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) to be represented in the Seimas.

Presidential elections were held in [Lithuania](17675.md) on 12 May 2024, alongside a [referendum on allowing multiple citizenships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Lithuanian_constitutional_referendum).[^cite_note-2024_Lithuanian_presidential_election_LRT-23] Incumbent President [Gitanas Nausėda](58519561.md) won re-election to a second term. When candidate registrations closed before the election, the [Lithuanian Central Election Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Central_Election_Commission) had confirmed fifteen viable registrations; of those, twelve proceeded to [collect signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomination_rules) from the minimum 20,000 voters. Four of those contenders were later repudiated or dropped out,[^cite_note-24] leaving eight on the [ballot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot).[^cite_note-2024_Lithuanian_presidential_election_EL9-25]

A second round was held on 26 May as no candidate received an absolute majority in the first round.[^cite_note-2024_Lithuanian_presidential_election_ap-26] This saw Nausėda and Prime Minister [Ingrida Šimonytė](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrida_%C5%A0imonyt%C4%97) facing off in the runoff, a rematch of the [2019 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Lithuanian_presidential_election) in which Nausėda had defeated Šimonytė. Nausėda won re-election, defeating Šimonytė in a second-round landslide with 75% of the vote – the largest margin of victory in a free election for any presidential candidate in the history of Lithuania.

Parliamentary elections were held in Lithuania on 13 and 27 October 2024 to elect the 141 members of the [Seimas](571739.md). Members were elected in 71 single-member constituencies using the [two-round system](25932.md), and the remaining 70 in a single nationwide constituency using [proportional representation](23298.md). The first round was held on 13 October and the second round on 27 October.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

The elections were won by the [Social Democratic Party of Lithuania](425771.md) (LSDP),[^cite_note-30] which secured 19.32% of the popular vote and 52 seats, up from 9.58% and 13 seats in the [previous elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Lithuanian_parliamentary_election) in 2020. The [Homeland Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Union) (TS–LKD), the largest party in the ruling [centre-right coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0imonyt%C4%97_Cabinet) in the [preceding Seimas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_Seimas), finished a distant second, securing 28 seats, down from its previous 50.

Following the first round of the election, the Social Democrats entered into coalition talks with the [Lithuanian Farmers and Greens Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Farmers_and_Greens_Union) (LVŽS) and the [Union of Democrats "For Lithuania"](70139653.md) (DSVL), which had split from the latter. After the second round, the consultations were expanded to include the [Liberals' Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberals'_Movement_\(Lithuania\)) (LS), which had been part of the outgoing conservative-led coalition, and [Dawn of Nemunas](75998749.md) (PPNA), a new nationalist party that finished in third place overall.[^cite_note-31]

The LSDP eventually reached a deal with DSVL and PPNA to form a coalition government.[^cite_note-32] The Social Democrats' decision to include the Dawn of Nemunas party, whose founder is known for making controversial statements, prompted domestic and international backlash.[^cite_note-2024_Lithuanian_parliamentary_election_openletter-33][^cite_note-34]

### Judicial branch

The judges of the [Constitutional Court of the Republic of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_the_Republic_of_Lithuania) (*Lietuvos Respublikos Konstitucinis Teismas*) for a single nine-year term are appointed by the [Seimas](571739.md) from the candidates presented by the President (three judges), Chairman of Seimas (three judges) and the chairman of the Supreme Court (three judges).

## Administrative divisions

Main article: [Subdivisions of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Lithuania)

Lithuania has a three-tier administrative division: the country is divided into [10 counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Lithuania) (Lithuanian: singular – *apskritis*, plural – *apskritys*) that are further subdivided into [60 municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Lithuania) (Lithuanian: singular – *savivaldybė*, plural – *savivaldybės*) which consist of over 500 [elderships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elderships_of_Lithuania) (Lithuanian: singular – *seniūnija*, plural – *seniūnijos*).

The [county governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_governor_\(Lithuania\)) (Lithuanian: *apskrities viršininkas*) institution and county administrations have been dissolved in 2010.[^cite_note-county-36]

Municipalities are the most important administrative unit. Some municipalities are historically called "district municipalities", and thus are often shortened to "district"; others are called "city municipalities", sometimes shortened to "city". Each municipality has its own elected government. In the past, the election of municipality councils occurred once every three years, but it now takes place every four years. The council appoints [elders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_\(administrative_title\)) to govern the elderships. Mayors are elected directly since 2015, being appointed by the council before that.[^cite_note-37]
