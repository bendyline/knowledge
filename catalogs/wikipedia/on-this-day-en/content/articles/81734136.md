**Anicet Ekane** (17 April 1951 – 1 December 2025) was a Cameroonian politician, activist and intellectual who dedicated nearly five decades to the struggle for democracy and independence in Cameroon. He became a central figure in opposition politics, leading the [African Movement for New Independence and Democracy](81735184.md) (MANIDEM).[^cite_note-\:1-1]

## Early life and education

Georges Anicet Ekane was born on 17 April 1951 at Laquintinie Hospital in Douala, Cameroon.[^cite_note-\:1-1] His father, Ekan'a Mbongo n'a Ndongo, was a chief accountant at the Compagnie Soudanaise and built estates in Douala, Bomono, and near the Mungo River. His father had twenty-two children including Ekane.[^cite_note-2] He attended the École Principale d'Akwa (later École Publique d'Akwa), where he completed his Certificat d'Études Primaires et Élémentaires (CEPE).[^cite_note-\:2-3] He studied at Collège Liberman, then Lycée Joss (where he obtained his probatoire). He later attended Collège Alfred Saker and Collège Saint Pierre de Lille (France), earning his Baccalauréat D.[^cite_note-\:2-3][^cite_note-4]

## Political career

The "Affaire Yondo Black et autres" of 1990, where [Yondo Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yondo_Black) and Anicet Ekane were arrested and tried for attempting to create a political party outside the one-party system, was his primary highlight into the opposition movement in Cameroon.[^cite_note-5] Ekane became the leader of MANIDEM, a party advocating for African independence, democracy, and social justice.[^cite_note-\:1-1][^cite_note-6] He played a pivotal role in supporting opposition leader [Maurice Kamto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Kamto), allowing him to represent MANIDEM in the October elections. This move made Ekane a target of government repression.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-\:0-8]

### Arrest and death

Ekane's life has been cited as emblematic of resistance to authoritarian rule in Cameroon.[^cite_note-9] He is regarded by some observers as a defender of democratic governance and national independence, despite facing significant personal consequences.[^cite_note-10] He was arrested, designated a "terrorist" by authorities, and held under severe prison conditions. Ekane was arrested on 24 October 2025, in Douala, the day after the publication of presidential election results that returned the 92-year-old [Biya](392319.md) to power. He was subsequently detained at the Secretary of State for Defence (SED) in [Yaoundé](57671.md).[^cite_note-11] He faced accusations of [insurrection](513246.md) and rebellion for contesting the election results. Reports suggest he was denied adequate medical care and oxygen, contributing to his death on 1 December 2025, at the age of 74.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-\:0-8]
