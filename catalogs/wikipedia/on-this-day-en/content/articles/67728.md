# Petrochemical



Petrochemical plant in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md)

**Petrochemicals** (sometimes abbreviated as **petchems**[^cite_note-1]) are the [chemical products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_\(chemistry\)) obtained from [petroleum](23195.md) by refining. Some [chemical compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_compound) made from petroleum are also obtained from other [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel), such as [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) or [natural gas](22131.md), or renewable sources such as [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [palm fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaeis) or [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane).

The two most common petrochemical classes are [olefins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkene) (including [ethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylene) and [propylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propylene)) and [aromatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromaticity) (including [benzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzene), [toluene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluene) and [xylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylene) [isomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomers)).

[Oil refineries](195137.md) produce olefins and aromatics by [fluid catalytic cracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_catalytic_cracking) of petroleum fractions. [Chemical plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_plant) produce olefins by [steam cracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_cracking) of [natural gas liquids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_liquids) like [ethane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethane) and [propane](23643.md). Aromatics are produced by [catalytic reforming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalytic_reforming) of [naphtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_naphtha). Olefins and aromatics are the building-blocks for a wide range of materials such as [solvents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvent), [detergents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detergent), and [adhesives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhesive). Olefins are the basis for [polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer) and [oligomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligomer) used in [plastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic), [resins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resin), [fibers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber), [elastomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastomer), [lubricants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubricant), and [gels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gels).[^cite_note-Hatch-2][^cite_note-HP-3]

Global ethylene production was 190 million tonnes and propylene was 120 million tonnes in 2019.[^cite_note-4] Aromatics production is approximately 70 million tonnes. The largest [petrochemical industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrochemical_industries) are located in the [United States](3434750.md) and [Western Europe](33800.md); however, major growth in new production capacity is in the [Middle East](19323.md) and [Asia](689.md). There is substantial inter-regional petrochemical trade.

Primary petrochemicals are divided into three groups depending on their [chemical structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_structure):

- [Olefins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkene) includes [ethene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylene), [propene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propene), [butenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butene) and [butadiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1,3-Butadiene). Ethylene and propylene are important sources of [industrial chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_chemicals) and [plastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastics) [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_\(business\)). Butadiene is used in making [synthetic rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_rubber).

- [Aromatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromatics) includes [benzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzene), [toluene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluene) and [xylenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylene), as a whole referred to as [BTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTX_\(chemistry\)) and primarily obtained from petroleum refineries by extraction from the reformate produced in [catalytic reformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalytic_reforming) using [naphtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_naphtha) obtained from petroleum refineries. Alternatively, BTX can be produced by aromatization of alkanes. Benzene is a raw material for [dyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyes) and synthetic detergents, and benzene and toluene for [isocyanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isocyanate) [MDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methylene_diphenyl_diisocyanate) and [TDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluene_diisocyanate) used in making [polyurethanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurethanes). Manufacturers use [xylenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylene) to produce plastics and synthetic fibers.

- [Synthesis gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesis_gas) is a [mixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixture) of [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide) and [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) used to produce [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol) and other chemicals. [Steam crackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_cracking) are not to be confused with [steam reforming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_reforming) plants used to produce [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) for [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia_production) production. Ammonia is used to make the [fertilizer](37401.md) [urea](31734.md) and methanol is used as a solvent and [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical) intermediate.

- [Methane](18582230.md), [ethane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethane), [propane](23643.md) and [butanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butane) obtained primarily from [natural gas processing plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_processing).

- [Methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol) and [formaldehyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaldehyde).

In 2007, the amounts of ethylene and propylene produced in steam crackers were about 115 M[t](31185.md) (megatonnes) and 70 Mt, respectively.[^cite_note-5] The output ethylene capacity of large steam crackers ranged up to as much as 1.0 – 1.5 Mt per year.[^cite_note-6]

Like [commodity chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_chemicals), petrochemicals are made on a very large scale. Petrochemical manufacturing units differ from commodity chemical plants in that they often produce a number of related products. Compare this with [specialty chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialty_chemical_industry) and [fine chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_chemical) manufacture where products are made in discrete batch processes.

Petrochemicals are predominantly made in a few manufacturing locations around the world, for example in [Jubail](632008.md) and [Yanbu](629638.md) Industrial Cities in Saudi Arabia, [Texas](29810.md) and [Louisiana](18130.md) in the US, in [Teesside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside) in the [Northeast of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_of_England) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md), in [Tarragona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarragona) in [Catalonia](6822.md), in [Rotterdam](26049.md) in the Netherlands, in [Antwerp](32149462.md) in [Belgium](3343.md), in [Jamnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamnagar_Refinery), [Dahej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahej) in [Gujarat](53707.md), [India](14533.md) and in [Singapore](27318.md). Not all of the petrochemical or commodity chemical materials produced by the chemical industry are made in one single location but groups of related materials are often made in adjacent manufacturing plants to induce industrial symbiosis as well as material and utility efficiency and other [economies of scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economies_of_scale). This is known in [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering) terminology as integrated manufacturing. Specialty and fine chemical companies are sometimes found in similar manufacturing locations as petrochemicals but, in most cases, they do not need the same level of large-scale infrastructure (e.g., pipelines, storage, ports, and power, etc.) and therefore can be found in multi-sector business parks.

The large-scale petrochemical manufacturing locations have clusters of manufacturing units that share utilities and large-scale infrastructures such as power stations, storage tanks, port facilities, road and rail terminals. In the United Kingdom, for example, there are four main locations for such manufacturing: near the River Mersey in North West England, on the Humber on the East coast of Yorkshire, in Grangemouth near the Firth of Forth in Scotland, and in Teesside as part of the [Northeast of England Process Industry Cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_of_England_Process_Industry_Cluster) (NEPIC). To demonstrate the clustering and integration, some 50% of the United Kingdom's petrochemical and commodity chemicals are produced by the [NEPIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_of_England_Process_Industry_Cluster) industry cluster companies in Teesside.

## History

In 1835, [Henri Victor Regnault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Victor_Regnault), a French chemist left [vinyl chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_chloride) in the sun and found white solid at the bottom of the flask which was [polyvinyl chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyvinyl_chloride). In 1839, [Eduard Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Simon) discovered polystyrene by accident by distilling [storax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storax_balsam). In 1856, [William Henry Perkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Perkin) discovered the first synthetic dye, [Mauveine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauveine). In 1888, [Friedrich Reinitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Reinitzer), an Austrian plant scientist observed [cholesteryl benzoate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesteryl_benzoate) had two different melting points. In 1909, [Leo Hendrik Baekeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Baekeland) invented [bakelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakelite) made from [phenol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenol) and [formaldehyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formaldehyde). In 1920, [Union Carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Carbide) built in [West Virginia](32905.md) first petrochemical plant in the world.[^cite_note-9] In 1928, [synthetic fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fuel) were invented using [Fischer-Tropsch process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer%E2%80%93Tropsch_process). In 1929, [Walter Bock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Bock) invented synthetic rubber [Buna-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buna-S) which is made up of [styrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styrene) and [butadiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1,3-Butadiene) and used to make car tires. In 1933, [Otto Röhm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_R%C3%B6hm) polymerized the first acrylic glass [methyl methacrylate](1985376.md). In 1935, [Michael Perrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Perrin) invented [polyethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyethylene). In 1937, [Wallace Hume Carothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Carothers) invented [nylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon). In 1938, [Otto Bayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Bayer) invented [polyurethane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurethane). In 1941, [Roy Plunkett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_J._Plunkett) invented [Teflon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teflon). In 1946, he invented [Polyester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyester). [Polyethylene terephthalate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyethylene_terephthalate) (PET) bottles are made from [ethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylene) and [paraxylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-Xylene). In 1949, Fritz Stastny turned [polystyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polystyrene) into foam. After World War II, [polypropylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polypropylene) was discovered in the early 1950s. In 1965, [Stephanie Kwolek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Kwolek) invented [Kevlar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevlar).[^cite_note-10]

## Aromatics



Chemicals produced from benzene

The most economically significant aromatic petrochemicals are the so-called [BTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTX_\(chemistry\)) fractions ([benzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzene), [toluene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluene), and mixed [xylenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylene)), which are derived from [naphtha](184342.md) via [fractional distillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_distillation). Heavier distillates contain heavier aromatics. [Naphthalene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naphthalene), the simplest polyaromatic compound, is found in [crude oil](23195.md) and [coal tar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_tar).[^cite_note-14] The heaviest aromatic compounds found in [bitumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitumen) are called [asphaltenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asphaltene).[^cite_note-15]

- [benzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzene) – the simplest aromatic hydrocarbon&#x20;

  - [ethylbenzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylbenzene) – made from benzene and ethylene&#x20;

    - [styrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styrene) – made by dehydrogenation of ethylbenzene; used as a monomer&#x20;

      - [polystyrenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polystyrene) – polymers with styrene as a monomer

  - [cumene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumene) – isopropylbenzene; a feedstock in the [cumene process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumene_process)&#x20;

    - [phenol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenol) – hydroxybenzene; often made by the cumene process

    - [acetone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetone) – dimethyl ketone; also often made by the cumene process

    - [bisphenol A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisphenol_A) – a type of "double" phenol used in polymerization in epoxy resins and making a common type of polycarbonate&#x20;

      - [epoxy resins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epoxy_resin) – a type of polymerizing glue from bisphenol A, epichlorohydrin, and some [amine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amine)

      - [polycarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate) – a plastic polymer made from bisphenol A and [phosgene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosgene) (carbonyl dichloride)

    - [solvents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvents) – liquids used for dissolving materials; examples often made from petrochemicals include ethanol, isopropyl alcohol, acetone, benzene, toluene, xylenes

  - [cyclohexane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclohexane) – a 6-carbon aliphatic cyclic hydrocarbon sometimes used as a non-polar solvent&#x20;

    - [adipic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adipic_acid) – a 6-carbon di[carboxylic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboxylic_acid), which can be a precursor used as a co-monomer together with a di[amine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amine) to form an alternating copolymer form of nylon.&#x20;

      - [nylons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon) – types of [polyamides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyamide), some are alternating copolymers formed from copolymerizing [dicarboxylic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicarboxylic_acid) or derivatives with diamines

    - [caprolactam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caprolactam) – a 6-carbon cyclic [amide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amide)&#x20;

      - [nylons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon) – types of [polyamides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyamide), some are from polymerizing caprolactam

  - [nitrobenzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrobenzene) – can be made by single nitration of benzene&#x20;

    - [aniline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aniline) – aminobenzene&#x20;

      - [methylene diphenyl diisocyanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methylene_diphenyl_diisocyanate) (MDI) – used as a co-monomer with [diols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diol) or [polyols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyol) to form [polyurethanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurethanes) or with di- or poly[amines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amine) to form [polyureas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurea)

  - [alkylbenzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkylbenzene) – a general type of aromatic hydrocarbon, which can be used as a precursor for a [sulfonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfonate) [surfactant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfactant) (detergent)&#x20;

    - [detergents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detergent) – often include [surfactants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfactant) types such as alkylbenzene sulfonates and nonylphenol ethoxylates

  - [chlorobenzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorobenzene)



Chemicals produced from toluene

- [toluene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluene) – methylbenzene; can be a solvent or precursor for other chemicals&#x20;

  - [benzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzene)

  - [toluene diisocyanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluene_diisocyanate) (TDI) – used as co-monomers with polyether polyols to form polyurethanes or with di- or poly[amines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amine) to form [polyureas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurea) [polyurethanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurethane)

  - [benzoic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzoic_acid) – carboxybenzene&#x20;

    - purified [terephthalic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terephthalic_acid) – often copolymerized to form [polyethylene terephthalate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyethylene_terephthalate)&#x20;

      - [polyesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyester)
