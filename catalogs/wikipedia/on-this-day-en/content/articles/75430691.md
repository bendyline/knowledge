The [opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opening_ceremony) of the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md) took place on 6 February 2026. It started at 20\:00 [CET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Time) (19\:00 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time)) and concluded at 23\:29.[^cite_note-1] It was held primarily at [San Siro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Siro) in [Milan](36511.md), but also included [Livigno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livigno), [Predazzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predazzo), and the streets of [Cortina](568829.md), Italy. This was the first opening ceremony of the Olympics which took place across four locations in the host country.[^cite_note-2] As mandated by the [Olympic Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Charter), the proceedings included an artistic program showcasing the culture of the host country and city, with *[harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony)* being a central theme,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] concluding with the [parade of athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Winter_Olympics_Parade_of_Nations) and the lighting of the [Olympic cauldrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_cauldron).



Olympic cauldron in Milan

The Games were [formally opened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_have_opened_the_Olympic_Games) by the [president of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy), [Sergio Mattarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Mattarella). This was the first Winter Olympics and first Olympic Games opening ceremony under the [IOC presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_International_Olympic_Committee) of [Kirsty Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsty_Coventry).[^cite_note-5]

## Preparations



[San Siro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Siro)

The opening ceremony team was composed by Marco Balich as Creative Lead, [Simone Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Ferrari_\(creative_director\)) as Creative Director & Deputy Creative Lead, Lulu Helbaek, Damiano Michieletto and Lida Castelli as Creative Directors[^cite_note-6] and produced by [Banijay Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banijay_Entertainment)'s Balich Wonder Studio. The Production Designer was Paolo Fantin, Costume Designer Massimo Cantini Parrini.[^cite_note-7] It is the first time that there are two cauldrons in two different cities. The ceremony primarily took place at the [San Siro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Siro) in [Milan](36511.md), while there were events, including the Parade of Nations at sites in [Cortina](568829.md), [Livigno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livigno), and [Predazzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predazzo), highlighting the Games' host sites.[^cite_note-programe-8][^cite_note-9] The ceremony featured about 1,200 volunteer performers and more than 1,400 costumes; the performers participated in nearly 700 hours of rehearsals. There were nearly 61,000 spectators at San Siro, with ticket prices reaching \$2,300 USD (€2,026).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] It was filmed by [Olympic Broadcasting Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Broadcasting_Services) (OBS) and broadcast by the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md)'s (IOC) global media rights holders.

The theme for the ceremony was "*Armonia*" (*Harmony*).[^cite_note-12] Balich explained that the word harmony derives from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek): "It means 'bringing together' in musical terms, different elements."[^cite_note-13]

## Proceedings



Flag bearers at the opening ceremony [Parade of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Winter_Olympics_Parade_of_Nations)

The ceremony began at 20\:00 CET with a 30-second countdown. At the end of the countdown, the text "Armonia" appeared on the screen, and fireworks were set off inside the stadium. The show itself began with a tribute to Italian artist [Antonio Canova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Canova), telling the story of [Cupid and Psyche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupid_and_Psyche) through a museum setting accompanied by dancers and music.[^cite_note-14] After this, Italian actress [Matilda De Angelis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilda_De_Angelis) 'conducted' Italian composers [Giacomo Puccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Puccini), [Gioachino Rossini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioachino_Rossini), and [Giuseppe Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi). Three hanging blue, red, and yellow paint tubes appeared over the stadium, releasing their colours while dancers performed, symbolising creativity and artistic expression. Their respective costumes showcased Italy's cultural heritage, cuisine, and history, most notably the character of [Pinocchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinocchio) and the Italian [Moka pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moka_pot). A brief tribute to the late Italian [pop culture icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_culture_icon) [Raffaella Carrà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaella_Carr%C3%A0) also occurred.[^cite_note-15] Afterwards, A performance by American singer [Mariah Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariah_Carey) followed, with her performing "[Nel blu, dipinto di blu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nel_blu,_dipinto_di_blu)" in Italian and her single "[Nothing Is Impossible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_for_It_All)".

At San Siro, a pre-filmed video segment introduced Italian President [Sergio Mattarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Mattarella) depicting him riding a [tram in Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Milan) driven by [Valentino Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentino_Rossi), before his entrance in the stadium together with [IOC](15147.md) President [Kirsty Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsty_Coventry) and the Games' organising committee president [Giovanni Malago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Malago). Following this, models wore costumes in the colors of the Italian flag designed by the late [Giorgio Armani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Armani). [Vittoria Ceretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittoria_Ceretti) passed the country's [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy) off to the [Carabinieri](45173.md); in Cortina, [Fulvio Valbusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulvio_Valbusa), [Giorgio Di Centa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Di_Centa), [Pietro Piller Cottrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Piller_Cottrer), and [Cristian Zorzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Zorzi) did the same to an all-women Carabinieri. Italian singer [Laura Pausini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Pausini) performed "[Il Canto degli Italiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Canto_degli_Italiani)", while a mountain choir did the same simultaneously in Cortina d'Ampezzo.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

The [Olympic rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_rings) were unveiled during a segment featuring dancers showcasing the 'harmony' between the city (Milan) and the mountains (Cortina), symbolising the two host cities of the Games. In addition, Italian actor [Pierfrancesco Favino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierfrancesco_Favino) delivered a recitation of [Giacomo Leopardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Leopardi)'s poem "[L'infinito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'infinito)".[^cite_note-18] The score was led by [Giovanni Andrea Zanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Andrea_Zanon?action=edit\&redlink=1) playing the 1716 *Berthier* [Stradivarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stradivarius) violin.[^cite_note-19] After this, the [parade of nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Winter_Olympics_Parade_of_Nations) commenced, beginning with [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) and ending with the host nation, [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics). Athletes from 93 countries paraded from four venues in Milan, Cortina d'Ampezzo, Livigno, and Predazzo,[^cite_note-20] against the backdrop of dance music provided by producer DJ Mace; the Italian athletes marched as a remixed version of "[Largo al factotum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largo_al_factotum)" from [Rossini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Rossini)'s opera *[The Barber of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Barber_of_Seville)* was heard.

Following the parade, Italian actress [Sabrina Impacciatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabrina_Impacciatore) and dancers celebrated the 100 years of the [Winter Olympic Games](34069.md), first showcasing all the past Winter Olympics in backwards chronological order in a premade animated video - also featuring the Games' mascots [Tina and Milo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_and_Milo) - and then live in the stadium. This entire segment featured in the premade video before the animated sequence. Music played during the segment included "[Prisencolinensinainciusol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisencolinensinainciusol)" by [Adriano Celentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriano_Celentano).[^cite_note-\:1-21] Prior to the official speeches by Coventry and Malagò, actress Brenda Lodigiani performed a segment on [Italian hand gestures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesticulation_in_Italian). President Mattarella then proceeded to declare the Games open. Afterward, Italian tenor singer [Andrea Bocelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Bocelli) performed "[Nessun dorma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nessun_dorma)".[^cite_note-22]

A segment promoting peace featured South African and American actress and [United Nations Messenger of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Messengers_of_Peace) [Charlize Theron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlize_Theron) and Italian rapper [Ghali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghali_\(rapper\)), the latter reciting the anti-war poem "Promemoria" by [Gianni Rodari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianni_Rodari) while dancers recreated the "[dove of peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dove_\(Picasso\))".[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

The [Olympic flag](731192.md) was raised in both Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo, carried by [Rebecca Andrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Andrade), [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge), [Cindy Ngamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cindy_Ngamba), [Pita Taufatofua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pita_Taufatofua), [Tadatoshi Akiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadatoshi_Akiba), [Maryam Bukar Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_Bukar_Hassan), [Nicolò Govoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%B2_Govoni?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Filippo Grandi](24484119.md), [Franco Nones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Nones) and [Martina Valcepina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Valcepina); the [Olympic Hymn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Hymn) was performed by Italian singer [Cecilia Bartoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecilia_Bartoli), American cellist [Yo-Yo Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yo-Yo_Ma), Chinese pianist [Lang Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lang_Lang), and the [Teatro alla Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Scala) children's choir members. The [Olympic oath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_oath) was stated at Cortina's Piazza Angelo Dibona by athletes (curler [Stefania Constantini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefania_Constantini) and luger [Dominik Fischnaller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominik_Fischnaller) of Italy), coaches, and judges.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

Before the lighting of the cauldrons, a tribute to the [European Union](9317.md) featuring the [anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthem_of_Europe) was shown. It was then followed by a short segment about Europe's involvement with space exploration and astronomy (with the [European Space Agency](10363.md)), showing Italian astronaut [Samantha Cristoforetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Cristoforetti) and a young girl witnessing a space-themed performance. The [Olympic flame](74217.md), which previously briefly entered the stadium, then proceeded to its final destination. The cauldron, inspired by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci)'s 'knot' geometric designs, was lit by Italian [alpine skiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_skiers) [Deborah Compagnoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_Compagnoni) and [Alberto Tomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Tomba) located in Milan's [Arco della Pace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arco_della_Pace) monument, while another was lit by Italian alpine skier [Sofia Goggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Goggia) at Cortina's Piazza Angelo Dibona.[^cite_note-27]

### Viewership

According to Auditel ratings, the opening ceremony was seen in Italy by an average of 9.2 million viewers.[^cite_note-78] In the United States, the ceremony was seen by 21.4 million viewers, a 34% increase from the [2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics_opening_ceremony).[^cite_note-79]

## Analysis

Critically, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*'s [Alexis Petridis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Petridis) gave the ceremony received mixed-to-positive reviews,[^cite_note-\:1-21] while *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))*'s Alison Herman described it as a "playful celebration of Italian culture."[^cite_note-80] In addition, Dominic Patten of *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood)* stated that the ceremony was "a beautiful but baffling Italian buffet."[^cite_note-81]

## Controversy

During the Parade of Nations, U.S. Vice President [JD Vance](53396477.md) and the [Israeli team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) were [booed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booing) by some of the audience present at San Siro.[^cite_note-82] American broadcaster [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympics_on_NBC) appeared to have cut the booing of Vance from footage.[^cite_note-83] While the negative reception was heard on OBS broadcasts, NBC's telecast received accusations of censorship.[^cite_note-NYT-Vance-Olympics-84]

The commentary of the opening ceremony aired by Italian broadcaster [RAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAI) was heavily criticized. Numerous gaffes were made by director [Paolo Petrecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Petrecca); in addition to speaking over images and the other two commentators, Fabio Genovesi and [Stefania Belmondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefania_Belmondo), Petrecca confused actress [Matilda De Angelis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilda_De_Angelis) with singer [Mariah Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariah_Carey), mistook [IOC president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_International_Olympic_Committee) [Kirsty Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsty_Coventry) for [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy) [Sergio Mattarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Mattarella)'s daughter, made offensive comments during the athletes' parade, and failed to recognize the Olympic champions of the [women's national volleyball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_women's_national_volleyball_team) (with the exception of [Paola Egonu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paola_Egonu)).[^cite_note-85] Rai Sport journalists announced that, in protest against Petrecca's commentary, they would withdraw their signatures from their reports, links, and commentary from 5\:00 p.m. on Monday, February 9th, until the end of the Olympic Games; they will then go on strike for three days.[^cite_note-86] The editorial board, the internal journalists' representation, has requested that the union statement be read during the Olympic news broadcasts and on the programs "Mattina Olimpica" and "Notti Olimpiche." The CEO of RAI asked him to take responsibility for both the commentary of the Olympic opening ceremony and for the tense relations with the editorial staff. On 19 February, Petrecca handed back his mandate to Rai CEO Giampaolo Rossi, announcing that he would be leaving his post at the end of the Olympic Games.[^cite_note-87]

When declaring the Games open, Italian President [Sergio Mattarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Mattarella) incorrectly stated "the 15th Olympic Winter Games" instead of the 25th.[^cite_note-88]
