**South Khorasan province** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): استان خراسان جنوبی)[^cite_note-4] is one of the 31 [provinces of Iran](448160.md), located in the eastern part of the country. Its capital is the city of [Birjand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birjand).[^cite_note-North,_Razavi,_and_South_Khorasan_Provinces_Establishment-5] In 2014, the province was placed in [Region 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Iran).[^cite_note-6] This province is bordered to the north by [Razavi Khorasan province](1289401.md), to the northwest by [Semnan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semnan_province), to the west by [Isfahan](448061.md) and [Yazd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazd_province) provinces, to the south by [Kerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_province) province, and to the southeast by [Sistan and Baluchestan](448105.md) province.

South Khorasan's area is 151,913 square kilometers (58,654 square miles), making it the third-largest province in Iran after Kerman and Sistan and Baluchestan. According to the 2016 census, South Khorasan's population is 768,898,[^cite_note-2016_South_Khorasan_Province-2] making it the fourth least populous province in the country. With only about 5 inhabitants per square kilometre or 13 inhabitants per square mile, it is the most sparsely populated province in Iran.

South Khorasan province is split into two distinct regions: the "mountainous and elevated" areas in the north and northwest, and the "low and flat" central and southern plains. The tallest mountains in the province include Kamarak and Shah Kuh. It experiences a "dry and desert" climate, which can be further divided into "hot and dry" and "mild and dry" categories. The northern and northwestern parts are more fertile, while the southern and southwestern plains endure a dry and harsh climate. The rivers in this region are seasonal, with no permanent rivers present.

## History



Map of Iran during [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate). Toon ([Ferdows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdows)), [Qaen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaen) and [Tabas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabas) are the cities indicated in [Quhistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quhistan) (Nowadays South Khorasan Province).

[Greater Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan) has witnessed the rise and fall of many dynasties and governments in its territory throughout history. Various tribes of the Arabs, Turks, Kurds and Turkmens brought changes to the region time and time again.[^cite_note-7] Ancient geographers of Iran divided Iran ("Iran-Shahr") into eight segments, of which the most flourishing and largest was the territory of Greater Khorasan. [Esfarayen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esfarayen), among other cities of the province, was one of the focal points for the residence of the Aryan tribes after entering Iran.

The [Parthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia) empire was based near [Merv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merv) in Khorasan for many years. At Parthians times, [Esfarayen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esfarayen) was one of the important villages of [Nishapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishapur).

During the [Sassanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid) dynasty, the province was governed by a *Spahbod* (Lieutenant General) called "Padgoosban" and four margraves, each commander of one of the four parts of the province.

Khorasan was divided into four parts during the [Muslim conquest of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia), each section being named after the four largest cities, Nishapur, [Merv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merv), [Herat](14128.md), and [Balkh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh).

In the year 651, the army of Islamic Arabs invaded Khorasan. The territory remained in the hands of the [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid) clan until 820, followed by the rule of the Iranian Taherid clan in the year 896 and the [Samanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samanid) dynasty in 900.

[Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) conquered Khorasan in 994 and in the year 1037 [Toghrül](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toghr%C3%BCl), the first of the [Seljuq empire rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Turks) conquered Nishapur.

Mahmud of Ghazni retaliated against the invaders several times, and finally the Ghaznavids defeated [Sultan Sanjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Sanjar). But there was more to come, as in 1157 Khorasan was conquered by [the Khwarazmids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarezmid_Empire) and because of simultaneous attacks by the Mongols, Khorasan was annexed to the territories of the Mongol [Ilkhanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate).

In the 14th century, a flag of independence was hoisted by the [Sarbedaran movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarbadars) in [Sabzevar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabzevar), and in 1468, Khorasan came into the hands of [Tamerlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamerlane) and the city of [Herat](14128.md) was declared as the capital.

In 1507, Khorasan was occupied by [Uzbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbeks) tribes. After the death of [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) in 1747, parts of it were occupied by the Afghans for a short period.

In these periods, Birjand was a small part of [Quhistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quhistan) which almost encompasses the borders of present-day South Khorasan. The main cities of Quhistan were Toon (now [Ferdows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdows)) and [Qaen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaen). Birjand grew in recent centuries, especially during the [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) and found its important role in this region.

This region was a place of refuge for some movements like the [Ismaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismaili), and was the target of Arab refugees who escaped from the tyranny of the [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid) caliphate. Zoroastrian vestiges also exist in the area.

In 1824, Herat became independent for several years when the Afghan Empire was split between the Durranis and [Barakzais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barakzai_dynasty). The Persians [sieged the city in 1837](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Herat_\(1838\)), but the British assisted the Afghans in repelling them. In 1856, the Persians launched another invasion, and briefly managed to recapture the city; it led directly to the [Anglo-Persian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Persian_War). In 1857 hostilities between the Persians and the British ended after the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1857\)) was signed, and the Persian troops withdrew from Herat.[^cite_note-8] Afghanistan reconquered Herat in 1863 under [Dost Muhammad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dost_Muhammad_Khan), two weeks before his death.[^cite_note-9]

This new province is but the old [Quhistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quhistan) which was included into [greater Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan) in the Iranian administrative planning. However, historically Qohistan forms a separate entity, with a distinct culture, history, environment and ecology.

South Khorasan is one of the three provinces that were created after the division of [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan_Province) in 2004. While at the beginning, the newly created "South Khorasan" included only [Birjand County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birjand_County) and some new counties detached from that county (i.e. [Nehbandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nehbandan_County), [Darmian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmian_County) and [Sarbisheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarbisheh_County)), in subsequent years, all northern and western cities and territories of the old Quhistan (such as [Qaen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaen_County), [Ferdows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdows_County) and [Tabas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabas_County)) have been annexed into South Khorasan, which as of 2016 consists of 11 counties.

Khorasan was the largest province of Iran until it was divided into three provinces on 29 September 2004. The provinces approved by the [parliament of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis_of_Iran) (on 18 May 2004) and the [Council of Guardians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Guardians) (on 29 May 2004) were [Razavi Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razavi_Khorasan), [North Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Khorasan), and South Khorasan.[^cite_note-North,_Razavi,_and_South_Khorasan_Provinces_Establishment-5]

## Demographics

### Population

At the time of the 2006 National Census, the province's population was 600,568 in 159,255 households.[^cite_note-2006_South_Khorasan_Province-10] The following census in 2011 counted 662,534 people in 183,114 households.[^cite_note-2011_South_Khorasan_Province-11] The 2016 census measured the population of the province as 768,898 in 223,984 households.[^cite_note-2016_South_Khorasan_Province-2] It is the least densely populated province.

## South Khorasan today

The major ethnic group in this region are the [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people).[^cite_note-Ethnologue-Pashto-22] South Khorasan is known for its famous rugs as well as its [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron), [barberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barberry) which is produced in almost all parts of the province.
