[Presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_primaries) and caucuses are going to be organized by the [Republican Party](32070.md) to select delegates to the [2028 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_Republican_National_Convention) to determine the party's nominee for [president](24113.md) in the [2028 election](77541080.md). The primaries and caucuses will take place in all 50 U.S. states, [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), and five U.S. territories. No primary dates have been determined.

Incumbent President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) is constitutionally ineligible to be elected to a third term as president under the [Twenty-second Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-second_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution). It is disputed whether he is also ineligible to serve as [vice president](32759.md) under the [Twelfth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution).

If no member of the [Trump family](52170275.md) runs, it would be the first Republican primary since [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries) without a Trump, as Donald Trump ran for president in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries), [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries).

#### Dan Greaney

[Dan Greaney](5199948.md) is a television writer, lawyer, and [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) graduate. Greaney is credited with writing the episode of *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons)* "[Bart to the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bart_to_the_Future)" in 2000. The episode presented the possibility of a [Donald Trump](4848272.md) presidency, which took place for the first time in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-4] On May 26, 2026, Greaney announced his intention to run for president in 2028 on his [Instagram](31591547.md) account.[^cite_note-5] In a press release, his campaign stated he would run as a "progressive [Republican](32070.md) in the tradition of [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) and [Teddy Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt)," naming [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care) and the [Green New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_New_Deal) as policy planks.[^cite_note-independentmay26-2] He held his official campaign announcement on June 5, 2026.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-klee-7]

#### Royce White

[Royce White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royce_White) is a former professional basketball player. He is mainly known for his [NBA](22093.md) career, playing three games with the [Sacramento Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Kings) in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Sacramento_Kings_season). White was a candidate for [Minnesota's 5th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota's_5th_congressional_district) in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Minnesota#District_5), but lost the Republican primary. He ran for [U.S. Senate](24909346.md) in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota), but was defeated in the general by [Amy Klobuchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Klobuchar). White ran for Senate once more in [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota), coming in third in the [Republican primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota#Republican_primary). On August 12, the day following his loss in the primary, White announced he would seek the Republican presidential nomination.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-roycewhite-3]

#### Don Bacon

[Don Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bacon) has served as the U.S. representative for [Nebraska's 2nd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska's_2nd_congressional_district) since 2017. He mentioned in a podcast interview which was reported on in September 2026 by *The Hill*[^cite_note-13] and *The Washington Examiner*[^cite_note-14] that he was interested in a presidential run in 2028.

#### Chris Christie

[Chris Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Christie) served as [governor of New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_Jersey) from 2010 to 2018, and ran two unsuccessful bids for president in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries). When discussing 2028 at the Granite State Summit in New Hampshire, Christie stated that he was considering a run for president.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

#### Ted Cruz

[Ted Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Cruz) has served as a United States senator from [Texas](29810.md) since 2013 after being appointed as the 3rd [Solicitor General of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitor_General_of_Texas) by then [Texas Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Attorney_General) [Greg Abbott](1492735.md), he served this role from 2003 to 2008. Initially reluctant to support Trump, Cruz has become a key Republican figure and a close ally of Trump.[^cite_note-17] Cruz was born in [Canada](5042916.md) after his father, preacher [Rafael Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Cruz), immigrated there from Cuba, though he is still able to fulfill the natural-born citizen requirement due to his mother's American citizenship which he inherited.[^cite_note-18] He now serves as the chair of the [Senate Commerce Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Commerce,_Science,_and_Transportation) after serving as the ranking member from 2023 to 2025. [The runner-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Cruz_2016_presidential_campaign) to Donald Trump in the [2016 Republican Party presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries), Cruz has said that he expects to seek the presidency again at some point. In May 2026, Cruz travelled to early primary state Iowa, prompting speculation that he was laying the groundwork for a presidential run.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] While speaking on Texas Lt. Governor [Dan Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Patrick_\(politician\))'s podcast in August, Cruz stated that "it's no secret that I want to be president."[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

#### Ron DeSantis

[Ron DeSantis](36729527.md) has served as the 46th [governor of Florida](233840.md) since 2019 and previously ran for the Republican presidential nomination in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_DeSantis_2024_presidential_campaign). He is barred by the state's constitution from seeking a third term as governor. He expressed an openness towards running for president in a March 2026 podcast interview with [Sean Hannity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Hannity).[^cite_note-23] The *[Florida Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Phoenix)* described it as DeSantis "leaving the door open" for a potential run.[^cite_note-24] Later in May, DeSantis once again left the door open to run for president, responding "You never know" when asked if he has plans to run.[^cite_note-25] Polls from around that time show he was trailing behind Rubio and Vance.[^cite_note-26]

#### John Kennedy

[John Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kennedy_\(Louisiana_politician\)) has served as a [U.S. senator](24909346.md) from [Louisiana](18130.md) since 2017. He said in a New Hampshire interview at Politics and Eggs that he planned to run for re-election but he would "never say never" to a possible presidential run.[^cite_note-27] He said in a June 2026 interview at [New Hampshire Institute of Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_Institute_of_Politics) at [Saint Anselm College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Anselm_College) that he had been contacted by donors about a run for president and that he was open to the idea.[^cite_note-28]

#### Rand Paul

[Rand Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul) has served as a [U.S. senator](24909346.md) from [Kentucky](16846.md) since 2011. He previously [ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Paul_2016_presidential_campaign) for [president](24113.md) in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries), dropping out after finishing fifth in the [2016 Iowa Republican presidential caucuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Iowa_Republican_presidential_caucuses).[^cite_note-29] Paul stated in a June interview with *[Semafor](72046207.md)* that he would "look at" a presidential run after the midterms conclude.[^cite_note-30] In September 2026, Paul said on Newsmax that he plans to “look thoroughly at it as we see the results come in”[^cite_note-31] and told *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* he’s "50-50 on making the race."[^cite_note-32]

#### JD Vance

[JD Vance](53396477.md) has served as the 50th [vice president of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_president_of_the_United_States) since 2025. He previously served as a U.S. senator from [Ohio](22199.md) from 2023 to 2025. In an interview in November 2025, he acknowledged giving consideration to running for president in 2028 and planned to speak with Trump about it after the midterms, but said he wants to focus on winning the midterms first.[^cite_note-33] In February 2026, Reuters reported that Vance and Rubio had become the central figures in speculation over Trump's possible Republican successor, while noting that Trump had declined to endorse either man.[^cite_note-34] In May 2026, Vance travelled to early primary state Iowa to support representative [Zach Nunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Nunn), fueling speculation that he was laying groundwork for a presidential run.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

## Timeline

See also: [Timeline of the 2028 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_2028_United_States_presidential_election)

### 2024

- **November 6**: [Donald Trump](4848272.md) is declared the winner of the [2024 presidential election](59568546.md) by a consensus of major news outlets projecting the results, defeating incumbent [Vice President](32759.md) [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris).

### 2025

- **January 20**: [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and [JD Vance](53396477.md) are [inaugurated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump) as the [47th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_United_States) [president](24113.md) and [50th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_vice_presidents_of_the_United_States) [vice president](32759.md) of the United States.

- **February 22**: Vice President Vance wins the 2025 [CPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Political_Action_Conference) presidential straw poll with 61%, with [Steve Bannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bannon) in second at 12%.

  [^cite_note-74]

- **March 19**: *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reports that Trump and the Republican Party had threatened, signed executive actions, and ordered investigations into their political opponents, critics, and organizations aligned with the Democratic Party and progressive politics in an attempt to hobble Democrats' ability to compete in future elections.

  [^cite_note-75]

- **March 30:** [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) reports that [Trump would not rule out seeking a third term as president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_third_term_proposal), with Trump saying he "was not joking" about a third term, and telling NBC "there are methods" for doing so, including running for vice president with [JD Vance](53396477.md) running for president and, upon winning, passing the role to Trump.

  [^cite_note-76]

- **April 24**: [The Trump Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trump_Organization) begins selling hats and T-shirts with "Trump 2028" written on them, provoking questions about whether or not Trump would attempt to run for a third term.

  [^cite_note-77]

- **May 4**: Trump says in an interview with [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News) that he will only serve two terms as president and will not run again after winning twice, mentioning both Vance and [Secretary of State](32293.md) [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) as his potential successors.

  [^cite_note-78]

  [^cite_note-79]

- **September 30**: In an [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office) meeting with congressional leaders prior to the [2025 government shutdown](81225965.md), Trump displays red hats with "Trump 2028" emblazoned on the front.

  [^cite_note-80]

### 2026

- **March 28**: Vice President Vance wins the 2026 [CPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Political_Action_Conference) presidential straw poll with 53%, with [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) in second at 35%.

  [^cite_note-81]

- **May 26**: Television writer [Dan Greaney](5199948.md) announces his candidacy for the Republican nomination, marking the first candidate to be mentioned as declared in the press. His key policies include [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care) and the [Green New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_New_Deal).

  [^cite_note-independentmay26-2]

- **June 8**: [Gregory Bovino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Bovino), the former commander-at-large of the [United States Border Patrol](567453.md), forms an exploratory committee for a possible run for the Republican nomination.

  [^cite_note-82]

- **August 12**: Former [NBA](22093.md) basketball player [Royce White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royce_White) announces his intention to run for the Republican nomination following his loss in the [Republican primary for Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota#Republican_primary) in [Minnesota](19590.md).

  [^cite_note-roycewhite-3]

- **September 9–10**: The [2026 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Republican_National_Convention) is held in [Dallas, Texas](53838.md).

  [^cite_note-83]

- **September 10**: The [Republican National Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_National_Committee) approves the 2028 Republican presidential nominating calendar, keeping [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_caucuses) as the first contest, followed by [New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_presidential_primary), [Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_presidential_caucuses) and [South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_presidential_primary).
