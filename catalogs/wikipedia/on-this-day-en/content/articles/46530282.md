A **robotaxi**, also known as **robot taxi**, **robo-taxi**, **self-driving taxi** or **driverless taxi**, is an [autonomous car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_car) ([SAE automation level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_automation_level) 4 or 5) operated for a [ridesharing company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridesharing_company).

Consultancy firms predict that robotaxis operated in an [autonomous mobility on demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_mobility_on_demand) (AMoD) service could be a large application of autonomous cars at scale, especially in [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area).[^cite_note-gauging-1] Market forecasts suggest they have a positive impact on [congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestion_\(traffic\)) and [parking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking_space) over private cars,[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] while some firms such as [Kearney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kearney_\(consulting_firm\)) predict a negative impact due to [induced demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_demand).[^cite_note-7] Studies on the impacts on [road safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_safety) are mixed, with crash rates higher than humans in certain pilots.[^cite_note-gauging-1][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

Robotaxis could also reduce urban [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution) and [energy consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_efficiency_in_transport), since less weight and range is necessary compared to individually owned vehicles.[^cite_note-10] The expected reduction in number of vehicles means less [embodied energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embodied_energy);[^cite_note-11] however energy consumption for redistribution of empty vehicles must be taken into account.[^cite_note-12] Robotaxis would reduce operating costs by eliminating the need for a human driver, which might make it an affordable form of transportation and increase the popularity of [transportation-as-a-service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_as_a_service) (TaaS) as opposed to individual [car ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_ownership).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

Such developments could lead to job destruction[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] and new challenges concerning operator liabilities.[^cite_note-19] In 2023, some robotaxis caused congestion when they blocked roads due to lost cellular connectivity, and others failed to properly yield to emergency vehicles.[^cite_note-cbsnews.com-20] As of 2023, there has been one fatality associated with a robotaxi,[ a pedestrian who was hit by an Uber test vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber_developmental_self-driving_car_pedestrian_death) in 2018.&#x20;

Predictions of the widespread and rapid introduction of robotaxis – by as early as 2018 – have not yet been realized.[^cite_note-21] Several companies provide robotaxi services of varying degrees of automation in parts of the world. As of 2025, the number and scope of these services is rapidly expanding, but they still all operate at a financial loss.[^cite_note-revolution-22] An [American Automobile Association](298817.md) survey in 2025 said that only 13% of respondents would trust a self-driving vehicle.[^cite_note-23]

### Vehicle costs

So far, most trials have involved specially modified passenger cars with space for two or four passengers sitting in the back seats behind a partition. [LIDAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidar), cameras and other sensors have been used on most vehicles. The cost of early vehicles was estimated in 2020 at up to US\$400,000 due to custom manufacture and specialized sensors.[^cite_note-24] However, the prices of some components such as LIDAR have fallen significantly.[^cite_note-25] In January 2021, Waymo stated its costs were approximately \$180,000 per vehicle, and its operating cost at \$0.30 per mile (\~\$0.19 per km), well below Uber and Lyft, but this excludes the cost of fleet technicians and customer support.[^cite_note-WaymoJan21-26] [Baidu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidu) announced in June 2021 it would start producing purpose-built robotaxi vehicles for 500,000 yuan (\$77,665) each under the [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Go) brand.[^cite_note-27] Tesla has discussed a sub-\$25,000 vehicle omitting the use of sensors and solely relying on camera data and plans to enter the market with a purpose-built vehicle for Robotaxi service, the [Tesla Cybercab](78086975.md), in 2026.[^cite_note-Isaacson2023-28]

### Passenger tests

Several companies are testing robotaxi services, especially in the United States and in China. All operate only in a [geo-fenced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geo-fence) area. Service areas for robotaxis, also known as the Objective Design Domain (ODD), are specially designated zones where robotaxis can provide service.[^cite_note-29]

Separate to these efforts have been trials of larger [shared autonomous vehicles](5071866.md#Shared_autonomous_vehicles) on fixed routes with designated stops, able to carry between 6 and 10 passengers. These shuttle buses have thus far operated at low speeds.

### Current obstacles to robotaxis

At present, it is not only technical issues that hinder the widespread use of robotaxis, but also social issues. Consumers' concerns about the reliability and safety of self-driving taxis are an obstacle to broad acceptance. For example, system failures during the service process and the perception of the risks of an accident reduces the number of users.[^cite_note-30] In addition, consumers have doubts about whether the robotaxi can cope with complex urban environments or severe weather conditions.

### Licenses

In February 2018, Arizona granted Waymo a Transportation Network Company permit.[^cite_note-31]

In February 2022, the [California Public Utilities Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Public_Utilities_Commission) (CPUC) issued Drivered Deployment permits to Cruise and Waymo to allow passenger service in autonomous vehicles with a safety driver present in the vehicle. These carriers must hold a valid [California Department of Motor Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Department_of_Motor_Vehicles) (DMV) Deployment permit and meet the requirements of the CPUC Drivered Deployment program.[^cite_note-32] In June 2022, Cruise received approval to operate a commercial robotaxi service in [San Francisco](49728.md).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

In April 2022, China gave [Baidu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baidu) and [Pony.ai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pony.ai) its first permits to deploy robotaxis without safety drivers on open roads within a 23 square mile area in the [Beijing Economic-Technological Development Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Economic-Technological_Development_Area).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

In August 2023, the CPUC approved granting additional operating authority for Cruise LLC and Waymo LLC to conduct commercial passenger service using vehicles without safety drivers in San Francisco.[^cite_note-california-37][^cite_note-SF-38] The approval includes the ability for both companies to charge fares for rides at any time of day.[^cite_note-california-37][^cite_note-39] California subsequently enlarged Waymo's permitted operating area to specified [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area) localities, to airports and freeways in the area, and, in late 2025, to the entire Bay Area including the North and East Bay and, after securing an additional permit, to [Sacramento, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento,_California).[^cite_note-40]

## History

See also: [History of self-driving cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_self-driving_cars)

### First trials

On 18 August 2016, [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) CEO Travis Kalanick announced the company would be opening their autonomous taxi fleet to the public in [Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania](25101.md) in the next few weeks.[^cite_note-uber-41] In a race to be first, [NuTonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NuTonomy) surprise-launched their robotaxi service a week later, though only to select members of the public through a closed-invitation and in a limited district in Singapore.[^cite_note-nutonomy-42] Uber proceeded with their public launch in September 2016, with customers being assigned human or autonomous cars interchangeably when requesting rides through the regular Uber app.[^cite_note-uber2-43]

NuTonomy later signed three significant partnerships to develop its robotaxi service: with [Grab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grab_\(application\)), Uber’s rival in Southeast Asia, with [Groupe PSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupe_PSA), which is supposed to provide the company with [Peugeot 3008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_3008) SUVs and the last one with [Lyft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyft) to launch a robotaxi service in [Boston](24437894.md), Massachusetts.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

In August 2017, Cruise Automation, a self-driving startup acquired by [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) in 2016, launched the beta version of a robotaxi service for its employees in San Francisco using a fleet of 46 [Chevrolet Bolt EVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Bolt_EV).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

### Baidu Apollo

In September 2019, Baidu's autonomous driving unit Apollo launched [Apollo Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Go) robotaxi service, with an initial fleet of 45 autonomous vehicles. Apollo Go has since expanded to more than 10 Chinese cities.[^cite_note-76]

In August 2022, Baidu secured the first permits in China to deploy fully driverless taxis in the cities of Wuhan and Chongqing.[^cite_note-77]

In May 2024, Baidu unveiled the Apollo ADFM, claimed to be the world's first Level 4 autonomous driving foundation model, along with the sixth-generation Apollo Go robotaxi, which can be produced for under \$30,000.[^cite_note-78] The company also said by April 2024, Apollo had accumulated over 100 million kilometers of autonomous driving without major accidents.

In August 2024, Apollo Go had deployed 400 robotaxis operating fully autonomously without safety personnel on board in Wuhan, offering 24/7 service to 9 million residents.[^cite_note-79] Baidu aimed for Apollo Go to achieve operational unit breakeven in Wuhan by the end of 2024.

### GM Cruise

In January 2020, GM subsidiary [Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_\(autonomous_vehicle\)) exhibited the Cruise Origin, a [Level 4–5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-driving_car#Classification) driverless vehicle,[^cite_note-80] intended to be used for a [ride hailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ride_hailing) service.[^cite_note-81]

In February 2022, Cruise started driverless taxi service in San Francisco.[^cite_note-cruise_2022-02-71][^cite_note-82] Also in February 2022, Cruise petitioned U.S. regulators ([NHTSA](989530.md)) for permission to build and deploy a self-driving vehicle without human controls.[^cite_note-83] As of April 2022, the petition was pending.[^cite_note-84]

In April 2022, their partner [Honda](13729.md) unveiled its Level 4 mobility service partners to roll out in central Tokyo, Japan in the mid-2020s using the Cruise Origin.[^cite_note-85]

Autonomously operated Cruise vehicles may interfere with emergency vehicles;[^cite_note-The_Guardian-86] at least one had collided with a fire truck.[^cite_note-87]

On 2 October 2023, a Cruise vehicle operating autonomously and without driver supervision collided with a pedestrian. Instead of stopping immediately, the vehicle misidentified the collision mechanics and presumed it was crashed into from the side. Consequently, the vehicle proceeded to drag the pedestrian under the car for 20 ft (6.1 m) until it came to a stop on the side of the road. As both the response of the vehicle was deemed unacceptable and the company appears to have withheld details of the crash from regulators, California regulators revoked the license to operate these cars. Cruise recalled all of its 950 vehicles in November 2023.[^cite_note-The_Guardian-86][^cite_note-88]

These decisions were enacted in parallel with the exposure of safety risks, identified earlier within the Cruise company, regarding proper vehicle behavior around children and around construction sites.[^cite_note-89]

### Tesla

See also: [Tesla next-generation vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_next-generation_vehicle)

[Tesla](5533631.md)'s CEO [Elon Musk](909036.md) has [predicted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_predictions_for_autonomous_Tesla_vehicles_by_Elon_Musk) since 2019 that Tesla would have robotaxis on the road within years.[^cite_note-90] He announced the plans for Tesla's robotaxi on 10 October 2024.[^cite_note-91] During that event, Tesla demonstrated two new vehicles, the two-seater [Tesla Cybercab](78086975.md) and the 14-seater (plus standing room) [Tesla Robovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Robovan), which can carry up to 20 passengers. The company also reiterated that all of their other models of cars and pickup trucks would be usable as robotaxis after a software update and regulatory approval, which they expected at the earliest in California and Texas in 2025.

On 22 June 2025, Tesla launched their commercial taxi service [Tesla Robotaxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Robotaxi) to a small group of invited users in Austin, Texas. Except for having *Robotaxi* written on the front doors, Tesla said the vehicles were be unmodified cars from their factory. Rides were priced at a flat rate of \$4.20 within a geofenced area. While no one was in the driver's seat, a Tesla employee was still present in the front passenger seat for safety reasons.[^cite_note-92]

In January 2026, the service began to integrate unsupervised vehicles into its fleet in a limited manner,[^cite_note-93] and stated it planned to expand to seven additional cities in the first half of 2026.[^cite_note-\:0-94] In April 2026, the service expanded to Dallas and Houston in Texas with unsupervised vehicles.[^cite_note-\:2-95] In July 2026, the service expanded to Miami, Orlando, and Tampa with unsupervised vehicles outside of Texas for the first time.[^cite_note-\:3-96][^cite_note-\:5-97]

In September 2026, the service began to rollout their purpose built vehicle, the Tesla [Cybercab](78086975.md). This vehicle features no manual controls and only two seats with the goal of optimizing production and operational costs. Cybercab rollout initially began in Austin with plans to expand to additional cities within their network in the near future.[^cite_note-\:6-98]

### Uber Advanced Technology Group

[Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) began development of self-driving vehicles in early 2015. In September 2016, the company started a trial allowing a select group of users of its [ride-hailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridesharing_company) service in Pittsburgh to order robotaxis from a fleet of 14 modified [Ford Fusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Fusion_Hybrid).[^cite_note-99] The test extended to San Francisco with modified [Volvo XC90s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_XC90) before being relocated to Tempe, Arizona in February 2017.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

In March 2017, one of Uber's robotaxis crashed in self-driving mode in Arizona, which led the company to suspend its tests before resuming them a few days later.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] In March 2018, Uber paused self-driving vehicle testing after the [death of Elaine Herzberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Elaine_Herzberg) in Tempe, Arizona, a pedestrian struck by an Uber vehicle while attempting to cross the street, while the onboard safety engineer was watching videos.[^cite_note-sagere-104] Uber settled with the victim's family.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-sagere-104]

In January 2021, Uber sold its self driving division, Advanced Technologies Group (ATG), to [Aurora Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_Innovation) for \$4 billion while also investing \$400 million into Aurora for a 26% ownership stake.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

### Waymo



A Waymo [Jaguar I-Pace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_I-Pace) as it autonomously drives through San Francisco, California

In early 2017, [Waymo](29156200.md), the [Google](1092923.md) self-driving car project that became an independent company in 2016, started a large public robotaxi test in [Phoenix](49121.md), Arizona, using 100 and then 500 more [Chrysler Pacifica Hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_Pacifica_Hybrid) minivans provided by [Fiat Chrysler Automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Chrysler_Automobiles) as part of a partnership between the two companies.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] Waymo also signed a deal with [Lyft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyft) to collaborate on self-driving cars in May 2017.[^cite_note-111] In November 2017, Waymo revealed it had begun to operate some of its automated vehicles in Arizona without a safety driver behind the wheel.[^cite_note-112] In December 2018, Waymo started self-driving taxi service, dubbed Waymo One, in Arizona for paying customers.[^cite_note-waymo_one_2018-55] By November 2019, the service was operating autonomous vehicles without a safety backup driver.[^cite_note-verge-201912-113][^cite_note-vox-2020-02-114]

After testing starting in 2021[^cite_note-115] and an application in late 2022,[^cite_note-116] in August 2023 Waymo received a permit to offer unrestricted commercial driverless taxi service in San Francisco.[^cite_note-SF-38] Service was expanded into [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley) during 2025.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

As of December 2025, in addition to Phoenix and the San Francisco Bay Area, Waymo is operating in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles), California, [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Austin), Texas, and [Atlanta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Atlanta), and has announced expansion into 24 more metropolitan areas.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] In October 2025, Waymo announced it would start providing its services in [London](17867.md), its first international operating base, from 2026.

In February 2026, Waymo used an [artificial intelligence](1164.md) model trained off of [Genie 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genie_3) called the Waymo World Model to create edge-case scenarios for training their robotaxis.[^cite_note-pcmag-wymo-121]

As of June 2026, Waymo operates public commercial robotaxi services in 10 US metropolitan areas, has 3,871 robotaxis in service, provides 500,000 paid rides per week and had logged 200 million fully autonomous miles.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-Reuters_2026-02-03-123][^cite_note-124]

### XPeng

In 2026 XPeng announced production of its first vision-only robotaxi that operates without Lidar or high-definition maps. It began testing Level 4 service in Guangzhou.[^cite_note-125]

## Profitability

Robotaxi services operate at a loss as of 2025, with operating costs per mile of about \$7 to \$9, compared to \$1 a mile for personal cars. The consultancy [McKinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey_&_Company) estimated that bringing costs down to less than \$2 a mile would take until 2035.[^cite_note-revolution-22]
