**Mikhail Mikhailovich Kasyanov**[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] (born 8 December 1957) is a Russian politician who served as [Prime Minister of Russia](339344.md) from 2000 to 2004. Previously, he had served as [First Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Russia) in 2000 and [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(Russia\)) from 1999 to 2000. During the 1990s, he worked in President [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin)'s administration in different positions before joining President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md)'s first administration. He has the federal state civilian service rank of [1st class Active State Councillor of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_class_Active_State_Councillor_of_the_Russian_Federation).[^cite_note-3]

Since leaving the government over disagreements on economic policy in 2004, he has become one of the leading critics of President Putin and an [opposition leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_opposition). In 2008, Kasyanov was a [candidate in the election of President of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Russian_presidential_election) but in the middle of the campaign was denied participation on political grounds. In 2010, he co-founded the coalition [People's Freedom Party "For Russia without Lawlessness and Corruption"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Freedom_Party_%22For_Russia_without_Lawlessness_and_Corruption%22) and became one of the leaders of the [People's Freedom Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Freedom_Party_\(Russia\)).&#x20;

He was an active speaker during the [2011–2013 Russian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%932013_Russian_protests) for fair elections. Since 2015, he has served as the leader of the political party [People's Freedom Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Freedom_Party_\(Russia\)) (PARNAS). From 2005 to 2007, he was one of the leaders of the opposition coalition [The Other Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Other_Russia_\(coalition\)) and democratic protest movement.

## Early life

Kasyanov was born on 8 December 1957 in the settlement of [Solntsevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solntsevo_District) near Moscow. His father was a teacher of mathematics and the headmaster of a local school. Joining the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army), his father participated in some major actions of the [Second World War](32927.md) and fought until the victory in 1945.[^cite_note-svoboda-4]

Kasyanov's mother was an economist, a head of a department of a governmental construction company (Glavmosstroy). In his childhood Mikhail studied at a music school and played the cello. In high school he played in a rock group.[^cite_note-svoboda-4] In 1974 Kasyanov entered the Moscow Automobile and Road Construction University.[^cite_note-5]

In 1976–1978, Kasyanov served in the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ground_Forces) in the [guard of honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_of_honour) at the [Office of the Commandant of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandant's_Office_of_the_Moscow_Kremlin), which is today the [Kremlin Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin_Regiment) of the [Federal Protective Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Protective_Service_\(Russia\)). From 1978–1981, he became a technician and then an engineer at the scientific institute of the State Committee for Construction of USSR. At the time he was still studying at the Moscow Automobile and Road Construction University. He finished in 1981.[^cite_note-6]

For nine years after graduation Kasyanov worked at the [State Planning Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosplan) (GosPlan) as an engineer, economist, leading specialist and chief of sector. In 1987 he was awarded the diploma of the Highest Economy Courses at GosPlan.[^cite_note-7]

## Career

### Ministry of Economy (1990–1993)

In 1990–1993, Kasyanov was working in the State Committee of Economy of the Russian Federation and then in the [Ministry of Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economic_Development_\(Russia\)). In 1993 the Minister of Finance [Boris Fyodorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Fyodorov) invited Kasyanov to join the Ministry. He was appointed the head of Foreign Loans and External Debt Department.

### Ministry then Minister of Finance (1995–1999)

In 1995 Kasyanov became the deputy Minister of Finance.

In 1994–1996, Kasyanov was occupied with the restructuring of Soviet externals debts (about \$150 bn).[^cite_note-8] In 1996 Kasyanov reached an agreement with the [Paris Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Club) and the [London Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Club) of creditors for a comprehensive restructuring of Soviet debts for a period of 25 years with a 7-year grace period. Thanks to this agreement Russia was able to gain access to international capital markets.[^cite_note-9]

In 1996, Kasyanov gave the [road show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_roadshows) of Russia's economy development prospects at International finance centers. As a result, Russia, for the first time since 1913 issued securities, [eurobonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobond_\(external_bond\)) on the capital markets of Europe and the United States, and Russian banks and companies were able to take cheap foreign capital for investments into the economy of the country.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The [1997 Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis) and the [1998 Russian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Russian_financial_crisis) led to Russia defaulting on its debts, and the [ruble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruble) was dramatically devaluated. Kasyanov was the head of the working team for restructuring Russian government external debts and of the private banks of the country. At the same time he negotiated with Russian creditors for restructuring domestic debts. Kasyanov negotiated with the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) which granted favorable perspectives for reaching profitable agreements for Russia. As a result of this, the ruble rate was stabilised, which in its own turn helped reduce the inflation and restored production and services parts of Russian economy. These successes proved Kasyanov to be a good negotiator: he was called "the principal financial diplomat of the country".[^cite_note-12]

### Minister of Finance (1999–2000)

In February 1999 he was appointed First Deputy Minister and in May 1999 [President Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin) appointed Kasyanov Minister of Finance of the Russian Federation.[^cite_note-13]

In June 1999, Minister Kasyanov became a member of the [Security Council of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council_of_Russia).[^cite_note-riaru-14]

Kasyanov was occupied with the problem of budget deficit and external debt. His time as Finance Minister saw Russia's first non-deficit budget. In July Russia received the first post-crisis loan from the IMF.[^cite_note-riaru-14]

The government of [Sergei Stepashin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Stepashin) worked for three months; however, Kasyanov retained his post in the new government of [Vladimir Putin](32817.md). He continued the negotiations with international creditors on a global restructuring of the USSR's debts.

The [war in Chechnya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Chechen_War) provoked the suspension of the negotiations with the London club of creditors. Nevertheless, Kasyanov was able to resolve the principal problems of Russian debts in a span of several months. This fact facilitated the transition towards economic growth in Russia.[^cite_note-15] As a result of non-deficit [budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_budget_of_Russia) and prudent financial policy in general the multi-month delays in salary payments and pensions were overcome. On 10 January 2000 Kasyanov was appointed the first Vice Premier of the [Russian government](9787373.md). The situation was such that effectively Kasyanov was in charge of the government, serving as its chairman, as Putin was the Acting President at the time. He was still the head of the Ministry of Finance.[^cite_note-16]

### Prime Minister (2000–2004)



Putin, Kasyanov and Ukrainian Prime Minister [Viktor Yushchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yushchenko) in Moscow, 2000

On 17 May 2000, Russian [State Duma](1010187.md) approved Mikhail Kasyanov's Prime Minister candidacy. The Chairman of the [Cabinet](9787373.md) started pursuing the policy of active collaboration with business community and civil society.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

During its four-year term [Kasyanov's Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Kasyanov's_Cabinet) launched a number of structural reforms – tax and budget reform, liberalization of capital control and external trade, customs reform, reorganization of national infrastructure, pension reform, creation of land market and others. Successful implementation of systemic transformation measures led Russia to a trajectory of sustainable economic growth. Inflation was reduced significantly, the economy and people's income grew by one third while oil prices were at the level of \$20–25 per barrel.[^cite_note-19]



Kasyanov with Vladimir Putin, 2000

Experts think that the tax reform of 2000-2002 was one of the most successful reforms of Kasyanov's Cabinet. A flat income tax rate (13%) was introduced. All turnover taxes were eliminated. [VAT](52177473.md) was reduced to 18% and social taxes — to 26%. These measures together with the policy of strong fiscal discipline significantly increased budget revenue.[^cite_note-20]

In 2003, Kasyanov's Cabinet reformed the taxation of [petroleum sector](10450931.md) and established a special [Stabilization fund](10329523.md).[^cite_note-21] As a result, the budget revenue stream from this sector has increased and the Stabilization Fund has started accumulating significant amounts of foreign exchange, creating a financial airbag for the country. It was a time of an overall [financial liberalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalization).



Putin, Kasyanov and Deputy Prime Minister [Viktor Khristenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Khristenko) at the 7th Russia-EU Summit in Moscow, May 2001

An important achievement of Kasyanov's Cabinet was the successful implementation of the small business encouragement policy. Introduction of simplified [taxation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax), easy state registration of businesses and ban on frequent inspections by state agencies were among those measures.[^cite_note-22]

It was during Mikhail Kasyanov's time as Russian Prime Minister that the transfer of agricultural lands to [private property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property) began. Additionally to that credit support mechanism for agricultural production was introduced and Russia very rapidly again became one of the leading exporters of grain after decades of huge annual import of [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat).[^cite_note-23]

Kasyanov was dismissed, along with the entire Russian cabinet, by President Vladimir Putin on 24 February 2004 after more than three years in office, two weeks before the [2004 Russian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Russian_presidential_election) on 14 March. There were disagreements between Kasyanov and Putin on matters of policy. The President stated on national television "This decision bears no relation to any assessment of the performance of the former composition of the government. It was dictated by my desire to once again delineate my position on the issue of what development course the country will take after March 14, 2004."[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Political activity after resignation

### After leaving the government (2004–2008)

On the first anniversary of his dismissal (24 February 2005) Kasyanov gave a press conference at which he said that he might run for president in the 2008 elections. His bid was supported by [Leonid Nevzlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Nevzlin),[^cite_note-26] [Boris Berezovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Berezovsky_\(businessman\))[^cite_note-27] and [Garry Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov).

In April 2006, Mikhail Kasyanov was elected Chairman of the People's Democratic Union (PDU), a newly launched NGO. PDU was one of the co-founders of the first "The Other Russia" conference[^cite_note-28] in July 2006 and of "The Other Russia" coalition that was set up at the conference. Kasyanov and the PDU were actively involved in the coalition's work and took part in the [Dissenters' Marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissenters_March)[^cite_note-29] in Moscow and St. Petersburg – the first protest manifestations in many years. On 3 March 2007, Kasparov and Kasyanov spoke against Putin's government to thousands of supporters at the [Saint Petersburg Dissenters' March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Dissenters'_March).[^cite_note-30]

At a presentation in [International Institute for Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies) 26 June 2006, Kasyanov criticized Putin's administration. He said: "Separation of powers has been effectively demolished and replaced by the so-called ‘[Vertical of Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_of_Power)' which is based on the false idea that all the meaningful social and political processes must be kept under control by the state. The government and parliament cannot function any longer without daily instructions. The judiciary is increasingly servile. Independent TV does not exist any more at the federal level and is being quickly uprooted in the regions. Moreover, the state-owned companies and the state itself increase their grip over the electronic and printed media. Responsibility of the regional level of power is totally destroyed by the abolishment of direct elections for the governors."[^cite_note-31]

PDU had regional branches in 75 regions of Russia.[^cite_note-32] In June 2007 Kasyanov was nominated by the PDU as a candidate for the presidential elections. In September 2007 the new political party [People for Democracy and Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_for_Democracy_and_Justice) ([Народ за демократию и справедливость](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9D%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B4_%D0%B7%D0%B0_%D0%B4%D0%B5%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%BA%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B8%D1%8E_%D0%B8_%D1%81%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BB%D0%B8%D0%B2%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D1%8C?action=edit\&redlink=1)) was established on the basis of the PDU, and Kasyanov was elected its chairman.

#### Fraud accusations and court case (2005–2007)

Allegations that Kasyanov took a two percent commission in exchange for ignoring bribes and illegal business ventures whilst he was working at the [Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(Russia\)) between 1993 and 1999 were made in the state-controlled [Russian media](15861222.md) which branded him as *"Misha 2 percent"*.[^cite_note-spiegel150307-33][^cite_note-putinsrussia-34] In an article by Peter J. Stavrakis entitled "Russia's evolution as a predatory state" (part of a compilation entitled *"Russia's uncertain economic future"*, written for the [United States Congressional Joint Economic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congressional_Joint_Economic_Committee)), the allegations were unsurprisingly described as credible.[^cite_note-35]

In July 2005, State Duma deputy from the [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia) faction [Alexander Khinshtein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Khinshtein) made accusations against Mikhail Kasyanov; according to Khinshtein, Kasyanov, when he was prime minister, privatized *Sosnovka-1*, the former state [dacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacha) of [Mikhail Suslov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Suslov), a former member of the [Politburo of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politburo_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union), in 2003 at a reduced price.[^cite_note-36]

The [Prosecutor General's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecutor_General's_Office?action=edit\&redlink=1) used the published materials as a reason to initiate a criminal case on 1 July 2005 against the general director of FSUE VPK Invest Ramil Gaisin under Article 165 Part 3 of the Criminal Code of the Russian Federation ("causing property damage on an especially large scale by deception or abuse of trust").[^cite_note-37] The publication of the materials and the message from the Prosecutor General's Office about the initiation of a criminal case coincided with Kasyanov's stay on vacation abroad:[^cite_note-38]

> I returned to Moscow despite the threats made against me. I have no doubt that the systematically launched slanderous campaign to discredit me, based on lies and distortion of facts, is part of the general strategy of the authorities to completely cleanse the political field. The results of the implementation of such a strategy are obvious - growing social alienation and uncertainty among citizens about their future, a slowdown in economic growth against the backdrop of record export prices, and a steady decline in Russia's international authority. Creating conditions for a real, non-sham political process involving public discussion of different points of view about the situation in the country and freedom to choose between them is a task that I will continue to work on.

In July 2005, the Russian Office of Public Prosecutor started investigating the privatization of two houses formerly owned by the government. According to the court verdict in March 2007, Kasyanov was to return a house and pay 108,135,000 rubles in damages to the government for using the property illegally (approx. USD 4,150,000 or €3,130,000). In 2007, Kasyanov was still planning to appeal.[^cite_note-39]

Khinshtein's assumptions were not confirmed. The *Sosnovka-1* facility was acquired by Kasyanov legally after he left government service. In 1996, the Evikhon company leased the facility for 49 years from the [State Property Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Agency_for_State_Property_Management), then *Sosnovka-1* was transferred to the management of the VPK-Invest company, which in 2003 received the right to sell dachas. First, *Sosnovka-1* was acquired by the Amelia company, and in 2004, it was resold to Mikhail Kasyanov. In 2006, the Moscow Arbitration Court recognized Kasyanov as a bona fide purchaser and refused to return the property to the state. However, the [Federal Agency for State Property Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Agency_for_State_Property_Management) filed new claims, and in 2007, by a court decision, the dacha was returned to state ownership.[^cite_note-40]

A *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)* article from 2007 notes that Kasyanov insists that his only earnings as a [public servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service) were his government salary, and he has only been involved in private business ventures for "one year" since leaving the post of prime minister. The same article also supported Khinshtein's claim about *Sosnovka-1*.[^cite_note-spiegel150307-33]

### 2008 presidential candidacy

Main article: [Mikhail Kasyanov 2008 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Kasyanov_2008_presidential_campaign)



Mikhail Kasyanov at a rally of the [Solidarnost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarnost) movement on 21 February 2009

In 2006, Kasyanov declared that he desired to establish a new political party and run for president [in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Russian_presidential_election).[^cite_note-fas-41]

Kasyanov left the opposition group [The Other Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Other_Russia_\(coalition\)) in July 2007 due to the group's failure to agree on a single presidential candidate.[^cite_note-leaveOthRus-42]

Kasyanov established the Russian Popular Democratic Union party. However, the Federal Registration Service blocked the party from participating in the [2007 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Russian_legislative_election).[^cite_note-fas-41]

It was not known whether Kasyanov would continue his candidacy after [Garry Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov) entered the race, but on 8 December 2007 he reaffirmed he would run in the election.[^cite_note-43] That day Russian Popular Democratic Union Party nominated Kasyanov as their presidential candidate.[^cite_note-44]

On 16 January 2008, he announced that he had finished collecting the 2 million signatures necessary to run as a candidate.[^cite_note-45] Later that month, however, the Central Election Commission rejected his candidacy on the grounds that 13.36% of the signatures were invalid,[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] and he was disqualified.[^cite_note-48] Kasyanov appealed the decision to the Supreme Court,[^cite_note-49] which rejected the appeal on 6 February 2008.[^cite_note-50]



Kasyanov during the [2011–2013 Russian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%932013_Russian_protests)



Kasyanov takes part in a march in memory of [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Boris_Nemtsov) opposition politician [Boris Nemtsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Nemtsov) in Moscow, 1 March 2015

Kasyanov claimed that the decision to prevent his candidacy was taken by Putin himself, who he claimed was afraid that, in a fair election, [Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) might lose.[^cite_note-51]

Kasyanov described the elections as a farce and called for a boycott.[^cite_note-52]

### Author: "Without Putin" (2009)

The presentation of Kasyanov's book *Without Putin* in the [Ukrainian](31750.md) capital [Kyiv](585629.md) on 18 December 2009 was cancelled merely minutes before its planned opening.[^cite_note-Kyiv-53] Kasyanov's press secretary claimed that the electricity went out in the Premier Palace Hotel on [Taras Shevchenko Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_Boulevard?action=edit\&redlink=1) minutes before the presentation was to start and that some 20 young men had blocked the entrance to the hotel to keep attendees from entering.[^cite_note-Kyiv-53]

### Kadyrov's threats against Kasyanov (2016)

In February 2016, Kasyanov's image was posted online by Chechen leader [Ramzan Kadyrov](647880.md) on his social media account, with Kasyanov being in cross hairs of a sniper rifle. This was seen as a threat against Kasyanov.[^cite_note-54] On 10 February, Kasyanov was attacked in a Moscow restaurant by a dozen men who yelled death threats at him.[^cite_note-55]

### Defection over Ukraine (2022)

He opposed the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-thehill-2023-11-25-56] In May 2022, Kasyanov participated in the 8th "Russia Forum" in [Vilnius](32597.md), together with [Khodorkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Khodorkovsky), the Russian oil oligarch and former head of Russian oil and gas giant [Yukos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukos), the head of the US-think tank [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House), the head of the US-government funded broadcaster [Radio Free Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe/Radio_Liberty) and others. The aim of the "anti-Putin summit" was to develop a strategy on how to "de-Putinise" Russia and "slay the Russian bear", meaning [Vladimir Putin](32817.md).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

In June 2022, Kasyanov told [Agence France-Presse](471981.md) that he had temporarily left Russia, without providing additional details[^cite_note-59] and as of June 2023, he is in the [Latvian](17514.md) capital of [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga).

In November 2023, Russia's Justice Ministry said he was added to the list of "[foreign agents](39572380.md)".[^cite_note-thehill-2023-11-25-56] In November 2025, he was included in the Russian government's federal database of “terrorists and extremists”.[^cite_note-60]
