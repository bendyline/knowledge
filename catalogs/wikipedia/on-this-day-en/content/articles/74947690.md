The **2026 Jersey general election** was held on 7 June 2026 to elect nine senators, 28 deputies and 12 connétables to the [States Assembly](2860948.md).[^cite_note-date-2]

A total of 92 candidates stood for election, including 17 candidates for the nine senatorial seats.[^cite_note-3] Six of the 12 connétable seats [were uncontested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Results_of_the_2026_Jersey_general_election).&#x20;

Following the election, incumbent [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Jersey) [Lyndon Farnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Farnham) was the only States member-elect nominated for the office, meaning that he was selected as Chief Minister-designate when the new [States Assembly](2860948.md) met on 19 June 2026.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

## Electoral system

See also: [Elections in Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Jersey)

The 2026 election was held after the reintroduction of nine island-wide [senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senator_\(Jersey\)), a role that had been abolished before the [2022 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Jersey_general_election). The number of [deputies](2860948.md#Deputies) was reduced from 37 to 28, with one seat removed from each of the nine deputy constituencies. The election of one [connétable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conn%C3%A9table_\(Jersey_and_Guernsey\)) from each of the twelve parishes was maintained.[^cite_note-7]

The election was also the first Jersey general election in modern times to be held on a Sunday rather than a Wednesday, following a decision by the [States Assembly](2860948.md).[^cite_note-8]

Prior to polling day, [automatic voter registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_voter_registration) was introduced to Jersey's system of voting for the first time. Eligible islanders would now be included on the electoral register without needing to manually sign up.[^cite_note-9]

## Campaign conduct

The [Jersey Electoral Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_Electoral_Authority) intervened several times during the campaign. In May 2026, after a complaint that an [incumbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incumbent) [Connétable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conn%C3%A9table_\(Jersey_and_Guernsey\)) had used an official mailing list and website to promote his candidacy, the Authority reminded elected members that [parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parishes_of_Jersey) resources should not be used for electoral purposes.[^cite_note-10] It also issued a statement correcting suggestions that it had approved material published by [Value Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_Jersey).[^cite_note-11] In June 2026, the Authority criticised election-related misinformation on social media, describing it as a 'poison' that harmed the democratic process.[^cite_note-12] One States Member criticised the Authority, saying that the required compliance forms had not been ready in time.[^cite_note-13]

The [Commonwealth Parliamentary Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Parliamentary_Association) sent a delegation of Commonwealth parliamentarians to [observe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_monitoring) the election process.[^cite_note-14] After the election the International Election Observation Mission and the [Jersey Electoral Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_Electoral_Authority) both raised concerns about whether election law provided a level playing field between registered [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties_in_Jersey) and non-party campaign groups.[^cite_note-15]

## Turnout

Turnout in 2026 was reported at 32.4%, down from 41.6% at the previous election in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Jersey_general_election). However, the number of people who cast a vote increased from 25,264 in 2022 to 27,691 in 2026. The lower turnout percentage was attributed largely to the introduction of [automatic voter registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_voter_registration), which increased the number of people on the electoral roll from 60,701 to 85,470.[^cite_note-16]

[Voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) in Jersey has historically been low by international standards. Statistics Jersey’s 2025 Better Life Index ranked Jersey last for civic engagement among all OECD member and partner countries following the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Jersey_general_election)[^cite_note-17]

## Political parties and groups

Main article: [Political parties in Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties_in_Jersey)

Two registered political parties participated in the election: Reform Jersey and People First. In addition, Value Jersey, a political movement rather than a registered party, promoted a policy platform that was endorsed by some independent candidates.

### Reform Jersey

Main article: [Reform Jersey](43334542.md)

[Reform Jersey](43334542.md) fielded 16 candidates in the 2026 election, including nine sitting members seeking re-election.[^cite_note-18] Seven Reform Jersey candidates were elected. Party leader [Sam Mézec](44767729.md), who stood for one of the nine Senatorial seats, was not elected.&#x20;

Their manifesto was entitled *Turning the Tide*.[^cite_note-19] It included costed key pledges on housing support for young [Jersey people](52976248.md) returning from university in the UK, developing new family-sized homes and [affordable housing](3531662.md), reducing the use of overseas [consultants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consultant), improving bus and cycle infrastructure, renovating [Fort Regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Regent), introducing tax allowances for pensioners, reducing Jersey's Marginal Rate on tax and removing the upper earnings cap on Social Security contributions.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

### People First Party

A new left-wing political party called [People First Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_First_Party_\(Jersey\)) fielded two candidates, neither of whom were elected.[^cite_note-22]

### Value Jersey

Main article: [Value Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_Jersey)

Nine independent candidates publicly associated themselves with the political movement [Value Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_Jersey), six of whom were elected.[^cite_note-23] Its 'blueprint' argued that Jersey’s affordability and prosperity problems stem from a stalled economy, weakened productivity and rising costs for households, businesses and government. It called for disciplined government, stronger growth and greater competition as practical steps to reduce costs, restore confidence and avoid creating larger problems for the future.[^cite_note-24]

Ahead of the 2026 vote, the [States Assembly](2860948.md) introduced a voluntary code of practice, requiring candidates to declare if they are a member of a non-party political group. Due to its status as a self-declared political movement, Value Jersey asserted these regulations would not apply, including requirements to declare where funding comes from.[^cite_note-25] Some politicians in Jersey have called for Value Jersey to be more transparent with its funding and exactly which candidates are aligned with its movement. In response to scrutiny, Value Jersey clarified that the movement had received 58 donations from anonymous supporters, including a donation of £10,000 and a donation from an anonymous UK-based individual with business interests in Jersey.[^cite_note-26]

## Results

### Overview

Independent candidates won 42 of the 49 seats. Six of those independents were publicly associated with support of [Value Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_Jersey), while [Reform Jersey](43334542.md) won seven seats, down from ten at the [2022 Jersey general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Jersey_general_election).[^cite_note-27]

ITV News described the 2026 election as a vote for stability, contrasting it with the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Jersey_general_election), which it characterised as being defined by a desire for change. It noted that five of the nine elected Senators were serving members of the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_\(Jersey\)), including [Ian Gorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Gorst), [Lyndon Farnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Farnham) and Elaine Millar, who finished second, third and fourth respectively in the Senatorial contest. But eight sitting States Members failed to be re-elected, including three ministers.[^cite_note-28] [Reform Jersey](43334542.md) leader [Sam Mézec](44767729.md) was among those defeated, finishing tenth in the island-wide election for Senator, in which the top nine candidates were elected.[^cite_note-29]

Local media noted the increased representation of women in the States Assembly. *Bailiwick Express* reported that women had won 22 of the 49 seats, representing approximately 45% of elected members,[^cite_note-30] while the *Jersey Evening Post* headlined its front page coverage: ‘It shows how far Jersey has come’.[^cite_note-31]

BBC News highlighted that [Gabriel Raimondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Raimondo), elected ten days after his 18th birthday, was the youngest ever States Member.[^cite_note-32]

## Government formation

Main article: [Government of Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Jersey)

### Chief Minister

On 11 June 2026, the [States Assembly](2860948.md) announced that incumbent [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Jersey) [Lyndon Farnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Farnham) was the only Member nominated to stand for the role, having received eight nominations from fellow States Members-elect. As only one Member had put their name forward, no vote was required under the [Standing Orders of the States Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Orders_of_the_States_Assembly?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-44]

Farnham made a statement and answered questions at the States meeting on 19 June, after which he was selected as Chief Minister designate. Other ministers, including Chief Minister are scheduled to formally take up their positions on 29 June 2026.[^cite_note-\:0-4]
