**İznik** (Turkish pronunciation: [\[izˈnik\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Turkish)) is a municipality and [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Turkey) of [Bursa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursa_Province), [Turkey](11125639.md).[^cite_note-2] Its area is 753 km<sup>2</sup>,[^cite_note-3] and its population 44,236 (2022).[^cite_note-tuik-1] The town is at the site of the ancient city of [Nicaea](77588.md), from which the modern name derives. The town lies in a fertile basin at the eastern end of [Lake İznik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_%C4%B0znik), with ranges of hills to the north and south. As the crow flies, the town is only 90 kilometres (56 miles) southeast of [Istanbul](3391396.md) but by road it is 200 km (124 miles) around the [Gulf of İzmit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_%C4%B0zmit). It is 80 km (50 miles) by road from [Bursa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursa).&#x20;

İznik has been a district centre of the province of Bursa since 1930 but belonged to the district of [Kocaeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmit) between 1923 and 1927. It was a township of [Yenişehir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeni%C5%9Fehir,_Bursa) district (connected to [Bilecik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilecik) before 1926) between 1927 and 1930.&#x20;

Ancient Nicaea was ringed with walls that survive to this day, despite having been pierced in places to accommodate roads. Inside the walls stands the [Ayasofya Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia,_%C4%B0znik) where the [Second Council of Nicaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Council_of_Nicaea) was held in A.D. 787. The town is famous for the [Iznik tiles and pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iznik_pottery).&#x20;

## Etymology

İznik derives from the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) name of the city, Νίκαια *Nikaia* (Latinized as [Nicaea](77588.md)), prefixed with εἰς *eis*, meaning 'to' or 'into'. The [Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish) spelling is ازنيق: İznîq.&#x20;

İznik appears as نيقية (Nîkıye) in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) sources, while [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta), who visited the area, recorded it as يزنيك (Yiznîk).[^cite_note-4]&#x20;

## History

For the history before the Ottoman conquest, see [Nicaea](77588.md).

In ancient times, this was the site of [Nicaea](77588.md), a Hellenistic city founded by Antigonus in 316 BC.&#x20;

In 1331, [Orhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orhan) [captured the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Nicaea_\(1328%E2%80%9331\)) from the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and for a short period the town became the capital of the expanding [Ottoman Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Emirate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaby198919–20-5] The large church of [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia,_%C4%B0znik) in the centre of the town was converted into the Orhan Mosque[^cite_note-6] and a *medrese* (theological school-[Süleyman Paşa Medresesi](https://www.turkiyenintarihieserleri.com/?oku=1646)) and hamam (bathhouse) were built nearby.[^cite_note-7] In 1334 Orhan built another mosque and an *[imaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaret)* (soup kitchen) just outside the Yenisehir gate (Yenişeh Kapısı) on the south side of the town.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaby198920-8]&#x20;

The Moroccan traveller [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) stayed in Iznik at the end of 1331 soon after the capture of the town by Orhan.[^cite_note-9] According to Ibn Battuta, the town was in ruins and only inhabited by a small number of people in the service of the sultan. Within the city walls were gardens and cultivated plots with each house surrounded by an orchard. The town produced fruit, walnuts, chestnuts and large sweet grapes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaby198920-8][^cite_note-10]&#x20;

A census in 1520 recorded 379 Muslim and 23 Christian households while another one taken a century later in 1624 recorded 351 Muslim and 10 Christian households. Assuming five members for each household, these figures suggest that the population was around 2,000. Estimates made in the 18th and 19th centuries arrived at similar numbers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaby198920–21-11] The town was poor and the population small even when ceramic production was at its peak during the second half of the 16th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaby198921-12]&#x20;

&#x20;The Byzantine city is estimated to have had a population of 20,000–30,000 but in the Ottoman period the town was never prosperous and occupied only a small fraction of the walled area. It was, however, a centre for the production of highly decorated [fritware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritware) vessels and what are known as [İznik tiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0znik_tiles) during the 16th and 17th centuries.



[Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) remains behind the Archaeological Museum of Iznik

In 1677 the English clergyman [John Covel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Covel) visited Iznik and found only a third of the town occupied.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECovel1893\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsearlyvoyagestrav00dallrichpagen345_281\]-13] In 1745 the English traveller [Richard Pococke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Pococke) reported that Iznik was no more than a village.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPococke1745\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsgri_33125009339611pagen533_123\]-14] A succession of visitors described the town in unflattering terms. For example in 1779, the Italian archaeologist Domenico Sestini wrote that Iznik was nothing but an abandoned town with no life, no noise and no movement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaby198920-8][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESestini1789\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidYfsOAAAAQAAJpgPA219_219–220\]-15] In 1797 [James Dallaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dallaway) described Iznik as "a wretched village of long lanes and mud walls...".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaby198920-8][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDallaway1797\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsconstantinoplean00dallpage169_169\]-16]&#x20;

During the [Greco-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_\(1919%E2%80%931922\)), the [Greek army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_army) occupied Iznik in July 1920 as part of their offensive in western Anatolia.[^cite_note-17] The town's buildings suffered damage, including the [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia,_%C4%B0znik) and the [Green mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Mosque,_%C4%B0znik) landmarks.[^cite_note-18] After a Turkish victory in the [Battle of the Sakarya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Sakarya), the defeated Greek army gradually retreated and Turkish troops entered İznik uncontested on 28 November 1920.[^cite_note-19] In the aftermath of the occupation, an irregular militia led by "Cemal Bey"[^cite_note-20] massacred what remained of Iznik's [Greek population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Genocide).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]&#x20;

## Pottery and tiles



Iznik tiles at courtyard of the [Süleymaniye Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCleymaniye_Mosque) in [Istanbul](3391396.md)

Main article: [Iznik pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iznik_pottery)

Iznik's main period of importance came in the 15th century with the development of a pottery and tile making industry. Iznik [ceramic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic) [tiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tile) ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *İznik Çini*.) were used to decorate many of the mosques designed by [Mimar Sinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimar_Sinan) in [Istanbul](3391396.md). However, the ceramics industry declined in the 17th century[^cite_note-24] and İznik was reduced to a minor agricultural settlement when it was bypassed by the railway in the 19th century.&#x20;

## Main sights

A number of monuments were erected by the early Ottomans in the period between the conquest in 1331 and 1402 when the town was sacked by [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur). Among those that have survived are:&#x20;

- İznik was originally ringed with 5 km (3 mi) of walls that were about 10 m (33 ft) high and enclosed within a double ditch on the landward sides. The walls incorporated over 100 towers. Large gates on the three landward sides of the walls provided the only entrances to the city. The western part of the walls rose up beside the lake which is sufficiently large that it cannot easily be blockaded from the land. Today the walls are ruined but enough still survives to provide a pleasant walking route.

  [^cite_note-25]

- [Yeşil Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye%C5%9Fil_Mosque_of_Iznik) (Green Mosque), built for [Çandarlı Kara Halil Hayreddin Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87andarl%C4%B1_Kara_Halil_Hayreddin_Pasha), the first [Grand Vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Vizier) of the Ottoman Empire between 1378 and 1391. It is located near the Lefke Gate on the east side of the town. Damaged in 1922 during the [Greco-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_\(1919%E2%80%931922\)), it was restored between 1956 and 1969.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaby198920-8]

  [^cite_note-26]



Exterior view of [Nilüfer Hatun Imareti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nil%C3%BCfer_Hatun_Imareti) and mosque



[Lake İznik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_%C4%B0znik), 2019

- [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia,_Iznik), also known as Aya Sofya,

  [^cite_note-27]

  &#x20;([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Ἁγία Σοφία, "'[Holy Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Wisdom)') is a [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine)-era former [church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_building) which was built by [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) in the middle of the city in the 6th century.

  [^cite_note-28]

  &#x20;It was here that the [Second Council of Nicaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Council_of_Nicaea), a gathering of Christian bishops, was held in AD 787. After controversial rebuilding, it is now the Ayasofya Mosque ([Turkish:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) Ayasofya Cami).

  [^cite_note-29]

- [Hacı Özbek Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hac%C4%B1_%C3%96zbek_Mosque) (1333). This mosque was built only three years after the Ottoman conquest. The portico on the west side of the building was demolished in 1940 to widen the road.

  [^cite_note-30]

- [Basilica of Saint Neophytos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Neophytos), the ruins of a historical basilica thought to be the meeting place of the [First Council of Nicaea](11118.md), which has a very important place in [Christian history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Christianity).

- [Nilüfer Hatun Soup Kitchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nil%C3%BCfer_Hatun_Imareti) (*Nilüfer Hatun Imareti*) Built in 1388, the building was abandoned for many years but was restored in 1955 and is now a museum.

  [^cite_note-31]

- Süleyman Pasha Madrasa (mid-14th century). This is one of two surviving [medreses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa) in the town. It was restored in the 19th century and again in 1968.

  [^cite_note-32]

- Mausoleum of Çandarlı Hayreddin Pasha (14th century). The main chamber contains fifteen [sarcophagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus). A lower room contains three more sarcophagi including that of Ottoman-Tunisian statesman [Hayreddin Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayreddin_Pasha). It is located in a cemetery outside the Lefke gate to the east of the town.

  [^cite_note-33]

- [Kilns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiln) Slight traces remain of the kilns used to make the pottery and tiles that once made İznik famous.

Several monuments survived into the 20th century but were destroyed during the [Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_\(1919%E2%80%931922\)). These include:&#x20;

- Church of the Koimesis/Dormition (6th–8th century but rebuilt after the 1065 earthquake). The only church in the town that was not transformed into a mosque,

  [^cite_note-34]

  &#x20;it was decorated with 11th-century Byzantine mosaics which survive only in photographs.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMango1959-35]

  [^cite_note-36]

- Eşrefzâde Rumi Mosque (15th century). Eşrefzâde Rumi was married to the daughter of [Hacı Bayram-ı Veli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hac%C4%B1_Bayram-%C4%B1_Veli). He founded a [sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi) sect and after his death in 1469–70 his tomb became a pilgrimage site.

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaby198920-8]

  &#x20;The mosque has been restored and the tomb is decorated with Iznik tiles.

  [^cite_note-37]

- Seyh Kutbeddin Mosque and Mausoleum (15th century). The mosque and mausoleum have been rebuilt.

## Sport

The [İznik Ultramarathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0znik_Ultramarathon) is a 130 km (81 mi) endurance running event that has taken place around [Lake İznik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_%C4%B0znik) every April since 2012. It is the country's longest single-stage athletics competition.[^cite_note-41]&#x20;
