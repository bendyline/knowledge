[North Korea](21255.md) has tested numerous missiles since 1984.[^cite_note-CSISlist-1] North Korea has tested [short-range ballistic missile](1320069.md) (SRBMs), [intermediate-range ballistic missile](337326.md) (IRBMs), [intercontinental ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) (ICBMs), [maneuverable reentry vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maneuverable_reentry_vehicle) (MaRV) ballistic missiles, and [cruise missiles](6590.md).[^cite_note-CSISlist-1] Some North Korean missile tests have failed (*i.e.*, with an explosion in flight), while others have been successful.[^cite_note-nti._Unde-2] North Korea claimed to have tested a [hypersonic weapon](59574659.md) for the first time in 2021 and several times thereafter, although it is unclear whether it actually did so.[^cite_note-3]

North Korea has fired a number of SRBMs into the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan), in what have been interpreted as political gestures.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

North Korea established its [Tonghae Satellite Launching Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonghae_Satellite_Launching_Ground) in 1984 and began tests of strategic missiles.[^cite_note-nti._TheC-8] Fifteen were carried out under the rule of [Kim Il Sung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung); fourteen of these were carried out at Tonghae.[^cite_note-nti._TheC-8] After his death in 1994, he was succeeded by his son [Kim Jong Il](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong_Il), who paused testing for four years before resuming in 1998 with a test of the [Taepodong-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taepodong-1) missile.[^cite_note-nti._Unde-2] Following an international backlash, Kim Jong Il paused testing again before lifting the moratorium in 2006.[^cite_note-nti._Unde-2] Ultimately, 16 missile tests were undertaken under Kim Jong Il, mostly at the Kittaeryong site near [Wonsan](527626.md).[^cite_note-nti._Unde-2] Since [Kim Jong Un](5962511.md) took power in 2011, North Korea steeply increased the number of missile tests, tallying 129 as of April 2023.[^cite_note-CSISlist-1] Under Kim Jong Un, North Korea conducted tests at many sites around the country, and many appeared to be operational/training tests in preparation for war, rather than developmental tests.[^cite_note-nti._Unde-2] The Tonghae site became dormant in 2009; space launches were shifted to the [Sohae Satellite Launching Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohae_Satellite_Launching_Station) and developmental missile testing shifted to the Wonsan site.[^cite_note-nti._Unde-2] Older missiles in North Korea's arsenal include various [Scud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud_missile) and Nodong missiles, while newer missiles in development include the [Pukguksong-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukguksong-1), [Pukguksong-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukguksong-2), [Hwasong-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwasong-10) (Musudan), and [Unha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unha)-3.[^cite_note-nti._Unde-2]

## Events related to missile tests

### 2016

On February 7, 2016, roughly a month after an [alleged hydrogen bomb test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2016_North_Korean_nuclear_test), North Korea claimed to have put a [satellite](27683.md) into [low Earth orbit](47568.md). [Japanese Prime Minister](24833.md) [Shinzō Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinz%C5%8D_Abe) had warned the North to not launch the rocket, and if it did and the rocket violated [Japanese](15573.md) territory, it would be shot down. North Korea launched the rocket anyway, claiming the satellite was purely intended for peaceful, scientific purposes. Several nations, including the [United States](3434750.md), Japan, and [South Korea](27019.md), have criticized the launch, and despite North Korean claims that the rocket was for peaceful purposes, it has been heavily criticized as an attempt to perform an [ICBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) test under the guise of a peaceful satellite launch. [China](5405.md) also criticized the launch, however urged "the relevant parties" to "refrain from taking actions that may further escalate tensions on the Korean peninsula".[^cite_note-192]

While some North Korean pronouncements have been treated with skepticism and ridicule, analysts treated the unusual pace of North Korean rocket and nuclear testing in early 2016 quite seriously. Admiral [Bill Gortney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gortney), head of the [North American Aerospace Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aerospace_Defense_Command), told Congress in March 2016, "It's the prudent decision on my part to assume that \[Kim Jong Un] has the capability to miniaturize a nuclear weapon and put it on an ICBM," suggesting a major shift from a few years earlier.[^cite_note-193]

North Korea appeared to launch a missile test from a submarine on April 23, 2016; while the missile only traveled 30 km, one U.S. analyst noted that "North Korea's sub launch capability has gone from a joke to something very serious".[^cite_note-194] North Korea conducted multiple missile tests in 2016.[^cite_note-195]

### 2017

In early August 2017, North Korea threatened to attack [Guam](11974.md) using four [Hwasong-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwasong-12) missiles. These missiles were planned to overfly Japan.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197]

On August 29, 2017, United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres has condemned the latest North Korea Ballistic Missile Launch and termed it as violation of relevant UN Security Council resolutions, as according to press reports, early Tuesday morning, the North Korea Ballistic Missile travelled some 2,700 kilometers, flying over Japan before crashing into the Pacific Ocean.[^cite_note-198]

On September 3, 2017, North Korea claimed to have successfully tested a thermonuclear bomb, also known as a hydrogen bomb (see [2017 North Korean nuclear test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_North_Korean_nuclear_test)). Corresponding seismic activity similar to an earthquake of magnitude 6.3 was reported by the [USGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USGS) making the blast around 10 times more powerful than previous detonations by the country.[^cite_note-199] Later the bomb yield was estimated to be 250 kilotons, based on further study of the seismic data.[^cite_note-200] The test was reported to be "a perfect success".[^cite_note-201]

### 2018

Indonesian authorities detained the North Korean's second-largest cargo ship, the Wise Honest, in April for having been photographed loading what appeared to be [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea's_illicit_activities#Coal) in North Korea. The ship's [automatic identification system](1825702.md) signal had been turned off since August 2017, trying to conceal its course. In July 2018 the [U.S. Justice Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Justice_Department) secured a sealed seizure warrant for the ship.[^cite_note-nyt02-202]

### 2019

The U.S. seized the [Wise Honest in Indonesia under its warrant](#2018) in May and put it under tow to [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa). The Justice Department said it was the first time the United States had seized a North Korean cargo vessel for international sanctions violations. The sanctions are intended "ultimately \[to] pressure North Korea to dismantle its nuclear program".[^cite_note-nyt02-202]

Members of the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council) (UNSC), including UK and France, condemned North Korea's recent missile launches. The nations urged Pyongyang to resume negotiations, citing the missile launches as violation of UNSC resolutions.[^cite_note-203]

On October 2, North Korea confirmed testing a new ballistic missile launched from a submarine, and called it a "significant achievement" towards dealing with external threats and boosting its military power.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-BBC_News_2019-205]

In December, [Planet Labs](41200593.md) released new satellite images of a factory unit where North Korea develops military equipment used in launching long-range missiles, indicating the construction of a new arrangement. The revelation has raised fear that North Korea might launch a rocket or missile to seek concessions in stagnant [nuclear negotiations with the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea%E2%80%93United_States_relations)[^cite_note-206]

### 2020

The [Vice Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chairman_of_the_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff), General [John Hyten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hyten), said on January 17 that North Korea is building new missiles, capabilities and weapons "As fast as anybody on the planet." He further stated that North Korea is learning from its mistakes while making advances in its missile programs.[^cite_note-207] However, [Under Secretary of Defense for Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_for_Policy) [John Rood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rood) later told the House Armed Forces Committee on January 28 that North Korea did not go through with conducting a major missile launch which had been scheduled to take place sometime between late December and early January.[^cite_note-208]

### 2021

North Korea conducted eight missile tests in 2021, compared with four missile tests in 2020.[^cite_note-cnn_may_4_2022-146]

### 2022

A ballistic missile was launched by North Korea on January 5, 2022, off its east coast, as per military officials in Seoul and Japan. The exact munition used for the testing remains unclear. However, the officials did cite the munition as a short-range projectile, which was presumed as a ballistic missile launched from an inland region into the Sea of Japan. Officials claimed to be maintaining "readiness posture" and close monitoring of the situation with its defense allies in the US.[^cite_note-209]

North Korea's 11th missile launch of 2022 was its March 24 launch of an ICBM, marking its first successful ICBM launch since 2017.[^cite_note-210]

According to Japanese Minister of Defense [Nobuo Kishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobuo_Kishi), one or more missiles from North Korea's June 5, 2022 launch had a variable trajectory presumably designed to evade missile defenses.[^cite_note-reuters_5_June_2022-150]

On October 4, 2022, North Korea launched a missile that flew over and past Japan, prompting Japanese prime minister [Fumio Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida) to release an announcement warning citizens to take shelter and other precautionary measures.[^cite_note-211] The missile, likely another ICBM, is said to have landed in the Sea of Japan without incident.[^cite_note-aljazeera.com-156]

In early October 2022, the U.S. called an emergency UN Security Council meeting, at which it accused Russia and China of protecting the North from stronger sanctions. It also extended its naval drills alongside Japan and South Korea, redeploying the [USS Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Ronald_Reagan) aircraft carrier.[^cite_note-212]

The same day as the October 14 missile test saw the firing of hundreds of North Korean artillery shells into maritime buffer zones, North Korea's third violation of the buffer zones established by the [Pyongyang Joint Declaration of September 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyongyang_Joint_Declaration_of_September_2018).

On 2 November 2022, North Korea was reported to have launched 23 missiles of various types. At the same time, more than 100 artillery rounds were fired and again violated the buffer zones established by the 2018 agreement. In response to the missile launches, air raid sirens were activated on Ulleung island, and three [AGM-84H/K SLAM-ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-84H/K_SLAM-ER) missiles were fired by South Korean warplanes.[^cite_note-Smith-163][^cite_note-213]

On 3 November 2022, North Korea reportedly fired at least one ballistic missile off its east coast, including one, believed to be a long-range missile, that flew over and past Japan. The launch triggered the Japanese emergency broadcast system, which alerted residents in the prefectures of Miyagi, Yamagata, and Niigata to stay indoors.[^cite_note-\:1-164]

On 19 November 2023, [ROK Defense Minister](5985967.md) [Shin Won-sik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_Won-sik) informed the [Korean Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Broadcasting_System) that an anticipated third satellite launch by North Korea was expected by the end of that month.[^cite_note-Mahadzir-11-20-2023-214] On the evening of 21 November 2023, North Korea’s state-run [Korean Central News Agency](632399.md) (KCNA) that a [Chŏllima 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%8Fllima_1) rocket carrying a [Malligyong-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malligyong-1) reconnaissance satellite was launched from the [Sohae Satellite Launching Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohae_Satellite_Launching_Station), with [North Korean leader](9745167.md) [Kim Jong Un](5962511.md) present.[^cite_note-Mahadzir-11-22-2023-215] On 23 November 2023, North Korea terminated the [bi-lateral 2018 Comprehensive Military Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2018_inter-Korean_summit#19_September_\(Day_2\)) in response to South Korea’s decision to suspend a part of that agreement in reaction to North Korea’s latest satellite launch.[^cite_note-Mahadzir-11-22-2023-215][^cite_note-Mahadzir-11-23-2023-216]

### 2024

In October 2024 North Korean leader Kim Jong Un visited missile bases to inspect their readiness for "strategic deterrence" actions. During his visit, Kim emphasized that U.S. nuclear capabilities pose a growing threat to North Korea. In response to perceived external threats, North Korea has intensified the development of its ballistic missile program and nuclear arsenal. These actions have drawn widespread international condemnation and led to the imposition of additional sanctions aimed at curbing the country's military advancements.[^cite_note-217]

In January 2025, response to joint U.S.-South Korean military drills, North Korea carried out a cruise missile test, emphasizing its commitment to strong counteractions. At the same time, President Trump signalled his intention to reopen diplomatic discussions with Kim Jong Un.[^cite_note-218]

### 2025

On November 7, 2025, reports from both South Korea and Japan identified North Korea for launching a ballistic missile in the direction of [Japanese](15573.md) territory. [^cite_note-219]

### 2026

On January 4, 2026, at around 7\:50 AM, South Korea reported that several ballistic missiles were launched towards the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan).[^cite_note-220]
