The **Emirate of Sharjah** ([/ˈʃɑːrdʒə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): إِمَارَة ٱلشَّارِقَة *_Imārat Aš-Šāriqah_*) is one of the [emirates of the United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_of_the_United_Arab_Emirates). It covers 2,900 square kilometres (1,100 sq mi) and has a population of over 1,800,000 (2022).[^cite_note-abu-3] It comprises the capital city of [Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah), after which it is named, and other minor towns and [exclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) such as [Kalba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalba), [Al Dhaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Dhaid), [Dibba Al-Hisn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibba_Al-Hisn) and [Khor Fakkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khor_Fakkan).

The emirate is an [absolute monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy).[^cite_note-4] It has been ruled by [Sultan bin Muhammad Al-Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_bin_Muhammad_Al-Qasimi) since 1972, except for a seven-day period during [an attempted coup d'etat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Sharjawi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) by his brother, Sheikh Abd al-Aziz bin Muhammad Al Qasimi.[^cite_note-5]

## History



Older residential area of Sharjah, displaying the local architecture

Human settlement in the area covered by the emirate has existed for over 120,000 years, with significant finds made of early axes and stone tools as well as Copper and [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age_in_the_United_Arab_Emirates) implements in [Al Dhaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaid), [Al Thuqeibah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Thuqeibah), [Mileiha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mleiha), [Tell Abraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_Abraq), [Muwailah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muweilah), [Al Madam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Madam) and [Jebel Faya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Faya).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Archaeological finds in the Mleiha area point to human habitation consistent with the spread of humanity from Africa to the wider world, evidenced by finds displayed at the [Mleiha Archaeological Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mleiha_Archaeological_Centre).[^cite_note-8]

Historically the emirate was one of the wealthiest towns in the region.[^cite_note-9]

Around 1727, the [Al Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Qasimi) clan took control of Sharjah and declared the polity independent.[^cite_note-10]

The first in a long series of maritime skirmishes between the [Al Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Qasimi) and British vessels took place in 1797, when the British-flagged [snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_\(ship\)) the *Bassein* was seized and released two days later. The cruiser *Viper&#x20;*&#x77;as subsequently attacked off [Bushire](205669.md). The Al Qasimi leader, [Saqr bin Rashid Al Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqr_bin_Rashid_Al_Qasimi), protested innocence in both cases.

A period of great instability followed along the coast, with a number of actions between British and [Al Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Qasimi) vessels alongside various changes of leadership and allegiances between the Rulers of [Ras Al Khaimah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_al-Khaimah), [Ajman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajman) and Sharjah with [Sheikh Sultan bin Saqr Al Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Bin_Saqr_Al_Qasimi) claiming sovereignty over 'all the Joasmee ports' in 1823, a claim recognised by the British at the time.

On 8 January 1820, [Sheikh Sultan bin Saqr Al Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Bin_Saqr_Al_Qasimi) signed [the General Maritime Treaty with Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_General_Maritime_Treaty_with_Britain), accepting protectorate status in order to resist [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks) domination. Following the expiration of a further, ten year, treaty in 1843, on 4 May 1853 Sharjah, along with other Sheikhdoms on what was then known as the 'Arabian Coast' signed the Perpetual Treaty of Peace, which gave rise to the collective name [Trucial States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trucial_States) being given to the emirates of the coast.[^cite_note-11]

Like four of its neighbours, [Ajman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajman), [Dubai](211583.md), [Ras Al Khaimah](61770159.md), and [Umm Al Quwain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_Al_Quwain), its position along trade routes to [India](14533.md) made it important enough to be recognised as a [salute state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salute_state).[^cite_note-12]

Throughout the 19th and early 20th centuries, Sharjah was an important pearl fishing port. A British marine survey of 1830 found 'three to four hundred boats' fishing in the season, earning the ruler 100,000 [Maria Theresa Dollars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa_thaler).[^cite_note-13]

In 1932, [Imperial Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Airways) established a regular air service through Sharjah, which was an overnight stop on the Eastern British Empire route. The [Mahatta Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatta_Fort) was built to house the airline's guests.

On 2 December 1971, [Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh) [Khalid bin Mohammed Al Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_bin_Mohammed_Al_Qasimi) joined Sharjah to the United Arab Emirates.[^cite_note-nyt-14] On 24 January 1972 however, the former ruler Sheikh [Saqr bin Sultan Al Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqr_bin_Sultan_Al_Qasimi) staged a leftist coup. Having previously deposed Saqr in 1965, Sheikh Khalid had ordered the demolition of [Sharjah Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_Fort_\(Al_Hisn_Sharjah\)) (Al Hisn Sharjah) to expunge Saqr's memory. Saqr took over Khalid's palace, holding him inside and in the ensuing confusion Sheikh Khalid was killed. Saqr was arrested and Khalid's brother, [Sultan bin Muhammad Al-Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_bin_Muhammad_Al-Qasimi), an author and historian, came to power.[^cite_note-15]

In 1987, Sultan's brother Abdulaziz [staged a coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Sharjawi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) while Sultan was abroad. Huge state debt was stated as the reason. Then-[UAE President](9480572.md) [Zayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayed_bin_Sultan_Al_Nahyan) denounced the coup, and a deal was reached for Sultan to be restored, while Abdulaziz would become the deputy ruler. Sultan, however, dismissed Abdulaziz quite quickly.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] In 1999, the Crown Prince (Sultan's eldest son) died of a drug overdose while on vacation in their palace in England.[^cite_note-18] Sultan made the decision to testify in front of a UK court. The new Crown Prince, Sheikh Sultan bin Mohammed bin Sultan Al Qasimi, was appointed from a remote branch of the family.

## Geography



The city of [Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah), the largest population centre in the emirate



[Khor Fakkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khor_Fakkan) beach, with the [Western Hajar Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hajar_Mountains) in the background

Sharjah is the third largest emirate in the United Arab Emirates, and is the only one to have land on both the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md). The emirate covers 2,590 square kilometres (1,000 sq mi) which is equivalent to 3.3 per cent of the UAE's total area, excluding the islands. It has a population of over 1,800,000 (2022).[^cite_note-abu-3] The city lies 170 km (110 miles) away from the UAE capital city [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md).

The emirate of Sharjah comprises the city of [Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_\(city\)) (the seat of the emirate), and other minor towns and [enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclave). The [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) of Sharjah, which overlooks the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), has a population of 1,600,000 (2022 census). Sharjah City borders Dubai to the south and Ajman to the north, and the three form a [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai-Sharjah-Ajman_metropolitan_area).[^cite_note-GulfNews2012-20] Sharjah also owns three enclaves on the east coast, bordering the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md). These are [Kalba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalba), [Dibba Al-Hisn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibba_Al-Hisn), and [Khor Fakkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khor_Fakkan), which provide Sharjah with a major east coast port. In the Persian Gulf, the island of [Sir Abu Nu'ayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Abu_Nu'ayr) belongs to Sharjah,[^cite_note-abu-3] and [Abu Musa](931858.md) is claimed by UAE, but controlled by [Iran](14653.md). Sharjah has an [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) containing the village of [Nahwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahwa), located inside the [Omani](22316.md) [enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclave) of [Madha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madha) which borders [Fujairah](58033142.md) and Sharjah. The emirate also encompasses some important oasis areas, the most famous of which is the fertile Dhaid region, where a range of vegetables and fruits are cultivated.[^cite_note-21]

## Government

See also: [Government of Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sharjah)

The Emirate of Sharjah is led by the ruler of Sharjah. It has been led by Sheikh [Sultan bin Muhammad Al-Qasimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_bin_Muhammad_Al-Qasimi) since 1972.[^cite_note-22] The ruler of Sharjah appoints members of the Sharjah Executive Council and the Sharjah Consultative Council to manage the day-to-day affairs of the emirate.[^cite_note-23] Sharjah has devolved some authority over local decisions to Emiri Diwans (Ruler's Courts) in areas such as [Kalba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalba) and [Khor Fakkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khor_Fakkan). Nevertheless, the Sharjah Executive Council retains authority to manage government departments and affairs in line with policies and decrees by the ruler of Sharjah.[^cite_note-24] Collectively, the government of Sharjah has over 126 government departments and public organisations under its responsibility and control[^cite_note-25] with a number of services provided through a Sharjah [e-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-government) portal.[^cite_note-26]

### Commerce and Tourism Development Authority



The Emirates Eye, the [ferris wheel](175541.md) at Al-Qasbah in Sharjah City

The Sharjah Commerce and Tourism Development Authority (SCDTA) was established by decree in 1996 to "promote commercial and tourism activities" in the emirate. The state-backed authority is responsible for, among other things, creating policies for local and international exhibitions and marketing Sharjah to attract foreign investment and tourists. [^cite_note-27]

### Investment and Development Authority

The [Sharjah Investment and Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_Investment_and_Development_Authority) (Shurooq) was established in 2009 as an independent government body to "oversee the social, cultural, environmental and economic development of Sharjah in line with its [Islamic](6037917.md) identity." It focuses on three areas for the emirate; investment, development, and asset and property management. Shurooq's developments include:

- [Al Qasba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Qasba_Sharjah) – 10,000-acre project including a 1 km (0.62 miles)-long man-made [canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) and 60 m (200 ft) high observation wheel, it was made open for the public in 2005. It includes Masrah Al Qasba, a theatre, the Maraya Art Centre, the Multaqa Al Qasba meetings and conference centre and a children's Fun Zone. The project, managed by the Al Qasba Development Authority (an affiliate of Shurooq) has an architectural design including pedestrian bridges, boats, and lagoons. It attracted two million visitors in 2011.

- Al Majaz Waterfront – Shurooq opened the Al Majaz Waterfront in December 2011 after redeveloping it. The new park, located between Jamal Abdul Nasser Street and Khalid Lagoon Corniche, covers an area of 3 square kilometres (1.2 sq mi) for recreation, including six new buildings with restaurants and a 100 m (330 ft) tall water fountain.

- [Heart of Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_of_Sharjah) – The Heart of Sharjah, slated for completion in January 2015, has been proposed by Shurooq as a tourist and trade destination with commercial, cultural, and residential projects as well as hotel, archaeological sites, museums, and commercial space.

- The Chedi Khorfakkan Resort – The Chedi Khorfakkan, scheduled to open in 2015, is under construction an hour outside of Sharjah city centre. Shurooq has stated that once complete, the project will include a tourist resort and a fort on the Al Suwifa Mountain.

### Decency laws

Conservative Sharjah is the only Emirate in the UAE in which the sale of [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(drug\)) is prohibited, although its consumption in one's own home is permissible if one is in possession of a valid Alcohol Licence (as is the transportation of alcohol between the place of sale and the home). The only place this prohibition is relaxed is the members-only sporting club, the [Sharjah Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_Wanderers).

Sharjah also maintains the strictest decency laws in the UAE, introduced in 2001, with a conservative dress code required for both men and women. Mixing between unmarried men and women is illegal: "A man and a woman who are not in a legally acceptable relationship should not be alone in public places, or in suspicious times or circumstances," according to a booklet published by the municipality in 2001.[^cite_note-six-28]

The 2022 census for the Emirate of Sharjah noted that the population of the emirate had reached 1.8 million, representing a 22% increase from the prior census in 2015 which had tallied a population of 1.4 million for the emirate.[^cite_note-\:0-30] Emirati citizens represented 11.5% of the total population, with 208,000 citizens in the 2022 census - almost evenly divided between males and females. However, when considering non-nationals, there were 1.1 million men living the emirate compared to 500,000 women. in 2022.

Over 51% of the population were in the 20-39 age group, and [Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah) was the largest settlement with 1.6 million residents, followed by [Khor Fakkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khor_Fakkan) with 53,000 and [Kalba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalba) with 51,000.[^cite_note-\:0-30]

## Free trade zones

Sharjah has two main free trade zones: the seaport and free trade zone of Hamriyah and the Sharjah Airport International Free Trade Zone (SAIF Zone).

The [Hamriyah Free Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamriyah_Free_Zone) Authority was established by an Emiri decree issued on 12 November 1995 and consists of a port and associated logistics and trade facilities.[^cite_note-31]

Also established in 1995, the SAIF Zone is adjacent to Sharjah International Airport and is currently home to some 2,500 companies.

The Sharjah Research, Technology & Innovation Park (SRTIP) is another free trade zone in Sharjah emirate. The Sharjah Research, Technology & Innovation Park aims to develop and manage an innovation ecosystem that promotes Research and Development.[^cite_note-32]

## Culture



[Dibba Al-Hisn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibba_Al-Hisn) beach in the [gulf of Oman](12896.md).

Sharjah has long invested considerable resources in building a strong cultural identity and heritage and was named in 1998 as [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s '[Arab Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Capital_of_Culture)', an event commemorated by a large pillar erected opposite the Sharjah Desert Park and Arabian Wildlife Centre between Sharjah and Dhaid cities. This monument was moved to a new location at the entrance to Sharjah's University City, where it was joined by a monument to Sharjah's 2014 nomination as Islamic Culture Capital[^cite_note-35] by the Islamic Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization ([ISESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Educational,_Scientific_and_Cultural_Organization)), a specialised institution of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) in the fields of Education, Science and Culture.[^cite_note-36]

### Sharjah Art Foundation

The [Sharjah Art Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_Art_Foundation) works on a broad range of contemporary art and cultural programs to the communities of Sharjah, the UAE and the region, including the [Sharjah Biennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_Biennale) which first took place in 1993. Founded in 2009, SAF also organises the annual March Meeting, residencies, production grants, commissions, exhibitions, research, publications and a growing art collection as well as a number of education and public programmes.[^cite_note-37]

## Stamps





Stamps of Sharjah

Main article: [Postage stamps and postal history of Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamps_and_postal_history_of_Sharjah)

In 1963, Britain ceded responsibility for the Trucial States' postal systems. An American philatelic entrepreneur by the name of [Finbar Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finbar_Kenny) saw the opportunity to create a number of editions of stamps aimed at the lucrative collector's market and in 1964 concluded a deal with a number of Trucial States to take the franchise for the production of stamps for their respective governments. Kenny had made something of a speciality out of signing these deals, also signing with the Rulers of Ajman and Fujairah in 1964—and getting involved in a bribery case in the USA over his dealings with the government of the Cook Islands. The issue of stamps from [Ajman's dependency of Manama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manama,_Ajman)—a tiny agrarian village in the remote plains at which a 'post office' was opened—is a perfect example.

These stamps, luridly illustrated and irrelevant to the actual emirates they purported to come from (editions included 'Space Research' and 'Tokyo Olympic Games') became known as 'dunes'. Their proliferation quickly devalued them.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

Sharjah is therefore known by many [stamp collectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamp_collecting) for these issues by the [Sharjah Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamps_and_postal_history_of_Sharjah) shortly before the formation of the United Arab Emirates.[^cite_note-42] Many of these items are not listed by many popular catalogues.[^cite_note-43]

## Transportation

### Aviation



[Sharjah International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_International_Airport)

International airports in the city of Sharjah include the [Sharjah International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah_International_Airport) and [Port Khalid Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Khalid_Sharjah?action=edit\&redlink=1)

Sharjah International Airport ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association_airport_code): SHJ) has connections to all major international locations. It is 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) from Sharjah City Centre and about 15 kilometres (9.3 mi) away from Dubai. It is a major cargo airport and the main base of [Air Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Arabia). The airport served total 4,324,313 passengers and 51,314 flights in 2007. It also handled 570,363 tonnes (561,355 long tons; 628,718 short tons) of cargo in the same year. Over 60,000 aircraft movements were recorded in 2009.[^cite_note-eight-45] It handled 6,634,570 passengers in 2011.[^cite_note-nine-46]

As of 2012, it has 34 international carriers and 13 cargo airlines. In 2008, Sharjah International Airport announced it would invest AED662m (US\$180m) to renovate the airport's buildings, build a new terminal and increase car parking. The project is slated for completion in 2015, by which time the airport aims to be handling eight million passengers annually.

Sharjah International Airport is the main base of [Air Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Arabia), the Middle East's largest low-cost carrier. It was the Middle East's first budget airline when launched by the ruler of Sharjah in 2003. The number of passengers flying to its near 70 destinations grew six per cent in 2011 to 4.7 million. Net profit for 2011 was AED274m (US\$74.6m), down 13 per cent on the previous year. The airline, which also has hubs in [Egypt](8087628.md) ([Borg El Arab Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borg_El_Arab_Airport), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria)) and [Morocco](19291.md) ([Mohammed V International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_V_International_Airport), [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca)), delayed plans to establish a fourth hub in [Jordan](7515964.md) in 2012.

### Road

The Sharjah Public Transport Corporation (SPTC) started the public transport system in Sharjah on 23 May 2008 with 11 buses running on the first route, Route 14 from Sharjah International Airport to Al-Sharq terminal. By November 2008, 142 buses are expected to operate on 18 routes.[^cite_note-ten-47] In February 2010, there were 115 Sharjah intercity buses, which make 250 trips daily. The tariff for these intercity buses ranges from AED 5 to AED 30.[^cite_note-eleven-48] In 2014, the [Sharjah Roads and Transport Authority (SRTA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_and_Transport_Authority_\(Sharjah\)) and the SPTC got absorbed onto the authority.[^cite_note-49]

Metered Taxis are available in Sharjah for the intra-city as well as the inter-city travel. The base fare is AED 11.50 with AED 1 charged for every 650 metres (2,130 ft) of travel.[^cite_note-twelve-50] For intra-city travel, the minimum fare is AED 11.50 and for Sharjah to Dubai travel, the base fare is AED 20.
