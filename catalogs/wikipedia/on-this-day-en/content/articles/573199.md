**Epic Games, Inc.** is an American [video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_developer) and [software developer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_development) and [publisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_publisher) based in [Cary, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cary,_North_Carolina). The company was founded by [Tim Sweeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Sweeney_\(game_developer\)) as **Potomac Computer Systems** in 1991, originally located in his parents' house in [Potomac, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac,_Maryland). Following its first commercial video game release, *[ZZT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZZT)* (1991), the company became **Epic MegaGames, Inc.** in early 1992 and brought on [Mark Rein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Rein_\(software_executive\)), who has been its [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_president) since. After moving the headquarters to Cary in 1999, the studio changed its name to Epic Games.

Epic Games developed [Unreal Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Engine), a commercially available [game engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_engine) which also powers its internally developed video games like *[Fortnite](57813623.md)* and the *[Unreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_\(video_game_series\))*, *[Gears of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears_of_War)*, and *[Infinity Blade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Blade)* series. In 2014, Unreal Engine was named the "most successful videogame engine" by *[Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records)*.[^cite_note-9] Epic Games owns the game developers [Psyonix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyonix), [Mediatonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediatonic), and [Harmonix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonix), and operates studios in multiple locations around the world. While Sweeney remains the controlling shareholder, [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent) acquired a 48.4% outstanding stake, equating to 40% of total Epic, in the company in 2012, as part of an agreement aimed at moving Epic towards a [games as a service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_as_a_service) model. Following the release of the popular *[Fortnite Battle Royale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Battle_Royale)* in 2017, the company gained additional investments that enabled it to expand its Unreal Engine offerings, establish [esports](564204.md) events around *Fortnite*, and launch the [Epic Games Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Games_Store). As of April 2022, the company has a US\$32 billion equity valuation.

## History

### Potomac Computer Systems (1991–1992)

Potomac Computer Systems was founded by [Tim Sweeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Sweeney_\(game_developer\)) in 1991.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10] At the time, Sweeney was studying [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) and living in a dorm at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park). He frequently visited his parents, who lived in nearby [Potomac, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac,_Maryland), where his [personal computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_computer), used for both work and leisure, was situated.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10] Out of this location, Sweeney started Potomac Computer Systems as a [computer consulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_consulting) business but later figured that it would be too much work he would have to put into keeping the business stable, and scrapped the idea.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10]

After finishing his game *[ZZT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZZT)*, Sweeney opted to re-use the Potomac Computer Systems name to release the game to the public in January 1991.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10][^cite_note-11] It was only with the unexpected success of *ZZT*, caused in most part by the easy modifiability of the game using Sweeney's custom ZZT-oop programming language,[^cite_note-Plante-2012-12] that made Sweeney consider turning Potomac Computer Systems into a video game company.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10] *ZZT* was distributed as shareware through [bulletin board systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_board_system), while all orders were fulfilled by Sweeney's father, Paul Sweeney.[^cite_note-Pitcher-2013-13] The game sold several thousand copies as of May 2009, and Paul Sweeney still lived at the former Potomac Computer Systems address at the time, fulfilling all orders that eventually came by mail.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10][^cite_note-Pitcher-2013-13] The final copy of *ZZT* was shipped by Paul Sweeney in November 2013.[^cite_note-Pitcher-2013-13]

### Epic MegaGames (1992–1999)



Epic Games founder and CEO [Tim Sweeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Sweeney)

In early 1992, Sweeney found himself and his new-found video game company in a business where larger studios, such as [Apogee Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_Realms) and [id Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Id_Software), were dominant, and he had to find a more serious name for his.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10] As such, Sweeney came up with "Epic MegaGames", a name which incorporated "Epic" and "Mega" to make it sound like it represented a fairly large company (such as Apogee Software), although he was its only employee.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10] Sweeney soon underwent searching for a business partner, and eventually caught up with [Mark Rein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Rein_\(software_executive\)), who previously quit his job at id Software and moved to [Toronto](64646.md), Ontario.[^cite_note-Plante-2012-12][^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10] Rein worked remotely from Toronto, and primarily handled sales, marketing and publishing deals; business development that Sweeney found to have significantly contributed to the company's growth.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10] Some time this season, the company soon had 20 employees consisting of programmers, artists, designers and composers.[^cite_note-14] Among them was the 17-year old [Cliff Bleszinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff_Bleszinski), who joined the company after submitting his game *Dare to Dream* to Sweeney.[^cite_note-15] The following year, it had over 30 employees.[^cite_note-16]

In 1996, Epic MegaGames produced a shareware isometric shooter called *[Fire Fight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Fight)*, developed by Polish studio Chaos Works. It was published by [Electronic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Arts).[^cite_note-17] By 1997, Epic MegaGames had 50 people working for it worldwide.[^cite_note-18] In 1998, Epic MegaGames released *[Unreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_\(1998_video_game\))*, a [3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_computer_graphics) [first-person shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_shooter) co-developed with [Digital Extremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Extremes), which expanded into a series of [*Unreal* games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_\(video_game_series\)). The company also began to license the core technology, [Unreal Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Engine), to other game developers.[^cite_note-19]

### Epic Games (1999–present)

#### *Unreal* and personal computer games (1999–2006)

In February 1999, Epic MegaGames announced that it had moved its headquarters to a new location in [Cary, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cary,_North_Carolina), and would henceforth be known as simply Epic Games.[^cite_note-epicshop-20] Rein explained that "*Unreal* was first created by developers who were scattered across the world, eventually, the team came together to finish the game and that's when the real magic started. The move to North Carolina centralizes Epic, bringing all of the company's talented developers under one roof."[^cite_note-epicshop-20] Furthermore, Sweeney stated that the "Mega" part of the name was dropped because they no longer wanted to pretend to be a big company, as was the original intention of the name when it was a one-man team.[^cite_note-Edwards-2009-10] The follow-up game, *[Unreal Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Tournament)*, shipped to critical acclaim the same year,[^cite_note-21] at which point the studio had 13 employees.[^cite_note-22]

The company launched the [Make Something Unreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_Something_Unreal) competition in 2004, aiming to reward video game developers who create [mods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mod_\(video_games\)) using the [Unreal game engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Engine). [Tripwire Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripwire_Interactive) won US\$80,000 in cash and computer hardware prizes over the course of the contest in the first contest in 2004.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

#### *Gears of War* and console games (2006–2012)



Cliff Bleszinski, Lead Designer on Gears of War, demos the game live from Grauman's Chinese Theatre during the Xbox Press Briefing at E3 2006.

Around 2006, the personal computer video game market was struggling with [copyright infringement](18948365.md) in the form of software piracy, and it became difficult to make single-player games, elements that had been part of Epic's business model to that point. The company decided to shift focus into developing console systems, a move which Sweeney called the start of the third major iteration of the company, "Epic 3.0".[^cite_note-poly_v4-25] In 2006, Epic released the [Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360) shooter *[Gears of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears_of_War_\(video_game\))*, which became a commercial success for the company, grossing about \$100 million off a \$12 million budget.[^cite_note-polygon_gow4_cost-26][^cite_note-poly_v4-25] A year later, the company released *[Unreal Tournament 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Tournament_3)* for PC and acquired a majority share in [People Can Fly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Can_Fly).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

In 2008, Epic Games released *[Gears of War 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears_of_War_2)*,[^cite_note-29] selling over three million copies within the first month of its release.[^cite_note-30]

Epic Games released [Epic Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Citadel) on September 1, 2010 as a [tech demo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_demonstration) to demonstrate the [Unreal Engine 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Engine_3) running on [Apple](856.md) [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS), within [Adobe Flash Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Flash_Player) [Stage3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage3D) and using [HTML5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML5) [WebGL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebGL) technologies. It was also released for [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\)) on January 29, 2013. Epic Games worked on an [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS) game, *[Infinity Blade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Blade)*,[^cite_note-31] which was released on December 9, 2010.[^cite_note-32] The third game in the series, *[Gears of War 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears_of_War_3)*, came out in 2011.[^cite_note-33]

In 2011, Epic's subsidiary [Titan Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_Studios) was dissolved.[^cite_note-34] At the 2011 [Spike Video Game Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_Video_Game_Awards), Epic Games announced its new game *[Fortnite](57813623.md)*.[^cite_note-35] In March 2012, Epic Games China became independent from Epic as Ying Pei Games ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 英佩游戏), developing *Mercenary Ops* at the tim&#x65;*,* while Epic still retained a minority stake.[^cite_note-36]

In June 2012, Epic announced that it was opening up a new studio, Epic Baltimore, made up of members of [38 Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38_Studios)' [Big Huge Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Huge_Games).[^cite_note-37] Epic Baltimore was renamed to Impossible Studios in August 2012.[^cite_note-38] However, the studio ended up closing its doors in February 2013.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

Epic fully acquired People Can Fly in August 2012, rebranding it as Epic Games Poland in November 2013 as it began work on *Fortnite* alongside Epic.[^cite_note-41] Epic alongside People Can Fly made one last game in the *Gears of War* series that served as a prequel to the other games, *[Gears of War: Judgement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears_of_War:_Judgement)*, which was released in 2013. At this point, Epic had considered developing a fourth main title for *Gears of War*, but estimated that its budget would be at least \$100 million.[^cite_note-polygon_gow4_cost-26] Additionally, they had suggested the idea of a multiplayer-only version of *Gears of War* that featured improved versions of maps based on user feedback, similar to the concept behind *Unreal Tournament*, but Microsoft rejected this idea. Epic recognized the troubles of being held to the business objectives of a publisher and began to shift the company again.[^cite_note-poly_v4-25]

#### Games as a service and Tencent shareholding (2012–2018)

An inside look at Epic Games, 2015

Coupled with their desire to move away from being beholden to a publisher, Epic Games observed that the video game industry was shifting to a [games-as-a-service model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_as_a_service) (GaaS). Sweeney stated, "There was an increasing realization that the old model wasn't working anymore and that the new model was looking increasingly like the way to go."[^cite_note-poly_v4-25] In an attempt to gain more GaaS experience, they made an agreement with Chinese [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent), who had several games under their banner (including [Riot Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_Games)' *[League of Legends](21838352.md)*) operating successfully as games as a service.[^cite_note-42] In exchange for Tencent's help, Tencent acquired approximately 48.4% of Epic then issued share capital, equating to 40% of total Epic – inclusive of both stock and employee stock options, for \$330 million in June 2012. Tencent Holdings has the right to nominate directors to the board of Epic Games and thus counts as an associate of the Group.[^cite_note-tencent-7] However, Sweeney stated that Tencent otherwise has very little control on the creative output of Epic Games.[^cite_note-poly_v4-25] Sweeney considered the partial acquisition by Tencent as the start of "Epic 4.0", the fourth major iteration of the company, allowing the company to be more agile in the video game marketplace.[^cite_note-poly_v4-25][^cite_note-Crecente-2018-6]

Around this point, Epic had about 200 employees.[^cite_note-poly_v4-25] A number of high-profile staff left the company months after the Tencent deal was announced for various reasons. Some notable departures included:[^cite_note-43]

- [Cliff Bleszinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff_Bleszinski), then the design director, announced he was leaving Epic Games in October 2012 after 20 years with the company. His official reason was "It's time for a much-needed break".

  [^cite_note-44]

  &#x20;Bleszinski later stated that he had become "jaded" about the gaming industry in the lead-up to Tencent's involvement. After Tencent's investment, Bleszinski attempted to renegotiate his contract but failed to come to terms, making him think about retirement instead. He opted to stop coming to work, spending his time at his beach house, eventually leading Sweeney to come down and have a heart-to-heart discussion with Bleszinski on the new direction Epic was going, and asking him to make a firm decision regarding his commitment to Epic. Bleszinski opted to write his resignation letter the next day.

  [^cite_note-poly_v4_depart-45]

  &#x20;After about two years, Bleszinski started [Boss Key Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boss_Key_Productions) in 2014.

- President [Mike Capps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Capps_\(executive\)) announced his retirement in December 2012, and cited as reasons the birth of a baby boy he was having with his wife and his plans to be a [stay-at-home dad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stay-at-home_dad).

  [^cite_note-46]

  &#x20;He subsequently announced quitting his advisory role as well as his affiliation with the company in March 2013.

  [^cite_note-47]

- [Rod Fergusson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Fergusson), who had been a lead developer for the *Gears of War* series, left Epic in August 2012. Fergusson stated that he had seen the direction that the Tencent acquisition would have taken the company, and was not interested in the free-to-play style of games but instead wanted to continue developing a "AAA, big-narrative, big-story, big-impact game".

  [^cite_note-48]

  &#x20;Fergusson briefly joined [Irrational Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_Games), owned by [2K Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2K_Games), to help complete *[BioShock Infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioShock_Infinite)*. While there, Fergusson talked with 2K about potentially continuing the *Gears of War* series, leading to talks between 2K Games, Epic, and Microsoft.

  [^cite_note-poly_v4-25]

  &#x20;As a result, Microsoft acquired the rights to *Gears of War* on January 27, 2014, eventually assigned those to [Microsoft Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Studios); Fergusson moved to Black Tusk Studios, owned by Microsoft Game Studios, to take on lead development for a new *Gears* title, with the studio being rebranded as [The Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Coalition_\(video_game_developer\)). The first game since the acquisition, *[Gears of War 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears_of_War_4)*, was released in October 2016.

  [^cite_note-49]

  [^cite_note-50]

- [Adrian Chmielarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Chmielarz), the founder of People Can Fly, who joined Epic when his studio was acquired earlier in 2012, decided to leave after Tencent's acquisition, stating that he and other former People Can Fly members did not believe the free-to-play games as a service direction fit their own personal vision or direction they wanted to go. Chmielarz and these others left Epic in late 2012 to form [The Astronauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Astronauts_\(company\)).

  [^cite_note-poly_v4_depart-45]

- Lee Perry, a lead designer on both *Unreal* and *Gears of War* series, felt that Epic has started to grow too large to maintain a role as an eccentric game developer. Coupled with the studio's need for more management to support the games as a service model, Perry felt that their creative freedom would become limited. He and five other senior people left Epic to form a new studio, Bitmonster.

  [^cite_note-poly_v4_depart-45]

Epic continued its goal to deliver games as a service following these departures. *Fortnite* was to serve as their testbed for living games, but with the shifts in staff and its engine from Unreal Engine 3 to 4, its release suffered some setbacks. Epic started additional projects; the free-to-play and community-developed *[Unreal Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Tournament_\(2014_video_game\))*, first announced in 2014,[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] and the free-to-play [multiplayer online battle arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplayer_online_battle_arena) game *[Paragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragon_\(video_game\))*, launched in 2016 for [Microsoft Windows](18890.md) and [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4).[^cite_note-53] Epic also released a remastered version of *Shadow Complex* for newer consoles and computers in 2015,[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] and its first foray into [virtual reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_reality) with the release of *[Robo Recall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robo_Recall)* for the [Oculus Rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oculus_Rift).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

The investment infusion from Tencent allowed Epic Games to relicense the Unreal Engine 4 engine in March 2015 to be free for all users to develop with, with Epic taking 5% royalties on games developed with the engine.[^cite_note-58]

In June 2015, Epic agreed to allow Epic Games Poland's departure from the company and sold its shares in the studio; the studio reverted to its former name, People Can Fly. The *[Bulletstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletstorm)* IP was retained by People Can Fly who has since launched a remastered version called *[Bulletstorm: Full Clip Edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletstorm#Remastered_version)* on April 7, 2017, published by [Gearbox Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gearbox_Software).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

#### *Fortnite* success (2018–present)



Epic's *Fortnite* exhibition space at [E3 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Entertainment_Expo_2018)

By July 2017, *Fortnite* was finally in a state for public play.[^cite_note-61] Epic launched the title through a paid [early access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_access) then, with a full [free-to-play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-to-play) release expected in 2018.[^cite_note-62] Following on the popularity of *[PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayerUnknown's_Battlegrounds)*, a [battle royale game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_royale_game) released earlier in 2017, Epic developed a variant of *Fortnite* called *[Fortnite Battle Royale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Battle_Royale)*, which was released in September 2017[^cite_note-63] as a free-to-play title across computer, console, and mobile platforms. *Fortnite Battle Royale* quickly gained an audience, amassing over 125 million players by May 2018 with estimates of having earned over \$1 billion by July 2018 through microtransactions, including its [battle pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_pass) system. Epic Games, which had been valued at around \$825 million at the time of Tencent's acquisition, was estimated to be worth \$4.5 billion in July 2018 due to *Fortnite Battle Royale*, and expected to surpass \$8.5 billion by the end of 2018 with projected growth of the game.[^cite_note-bloomberg_value-64] Player count continued to expand when Epic broke new ground by convincing Sony to change its stance on [cross-platform play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-platform_play) allowing players on any device to compete with each other in *Fortnite Battle Royale*.[^cite_note-65] *Fortnite* has drawn nearly 250 million players as of March 2019.[^cite_note-66]

*Fortnite*'s commercial success enabled Epic to make several changes to its other product offerings. In July 2018, it reduced the revenue cut that it took for assets sold on the Unreal Engine Marketplace from 30% to 12%.[^cite_note-67] Epic launched the [Epic Games Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Games_Store) digital storefront to compete with services like [Steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_\(service\)) and [GOG.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOG.com), not only taking a 12% cut of revenue compared to the industry standard of 30%, but also eliminated the 5% cut for games using the Unreal engine sold via the storefront.[^cite_note-68] However the company also refocused its development efforts to provide more support for Unreal and *Fortnite* by ending support for *Paragon*[^cite_note-69] and *Unreal Tournament*.[^cite_note-70]

The financial success of *Fortnite* brought additional investment into Epic Games. Epic Games was one of eleven companies selected to be part of the [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney) Accelerator program in 2017, providing Epic equity investment and access to some of Disney's executives, and a potential opportunity to work with Disney in the future. Disney had selected both Epic and [aXiomatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AXiomatic) as potential leads in the growing esports arena.[^cite_note-71]

Epic has used its windfall to support its products. In January 2019, following a dispute between [Improbable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improbable_\(company\)) and [Unity Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_Technologies) over changes to the acceptable uses of the Unity game engine, Epic announced it was partnering with Improbable to launch a \$25 million fund to help bring developers it believes affected by these changes towards solutions that are more open and would have fewer service compatibilities.[^cite_note-72] Epic launched a \$100 million prize pool in February 2019 for *Fortnite*-related esports activities that it plans to run from 2019 onward.[^cite_note-73] To expand its esports initiatives, Epic Games hired Nate Nanzer from [Blizzard Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blizzard_Entertainment) and its commissioner of the [Overwatch League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwatch_League) in May 2019.[^cite_note-74] At the 2019 [Game Developers Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Developers_Conference), Epic announced it was launching a \$100 million MegaGrants initiative, allowing anyone to apply for up to \$500,000 in funding to support game development using the Unreal Engine or for any project, even if not directly games-related, that would benefit the Unreal Engine.[^cite_note-75] One of the first major funded entities under this was the [Blender Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blender_Foundation) in July 2019, having received \$1.2 million from the MegaGrants funding, to help it to improve and professionalize its [Blender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blender_\(software\)) tools for 3D art creation.[^cite_note-76]

Epic Games was given the [British Academy of Film and Television Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_of_Film_and_Television_Arts) Special Award in June 2019 for its past and continuing developments for the Unreal Engine,[^cite_note-77] a software which also earned it the Engineering Excellence Award from the [Hollywood Professional Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Professional_Association).[^cite_note-78]

Epic announced in March 2020 that it was establishing a new multi-platform publishing label, Epic Games Publishing. Rowe Price Group Inc. and Baillie Gifford.[^cite_note-83] The company partnered with [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan) and [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros.) to acquire distribution rights for *[Inception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inception)*, *[Batman Begins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_Begins)* and *[The Prestige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prestige)* as part of "Movie Nite" on Fortnite's "Party Royale" island. Epic purchased [Cary Towne Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cary_Towne_Center) in Cary, North Carolina, in January 2021, which had been scheduled to be closed and demolished after 2020, to be its new headquarters and campus, with the conversion to be complete by 2024.[^cite_note-90] All of the existing mall's structure, except for a section housing the Belk department store, was razed. On February 9, 2023, Epic released the Postparty application.[^cite_note-103] Postparty is a gameplay clipping [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) designed for people who do not have access to clipping software or who require a way to share gaming videos and highlights (it is frequently used on gaming consoles) clipped (30 seconds max), edited to the user's preference (using the in-app editing tools and other editing software), and then posted to social media.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] It is most commonly associated with creating and sharing video clips in *[Fortnite](57813623.md)*, but it is also associated with clipping other applications created by Epic, such as *[Rocket League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_League)*. ## Acquisitions

In 2008, Epic acquired Utah based [Chair Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chair_Entertainment), developer of *[Undertow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undertow_\(video_game\))*.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] Summer 2009 saw the launch of Chair's *[Shadow Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Complex)*, an adventure game inspired by the *[Metroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metroid)* series.[^cite_note-129]

Epic announced in October 2018 that it had acquired \$1.25 billion in investment from seven firms: [KKR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohlberg_Kravis_Roberts), [ICONIQ Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICONIQ_Capital), Smash Ventures, [aXiomatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AXiomatic), [Vulcan Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcan_Capital_Management), [Kleiner Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleiner_Perkins), and [Lightspeed Venture Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightspeed_Venture_Partners). Besides expanding support for *Fortnite* and the Epic Games Store, these investments allowed Epic to acquire additional firms. In January 2018, it was announced that Epic had acquired [Cloudgine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudgine), a developer of cloud-based gaming software.[^cite_note-cloudengine-133] The company also announced the acquisition of Kamu, a firm that offered anti-cheat software called [Easy Anti-Cheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easy_Anti-Cheat), in October 2018.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] A year later, in January 2019, Epic acquired 3Lateral and Agog Labs. 3Lateral is known for its "digital human" creations, using a combination of digital technology, motion capture, and other tools to create photo-realistic human subjects in real-time. Epic plans to add some of 3Lateral's features to the Unreal Engine.[^cite_note-3lateral-136] Agog had developed SkookumScript, a platform for scripting events in video games; on the announcement of this acquisition, Agog stated it will stop the development of SkookumScript to work more on Unreal Engine scripting support.[^cite_note-agog-137]

Epic acquired [Psyonix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyonix), the developer of *[Rocket League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_League)*, in May 2019. Epic and Psyonix have had a past history, as Psyonix was originally founded a few miles from Epic's headquarters and had contributed to Epic's *Unreal Tournament*.[^cite_note-Grayson-138][^cite_note-Statt-139] Besides ongoing support for *Rocket League*, Psyonix developed an arcade-style car racing game inside of *Fortnite*, named *Rocket Racing*, which was added in December 2023.[^cite_note-epic_hmx_psyonix-140]

In November 2019, Epic acquired Quixel, the world's largest [photogrammetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photogrammetry) asset library which makes 3D models of objects based on real-world high-definition photography. Epic acquired SuperAwesome, a firm that has developed services to support child-safe games and services around games, in September 2020, to incorporate these elements more into Epic's portfolio and to offer to other developers, particularly for games built around Epic's vision of metaverse games.[^cite_note-superawesome-148][^cite_note-149]

### Unreal Engine

### Lore

Lore is a software system for [version control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Version_control). Epic Games originally developed Unreal Revision Control for internal usage. They have released it as Lore to be an open-source version control system designed entirely around the large file needs of video game and multimedia projects. Lore is designed to be fast and efficient for large files including binary files, and be easy-to-use including for 3D artists and more. It has access control features to ensure different teams can protect access to their assets.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173]

Lore uses content addressed storage based on hashes to achieve automatic deduplication, integrity validation, and efficient caching. Revisions use [Merkle trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkle_trees) to create a history. On-demand hydration allows developers to download a fraction of a repository as needed. Partitions provide access control to individual teams with separate NDAs. Lore uses a centralized server (like Perforce), but adds intelligent caching to scale throughput. This gives teams a single source of truth while avoiding the bottleneck of every request hitting the origin server.

Lore is most similar to [Perforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perforce#P4) in prior art. It has file-level locking for unmergeable content, but unlike Perforce it has partitions for sub-repositories instead of per-path ACL.[^cite_note-174]

## Legal issues

See also: [Fortnite Battle Royale § Past and ongoing concerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Battle_Royale#Past_and_ongoing_concerns)

### Litigation with Silicon Knights

&#x20;On July 19, 2007, Canadian game studio [Silicon Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Knights) sued Epic Games for failure to "provide a working game engine", causing the Ontario-based game developer to "experience considerable losses".[^cite_note-201] The suit alleged that Epic Games was "sabotaging" Unreal Engine 3 licensees. Epic's licensing document stated that a working version of the engine would be available within six months of the Xbox 360 developer kits being released. Silicon Knights claimed that Epic missed this deadline and that when a working version of the engine was eventually released, the documentation was insufficient. The game studio also claimed Epic had withheld vital improvements to the game engine, claiming they were game-specific, while also using licensing fees to fund the development of its own titles rather than the engine itself.[^cite_note-202]

In August 2007, Epic Games [counter-sued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterclaim) Silicon Knights, alleging the studio was aware when it signed on that certain features of Unreal Engine 3 were still in development and that components would continue to be developed and added as Epic completed work on *[Gears of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears_of_War_\(video_game\))*. Therefore, in a statement, Epic said that "SK knew when it committed to the licensing agreement that Unreal Engine 3 may not meet its requirements and may not be modified to meet them".[^cite_note-Brightman-2007-203] Additionally, the counter-suit claimed that Silicon Knights had "made unauthorized use of Epic's Licensed Technology" and had "infringed and otherwise violated Epic's intellectual property rights, including Epic's copyrighted works, trade secrets, know how and confidential information" by incorporating Unreal Engine 3 code into its own engine, the Silicon Knights Engine.[^cite_note-Brightman-2007-203] Furthermore, Epic asserted the Canadian developer broke the contract when it employed this derivative work in an internal title and a second game with [Sega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega),[^cite_note-204] a partnership for which it never received a license fee.[^cite_note-205]

On May 30, 2012, Epic Games defeated Silicon Knights' lawsuit and won its counter-suit for \$4.45 million on grounds of copyright infringement, misappropriation of trade secrets, and breach of contract,[^cite_note-206] an injury award that was later doubled due to prejudgment interest, attorneys' fees and costs.[^cite_note-vg247-207] Consistent with Epic's counterclaims, the presiding judge, [James C. Dever III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_C._Dever_III), stated that Silicon Knights had "deliberately and repeatedly copied thousands of lines of Epic Games' copyrighted code, and then attempted to conceal its wrongdoing by removing Epic Games' copyright notices and by disguising Epic Games' copyrighted code as Silicon Knights' own".[^cite_note-vg247-207] Dever stated that evidence against Silicon Knights was "overwhelming", as it not only copied functional code but also "non-functional, internal comments Epic Games' programmers had left for themselves".[^cite_note-vg247-207]

As a result, on November 7, 2012, Silicon Knights was directed by the court to destroy all game code derived from Unreal Engine 3, all information from licensee-restricted areas of Epic's Unreal Engine documentation website, and to permit Epic Games access to the company's servers and other devices to ensure these items have been removed. In addition, the studio was instructed to recall and destroy all unsold retail copies of games built with Unreal Engine 3 code, including *[Too Human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Too_Human)*, *[X-Men Destiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Men_Destiny)*, *The Sandman*, *The Box*/*Ritualyst*, and *Siren in the Maelstrom* (the latter three titles were projects never released, or even officially announced).[^cite_note-208]

On May 16, 2014, Silicon Knights filed for bankruptcy and a Certificate of Appointment was issued by the office of the [Superintendent of Bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_Bankruptcy), with Collins Barrow Toronto Limited being appointed as [trustee in bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trustee_in_bankruptcy).[^cite_note-209]

### Apple and Google disputes

Main articles: [Epic Games v. Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Games_v._Apple) and [Epic Games v. Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Games_v._Google)

Since as early as 2017, Tim Sweeney had questioned the need for digital storefronts like Valve's Steam, Apple's iOS App Store, and Google Play, to take a 30% [revenue sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_sharing) cut, and argued that when accounting for current rates of content distribution and other factors needed, a revenue cut of 8% should be sufficient to run any digital storefront profitably.[^cite_note-210] When Epic brought *Fortnite Battle Royale* to mobile devices, the company initially offered a [sideloaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sideloading) package for Android systems to bypass the Google Play store, but eventually also made it a store app.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213]

On August 13, 2020, Epic Games updated *Fortnite* across all platforms, including the iOS and Android versions, to reduce the price of "V-Bucks" (the in-game currency) by 20% if they purchased directly from Epic. For iOS and Android users, if they purchased through the Apple or Google storefront, they were not given this discount, as Epic said it could not extend the discount due to the 30% revenue cut taken by Apple and Google.[^cite_note-214] Within hours, both Apple and Google had removed *Fortnite* from their storefronts stating the means of bypassing their payment systems violated both storefronts guidelines.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216] Epic immediately filed separate lawsuits against Apple and Google for antitrust and anticompetitive behavior in the [United States District Court for the Northern District of California](891692.md).[^cite_note-217] Epic did not seek monetary damages in either case but instead was "seeking injunctive relief to allow fair competition in these two key markets that directly affect hundreds of millions of consumers and tens of thousands, if not more, of third-party app developers."[^cite_note-218] In comments on social media the next day, Sweeney said that it undertook the actions as "we're fighting for the freedom of people who bought smartphones to install apps from sources of their choosing, the freedom for creators of apps to distribute them as they choose, and the freedom of both groups to do business directly. The primary opposing argument is: 'Smartphone markers \[sic] can do whatever they want.' This is an awful notion. We all have rights, and we need to fight to defend our rights against whoever would deny them."[^cite_note-219]

Apple responded to the lawsuit that it would terminate Epic's developer accounts by August 28, 2020, leading Epic to file a motion for a preliminary injunction to force Apple to return *Fortnite* to the App Store and prevent it from terminating Epic's developer accounts, as the latter action would leave Epic unable to update the Unreal Engine for any changes to iOS or macOS and leave developers that relied on Unreal at risk.[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] The court granted the preliminary injunction against Apple from terminating the developer accounts as Epic had shown "potential significant damage to both the Unreal Engine platform itself, and to the gaming industry generally", but refused to grant the injunction related to *Fortnite* as "The current predicament appears of \[Epic's] own making."[^cite_note-222] In September 2020, Epic Games, together with thirteen other companies, launched the [Coalition for App Fairness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_for_App_Fairness), which aimed for better conditions for the inclusion of apps into app stores.[^cite_note-223]

U.S. District Court Judge [Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvonne_Gonzalez_Rogers) issued her first ruling in *Epic Games v. Apple* on September 10, 2021.[^cite_note-224] Rogers found in favor of Apple on nine of ten counts brought up against it in the case, including Epic's charges related to Apple's 30% revenue cut and Apple's prohibition against third-party marketplaces on the iOS environment.[^cite_note-225] Rogers did rule against Apple on the final charge related to anti-steering provisions, and issued a permanent injunction that, in 90 days from the ruling, blocked Apple from preventing developers from linking app users to other storefronts from within apps to complete purchases or from collecting information within an app, such as an email, to notify users of these storefronts.[^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227] Rogers' ruling was upheld at the [Ninth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_Circuit) on appeals, and the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) declined to hear the case, leaving Rogers' order against Apple in place.[^cite_note-228] Though Apple did adjust its policies for third-party storefront, Epic argued these failed to achieve the goals set further. In April 2025, Rogers ruled in Epic's favor that Apple's changes were not sufficient. Rogers blocked Apple from taking fees from third-party stores or restricting how they are shown in apps, subsequently leading Epic to bring the Epic Games Store back to iOS within a week.[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230]

Google initially sought to negotiate with Epic but later filed its own countersuit against Epic for breach of contract. The Google case was set as a jury trial, held in November and December 2023. Before this, other groups had filed their own lawsuits against Google for similar reasons as Epic, including a coalition of states and the [Match Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_Group), but these were settled just ahead of the trial.[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232] The jury found for Epic on all claims made, determining that Google maintained a monopoly on the Android marketplace by how it managed the Play Store and used its leverage as a big tech firm to make deal with partners, including some deals made as a result of the earlier settlements. A second phase of this trial to determine remedies is scheduled to occur in January 2024.[^cite_note-233]

### FTC child privacy settlement

In December 2022, Epic Games was fined a combined \$520 million after the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission) accused the company of separate accounts related to *Fortnite*, one for violating [COPPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COPPA) related to children's privacy by collecting personal data without parent or guardian consent, exposing children and teens to potential harassment, and a second related to misleading users into making unwanted purchases while playing the game.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237] Epic Games said "No developer creates a game with the intention of ending up here. The laws have not changed, but their application has evolved and long-standing industry practices are no longer enough. We accepted this agreement because we want Epic to be at the forefront of consumer protection and provide the best experience for our players. Over the past few years, we've been making changes to ensure our ecosystem meets the expectations of our players and regulators, which we hope will be a helpful guide for others in our industry."[^cite_note-238] Epic ultimately settled with the FTC, agreeing to pay \$245 million in December 2024 in relation to the use of [dark patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_pattern) it used to drive users to buy V-bucks, with \$72 million being issued as refunds by the FTC to more than 600,000 users that had filed claims in the case.[^cite_note-239]

## Criticism

Since the partial investment by the Chinese company Tencent, some consumers have become wary of Epic Games' reliability and use of their data, particularly [in relationship with the Epic Games Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Games_Store#Other_criticisms). These concerns have been connected to broader issues of general distrust of the Chinese government and Chinese corporations among some Western video game players. Epic has stated that Tencent does not have access to any of this private data nor provides this to the Chinese government.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241]

In late March 2020, accusations began circulating on social media that the Epic Games social networking app *[Houseparty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houseparty_\(app\))* led to other services such as [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) and [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify) being [hacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_hacker). However, both Epic and Life on Air claimed this was a [smear campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smear_campaign) against its product and offered a \$1 million bounty for anyone able to substantiate their claim.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243][^cite_note-244]

On August 13, 2020, Epic released a version of *Fortnite* that included a permanent discount on V-bucks across all platforms (except iOS and Android devices) if they purchased directly through Epic, bypassing Apple and Google's storefronts. Both [Apple](856.md) and [Google](1092923.md) immediately delisted the game for violating the storefronts' terms of service by including its own storefront, which led Epic to [file lawsuits against both companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Games_v._Apple) the same day, accusing them of antitrust behavior in how they operate their app stores. While Apple was ultimately victorious in a bench trial in September 2021 and upheld through appeals, Google's actions were found to be monopolistic by a jury trial in December 2023.

In May 2025, Disney and Epic Games (which The Walt Disney Company owns a 9% in ownership stakes) announced that the video game *[Fortnite](57813623.md)* would bring [Darth Vader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darth_Vader) as a non-playable character, with [James Earl Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Earl_Jones)' voice being reconstructed using generative AI with permission by his estate to serve as an AI chatbot.[^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246] Prior to this, in 2022, Jones signed a deal with Lucasfilm, which allowed the company to utilize generative AI in future projects to recreate his voice.[^cite_note-247] This technology was first used in the Disney+ miniseries *[Obi-Wan Kenobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obi-Wan_Kenobi_\(TV_series\))*.[^cite_note-248] The announcement of the Darth Vader AI chatbot was met with backlash from people who felt that while the appropriate permissions were obtained from the estates of anyone whose likeness is being used, it still felt creepy doing this one year after Jones' death. Similar comparisons were made to the digital reconstruction of [Ian Holm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Holm)[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250] in *[Alien: Romulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien:_Romulus)*.[^cite_note-251] Two days after the character was added, it was reported that several players in the game made the character say [racial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism) and [homophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia) slurs using Jones' voice. Shortly after clips of the chatbot saying such words went viral on social media, Epic Games pushed out a hotfix for *Fortnite* that restricts the responses it can say.[^cite_note-252] On May 19, 2025, [SAG-AFTRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAG-AFTRA) filed an unfair labor charge against Epic Games and Llama Productions for infringing on the union's right to negotiate major changes to its collective bargaining agreement.[^cite_note-253]
