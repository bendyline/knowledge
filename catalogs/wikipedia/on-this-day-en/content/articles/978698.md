**Karaj** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): کرج; pronounced [\[kæˈɾædʒ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Persian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/40/Karaj.ogg/Karaj.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/40/Karaj.ogg/Karaj.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>)[^cite_note-6] is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Iran_by_province) in the [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Karaj_County\)) of [Karaj County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaj_County), [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_province) province, [Iran](14653.md), serving as capital of the province,[^cite_note-Alborz_Province_Establishment_Law-7] the county, and the district.[^cite_note-Tehran_Province_Structure-8] Earliest evidence of inhabitation in Karaj can be dated to the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) at Tepe Khurvin.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The city was developed under the rule of the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran) and [Qajar Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) and is home to historical buildings and memorials from those eras. This city has a unique climate due to access to natural resources such as many trees, rivers, and green plains. After [Tehran](57654.md), Karaj is the largest immigrant-friendly city in Iran, so it has been nicknamed "Little Iran."

Although the county hosts a population of approximately 1.97 million, as recorded in the 2016 census,[^cite_note-2016_Alborz_Province-3] most of the 1,419 km<sup>2</sup> (548 sq mi) county consists of rugged mountains. Karaj is also the third most populous city in Iran after Tehran and Mashhad and the sixteenth largest metropolis in the [Middle East](19323.md), with a metropolitan population of nearly 3,599,000 as of 2025.[^cite_note-11] The urban area is the fourth-largest in Iran, after Tehran, [Mashhad](406512.md), and [Isfahan](223149.md).[^cite_note-12] [Eshtehard County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eshtehard_County)[^cite_note-Eshtehard_County_Establishment-13] and [Fardis County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fardis_County)[^cite_note-Fardis_County_Establishment-14] were split off from [Karaj County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaj_County) since the previous census.

## History



Shah-Abbasi [Caravansary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravansary)

The area around Karaj has been inhabited for thousands of years, as shown by the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) site of Tepe Khurvin and the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) site of Kalak.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-Iranica-16] However, the present-day city of Karaj is mostly the result of modern industrial development in the 20th century.[^cite_note-Iranica-16]

Karaj was mostly significant as a stage on the road between Tehran and [Qazvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazvin).[^cite_note-Iranica-16] In [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid) times, a stone bridge was built that served as the main crossing into the town.[^cite_note-Iranica-16] The large [Shah-Abbasi Caravansarai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Abbasi_Caravansarai,_Karaj), located at the southeast of Towhid Square, was built in the same era, under the rule of [Šāh Esmāil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Ismail).

In 1810, the Qajar prince Soleyman Mirza built the [Soleymaniyeh Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soleymaniyeh_Palace) at Karaj to serve as a summer resort.[^cite_note-Iranica-16] The palace had four towers and was surrounded by gardens. Its reception room featured a pair of paintings by [Abdallah Khan Naqqashbandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdallah_Khan).[^cite_note-Iranica-16] By 1860, however, the palace was described as abandoned and only used as a shelter for travelers.[^cite_note-Iranica-16] [Naser al-Din Shah Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naser_al-Din_Shah_Qajar) later renovated the palace.[^cite_note-Iranica-16] In 1917, a School of Farming was established on the site, replacing the earlier Mozaffari Agricultural School in Tehran.[^cite_note-Iranica-16] Later, [Reza Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah) granted it to the [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran)'s new Faculty of Agriculture.[^cite_note-Iranica-16]

In the 1930s, plans were drawn up for a large industrial complex covering 216 [hectares](24919513.md) on the south side of the village.[^cite_note-Iranica-16] This "Industrial Model Town of Karaj" was intended to be the site of the country's first [steel mills](1143008.md), capitalizing on easy access to water and coal from the Alborz.[^cite_note-Iranica-16] However, the construction equipment imported from Germany was impounded by the British going through the [Suez Canal](29323.md) in 1940, and the planned complex was never built.[^cite_note-Iranica-16]

A major industrial complex, the first privately owned one in Karaj, was built in the 1960s by Mohammad-Sadeq Fateh.[^cite_note-Iranica-16] This complex, called Shahrak-e Jahanshahr, included oil, tea, and textile factories as well as housing for the workers.[^cite_note-Iranica-16]



[Morvarid Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morvarid_Palace)

The [Morvārid Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morvarid_Palace) was constructed in the nearby [Mehršahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrshahr) district, during the [Pahlavi era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_era). It was designed by the *[Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lloyd_Wright) Foundation* ([Taliesin Associated Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliesin_Associated_Architects)) on instructions from [Shams Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shams_Pahlavi), elder sister of [Mohammad Reza Pahlavi](20065598.md). In this period, it was a transit and industrial town.

The majority of the structure is now controlled by the [Basij Organization](2892082.md), and some sections of it are open to the public under the operation of the [Cultural Heritage Organization of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Heritage_Organization_of_Iran).

Other historical sites of the city include the Mausoleum of Šāhzāde Soleymān, [Emāmzāde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imamzadeh) Rahmān, Emāmzāde Zeyd, and [Palang Ābād](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palangabad_District) e [Eštehārd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eshtehard_County).[^cite_note-17]

### Population

At the time of the 2006 National Census, the city's population was 1,377,450 in 385,955 households, when it was in [Tehran](202698.md) province.[^cite_note-2006_Tehran_Province-20] The 2016 census measured the population of the city as 1,592,492 people in 508,420 households,[^cite_note-2016_Alborz_Province-3] by which time the county had been separated from the province in the establishment of [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_province) province.[^cite_note-Alborz_Province_Establishment_Law-7]

The economic base of Karaj is its proximity to [Tehran](57654.md). It is due to the transportation of products between Tehran and the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). Chemicals, fertilizers and processed agricultural goods are also produced in the city.

Zowb Āhan, the avenue leading to an industrial plant, is located at the south of Ostandar Square. *Zowb e Āhan* or *Zowb Āhan*, literally "steel mill", was a contract between the [Pahlavi](338883.md) government and a consortium from [Nazi Germany](21212.md). The establishment of the factory *Zowb Āhan e Karaj* was halted by the beginning of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), and it was never launched.

Šahrak-e Jahānšahr was the first modern private industrial and housing complex of Karaj, built in the 1960s. The factories *Jahān Čit* (textile factory), *Rowqan Nabāti e Jahān* (oil factory), and *Čāy e Jahān* (tea factory), were established at the complex. It is one of the largest industrial zones of the nation, with a 20% share of the national GDP.

The [special economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zone) of Payam, with an area about 3,600 ha (36 km<sup>2</sup>; 8,900 acres) within the territory of [Payam International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payam_International_Airport), was established in Karaj for development of air cargo and postal transportation, cold store, and packing services, as well as perishable and time sensitive exports. It is the only SEZ in the region with the privilege of its own [airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payam_Air).

### Women's clothing

Small and medium women's clothing companies in Karaj produce all kinds of daily clothes and leather boots. Karaj was one of the few Iranian cities in the 2010s that controlled the damage to the [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_Iran) in the process of producing women's clothing. Karaj women's over-the-knee boots are exported to the whole country and have a significant contribution to the city's economy.[^cite_note-29]

### Road

The highway system of Karaj includes Tehran–Karaj Highway, Karaj Special Road, and the old road of Karaj (Fath Highway). [Bākeri Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakeri_Expressway) is one of the main north-to-south routes in west Tehran, which is connected to the Tehran–Karaj Highway.[^cite_note-32] Tehran–Karaj Highway is one of the busiest sections in Iran with [AADT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AADT) of 217084.[^cite_note-33] Karaj–[Qazvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazvin) has an AADT of 79606.

The aerial transport of Karaj is served by the [Payam International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payam_International_Airport), which was established in 1990, and was officially opened in 1997.



A view of the Karaj-Chalous Road ([Road 59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_59_\(Iran\))) and the Karaj River

### Bus

Currently, the total number of buses in Karaj and the suburbs are 1,600 units. More than 80 lines serve citizens.

Preparing Alborz Card[^cite_note-34] can reduce the cost of travel tickets.



Payam Airport

### Metro

The Karaj Metro consists of two lines. Line One travels from west to east through the city (from Karaj Station to Golshahr Station). It continues eastward to become [Line Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Metro_Line_5) of the [Tehran Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Metro). On its way to Tehran, it stops at Chitgar park and Azadi stadium. The second line of Karaj metro (north to south) is under construction and opened for limited service in February 2023.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

### Air

[Payam International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payam_International_Airport), is an international airport located in Karaj, 40 kilometers (25  mi) from Tehran, in the Alborz Province of Iran. The airport was established in 1990, but was not opened officially until 1997. Payam Aviation Services Co. operates the airport as part of Payam Special Economic Zone. Payam Air previously operated an airmail hub at the airport. Currently, this airport and its special area are used for purposes such as cargo transit, commercial transportation, and goods mail in the development of the country's communication and information technology industries.

See also: [Sport in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Iran)



[Karaj Enqhelab Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enghelab_Stadium_\(Karaj\))

Karaj was formerly home of the [Persian Gulf Pro League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_Pro_League) club [Saipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saipa_F.C.) for several years, and this club won its first league championship in this city; in 2014 the team relocated to Tehran. Currently the only professional football team in city is [Oxin Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxin_Alborz_F.C.) that play in the [Azadegan League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadegan_League). The Home stadium of Oxin Alborz F.C. is [Enghelab Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enghelab_Stadium_\(Karaj\)) that located in Karaj Enghelab sport complex and has a capacity of 15,000 people. The stadium held a match between [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Melli) and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team) in June 2009. The stadium also held an international match, on May 1, 2012, where Iran's National Football team played [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_national_football_team).

[Saipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saipa_Tehran_VC) volleyball team lost to [Kalleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalleh_Mazandaran_VC) in this stadium in 2011–12 and became the runner-up in the final match of the country's volleyball premier league. Saipa has won the runner-up title of [Iran Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Volleyball_Super_League) seven times.

One of the international ski resorts of Iran The, [Dizin ski resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizin), is located a few kilometers north-east of the city, in the [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz). In Dizin, along with skiing facilities, there are tennis courtyards, a slope for skiing on turf, some altitudes for mountain climbing and walking as well as riding and some routes for cycling.[^cite_note-37] Karaj also has an international tennis complex which is used to training and tournaments. Jahanshahr International Tennis Complex is located in Bagh Fateh. This park has eight practice courts and one competition court with a capacity of 1,200 people. It is the only tennis complex in Iran that has covered courts.
