**Kenneth Franklin McKenzie Jr.**[^cite_note-2] (born 1956 or 1957) is a retired [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) [general](2965165.md) who served as the 14th commander of the [United States Central Command](338482.md) from March 28, 2019, to April 1, 2022.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-CENTCOM-4] He served as [Director of the Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_the_Joint_Staff) from July 5, 2017, after having previously served for two years as Director of Strategic Plans and Policy (J-5) on the [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff).[^cite_note-5]

In May 2022, he became the executive director of the Global and National Security Institute, [University of South Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Florida), and in July 2022, he was appointed executive director for the Florida Center for Cybersecurity. He also serves as the President, Board of Directors, of the Institute for Applied Engineering, University of South Florida.[^cite_note-6] On July 1, 2026, he became [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_The_Citadel) of [The Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Citadel) in [Charleston, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina).[^cite_note-7]

## Early life and education

A native of [Birmingham, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama), McKenzie was [commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_\(document\)) in 1979, via the [Naval Reserve Officers Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Reserve_Officers_Training_Corps) at [The Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Citadel). McKenzie holds an advanced degree from The Citadel Graduate College and is an honors graduate of the Marine Corps Command and Staff College as well as the [School of Advanced Warfighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Advanced_Warfighting).[^cite_note-8]

## Military career

As an infantry officer, McKenzie's assignments have included command of the [1st Battalion, 6th Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Battalion,_6th_Marines), and of the [22nd Marine Expeditionary Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Marine_Expeditionary_Unit), which he led on deployments to both [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-9] He also served as Military Secretary to two [Commandants of the Marine Corps](7708.md).

McKenzie's general officer posts have included deputy director of Operations for the [National Military Command Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Military_Command_Center) in [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon). In 2008, he was selected by the [Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md) to serve as director of his new administration [transition team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_transition), overseeing the transition of military forces under incoming President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama). He returned to Afghanistan serving as Deputy Chief of Staff for Stability under the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force),[^cite_note-10] followed by a tour as Director of Strategy, Plans and Policy at [United States Central Command](338482.md). He then returned to the Pentagon to serve as the Marine Corps Representative to the [Quadrennial Defense Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrennial_Defense_Review) and, after receiving his third star, was appointed Commanding General of [United States Marine Forces Central Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Forces_Central_Command).[^cite_note-11]

Gen. McKenzie at [Hamid Karzai International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_International_Airport) during the [2021 Fall of Kabul](68481047.md)

As CENTCOM commander, McKenzie oversaw the successful high-profile [special forces raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Abu_Bakr_al-Baghdadi) in [Syria](7515849.md) to kill or capture then-[Islamic State](9087364.md) leader [Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_al-Baghdadi) in October 2019,[^cite_note-12] and the 2020–2021 withdrawals and reductions of U.S. troops [from Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_United_States_troops_from_Iraq_\(2020%E2%80%932021\)).[^cite_note-13]

During the [2020–2021 Afghanistan withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_United_States_troops_from_Afghanistan_\(2020%E2%80%932021\)), McKenzie replaced General [Austin "Scott" Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_S._Miller) as leader of U.S. and NATO forces in Afghanistan upon the latter's resignation on July 12, 2021, with Miller's departure perceived by some as "the symbolic end of the U.S. military mission in Afghanistan."[^cite_note-14] One month later, McKenzie was responsible for the [August 2021 Kabul drone strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2021_Kabul_drone_strike) which killed 10 civilians, 7 of whom were children. The main target of the strike, aid worker Zemerai Ahmadi, had been misidentified as an [ISIS-Khorasan](48029263.md) militant. McKenzie later acknowledged the strike was a "mistake" and stated, “I offer my sincere apology” during a press briefing.[^cite_note-abcstory-15][^cite_note-16]

McKenzie retired from active duty on 1 April 2022, after relinquishing command of CENTCOM to General [Michael Kurilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kurilla).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-CENTCOM-4][^cite_note-18]

On 23 March 2026, it was announced General McKenzie would become the 21st President of his alma mater, [The Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Citadel).[^cite_note-19]
