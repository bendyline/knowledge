The **Rastriya Swatantra Party** (**RSP**)[^cite_note-8] is a [centrist](39619035.md) [political party in Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Nepal). It has been the governing party of Nepal since the [2026 general election](69672716.md) with 182 seats out of 275 in the [House of Representatives](2707214.md).

The RSP was founded by broadcaster [Rabi Lamichhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_Lamichhane) in 2022 in order to contest the [2022 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nepalese_general_election).[^cite_note-9] The party won 10.70% of the party list vote and became one of seven [national parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Nepal) in the [Federal Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Parliament_of_Nepal). It joined the [coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Dahal_cabinet) of [Pushpa Kamal Dahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushpa_Kamal_Dahal) with Lamichhane as deputy prime minister. The party withdrew from the cabinet after Lamichhane's citizenship was declared invalid by the [Supreme Court of Nepal](2707227.md) in 2023. The party again became a coalition partner in the Prachanda-led government with four cabinet ministries from March to July 2024. Lamichhane was arrested in late 2024 following accusations of cooperative fraud and [Dol Prasad Aryal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dol_Prasad_Aryal) served as acting president. In the aftermath of the [2025 Gen Z protests](81013780.md), fan favourite [Balen Shah](70833999.md) joined the RSP, whose political agenda reshaped the party's identity. The RSP won a near two-thirds majority in the 2026 election with Shah as prime minister.

Classified as a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) party with [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist) appeal, the RSP has campaigned on [constitutional socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democratic_constitutions_with_references_to_socialism), [secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism) and a [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state). It also supports the *Right to Recall* provisions for its elected MPs, pledging [anti-corruption](58111406.md) stands and calling for a shift towards [liberal economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_economy) in Nepal.

## History

### Foundation and early years (2022–2025)

On 16 June 2022, [Rabi Lamichhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_Lamichhane) resigned as managing director of now defunct [Galaxy 4K TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nepali_television_stations) and announced that he would be contesting in the [2022 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nepalese_general_election) for a seat at the [House of Representatives](2707214.md).[^cite_note-10] On 21 June 2022, he announced the formation of Rastriya Swatantra Party along with a 21-member central committee.[^cite_note-RL-11][^cite_note-JR-12] The party was formally registered in the [Election Commission](24070791.md) on 1 July 2022 with Rabi Lamichhane as the founding president and a bell inside a circle as its election symbol.[^cite_note-13] [^cite_note-14][^cite_note-IP-15]

The party fielded candidates in 131 constituencies for the [2022 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nepalese_general_election), but did not contest the [provincial elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nepalese_provincial_elections). The party won seven direct seats and 13 proportional seats to the House of Representatives.[^cite_note-16] It received 10.70% of the party list vote and became one of seven [national parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Nepal) in the [Federal Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Parliament_of_Nepal).[^cite_note-s-17] The RSP was the [fourth-largest national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Nepal#National_parties) party in Nepal after the [2022 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nepalese_general_election).[^cite_note-NationalHerald-18] Following the election, the party joined the [coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Dahal_cabinet) of [Pushpa Kamal Dahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushpa_Kamal_Dahal) with party president [Rabi Lamichhane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_Lamichhane) serving as [deputy prime minister](58719229.md).[^cite_note-19] [Indira Rana Magar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Ranamagar) from the party was elected as the deputy speaker of the House of Representatives.[^cite_note-20] The party was a coalition partner in the Prachanda-led government with four cabinet ministries from 6 March 2024 to 12 July 2024.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Previously, it was a junior ally in the [Prachanda-led](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Dahal_cabinet) government from 26 December 2022 until 5 February 2023.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

The party withdrew from the cabinet after Lamichhane's citizenship was declared invalid by the [Supreme Court](2707227.md), but continued to support the government.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Following the decision, [Dol Prasad Aryal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dol_Prasad_Aryal) became acting president of the party.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The party replaced list MP [Dhaka Kumar Shrestha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_Kumar_Shrestha) and expelled him from the party following corruption accusations.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

The party contested the by-elections in 2023 and gained a seat in [Tanahun 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanahun_1_\(constituency\)) while Lamichhane was re-elected from [Chitwan 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitwan_2_\(constituency\)).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The party rejoined the coalition government of Dahal in March 2024, but the cabinet was dissolved after a breakdown in the alliance a month later.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The party expelled general secretary Mukul Dhakal from the party for violating party rules.[^cite_note-35]

Party president Lamichhane was arrested in October 2024 following accusations of cooperative fraud and [Dol Prasad Aryal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dol_Prasad_Aryal) served as acting president.[^cite_note-36] In June 2025, Kabindra Burlakoti was appointed as general secretary and [Manish Jha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manish_Jha_\(politician\)) was appointed as party spokesperson.[^cite_note-37]

### Gen Z protests (2025–present)

Following the Gen Z protests, former MP [Sumana Shrestha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumana_Shrestha) resigned from the party.[^cite_note-38] Chief whip [Santosh Pariyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santosh_Pariyar) also left the party and joined the [Pragatisheel Loktantrik Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragatisheel_Loktantrik_Party).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] In December 2025, [Bibeksheel Sajha Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibeksheel_Sajha_Party) merged into the party.[^cite_note-bibeksheel-sajha-merger-2025-1] The party also signed an agreement to merge with [Ujyaalo Nepal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujyaalo_Nepal_Party) and to bring [Kathmandu Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Kathmandu) [Balen Shah](70833999.md) into the party.[^cite_note-41] The agreement with Ujyaalo Nepal Party later collapsed and the merger did not go ahead.[^cite_note-42] The party won a near-supermajority of 182 out of 275 seats in the 2026 national elections, and its candidate Balen Shah became prime minister on 27 March 2026.[^cite_note-NationalHerald-18][^cite_note-43]

## Organization

The party had announced that it would have no [sister organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_organization) and only have members, not [cadres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadre_\(politics\)). It had also announced that [primary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisan_primary) would be held to select candidates for elections from the party.[^cite_note-JR-12][^cite_note-RL-11] The party selected candidates for the 2022 general election through a [primary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_election).[^cite_note-44] The party holds its [General Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Convention_of_the_Rastriya_Swatantra_Party?action=edit\&redlink=1) every 4–5 years.

## Ideology

The party supports [constitutional socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democratic_constitutions_with_references_to_socialism), [progressivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism), [secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism), [pragmatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatism), [participatory democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participatory_democracy), [social democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy), a [liberal economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_economy) with a [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state), [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism), and [political freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_freedom).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-\:0-46][^cite_note-rsp-ideology-social-democracy-47] The party has been described as [centrist](39619035.md) by scholars and different members within the party.[^cite_note-RS-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] In 2026, *[Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle)* referred to the party as "centrist-liberal."[^cite_note-DW-5] The RSP has also been regarded as a [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist) party.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

As a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) party,[^cite_note-JOL-6] the party strongly follows *Right to Recall* provisions for its elected MPs,[^cite_note-55] anti-corruption stands pledging to probe assets of public officeholders since 1990 alongside [social justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice) to marginalized communities, [meritocracy in civil services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocracy), unified health service transparency, envisioned road transport model, amendment of land acquisition and environmental clearance laws, single-window service delivery system to fast-track projects,[^cite_note-56] ‘*National Knowledge Bank’* to connect foreign experts with domestic sectors in commercial industries, digital-first governance and a strict oversight on [BFIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_institution) protecting small depositors and pension funds under its [political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_spectrum).[^cite_note-57]

## Policy platform

### Natural order of the economy

The party endorses an economy with individual rights, where industrial and commercial activities are off-limits to the government, which cannot interfere with economic relations between individuals, groups, classes, or entire nations. The party supports the idea that [economic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_freedom) will drive the economy to prosperity and that planning and interference by the government disrupt the economy and have adverse effects.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

### Electoral reform

It also supports the introduction of [recall elections](129203.md), the [right to reject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_reject) and a provision for [absentee ballots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absentee_ballot). The party also supports [directly electing](652563.md) the [prime minister](2888818.md) and directly electing [chief ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_minister_\(Nepal\)) for the seven provinces of Nepal.[^cite_note-RL-11][^cite_note-JR-12][^cite_note-62]

The party in its manifesto for the 2022 elections also supported the idea of a non-partisan [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Nepal) elected by an expanded electoral college which would incorporate elected representatives from ward chairs to federal lawmakers and also touted the idea of the chairman of the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Nepal\)) acting as the de facto vice-president.[^cite_note-63] The party also seeks to end political appointments in constitutional bodies and has called for the Constitutional Council to be scrapped, and for appointments to constitutional bodies be recommended by the National Assembly and confirmed by the House of Representatives instead. The party has also called for the dissolution of [provincial assemblies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_assemblies_of_Nepal), [district co-ordination committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Coordination_Committee) and has stood in favour of a provincial council which would be elected by the heads of local governments in the province. The party chair Rabi Lamichhane did not cast his vote at the [2022 provincial elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nepalese_provincial_elections).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

### Economic and social welfare

The party sees the [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector), [co-operatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-operatives) and the [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector) as the three pillars of the economy. The party favors [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_\(economics\)) and seeks to establish a growing role of the private sector as an alternative to the public sector in order to deliver services to the people and for economic growth. The party wants to end income tax for people in the lowest octile of earners in the country. The party also seeks to establish [food banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_bank) in every ward in the country and to guarantee nutrition assistance for people living under the poverty line by issuing them [ration cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ration_cards). The party also seeks to expand the current [social welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_welfare) system and to include private [insurance companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_companies) in the social welfare system.[^cite_note-RSP-66]

### Healthcare

The party supports the establishment of a [single-payer healthcare system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-payer_healthcare_system) and the establishment of at least one laboratory and a 750-bed hospital in each of the seven provinces, a 50 to 100-bed hospital in each of the 77 districts, a 6 to 25-bed hospital in each local unit and a clinic in each ward in the country. The party also wants to establish a centralized national [ambulance service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambulance_service), health and quality assurance accreditation board, a food and drug department, a central disease board and a health innovation & development board. The party also wants to create a health workers act for public healthcare workers.[^cite_note-RSP-66]

### Corruption

The party seeks to crackdown on [corruption](3608404.md) by introducing a Whistleblower Protection Act to protect [whistleblowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistleblower) and by establishing an Anti-Corruption Police under the [Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority](40082311.md).[^cite_note-RSP-66]

### Women and sexual minorities

The party has also called for the establishment of shelters for victims of abuse and the creation of a fund to help victims of [human trafficking](19333662.md), [domestic violence](18978563.md) and [acid attack](30862640.md) victims. The party also seeks to promote entrepreneurship among women and [sexual minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_minorities) by establishing funds for businesses where at least sixty percent of the shareholders or employees are female or of a sexual minority group.[^cite_note-RSP-66]
