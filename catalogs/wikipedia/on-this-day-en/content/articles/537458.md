# List of artificial objects on the Moon

&#x20;This is a partial list of artificial materials left on the [Moon](19331.md), many during the missions of the [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program). The table below does not include lesser Apollo mission artificial objects, such as a hammer and other tools, [retroreflectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_Ranging_Retroflector), [Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Packages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Lunar_Surface_Experiments_Package), or the commemorative, artistic, and personal objects left by the twelve [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Apollo) [astronauts](664.md), such as the [United States flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Flag_Assembly), the [commemorative plaques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_plaque) attached to the ladders of the six [Apollo Lunar Modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Lunar_Module), the silver [astronaut pin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_pin) left by [Alan Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Bean) in honor of [Clifton C. Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifton_C._Williams) whom he replaced, the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible) left by [David Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Scott), the *[Fallen Astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallen_Astronaut)* statuette and memorial plaque placed by the crew of [Apollo 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_15), the [Apollo 11 goodwill messages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11_goodwill_messages) disc, or the golf balls[^cite_note-1] [Alan Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Shepard) hit during an [Apollo 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_14) moonwalk.

A map depicting all known locations of artificial objects on the Moon from 1959 to 2025

Five [S-IVB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-IVB) third stages of [Saturn V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_V) rockets from the Apollo program crashed into the Moon, and are the heaviest human-made objects on the lunar surface. Humans have left over 187,400 kilograms (413,100 lb) of material on the Moon. Besides the 2019 [Chang'e 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_4) and [SLIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Lander_for_Investigating_Moon) missions, the only artificial objects on the Moon that are still in use are the retroreflectors for the [Lunar Laser Ranging experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Laser_Ranging_experiment) left there by the [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11), 14, and 15 astronauts, [Chandrayaan-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrayaan-3) lunar lander, and by the Soviet Union's [Lunokhod 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunokhod_1) and [Lunokhod 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunokhod_2) missions.[^cite_note-2]

Objects at greater than 90 degrees east or west are on the [far side of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_side_of_the_Moon), including [Ranger 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranger_4), [Lunar Orbiter 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Orbiter_1), [Lunar Orbiter 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Orbiter_2), [Lunar Orbiter 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Orbiter_3), [Chang'e 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_4) lander and *[Yutu-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yutu-2)* rover.

Because of increasing numbers of missions to and objects at the Moon, a global registry of lunar activities has been proposed in 2023 by the [Open Lunar Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Lunar_Foundation).[^cite_note-Open_Lunar_Foundation_2023-3]
