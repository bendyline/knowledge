# Timeline of the WNBA

&#x20; The following is a timeline of organizational changes in the [Women's National Basketball Association](57721.md) (WNBA), a women's professional [basketball](3921.md) league in the United States that began play in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_WNBA_season) with 8 teams and now comprises 15 teams (scheduled to expand to 18 by 2030). This article includes expansions, contractions, and relocations.[^cite_note-SI-1][^cite_note-2]

### Relocated teams

- [Orlando Miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Miracle) (1999–2002) – relocated to [Connecticut](6466.md), to become the [Connecticut Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Sun) (2003–2026), relocating to [Houston](13774.md), to become the [Houston Comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Comets) in 2027.

- [Utah Starzz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Starzz) (1997–2002) – relocated to [San Antonio](53848.md) to become the San Antonio Silver Stars (2003–2013), rebranded as the [San Antonio Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Stars) (2014–2017), and relocated again to [Paradise, Nevada](124384.md), to become the [Las Vegas Aces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Aces) (2018–present)

- [Detroit Shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Shock) (1998–2009) – relocated to [Tulsa, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa,_Oklahoma), to become the [Tulsa Shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa_Shock) (2010–2015) and then to [Arlington, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Texas), to become the [Dallas Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Wings) (2016–present)

### 2028–2030: Cleveland, Detroit, and Philadelphia to be added (18 teams)

On June 30, 2025, the WNBA announced that it would expand to [Cleveland](80322834.md) (2028), [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_WNBA_team) (2029), and [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_WNBA_team) (2030), bringing the total to 18 teams.[^cite_note-WNBA_expansion-11] Cleveland and Detroit previously were WNBA markets.

#### Background

The WNBA's exponential growth and popularity in recent years has led to rising expansion fees. The [Golden State Valkyries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Valkyries), the league’s 13th franchise that began play in May [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WNBA_season), paid \$50 million to join, while the [Toronto Tempo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Tempo) and [Portland Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Fire) — the 14th and 15th franchises scheduled to begin play in 2026 — paid \$115 million and \$125 million, respectively.[^cite_note-SBJ-12]

The deadline for the next expansion bids was January 30, 2025. At the time, the bids were believed to be for a 16th expansion team. League commissioner [Cathy Engelbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathy_Engelbert) had said she was aiming to have the league expand to 16 teams by 2028.[^cite_note-13] However, as expansion fees continued to rise, the league rethought its original plan of adding just one more expansion team. Each new team will pay \$250 million in franchise fees to join the league.[^cite_note-14]

#### Cleveland Sirens

[Cleveland](5951.md) was previously home to the [Rockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Rockers), one of the WNBA’s original eight members when the league began play in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_WNBA_season). However, the franchise folded after the [2003 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_WNBA_season) when former owner [Gordon Gund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Gund) could not sell the team due to tumbling revenue and erratic attendance.[^cite_note-SBJ-12]

The new team will play at [Rocket Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Arena), home of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md)'s (NBA) [Cleveland Cavaliers](72868.md).[^cite_note-Yahoo_Sports-15] On August 4, 2026, the team's name was revealed as the [Cleveland Sirens](80322834.md).[^cite_note-16]

#### Detroit

[Detroit](8687.md) was previously home to the [Shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Shock), one of the WNBA’s first expansion teams when it began play in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_WNBA_season). Between 1998 and [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_WNBA_season), the Shock won three [WNBA championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_Championship). However, the team’s low fan attendance led the franchise to relocate to [Tulsa, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa,_Oklahoma), in 2009, where it played until [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_WNBA_season). The franchise moved again in 2015, to [Arlington, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Texas), and are now called the [Dallas Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Wings).[^cite_note-17]

The new team will play at [Little Caesars Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Caesars_Arena), home of the NBA's [Detroit Pistons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Pistons).[^cite_note-Yahoo_Sports-15] The WNBA coincidentally filed a trademark application for the name “Detroit Shock” the same day [Tom Gores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Gores), owner of the Pistons, submitted his offer for a WNBA expansion team, a potential sign of the league’s intentions to revive the team name.[^cite_note-SBJ-12]

#### Philadelphia

[Philadelphia 76ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_76ers) owner [Josh Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Harris_\(businessman\)) of [Harris Blitzer Sports & Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_Blitzer_Sports_&_Entertainment) (HBSE), which also owns the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL), submitted a bid for an expansion team. When Harris partnered with [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast) on a [new arena in South Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Philadelphia_Arena), the priority was to house a WNBA team along with the NBA's [Philadelphia 76ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_76ers) and the NHL's [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers).[^cite_note-18]
