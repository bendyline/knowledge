The **United Arab Emirates Armed Forces** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): القوات المسلحة لدولة الإمارات العربية المتحدة, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Al-Quwwāt al-Musallaḥa li-Dawlat al-ʾImārāt al-ʿArabīyyah al-Muttaḥidah*) are the armed forces of the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md). They consist of the [UAE Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_Army), [UAE Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_Navy), [UAE Air Force](10815770.md), [UAE Presidential Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_Presidential_Guard), UAE Joint Aviation Command, and the UAE National Guard.

They were nicknamed "Little Sparta", a nickname by former [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) [General](2965165.md) and [Secretary of Defense](43998.md) [James Mattis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Mattis), due to their active and effective military role and power projection in the surrounding region compared to their relative size.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## History



A falcon was added to the original Trucial Oman Levies insignia to signify the union of the emirates and formation of a force.

The United Arab Emirates military was formed from the [Trucial Oman Levies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trucial_Oman_Levies), which was established on 11 May 1951 and was renamed the Trucial Oman Scouts in 1956.[^cite_note-9] The Trucial Oman Scouts was turned over to the United Arab Emirates as the nucleus of its defense forces in 1971 with the formation of UAE and was absorbed into a united military called the Union Defence Force (UDF). The Union Defence Force was established officially as the military of the United Arab Emirates on 27 December 1971 from a directive issued by [Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayed_bin_Sultan_Al_Nahyan).[^cite_note-GHQ-10]

As the Union Defence Force, every emirate was responsible for the equipment and training of its defense forces. In the event of an attack on any one of the seven emirates, the Union Defence Force would be mobilized from every emirate to defend the emirate under attack. In 1974, the name was changed to the Federal Armed Forces. On 6 May 1976, the Federal Armed Forces were unified as a single body. May 6 is celebrated annually as the Military Union Day. As a result of the union of forces, the number of personnel formed a [brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade) and were referred to as the Yarmouk Brigade.[^cite_note-GHQ-10] After the union of the armed forces in 1976, the Yarmouk Brigade was officially renamed the United Arab Emirates Armed Forces. In 1976 the official UAE Armed Forces insignia, uniform, military academies, air force, and naval force were established and the military General Headquarters (GHQ) was formed in [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md).[^cite_note-GHQ-10] UAE Armed Forces are equipped with weapon systems purchased from a variety of outside countries, including France, the United States, and the United Kingdom. Some officers are graduates of the [United Kingdom](31717.md)'s [Royal Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy_Sandhurst) at [Sandhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhurst,_Berkshire), with others having attended the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) at [West Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Point,_New_York), the [Royal Military College, Duntroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_College,_Duntroon), and [St Cyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_sp%C3%A9ciale_militaire_de_Saint-Cyr), the military academy of [France](5843419.md).

The United Arab Emirates Armed Forces participated in multiple conflicts, including those in the Middle East. From 1977 to 1979, the UAE Army contributed 750 men to the [Arab Deterrent Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Deterrent_Force) peacekeeping mission in Lebanon.[^cite_note-Yates_2020_329-11] During 1990–1991, the Armed Forces participated in the first [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War). 10 UAE soldiers lost their lives in liberating Kuwait.[^cite_note-Yates_2020_329-11] UAE Armed Forces were deployed in Eastern Europe and joined NATO's [Kosovo Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Force) peacekeeping mission, undertaking aid missions to thousands of fleeing refugees on the Albanian border. This was the first time the UAE troops' uniform was switched to the woodland camouflage compared to their home desert camouflage.[^cite_note-kfor-12] UAE Armed Forces participated in the peacekeeping mission in Somalia from 1993-94. The UAE Presidential Guards were deployed to maintain security in [War in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan) against the Taliban. In March 2011, UAE joined the enforcement of the [no-fly-zone over Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Odyssey_Dawn) by sending six [F-16](11642.md) and six [Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) [multi-role fighter aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft)[^cite_note-13] and in 2015 joined the Saudi-led coalition intervention in Yemen by sending 30 UAEAF F-16 Desert Falcons to Yemen. The intervention was followed by UAE ground troops in Southern Yemen, focusing on targeting "terrorist" cells such as Al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula and Islamic State.

UAE introduced a mandatory [military conscription](5735.md) of 16 months for adult males in 2014 to expand its reserve force.[^cite_note-14] The date of the first death in the line of duty of a UAE soldier was on 30 November 1971 during the [Seizure of Abu Musa and the Greater and Lesser Tunbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizure_of_Abu_Musa_and_the_Greater_and_Lesser_Tunbs), which is celebrated annually as the [Commemoration Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commemoration_Day). The largest loss of life in the history of the UAE military occurred on Friday, 4 September 2015, when 52 soldiers were killed in the [Marib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'rib) area of Yemen by an [Tochka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OTR-21_Tochka) missile that targeted a weapons cache and caused an explosion.[^cite_note-\:52-15] Names of UAE soldiers who died in the line of duty are inscribed in the UAE Armed Forces memorial, the [Oasis of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahat_Al_Karama), in Abu Dhabi.

Since 1965, the UAE has hired foreign veterans to advise and train its Armed Forces as well as private military contractors.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] A report released in October 2022 revealed that several retired US military personnel work as military contractors or consultants for the UAE. The report obtained through the [Freedom of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_\(United_States\)) revealed that in seven years, nearly 280 American military veterans sought federal permission to work for the Emirates. Hundreds of US military veterans were also known to have been hired by the UAE government or state-owned firms. Experts claimed that the Emirati military was the Arab world's most powerful due to the influx of American veterans. UAE ambassador to the US, [Yousef Al Otaiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousef_Al_Otaiba), said the US played a crucial role in the Emirates' progress and security. However, the extent of Emirati dependence on US military contractors is not fully known.[^cite_note-18]

## Branches

### Army

Main article: [United Arab Emirates Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_Army)

As part of the military of the United Arab Emirates, the Army (called Land Forces in Arabic) is responsible for land and ground-based operations.

- Medical Corps form part of the Army and are responsible for military medical support to the rest of the UAE Armed Forces.

### Navy

Main article: [United Arab Emirates Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_Navy)

As of 2024, the United Arab Emirates consisted of 3,000 personnel[^cite_note-19] and 79 vessels.[^cite_note-20] In the 2000s, the Navy underwent a modernization program, transitioning from a small coastal force made up of aging decommissioned Western ships[^cite_note-21] to a force capable of sustaining blue-water operations.[^cite_note-22] As of 2026, Major General Hamidi al-Rumaithi is the commander of the force.[^cite_note-23]

- United Arab Emirates Marines – UAE maintained a battalion-sized Marine force called UAE Marines until 2011, when it was merged into UAE-PG.

- [United Arab Emirates Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_Coast_Guard) – a coast guard agency of the United Arab Emirates, and is primarily responsible for the protection of the UAE coastline through regulation of maritime laws, maintenance of seamarks, border control, anti-smuggling operations, and other services.

### Air Force

Main article: [United Arab Emirates Air Force](10815770.md)

The [United Arab Emirates Air Force](10815770.md) has about 4,000 personnel as of 2017.[^cite_note-24] The air force agreed in 1999 to purchase 80 [US](3434750.md) [F-16](11642.md) multirole fighter aircraft. Next to that, they included other equipment such as 60 [Mirage 2000s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_2000), British [Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Hawk) aircraft, and French [helicopters](8286923.md). The air defense has a [Hawk missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIM-23_Hawk) program for which the United States has been providing training. The UAE has taken delivery of two out of five Triad I-Hawk batteries.

- The [United Arab Emirates Air Force](10815770.md) is responsible for protecting the country's airspace and civil defense aircraft.

### Presidential Guard

Main article: [UAE Presidential Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE_Presidential_Guard)

The United Arab Emirates Presidential Guard (UAE-PG) was formed in 2011 by merging the Amiri Guard, Special Operations Command, and the Marine Battalion from the UAE Navy. UAE requested that training support be provided by the U.S. Marine Corps (USMC).[^cite_note-25] The U.S. State Department approved a foreign military sales (FMS) Training Case for UAE-PG in October 2011. Marine Corps Training Mission UAE (MCTM-UAE) operates under chief of mission authority as a Title 22 FMS training case.[^cite_note-26]

### Gulf War

Main article: [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War)

UAE sent forces to assist [Kuwait](7515890.md) during the 1990–1991 [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) where some hundred UAE troops participated in the conflict as part of the [GCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperation_Council_for_the_Arab_States_of_the_Gulf) [Peninsula Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula_Shield) force that advanced into [Kuwait City](57021.md).[^cite_note-GW-27] UAE air force carried out strikes against Iraqi forces. UAE Armed Forces participated in the coalition with an army battalion along with a squadron of [Dassault Mirage 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_5) and [Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_2000).[^cite_note-GW-27] 6 UAE troops were killed in action.[^cite_note-28]

### United Nations Operation in Somalia II

Main article: [UNOSOM II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNOSOM_II)

UAE Armed Forces participated in [UNOSOM II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNOSOM_II), which was an intervention launched in March 1993 until March 1995, and committed resources to the United Nations mission.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

### Lebanon

See also: [South Lebanon conflict (1985–2000)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_conflict_\(1985%E2%80%932000\))

UAE military field engineers arrived in [Lebanon](17771.md) on 8 September 2007 in [Beirut](37428.md) to clear areas of south Lebanon from [mines](18172.md) and [cluster bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_bombs).

### War in Afghanistan

Main article: [War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)](19666611.md)

UAE Armed Forces were deployed in 2003 to Afghanistan, mainly to support construction. UAE special forces would establish [fire support base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_support_base) around UAE-supported projects which included funding tarmac roads, clinics, a Pashtun radio station, and a mast provided by [e&](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E&)(formerly [Etisalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etisalat)) which provided competition for other mobile networks in [Helmand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmand).[^cite_note-31] Their activities include driving into "remote and impoverished" Afghan villages, distributing aid and sitting down with the village "elders" to inquire about their needs. They would then fund projects while the contracts went out to local tender. UAE Armed Forces used their ties to Islam and ability to fund projects to try to reduce the local suspicion of NATO in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-32]

### Saudi-led intervention in Yemen

Main article: [Saudi Arabian-led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen)

In 2015, the UAE participated in the [Saudi Arabian-led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen) to influence the outcome of the [Yemeni Civil War (2015–)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_\(2015%E2%80%93present\)).[^cite_note-33] During this war, Emirati special forces conducted an amphibious assault on [Aden](38050.md), which, according to [the Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist), impressed observers in the West.[^cite_note-34] On 4 September 2015, 52 UAE soldiers (together with 10 Saudi and 5 Bahraini soldiers) were killed when a [Houthi](17240332.md) missile hit an ammunition dump at a military base in [Ma'rib Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'rib_Governorate),[^cite_note-35] marking the "highest death toll on the battlefield in the country's history".[^cite_note-36]

In 2016, during the [Battle of Mukalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mukalla_\(2016\)), UAE Armed Forces liberated the [port of Mukalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Mukalla) from [AQAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Arabian_Peninsula) forces in 36 hours after being held by AQAP for more than a year with the US defense secretary James Mattis calling the UAE led operation a model for American troops.[^cite_note-37] In 2018, the [Associated Press](18935732.md) in a report mentioned that UAE struck deals with AQAP militants by recruiting them against fighting the Houthis and providing them with money. The report continued to state that the United States was aware of Al-Qaeda joining ranks with UAE and has held off drone strikes against Al-Qaeda.[^cite_note-38] UAE Brigadier General Musallam Al Rashidi responded to the report by stating that Al Qaeda cannot be reasoned with in the first place stating that "There's no point in negotiating with these guys."[^cite_note-39] The UAE military stated that accusations of allowing AQAP to leave with cash contradict its primary objective of depriving AQAP of its financial strength.[^cite_note-TIDx-40] The notion of Al Qaeda joining ranks with UAE Armed Forces and the United States holding off drone strikes against Al Qaeda has been denied by [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) with Colonel Robert Manning, spokesperson of the Pentagon, calling the news source "patently false".[^cite_note-auto-41] According to The Independent, AQAP activity on social media as well as the number of reported attacks conducted by it has decreased since UAE intervention.[^cite_note-TIDx-40]

On 30 April 2018 the UAE armed forces, as part of the Saudi-led intervention in [Yemen](350939.md), [landed troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_takeover_of_Socotra) on the island of [Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra).[^cite_note-42] [The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent) newspaper reported that UAE has politically annexed the island and built a communications network, and conducted census and provided Socotra residents with free healthcare and work permits in [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md).[^cite_note-43] On 14 May 2018, a deal was brokered between UAE and Yemen for a joint military training exercise and the return of administrative control of Socotra's airport and seaport to Yemen.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

In June 2018, an offensive was carried out by UAE-led troops in [Hodeidah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hudaydah).[^cite_note-47] In June 2019, the UAE announced a partial withdrawal of its troops by reducing armed forces fighting in [Yemen](350939.md). An official from the UAE called the move a "strategic" redeployment.[^cite_note-48] According to a [Reuters](18998750.md) report, the Gulf nation ordered the withdrawal of its troops following security concerns, after tensions with [Iran](14653.md). UAE stated that it is shifting its focus from Houthi rebels to [ISIS](9087364.md) and [al-Qaeda](1921.md) in Yemen.[^cite_note-49]

### Islamic State

In 2015, the UAE Air Force targeted Islamic State targets in Syria. One of them was [Major Mariyam Al Mansouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariam_Al_Mansouri), the first female UAE Air Force pilot.[^cite_note-50]

### Somalia

UAE forces have assisted the military of [Somalia](27358.md) against [al-Shabaab](8726463.md) forces in the country. In February 2024, three UAE soldiers were killed in an attack against the Gordon Base in Mogadishu, perpetrated by a former Somali soldier recruited by Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-51]

## Expansion



[UAEAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAEAF) C-17A Globemaster III

In 1989, the UAE purchased [Scud-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwasong-5) ballistic missiles from North Korea.[^cite_note-52] UAE went on an expansion drive in 1995, which began with the 1992–93 acquisition of 436 [Leclerc tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leclerc_tank) and 415 [BMP-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-3) armored vehicles. It had learned from the Iranian experiences with having a single supplier for its military and has diversified its arms purchases, purchasing weaponry mainly from [Russia](25391.md), the [United States](3434750.md), the [UK](31717.md), [Ukraine](31750.md), [France](5843419.md), [Italy](14532.md) and [Germany](11867.md). It has also taken care to invest in the systems it has purchased and standardize them according to NATO/GCC Specifications. The equipment purchases were also followed by a program to increase manpower numbers and [Emiratisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiratisation) program for the Armed forces.

In 2008, UAE bought [MIM-104 Patriot](52024.md) missiles[^cite_note-defenseindustrydaily.com-53] and related radar, support services for the Patriot systems.

In 2011, during a war scare with Iran over the [Straits of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Hormuz), the UAE announced a purchase of US\$3.48 billion worth of American missile systems: 2 radar systems, 96 missiles, spare parts, and training.[^cite_note-defenseindustrydaily.com-53] UAE was the first country to acquire the [Terminal High Altitude Area Defense System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_High_Altitude_Area_Defense_System) (THAAD). A contract worth \$1.96 billion was agreed for Lockheed Martin Corp to supply two THAAD anti-missile batteries.[^cite_note-54]

In November 2019, [South Africa](17416221.md) blocked the supply of arms to [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Oman](22316.md), [Algeria](358.md), and [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) following a dispute in the inspection clause of its agreement. According to a report by [Reuters](18998750.md), the UAE and the other mentioned countries refused to allow officials from South Africa to inspect their facilities. The dispute arose as the UAE and the other countries refused the inspections, stating that they violated their sovereignty. According to the industry, the inspection row puts business at risk and could cause the loss of up to 9,000 jobs at defense firms and supporting industries in South Africa. UAE began firing trials with China, India, and Serbia to replace the South African RDM as the preferred supplier of ammunition.[^cite_note-55]

## Industry



Battle tested UAE manufactured [APC](19278686.md) [Nimr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimr_\(armored_personnel_carrier\)) restored from [Operation Decisive Storm](46230181.md) on display in [IDEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Defence_Exhibition) 2017

While the UAE is a net importer of military equipment, the last decade saw an expansion of the local industry.[^cite_note-56] Projects are spearheaded by the [Edge Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_Group), an umbrella company that absorbed several local industries and investment funds to streamline the Emirate defense industry.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

The [Abu Dhabi Ship Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Dhabi_Ship_Building) (ADSB) produces a range of ships and is a contractor in the [Baynunah Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baynunah_class_corvette), a program to design, develop, and produce 5–6 corvettes customized for operation in the waters of the Arabian Gulf. It has produced ammunition, military transport vehicles, and unmanned aerial vehicles. A joint venture agreement was signed in Abu Dhabi on 28 November 2007 between Tawazun Holding LLC, an investment company established by the Offset Program Bureau (OPB), Al-Jaber Trading Establishment, part of Al-Jaber Group, and Rheinmetall Munitions Systems, to set up the Al-Burkan munition factory at the Zayed Military City in Abu Dhabi. OPB signed four Memorandums of Understanding with companies from [Europe](9239.md) and [Singapore](27318.md) at the Paris Eurosatory 2008 defense exhibition on June 20, [Rheinmetall Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinmetall) and [Diehl Defence Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diehl_Defence) of [Germany](11867.md), Singapore Technologies Engineering ([ST Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ST_Engineering)), and [Thales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group) of [France](5843419.md). Tawazun has partnered with [Saab](26968.md) on radar development.[^cite_note-59]
