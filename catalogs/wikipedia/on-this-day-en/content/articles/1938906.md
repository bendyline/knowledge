The **mass media in the Republic of Ireland** includes all the media and communications outlets of the Republic.

## Print media

See also: [List of newspapers in the Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland)

Ireland has a traditionally a competitive print media, which is divided into daily national newspapers and weekly regional newspapers, as well as national Sunday editions. Competition from international markets is also strong in Ireland many publications from the US, the UK and Central Europe are widely available in Ireland. The strength of the UK press is a unique feature of the Irish print media scene, with the availability of a wide selection of British published newspapers and magazines, many of these UK editions produce specialist versions for the Irish market e.g. *Irish Daily Mail* and the *Irish Sun*.[^cite_note-Media_Landscape-1] Some of the most popular national newspapers include *[The Irish Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irish_Times)*, the *[Irish Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Independent)* and the *[Irish Examiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Examiner)*. Local and regional papers include *[The Kerryman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kerryman)*, the *[Evening Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Herald)* and the *[Evening Echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Echo)*. The use of digitised versions of newspapers and websites is increasingly becoming popular, however readership amongst physical newspapers is still high in Ireland when compared to other European countries.

Newspapers are popular in Ireland. According to the National Newspapers of Ireland and Joint National Readership Survey 91% of Irish adults regularly read newspapers.[^cite_note-2] The [market penetration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper_circulation) for daily newspapers runs at 190% and 350% for Sunday titles. For comparison, US newspaper market penetration is only 51%.

There are several daily newspapers in Ireland, including the *[Irish Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Independent)*, *[The Irish Examiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irish_Examiner)*, *[The Irish Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irish_Times)*, *[Irish Daily Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star_\(Ireland\))*, and the *[Evening Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Herald)*. The best selling of these is the *Irish Independent*, which is published in both tabloid and [broadsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadsheet) formats.

The leading Sunday newspaper in terms of circulation is the *Sunday Independent* which has over a million readers each week, a very large number considering that Ireland has only 1.25 million households. Other popular papers include *[The Sunday Business Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Business_Post)*, *[Irish Mail on Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Mail_on_Sunday)* and the *[Sunday World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_World)* (the latter published in separate editions for the Republic and Northern Ireland).

One noted trend in Irish newspapers is the presence of Irish editions of UK newspapers, these include *[The Irish Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irish_Sun)*, *[Irish Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mirror)*, and the Irish edition of *[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Times_\(UK\))*.

## Linear and digital broadcasting

[RTÉ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89) is the [public service broadcaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_service_broadcaster) of Ireland and is funded by a licence fee and advertising.[^cite_note-3] RTÉ operates four national television channels; [RTÉ One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_One), [RTÉ2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%892), [RTÉ News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_News_\(TV_channel\)) and [RTÉ KIDSjr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_KIDSjr). [Virgin Media Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Media_Television_\(Ireland\)) operates four channels ([Virgin Media One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Media_One), [Virgin Media Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Media_Two), [Virgin Media Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Media_Three) and [Virgin Media Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Media_Four)) and remains Ireland's biggest independent broadcasters.

[TG4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TG4) is a public service broadcaster for [Irish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language) speakers. All of these channels are available on [Saorview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saorview), the national [free-to-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-to-air) [digital terrestrial television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_terrestrial_television) service.[^cite_note-4] HD variants of RTÉ One and RTÉ2 are available on Saorview, as are [timeshift variants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeshift_channel) (+1 channels).

Subscription services include [Virgin Media Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Media_Ireland) (formerly UPC Ireland), [Eir (telecommunications)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eir_\(telecommunications\)) and [Sky Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Ireland), which offer a wide variety of television channels from outside of Ireland, increasing competition for Irish broadcasters. The presence of on-demand streaming services such as [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) have also increased pressure on private and public broadcasters in Ireland.

Most of the major broadcasters in Ireland operate streaming services, such as [RTÉ Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Player), [Virgin Media Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Media_Player) and [TG4 Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TG4_Player). Some Irish owned channels are subscription only; these include [Eir Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eir_Sport) channels.

## Radio broadcasting

Radio listenership is still very high, in Ireland with 83% of Irish adults tuning into radio each day.[^cite_note-5]

There are many national radio services operated by public broadcasters ([RTÉ Radio 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Radio_1), [RTÉ 2fm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_2fm), [RTÉ lyric fm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_lyric_fm) and [RTÉ Raidió na Gaeltachta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Raidi%C3%B3_na_Gaeltachta)) and private broadcasters ([Today FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_FM), [Newstalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newstalk) and [Ireland's Classic Hits Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland's_Classic_Hits_Radio).

Until 31 March 2021, a [national DAB service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Radio_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland) was also available, which was largely made up of RTÉ's stations but included digital-only stations [RTÉ 2XM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_2XM), [RTÉ Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Gold), [RTÉ Pulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Pulse), [RTÉ Radio 1 Extra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Radio_1_Extra) and [RTÉ Jr Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Jr_Radio). Since the closure of the DAB multiplex, these stations are available via other means, such as Saorview and the internet.

A large number of regional and local radio stations are available countrywide. A survey showed that a consistent 85% of adults listen to a mixture of national, regional and local stations on a daily basis.[^cite_note-6]

Radio and television broadcasters are regulated by the [Coimisiún na Meán](73295318.md).

## Film and television industry

Supported by the Screen Ireland (formerly [Irish Film Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Film_Board)), the Irish film ad television industry has grown significantly since the 1990s, with the promotion of indigenous films such as *[Intermission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermission_\(film\))* and *[Breakfast on Pluto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakfast_on_Pluto_\(film\))*, as well as the attraction of international productions such as *[Braveheart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braveheart)* and *[Saving Private Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saving_Private_Ryan)*. Many television shows such as the [Game of Thrones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_of_Thrones), [The Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings_\(2013_TV_series\)) and other international shows have been produced/filmed in the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland.

## Online

Along with the online websites from the traditional media (newspapers, magazines, TV and radio), some of which complement their traditional offerings, there are a number of online media outlets and portals, such as [TheJournal.ie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheJournal.ie), [Joe.ie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe.ie), [The Ditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ditch_\(website\)) (one of whose founders is [Paddy Cosgrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_Cosgrave)), Dublin Live and [Irish Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Central) (aimed at the Irish emigrant community founded by [Niall O'Dowd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niall_O'Dowd) of the *[Irish Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Voice)* in the US).

## Music

[The Cranberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cranberries) are a famous rock band from Ireland.[^cite_note-7] Their lead singer, [Dolores O'Riordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolores_O'Riordan), died in 2018, causing The Cranberries to disband; one of their most popular songs is *[Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_\(The_Cranberries_song\))*, frequently used in the Irish TV show *[Derry Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derry_Girls)*.[^cite_note-8]

[u2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2) is another rock band from Ireland.
