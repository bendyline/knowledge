**Huntingdon** is a [market town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town) in the [Huntingdonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdonshire) district of [Cambridgeshire](6288.md), England. The town was given its [town charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_charter) by [King John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John,_King_of_England) in 1205. It was the [county town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_town) of the historic county of Huntingdonshire. [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) was born there in 1599[^cite_note-EB1911-2] and became one of its [Members of Parliament](3776360.md) (MP) in 1628. The former [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) [Prime Minister](24150.md) (1990–1997) [John Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Major) served as its MP from [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_United_Kingdom_general_election) until his retirement in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_general_election).

## History

During the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), in 274, a massive [coin hoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_hoard) dating to the reign of [Tetricus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetricus_I) and Roman Emperor [Aurelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelian) was hidden in a field near the town. Consisting of 9,724 [Roman coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_coins), and discovered in 2018, the [Muddy Hoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muddy_hoard) is considered to date the largest treasure trove of Cambridgeshire.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

Huntingdon was founded by the [Anglo-Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons) and [Danes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danes_\(Germanic_tribe\)). It is first mentioned in the [Anglo-Saxon Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle) in 921, where it appears as *Huntandun*. It appears as *Huntedun* in the [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book) of 1086. The name means "The huntsman's hill" or possibly "Hunta's hill".[^cite_note-5]

Huntingdon seems to have been a staging post for Danish raids outside [East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Anglia) until 917, when the Danes moved to [Tempsford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempsford), now in [Bedfordshire](69769.md), before they were crushed by [Edward the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_the_Elder). It prospered successively as a bridging point of the [River Great Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Great_Ouse), a market town, and in the 18th and 19th centuries a [coaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach) centre, notably at the *George Hotel*. The town has [a well-preserved medieval bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Bridge,_Huntingdon) that used to serve as the main route of [Ermine Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ermine_Street) over the river. The bridge only ceased to be the sole crossing point to [Godmanchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godmanchester) in 1975, with the building of what is now the [A1307](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1307_road) (formerly [A14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A14_road_\(Great_Britain\))) [bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bypass_route).



Sebastopol cannon

The town's valuable trading position was secured by [Huntingdon Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdon_Castle), of which only the earthworks of the [motte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motte) survive. The site is a [Scheduled Ancient Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Ancient_Monument) and home to a [beacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beacon) used to mark the 400th anniversary of the [Spanish Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armada).

In 1746, the nurserymen Wood and Ingram of nearby [Brampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brampton,_Cambridgeshire) developed an [elm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elm)-tree cultivar, *[Ulmus × hollandica 'Vegeta'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulmus_%C3%97_hollandica_'Vegeta')*, which they named the Huntingdon Elm after the town.[^cite_note-6]

Original documents on Huntingdon's history, including the borough [charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter) of 1205, are held by [Cambridgeshire Archives and Local Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_Archives_and_Local_Studies) at the County Record Office, Huntingdon.[^cite_note-7]

Parts of Huntingdon, including the centre, were struck by [an F1/T3 tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_United_Kingdom_tornado_outbreak) on 23 November 1981, during a record-breaking nationwide tornado outbreak on that day.[^cite_note-8] The centre suffered moderate damage.

Between the railway station and the old hospital building, stands a replica cannon installed in the 1990s to replace one from the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), scrapped for the war effort in the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). However, it faces in the opposite direction from the original. [St Mary's Street drill hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Street_drill_hall,_Huntingdon) was built in the late 19th century.[^cite_note-9]

### George Hotel

The George Hotel on the corner of High Street and George Street was once a posting house. It was named after [Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George) of England in 1574 and bought some 25 years later by Henry Cromwell, grandfather of [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell).[^cite_note-EB1911-2] King [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England) made the George his headquarters during the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War) in 1645. Later the highwayman [Dick Turpin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Turpin) is said to have been a customer when it was a coaching inn on the [Great North Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_North_Road_\(Great_Britain\)). A theatre was built to the rear of the George in about 1799. The Lincoln company of actors managed by [Thomas Shaftoe Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Shaftoe_Robertson) and later [Fanny Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanny_Robertson) performed here in race weeks.[^cite_note-10] Two wings of the inn burnt down in the mid-19th century, but two were saved, including one with a balcony overlooking the yard. Since 1959, the courtyard and balcony have been used for [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare) performances by a company run by the Shakespeare at the George Trust. These performances took place until 2024 when the Green King company who run the George Hotel decided it was not in their best interest to continue Shakespeare at the George, ending its 65-year run.[^cite_note-11]

## Government



Huntingdon welcome sign

Huntingdon has a town council with 19 councillors elected every four years.[^cite_note-12] Two of them serve also as mayor and deputy mayor.[^cite_note-13] Meetings are normally held once a month at [Huntingdon Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdon_Town_Hall).[^cite_note-14]

Since boundary changes in 2018,[^cite_note-15] four [Huntingdonshire District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdonshire_District_Council_elections) wards cover parts of Huntingdon: *Huntingdon North*, *Huntingdon East, The Stukeleys* (covering Stukeley Meadows), and *Brampton* (covering Hinchingbrooke).[^cite_note-OSelec-16] The three wards each elect two or three councillors.[^cite_note-17] The main offices of Huntingdonshire District Council are in Huntingdon itself.

The third tier of local government is [Cambridgeshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_County_Council) providing county-wide services such as roads, education, social services, libraries and heritage protection.[^cite_note-18] Following changes to electoral boundaries in 2017,[^cite_note-19] Huntingdon is covered by three county divisions, *Huntingdon North and Hartford, Huntingdon West,* and *Huntingdon South and Godmanchester,* each electing one county councillor.[^cite_note-CCC1-20]

The fourth tier of local government is [Cambridgeshire and Peterborough Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_and_Peterborough_Combined_Authority), which is headed by a mayor. The Mayor of Cambridgeshire & Peterborough as of May 2025 is [Paul Bristow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Bristow) (Conservative).[^cite_note-combined-21]

Huntingdon lies in the [parliamentary constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_constituency) of [the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdon_\(constituency\)) (formerly [Huntingdonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdonshire_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\))). [Ben Obese-Jecty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Obese-Jecty) [MP](3776360.md) [(Conservative)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) was elected to this seat in the [House of Commons](13828.md) in 2024, replacing [Jonathan Djanogly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Djanogly).[^cite_note-OSelec-16]

## Geography

Situated approximately midway between the cities of [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) and [Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough), the town lies on the north bank of the [River Great Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Great_Ouse) opposite [Godmanchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godmanchester) and close to the market town of [St Ives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Ives,_Cambridgeshire) to the east and the village of [Brampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brampton,_Cambridgeshire) to the west. Huntingdon incorporates the village of [Hartford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Cambridgeshire) to the east and the developing areas of Oxmoor, Stukeley Meadows and Hinchingbrooke to the north and west.

Between Godmanchester, Huntingdon and Brampton lies [Portholme Meadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portholme), England's largest.[^cite_note-22] Its 257 acres (104 hectares) contain many rare species of grass, flowers and [dragonfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonfly). It is the only known British habitat of the marsh dandelion. It acts as a natural reservoir for water in times of flood, enabling the river to run off slowly, so helping to preclude flooding in nearby towns. It has also served as a horse racecourse and once was a centre for aviation.

### Business

Huntingdon is home to many local businesses, including [Huntingdon Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdon_Racecourse). Hinchingbrooke Business Park also contains offices and warehouses.

### Climate

The nearest weather station for long-term data is at [RAF Wyton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Wyton), 3 mi (5 km) north-east of the town centre. More recently Monks Wood, 5 mi (8 km) to the north-west, has also provided data.

Like most of Britain, Huntingdon has a temperate, maritime climate free of temperature extremes, with rainfall spread fairly evenly over the year. The absolute maximum recorded at Wyton was 35.4 °C (95.7 °F)[^cite_note-23] in August 1990; the temperature at Monks Wood rose in July 2022 to 39.8 °C (103.6 °F).[^cite_note-24] The mean annual warmest day is 29.7 °C (85.5 °F),[^cite_note-25] and on 16 days a year will rise to 25.1 °C (77.2 °F) or above.[^cite_note-26]

Typically 46.5 nights of the year report an air frost.[^cite_note-auto-27] The absolute minimum at Wyton was −16.1 °C (3.0 °F)[^cite_note-28] in January 1982. The mean for the annual coldest night of the year is −7.7 °C (18.1 °F).[^cite_note-29]

With annual rainfall around 600 millimetres (23+1⁄2 inches) a year,[^cite_note-auto-27] the Huntingdon area is among the driest in the UK—111.1 days on average record at least 1 mm of rain.[^cite_note-auto-27] Averages mentioned refer to a mix of the period 1971–2000 and 1991-2020.

## Demography

### Population

Between 1801 and 1901, the current area of Huntingdon consisted of four parishes: Huntingdon All Saints, Huntingdon St Benedict, Huntingdon St John and Huntingdon St Mary. The populations of these were counted in the ten-year [UK census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_in_the_United_Kingdom) and ranged in the period between 2,368 in 1801 and 4,735 in 1891.[^cite_note-Camin-33] (The census was omitted in 1941.)

## Culture and community



The [Old Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Bridge,_Huntingdon) across the [Great Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Great_Ouse), to [Godmanchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godmanchester)&#x20;

The former [Literary and Scientific Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_and_Scientific_Institute,_Huntingdon) is now Commemoration Hall.

Following the 2013 closure of [RAF Brampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Brampton), once home to Headquarters [RAF Support Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Support_Command), there are two operational [RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF) stations within 4 mi (6 km) of the town: [RAF Wyton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Wyton), once a major flying station but now a facility of the [Defence Equipment and Support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Equipment_and_Support) arm of the MOD; and [RAF Alconbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Alconbury) currently occupied by the [United States Air Force](32090.md).

### Legends

[Hinchingbrooke House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinchingbrooke_House), once a convent, is said to be haunted. The bridge over the Alconbury Brook named Nun's Bridge is said also to be haunted, by one of the nuns who once lived at the convent.[^cite_note-34] She is said often to be accompanied by another ghost that resembles a nurse. The myth goes that the nun had a monk lover who caused them to be murdered.

## Media

Local news and television programmes are provided by [BBC East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_East) and [ITV Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Anglia). Television signals are received from the [Sandy Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Heath_transmitting_station) TV transmitter.[^cite_note-35]

Local radio stations are [BBC Radio Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Cambridgeshire), [Heart East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_East), [Greatest Hits Radio East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits_Radio_East), [Star Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Radio_\(Cambridge_and_Ely\)) and [HCR FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdon_Community_Radio), a community based station that broadcast from the town.[^cite_note-36]

The Hunts Post is the town's local weekly newspaper.[^cite_note-37]

## Education

The local primary schools are Hartford Junior School, Huntingdon Primary School, Thongsley Fields Primary School, St John's Primary School, Stukeley Meadows Primary School and Cromwell Academy Primary School. Spring Common School is a special-needs school. Secondary schools include [St Peter's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Peter's_School,_Huntingdon) and [Hinchingbrooke School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinchingbrooke_School). Further education colleges include [Huntingdonshire Regional College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdonshire_Regional_College), Hinchingbrooke School sixth-form college and St Peter's sixth form.

## Transport

### Railway



Huntingdon station

[Huntingdon railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntingdon_railway_station) is sited on the [East Coast Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_Main_Line). Services that stop here are operated by [Govia Thameslink Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govia_Thameslink_Railway), on the [Thameslink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thameslink) and [Great Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_route) routes.

Great Northern services operate between [Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough_railway_station) and [London Kings Cross station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_King's_Cross_railway_station); trains take just over an hour to reach the capital. Thameslink services run between Peterborough and [Horsham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsham_railway_station), in [West Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sussex), via [St Pancras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Pancras_railway_station) and [Blackfriars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackfriars_station).[^cite_note-38]

### Buses

Bus services are operated primarily by [Stagecoach East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_East) and [Whippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whippet_\(bus_company\)). Routes serve the town, including [Hinchingbrooke Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinchingbrooke_Hospital), and connect Huntingdon with Peterborough, St Neots, Ramsey, St Ives and Cambridge.[^cite_note-39]

### Air

[Luton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Luton_Airport) and [Stansted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stansted_Airport) airports are located within 40 miles (64 km) of the town.

## Religious sites



All Saints' Church, from Market Square

There are four [Church of England](5955.md) churches in Huntingdon; once there were more, which together with those in the adjacent villages Great and Little Stukeley are members of the Huntingdon Team Ministry[^cite_note-40] in the [Diocese of Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Ely). The four are [All Saints'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Saints'_Church,_Huntingdon) (next to the Market Square), [St Mary's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Church,_Huntingdon) (opposite Pathfinder House), St Barnabas (on the Oxmoor estate) and All Saints', [Hartford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Cambridgeshire).

Huntingdon [Methodist Church](20119.md) is in the High Street.[^cite_note-41] Medway Christian Fellowship is based on Medway Road.[^cite_note-42]
