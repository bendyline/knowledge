**Manipur** ([/ˌmænɪˈpʊər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), IPA: [\[məɳiˈpʊɾ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu))[^cite_note-12] is a [state](375986.md) in [northeastern India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India), with [Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal) as its capital city. It borders the Indian states of [Assam](186162.md) to the west, [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram) to the south, and [Nagaland](408677.md) to the north and shares the [international border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Myanmar_border) with [Myanmar](19457.md), specifically the [Sagaing Region](493361.md) to the east and [Chin State](493366.md) to the southeast. Covering an area of 22,330 square kilometers (8,621 sq mi), the state consists mostly of hilly terrain with the 1813-square-kilometre (700 sq mi) [Imphal Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal_Valley) inhabited by the [Meitei (Manipuri) community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people), historically a [kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_\(princely_state\)). Surrounding hills are home to [Naga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_people) and [Kuki-Zo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_people) communities, who speak [Tibeto-Burman languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The official language and [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca), [Meitei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language) (Manipuri), also belongs to the Tibeto-Burman family.[^cite_note-15]

During the days of the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), Manipur was one of the [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state).[^cite_note-16] Prior to the British departure in 1947, Manipur acceded to the [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India), along with 550 other princely states approximately.[^cite_note-Furber-17] In September 1949, the ruler of Manipur signed a merger agreement with India, giving up the rule of the kingdom and obtaining a [privy purse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_purse_in_India) in return.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoudhury,_Northeast_India:_A_Political_History2023136–137-18] Many Meitei people feel that their self-determination was violated by the agreement since the legislature elected under the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_State_Constitution_Act_1947) was not consulted.[^cite_note-HRW-19] Ethnic and separatist concerns have resulted in a long-running [insurgency against Indian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Manipur).[^cite_note-HRW-19] From 2009 through 2018, the conflict was responsible for the violent deaths of over 1000 people.[^cite_note-satp9413-20]

The Meitei people represent around 53% of the population of Manipur state, followed by various Naga tribes at 20% and Kuki-Zo tribes at 16%.[^cite_note-censusindia.gov.in-21] Manipur's ethnic groups practice a variety of religions.[^cite_note-census2011-22] According to 2011 census, [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) and [Christianity](5211.md) are the major religions of the state.[^cite_note-census2011-22]

Manipur has primarily an agrarian economy, with significant hydroelectric power generation potential. It is connected to other areas by daily flights through [Imphal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal_Airport), the second largest in northeastern India.[^cite_note-manipuraai-23] Manipur is home to many sports and the origin of [Manipuri dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuri_dance),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReginald_Massey2004177–184-24] and is credited with introducing [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo) to Europeans.[^cite_note-nam.ac.uk-25]

## Name

The name Manipur ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language): मणिपुर, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Sanskrit): *maṇipura*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) ''city of jewels'') was chosen by [Gharib Nawaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharib_Nawaz_\(Manipur\)) in the eighteenth century. It is named after [a kingdom of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipura_\(Mahabharata\)) mentioned in the *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)* Hindu epic. Previously, it had been known as Kangleipak ([Meitei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language): ꯀꯪꯂꯩꯄꯛ)[^cite_note-Sanajaoba_pa89-26][^cite_note-27] or Meiteileipak (ꯃꯩꯇꯩꯂꯩꯕꯥꯛ, 'land of the Meiteis').[^cite_note-Sanajaoba1988-28] Later, the work *Dharani Samhita* (1825–34) popularised the [Sanskrit legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskritisation) of the origin of Manipur's name.[^cite_note-29]

Other names include Sanaleibak ([Meitei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language): ꯁꯅꯥꯂꯩꯕꯥꯛ, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'the land of gold'), not given because Manipur has a lot of the gold mines, but because of its happiness and prosperity.[^cite_note-30] This name was mentioned in the 11th-12th century constitution, the [Loiyumba Shinyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loiyumba_Shinyen),<sup>[10](#endnote_Sanajaoba1988")</sup> and is still heard in the official song of Manipur, "[Sana Leibak Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sana_Leibak_Manipur)".

## History

Main articles: [History of Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Manipur) and [Manipur (princely state)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_\(princely_state\))

### Antiquity

Main article: [Kangleipak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangleipak)



A match of *[Sagol Kangjei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagol_Kangjei)* ([Meitei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language) for '[Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo)') depicted in a stamp of the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_India)

The history of Manipur Meiteis is chronicled in [Puyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puya_\(Meitei_texts\)) or Puwaris (stories about the forefathers), namely, the Ninghthou Kangbalon, Cheitharol Kumbaba, Ningthourol Lambuba, Poireiton Khunthokpa, Panthoibi Khongkul, and so forth in the [Meitei script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_script), which is comparable to the [Thai script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_script). The historical accounts presented here were recordings from the eyes and the judgment of Meitei kings and [Maichou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maichou) (Meitei scholars).

#### The Kingdoms of Möng Kawng and Möng Mao

According to the Tai chronicles, Manipur (Kahse) is one of the territories conquered by Sam Lông Hpa (1150–1201), the first [Chao Pha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Pha) of [Möng Kawng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6ng_Kawng_State).[^cite_note-31] A 14th-century inscription from [Pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagan), [Myanmar](19457.md), mentions Kasan (Manipur) as one of the 21 states under the [Mong Mao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6ng_Mao) ruler Thonganbwa (1413–1445/6); he later was captured by the Governor of [Taungdwingyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taungdwingyi).[^cite_note-32]

### Medieval

#### Vassal State of the Toungoo empire

[Bayinnaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayinnaung), the ruler of [Toungoo dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Toungoo_Empire) ordered the invasion of Manipur in 1559. He had recalled [Binnya Dala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binnya_Dala_\(minister-general\)) from [Chiang Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Mai) to lead the invasion. The three armies mostly made up of army from [Kale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalay), [Mohnyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6ng_Yang_State), [Mogaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6ng_Kawng_State), [Momeik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6ng_Mit_State) and [Sanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanda_\(state\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) led the invasion, the King of Manipur surrendered without any resistance and Manipur became a [vassal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_state) of the [Toungoo empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Toungoo_Empire).[^cite_note-vbl-154-155-33]



Toungoo empire in 1580

#### Vassal state of Konbaung dynasty

In the 18th century, [Bodawpaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodawpaya), the king of Burma acquired the Manipur (1814) along with the western kingdoms of [Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U) (1784), [Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Assam) (1817).

By the medieval period, marriage alliances between the royal families of Manipur, [Ahom kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom) and [Burma](19457.md) had become common.[^cite_note-Sanajaoba1988p3-34] Medieval era manuscripts discovered in the 20th century, particularly the *Puya*, provide evidence that Hindus from the Indian subcontinent had married Manipur royalty by at least the 14th century. In centuries thereafter, royal spouses came also from what is now Assam, [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal), and [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) and from other [South Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Indian) kingdoms as well.[^cite_note-Sanajaoba1988p13-35] Another manuscript suggests that Muslims arrived in Manipur in the 17th century, from what is now [Bangladesh](3454.md), during the reign of Meidingu [Khagemba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khagemba).[^cite_note-Sanajaoba1988p13-35] The socio-political turmoil and wars, particularly the persistent and devastating [Anglo-Burmese wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Burmese_wars), affected the cultural and religious demography of Manipur.[^cite_note-Sanajaoba1988p15-36]

### British colonial period

Main article: [Manipur (princely state)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_\(princely_state\))

In 1824, the ruler of Manipur entered into a [subsidiary alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiary_alliance) with the British Empire in the Indian subcontinent, which became responsible for Manipur's external defence. The British recognised that the state remained internally self-governing, as a [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state).[^cite_note-37] During [World War II](32927.md), Manipur was the scene of many fierce battles between Japanese invaders and British Indian forces. The [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) were beaten back before they could enter [Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Imphal), which was one of the turning points of the overall war in South Asia.[^cite_note-38] The [Battle of Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Imphal), known to the Manipuris as *Japan Laan*, are among the lesser-known battles of the Second World War. Yet the Allied Victory in this battle was a turning point against the Japanese in East Asia.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

### Post-colonial history

After the war, the Maharaja took the advice of the [British Cabinet Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Cabinet_Mission_to_India) and the [Chamber of Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Princes) to introduce democratic reforms in the state.[^cite_note-47] The [Manipur State Constitution Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_State_Constitution_Act_1947) was promulgated in July 1947 to give the state an elected legislative assembly and an appointed prime minister.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESudhirkumar_Singh,_Socio-religious_and_Political_Movements2011130–131-48][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETarapot,_Bleeding_Manipur2003172-49] Elections to the assembly were held only in the following year.[^cite_note-50]

Following the decision to [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India), all the princely states were advised to "accede" to one of the new dominions. The Maharaja [acceded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_of_Accession) to India on 11 August 1947 and signed a [standstill agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standstill_agreement_\(India\)) to continue all the pre-existing arrangements it had with British India.[^cite_note-Furber-17][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESanatomba,_Interrogation_into_the_political_status_of_Manipur2015169-53] Some Meitei people argue that the king was in no legal position to sign the instrument of accession at the time.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

Over the next two years, the multitude of princely states of India were extensively reorganised as India moved towards becoming a [constitutional republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India). Proposals for reorganisation were also made for Manipur but discarded as being unsuitable.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETarapot,_Bleeding_Manipur2003173-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParratt,_Wounded_Land2005103–104-57] Eventually Manipur was turned into a centrally administered province (called a 'Part C' state, later renamed [union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory_\(India\))) by asking the Maharaja to sign a merger agreement.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] He is believed to have signed it under duress,[^cite_note-60] and without opportunity to consult the elected legislature..[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

As a centrally-administered province, Manipur was governed by a Chief Commissioner, as per the Constitution of India. An advisory council with nominated members was provided to advise the Chief Commissioner.[^cite_note-Sinha-65] In 1956, the status changed to a union territory, with a Lieutenant Governor advised by a territorial council with mostly elected members.[^cite_note-Sinha-65][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKumar,_Union_Territories199144–45-66] In 1963, Manipur was provided a legislative assembly and a council of ministers headed by a chief minister.[^cite_note-Sinha-65][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKumar,_Union_Territories199149–53-67] It was made a fully-fledged state in 1972 under the [North-Eastern Areas (Reorganisation) Act, 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Eastern_Areas_\(Reorganisation\)_Act,_1971).[^cite_note-Sinha-65][^cite_note-68]

### Insurgency



Kangla Gate, the west entrance to the [Kangla Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangla_Palace)

The disaffection of the populace before 1972, led to armed insurgencies. The first armed opposition group, the [United National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_National_Liberation_Front) (UNLF), was founded in 1964 demanding independence from India. Over time, more groups formed, each with a different goal, and deriving support from diverse ethnic groups in Manipur. The [People's Revolutionary Party of Kangleipak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Revolutionary_Party_of_Kangleipak) (PREPAK) was formed in 1977, and the [People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army_of_Manipur) (PLA) in 1978, which were suspected to have received arms and training from China.[^cite_note-FM-69] In 1980, the [Kangleipak Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangleipak_Communist_Party) (KCP) was formed. These groups began a spree of bank robberies and attacks on police officers and government buildings.[^cite_note-FM-69] The state government appealed to the central government in [New Delhi](51585.md) for support in combating this violence.[^cite_note-HRW-19]

Even before the [Naga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_people)-dominated hill districts of Manipur participated in the [Naga insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_insurgency) driven by the [Naga National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_National_Council) and, later, [Natiional Socialist Council of Nagaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist_Council_of_Nagaland). In the late 1980s the [Kuki-Zo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki-Zo_people) started their own insurgency demanding separation from the Manipur state.

From 1980 to 2004, the Indian government referred to Manipur as a *disturbed area,* where the [Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_\(Special_Powers\)_Act) (AFSPA) would operate. The law allow the military to treat private and public spaces in the same manner, detain individuals for up to 24 hours with unlimited renewals, perform warrantless searches, and to shoot and kill individuals who break laws, carry weapons, or gather in groups larger than four. Legal immunity applies to the armed forces.[^cite_note-70] Since 1980, the application of the AFSPA has been at the heart of concerns about human rights violations in the region, such as arbitrary killings, torture, cruel, [inhuman and degrading treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhuman_or_degrading_treatment), and [forced disappearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_disappearance). Its continued application has led to numerous protests, notably the longstanding hunger strike by [Irom Sharmila Chanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irom_Sharmila_Chanu).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

In 2004, the government lifted the *disturbed* status after a violent attack on a local woman. The rape of a Manipuri woman, [Thangjam Manorama Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thangjam_Manorama_Devi), by members of the [Assam Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Rifles) paramilitary led to wide protests including a nude protest by the [Meira Paibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meira_Paibi) women's association.[^cite_note-73]

#### 18 June Uprising

Main article: [2001 Manipur Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Manipur_Uprising)

On 18 June 2001, a massive protest broke out in Imphal Valley. The protest was triggered by the Government of India's decision to extend the [ceasefire](592591.md) agreement with the [NSCN-IM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSCN-IM) "without territorial limits", which was widely perceived as a threat to Manipur's territorial integrity. Demonstrators set fire to the Manipur Legislative Assembly and other government buildings in Imphal. Security forces opened fire to stop the crowds, killing 18 people.[^cite_note-74]

#### 2023 Manipur violence

Main article: [2023 Manipur violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Manipur_violence)

On 3 May 2023, [ethnic violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%932025_Manipur_violence) broke out between [Meitei people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people) and [Kuki people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_people) which led to extensive violence and arson, resulting in the displacement of 60,000 people from their homes and hundreds more dead and hospitalised.[^cite_note-75] According to data released by [Manipur Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_Police), as of September 15, 2023, 175 people were killed, 1,118 people were injured, and 33 people were missing. 96 bodies remained unclaimed. 5,172 cases of arson including destruction of 4,786 houses and 386 religious places, out of which there were 254 churches and 132 temples, were reported since May. Out of 5,668 arms lost, 1,329 arms, 15,050 ammunition and 400 bombs were recovered.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] [Indian army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_army) troops were deployed to control the situation.[^cite_note-79] International outrage resulted on 20 July 2023, from a viral video that two Kuki women had been paraded naked and allegedly gang-raped by a mob of Meitei men.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

## Geography



[Loktak Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loktak_Lake), the largest fresh water lake in the state.

The state lies at a latitude of 23°83'N – 25°68'N and a longitude of 93°03'E – 94°78'E. The total area covered by the state is 22,327 km<sup>2</sup> (8,621 sq mi). The capital lies in an oval-shaped valley of approximately 700 sq mi (2,000 km<sup>2</sup>), surrounded by blue mountains, at an elevation of 790 m (2,590 ft) above sea level.\[^cite_note-[www.e-pao.net-83](http://www.e-pao.net-83/)] The slope of the valley is from north to south. The mountain ranges create a moderate climate, preventing the cold winds from the north from reaching the valley and barring cyclonic storms.[^cite_note-84]

The state is bordered by the Indian states of [Nagaland](408677.md) to its north, [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram) to its south, Assam to its west, and shares an international border with [Myanmar](19457.md) to its east.[^cite_note-HG-85]



A tree amid Manipur hills.

The state has four major river basins: the [Barak River Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_River) ([Barak Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_Valley)) to the west, the [Manipur River Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_River) in central Manipur, the [Yu River Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaw_Valley) in the east, and a portion of the Lanye River Basin in the north.[^cite_note-86] The water resources of Barak and Manipur river basins are about 1.8487 Mham (million hectare metres). The overall water balance of the state amounts to 0.7236 Mham in the annual water budget.[^cite_note-87] (By comparison, India receives 400 Mham of rain annually.[^cite_note-88])

The Barak River, the largest of Manipur, originates in the Manipur Hills and is joined by tributaries, such as the Irang, Maku, and [Tuivai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuivai_River). After its junction with the Tuivai, the Barak River turns north, forms the border with [Assam State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_State), and then enters the [Cachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachar) Assam just above [Lakhipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhipur). The Manipur river basin has eight major rivers: the [Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_River), [Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal_River), [Iril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iril_River), Nambul, Sekmai, Chakpi, Thoubal and Khuga. All these rivers originate from the surrounding hills.[^cite_note-GW-89]



Landscape view for dawn at Senapati, Manipur

Almost all the rivers in the valley area are in the mature stage and therefore deposit their sediment load in the [Loktak Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loktak_Lake).\[^cite_note-[www.e-pao.net-83](http://www.e-pao.net-83/)] The rivers draining the Manipur Hills are comparatively young, due to the hilly terrain through which they flow. These rivers are corrosive and become turbulent in the rainy season. Important rivers draining the western area include the Maku, [Barak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_River), Jiri, Irang, and Leimatak. Rivers draining the eastern part of the state, the Yu River Basin, include the Chamu, Khunou and other short streams.[^cite_note-GW-89]

Manipur may be characterised as two distinct physical regions: an outlying area of rugged hills and narrow valleys, and the inner area of flat plain, with all associated landforms. These two areas are distinct in physical features and are conspicuous in flora and fauna. The valley region has hills and mounds rising above the flat surface. The Loktak Lake is an important feature of the central plain. The total area occupied by all the lakes is about 600 km<sup>2</sup>. The altitude ranges from 40 m at [Jiribam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiribam) to 2,994 m at [Mount Tempü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Temp%C3%BC) peak along the border with Nagaland.

The soil cover can be divided into two broad types, viz. the red [ferruginous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_oxide) soil in the hill area and the [alluvium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvium) in the valley. The valley soils generally contain loam, small rock fragments, sand, and sandy clay, and are varied. On the plains, especially flood plains and deltas, the soil is quite thick. The topsoil on the steep slopes is very thin. Soil on the steep hill slopes is subject to high erosion, resulting in [gullies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gullies) and barren rock slopes. The normal pH value ranges from 5.4 to 6.8.[^cite_note-90]

### Flora



Flowers carpeting the foothills

*[L. mackliniae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilium_mackliniae)* is an endemic flower to Manipur and was declared the official state flower in 1989.[^cite_note-91]

Natural vegetation occupies an area of about 17,418 km<sup>2</sup> (6,725 sq mi),[^cite_note-92] or 77.2% of the total geographical area of the state, and consists of short and tall grasses, reeds and [bamboos](39029.md), and trees. About a third of Manipur's forests are protected: 8.42% of the forested land is categorized under Reserved Forests, and 23.95% under Protected Forests.[^cite_note-FD-93]

There are six major types of forests in Manipur, and 10 subtypes. The six major forest types, according to the state's Forest Department, include Tropical Wet Evergreen Forest, Tropical Moist Deciduous Forests, Sub-Tropical Pine Forest, Tropical Dry Deciduous Forest, Montane Wet Temperate Forest and Sub-alpine Forest.[^cite_note-FD-93]

There are forests of [teak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teak), [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine), [oak](39696.md), [uningthou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoebe_hainesiana), leihao, bamboo, and [cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arundo). [Rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber_tree), [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea), [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus_%C3%97_sinensis), and [cardamom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardamom) are grown in hill areas. [Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) is a staple food for Manipuris.[^cite_note-HG-85]

### Climate



The [Dzüko Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dz%C3%BCko_Valley) which exists between the boundaries of Manipur and Nagaland has a temperate climate.

Manipur's climate is largely influenced by the topography of the region. Lying 790 metres above sea level, Manipur is wedged among hills on all sides. This northeastern corner of India enjoys a generally amiable climate, though the winters can be cool. The maximum temperature in the summer months is 32 °C (90 °F). The coolest conditions are in January, and the warmest month is July.

The state receives an average annual rainfall of 1,467.5 mm (57.78 in) between April and mid-October. Precipitation ranges from light rain or mizzle to heavy downpours. The capital city [Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal) receives an annual average of 933 mm (36.7 in). Rainfall in this region is caused by The South Westerly [Monsoon](57630.md) picking up moisture from the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) and heading towards the [Eastern Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Himalaya) ranges. This normal rainfall pattern of Manipur enriches the soil and much of the agrarian activities are dependent on it as well.

Manipur is already [experiencing climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_India), especially [changes in weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impacts_of_climate_change), with both increased [variability in rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather) as well as increasingly severe changes in temperature.[^cite_note-94]

### People

Main article: [Manipuri people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuri_people)

The [Meiteis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people)[^cite_note-Khomdan_Singh_Lisam_pp_322-97] (synonymous to the [Manipuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuris)) constitute the majority of the state's population. They inhabit the [Imphal Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal_Valley). Nagas and Kuki-Zo are the major tribe conglomerates of tribes inhabiting the surrounding hills. The Kuki-Zos consist of several tribes including [Gangte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangte_people), [Hmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmar_people), [Paite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paite_people), [Simte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simte_people), [Sukte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukte_people), [Thadou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thadou_people), [Vaiphei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaiphei_people), [Zou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zou_people), and some smaller tribes. They speak [Kuki-Chin languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki-Chin_languages) and predominate the southern hill districts of Manipur. The prominent Naga tribes are [Mao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_people_\(India\)), [Maram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maram_people), [Maring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maring_people_\(India\)), [Liangmai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangmai_people), [Poumai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poumai_people), [Rongmei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongmei_people) and [Tangkhul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangkhul_people), each speaking its own language. They predominate the northern hill districts of Manipur. In addition, several smaller tribes that were classified as "Old Kuki" by the British administrators populate [Chandel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandel_district) and neighbouring districts. Some of them now classify themselves as Nagas while others retain the Kuki classification.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

Other than Meitei language, in Manipur, there is considerable linguistic diversity, as is the case in most of the Northeast India. Almost all of the languages are [Sino-Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan), with many different subgroups represented. [Ningthoukhong Gopinath Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningthoukhong_Gopinath_Mandir)

The [Meitei ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people) (aka [Manipuri people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuri_people)) is the majority group following Hinduism in Manipur, beside other minor immigrants from other parts of India following the same faith. Among the indigenous communities of Manipur, [Meiteis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiteis) are the only Hindus as no other indigenous ethnic groups follow this faith. According to the [2011 Census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India), about 41.39% of the Manipuri people practise [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism). The Hindu population is heavily concentrated in the Meitei dominant areas of the Manipur Valley ([Imphal Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal_Valley)), among the Meitei people. The districts of Bishnupur, Thoubal, Imphal East, and Imphal West all have Hindu majorities, averaging 67.62% (range 62.27–74.81%) according to the 2011 census data.[^cite_note-religion-110]

Vaishnavite Hinduism was the state religion of the [Kingdom of Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_Kingdom). In 1704, Meitei King [Charairongba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charairongba) accepted Vaishnavism and changed his traditional Meitei name into Hindu name, Pitambar Singh.[^cite_note-112] During the 13th century, Meitei King Khumomba constructed a Lord Hanuman temple.[^cite_note-113] The Vishnu temple at Lamangdong was constructed during 1474 CE (during the reign of Meitei King [Kiyamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senbi_Kiyamba)), by Brahmins immigrating from the neighborhood [Shan State](493376.md). As per the legends, the temple was constructed to house the Vishnu emblem given to King Kiyamba by King Khekhomba of Shan. Phurailatpam Shubhi Narayan was the first Brahmin priest of this temple.[^cite_note-114]

### Christianity

Main article: [Christianity in Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Manipur)



St. Joseph's Cathedral at Imphal

Christianity is the religion of 41% of the people in the state, but is the majority in rural areas with 53%, and is predominant in the hills. It was brought by Protestant missionaries to Manipur in the 19th century. In the 20th century, a few Christian schools were established, which introduced Western-type education. Christianity is the predominant religion among tribals of Manipur and tribal Christians make up the vast majority of the Christian population in Manipur.

### Islam

Main article: [Meitei Pangals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_Pangals)

The [Meitei Pangals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_Pangals) ([Meitei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language): [ꯃꯩꯇꯩ ꯄꯥꯡꯒꯜ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EA%AF%83%EA%AF%A9%EA%AF%87%EA%AF%A9_%EA%AF%84%EA%AF%A5%EA%AF%A1%EA%AF%92%EA%AF%9C)), also known as Meitei Muslims or Manipuri Muslims, are the third largest religious majority group in the state, comprising less than 8.3%[^cite_note-116] of the state population. The majority belong to the [Hanafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi) school of [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) Islamic thought.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118][^cite_note-Hazra_2014-115][^cite_note-119] In 1991, the Quran was translated into the Meitei language for the first time.[^cite_note-120] (See [History of Manipuri Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_Pangals#History))

### Sanamahism

Main article: [Sanamahism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanamahism)



The symbol of Sanamahism religion, the indigenous [ethnic religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_religion) of the [Meitei people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people).



A reconstructed ancient temple dedicated to Meitei God [Pakhangba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakhangba) of [Sanamahism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanamahism) inside the [Kangla Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangla_Fort), [Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal)

[Sanamahism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanamahism) is the indigenous, polytheistic and animistic [ethnic religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_religion) of the Meitei people.[^cite_note-lintner2015p113-121] Sanamahist worship concentrates on the household deity [Lainingthou Sanamahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lainingthou_Sanamahi). The ancient Meiteis worshiped a Supreme deity, Salailen, and followed their ancestors. Their ancestor worship and animism was based on [Umang Lai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umang_Lai) – ethnic governing deities worshiped in the [sacred groves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_groves). Some of the traditional [Meitei deities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_deities), or Lais are Atiya Sidaba, [Pakhangba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakhangba), [Sanamahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lainingthou_Sanamahi), and [Panthoibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthoibi). Out of the 233,767 people who opted for the "Other religion" option, 222,315 were Sanamahists.

### Other religions

The various other religions were mostly followers of tribal folk religions, 6,444 were [Heraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haipou_Jadonang#Heraka_movement), 2,032 were [Jewish](15624.md) and 1,180 were from other tribal religions such as [Tingkao Ragwang Chapriak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingkao_Ragwang_Chapriak).

Main articles: [Government of Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Manipur) and [Manipur Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_Legislative_Assembly)



The [emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem_of_Manipur) of the [Government of Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Manipur) depicts [Kangla Sha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangla_Sha) ([Kangla Sa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangla_Sa)), an [ancient Meitei deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Meitei_deity), and a [Meitei language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language) text written in [Meitei script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_script)

The government of Manipur is a collective assembly of 60 elected members, of which 19 are reserved for [Scheduled Tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes) and 1 for [Scheduled Castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes).[^cite_note-122] The state sends two representatives to the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) of the [Parliament of India](410238.md). The state sends one representative to the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md). The legislature of the state is Unicameral.[^cite_note-123] Representatives are elected for a five-year term to the state assembly and the Indian parliament through voting, a process overseen by the offices of the [Election Commission of India](47863083.md).[^cite_note-124]

The state has [one autonomous council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadar_Hills_Autonomous_District_Council).

For administrative purposes, the state of Manipur is divided into 16 districts, each headed by a [Deputy Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_magistrate) (DC), who is an officer of the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) (IAS). The Deputy Commissioner also serves as the Collector and the District Magistrate. Each district is further divided into subdivisions, which are headed by [Sub-Divisional Officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-divisional_magistrate) (SDOs). For development administration, each district is divided into [Community Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_development_block) (CD) Blocks, which are headed by Block Development Officers (BDOs).[^cite_note-\:0-125]

Other key district officials include the [Superintendent of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_police_\(India\)) (SP), an [Indian Police Service](451884.md) officer who oversees the police administration in the district.

For local governance, the state has a three-tier [Panchayati Raj system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_raj) for rural areas and [municipal bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_governance_in_India) for urban areas. There are nine Municipal Councils (MCs), 18 Nagar Panchayats (NPs), and one Small Town Committee, making a total of 28 urban local bodies.[^cite_note-126]

#### Security and insurgency

Further information: [2023-2026 Manipur conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023-2026_Manipur_conflict), [Insurgency in Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Manipur), [Human rights abuses in Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_in_Manipur), and [Thangjam Manorama § Protests against the AFSPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thangjam_Manorama#Protests_against_the_AFSPA)



[Irom Chanu Sharmila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irom_Chanu_Sharmila), the world's longest hunger striker, who went on [hunger strike](222292.md) for more than 500 weeks, demanding the repealing of the [Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_\(Special_Powers\)_Act,_1958) in Manipur

[^cite_note-127]

The violence in Manipur extends beyond the conflict between Indian security forces and insurgent armed groups. There is violence between the [Meitei ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_ethnicity), various [Naga tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_tribes), various [Kuki tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_tribes), and other tribal groups.[^cite_note-HRW-19]

Splinter groups have arisen within some of the armed groups, and disagreement between them is rife. Other than the UNLF, PLA, and PREPAK, Manipuri insurgent groups include the Revolutionary Peoples Front (RPF), Manipur Liberation Front Army (MLFA), Kanglei Yawol Kanba Lup (KYKL), Revolutionary Joint Committee (RJC), Kangleipak Communist Party (KCP), Peoples United Liberation Front (PULF), Manipur Naga People Front (MNPF), National Socialist Council of Nagaland (NSCN-K), National Socialist Council of Nagaland (NSCN-I/M), United Kuki Liberation Front (UKLF), Kuki National Front (KNF), Kuki National Army (KNA), Kuki Defence Force (KDF), Kuki Democratic Movement (KDM), Kuki National Organisation (KNO), Kuki Security Force (KSF), Chin Kuki Revolutionary Front (CKRF), Kom Rem Peoples Convention (KRPC), Zomi Revolutionary Volunteers (ZRV), Zomi Revolutionary Army (ZRA), Zomi Reunification Organisation (ZRO), and Hmar Peoples Convention (HPC).[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-HRW-19]

The Meitei insurgent groups seek independence from India. The Kuki insurgent groups want a separate state for the Kukis to be carved out from the present state of Manipur. The Kuki insurgent groups are under two umbrella organisations: the Kuki National Organisation (KNO) and United Peoples Forum.[^cite_note-129] The Nagas wish to annex part of Manipur and merge with a greater Nagaland or Nagalim, which is in conflict with Meitei insurgent demands for the integrity of their vision of an independent state. There have been many tensions between the tribes and numerous clashes between Naga and Kukis, Meiteis and Muslims.[^cite_note-HRW-19]

According to SATP in 2014, there had been a dramatic decline in fatalities in Manipur since 2009. In 2009, 77 civilians died (about 3 per 100,000 people).[^cite_note-satp9413-20] From 2010 onward, about 25 civilians have died in militant-related violence (about 1 per 100,000 people), dropping further to 21 civilian deaths in 2013 (or 0.8 per 100,000 people). However, there were 76 explosions in 2013 compared to 107 in 2012. Different groups have claimed responsibility for the explosions, some claiming they were targeting competing militant groups, others claiming their targets were state and central government officials.[^cite_note-satp-130] As a point of comparison, the average annual global rate of violent death between 2004 and 2009 was 7.9 per 100,000 people.[^cite_note-gede-131]

## Economy



Bamboo is common in Manipur, and an important contributor to its economy as well as cuisine. Above is *soibum yendem eromba*, a bamboo shoot dish of Manipur.

The 2012–2013 gross state domestic product of Manipur at market prices was about ₹10,188 crore (US\$1.1 billion).[^cite_note-132] Its economy is primarily agriculture, forestry, cottage and trade driven.[^cite_note-ghg-133] Manipur acts as India's "Gateway to the East" through [Moreh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moreh,_India) and [Tamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamu,_Burma) towns, the land route for trade between India and Burma and other countries in Southeast Asia, East Asia, Siberia, the Arctic, Micronesia and Polynesia. Manipur has the highest number of handicraft units and the highest number of craftspersons in the northeastern region of India.[^cite_note-Manipur_Economy_-_Snapshot-134]

### Electricity

Manipur produced about 0.1 GWh (0.36 TJ) of electricity in 2010 with its infrastructure.[^cite_note-135] The state has [hydroelectric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power) generation potential, estimated to be over 2 GWh (7.2 TJ). As of 2010, if half of this potential is realised, it is estimated that this would supply [24/7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24/7_service) electricity to all residents, with a surplus for sale, as well as supplying the Burma power grid.[^cite_note-136]

### Agriculture

Manipur's climate and soil conditions make it ideally suited for horticultural crops. Growing there are rare and exotic [medicinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicinal_plants) and [aromatic plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromatic_plants).[^cite_note-Manipur_Economy_-_Snapshot-134] Some cash crops suited for Manipur include [lychees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lychee), [cashews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashew), [walnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walnut), [oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)), [lemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon), [pineapples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), [papayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya), [passion fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passiflora_edulis), [peaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peach), [pears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pear), and [plums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plum).[^cite_note-ghg-133] The state is covered with over 3,000 km<sup>2</sup> (1,200 sq mi) of bamboo forests, making it a major contributor to India's bamboo industry.[^cite_note-Manipur_Economy_-_Snapshot-134]

Agriculture in Manipur includes a number of [smallholding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallholding) farms, many of whom are owned by women.[^cite_note-RM-137] Climate change, especially changes in temperature and weather are hurting small farmers in the state.[^cite_note-138] Like [rural women in other parts of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_climate_change), women in agriculture in Manipur are harmed more by the changes in weather, because of less access to support from local governments.[^cite_note-RM-137]

### Transportation infrastructure



Imphal airport is the second largest airport in India's northeast.

[Tulihal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulihal_Airport), Changangei, Imphal, the only airport of Manipur, connects directly with [Delhi](37756.md), [Kolkata](47905.md), [Guwahati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guwahati), and [Agartala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agartala). It has been upgraded to an [international airport](1519705.md). As India's second largest airport in the northeast, it serves as a key logistical centre for northeastern states. The Tulihal Airport has been renamed Bir Tikendrajit Airport.[^cite_note-manipuraai-23] [National Highway NH-39](44397098.md) links Manipur with the rest of the country through the railway stations at [Dimapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimapur) in Nagaland at a distance of 215 km (134 mi) from Imphal.

[National Highway 53 (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_53_\(India\)) connects Manipur with another railway station at [Silchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silchar) in Assam, which is 269 km (167 mi) away from Imphal. The road network of Manipur, with a length of 7,170 km (4,460 mi) connects all the important towns and distant villages. However, the road condition throughout the state is often deplorable.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] In 2010, Indian government announced that it is considering an Asian infrastructure network from Manipur to [Vietnam](202354.md).[^cite_note-141] The proposed [Trans-Asian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Asian_Railway) (TAR), if constructed, will pass through Manipur, connecting India to [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma), [Thailand](30128.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Singapore](27318.md).

### Tourism

The tourist season is from October to February when it is often sunny without being hot and humid. The culture features martial arts, dance, theatre and sculpture. Greenery accompanies a moderate climate. The seasonal [Shirui Lily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirui_Lily) plant at [Ukhrul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukhrul) (district), [Dzüko Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dz%C3%BCko_Valley) at Senapati, [Sangai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangai) ([Brow antlered deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brow_antlered_deer)) and the [floating islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_islands) at [Loktak Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loktak_Lake) are among the rarities of the area. [Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo), which can be called a royal game, originated in Manipur.

48 km (30 mi) from [Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal), lies the largest fresh water lake in northeast India, the [Loktak Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loktak_Lake), a miniature inland sea. #### Hills and valleys

Kaina is a [hillock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillock) about 921 m (3,022 ft) above sea level. It is a sacred place for Manipuri Hindus. The legend is that, Shri Govindajee appeared in the dream of his devotee, Shri Jai Singh Maharaja, and asked the saintly king to install in a temple, an image of [Shri Govindajee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Krishna). It was to be carved out of a [jack fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_fruit) tree, which was then growing at [Kaina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaina_\(Manipur\)). It is 29 km (18 mi) from Imphal. The [Dzüko Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dz%C3%BCko_Valley) is in Senapati district bordering with Kohima. There are seasonal flowers and number of flora and fauna. It is at an altitude of 2,438 m (7,999 ft) above sea level, behind [Mount Japfü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Japf%C3%BC) in Nagaland. The rare Dzüko lily is found only in this valley.

This ecosystem contains 17 rare species of mammals.\[^cite_note-[www.e-pao.net-83](http://www.e-pao.net-83/)] It is the only floating national park of the world.[^cite_note-151] Six km (3.7 mi) to the west of [Imphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal), at the foot of the pine growing hillocks at Iroisemba on the Imphal-Kangchup Road are the [Zoological Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoological_Gardens). #### Waterfalls

Sadu Chiru waterfall is near Ichum Keirap village[^cite_note-152] 27 km (17 mi) from Imphal, in the Sadar hill area, Senapati district. This consists of three falls with the first fall about 30 m (98 ft) high. Agape Park is in the vicinity.

#### Natural caves

Thalon Cave (around 910 m (2,990 ft) above sea level) is one of the historical sites of Manipur under [Tamenglong district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamenglong_district). It is around 185 km (115 mi) from the state capital and around 30 km (19 mi) from Tamenglong district headquarters in north side. From Thalon village, this cave is 4–5 km (2.5–3.1 mi).[^cite_note-153] Khangkhui Cave is a natural limestone cave in [Ukhrul district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukhrul_district). The big hall in the cave is the darbar hall of the Devil King living deep inside while the northern hall is the royal bedroom, according to local folklore. During World War II, villagers sought shelter here. This cave is an hour's trek from Khangkui village.[^cite_note-154]

## Education

See also: [List of institutions of higher education in Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_institutions_of_higher_education_in_Manipur)



The [Sanamahi Kiyong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanamahi_Kiyong) shrine is the central body of the [University of Sanamahi Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nongmaiching_Ching#University_of_Sanamahi_Culture) in the [Nongmaiching Ching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nongmaiching_Ching) mountain.

Under the 10+2+3 plan, students may enroll in general or professional degree programs after passing the Higher Secondary Examination (the grade 12 examination). ## Art and culture&#x20;

Further information: [Meitei architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_architecture), [Meitei culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_culture), [Meitei dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_dances), [Meitei mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_mythology), [Meitei festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_festivals), [Meitei folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_folklore), [Meitei folktales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_folktales), [Meitei religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_religion), and [Meitei literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_literature)

Secular theatre is mostly confined to themes that are not religious; it is performed in the secular or profane spheres. In these are *Shumang lila* and *Phampak lila* (stage drama). *Shumang lila* is very popular. Etymologically Shumang lila is the combination of "Shumang" (courtyard) and "[Lila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lila_\(given_name\))" (play or performance). It is performed in an area of 13×13 ft in the centre of any open space, in a very simple style without a raised stage, set design, or heavy props such as curtains, background scenery, and visual effects. It uses one table and two chairs, kept on one side of the performance space. Its claim as the "theatre of the masses" is underlined by the way it is performed in the middle of an audience that surrounds it, leaving one passage as entrance and exit.

The world of *Phampak lila* (stage drama) performed in the proscenium theatre is similar, in form, to the Western theatrical model and Indian [Natyasastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natyasastra) model though its contents are indigenous. The so-called modern theatre descended on Manipur theatre culture with the performance of Pravas Milan (1902) under the enthusiastic patronage of [Sir Churchand Maharaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_Churchand_Singh) (1891–1941). The pace of theatrical movement was geared up with the institution of groups such as [Manipur Dramatic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_Dramatic_Union) (MDU) (1930), Arian Theatre (1935), Chitrangada Natya Mandir (1936), Society Theatre (1937), Rupmahal (1942), Cosmopolitan Dramatic Union (1968), and the Chorus Repertory Theatre of [Ratan Thiyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratan_Thiyam) (1976). These groups started experimenting with types of plays apart from historical and [Puranic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puranas) ones. Today Manipur theatre is well respected because of excellent productions shown in India and abroad. Manipur plays, both *Shumang lila* and *stage lila*, have been a regular feature in the annual festival of the [National School of Drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_School_of_Drama), New Delhi.

[Iskcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Society_for_Krishna_Consciousness) led by [Bhaktisvarupa Damodara Swami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaktisvarupa_Damodar_Swami) started a network of schools in Northeastern India, where more than 4,000 students receive education centred on Vaishnava spiritual values. In 1989 he founded "Ranganiketan Manipuri Cultural Arts Troupe", which has approximately 600 performances at over 300 venues in over 15 countries. Ranganiketan (literally "House of Colorful Arts") is a group of more than 20 dancers, musicians, singers, martial artists, choreographers, and craft artisans. Some of them have received international acclaim.

### Manipur dance (Ras Lila)

Main article: [Manipuri dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuri_dance)



The Shrine – the main theatre

[Manipuri dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur_dance) also known as Jagoi,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReginald_Massey2004177-155] is one of the major [Indian classical dance forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Indian_dance),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200483–84,_the_other_major_classical_Indian_dances_are:_Bharatanatyam,_Kathak,_Odissi,_Kathakali,_Kuchipudi,_Cchau,_Satriya,_Yaksagana_and_Bhagavata_Mela-156] named after the state of Manipur.[^cite_note-lochtefeld420-157][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReginald_Massey2004177–187-158] It is particularly known for its Hindu [Vaishnavism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnavism) themes, and exquisite performances of love-inspired dance drama of Radha-Krishna called [Raslila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raslila).[^cite_note-lochtefeld420-157][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReginald_Massey2004177-155][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERagini_Devi1990175–180-159] However, the dance is also performed to themes related to [Shaivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaivism), [Shaktism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaktism) and regional deities such as Umang Lai during [Lai Haraoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai_Haraoba).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReginald_Massey2004177–180-160][^cite_note-161] The roots of Manipur dance, as with all classical Indian dances, is the ancient [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) Sanskrit text *[Natya Shastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natya_Shastra)*, but with influences from the culture fusion between India and Southeast Asia, East Asia, Siberia, Micronesia and Polynesia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaryu_Doshi1989xv–xviii-162]

### Chorus Repertory Theatre

The auditorium of the theatre is on the outskirts of Imphal and the campus stretches for about 2 acres (8,100 m<sup>2</sup>). It has housing and working quarters to accommodate self-sufficiency of life. The theatre association has churned out internationally acclaimed plays like *[Chakravyuha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakravyuha)* and *Uttarpriyadashi*. Its 25 years of existence in theatre had disciplined its performers to a world of excellence. *[Chakravyuha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakravyuha)* taken from the Mahabharat epic had won Fringe Firsts Award, 1987 at the Edinburgh International Theater Festival. *[Chakravyuha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakravyuha)* deals with the story of [Abhimanyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhimanyu) (son of Arjun) of his last battle and approaching death, whereas *Uttarpriyadashi* is an 80-minute exposition of [Emperor Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Ashoka)'s redemption.

## Sports

Main articles: [Sports in Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Manipur), [Khong kangjei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khong_kangjei), [Mukna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukna), and [Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo)



[Marjing Polo Statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjing_Polo_Statue), the world's tallest polo player statue, standing inside the [Marjing Polo Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjing_Polo_Complex), dedicated to God [Marjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjing), the [Meitei deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_deity) of [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo), in the [Heingang Ching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heingang_Ching)

[Mukna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukna) is a popular form of wrestling.[^cite_note-163] Mukna Kangjei, or *Khong Kangjei*, is a game which combines the arts of [mukna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukna) (wrestling hockey) and [Kangjei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khong_kangjei) (Cane Stick) to play the ball made of seasoned [bamboo](39029.md) roots.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-ksl826-165]

[Yubi lakpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yubi_lakpi) is a traditional full contact game played in Manipur, India, using a coconut, which has some notable similarities to [rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_football).[^cite_note-ksl826-165] *Yubi lakpi* literally means "coconut snatching". The coconut is greased to make it slippery. There are rules of the game, as with all Manipur sports.[^cite_note-166] The coconut serves the purpose of a ball and is offered to the king, the chief guest or the judges before the game begins. The aim is to run while carrying the greased coconut and physically cross over the goal line, while the other team tackles and blocks any such attempt as well as tries to grab the coconut and score on its own. In Manipur's long history, Yubi lakpi was the annual official game, attended by the king, over the Hindu festival of Shree Govindajee.[^cite_note-167] It is like the game of [rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_sevens),[^cite_note-168] or American football.[^cite_note-ksl825830-169]

Oolaobi (Woo-Laobi) is an outdoor game mainly played by females. Meitei mythology believes that UmangLai Heloi-Taret (seven deities–seven fairies) played this game on the Courtyard of the temple of Umang Lai Lairembi. The number of participants is not fixed but are divided into two groups (size as per agreement). Players are divided as into Raiders (Attackers) or Defenders (Avoiders).[^cite_note-ksl826-165] *[Hiyang Tannaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiyang_Tannaba)*, also called *Hi Yangba Tanaba*, is a traditional boat rowing race and festivity of the Panas.[^cite_note-ksl826-165]

### Polo





The rules-based Polo game in 19th century Manipur (above), and modern Polo in the 21st century.

The origin of modern polo can be traced to Manipur where the world's oldest polo ground lies, [Imphal Polo Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imphal_Polo_Ground).[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] Captain Robert Stewart and Lieutenant Joseph Sherer[^cite_note-172] of the British colonial era first watched locals play a rules-based *pulu* or *sagolkangjei* (literally, horse and stick) game in 1859. They adopted its rules, calling the game polo, and playing it on their horses. The game spread among the British in Calcutta and then to England.[^cite_note-nam.ac.uk-25][^cite_note-Manipur,_Cradle_of_the_Modern_Game-173]

Apart from these games, some outdoor children's games are fading in popularity. Some games such as Khutlokpi, Phibul Thomba, and Chaphu Thugaibi remain very popular elsewhere, such as in [Cambodia](334751.md). They are played especially during the Khmer New Year.[^cite_note-174]

First of its kind in India, National Sports University will be constructed in Manipur.[^cite_note-175]

### Ningol Chakouba

Held on 9 November,[^cite_note-178] this is a social festival of the [Meitei people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people) of Manipur where married women (Ningol) are invited (Chakouba, literally calling to a meal; for dinner or lunch) to a feast at their parental house accompanied by their children. Besides the feast, gifts are given to the women/invitees and to their children. It is the festival that binds and revives the family relations between the women married away and the parental family. Nowadays, other communities have started celebrating this kind of a family-bonding festival.

### Kut

Held after the Harvest festival in November,[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180] this festival predominantly celebrated by Kuki-Chin-Mizo tribes in Manipur has become one of the leading festivals of the state. Kut is not restricted to a community or tribe — the whole state populace participates in merriment. On 1 November of every year, the state declared holiday for Kut celebration.

### Yaosang

Main article: [Yaosang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaosang)

Held in February or March,[^cite_note-181] Yaosang is considered to be one of the biggest festivals of Manipur. It is the Holi festival (festival of colour) but Yaosang is the regional name given by the people of Manipur.

### Khuado Pawi

Khuado Pawi is the harvest festival of the [Tedim people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedim_people) who were recognised as [Sukte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukte_people) and [Zomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomi) in India and [Myanmar](19457.md) respectively. The word Pawi means [festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival) in Tedim Zomi language. It is celebrated every year in the month of September–October after harvesting.[^cite_note-archive.tongsan.org-182]

### Cheiraoba

Main article: [Sajibu Nongma Panba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sajibu_Nongma_Panba)

Also known as Sajibugi Nongma Panba and held in March or April, *Cheiraoba* is the new year of Manipur.[^cite_note-ksl825830-169] It is observed on the first lunar day of the lunar month *Sajibu* (March/April) and so it is also popularly known as *Sajibu Cheiraoba*. People of Manipur immaculate and decorate their houses and make a sumptuous variety of dishes to feast upon after offering food to the deity on this day. After the feast, as a part of the ritual, people climb hilltops; in the belief that it would excel them to greater heights in their worldly life.[^cite_note-ksl825830-169]

## Geographical indication

### Chak-Hao (Black rice)

[Chak-Hao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chak-Hao_\(Black_rice\)) was awarded the [Geographical Indication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_geographical_indications_in_India) (GI) status tag from the [Geographical Indications Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_Indication_Registry_\(India\)), under the [Union Government of India](553883.md), on 20 April 2020 and is valid until 25 December 2027.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

Consortium of Producers of Chak-Hao (Black Rice) from Imphal, proposed the GI registration of Chak-Hao. After filing the application in December 2017, the rice was granted the GI tag in 2020 by the Geographical Indication Registry in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), making the name "Chak-Hao" exclusive to the rice grown in the region. It thus became the first rice variety from Manipur and Nagaland. It also is the 5th type of goods from Manipur and 4th from Nagaland to earn the GI tag respectively.[^cite_note-185]

The GI tag protects the rice from illegal selling and marketing, and gives it legal protection and a unique identity.
