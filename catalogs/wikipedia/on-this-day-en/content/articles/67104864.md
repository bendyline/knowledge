***KPop Demon Hunters*** is a 2025 American animated musical [urban fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_fantasy) film[^cite_note-ChosunBiz-20252-9][^cite_note-10] co-written and directed by [Maggie Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_Kang) and [Chris Appelhans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Appelhans). Produced by [Sony Pictures Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Animation) for [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix), the film stars the voices of [Arden Cho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arden_Cho), [Ahn Hyo-seop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahn_Hyo-seop), [May Hong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Hong), [Ji-young Yoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji-young_Yoo), [Yunjin Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunjin_Kim), [Daniel Dae Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Dae_Kim), [Ken Jeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Jeong), and [Lee Byung-hun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Byung-hun). The story follows a [K-pop](629945.md) girl group, [Huntrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntrix) (Rumi, Mira, and Zoey), who lead double lives as [demon hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demon_hunter). They face off against a rival [boy band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_band), the Saja Boys, whose members are secretly [demons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dokkaebi).

The film originated from Kang's desire to create a story inspired by her [Korean heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans), drawing on elements of [mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_mythology), [demonology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonology), and K-pop to craft a visually distinct and culturally rooted film. Production began in March 2021. The look of the film was influenced by [concert lighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_lighting), editorial photography, music videos, [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime), and [Korean dramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_drama). [The soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPop_Demon_Hunters_\(soundtrack\)) includes original songs by several musicians and a score by [Marcelo Zarvos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Zarvos).

The film began [streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming) on Netflix on June 20, 2025. By the end of the year, it became the [most-watched original title in Netflix history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-watched_Netflix_original_programming#Films), with over 325 million views. A [sing-along](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sing-along) version had [limited theatrical releases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_theatrical_release) on August 23–24 and October 31–November 2. Its theatrical release was the widest by number of theaters for a Netflix film, and the first to top the box office in the United States. The soundtrack was the first film soundtrack to have four songs in the top ten of the [*Billboard* Hot 100](423161.md) simultaneously, and was certified [double platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_platinum) in the US in October 2025.

*KPop Demon Hunters* received universal acclaim for its animation, visual style, [voice acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_acting), story, [humor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humour), and music. The recipient of [many accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accolades_received_by_KPop_Demon_Hunters), it won Best Animated Feature and Best Original Song ("[Golden](80331847.md)") at both the [83rd Golden Globe Awards](81659305.md) and the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md), and won multiple awards at the [53rd Annie Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Annie_Awards). A sequel is in development.

## Plot

Long ago, demons preyed on humans, feeding drained souls to their ruler, Gwi-Ma. In time, three women became [demon hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demon_hunter) and used their singing voices to create an effective though incomplete magical barrier against demons called the Honmoon.[^cite_note-13] As time passed, new trios of hunters emerged to maintain the Honmoon, with the ultimate goal of strengthening it into the Golden Honmoon—a final seal that would permanently banish demons.

In the present, the [K-pop](629945.md) girl group [Huntrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntrix)—Rumi, Mira, and Zoey—are the latest demon-hunting trio. Rumi is secretly half-demon, a fact known only to her and former hunter Celine, who raised Rumi. Disquieted by the demonic patterns gradually spreading across her skin, Rumi pushes forward the release and live performance of Huntrix's new single, "[Golden](80331847.md)", hoping it will turn the Honmoon gold and thereby erase her patterns. However, as they prepare for the performance, Rumi begins to [lose her voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysphonia).

In the demon world, Gwi-Ma grows enraged at his minions' failures. Led by Jinu, a human-turned-demon, five demons form a [boy band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_band), the Saja Boys, to steal Huntrix's fans and weaken the Honmoon, in exchange for Gwi-Ma promising to erase Jinu's painful human memories. Watching their debut, Huntrix quickly discover the Saja Boys' demonic nature and later attack them. As they fight, Jinu discovers Rumi's patterns, but helps hide them from her bandmates. Meeting privately, he tells her that feelings of shame enslave demons through voices from Gwi-Ma: 400 years prior, Gwi-Ma granted him fame and recognition that helped his family escape poverty, but then condemned him to the demon world; Jinu now lives with guilt over his family's downfall.

The Saja Boys grow more popular, weakening the Honmoon and allowing more demon attacks to occur. With the Idol Awards approaching, Huntrix rush to produce a new song, "[Takedown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takedown_\(song\))", to expose the Saja Boys. Rumi's discomfort with the song's harsh, demon-hating lyrics strains her relationship with Mira and Zoey. Rumi proposes a plan to Jinu: if he helps Huntrix win the awards and strengthen the Honmoon, he could freely stay in the human world. Later, she tells him her shame about her demon heritage weakened her voice, but talking with him healed it. Jinu says he no longer hears voices, thanks likewise to Rumi, and agrees to sabotage the Saja Boys. Gwi-Ma summons Jinu, reminds him he in fact abandoned his family for a life of wealth and comfort, then threatens to amplify the tormenting voices if he reneges on their deal.

At the Idol Awards, Huntrix perform "Golden", having shelved "Takedown" due to the contention it caused between them. However, impostor demons lure Mira and Zoey away from Rumi, while two other demons impersonate them to trick Rumi into performing "Takedown", during which they reveal her patterns. She flees the stage and runs into the real Mira and Zoey, who feel betrayed upon learning of her demonic nature and collusion with Jinu. Realizing his betrayal, Rumi confronts Jinu who admits to lying about his past. Gwi-Ma, strengthened by the influx of consumed souls and decaying Honmoon, enters the human world and casts a trance over the public, drawing them to a Saja Boys performance set to feed him more souls. A desperate Rumi meets with Celine and asks her to end her life. Celine refuses and discusses plans to restore the previous status quo. Rumi lashes out at Celine for never fully loving her and repudiates the now-destroyed Honmoon before leaving.

Rumi interrupts the Saja Boys' performance with an improvised song addressing her shame and self-acceptance, breaking Mira and Zoey out of Gwi-Ma's trance. Reunited, Huntrix fight back and free the crowd. A repentant Jinu sacrifices himself to save Rumi from an attack by Gwi-Ma, giving his restored soul to reinforce her; the empowered Huntrix defeat Gwi-Ma and the remaining Saja Boys, resealing the demons and creating a new rainbow Honmoon. No longer ashamed of her patterns, Rumi celebrates with Mira and Zoey. Afterwards, they meet their fans in public.

## Production

### Development

Maggie Kang directly facing the camera while seated

Creator and co-director [Maggie Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_Kang) in 2026

Director [Maggie Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_Kang) first pitched the idea that eventually became *KPop Demon Hunters* to film producer [Aron Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aron_Warner) in 2018, during which period he was working on *[Wish Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wish_Dragon)* (2021) for [Sony Pictures Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Animation).[^cite_note-26] Development on the film began that same year,[^cite_note-27] and on March 8, 2021, Sony publicly announced the film, under the working title *K-Pop: Demon Hunters*. Kang and [Chris Appelhans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Appelhans) would direct from a script by Hannah McMechan and Danya Jimenez, and Warner and Michelle L. M. Wong would serve as producers.[^cite_note-Variety-2021-4][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Mingjue Helen Chen and Ami Thompson were announced as production designer and art director, respectively.[^cite_note-Variety-2021-4]

To distinguish its visual style from contemporary animated features, Kang based the film on Korean mythology and shamanism.[^cite_note-Gaur-2025-30] She called the film her "love letter to K-pop" and to her Korean background.[^cite_note-Variety-2021-4] Kang explained that when developing the history of the demon hunters, they decided to play into [Korean shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_shamanism), and specifically the historical usage of song and dance as part of the rituals of Korean [shaman women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu_\(shaman\)).[^cite_note-Screen_Rant-2025-31] On character design, Kang said she wanted to differentiate the main characters from "[Marvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Universe) female superheroes that were just sexy and cool and badass" and instead combine those elements with "girls who had potbellies and burped and were crass and silly and fun".[^cite_note-32] She was also influenced by the films of [Bong Joon Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bong_Joon_Ho), which she said would combine multiple tones in a way that evokes animation.[^cite_note-Gaur-2025-30] She and Appelhans cited Bong's monster movie *[The Host](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Host_\(2006_film\))* (2006) as an inspiration for blending genres and moods.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Appelhans had planned to take a long break after directing *Wish Dragon*, but came on board after Kang told him her initial ideas.[^cite_note-Gaur-2025-30] He said that he "always wanted to do a film about the power of music—to unite, bring joy, build community".[^cite_note-Variety-2021-4] McMechan and Jimenez were approached to write by [Nicole Perlman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Perlman); McMechan told *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* that though the two "had never done animation before, and we didn't know anything about K-pop", they were "exactly what \[co-director] Maggie \[Kang] had been looking for, with our friendship and us being young girls".[^cite_note-35]

### Character design



2NE1 smiling for the camera





The members of Huntrix were inspired by various [K-pop girl groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-pop_girl_groups), including 2NE1, Blackpink, Itzy and Twice (*clockwise*).

The name *Huntrix* [portmanteaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) *hunter* with the Latin feminine [agent noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_noun) suffix [*-trix*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/-trix), to evoke the idea of [women warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_warriors_in_literature_and_culture).[^cite_note-36] The three members of Huntrix were modeled after K-pop girl groups like [Itzy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itzy), [Blackpink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpink), [Twice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twice), and [2NE1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2NE1).[^cite_note-Mashable-2025-37][^cite_note-ForbesGroupsInspired-38] The fashion and makeup of the characters took inspiration from [Givenchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Givenchy), [Jean Paul Gaultier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Paul_Gaultier), and [Alexander McQueen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_McQueen), with a focus on "infusing Korean traditional elements with *[haute couture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute_couture)*".[^cite_note-39] The character of Rumi was created in 2016 by Kang and her husband, [Radford Sechrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radford_Sechrist), for Sechrist's comic *Plastic Walrus*; Kang repurposed it for *KPop Demon Hunters*.[^cite_note-Salon-2025a-19][^cite_note-40] The character of Mira was inspired by Korean model Ahn So Yeon (professionally known as [Ellis Ahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Vogue_Korea_cover_models#2023)).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

The Saja Boys were inspired by Korean boy bands such as [Tomorrow X Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomorrow_X_Together), [BTS](39862325.md), [Stray Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stray_Kids), [Ateez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ateez), [BigBang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BigBang_\(South_Korean_band\)), and [Monsta X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsta_X).[^cite_note-ForbesGroupsInspired-38] Korean actor and singer [Cha Eun-woo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha_Eun-woo) was a key influence for Jinu, the group's leader.[^cite_note-44] Additionally, [Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_\(entertainer,_born_1994\)) of the Korean boy band [Exo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exo) served as a reference for Jinu's facial expressions.[^cite_note-45] The names of the members of the Saja Boys, with the exception of Jinu, reflect K-pop archetypes.[^cite_note-Salon-2025b-46] The term *Saja* also alludes to the *[jeoseung saja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeoseung_saja)* ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 저승사자; [Hanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanja): 저승使者), a figure in Korean folklore comparable to the [Grim Reaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grim_Reaper),[^cite_note-Baek-2025-14] and to the Korean word for lion, *saja* (사자; 獅子); the Saja Boys' logo uses a lion head and the band's fandom is called "the [Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion#Group_organisation)".[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

Baek Byung-yeul of *[The Korea Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Korea_Times)* said the styling of both groups connects to "the past and the present of Korea": the members of Huntrix "wear *[norigae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norigae)* pendants integrated into modern K-pop fashion, while Saja Boys perform in black [hanbok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanbok) and [traditional horsehair hats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gat_\(hat\)) for their song '[Your Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Your_Idol),' evoking the image of the jeoseung saja".[^cite_note-Baek-2025-14] Huntrix members wield traditional Korean weapons.[^cite_note-Baek-2025-14] Max Kim of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* noted the nods to Korean artists "who are seen today as the progenitors of contemporary K-pop" such as the [Jeogori Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeogori_Sisters), [The Kim Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kim_Sisters) and [S.E.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.E.S._\(group\))[^cite_note-50] Kang described the visual journey in the character design such as the Saja Boys starting in a "[bubble gum pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_gum_pop), very saccharine, super sweet look" and shifting darker until they fully take on the appearance of the *jeoseung saja*.[^cite_note-Bennett-2025-51] Appelhans said Huntrix's costumes when performing "Golden" symbolize "their kind of [MacGuffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacGuffin) of a dream", aspiring for perfection and irreproachability; the tearing away of Rumi's costume at the end of the film's second act represents the disruption of that dream.[^cite_note-Bennett-2025-51] Appelhans said he wanted the workings of K-pop to be reflected within the film and its plot.[^cite_note-Bennett-2025-51]

### Animation

The film was animated by [Sony Pictures Imageworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Imageworks) in its [Vancouver](32706.md) and [Montreal](7954681.md) facilities with Josh Beveridge as [animation director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animation_director).[^cite_note-Gaur-2025-30][^cite_note-VarietyOct20-58] Appelhans said its visual style was inspired by music videos, [concert stage lighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_lighting), [editorial photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photojournalism), [Korean dramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_drama), and anime.[^cite_note-Gaur-2025-30] Kang said that the team changed its idea after watching Sony's *[Spider-Verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Verse_\(franchise\))* films, celebrated for their hybrid 2D/3D animation. Instead of blending the two techniques, they focused on a [3D animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation#3D_computer_animation) style inspired by the "aesthetics and facial expressions of anime".[^cite_note-Gaur-2025-30] Beveridge similarly noted the inspiration of "[2D aesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_animation) but with three-dimensional language", making the film entirely in 3D [CGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_generated_imagery), and that it should have a "very bold graphic look".[^cite_note-Gaur-2025-30] Beveridge said the character art shifts to reflect the tone: glamorous for triumphant moments, angular and line-heavy for aggressive sequences, and "[chibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibi_\(style\))-esque" for comedy.[^cite_note-Gaur-2025-30] The first major scene to be animated was the initial encounter between Huntrix and the Saja Boys, which Beveridge said acted as a proof of concept for the character designs.[^cite_note-VarietyOct20-58] Kang sought to authentically represent Korean identity through character animation, focusing on facial features like eye and mouth shapes.[^cite_note-Bennett-2025-51] Although the characters speak English, the animators designed their mouth shapes to the Korean language.[^cite_note-Bennett-2025-51] For action scenes, the filmmakers used glitter effects inspired by the South Korean film *[The Villainess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Villainess)* (2017), with Beveridge stating this gave the scenes a feeling of dance-fighting: "You can get pretty violent if you put a lot of glitter on."[^cite_note-VarietyOct20-58] Precise detail was added to represent Korean cultural details such as the [Namsan Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan_Tower), [Korean cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_cuisine), the [Naksan Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_City_Wall), sign swords, and traditional tiger motifs.[^cite_note-ChosunBiz-20252-9]

A vertical painting in watercolor on paper with primarily black, white, and gray tones. It depicts a stylized tiger with a humorous, exaggerated expression and yellow eyes sitting at the bottom and looking up toward a magpie perched on the branch of a gnarled pine tree above.

A Korean folk painting of [the Tiger and the Magpie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minhwa#Magpie_and_tiger)

*[Salon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon.com)'s* Hanh Nguyen wrote that the animation of Jinu's pet tiger and magpie—which the creative team calls Derpy and Sussie respectively[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-BBC_Culture-2025-60]—are based on *[minhwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minhwa)*, a type of Korean folk art that was popular during the [Joseon period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon_period).[^cite_note-Salon-2025a-19] Images of tigers, magpies, and pine trees are the focus of the *Hojak-do* genre of *minhwa*; the portrayal of these animals took cues from the satirical, anti-authority character that *Hojak-do* took on during the 17th century.[^cite_note-Salon-2025a-19][^cite_note-The_Korea_Times-2025a-61] Park Han-sol of *The Korea Times* wrote that *minhwa* was often "whimsical" with "mischievous details", and depictions of tiger and magpie delivered "a playful jab at those in power".[^cite_note-The_Korea_Times-2025a-61] She said the gag of the magpie stealing the tiger's hat "feels like an affectionate nod to the long-running visual joke".[^cite_note-The_Korea_Times-2025a-61] Sechrist, acting as a [story artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Story_artist), designed the tiger with bright blue fur to enhance the character's magical feel, a design choice attributed to production designer Helen Mingjue Chen.[^cite_note-Salon-2025a-19] Kang commented that Derpy was initially conceived without a clear role, but they did not want him to be just a sidekick.[^cite_note-Salon-2025a-19] The team was inspired by a painting by Chen of a tiger statue turning into a real tiger next to an open-shirt Jinu, which led to the idea of Derpy being Jinu's pet, eventually becoming a means for Rumi and Jinu to exchange messages and communicate, "and then the bird tagged along".[^cite_note-Salon-2025a-19] Kang said the animals "live between these two worlds", with their nature left mysterious.[^cite_note-62]

### Choreography

The dance choreography was worked on by Jam Republic's Jo Na-In, [the Black Label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Black_Label)'s [Leejung Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leejung_Lee), and the group K-Tigers.[^cite_note-Billboard-2025-63] Jo, a South Korean dancer and choreographer, created the sequences for "Golden" and "Takedown".[^cite_note-Billboard-2025-63][^cite_note-DanceSpirit-64] The choreography for "[How It's Done](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_It's_Done_\(Huntrix_song\))" was done by Lee, and the choreography for "Your Idol" was done by K-Tigers' Ha Seong-jin. Choreography for the "Soda Pop" sequence was worked on by Lee and Ha.[^cite_note-DanceSpirit-64][^cite_note-HonoraryReporters-65] In an interview, Jo stated that she was inspired by "Golden" itself when developing the choreography for the song, indicating that "I focused on the storytelling that needed to be conveyed through the animation. I tried to concentrate more on expressing the lyrics and emotions."[^cite_note-DanceSpirit-64] On the animation aspect, Jo explained that she "made the movements bigger and more expansive" as well as keeping "close attention to details like facial expressions and fingertips to ensure they come across well onscreen".[^cite_note-Billboard-2025-63]

Fight scene choreography was contributed to by K-Tigers, including Taemin—who acted as the martial arts director—and Ha Seong-jin.[^cite_note-HonoraryReporters-65][^cite_note-66] When interviewed, Ha explained that he "decided to weave [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo) elements into \[the Saja Boys'] choreography. This way, it could showcase taekwondo not just as a sport, but also as something aesthetically powerful."[^cite_note-HonoraryReporters-65] Josh Beveridge, when discussing the fight choreography, noted the deliberate dance-fighting feel to the action, explaining that "\[s]ome of those fight choreographers had done some idol training, and in between the moves, they'd make these cute little adjustments. That is where the personality is."[^cite_note-VarietyOct20-58]

## Themes

Waist-up photo of a cosplayer with a purple braided wig, dressed in Rumi's yellow jacket

[Cosplayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosplayer) dressed as Rumi

The film's plot explores the power of community and friendship in overcoming cultural pressures. Arden Cho, who voices Rumi, said, "Without Mira and Zoey, she wouldn't have made it." According to the filmmakers, Rumi's arc parallels the experience of [coming out](71537.md), especially in terms of parental and societal expectations. Co-director Maggie Kang noted that the character's journey was deliberately written to mirror the process of revealing one's true self: "Rumi's story \[is] like she's coming out of the closet and coming clean to her parents who \[want] her to be something that she's not."[^cite_note-92] Kang commented that when developing Rumi's story they "talked a lot about [mixed heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_heritage)" as well as "[queer identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_identity), and [addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addiction) and falling back into addiction. We kind of described the demon part of you" where someone is hiding part of themselves from others.[^cite_note-Salon-2025b-46]

[Stephen Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Thompson_\(journalist\)) of [NPR](29697232.md) and Taylor Henderson of *[Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_\(magazine\))* magazine both similarly noted aspects of [queer coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer_coding) and queer allegory within Rumi's story,[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-Out-2025-94] with Henderson writing that "\[w]hile there aren't explicitly LGBTQ+ characters in the film, Rumi's struggle feels quite queer-coded. From an early age, \[Celine] instructed her to hide that part of herself from the world. The hiding grew into a deep shame, and she holds contempt for the half-demon part of herself."[^cite_note-Out-2025-94] Sulman Aziz Mirza of *[Psychiatric News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatric_News)* highlighted the focus on "shame, identity, and healing"—in particular, Rumi hiding her demonic heritage and patterns "isolates Rumi in shame, forcing her to skip bonding rituals like [bathhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jjimjilbang) visits and ultimately silencing her voice". However, "Rumi and Jinu, both burdened by shame, begin to heal through music."[^cite_note-Psychiatric_News-2025-95] Mirza called shame a universal experience so "Rumi's patterns could symbolize anything: depression, [queerness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer), trauma, [neurodivergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurodiversity), or being [biracial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biracial)."[^cite_note-Psychiatric_News-2025-95]

Writing for *The Korean Herald*, academic [Kim Seong-kon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Seong-kon) identified the three main themes as: "1. The world is not simply made of good and evil, or angels and demons. 2. Do not be ashamed of differences. 3. Hybridity is a strength, not a weakness."[^cite_note-96] Academics Putu Marvitta Adira Prastiwi and Nissa Puspitaning Adni elaborate on themes of gender equality and [female empowerment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_empowerment), writing "\[t]he solidarity and emotional support among the Huntrix members form\[s] a crucial foundation for building an autonomous and empowering female community, while simultaneously critiquing patriarchal pressures that demanded women hide their imperfections and vulnerabilities. Rumi's internal conflict and rejection of the demand to conceal her true self emphasize\[s] the necessity of dismantling oppressive symbolic systems so that women could express themselves fully and authentically."[^cite_note-97]

## Release

Sony Pictures Animation announced *KPop Demon Hunters* in March 2021 without a release timetable.[^cite_note-Variety-2021-4] In April 2022, it was first reported that Netflix registered a filing for the film.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] The film was confirmed to be coming to Netflix in February 2023 in a *[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider)* interview with [Sony Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures) film CEO [Tom Rothman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Rothman).[^cite_note-100] The release of *KPop Demon Hunters* on Netflix was the result of a 2021 agreement between Netflix and Sony.[^cite_note-Puck-2025-6][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] According to [Matthew Belloni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Belloni) of *[Puck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puck_\(media_company\))*, this agreement entailed Sony giving Netflix "a [first look](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-look_deal) at certain live-action and animated film projects", with Netflix in turn guaranteeing to [greenlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlight) a certain number of projects for joint development, which it would release and have control over.[^cite_note-Puck-2025-6] Under the terms of this deal, Netflix paid Sony \$125 million to cover both the budget for *KPop Demon Hunters* and a premium worth 25% of the budget;[^cite_note-Puck-2025-6][^cite_note-The_Hollywood_Reporter-2025-103] in exchange, Netflix would maintain all film rights, and would not need to pay any profit participation.[^cite_note-Puck-2025-6]

In June 2024, Netflix announced that *KPop Demon Hunters*, a film produced by Sony Pictures Animation, would be released in 2025.[^cite_note-104] In April 2025, the film was confirmed for a June 20 release.[^cite_note-Voice_Cast_Netflix_PR-1] In addition to streaming on Netflix, *KPop Demon Hunters* had a [limited theatrical release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_theatrical_release) at select cinemas in California and New York from June 20–26.[^cite_note-Oscar_eligibility-105] A [sing-along](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sing-along) version of the film was released theatrically in select theaters in North America, the UK, Australia, and New Zealand from August 23–24, 2025.[^cite_note-106] The sing-along version was subsequently released on Netflix, separately from the original version.[^cite_note-107] The film then had a second theatrical sing-along release from October 31 – November 2.[^cite_note-108]

The June 2025 theatrical release in the United States was enough to satisfy the eligibility requirements for the [Best Animated Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Feature) and [Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song) categories at the [Oscars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscars).[^cite_note-Oscar_eligibility-105][^cite_note-109] The subsequent international theatrical releases did not meet the requirements to qualify for the [BAFTA Awards](150200.md), due to the British theatrical run occurring two months after the streaming release, along with not having enough commercial screenings.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-Deadline-2025b-111] The British Academy rejected Netflix's exception appeal; according to *[Deadline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood)*, *KPop Demon Hunters* would have qualified if Netflix were to have given the film a simultaneous release in British cinemas and on streaming.[^cite_note-Deadline-2025b-111]

### Home media

The film is scheduled to be released on [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD), [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray), and [4K Ultra HD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray) on November 3, 2026,[^cite_note-112] as part of [the Criterion Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Criterion_Collection).[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

## Reception

### Viewership

On July 29, 2025, Netflix announced that *KPop Demon Hunters* had become the platform's "most watched original animated film of all time".[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] On August 26, Netflix said it had been watched 236 million times, passing *[Red Notice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Notice_\(film\))* (2021) as its most-watched film.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] In late August, *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* cited Netflix data showing the film's steady performance throughout the summer.[^cite_note-121] On September 3, Netflix stated it had passed 266 million views, overtaking the [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squid_Game_season_1) of *[Squid Game](68455171.md)* (2021) to become Netflix's most-watched title;[^cite_note-122] by 91 days after its release (the cutoff used by Netflix for recording official viewership), *KPop Demon Hunters* reached 325.1 million views.[^cite_note-123] As of December 24, it had received more that 500 million views.[^cite_note-124] [Nielsen Media Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research), which records streaming viewership on certain US television screens, reported that *KPop Demon Hunters* garnered 20.5 billion minutes of watch time in 2025, ranking as the most-streamed film of the year.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

### Box office

The sing-along version of *KPop Demon Hunters* was released in theaters on August 23, 2025.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-Variety-2025-128] It debuted in 1,700 theaters, dethroning the 698-theatre release of Netflix's *[Glass Onion: A Knives Out Mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_Onion:_A_Knives_Out_Mystery)* (2022). It was initially projected to gross \$18 million to \$20 million during its opening weekend.[^cite_note-AVClub-2025-129] It was the company's best-performing theatrical release, beating out *Glass Onion* (\$13.1 million in 2022).[^cite_note-Deadline-2025a-130] It was the first Netflix film to finish in first place over the weekend.[^cite_note-The_Wall_Street_Journal-2025-131] Netflix did not report grosses.[^cite_note-Variety-2025-128] In the United States and Canada, *KPop Demon Hunters* made \$19.2 million,[^cite_note-The_Numbers-2025-8][^cite_note-Deadline-2025a-130] ahead of *[Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_\(2025_film\))*, which was expected to remain in first during its third weekend.[^cite_note-Deadline-2025a-130]

Jeremy Fuster of *[TheWrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheWrap)* wrote that its theatrical release was set to open higher than the three-day opening weekends of *[The Bad Guys 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bad_Guys_2)* (\$22 million) and *[Elio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elio_\(film\))* (\$20.8 million); he said it would have made \$100 million domestically had it not first been streamed.[^cite_note-132] Matt Schimkowitz of *[The A.V. Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A.V._Club)* wrote that the theatrical release drew many moviegoers who had likely streamed it.[^cite_note-AVClub-2025-129] Ben Fritz of *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* noted that the acclaim led Netflix to make it a rare theatrical release.[^cite_note-The_Wall_Street_Journal-2025-131] Matthew Belloni of *Puck* noted that, due to the arrangement between Netflix and Sony for the production, most of the profit, including from any potentially subsequent franchise, would be accrued by Netflix, the distributor, rather than by Sony. For Netflix's film chief [Dan Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Lin), "this is a studio chairman's dream: a relatively cheap superhit" with evident avenues for exploitation across multiple businesses being available, whereas Sony's film chief Tom Rothman "gotta be kicking himself over this one".[^cite_note-Puck-2025-6]

### Critical response

*KPop Demon Hunters* received wide acclaim from both critics and audiences,[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] who praised its animation, music, voice acting, and story.[^cite_note-135] On the [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator) website [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), 92% of 106 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 7.7/10. The website's consensus reads: "Animated with infectious energy and vibrant colors, *KPop Demon Hunters* is jaunty family entertainment with a terrific soundtrack to boot."[^cite_note-136] [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic), which uses a [weighted average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean), assigned the film a score of 77 out of 100, based on 10 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.[^cite_note-137]

Brandon Yu, for *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, wrote that *KPop Demon Hunters* "is an original universe that is charming, funny and artfully punchy". Yu said it shared the same cinematic approach as the *Spider-Verse* animated films, also produced by Sony Pictures Animation, noting both as featuring effective usages of fluid action, striking art, and music that serves the storytelling.[^cite_note-NYT_review-138] Toussaint Egan of *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN)* similarly compared *KPop Demon Hunters* to *Spider-Verse*, while also highlighting the fight sequences, which he described as having "flashy choreography and anime-inflected verve".[^cite_note-IGN_review-139] Brian Tallerico of [RogerEbert.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RogerEbert.com) considered the visuals and artwork to be essential parts of the film's success, saying that they elevate storytelling and themes.[^cite_note-RogerEbert.com_review-140] Jeff Ewing of *[Collider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collider_\(website\)),* while praising the animation and catchiness of the songs, gave attention the strength of the voice cast, in particular the performances of Cho, Hong, Yoo, and Ahn.[^cite_note-141] Bill Goodykoontz of *[The Arizona Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Arizona_Republic)* praised the film for both its genre-hopping tone and its [family-friendliness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family-friendliness).[^cite_note-AzCentral_review-142] Kelechi Ehenulo of *[Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_\(magazine\))* described the film as "a cross between *[Buffy the Vampire Slayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffy_the_Vampire_Slayer)* and *[Popstars: The Rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popstars:_The_Rivals)*", praising the watchability, comedy, and story about shame, generationally-imposed emotional burdens, and embracing who you are.[^cite_note-Empire_review-143] Kevin Maher of *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times),* while describing the film as a "dazzling blast of kitsch ephemera" appearing on its surface as "a high-concept fever dream", focussed praise on how the themes of shame, anxiety, and mental health in both Rumi's story and the song "Golden" were able to resinate with the film's audience.[^cite_note-144]

Matt Goldberg of *TheWrap* commented that the plot would "be painfully overwrought" were it not for the comedy.[^cite_note-TheWrap_review-145] Goldberg and Yu felt its best comedy came from poking fun at the culture and tropes surrounding K-pop, K-dramas, and other K-media.[^cite_note-TheWrap_review-145][^cite_note-NYT_review-138] David Tizzard of *The Korea Times* said *KPop Demon Hunters* was both "a love letter to K-pop", and an unflinchingly critical examination of K-pop culture. He also praised the representation of Korean culture, stating that it captures and displays aspects of everyday life in South Korea "with an intimacy rarely seen in global content."[^cite_note-Korea_Times_review_2025-146] Tizzard and Peter Debruge of *Variety* both highlighted the usage of the magpie and tiger,[^cite_note-Korea_Times_review_2025-146][^cite_note-Variety_review-147] which Tizzard noted as being affectionately reinterpreted from Korean tradition.[^cite_note-Korea_Times_review_2025-146]

Isaiah Colbert of *[Io9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Io9)* praised the authentic musicality, such that even those watchers who are not fans of K-pop "would find themselves bobbing their head and humming along".[^cite_note-148] Debruge commented that because of the setting and immersion within the world of K-pop, the audience might not fully recognize the film is a [musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_film).[^cite_note-Variety_review-147] Wilson Chapman of *[IndieWire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndieWire)* thought the most impressive aspect of the music was the varied ways in which the songs are staged, such as a demon fight for "How It's Done", an opulent music video for "Golden", a creation montage for "Takedown", and an energetic dance number for "Soda Pop". Chapman considered this variation and versatility of the staging to be the means through which the K-pop elements are "a central and vital component of its storytelling", rather than a gimmick.[^cite_note-IndieWire_review-2025-149] Similarly, Tizzard, Goldberg, and Goodykoontz all noted the integration of the music and narrative,[^cite_note-AzCentral_review-142][^cite_note-TheWrap_review-145][^cite_note-Korea_Times_review_2025-146] with Tizzard commenting that the songs "aren't throwaway jingles; they're emotional climaxes",[^cite_note-Korea_Times_review_2025-146] Goldberg that the songs, while catchy, have plot stakes that serve to maintain tension, and Goodykoontz that the songs "don't just serve the story, but drive it."[^cite_note-TheWrap_review-145]

Angela Garcia of *[SLUG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLUG_\(magazine\))* wrote that due to the short runtime, *KPop Demon Hunters* "\[feels] like it's only scratched the surface of both the world and characters", with many ideas having been left underdeveloped.[^cite_note-150] Chapman also highlighted the short runtime, which he felt was both a benefit and the main flaw; by moving quickly, the film lacks depth at points, such as Mira and Zoey not receiving enough story focus, or not fully exploring Rumi's resentment towards Celine, and results in a rushed, underwhelming climax that provides an overly easy resolution.[^cite_note-IndieWire_review-2025-149] Goldberg also noted an occasional tonal whiplash, and argued that stronger [character arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_arc) were needed for Zoey and Mira leading into the climax to bridge the divide between the [comic relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_relief) and its more emotional moments.[^cite_note-TheWrap_review-145] Tallerico felt that the film works best when it moves at a speedy pace, considering its slower moments to be comparatively weaker and repetitive. Tallerico attributed this to the film "ha\[ving] too many characters over-explain\[ing] their emotional crutches and what they need to do to overcome them", and further argued that the film should have relied less on dialogue and more heavily on music and visuals for storytelling.[^cite_note-RogerEbert.com_review-140] Though Ehenulo found some aspects predictable and rushed, he nonetheless stated that the film deserved its success, praising its energy, music, and themes.[^cite_note-Empire_review-143]

### Accolades

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of accolades received by *KPop Demon Hunters*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accolades_received_by_KPop_Demon_Hunters).

*KPop Demon Hunters* garnered various awards, nominations, and accolades. It won Best Animated Feature at the [31st Critics' Choice Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Critics'_Choice_Awards),[^cite_note-CriticsChoice-151] the [83rd Golden Globe Awards](81659305.md),[^cite_note-GoldenGlobes-152] and the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md),[^cite_note-OscarWins-153] along with being featured in *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* as its 2025 Breakthrough of the Year.[^cite_note-154] The film also won ten awards at the [53rd Annie Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Annie_Awards), including for [Best Feature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Award_for_Best_Animated_Feature).[^cite_note-155] The song "Golden", from the soundtrack, won Best Original Song at the Critics' Choice Awards,[^cite_note-CriticsChoice-151] Golden Globes,[^cite_note-GoldenGlobes-152] and Academy Awards.[^cite_note-OscarWins-153] The song won [Best Song Written for Visual Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Song_Written_for_Visual_Media) at the [68th Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/68th_Grammy_Awards),[^cite_note-156] and won Best OST at the [2025 Melon Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Melon_Music_Awards) and [2025 MAMA Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_MAMA_Awards);[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] the song is the first K-pop song to win a Grammy Award or an Academy Award.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] Ejae, Audrey Nuna, and Rei Ami were honored at the 2026 [Billboard Women in Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Women_in_Music) for their work as the singing voices of Huntrix; this marked the first time a group had been collectively awarded.[^cite_note-162]

## Cultural impact

Group shot of four people wearing cosplay. The person on the left is dressed as Bobby. The other three are wearing Huntrix "Golden" outfits; one person is slightly crouched in front of the other two, holding a small blue tiger plush.

Attendees of a theatrical sing-along screening, cosplaying as the film's characters

*KPop Demon Hunters* furthered the visibility and popularity of South Korean culture as part of the [Korean Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Wave) and inspired an expansive [fandom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fandom). The film's popularity has been described as surprising and unexpected;[^cite_note-163] its streaming success was compared by Nate Jones of *[Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_\(website\))* to that of [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Pictures)'s *[Encanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encanto)* (2021).[^cite_note-164] Drew Taylor of *TheWrap* described it as a widespread phenomenon, citing the popularity of the soundtrack album, and Netflix's official shop adding a *KPop Demon Hunters* section, with T-shirts and plushies being among top five best-selling items of 2025.[^cite_note-TheWrap-2025-165] Others called the film an "animated sensation",[^cite_note-166] a "potential gold mine" for Netflix,[^cite_note-167] a "smash hit movie",[^cite_note-168] and the "biggest hit of Hollywood's summer".[^cite_note-169] *[The Cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cut_\(publication\))* said the characters, storyline, and soundtrack as reasons for its popularity, with Yoohyun Jung of *[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe)* suggesting that integration of Korean culture into the story was another reason for why it is popular.[^cite_note-170]

In late September, [Luminate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminate_\(company\)) made a presentation at the All That Matters conference in [Singapore](27318.md) about insights from global and [APAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific) music, saying that multimedia cross-promotion helped the performance, with over two-thirds of audio streams from the soundtrack coming from outside of the United States. Luminate also described the film as a "transmedia hit".[^cite_note-171] Sara Leila Sherman and Morton Sherman wrote in *[Psychology Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology_Today)* that *KPop Demon Hunters* became "more than entertainment", with sing-alongs bringing people together.[^cite_note-172] On September 12, Lars Brandle reported on *Billboard* that the soundtrack made up seven of the top twenty songs on the [ARIA Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARIA_Singles_Chart), and had led the chart for seven consecutive weeks.[^cite_note-173] Writing for *Billboard* that day, Thomas Smith noted that "Golden" topped the UK charts for a sixth consecutive week, becoming only the second K-pop song to do so.[^cite_note-174]

The film also made its way into sports and other popular culture. On September 2 at the [US Open](82729.md), [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md), after winning a tennis match with [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz), did a dance inspired by the song "Soda Pop".[^cite_note-175] The film was also featured in a *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* sketch on its [season premiere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live_season_51) hosted by [Bad Bunny](55943877.md), and included cameos from Ejae, Audrey Nuna and Rei Ami performing 30-second snippets from the songs "Golden" and "How It's Done" live, with [Bowen Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowen_Yang) portraying the character Jinu and singing lyrics from "Soda Pop".[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178] On October 7, Ejae, Nuna and Rei Ami gave a full-length performance of "Golden" during an episode of *[The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_Starring_Jimmy_Fallon)*, along with being interviewed by the host.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181] The singers repeated a performance of "Golden" during the 99th annual [Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy's_Thanksgiving_Day_Parade) in New York City on November 27, alongside a balloon themed after Derpy Tiger and a balloonicle themed after Sussie.[^cite_note-182]

### Korean Wave

*KPop Demon Hunters* has been noted within the context of the Korean Wave, a global increase in popularity of [South Korean culture and media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_South_Korea) that has acted as a form of [soft power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_power) for the country.[^cite_note-Foreign_Policy-2025-11][^cite_note-FanSupalakwatchana-183][^cite_note-184] Some, like Korean cultural scholar Park Jihyon, said that the popularity could likely become a major turning point in the Korean Wave's history.[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-2025a-185] Researchers such as Xin Fan and Salas Supalakwatchana have commented on the film as "a major cultural product within the Korean soft power landscape", and as a demonstration of the medium of animation as being "capable of merging entertainment value with strategic cultural promotion".[^cite_note-FanSupalakwatchana-183] Cho Jae-hyon of *[Hankook Ilbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankook_Ilbo)* opined that *KPop Demon Hunters* represents a new phase of the Korean Wave, in which Korean-themed content would be produced abroad rather than being created within South Korea and then exported. With *KPop Demon Hunters*, Cho highlighted the production team as largely being non-Korean.[^cite_note-The_Korea_Times-2025b-186] Moon Dong-yeol of *[The Hankyoreh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hankyoreh)* similarly argued that *KPop Demon Hunters* represents a denationalization of the Korean Wave, not being limited to production solely within South Korea.[^cite_note-187]

Both Cho and Min Seong-jae of *The Korea Times* have highlighted this new phase of the Korean Wave as being influenced by [Korean diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_diaspora) creators,[^cite_note-The_Korea_Times-2025b-186][^cite_note-188] with Cho noting that "Maggie Kang, a Korean Canadian, drew on her childhood memories of K-content to create the film."[^cite_note-The_Korea_Times-2025b-186] Grace Kao, for *[Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy)*, similarly noted the presence of many Korean diaspora songwriters and artists as part of the making of *KPop Demon Hunters*, and compared this to the indebtedness of K-pop to diaspora Koreans participating in music industries of the United States, Canada, and Australia.[^cite_note-Foreign_Policy-2025-11] Kao additionally characterized *KPop Demon Hunters* as a rebuke of a localization trend within K-pop, where local cultural elements are emphasized over strictly Korean ones.[^cite_note-Foreign_Policy-2025-11] She said it "shows that consumers are not looking for a product stripped of Korean cultural elements or Koreans themselves" and that it is not necessary for media to emulate American pop culture to achieve success.[^cite_note-Foreign_Policy-2025-11]

Michelle Yee Hee Lee of *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* wrote that *KPop Demon Hunters* had been overwhelmingly embraced by South Korea, even by businesses and government, due to its broad appeal as an animated film, with products and events based around the film.[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-2025a-185] Hae-rin Lee of *The Korea Times* said the global popularity boosted [tourism in South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_South_Korea), with fans interested in South Korea pop culture visiting recording studios, museum shops, cafés, and other attractions, including locations shown in the film, such as [Namsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan), [Naksan Park Fortress Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_City_Wall#Trail_tour), and [Bukchon Hanok Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukchon_Hanok_Village). Some businesses have released products related to the film, while fans are said to be embracing the food the main characters are shown eating.[^cite_note-189] The [National Museum of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Korea) in Seoul drew more than five million visitors, an unprecedented total that ranks it alongside the [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art) in New York City and the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum) in London. This surge was partly credited to the widespread popularity of *KPop Demon Hunters*.[^cite_note-190] Seoul's [Leeum Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeum_Museum_of_Art) opened an exhibition focused on tiger and magpie *minhwa*, including a *Hojak-do* painting from 1592 which is considered the oldest surviving piece in this style.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] Sookyung Seo of *[The Korea Economic Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Korea_Economic_Daily)* wrote that, partially due to the film's popularity, over 1.36 million international tourists were drawn to Seoul in July 2025, an increase of 23.1% from the same month in 2024, and of 18% from July 2019, according to the [Seoul Metropolitan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Government)'s data, and said to be the "largest monthly tally ever recorded".[^cite_note-193]

### Fandom

Group shot taken from the stage, facing the audience. The celebrities are crouched down with their backs to the audience while the audience is mostly standing to pose for the photo. Many people in the audience are wearing cosplay.

[May Hong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Hong), Danny Chung, Alan Lee, the stage host, and fans at [GalaxyCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GalaxyCon) in [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri) on October 11, 2025

*KPop Demon Hunters* has been noted as a cultural phenomenon by a variety of media outlets, many highlighting the soundtrack, characters, and story, as well as homages to anime and representation of fandom and Asian cultures.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196] Others noted that dance sequences had become popular on the social media platform [TikTok](56822861.md), and that [fanart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanart) had flooded other social media websites.[^cite_note-TheWrap-2025-165] Within South Korea, fans called the fandom "KeDeHun".[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-2025a-185] The film's success was also seen as a success for its representation of Asian characters in pop culture, with children not having to look to "[Pixar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixar) or [Disney princesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_princess) for costume inspiration".[^cite_note-197]

[BBC News](1139893.md) reported on dance classes themed on K-pop, particularly songs from the film, in [Belfast](5046.md), and said that K-pop has gained increased visibility.[^cite_note-198] Alex Vadukul of *[The New York Times](30680.md)* noted that an intense fandom had formed around Huntrix and the Saja Boys, and noted that [May Hong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Hong), who voices Mira, said it was "crazy being on the receiving end of \[the film's] fandom."[^cite_note-199] Yvonne Kim of *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)* said that *KPop Demon Hunters* transforms fandom "from a hobby into an action of creation". She added that it acknowledges the "sometimes-parasitic relationship between artist and listener" and the "unilateral power" of performers, and said that the fans are not different from their real-life counterparts.[^cite_note-200]

There has been an [internet challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_challenge) involving ramen noodles, based on [ramyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramyun) eaten by Rumi, Zoey, and Mira, while singing the song "How It's Done". It was reported that this resulted in severe burns and hospitalization among some fans who tried to recreate this scene, with doctors and hospitals warning about the dangers of eating instant noodles in extremely hot water, suggesting people eat the noodles more carefully.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203] During the 2025 [Halloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween) season, *KPop Demon Hunters*-related [costumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween_costume) saw significant popularity, with [Google searches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_search) for costumes of the characters dominating search rankings,[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206] and with costumes from major retailers like [Spirit Halloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_Halloween) quickly becoming sold out.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208] Local news media from across the United States similarly noted the high popularity of *KPop Demon Hunters* costumes.[^cite_note-209]

## Future

### Sequel

In interviews with *[Screen Rant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_Rant)* and *Variety*, director Maggie Kang expressed interest in a sequel and side stories to flesh out the *KPop Demon Hunters* universe.[^cite_note-Screen_Rant-2025-31][^cite_note-VarietySequel-210] She said that many questions that were raised remain unanswered fully, with additional stories capable of being explored,[^cite_note-Screen_Rant-2025-31] such as potential backstories for Mira and Zoey.[^cite_note-VarietySequel-210] Director Chris Appelhans likewise told *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_\(magazine\))* that there were many pathways available for further stories.[^cite_note-211] Matthew Belloni of *Puck* noted that, under the 2021 agreement between Sony and Netflix, Sony contractually holds the right to produce future installments of *KPop Demon Hunters*; however, any such installments would likely, just as for the first film, be released for streaming onto Netflix.[^cite_note-Puck-2025-6]

In July 2025, *TheWrap* reported that, following the success, Netflix had begun considering several potential follow-up projects, including two sequels, a television series, a short film, and a stage musical.[^cite_note-212] In a statement to *[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek)*, Netflix Films chairman [Dan Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Lin) commented that the company was "excited to explore what could be the next adventure for Huntrix", while further stating that time would need to be taken for producing any future sequels, in order to ensure that the "flair and uniqueness" of the original film is retained.[^cite_note-213] In August 2025, Belloni reported that Sony had just begun negotiations with Kang and Appelhans to both return for a follow-up project.[^cite_note-Puck-2025-6] On August 26, Pamela McClintock of *The Hollywood Reporter* stated that Sony Pictures and Netflix were in talks for the production of an animated sequel.[^cite_note-The_Hollywood_Reporter-2025-103] On November 5, 2025, it was announced that a sequel was planned for release in 2029.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216] However, in February 2026, Sony Pictures Animation president Kristine Belson suggested that the sequel would likely not be finished by 2029.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218] In March 2026, Kang and Appelhans were announced to be returning to write and direct.[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220] In the following month, Kang stated that the sequel is planned to be larger in scale and more eventful than its predecessor, while Ejae showed interest in exploring [trot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trot_\(music\)) and [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) in the planned film.[^cite_note-221] In September 2026, Netflix co-CEO Ted Sarandos confirmed that the film would receive a broad theatrical release.[^cite_note-222]

### Short film

On March 29, 2023, Sony announced that their LENS mentorship program would produce a short film based on *KPop Demon Hunters* after the release of the [Spider-Verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Verse_\(franchise\)) short film *[The Spider Within](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spider_Within)*.[^cite_note-223] In September 2025, the film, *Debut: A KPop Demon Hunters Story*, was [rated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Association_film_rating_system) PG by the [MPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Association).[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225]

### Other media

Rumi, Mira, and Zoey were added to the video game *[Fortnite](57813623.md)* as purchasable [outfits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_\(video_games\)) on October 2, 2025, alongside a *KPop Demon Hunters*-themed limited time mode and items.[^cite_note-226] On February 5, 2026, a second wave of *Fortnite* cosmetics were revealed, including Huntrix in their outfits from *Golden* and a Jinu skin.[^cite_note-227] In October 2025, it was announced that Netflix granted the global *KPop Demon Hunters* master toy licenses to both [Hasbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasbro) and [Mattel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattel), with toys scheduled to be sold at retail beginning in 2026.[^cite_note-228] On November 28, a digital artbook was released online; a physical edition was published in February 2026.[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230] To coincide with [Spotify Wrapped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify_Wrapped) for 2025, a brief animated video was released of Huntrix thanking their real-life fans for listening to "Golden".[^cite_note-231] In February 2026, it was announced that a [graphic novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_novel) adaptation of the film was scheduled for release in March 2026.[^cite_note-232] Between April 2026 and May 2026, the video game *[Cookie Run: Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Run:_Kingdom)* ran a collaboration update based on the film.[^cite_note-233] Mira, Rumi, Zoey, Jinu, Derpy and Sussie are available as playable characters in the Netflix Games version of *[Overcooked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overcooked).*[^cite_note-234]
