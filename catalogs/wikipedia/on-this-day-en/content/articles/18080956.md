**Kafr 'Aqab** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): كفر عقب) is the northernmost [Palestinian Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) neighborhood in [East Jerusalem](343508.md). It is part of the area annexed and included in municipal Jerusalem following its occupation by Israel in 1967. This area includes an additional approximate 64 km<sup>2</sup> (25 sq mi)[^cite_note-4] of the West Bank, including territory which previously included 28 villages and areas of the [Bethlehem](4312.md) and [Beit Jala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Jala) municipalities.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] Although the [Jerusalem Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Law) did not use the term, the [Israeli Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Supreme_Court) interpreted the law as an effective annexation of East Jerusalem.[^cite_note-Benvenisti2012-7] The [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) condemned the attempted change in status to Jerusalem and ruled the law "null and void" in [United Nations Security Council Resolution 478](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_478).

Due to the [Separation Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier) effectively cutting the neighborhood off from the rest of Jerusalem, while no such barrier exists between this neighborhood and [Ramallah](44132.md) and [Al-Bireh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bireh), this neighborhood is practically part of the metropolitan area of [Ramallah](44132.md).

## Location

Kafr 'Aqab is located 11.2 kilometers (7.0 mi) north of Central [Jerusalem](16043.md) and 2 kilometers (1.2 mi) southeast of [Ramallah](44132.md).

It is bordered by [Burqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burqa,_Ramallah) lands to the east, [Al Bireh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bireh) to the north, [Rafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafat,_Jerusalem) and [Qalandiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalandiya) to the west, and [Ar Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ar_Ram), Qalandiya and Qalandiya Camp to the south.[^cite_note-ARIJp4-8]

## History

The [Palestine Exploration Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)* (SWP) suggested that Kafr 'Aqab was the [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states) village *Kefreachab*, which was one of 21 villages given by King [Godfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfrey_of_Bouillon) as a [fief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief) to the [Church of the Holy Sepulchre](7810.md).[^cite_note-SWP11-9][^cite_note-10]

### Ottoman era

In 1517, the village was included in the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) with the rest of [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) and in the 1596 [tax-records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter) it appeared as *Kafr 'Aqba*, located in the *[Nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* of Jabal Quds of the *[Liwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_\(Arabic\))* of [Al-Quds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quds). The population was 47 households, all [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). They paid a fixed tax rate of 33,3% on agricultural products, including wheat, barley, olive trees, vineyards, fruit trees, goats and beehives in addition to "occasional revenues"; a total of 3,100 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e).[^cite_note-11]

In 1838, [Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) noted Kafr 'Aqab during his travels in the region,[^cite_note-12] as a Muslim village, part of *[El-Kuds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El-Kuds)* district.[^cite_note-13]

An official Ottoman village list sometime around 1870 listed *Kefr 'Akab* as having 15 houses and a population of 65, though the population count included men, only.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] In 1883, SWP described it as "a small [hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet_\(place\)) on the slope of a hillside, with a few olives."[^cite_note-16]

In 1896 the population of *Kefr 'akab* was estimated to be about 135 persons.[^cite_note-17]

### British Mandate era

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), Kafr 'Aqab had a population of 189 Muslims,[^cite_note-Census1922-18] increasing in the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) to 250 Muslims, in 59 houses.[^cite_note-Census1931-19]

In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) the population of Kafr 'Aqab consisted of 290 Muslims[^cite_note-1945p25-20] and the land area was 5,472 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam), according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-Hadawi57-21] Of this, 829 dunams were for plantations and irrigable land, 2,736 for cereals,[^cite_note-22] while 10 dunams were built-up areas.[^cite_note-23]

### Jordanian era

In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and after the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), Kafr 'Aqab came under [Jordanian](7515964.md) rule, and was annexed in 1950.

In 1961, the population was 410 persons.[^cite_note-24]

### Post-1967

After the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Kafr 'Aqab has been under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md). The population in the 1967 census conducted by the Israeli authorities was 287, of whom 8 originated from the Israeli territory.[^cite_note-67census-25]

Israel has confiscated 2,037 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of land from Kafr 'Aqab in order to construct the [Israeli settlement](15123.md) of [Kokhav Ya'acov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokhav_Ya'acov).[^cite_note-ARIJp15-26]

According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics), in 2006, Kafr 'Aqab had a population of 10,411.[^cite_note-27] Primary health care for Kafr 'Aqab is obtained in [Al-Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ram).[^cite_note-28] In 2007, Kfar 'Aqab was described as a middle class suburb.[^cite_note-Telegraph-29]

While Kafr 'Aqab was unilaterally annexed by [Israel](9282173.md) with the rest of East Jerusalem and falls under its full jurisdiction, it is separated from Jerusalem by the [Israeli West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier). Municipal inspectors never inspect the area beyond the barrier. This creates "a planning nightmare and developer's dream".[^cite_note-Haaretz-30]

The al-Faruq mosque in Kafr 'Aqab offers weekly classes in the Islamic teachings of [Hizb ut-Tahrir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hizb_ut-Tahrir).[^cite_note-Telegraph-29]

### 2020s

The Israeli Construction and Housing Ministry began preparing the master plan for a new Israeli neighborhood to be located at the northern edge of Jerusalem on land near the former Atarot airport currently inhabited by some 15 Palestinian families and will stretch up to the separation barrier, which will be the border between the new neighborhood and the Palestinian Kafr Aqab neighborhood. The land has been slated to become part of the Palestinian state under U.S. President Donald Trump's [peace plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Trump_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_plan). Though Kafr Aqab is considered to be part of Jerusalem, it is on the other side of the barrier wall.[^cite_note-31]
