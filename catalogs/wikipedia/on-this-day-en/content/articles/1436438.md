**Ogun State** [Listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b3/Ogun_State_03.ogg/Ogun_State_03.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> ([Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language): *Ìpínlẹ̀ Ògùn* [\[ìk͡pĩ́lɛ̀ ògũ̀\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Yoruba)) is a [state](226734.md) in [southwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_\(Nigeria\)) [Nigeria](21383.md). It is bordered to the south by [Lagos State](626168.md) and the [Bight of Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bight_of_Benin), to the east by [Ondo State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondo_State), and to the north by [Oyo](2227658.md) and [Osun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osun_State) states while its western border forms part of the [national border with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin%E2%80%93Nigeria_border) the [Republic of Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Benin). The capital and largest city is [Abeokuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeokuta), and the state is divided into 20 local government areas.

Of the 36 states in the country, Ogun is the [24th largest in area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_area) but among the [top fifteen most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_population), with an estimated population of about 6.4 million as of 2020. Geographically, the state lies primarily in the tropical [Nigerian lowland forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_lowland_forests) [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ecoregions_in_Nigeria), although parts of the state's north transition into the [Guinean forest–savanna mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_forest%E2%80%93savanna_mosaic) and some of the coastal south reach the [Central African mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_mangroves) [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ecoregions_in_Nigeria). The [Ogun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogun_River) and [Yewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yewa_River) rivers are the state's major waterways while the [Omo Forest Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omo_Forest_Reserve) in the southeastern part of the state is one of the most important conservation areas in the country—home to a variety of bird species along with some of Nigeria's last remaining [Nigeria-Cameroon chimpanzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria-Cameroon_chimpanzee) and [African forest elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_forest_elephant) populations.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Demographically, the [Yoruba people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people) are the largest ethnic group in the state—particularly the [Awori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awori_people), [Egba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egba_people), [Ijebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijebu_people), and [Yewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yewa) subgroups, as well as smaller groups like the [Ketu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketu_people), [Ohori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohori_people), [Ilaje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaje), [Ikale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okitipupa) and Anago.[^cite_note-6] Additionally, there are ethnic minorities of non-indigenous groups in urban areas and indigenous [Egun people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogu_people) along the border with Benin. In terms of religion, the majority of the state's population are Christian with a significant Muslim minority.

Historically, parts of modern Ogun State were included in several kingdoms, including the [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Benin), [Ijebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijebu_Kingdom), and [Oyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyo_Empire) states. In the late nineteenth century, British expeditions took control of the area and Abeokuta became a major center of missionary activity and education. During the early colonial period, the area was part of the [Southern Nigeria Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Nigeria_Protectorate), which later merged into [colonial Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Nigeria). After independence in 1960, the region was part of the [Western Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_State_\(Nigeria\)) until the creation of Ogun State in 1976 from the old [Western State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_State_\(Nigeria\)).

Economically, Ogun State is a major industrial hub with a growing base of factories and companies, especially along the [Lagos–Ibadan Expressway](46727447.md) corridor. Agriculture remains vital in rural areas, with key crops including [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean), and maize along with the indigenous [Ofada rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofada_rice). The state is also known for its cultural heritage, crafts, and textile traditions along with the arts. Ogun ranks in the [mid-range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_Human_Development_Index) in [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) and has the [eighth highest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_GDP) GDP in the country.

## History

In pre-colonial times, today's Ogun western portion which is now inhabited by the Egba and Yewa people belonged to the [kingdom of Oyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyo_Empire), which sank into civil war mid 1800s. South of Ogun, on the tiny island of [Lagos](85232.md), the British had a naval base near which the town of the same name grew rapidly.

Until the [Berlin Congo Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Conference) in 1885, Great Britain had focused on a few strategically placed bases for its merchant fleet and navy, such as Lagos and [Calabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabar), and was not interested in the communities developing there.

After the European colonial powers had staked out their spheres of interest 1885 in [Berlin](3354.md) (these were only valid if another power had not previously brought the area in question under its control) the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) quickly expanded thusly its territory in the assigned Niger region. Today's Ogun became part of the "Protectorate of Lagos" (as opposed to the [*Colony* of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Lagos); the border between these two is identical to the modern border between [Lagos State](626168.md) and Ogun State—inhabitants of a colony were treated as fully entitled subjects of the British crown, those in protectorates were not) in 1893 and later of the "Protectorate of Yorubaland", in 1906 of the "Protectorate of Southern Nigeria" and in 1914 of the whole of Nigeria. In 1899 it received a railway connection to Lagos, the "Boat Express" ran through Ogun to [Apapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apapa) and thus connected the region with the wider world. In 1899, it was several years earlier in this than other regions in West and Central Africa that were not connected to the coast.

In the 1930s, Ogun was a centre of the [Nigerian women's movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeokuta_Women's_Revolt) under the leadership of [Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funmilayo_Ransome-Kuti) ([Fela Kuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fela_Kuti)'s mother). Democracy in colonial Nigeria after 1922 only existed in Lagos and Calabar; Nigerians could not participate politically elsewhere (see [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nigeria#Governor_Clifford,_the_Clifford_Constitution)).

During the 1940s, food was strictly rationed in Nigeria. The transport of food from the more agrarian Ogun to the hungry metropolis of Lagos was severely penalized (Pullen Scheme, see [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nigeria#Rationing,_price_control,_agricultural_damage,_education_offensive)).

In the first elections in Ogun, 1954, the semi-socialist "Action Group" (AG) under [Ọbáfẹ́mi Awólọ́wọ̀](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obafemi_Awolowo) became the strongest party in the Western Region, to which Ogun also belonged.

After independence in 1960, the [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people) region, and Ogun in particular, was engulfed in conflict between the [Ọbáfẹ́mi Awólọ́wọ̀](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obafemi_Awolowo) and [Samuel Ládòkè Akíntọ́lá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladoke_Akintola) fractions of the AG party ("Operation Wetie", see [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nigeria#Independence,_First_Republic_\(1960/1963_-_1966\))). In July 1966, the then ruler of Nigeria, [Johnson Agulyi-Ironsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Aguiyi-Ironsi), was assassinated in [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan) in the second coup of the year, which was the prelude to the [Biafra War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Civil_War).

The state was formed on 3 February 1976 from part of the former "Western" state.

## Geography

Ogun State borders the Republic of [Benin](3459.md) to the West for about 185 km, [Oyo State](2227658.md) and [Osun State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osun_State) (for 84 km) to the North, [Ondo State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondo_State) to the east, [Lagos State](626168.md) to the South for about 283 km, and has 31.6 km of coastline on the [Bight of Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bight_of_Benin) to the south, Araromi beach belongs to Irokun land in [Ogun Waterside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogun_Waterside) (Ijebu province) local government but insistently claimed by [Ondo State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondo_State) due to the influx of [Ilaje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaje) migrants from neighbouring communities and due to no attention given to this area by the Ogun state government.

### Climate

Ogun has a Tropical wet and dry or savanna climate. The city's yearly average temperature is 29.34 °C (84.81 °F) and it is -0.12% lower than Nigeria's averages. Ogun receives about 141.58 mm (5.574 in) of precipitation and has 224.18 rainy days (61.42% of the time) annually.[^cite_note-8]

## Economy

The state has a very high concentration of industries (the second most industrialized after [Lagos State](626168.md)) and has one of the lowest incidences of [extreme poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_poverty) (around 5% of the population against a national average of 31%) according to [World Bank](36752.md) data from 2018.[^cite_note-9]

Major companies in Ogun include the [Dangote Cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangote_Cement) factory in Ibese,[^cite_note-10] [Nestle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestle_Nigeria),[^cite_note-11] [Lafarge Cement factory](41902084.md) in [Ewekoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewekoro), Memmcol in Orimerunmu,[^cite_note-12] Coleman Cables in [Sagamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagamu) and [Arepo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arepo,_Nigeria),[^cite_note-13] [Procter & Gamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procter_&_Gamble) in [Agbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ado-Odo/Ota).[^cite_note-14] In September 2024, the Ogun State Government announced the establishment of a \$5 million battery recycling plant by a British company.[^cite_note-15] Ogun State in the first half of 2026 (January to June) is the second-best performing state based on internally generated revenue (IGR).[^cite_note-16]

### Primary sector

Mining and agriculture are among the most important economic sectors in Ogun. [Limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk), [phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate) and [gravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel) are mined and grain, rice, maize, [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), [yams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_\(vegetable\)), bananas, [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean), [kola nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_nut), rubber, [palm oil](57561.md) and [palm kernels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_kernel) are harvested. The state is the largest producer of kolanut in Nigeria.

## Politics

The State government is led by a democratically elected governor who works closely with members of the state's house of assembly. The capital city of the state is Abeokuta.[^cite_note-two-thirds-spread-21]

### Electoral system

The governor of the state is selected using a modified two-round system. To be elected in the first round, a candidate must receive the plurality of the vote and over 25% of the vote in at least two -third of the State local government areas. If no candidate passes threshold, a second round will be held between the top candidate and the next candidate to have received a plurality of votes in the highest number of local government areas.[^cite_note-two-thirds-spread-21]

### Governor

As of 2020, the governor was [Prince Dapo Abiodun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapo_Abiodun), a member of the [All Progressives Congress (APC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Progressives_Congress), who headed the [Executive Council of Ogun State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_Ogun_State).[^cite_note-22] On Wednesday 29 May 2019, Dapo Abiodun was sworn in as the fifth Governor of the State at the [MKO Abiola Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKO_Abiola_Stadium), Kuto, Abeokuta.[^cite_note-23] He was re-elected for a second term in office in March 2023.

### Senatorial districts

See also: [List of Ogun State local government areas by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ogun_State_local_government_areas_by_area)

Ogun State is divided into three senatorial districts:

- **Ogun Central**: Ogun Central consists mostly of the Egba that occupies six local governments: Abeokuta North (Akomoje), Abeokuta South (Ake), Ewekoro (Itori), Ifo (Ifo), Obafemi Owode (Owode ẹgba) and Odeda (Odeda).

* **Ogun East**: Ogun East consists mostly of the Ijebu and the Remo that occupies 9 local governments: Ijebu East (Ogbẹrẹ), Ijebu North (Ijebu Igbo), Ijebu North East (Attan), Ijebu ode (Ijebu ode), Ikenne (Ikenne Remo), Odogbolu (Odogbolu), Ogun waterside (Abigi), Remo North (Ilisan Remo) and Sagamu (Sagamu).

- **Ogun West**: Ogun West consists mostly of the [Yewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yewa) that occupies 5 local governments: Ado odo Ota (Otta), Imeko Afon (Imeko), Ipokia (Ipokia), Yewa North (Ayetoro) and Yewa South (Ilaro).

## Religion



Shrine to the [Orisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orisha) (god) of fire and metal works, Ogun

Residents of Ogun State are mainly Muslims and Christians, with some adhering to traditional [Yoruba religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_religion).

The [Anglican Province of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Lagos) within the [Church of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Nigeria) includes the ten Dioceses of [Awori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Awori) led by Bishop [Johnson Akin Atere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Akin_Atere) (2009), [Egba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Egba) (1976) led by Bishop [Emmanuel Adekunle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Adekunle) (2009), [Egba West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egba_West) (2007) led by Bishop [Samuel Oludele Ogundeji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Oludele_Ogundeji) (2010), [Ifo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Ifo) (2007) led by Bishop [Nathaniel Oladejo Ogundipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Oladejo_Ogundipe) (2012), [Ijebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Ijebu) led by Bishop [Peter Rotimi Oludipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Rotimi_Oludipe) (2020), [Ijebu-North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijebu-North) led by Bishop [Solomon Kuponu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Kuponu) (2005), [Remo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Remo) led by Bishop [Michael Fape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fape) (2004, Archbishop of Lagos 2016–21), [Yewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Yewa), formerly Egbado led by Bishop [Michael Adebayo Oluwarohunbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Adebayo_Oluwarohunbi) (2014), and [Ijebu-South West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijebu-South_West) led by Bishop [Babatunde Ogunbanwo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babatunde_Ogunbanwo) (2009).

179,014 [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholics) (2020) in the [Dioceses of Abeokuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Abeokuta) (1997) with 60 parishes under Bishop [Peter Kayode Odetoyinbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kayode_Odetoyinbo?action=edit\&redlink=1) (2014) and [Ijebu-Ode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Ijebu-Ode) (1969) with 40 parishes under Bishop [Francis Obafemi Adesina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Obafemi_Adesina?action=edit\&redlink=1) (2019), both suffragans of the [Archdiocese of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Lagos).

## Education

Ogun state has three federal secondary schools; Federal Government Girls' College, Sagamu [^cite_note-29] and [Federal Government College, Odogbolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Government_College,_Odogbolu)[^cite_note-30] and Federal Science and Technical College, Ijebu-Imushin.[^cite_note-31]

Ogun state has two Federal Universities; the [Federal University of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Agriculture,_Abeokuta), Abeokuta (FUNAAB)[^cite_note-32] and [Tai Solarin University of Education, Ijebu Ode](https://tasued.edu.ng/) and one Federal college of education, FCE Osiele (both at Odeda Local government area), one state government college of education, named after the late Nigerian educationist of international repute Augustus Taiwo Solarin in 1994 as Tai Solarin College of Education (TASCE[^cite_note-33]), (formerly known as Ogun State College of Education, Ijagun, Ijebu-Ode, one Federal Polytechnic, Ilaro). One is named after late Nigerian business mogul and winner of 12 June 1993 election, Basorun Moshood Kasimawo Olawale Abiola as [Moshood Abiola Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshood_Abiola_Polytechnic) (MAPOLY[^cite_note-34]), formerly known as Ogun State Polytechnic, Ojere, Abeokuta, Another Gateway Polytechnic Saapade,[^cite_note-net.nbte.gov.ng-35] Remo (GAPOSA), Abraham Adesanya Polytechnic[^cite_note-net.nbte.gov.ng-35] Ijebu-Igbo (Aapoly) (formerly known as 'The Polytechnic Ijebu-Igbo) it was name after [Chief Abraham Aderibigbe Adesanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Adesanya) who was a Nigerian politician, lawyer and activist.

One state government universities: [Olabisi Onabanjo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olabisi_Onabanjo_University), [Ago Iwoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ago_Iwoye) (formerly known as Ogun State University).

Ogun State has a total of nine registered universities, the highest of any state in Nigeria. It has five [private universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university).[^cite_note-36] Amongst which are [Chrisland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrisland_University), [Hallmark University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallmark_University) in Ijebu-itele, Abeokuta [Bells University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bells_University_of_Technology) in [Ota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ota,_Ogun), [Covenant University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_University) and [Babcock University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babcock_University) in Ilisan-Remo, which was the first private university in the country.[^cite_note-ogunsmartcity-37]

The state has two major government hospitals: the [Federal Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Medical_Center,_Abeokuta) at Abeokuta, and the [Olabisi Onabanjo University Teaching Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olabisi_Onabanjo_University_Teaching_Hospital) in [Sagamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagamu). The [National Youth Service Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Youth_Service_Corps) ([NYSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Youth_Service_Corps)) Permanent Orientation Camp is located at Sagamu Local Government area of the state.[^cite_note-ogunsmartcity-37]

Ogun State government has begun the Itele road.[^cite_note-38]

## Transportation

Ogun benefits from its proximity to the metropolis of [Lagos](85232.md) and the new [deep-sea harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Port) and the new [Dangote refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangote_Refinery) in Lekki (as of 2024). The planned airport Lagos-Epe will be located next to the border to the state of Ogun.

### Railways

#### Nigerian Railway Company

Ogun benefits from the Lagos-[Abeokuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeokuta)-[Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan) standard rail link since 2021.

The planned *Apapa-Kajola Express* will connect the centre of the state with the Lagos port.[^cite_note-43]

Abeokuta also is connected with Lagos by 77 km of the [Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Railways) (built in 1899), which still is used for freight trains.

#### Lagos Mass Transit (Lamata)

The terminus of the "Red Line" of the Lagos suburban railway is located in Agbado, which is part of the Lagos agglomeration but belongs to the state of Ogun in administrative terms. This is why the trains and carriages of Lagos State will be parked, cleaned and maintained in Ogun.[^cite_note-44]

### Airport

The Ogun State Governor, [Dapo Abiodun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapo_Abiodun) on 7 October 2025 inaugurated the first commercial flight of the Gateway International Airport in [Ilishan-Remo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilishan-Remo). The airport plans to fly passengers internationally.[^cite_note-1st-commercial-flight-45] An aircraft owned by the airline [ValueJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ValueJet_\(Nigeria\)), with registration number 5N-BXS and 90 passengers on board, departed the airport at 10\:10 a.m. for Abuja as the first commercial flight to officially fly from the airport.[^cite_note-1st-commercial-flight-45]
