**Blackley** [/ˈbleɪkli/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b9/En-uk-Blackley.ogg/En-uk-Blackley.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b9/En-uk-Blackley.ogg/En-uk-Blackley.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> is a suburban area of [Manchester](20206.md), in the county of [Greater Manchester](79819.md), England. Historically in [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire), it is approximately 4 miles (6.4 km) north of [Manchester city centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_city_centre), on the [River Irk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Irk).

## History

The hamlet of Blackley was mentioned in the [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book). The name derives from the Anglo-Saxon Blæclēah, which means "dark wood" or "dark clearing". In the 13th and 14th centuries, Blackley was referred to as *Blakeley* or *Blakelegh*.[^cite_note-Blackley-1] By the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), Blackley had become a park belonging to the lords of Manchester.

Its value in 1282 was recorded as £6 13s 4d, a sum approximately equivalent in buying power to £333,500 today.[^cite_note-Blackley-1][^cite_note-2] The lords of Manchester leased the land from time to time. In 1473, [John Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Byron_\(died_1450\)) held the leases on Blackley village, Blackley field and Pillingworth fields at an annual rent of £33 6s 8d.

The Byron family continued to hold the land until the beginning of the 17th century, when Blackley was sold in parcels to a number of landowners.[^cite_note-Blackley-1] By the middle of the 17th century, Blackley was a rural village of just 107 inhabitants.

There was gradual residential development in the 1930s to 1960s, where most farmland was built upon.

Today, only pockets of the suburb are undeveloped green space, with farmland remaining close to the [M60 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M60_motorway_\(Great_Britain\)).



Crab Lane in 1908

Blackley has an important history in the chemical industry, starting with the Borelle Dyeworks founded by Angel Raphael Louis Delaunay in 1785 and run by [ICI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Chemical_Industries), who expanded it as a research centre for chemicals and pharmaceuticals.[^cite_note-3] There were several other dye and chemical works. Connolly's Ltd made telecommunication cabling, later becoming part of [BICC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Insulated_Callender's_Cables).[^cite_note-4]

## Governance

Blackley, since 2024, has been part of the parliamentary constituency of [Blackley and Middleton South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackley_and_Middleton_South). The current [Member of Parliament](3776360.md) is [Graham Stringer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Stringer) of the [Labour Party](19279158.md), who was first elected in 1997 for the predecessor constituency [Manchester Blackley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Blackley). The candidate of the [Reform UK](59757202.md) came second in the general election of 2024, with 21.1% of the vote compared to Stringer's 53.8%.

The suburb is split between the local government wards of [Higher Blackley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_Blackley) and [Charlestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlestown,_Manchester) on [Manchester City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City_Council). There are two Labour and one Reform UK councillor for each ward.

Blackley was historically in the [ancient parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_parish) of [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_\(ancient_parish\)),[^cite_note-5] in 1866 Blackley became a [civil parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_parish), on 26 March 1896 the parish was abolished to form [North Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Manchester).[^cite_note-6] In 1891 the parish had a population of 7332.[^cite_note-7]

## Economy

During the 1930s, residential development started to take place in the formerly rural village to provide more homes for Manchester's growing population. It became an area with a mixture of housing: mainly privately owned homes for owner occupation and private renting. In 2014, Blackley appeared on a list of *Top 10 Places to Live In Greater Manchester*.

The suburb is popular for commuters into [Manchester city centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_city_centre). The few opportunities to work in the suburb itself include the Hexagon Tower Development, close to [North Manchester Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Manchester_General_Hospital), which is a centre for scientific research into plastics and related innovation.

## Church of St Peter

Main article: [Church of St Peter, Blackley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_St_Peter,_Blackley)

The [Church of St Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_St_Peter,_Blackley) on Old Market Street is a [Gothic Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival) church, which was built in 1844 by [E. H. Shellard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._H._Shellard). It was erected at a cost of £3,162. It was designated a [Grade II\* listed building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_II*_listed_building) on 20 June 1988. The interior is aisled and particularly impressive for its complete 170 year old interior, with the extremely unusual survival of all the fine boxes and pews.

The churchyard contains the war graves of ten service personnel of [World War I](4764461.md), and seven of [World War II](32927.md).

## Green spaces

Blackley has several green spaces:

### Boggart Hole Clough

[Boggart Hole Clough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boggart_Hole_Clough) is a large woodland and urban country park. The park has a [bowling green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_green), tennis and basketball courts, a boating lake and a children's play area. It has its own permanent [orienteering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orienteering) course and an athletics track. There is an old stone bridge across the brook running through the clough.

The clough was designated a local nature reserve in 2008.[^cite_note-8]

### Blackley Forest

Blackley Forest is a [Site of Biological Importance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_of_Biological_Importance) and an example of one of the country's first Community Woodlands. It was planted to commemorate the Queen's coronation and also local people killed in the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). The area has had woodland on it since the [Norman Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest) in 1066, when there were wild boar, deer and eagles.[^cite_note-9]

The forest is a mix of [woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland), grassland and [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetlands), with a network of paths and steps. The stretch of the [River Irk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Irk) in the forest is fringed by [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch) trees with some colonies of autumn [crocus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocus).

### Heaton Park

[Heaton Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaton_Park), at around 650 acres (2.63 km<sup>2</sup>), is the biggest park in Greater Manchester, and one of the largest municipal parks in [Western Europe](33800.md), providing some 25% of Manchester's total green space. It is the grounds of [Heaton Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaton_Hall), a [Grade I listed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_building), neoclassical 18th-century country mansion. The hall was remodelled to a design by [James Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wyatt) in 1772,[^cite_note-10] and is open to the public as a museum and events venue.

Although the park is officially part of the [City of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Manchester), two of a number of entrances are accessed from the suburb of Blackley, on Middleton Road.[^cite_note-11]

## Transport

Blackley is well served by buses primarily along the main arterial routes of [Rochdale Road (A664)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A664_road), and [Cheetham Hill Road/Bury Old Road (A665)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A665_road) directly to and from Manchester city centre. Initially these would have been provided by the precursors to, and [Manchester Carriage and Tramways Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Carriage_and_Tramways_Company), and currently by [First Greater Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Greater_Manchester).

Other journeys are provided by [Stagecoach Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_Manchester), which took over [JPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JP_Travel) in April 2014. There are frequent [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Metrolink) trams from [Bowker Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowker_Vale) to and from Manchester city centre and as far south as [Altrincham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altrincham) and as far north as [Bury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury,_Greater_Manchester). The M60 orbital motorway is the northern boundary of Blackley.

## Sports

**Blackley Golf Club**



The course at Blackley

The award-winning Blackley Golf Club has occupied its present site close to the M60 motorway since 1937. The club celebrated its centenary in 2007, and a new clubhouse opened in 2009. The clubhouse offers use for wedding receptions and private hire. There is a golf shop, open to club members and the public.

The course's green is separated by fields from the motorway. A public footpath runs along the perimeter.

**Blackley Cricket Club**



Crab Lane cricket ground in Blackley

Blackley Cricket Club currently plays in the Greater Manchester Cricket League. The club is located on Crab Lane. It won the cricket league title for Greater Manchester in 2009.
