The **Orinoco Belt** is a territory in the southern strip of the eastern [Orinoco River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco_River) basin in [Venezuela](32374.md) which overlies the world's largest deposits of [petroleum](23195.md). Its local [Spanish](26825.md) name is *Faja Petrolífera del Orinoco* (Orinoco Petroleum Belt). The Orinoco Belt is located in [Guárico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%A1rico) and south of the [Anzoátegui](474415.md), [Monagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monagas), and [Delta Amacuro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Amacuro) states, and it follows the line of the river. It is approximately 600 kilometres (370 mi) from east to west, and 70 kilometres (43 mi) from north to south, with an area about 55,314 square kilometres (21,357 sq mi).

## Oil reserves

The Orinoco Belt consists of large deposits of extra [heavy crude oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_crude_oil). Venezuela's heavy oil deposits of about 1,200 billion barrels (1.9×10<sup>11</sup> m<sup>3</sup>), found primarily in the Orinoco Petroleum Belt, are estimated to approximately equal the world's reserves of lighter oil.[^cite_note-wec-1] [Petróleos de Venezuela S.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr%C3%B3leos_de_Venezuela_S.A.) has estimated that the producible [reserves of the Orinoco Belt](19100139.md) are up to 235 billion barrels (3.74×10<sup>10</sup> m<sup>3</sup>) which would make it the largest [petroleum](23195.md) reserve in the world, slightly ahead of the similar [unconventional oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconventional_\(oil_and_gas\)_reservoir) source in the Canadian [Athabasca oil sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athabasca_oil_sands), as well as above [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).[^cite_note-Figaro-2] In 2009, the [US Geological Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Geological_Survey) increased the estimated reserves to 513 billion barrels (8.16×10<sup>10</sup> m<sup>3</sup>) of oil which is "technically recoverable (producible using currently available technology and industry practices)." No estimate of how much of the oil is economically recoverable was made.[^cite_note-USGS_Venezuela-3]

The Orinoco Belt is currently divided into four exploration and production areas. These are: Boyacá (formerly Machete), Junín (formerly Zuata), Ayacucho (formerly Hamaca), and Carabobo (formerly Cerro Negro). The current exploration area is about 11,593 square kilometres (4,476 sq mi).

## Development

On January 7, 1936, Standard Oil of New Jersey, with the drilling of the well "La Canoa-1" located near the community of La Canoa, [Anzoategui](474415.md) state, began the exploitation of the Orinoco Oil Belt. That well was active for 44 days, producing approximately 1,000 net barrels of crude oil per day. The exploitation activities were then abandoned due to the difficulty in extracting the extra-heavy hydrocarbons. In 1938, the first well discovering hydrocarbons was drilled with the name "Zuata 1".

### Oil Sowing Plan 2005–2030

Venezuela power policy guidelines until 2030 are drawn up in the "**Oil Sowing Plan**" (**"Plan Siembra Petrolera"**), which includes six development projects and consists of two stages: one executed from 2005 to 2012, and another developed from 2012 to 2030. For the first period of plan, an overall investment of around US\$56 [billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1000000000000_\(number\)#1012)  was estimated between 2005 and 2012. 70% of that amount was to be financed by Venezuela — state operator — and the rest by the private sector.

### Production blocks

Production blocks will be developed by PDVSA in cooperation with foreign partners. In all partnership PDVSA owns 60%.[^cite_note-reuters120210-5]

#### Junin

Junín block 1 was discovered in 2009 and developed by PDVSA. The oil field is operated and owned by [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA). The total [proven reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proven_reserves) of the Junín-1 oil field are around 2 billion barrels (320,000,000 m<sup>3</sup>), and production is centered on 200,000 barrels per day (32,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-6]

Junin block 2 is under development in cooperation with [Petrovietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovietnam). [SNC-Lavalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNC-Lavalin) was awarded the engineering contract on March 10, 2010. It is expected to produce 200 thousand barrels per day (32×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/d) by 2011. The development will include also a heavy crude upgrader; however, its commissioning date is not specified.&#x20;

Junín Block 3 was discovered in 2009 and developed by [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA). The oil field is operated and owned by [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA). The total [proven reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proven_reserves) of the Junín-3 oil field are around 4.26 billion barrels (677,000,000 m<sup>3</sup>), and production is centered on 200,000 barrels per day (32,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-7]

Junin block 4 is developed in cooperation with [CNPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Petroleum_Corporation) (40%). It is expected to produce 400 thousand barrels per day (64×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/d); however, the commissioning date is not announced.&#x20;

Junin block 5 is developed in cooperation with [Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni) (40%). It is expected to produce 75 thousand barrels per day (11.9×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/d) by 2013 with late production of 240 thousand barrels per day (38×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/d). The development will include an oil refinery for production of motor fuels. Junin block 6 is developed in cooperation with a consortium of Russian oil companies, including [Rosneft](1313566.md), [Gazprom Neft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazprom_Neft), [Lukoil](1027881.md), [TNK-BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNK-BP) and [Surgutneftegaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgutneftegaz). It is expected to produce 450 thousand barrels per day (72×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/d); however, the commissioning date is not announced.[^cite_note-reuters120210-5]

Junín block 7 was discovered in 2009 and developed by [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA). The oil field is operated and owned by [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA). The total [proven reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proven_reserves) of the Junín-7 oil field are around 31 billion barrels (4.9×10<sup>9</sup> m<sup>3</sup>), and production is centered on 200,000 barrels per day (32,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-8]

Junín block 8 was discovered in 2009 and developed by [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA). The oil field is operated and owned by [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA). The total [proven reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proven_reserves) of the Junín-8 oil field are around 40 billion barrels (6.4×10<sup>9</sup> m<sup>3</sup>).[^cite_note-9] The same year, PDVSA and [Sinopec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinopec) signed an agreement to collaborate on the development of the block,[^cite_note-10] whose future production was estimated to be 200,000 barrels per day (32,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-11]

The Junín block 10 was discovered in 2009 and developed by [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA). The oil field is operated and owned by [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA). The total [proven reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proven_reserves) of the Junín-10 oil field are around 10.5 billion barrels (1.67×10<sup>9</sup> m<sup>3</sup>), and production is centered on 220,000 barrels per day (35,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-12]

#### Carabobo

Carabobo 1 is developed in cooperation with [Repsol YPF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repsol_YPF) (11%), [Petronas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petronas) (11%), [ONGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_Natural_Gas_Corporation) (11%), [Indian Oil Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Oil_Corporation) (3.5%), and [Oil India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_India) (3.5%). It consists of Carabobo block 1 North and block 1 Central. The expected production output will be 400 thousand barrels per day (64×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/d) by 2013. The upgrader is expected to be ready by 2017.[^cite_note-reuters120210-5]

Carabobo 3 is developed in cooperation with [Chevron Corporation](284749.md) (34%), Suelopetrol (1%) pct, and [Mitsubishi Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Corporation) and [Inpex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inpex) (5%). It consists of Carabobo block 2 South, block 3, and block 5. The expected production output will be 400 thousand barrels per day (64×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>3</sup> m<sup>3</sup>/d) by 2013. The upgrader is expected to be ready by 2017.[^cite_note-reuters120210-5]

Carabobo 2 will be developed in cooperation with [Rosneft](1313566.md) and Corporation Venezolana del Petroleo (CVP) – a subsidiary of Venezuela's state oil and gas company PDVSA. The agreement, signed by Rosneft CEO [Igor Sechin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Sechin) and Venezuelan Oil Minister, PDVSA chief Rafael Ramirez in the presence of President [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez), establishes a joint venture to develop the Carabobo 2 bloc in the southern Orinoco extra-heavy crude belt in Venezuela.

The signed memorandum established Rosneft's share will be 40 percent. Rosneft will pay CVP a bonus of \$1.1 billion, paid out in two installments: \$440 million within ten days after the establishment of the joint venture, and the rest after Rosneft makes the final decision on the project. In addition, Rosneft will issue a \$1.5 billion loan to CVP for five years. The loan will be provided in tranches of not more than \$300 million annually at the annual interest rate of LIBOR+5.5 percent. To develop the Carabobo 2 bloc Rosneft will invest a total of \$16 billion, according to CEO Igor Sechin. The Carabobo 2 bloc's reserves total 6.5 billion metric tons of crude. Commercial oil production at the bloc is expected to hit 400,000 barrels per day. Rosneft, along with several Russian oil companies ([Gazprom Neft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazprom_Neft), [Lukoil](1027881.md), [TNK-BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNK-BP) and [Surgutneftegaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgutneftegaz)), have formed a consortium to develop the Junin bloc 6 of the Orinoco belt in Venezuela.

The formation waters from the [boreholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borehole) show a main Na-Cl level (TDS up to 30g/L) with a dilution trend toward Na-HCO<sub>3</sub> composition (down to 1g/L). The stable isotope ratio of oxygen and hydrogen of water molecule reveal that the seawater mother water was modified during a high-temperature thrusting event (120–125 °C), forming <sup>18</sup>O-enriched diagenetic water (up to +4‰), which was diluted in recent times by glacial meltwater and presentday meteoric water.[^cite_note-\:0-13] The hypothetical presence of flood by a meteoric paleo-water also offers new hints to explain the low API gravity (<10°API biodegraded, extra heavy oil) and composition of the local crude.[^cite_note-\:0-13]
