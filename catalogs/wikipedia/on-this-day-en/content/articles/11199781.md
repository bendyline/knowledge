**Prosperous Armenia Party** (**PAP**; [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Բարգավաճ Հայաստան կուսակցություն, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Armenian): *Bargavach Hayastan kusakts’ut’yun*, abbreviated as **ԲՀԿ** **BHK**), is a [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative) [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Armenia) in [Armenia](10918072.md).[^cite_note-3] It was founded by businessman [Gagik Tsarukyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gagik_Tsarukyan) on 30 April 2004, when the constituent congress of the party took place.[^cite_note-4]

Ideologically, the party is positioned as [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics) on the political spectrum,[^cite_note-5] it advocates [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism),[^cite_note-EuFoA-6] [closer ties with Russia](2541825.md),[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] and regarding the [European Union](9317.md) it is [eurosceptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosceptic),[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] while on [social issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_issues_in_Armenia) they are [socially conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism).[^cite_note-EuFoA-6]

## Founding

Although Gagik Tsarukyan is widely credited as the founder of the PAP, Armenia's second president [Robert Kocharyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kocharyan) is believed to have been instrumental in the creation of the party.[^cite_note-11] [Armen Ashotyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armen_Ashotyan), a close ally of former president [Serzh Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serzh_Sargsyan), even claimed that Kocharyan himself established the PAP.[^cite_note-12] However, Kocharyan's influence over the party has been a subject of debate among observers of Armenian politics. In 2012, "most analysts" believed Kocharyan was "closely involved" with the party.[^cite_note-13] However, the more common view among political analysts today is that the PAP is a vehicle for Tsarukyan and that Kocharyan has no influence over the party.[^cite_note-14] In April 2026, Tsarukyan declared that the PAP would not support the candidacy of Kocharyan for prime minister in the case of an opposition victory in the [2026 Armenian parliamentary election](70183441.md).[^cite_note-15]

## Ideology

The party maintains a [pro-Russian](2541825.md) stance,[^cite_note-16] and advocates for the preservation of national and traditional values, families, and church and state relations as the most important priorities.[^cite_note-bhk.am-17] The party also claims to support building stronger democratic and neighborly relations within the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) region and with other [post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states). Despite being a soft eurosceptic party, the PAP does believe in maintaining strong relations and economic partnership with the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-bhk.am-17]

## Power base

According to sociologist [Karen Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Sargsyan_\(sociologist\)), the party base largely consists of provincial rural population.[^cite_note-18] [Kotayk Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotayk_Province) is widely considered its stronghold. During the [2012 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Armenian_parliamentary_election), the PAP came first and won around 47.5% of the vote in that province, well above the national average of 30%.[^cite_note-19] While in the 28th electoral district, which includes the city of [Abovyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abovyan) and several surrounding villages, the party won over 71%.[^cite_note-20]

## Electoral record

The party debuted in the [2007 Armenian parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Armenian_parliamentary_election), winning 25 seats and 15.1% of the votes, making it the second largest political party in parliament.

In the [2012 Armenian parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Armenian_parliamentary_election), it more than doubled its share of the vote to 30.12%, winning 35 seats and solidifying its position as the main opposition party.

In the [2017 Armenian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Armenian_parliamentary_election), the party participated as part of the [Tsarukyan Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsarukyan_Alliance). The alliance won 31 seats.

Following the [2018 Armenian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Armenian_parliamentary_election), the PAP lost five seats but was still the second largest party in the [National Assembly](4014302.md) and one of the two official opposition parties, the other being [Bright Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Armenia).

In May 2021, the party confirmed it would participate in the [2021 Armenian parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Armenian_parliamentary_election). Following the election, the party won just 3.95% of the popular vote, losing all political representation in the National Assembly. The party currently acts as an [extra-parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra-parliamentary) force.

## Activities

In 2014, the PAP and the [Armenian Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Liberal_Party) signed a cooperation agreement.[^cite_note-24]

On 9 November 2020, the party signed a joint declaration with the other member parties of the [Homeland Salvation Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Salvation_Movement) calling on Prime Minister [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) to resign during the [2020–2021 Armenian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932021_Armenian_protests).[^cite_note-25]
