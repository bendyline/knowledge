**Charsadda District** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): چارسده ولسوالۍ, [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language): ضلع چارسدہ) is a [district](639402.md) in the [Peshawar Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_Division) of the [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) province of [Pakistan](23235.md). Prior to its establishment as a separate district in 1998, it was a [tehsil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) within the [Peshawar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_District).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPCO19981-3] The district headquarter is the town of [Charsadda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charsadda), which was once part of the [Peshawar](66230.md) ex-metropolitan region.

## History

The district lies between 34 and 03' and 34-38' north latitudes and 71-28' and 71-53' east longitudes. Charsadda is located in the west of the [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) and is bounded by the [Malakand District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malakand_District) to the north, [Mardan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardan_district) to the east, [Nowshera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowshera,_Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa) and [Peshawar](66230.md) districts to the south and [Mohmand district](4557955.md) to the west. The district covers an area of 996 square kilometers.

Charsadda was once part of the kingdom of [Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara). However, around 516 BC Gandhara became part of the seventh [satrapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrapy) or province of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) and paid tribute to [Darius the Great of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great_of_Persia), until it was overthrown by [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in the 4th century BC.

After the death of Alexander in 323 BC, the Indian Emperor [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya) rose to power and brought Gandhara under his sway. According to a popular tradition, Emperor [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) built one of his stupas there. Actually, there were two stupas built by Ashoka, mentioned by the famous Chinese Buddhist pilgrim [Hieun Tsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang), who visited it in 630 AD. Both were near the town of Po-Lu-Sha: one was outside the eastern gate of the town, and the other 20 li (10 km) northeast of it, on the mount Dantaloka.[^cite_note-4]

To the east, a [Brahminical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahminical) temple stood while to the north, a monastery was located. According to Buddhists, this monastery was the place where [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buddha) preached the Law. The name Gandhara went out of usage after [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) [conquered the area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Laghman) and converted it to Islam in 1026.

The city of [Charsadda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charsadda), originally known as [Pushkalavati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushkalavati), is mentioned in the Hindu epic story the [Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana), where Bharata brings this new conquered city to one of his sons, Pushkala.[^cite_note-5]

[Bala Hisar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bala_Hissar,_Peshawar) was excavated twice by the head of the [Archaeological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Survey_of_India), [Sir John Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Marshall_\(archaeologist\)), in 1902 and by Sir [Mortimer Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortimer_Wheeler) in 1958. According to the South Asian Archaeology Research Group of [Bradford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_University), Wheeler suggested that Bala Hisar "was founded by the Persians in the sixth century BC as a colony guarding the eastern edge of their empire".[^cite_note-6]

## Important places

### Shabqadar

[Shabqadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabqadar) is a tehsil within the District Charsadda. It is 17 miles (27 km) northwest of Peshawar. A fort was built here by the Sikhs called Sharkargarh. The town was burnt by the [Mohmands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohmand_\(tribe\)) in 1897. It has since been rebuilt.

### Bibi Syeda Dheri

Bibi Syeda Dheri is a site half a mile to the north of Umarzai village in Charsadda tehsil. At this location, a mound rises to a height of 60 ft (18 m). It is believed to be the site of the stupa erected to commemorate the conversion by Buddha of goddess [Hariti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hariti) who used to devour children of the locality. There is also a shrine of a lady saint Bibi Syeda.

### Shar-i-Napursan

Shar-i-Napursan is an archaeological site in Charsadda tehsil near the village [Rajjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajjar_1). Excavations have unearthed two distinct settlements of the Buddhist period and two of the Muslim period. Coins of [Menander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menander_I), [Hermaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermaeus) and [Kanishka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanishka) have been unearthed.[^cite_note-18]

### Palatu Dheri



Piedestal of a Buddha statue, with Year 384 inscription, from Palatu Dheri.

[^cite_note-19]

Palatu Dheri is another archaeological site near Charsadda tehsil. A mile from Shar-i-Napursan is a mound which contains the remains of a stupa, which according to Hieun Tsiang, was built by one Deven. Some coins which connect them both to the first century AD have been unearthed. Other finds include the image of the goddess [Kalika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali). Three inscribed jars, which were presented by some laymen to "the Community of the Four Quarters", are now in the [Peshawar Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_Museum).
