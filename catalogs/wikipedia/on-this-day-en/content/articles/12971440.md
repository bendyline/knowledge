**Salvadorans** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Salvadoreños*), also known as **Salvadorians** or **Salvadoreans**, are citizens of [El Salvador](9356.md), a country in [Central America](6121.md). Most Salvadorans live in El Salvador, although there is also a significant Salvadoran [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora), particularly [in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Americans), with smaller communities in other countries around the world.[^cite_note-4]

El Salvador's population was 6,218,000 in 2010, compared to 2,200,000 in 1950.[^cite_note-WPP_2012-5] In 2010, the percentage of the population below the age of 15 was 32.1%, 61% were between 15 and 65 years of age, while 6.9% were 65 years or older.[^cite_note-WPP_2012-5]

## Demonym

Although not the academic standard, *Salvadorian* and *Salvadorean* are widely-used English demonyms used by those living in the United States and other English-speaking countries. All three versions of the word can be seen in most Salvadoran business signs in the United States and elsewhere in the world.

*Centroamericano/a* in Spanish and in English *Central American* is an alternative standard and widespread cultural identity term that Salvadorans use to identify themselves, along with their regional isthmian neighbors. It is a secondary demonym and it is widely used as an interchangeable term for El Salvador and Salvadorans. The demonym Central American is an allusion to the strong union that the Central America region has had since its independence. The term Central America is not only a regional cultural identity, but also a political identity, since the region has been united on various occasions as a single country such as the *United Provinces of Central America*, [Federal Republic of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Central_America), *National Representation of Central America*, and [Greater Republic of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Republic_of_Central_America). The same can be said for El Salvador's neighbors, specifically the original five states of [Central America](6121.md).

### Lithic era

El Salvador was inhabited by [Paleo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians), the [first peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) who subsequently inhabited, the [Americas](29833.md) during the [glacial episodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary_glaciation) of the [late Pleistocene period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene). Their paintings (the earliest of which date from 8000 BC) can still be seen in caves outside the towns of [Corinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinto,_Moraz%C3%A1n) and [Cacaopera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacaopera), both in Morazán. Originating in the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) period, these cave paintings exhibit the earliest traces of human life in El Salvador; these early Native Americans people used the cave as a refuge, Paleoindian artists created cave and rock paintings that are located in present-day El Salvador.

The [Lencas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenca) later occupied the caves and utilized them as spiritual spaces. Other ancient petroglyphs are located in [San José Villanueva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jos%C3%A9_Villanueva), La Libertad and [San Isidro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro,_Caba%C3%B1as), Cabañas. The rock petroglyphs in San Jose Villanueva near a cave in Walter Thilo Deininger National Park are similar to other ancient rock petroglyph around the country. Regarding the style of the engravings, it has been compared with the petroglyphs of La Peña Herrada ([Cuscatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuscatl%C3%A1n_Department)), el Letrero del Diablo (La Libertad) and la Peña de los Fierros ([San Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvador)). Other cave locations include the cave of Los Fierros and La Cuevona, both in Cuscatlán.

Native Americans appeared in the [Pleistocene era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene_era) and became the dominant people in the [Lithic stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithic_stage), developing in the [Archaic period in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_in_North_America) to the [Formative stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formative_stage), occupying this phase for thousands of years until European contact at the end of 16th century, spanning from the time of the arrival to the [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic) era to [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) during the early modern period.

Historically El Salvador has had diverse Native American cultures, coming from the north and south of the continent along with local populations mixed together. El Salvador belongs to the [Mesoamerican region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_region), where a myriad of indigenous societies have lived side by side for centuries with their unique cultures and speaking different [indigenous languages of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas) in the beginning of the [Formative stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formative_stage).[^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-El_Salvador_Antiguas_Civilizaciones-7][^cite_note-Mined_Historia_de_El_Salvador-8]

Evidence of [Olmec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec) civilization presence in western El Salvador can be found in the ruin sites of [Chalchuapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalchuapa) in the [Ahuachapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahuachapan) Department on boulders in Chalchuapa portraying Olmec warriors with helmets identical to those found on the [Olmec colossal heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec_colossal_heads). This suggest that the area was once an Olmec enclave, before fading away for unknown reasons. The Olmecs are believed to have lived in present-day El Salvador as early as 2000 BC in the beginning of preclassic period. The 'Olmec Boulder, ' is a sculpture of a giant head found near [Casa Blanca, El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Blanca,_El_Salvador) site in Las Victorias near Chalchuapa. "Olmecoid" figurines, such as the [Potbelly sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potbelly_sculpture), have been found through this area, in fact most are described as looking primeval proto-Olmeca. In the end of middle preclassic, by 650 BC, this culture would be replaced by the Maya.[^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-El_Salvador_Antiguas_Civilizaciones-7][^cite_note-Mined_Historia_de_El_Salvador-8]

The [Lenca people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenca_people) are an indigenous people of eastern El Salvador where population today is estimated at about 37,000. The Lenca was a matriarchal society and was one of the [first civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_civilization) to develop in El Salvador and were the first major civilization in the country. The pre-Conquest [Salvadoran Lenca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Lenca) had frequent contact with various Maya groups as well as other indigenous peoples of Central America. The origin of Lenca populations has been a source of ongoing debate amongst anthropologists and historians. Throughout the regions of Lenca occupation, Lenca pottery is a very distinguishable form of [Pre-Columbian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_art). Handcrafted by Lenca women, Lenca pottery is considered an ethnic marking of their culture. Some scholars have suggested that the Lenca migrated to the Central American region from South America around 3,000 years ago, making it the oldest civilization in El Salvador. Guancasco is the annual ceremony by which Lenca communities, usually two, gather to establish reciprocal obligations in order to confirm peace and friendship. [Quelepa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quelepa) is a major site in eastern El Salvador. Its pottery shows strong similarities to ceramics found in central western El Salvador and the Maya highlands. The Lenca sites of Yarumela, Los Naranjos in Honduras, and Quelepa in El Salvador, all contain evidence of the [Usulután](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usulut%C3%A1n)-style ceramics.

The [Cacaopera people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacaopera_people) are an indigenous people in El Salvador who are also known as the Matagalpa or Ulua. Cacaopera people spoke the [Cacaopera language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacaopera_language), a [Misumalpan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misumalpan_language). Cacaopera is an extinct language belonging to the Misumalpan family, formerly spoken in the department of Morazán in El Salvador. It was closely related to Matagalpa, and slightly more distantly to Sumo, but was geographically separated from other [Misumalpan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misumalpan_languages).

The [Xinca people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinca_people), also known as the Xinka, are a non-Mayan indigenous people of Mesoamerica, with communities in the western part of El Salvador near its border. The Xinka may have been among the earliest inhabitants of western El Salvador, predating the arrival of the Maya and the Pipil. The Xinca ethnic group became extinct in the Mestizo process.

El Salvador has two [Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_peoples) groups, the [Poqomam people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poqomam_people) and the [Ch'orti' people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch'orti'_people). The Poqomam are a Maya people in western El Salvador near its border. Their indigenous language is also called Poqomam. The Ch'orti' people (alternatively, Ch'orti' Maya or Chorti) are one of the indigenous Maya peoples, who primarily reside in communities and towns of northern El Salvador. The Maya once dominated the entire western portion of El Salvador, up until the eruption of the [lake ilopango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_ilopango) super volcano. Mayan ruins are the most widely conserved in El Salvador and artifacts such as [Maya ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_ceramics) [Mesoamerican writing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_writing_systems) [Mesoamerican calendars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_calendars) and [Mesoamerican ballgame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_ballgame) can be found in all Maya ruins in El Salvador which include [Tazumal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tazumal), [San Andrés, El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Andr%C3%A9s,_El_Salvador), [Casa Blanca, El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Blanca,_El_Salvador), [Cihuatán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cihuat%C3%A1n), and [Joya de Cerén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joya_de_Cer%C3%A9n).

The Mangue people, also known as Chorotega, spoke the [Mangue language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangue_language), a now-extinct [Oto-Manguean language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oto-Manguean_language). They occupied land near the eastern El Salvador border, near the gulf.

The [Pipil people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipils) are an indigenous people who live in western El Salvador. Their language is called Nahuat or Pipil, related to the Toltec people of the [Nahua peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuatl_Nation?action=edit\&redlink=1) and were speakers of early Nahuatl languages. However, in general, their mythology is more closely related to the [Maya mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_mythology), who are their near neighbors and by oral tradition said to have been adopted by Ch'orti' and Poqomam Mayan people during the Pipil exodus in the 9th century CE. The culture lasted until the Spanish conquest, at which time they still maintained their Nawat language, despite being surrounded by the Maya in western El Salvador. By the time the Spanish arrived, Pipil and Poqomam Maya settlements were interspersed throughout western El Salvador. The Pipil are known as the last indigenous civilization to arrive in El Salvador, being the least oldest and were a determined people who stoutly resisted Spanish efforts to extend their dominion southward. The Pipil are direct descendants of the Toltecs, but not of the Aztecs.

### Spanish conquest (1522)



[Andrés Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Ni%C3%B1o)'s route expeditions (1519, 1522 and 1524).

By 1521, the indigenous population of the Mesoamerican area had been drastically reduced by the [smallpox epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_smallpox) that was spreading throughout the territory, although it had not yet reached pandemic levels in Cuzcatlán or the northern portion Managuara.[^cite_note-Peters2005-9][^cite_note-Card2007-10][^cite_note-11] The first known visit by Spaniards to what is now Salvadoran territory was made by the admiral [Andrés Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Ni%C3%B1o), who led an expedition to Central America. He disembarked in the [Gulf of Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Fonseca) on 31 May 1522, at [Meanguera island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meanguera_del_Golfo), naming it Petronila,[^cite_note-Valdés2006-12] and then traversed to [Jiquilisco Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiquilisco_Bay) on the mouth of [Lempa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lempa_River). The first indigenous people to have contact with the Spanish were the Lenca of eastern El Salvador.

[El Salvador](9356.md) has the largest population density in [Latin America](18524.md), and is the third most populated country in [Central America](6121.md) after [Honduras](13394.md) and [Guatemala](17238567.md), from the 2005 census, the population exceeds 6 million. As is the case elsewhere in [Latin America](18524.md), there is no clear distinction between White and Mestizo Salvadorans, the large majority of the population have varying proportions of [Spanish](26667.md) and [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) ancestry. In addition, many Salvadorans have more recent ancestry from [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), [German](152735.md), [Swiss](7351032.md), [English](18803164.md), [Irish](775859.md), and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_people) descent. A majority of [Central European](5188.md) settlers in El Salvador arrived during World War II as refugees from the [Czech Republic](5321.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Hungary](13275.md), [Poland](22936.md), and [Switzerland](26748.md). In northern departments like the [Chalatenango Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalatenango_Department), it is well known that residents in the area are of pure Spanish descent. The governor of San Salvador, [Francisco Luis Héctor de Carondelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Luis_H%C3%A9ctor_de_Carondelet), ordered families from northern Spain ([Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\)) and [Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturias)) to settle the area to compensate for the lack of indigenous people to work the land; it is not uncommon to see people with blond hair, fair skin, and blue or green eyes in municipalities like [Dulce Nombre de María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulce_Nombre_de_Mar%C3%ADa), [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma,_El_Salvador), and [El Pital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_El_Pital). However, the majority of Salvadorans of full Spanish descent possess Mediterranean racial features: olive skin and dark hair and eyes (black or dark brown) and identify with the [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) majority, As for the mestizo / castizo population, it dates back to the time of the discovery of America, Because there were no Spanish women, the Spaniards maintained relationships with Amerindian women, before the discovery, El Salvador was the second Central American country with the least indigenous population, and due to the hostility of the Spanish and added to the diseases brought by them, the population was greatly reduced and precipitously, the Amerindian men were more affected than the Amerindian women, in the first years of the colony, 50% of the population Salvadoran was Mestizo and White, in 1805, 78% of the inhabitants of El Salvador were Mestizo and White.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

Later, in the post-colonial era, the country received several groups of European immigrants, mainly from Spain and Italy, mainly between 1880 and 1930, when several Europeans emigrated to the country, immigration had a great demographic impact, the population of El Salvador went from 480 thousand to 1.2 million inhabitants[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

#### Arab Salvadorans



*Arab Salvadorans* include [Palestinian Salvadoran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Salvadoran), Lebanese Salvadoran, Syrian Salvadoran and Egyptian Salvadoran.

There is a significant with at least partial Arab descent (of about 200,000);[^cite_note-26] mostly from [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) (especially from the area of [Bethlehem](4312.md)), but also from Lebanon. [Salvadorans of Palestinian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Salvadoran) numbered around 150,000 individuals, while Salvadorans of [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people) descent is around 30,000.[^cite_note-27] There is also a small community of [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_El_Salvador) who came to El Salvador from France, Germany, [Morocco](19291.md), [Tunisia](30188.md), and [Turkey](11125639.md).

Arab immigration in El Salvador began at the end of the 19th century in the wake of the repressive policies applied by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) against [Maronite Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite_Catholics). Several of the destinations that the Lebanese chose at that time were in countries of the Americas, including El Salvador. This resulted in the Arab diaspora residents being characterized by forging in devoutly Christian families and very attached to their beliefs, because in these countries they can exercise their faith without fear of persecution, which resulted in the rise of Lebanese-Salvadoran, Syrian-Salvadoran and Palestinian-Salvadoran communities in El Salvador.[^cite_note-28]

Currently, the [Palestinian](23267.md) community forms the largest Arab diaspora population in El Salvador, with 150,000 direct descendants, followed by the [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people) community with more than 30,000 direct descendants. Both are almost entirely composed of [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) and [Orthodox Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodoxy#Christianity).[^cite_note-29]

Inter-ethnic marriage in the Lebanese community with Salvadorans, regardless of religious affiliation, is very high; most have only one father with Lebanese nationality and mother of Salvadoran nationality. As a result, some of them speak Arabic fluently. But most, especially among younger generations, speak Spanish as a first language and Arabic as a second.[^cite_note-30]

Arab-Salvadoreans and their descendants have traditionally played an outsized role in El Salvador's economic and political life, with many becoming business leaders and noteworthy political figures.

According to the 2024 Salvadoran census, about 1.2% of the population are of full or predominantly indigenous origin. Currently the only native groups of El Salvador are the Pipil people, Lenca people and Cacaopera people; before the Spanish Conquest, the territory also hosted small enclaves of [Maya peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_peoples): the [Pogomam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poqomam_people)/[Chorti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorti_people), the [Xinca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinca_people), and the [Mengue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangue_language). The [Pipil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipil_people) are located in the west and central part of the country; the [Lenca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenca) are found east of the [Lempa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lempa_River); and there are small populations of [Cacaopera people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacaopera_people) in the [Morazán Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraz%C3%A1n_Department).[^cite_note-El_Salvador_Antiguas_Civilizaciones-7][^cite_note-Mined_Historia_de_El_Salvador-8][^cite_note-Algunos_grupos_cerámicos_pipiles-31]

The official number of indigenous people in El Salvador has been criticized by indigenous organizations and academics as too small and many accuse the government of denying the existence of indigenous Salvadorans in the country.[^cite_note-32] According to the National Salvadoran Indigenous Coordination Council (CCNIS) and CONCULTURA (National Council for Art and Culture at the Ministry of Education), approximately 70,000 or 1 per cent of Salvadorian peoples are indigenous.[^cite_note-El_Salvador_-_Indigenous_peoples-33] Nonetheless, very few Amerindians have retained their customs and traditions, having over time assimilated into the dominant Mestizo/Spanish culture. The low numbers of indigenous people may be partly explained by historically high rates of old-world diseases, absorption into the mestizo population, as well as mass murder during the [1932 Salvadoran peasant uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Salvadoran_peasant_uprising) (or *La Matanza*). This massacre saw (estimates of) up to 30,000 [peasants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasants) killed in a short period of time. Many authors note that since *La Matanza* the indigenous in El Salvador have been very reluctant to describe themselves as such (in census declarations for example) or to wear indigenous dress or be seen to be taking part in any cultural activities or customs that might be understood as indigenous.[^cite_note-34] Departments and cities in the country with notable indigenous populations include [Sonsonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonsonate_Department) (especially [Izalco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izalco), [Nahuizalco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuizalco), and [Santo Domingo de Guzmán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Domingo_de_Guzm%C3%A1n,_El_Salvador)), [Cacaopera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacaopera), and [Panchimalco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchimalco), in the [San Salvador Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvador_Department).[^cite_note-El_Salvador_-_Indigenous_peoples-33]

The [culture of El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_El_Salvador) is a [Central American](6121.md) culture nation influenced by the clash of ancient [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica) and medieval [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula). [Salvadoran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran) culture is influenced by [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) culture ([Lenca people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenca_people), [Cacaopera people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacaopera_people), [Maya peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_peoples), [Pipil people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipil_people)) as well as [Latin American culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_culture) ([Latin America](18524.md), [Hispanic America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America), [Ibero-America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibero-America)). [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) culture and the [Catholic Church](606848.md) dominates the country. Although the [Romance language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages), [Castilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilian_Spanish) [Spanish](26825.md), is the official and dominant language spoken in El Salvador, [Salvadoran Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Spanish) which is part of [Central American Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Spanish) has influences of [Native American languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas) of El Salvador such as [Lencan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lencan_languages), [Cacaopera language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacaopera_language), [Mayan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayan_languages) and [Pipil language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipil_language), which are still spoken in some regions of El Salvador.

[Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) culture dominates the country, heavy in both Native American Indigenous and European Spanish influences. A new composite population was formed as a result of intermarrying between the native [Mesoamerican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican) population of Cuzcatlan with the European settlers. The [Catholic Church](606848.md) plays an important role in the Salvadoran culture. [Archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop) [Óscar Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Romero) is a national hero for his role in resisting human rights violations that were occurring in the lead-up to the Salvadoran Civil War.[^cite_note-Eaton-40] Significant foreign personalities in El Salvador were the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) priests and professors [Ignacio Ellacuría](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacio_Ellacur%C3%ADa), [Ignacio Martín-Baró](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacio_Mart%C3%ADn-Bar%C3%B3), and [Segundo Montes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segundo_Montes), who were murdered in 1989 by the Salvadoran Army during the height of the civil war.

Painting, ceramics and textiles are the principal manual artistic mediums. Writers [Francisco Gavidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Gavidia) (1863–1955), [Salarrué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salarru%C3%A9) (Salvador Salazar Arrué) (1899–1975), [Claudia Lars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Lars), [Alfredo Espino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Espino), [Pedro Geoffroy Rivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Geoffroy_Rivas), [Manlio Argueta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manlio_Argueta), [José Roberto Cea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Roberto_Cea), and poet [Roque Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Dalton) are among the most important writers from El Salvador. Notable 20th-century personages include the late filmmaker Baltasar Polio, female film director [Patricia Chica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Chica), artist [Fernando Llort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Llort), and [caricaturist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caricaturist) [Toño Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To%C3%B1o_Salazar).

Amongst the more renowned representatives of the graphic arts are the painters [Augusto Crespin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Crespin), [Noe Canjura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noe_Canjura), [Carlos Cañas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ca%C3%B1as), [Giovanni Gil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Gil), Julia Díaz, Mauricio Mejía, María Elena Palomo de Mejía, [Camilo Minero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilo_Minero), Ricardo Carbonell, Roberto Huezo, Miguel Ángel Cerna, (the painter and writer better known as MACLo), Esael Araujo, and many others. For more information on prominent citizens of El Salvador, check the [List of Salvadorans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Salvadorans).
