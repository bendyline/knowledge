**Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham** (**HTS**)[^cite_note-29] was a [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [Islamist](15012.md) political and paramilitary group involved in the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-newsok-26] It was formed on 28 January 2017 as a merger of several armed groups: [Jabhat Fatah al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front#As_Jabhat_Fatah_Al-Sham_\(July_2016_%E2%80%93_January_2017\)) (JFS), [Ansar al-Din Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Din_Front), [Jaysh al-Sunna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaysh_al-Sunna), [Liwa al-Haqq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_al-Haqq_\(Idlib\)) and the [Nour al-Din al-Zenki Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nour_al-Din_al-Zenki_Movement).[^cite_note-new_entity-2][^cite_note-Mona_Alami-32] The unification process was held under the initiative of [Abu Jaber Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Jaber_Sheikh), an Islamist militant commander who had been the second [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) of [Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham). HTS, along with other [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition) groups, [launched an offensive](78460726.md) that led to the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) on 8 December 2024, more than 13 years after the start of the [Syrian revolution](39750126.md).[^cite_note-new_entity-2]

Proclaiming the nascent organisation as "a new stage in the life of the blessed revolution", [Abu Jaber Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Jaber_Sheikh) urged all factions of the [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition) to unite under its Islamic leadership and wage a "popular *[jihad](16203.md)*" to achieve the objectives of the 2011 revolution, which he characterised as the ousting of the [Ba'athist regime](57134800.md) and [Hezbollah](13919.md) militants from [Syrian territories](7515849.md), and the formation of [an Islamic government](2646438.md).[^cite_note-33] After the announcement, additional groups and individuals joined. The merged group has been primarily led by Jabhat Fatah al-Sham and former [Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham) leaders, although the High Command also has representation from other groups.[^cite_note-Reuters01-28-17-34] The Nour al-Din al-Zenki Movement[^cite_note-defect-35] split from Tahrir al-Sham in July 2017, and the [Ansar al-Din Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Din_Front) in 2018.[^cite_note-auto9-36]

The formation of HTS was followed by a string of assassinations of its supporters. In response, HTS launched a successful crackdown on [al-Qaeda](1921.md) loyalists, which cemented its power in [Idlib](2528679.md). HTS then pursued a "Syrianisation" programme, focused on establishing a stable civilian administration that provides services and connects to humanitarian organizations in addition to maintaining law and order.[^cite_note-Mona_Alami-32] Tahrir al-Sham's strategy was based on expanding its territorial control in Syria, establishing governance and mobilising popular support. In 2017, HTS permitted [Turkish troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_troops) to patrol North-West Syria as part of a ceasefire brokered through the [Astana negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astana_Platform). Its policies brought it into conflict with [Hurras al-Din](56718004.md), al-Qaeda's Syrian wing, including [militarily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlib_Governorate_clashes_\(June_2020\)).[^cite_note-37] HTS had an estimated 6,000–15,000 members in 2022.

From 2017 to 2024, HTS gave allegiance to the [Syrian Salvation Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government) (SSG), which served as its civilian administration in [Idlib Governorate](7445043.md).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] While the organisation officially adhered to the [Salafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement) school, the High Council of Fatwa of the Syrian Salvation Government – to which it is religiously beholden – consisted of *[ulema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulama)* from [Ash'arite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash'ari) and [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) traditions as well. In its legal system and educational curriculum, HTS implemented [Shafi'ite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i_school) thought and taught the importance of the four classical [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) *[madhahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab)* (schools of law) in [Islamic jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh).[^cite_note-auto1-40]

From 2021 to 2024, HTS was the most powerful military faction within the [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition_\(2011%E2%80%932024\)).[^cite_note-Hay'at_Tahrir_al-Sham-41] Τhe organisation was designated a [terrorist](30636.md) group by [United Nations Security Council Resolution 2254](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_2254),[^cite_note-42] which classified the group's precursor, [Jabhat al-Nusra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front).[^cite_note-43] After the [fall of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Damascus_\(2024\)) in December 2024, the SSG was replaced by the [Syrian transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government). On 29 January 2025, at the [Syrian Revolution Victory Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Revolution_Victory_Conference) held in Damascus, [Hassan Abdul Ghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Abdul_Ghani), spokesperson for the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command), announced the dissolution of HTS and several armed factions and declared that they would become part of "state institutions".[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-Dissolution-16][^cite_note-45] Throughout 2025 and 2026, a number of countries revoked their designations of HTS as a terrorist organization, including the United States, UK, Canada, and Japan.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-\:7-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-arabnews.jp-49] The UN Security Council and European Union also lifted sanctions on the organization.[^cite_note-press.un.org-50][^cite_note-\:8-51]

### Predecessors

Before becoming the emir of al-Nusra Front, [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) started his military career in 2003, travelling from the Syrian capital Damascus to Iraq, where he resisted the [U.S. occupation of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._occupation_of_Iraq). He joined [al-Qaeda in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_Iraq) and fought in the [Iraqi insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%932006_phase_of_the_Iraqi_insurgency) against the U.S. occupation.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-52][^cite_note-Raya_Jalabi-53] After his imprisonment by the U.S. military in 2006 and subsequent release by the Iraqi government in 2011, al-Sharaa was tasked with establishing [al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front) as [al-Qaeda's](1921.md) branch in Syria. Between 2011 and 2012, he coordinated with [Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_al-Baghdadi), leader of the [Islamic State of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_of_Iraq) (ISI), to expand al-Qaeda's mission in Syria.[^cite_note-Raya_Jalabi-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-52]

In April 2013, Baghdadi announced his group's expansion into Syria and declared the creation of the [Islamic State](9087364.md) (ISIL) and unilaterally demanded al-Nusra's merger into ISIL. Al-Sharaa denounced the move; and maintained his *[bay'ah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay'ah)* (trans: "pledge of allegiance") to al-Qaeda.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-52] al-Qaeda emir [Ayman al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahiri) also denounced al-Baghdadi's announcement, asserting that [Syria](7515849.md) was the "spatial state" of al-Nusra Front. Zawahiri officially demanded the dissolution of the new entity and urged Baghdadi to withdraw all his troops from Syria.[^cite_note-Atassi-55][^cite_note-Lund2-56][^cite_note-Bunzel-57] In a letter addressed to the leaderships of ISIL and al-Nusra Front in June 2013, Ayman al-Zawahiri directly rebuked al-Baghdadi's moves by recognizing al-Nusra Front as the only official Syrian branch of al-Qaeda and demanded the dissolution of ISIL.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-Atassi-55]

By January 2014, hostile rhetoric between ISIL and al-Nusra Front escalated into violent conflict.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] Al-Nusra Front formally cut all factional links to ISIL in February 2014 when al-Qaeda's general command issued an official statement disavowing and terminating all relations with ISIL.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-Lund2-56][^cite_note-62] In July 2016, al-Nusra Front under al-Sharaa split from al-Qaeda, rebranding as [Jabhat Fatah al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front#As_Jabhat_Fatah_Al-Sham_\(July_2016_%E2%80%93_January_2017\)).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] Before that time, HTS espoused hardline Islamist views, such as viewing non-Muslims as second-class citizens, but after 2016, it began moderating some of its positions in an effort to gain wider legitimacy.[^cite_note-\:4-65]

### Background

Main article: [Jabhat Fateh al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabhat_Fateh_al-Sham)

See also: [October 2016 Idlib Governorate clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2016_Idlib_Governorate_clashes) and [Second Battle of Maarat al-Nu'man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Maarat_al-Nu'man)

Al-Nusra Front co-operated with [Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham) throughout most of 2015 and 2016. Leading Ahrar al-Sham cleric [Abu Jaber Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Jaber_Sheikh) saw Jabhat al-Nusra's prior affiliation to al-Qaeda as detrimental to their cause. He also sought to unify Islamist rebel factions.[^cite_note-Petkova_2018_After_Sochi-66][^cite_note-new_entity-2][^cite_note-Joscelyn_2016_unite_behind-67] He briefly led a more Islamist and less nationalist faction within Ahrar al-Sham, [Jaysh al-Ahrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaysh_al-Ahrar), which supported a merger with Jabhat Fatah al-Sham, before dissolving the group in early 2017. There were merger talks in late 2016, but these broke down. By January 20, 2017, JFS started launching [attacks on rival Islamist groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlib_Governorate_clashes_\(January%E2%80%93March_2017\)) in Idlib, particularly Ahrar al-Sham, before merging with several other allied Islamist groups into "Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham".[^cite_note-Combating_Terrorism_Center_at_West_Point_2018_How_al-Qa'ida_Lost_Control-68]

### Formation

Upon its founding, HTS was described by [Abu Jaber Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Jaber_Sheikh), who became the organisation's first [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir), as "an independent entity" formed through the unification of previous groups and factions.[^cite_note-69]

Charles Lister, an analyst specialising in Syria, noted that Ahrar al-Sham lost around 800–1,000 defectors to HTS but gained 6,000–8,000 fighters through the merger of [Suqor al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suqor_al-Sham), [Jaish al-Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Mujahideen), the [Fastaqim Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastaqim_Union), the western Aleppo units of the [Levant Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant_Front), and the Idlib-based units of [Jaysh al-Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaysh_al-Islam). JFS lost several hundred fighters to Ahrar al-Sham but gained 3,000–5,000 fighters from its merger with Harakat Nour al-Din al-Zinki, Liwa al-Haq, Jaish al-Sunna, and Jabhat Ansar al-Din into HTS.[^cite_note-Combating_Terrorism_Center_at_West_Point_2018_How_al-Qa'ida_Lost_Control-68] Nour al-Din al-Zenki previously received support from the United States.[^cite_note-70]

### Consolidation of power (January–August 2017)

#### January



HTS fighters in the village of Mushairfa, northeast of [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama), during the [northeastern Hama offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Hama_offensive_\(2017\)) in October 2017.

Throughout January 2017, intense fighting broke out between [al-Qaeda](1921.md) loyalists of [al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front), before the JFS-[Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham) merger to form HTS on 28 January. Soon after the merger, its new emir Abu Jaber Sheikh announced a ceasefire deal aimed at uniting all [opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition_\(2011%E2%80%932024\)) militia factions under a central command. The creation of HTS was described as by the [Henry Jackson Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Jackson_Society) as a reshaping of dynamics since the [2011 Syrian Revolution](39750126.md), with a potential to shift the balance of power in the Syrian civil war and diminish al-Qaeda's influence in northern Syria.[^cite_note-reshapes-71] Between 2017 and 2019, HTS launched a series of crackdowns against al-Qaeda loyalists while also targeting [Islamic State](9087364.md) (IS) cells through military operations.[^cite_note-\:0-72]

On 30 January, *[Asharq al-Awsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asharq_Al-Awsat)* reported that HTS had about 31,000 fighters, bolstered by the inclusion of various breakaway factions from [Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham).[^cite_note-surfacing-17]

#### February

On 3 February, a US airstrike struck a HTS headquarters in [Sarmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmin), killing 10 members of HTS and 2 members [Jund al-Aqsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jund_al-Aqsa).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-11_terrorists_killed-74]

Civilians in the rebel regions that HTS controls have resisted it. On 3 February, hundreds of Syrians demonstrated under the slogan "There is no place for [al-Qaeda](1921.md) in Syria" in the towns of [Atarib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atarib), [Azaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azaz), [Maarat al-Nu'man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maarat_al-Nu'man) to protest against HTS. In response, supporters of HTS organized [counter-protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-protest) in [al-Dana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Dana,_Syria), Idlib, Atarib, and [Khan Shaykhun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Shaykhun).[^cite_note-75] In Idlib pro-Ha'yat Tahrir al-Sham protests were held waving pictures of its emir [Abu Jaber Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Jaber_Sheikh) on 3 February 2017.[^cite_note-76]

On 4 February 2017, a US airstrike killed former al-Qaeda commander [Abu Hani al-Masri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Hani_al-Masri?action=edit\&redlink=1), who was a part of Ahrar al-Sham at the time of his death. It was reported that he was about to defect to HTS before his death.[^cite_note-11_terrorists_killed-74] Around 8 February, [Abu Muhammad al-Maqdisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Muhammad_al-Maqdisi) confirmed that 2 senior Jabhat Fateh al-Sham leaders loyal to al-Qaeda, including former al-Nusra deputy leader [Sami al-Oraydi](46871511.md), left Tahrir al-Sham after its formation.[^cite_note-formation-77]

A speech was released by Abu Jaber Sheikh on 9 February.[^cite_note-78] He emphasized his group as being an "independent entity" and praised his "brothers" in the "Syrian [Jihad](16203.md)" for their "heroic" resistance against [Ba'athist forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Socialist_Ba'ath_Party_%E2%80%93_Syria_Region), [Hezbollah](13919.md) and Russians. The statement urged all [opposition factions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition_\(2011%E2%80%932024\)) to join forces with HTS and warned [Syrian Sunnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam_in_Syria); asserting that Iran will "enslave the region" if the rebels lost the war.[^cite_note-jaber-79]

On 12 February, [al-Bunyan al-Marsous Operations Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bunyan_al-Marsous_Operations_Room), of which Tahrir al-Sham was a member, launched an [offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraa_offensive_\(February_2017\)) against the [Syrian Army](3423927.md) in [Daraa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraa)'s Manshiyah district. HTS forces reportedly began the attack with 2 [suicide bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bomber) and [car bombs](155282.md).[^cite_note-80]

On 13 February, clashes erupted between the previously allied Tahrir al-Sham and [Jund al-Aqsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jund_al-Aqsa), also called Liwa al-Aqsa, in northern Hama and southern Idlib.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

On 15 February, Ahrar al-Sham published an infographic on its recent defections, claiming that only 955 fighters had defected to Tahrir al-Sham.[^cite_note-formation-77] On 22 February, the [Combating Terrorism Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combating_Terrorism_Center) reported that Jabhat Fateh al-Sham had formed the Tahrir al-Sham group due to its fear of being isolated, and to counter Ahrar al-Sham's recent expansion during the clashes in Idlib Governorate.[^cite_note-formation-77]

On 22 February, the last of Liwa al-Aqsa's 2,100 militants left their final positions in [Khan Shaykhun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Shaykhun), with unconfirmed reports in pro-government media that they were to join IS in Raqqa Governorate after a negotiated withdrawal deal with Tahrir al-Sham and the Turkistan Islamic Party.[^cite_note-leaves_for_Raqqa-83] Afterward, Tahrir al-Sham declared the termination of Liwa al-Aqsa, and promised to watch for any remaining cells.[^cite_note-declare_terminating-84]

On 26 February, a US airstrike in al-Mastoumeh, Idlib Governorate, killed [Abu Khayr al-Masri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Khayr_al-Masri), who was the deputy leader of al-Qaeda.[^cite_note-war-85][^cite_note-drone_strike-86] The airstrike also killed another Tahrir al-Sham militant.[^cite_note-2_Tahrir_al-Sham_fighters_killed-87][^cite_note-AQ_deputy_killed2-88] Abu Khayr's death left HTS freer to move away from al-Qaeda's any remaining influence.[^cite_note-Combating_Terrorism_Center_at_West_Point_2018_How_al-Qa'ida_Lost_Control-68]

#### March

In early March 2017, local residents in Idlib Governorate who supported FSA factions accused HTS of doing more harm than good, saying that all they've done is "kidnap people, set up checkpoints, and terrorize residents".[^cite_note-rally_against-89]

On 16 March, a [US airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_al-Jinah_airstrike) struck the village of al-Jinah, just southwest of [Atarib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atarib), killing at least 29 and possibly over 50 civilians; the US claimed the people targeted in the strike were "al-Qaeda militants" but the [Syrian Observatory for Human Rights](34029669.md) (SOHR), local residents and local officials have said that the building struck was a mosque filled with worshipers, which was subsequently confirmed by [Bellingcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellingcat).[^cite_note-LAtimes-90][^cite_note-aljazeera-91][^cite_note-92]

On the morning of 21 March, according to pro-government media, a US drone strike in [Darkush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkush), Idlib Governorate, killed Abu Islam al-Masri, described as an Egyptian high-ranking HTS commander, and Abu al-'Abbas al-Darir, described as an Egyptian HTS commander;[^cite_note-high_ranking_jihadist_killed-93] however, the [Institute for the Study of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_War) reported that the commander killed was Sheikh Abu al-Abbas al-Suri.[^cite_note-94]

On 24 March, two [flatbed trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbed_truck) carrying [flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flour) and belonging to an [IHH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHH_\(Turkish_NGO\))-affiliated Turkish relief organization were stopped at a HTS checkpoint at the entrance to [Sarmada](21140171.md). HTS then seized the trucks and the flour, which was intended for a [bakery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakery) in [Saraqib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraqib). The seizure caused 2,000 families in the area to be cut off from a free supply of [bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread).[^cite_note-95]

In April 2017, [Jaysh al-Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaysh_al-Islam) attacked HTS and expelled it from the territories under its control in Eastern Ghouta.[^cite_note-Petkova_2018-96]

On 3 May, HTS arrested [Suhail Muhammad Hamoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhail_Muhammad_Hamoud), "Abu [TOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGM-71_TOW)", a former FSA fighter, in a house raid in Idlib. Earlier, al-Hamoud had published a photograph of him [smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking) in front of a HTS billboard that [prohibited smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_fatwa).[^cite_note-97]

#### May

According to reports from pro-government [Al-Masdar News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Masdar_News), on 20 May, the main faction of the Abu Amara Battalions joined Tahrir al-Sham, which "now boasts a fighting force of some 50,000 militants" according to one pro-government media source.[^cite_note-joins-98] However, the [covert operations](613453.md) unit of the Abu Amara Battalions based in Aleppo remained independent.[^cite_note-all4syria.info-99]

On 29 May, HTS arrested the FSA commander and activist [Abdul Baset al-Sarout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Baset_al-Sarout) after accusing him of participating in an anti-HTS protest in [Maarat al-Nu'man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maarat_al-Nu'man).[^cite_note-100]

#### June

On 2 June 2017, defectors from the Northern Brigade's Commandos of Islam Brigade reportedly joined Tahrir al-Sham, although Captain Kuja, leader of the unit, stated that he is still part of the Northern Brigade.[^cite_note-north-101]

#### July

During 18–23 July, HTS launched a series of attacks on Ahrar al-Sham positions, which were quickly abandoned.[^cite_note-Combating_Terrorism_Center_at_West_Point_2018_How_al-Qa'ida_Lost_Control-68] On 20 July 2017, the Nour al-Din al-Zenki Movement led by Sheikh Tawfiq Shahabuddin announced its withdrawal from Tahrir al-Sham amid [widespread conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlib_Governorate_clashes_\(July_2017\)) between HTS and Ahrar al-Sham, and became an independent Islamist group.[^cite_note-defect-35] On 23 July 2017, Tahrir al-Sham expelled the remnants of [Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham) from Idlib, capturing the entire city[^cite_note-102] as well as 60% of the [Idlib Governorate](7445043.md).[^cite_note-103] HTS was now the dominant armed group in opposition-held Northwestern Syria.[^cite_note-Combating_Terrorism_Center_at_West_Point_2018_How_al-Qa'ida_Lost_Control-68]

#### August

On 18 August 2017, HTS captured 8 rebel fighters from the town of [Madaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madaya,_Syria) after it accused them of wanting to return to Madaya during a [ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Civil_War_ceasefires#Fifth_ceasefire_attempt_\(July%E2%80%93August_2017\)).[^cite_note-104]

#### Attacks (early 2017)

Syrian intelligence commander [Hassan Daaboul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Daaboul) was among the 40 assassinated by Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham, in twin bomb attacks at complexes of the [Ba'athist secret police](19204147.md) in [Homs](77533.md).[^cite_note-jihadism_in_Syria-105] The explosion killed Ibrahim Darwish, a Brigadier General and the state security branch's chief.[^cite_note-106] Abu Yusuf al-Muhajir, a Tahrir al-Sham military spokesman was interviewed by Human Voice on the bombings.[^cite_note-107] Twenty-six names were released.[^cite_note-108] HTS leader Abu Mohammed al-Julani mentioned the Homs attack, stating that it was a message for the "defeatist politicians" to "step aside".[^cite_note-109] It has been disputed that the raid resulted in the death of Ibrahim Darwish.[^cite_note-110]

On 11 March 2017, Tahrir al-Sham carried out a [twin bombing attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2017_Damascus_bombings) in the Bab al-Saghir area of Damascus's Old City, killing 76 and wounding 120. The death toll included 43 Iraqi pilgrims, whom HTS claimed were "Iranian militias" supporting [Assad regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_regime)'s dictatorship.[^cite_note-twin_bombings-111] The attacks were at a shrine frequented by Shi'ite pilgrims and militiamen.[^cite_note-112] In a statement released the following day, Tahrir al-Sham stated that the attacks targeted [Iran](14653.md)-backed militants fight on behalf of [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md) and condemned Khomeinist militants for "killing and displacing" Syrians.[^cite_note-113]

### Leadership passes from Abu Jaber (late 2017)

From September to November 2017, there were a series of assassinations of HTS leaders, in particular foreign clerics associated with the most hardline elements, such as Abu Talha al-Ordini, Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Mohajer, Abu Sulaiman al-Maghribi, Abu Yahya al-Tunisi, Suraqa al-Maki and Abu Mohammad al-Sharii, as well as some local military leaders, including Abu Elias al-Baniasi, Mustafa al-Zahri, Saied Nasrallah and Hassan Bakour. There was speculation that the assassinations were carried out either by pro-Turkish perpetrators, given the hostility between Turkey and HTS in Idlib, or by supporters of al-Sharaa's attempt to turn the organization away from hardline Salafi-jihadi positions. There were also high-profile defections from HTS in the same period, including [Abdullah al-Muhaysini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_al-Muhaysini) and [Muslah al-Alyani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslah_al-Alyani?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Syria_From_Within_{{pipe}}_Chatham_House_2017-114] In December, HTS arrested several prominent jihadi activists, former members of al-Nusra who remained loyal to al-Qaeda and rejected HTS's turn away from Salafi-jihadist positions. The move was interpreted as an attempt to re-establish as a more pragmatic, pan-Sunni group, with a civilian structure. Al-Qaeda leader [Ayman al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahiri) denounced this turn.[^cite_note-Syria_Deeply_2017_steps-115][^cite_note-Shelton_2017-116]

HTS announced Abu Jaber's resignation as the group's leader on 1 October 2017. He was succeeded by al-Nusra Front founder [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md), who had already been the de facto military commander.[^cite_note-Hashim_al-Sheikh-117][^cite_note-Syria_Deeply_4_Oct_2017-118] On 1 October 2017, the Ibn Taymiyyah Battalions based in the town of [Darat Izza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darat_Izza) defected from Tahrir al-Sham.[^cite_note-ibn-119] In October 2017, Russia claimed to have injured Abu Mohammed al-Joulani in an air raid; HTS denied the claim.[^cite_note-The_New_Arab_2017_strike_injured-120][^cite_note-Syria_Deeply_4_Oct_2017-118] HTS established the [Syrian Salvation Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government) as a technocratic civilian administration in Idlib, that rivalled the [Syrian Interim Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Interim_Government) recognized by other rebels.[^cite_note-Petkova_2018-96]

In early 2018, there were reports that HTS had been significantly weakened, and now had "a small presence in Eastern Ghouta and declining influence in Idlib, northern Hama, and western Aleppo provinces", with just 250 men in Eastern Ghouta[^cite_note-Petkova_2018-96] and a total of 12,000 fighters.[^cite_note-Combating_Terrorism_Center_at_West_Point_2018_How_al-Qa'ida_Lost_Control-68]

In February 2018, Tahrir al-Sham was accused of killing Fayez al-Madani, an opposition delegate tasked with negotiations with the government over [electricity delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_delivery) in the northern [Homs Governorate](7445154.md), in the city of [al-Rastan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Rastan). Hundreds of people, including fighters of the Men of God Brigade, part of the [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army)'s National Liberation Movement group,[^cite_note-wordpress.com-121][^cite_note-wordpress.com1-122][^cite_note-123] proceeded to demonstrate against HTS in the city on 13 February. In response, HTS withdrew from Rastan and handed over its headquarters in the city to the Men of God Brigade.[^cite_note-124] Meanwhile, al-Qaeda loyalists formed the anti-HTS [Guardians of Religion Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_Religion_Organization) (Hurras al-Din) in February 2018, establishing it as the successor group of [al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front).[^cite_note-125]

HTS was left excluded from the 24 February ceasefire agreement on Eastern Ghouta. In late February, a group of armed factions, including [Failaq al-Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Rahman_Legion) and [Jaysh al-Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaysh_al-Islam), wrote to the UN declaring they were ready to "evacuate" remaining HTS fighters from Eastern Ghouta within 15 days.[^cite_note-Petkova_2018-96] At the same time in Idlib Governorate, Ahrar al-Sham, Nour al-Din al-Zinki and Soqour al-Sham entered into [conflict with HTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Liberation_Front%E2%80%93Tahrir_al-Sham_conflict), taking significant territory.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-Petkova_2018-96]

During late 2017 and early 2018, it co-operated with Turkey in Idlib, leading to deepening tensions between the more pragmatic leadership and more hardline (especially foreign fighters) elements hostile to working with the Turkish government. Some of the latter split in February 2018 to form [Huras al-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huras_al-Din). The HTS leadership also cracked down on remaining IS splinter cells active in Idlib. By August, when HTS entered into (unsuccessful) negotiations with Russia and Turkey, HTS was estimated to have around 3,000–4,000 foreign fighters, including non-Syrian Arabs, out of a total of 16,000 HTS fighters. On 31 August, Turkey declared HTS a terrorist organization.[^cite_note-Petkova_2018_After_Sochi-66]

By early 2018, HTS had cracked down on the majority of competing factions and established military control over 80% of the greater [Idlib](2528679.md) region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKhalifa2023258-127] In the summer of 2018, HTS strengthened its crackdown campaign against cells affiliated with IS organization in Idlib and Hama regions.[^cite_note-\:0-72]

### 2019

### Revival and victory in Idlib

In January 2019, HTS was able to seize dozens of villages from rivals, and afterwards, a deal was reached in which the civil administration was to be led by HTS in the whole rebel-held Idlib Governorate.[^cite_note-128]

In the wake of the [5th Idlib inter-rebel conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_for_Liberation%E2%80%93Tahrir_al-Sham_conflict), HTS gained control of nearly the entire Idlib pocket, after defeating the Turkish-backed [National Front for Liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_for_Liberation). Following their victory, Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham would immediately violate the ceasefire treaty brokered by [Turkey](11125639.md) and [Russia](25391.md) by placing combat units in the demilitarized zone along the Idlib-Syrian Government border, and attack [SAA](3423927.md) encampments near the area. In response to these attacks, Syrian president Bashar al-Assad increased the number of troops garrisoned near Idlib, which some have argued is an impending renewed offensive in the region, following the [Northwestern Syria Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Syria_campaign_\(October_2017%E2%80%93February_2018\)), where pro-government forces retook the formerly rebel-controlled [Abu al-Duhur Military Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_al-Duhur_Military_Airbase) that was captured by the [FSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) and [Army of Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Conquest) in 2015. In 2019, the U.S. Deputy Assistant Secretary Defense [Michael Mulroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mulroy) stated that "Idlib is essentially the largest collection of al Qaeda affiliates in the world."[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] On 10–11 July, 57 pro-government fighters were killed when HTS militants attacked Syrian positions near the fortified village of Hamamiyat. 44 militants were also killed.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

### 2020–2022

### Cooperation with Turkey

HTS successfully defended Idlib from 2019 to 2020 government offensives. During this period, HTS cemented its security partnership with the [Turkish military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_military_operation_in_Idlib_Governorate) against the [Assad regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_regime).[^cite_note-Hay'at_Tahrir_al-Sham-41] The opposition and Islamist groups led by HTS suffered heavy losses as a result of the [Dawn of Idlib 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Syria_offensive_\(December_2019_%E2%80%93_March_2020\)) operation launched under the leadership of the Assad regime between December 2019 and March 2020, and many cities and towns from the Idlib Pocket fell under the control of regime forces. During this time, Turkey was protesting the siege of Turkish military observation points deployed according to the [Astana agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_peace_process) by regime forces and wanted Assad to stop his attack on the Idlib region.[^cite_note-135] After 34 Turkish soldiers lost their lives in the [Balyut attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Balyun_airstrikes) carried out by Assad's troops, Turkey launched [Operation Spring Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Spring_Shield). As a result of the Turkish counterattack, regime forces and their supporters suffered heavy losses.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] Turkey's counter-offensive halts regime forces advance into Idlib. Taking advantage of this opportunity, the opposition led by HTS launched a counterattack. This cooperation with Turkey prevented HTS and other opposition groups from losing Idlib and forced deportation to the Turkish border.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

On 1 March 2021 it was reported that Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham intensified its campaign against al-Qaeda affiliate [Hurras al-Din](56718004.md) in Idlib.[^cite_note-almcam-141] Since 2021, HTS has started implementing various reconstruction projects in areas under its control, with a focus on establishing civil institutions in opposition-held territories. These included the [Bab al-Hawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_al-Hawa_Border_Crossing) Industrial City project and re-opening of al-Ghazawiya crossing point to connect with the [Syrian Interim Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Interim_Government) held territories.[^cite_note-jusoor.co-142]

After achieving stability in Idlib in 2021, HTS launched the policy of repatriating confiscated properties of minorities in North-West Syria. These also included the re-building of destroyed churches in Idlib. HTS commanders and [SSG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government) officials have since initiated regular meetings to engage with priests and representatives the [Christian community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians_in_syria) in [Idlib](2528679.md).[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]

*[The Washington Post](102226.md)* reported in January 2022 that the group was "trying to convince Syrians and the world that it is no longer as radical and repressive as it once was", voicing rhetoric about combating extremism, and shifting its focus to providing services to the refugees and residents of Idlib Governorate through the Salvation Government.[^cite_note-soften-1-2022-145] On 7 January, Abu Muhammad al-Joulani announced the inauguration of the Aleppo-Bab al-Hawa International Road, presenting the event as part of "a comprehensive plan... to achieve development and progress for the region".[^cite_note-jusoor.co-142]

In August 2022, HTS ideologue [Abu Maria al-Qahtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Maria_al-Qahtani) issued a statement demanding the dissolution of al-Qaeda and urged all AQ branches to cut ties from the organization.[^cite_note-146] In 2022, HTS took a significant amount of territory and several key settlements during the [October 2022 Aleppo clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Aleppo_clashes_\(October_2022\)).

### 2023

In 2023, it was reported that Western hostilities towards HTS had decreased, but were still marked by mutual rivalry, due to conflict with American interests in the region.[^cite_note-International_Crisis_Group-147] The U.S. State Department alleged in 2023 that the group obstructed humanitarian efforts in Idlib through licensing and registration protocols as well as through the imposition of taxes and fees on Western NGOs and regulations on aid distribution and beneficiary selection.[^cite_note-\:32-148]

In a speech before the revolutionary conference of [Syrian Salvation Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government) in May 2023, Julani announced intentions to transform the strategic map of the conflict. Stating that Tahrir al-Sham has achieved remarkable military preparation, Julani asserted that "[Aleppo](159244.md) is the gate to [Damascus](8914.md) and it will be under focus for one or two years."[^cite_note-149] In May 2023, HTS and SDF announced separate proposals to host millions of [Syrian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_Syrian_civil_war) stranded across the neighboring countries, following the [Arab League](52625.md)'s reinstatement of the [Assad government](57134800.md).[^cite_note-150]

In June 2023, Tahrir al-Sham and the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) initiated formal diplomatic talks, concluding an agreement to initiate trade of fuel supplies between [Rojava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria) and [Idlib](7445043.md). The meetings were held against the backdrop of growing tensions between Turkey and SDF, and SDF's intention to deploy HTS as a check on the growing [Turkish influence in northern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_occupation_of_northern_Syria). For their part, HTS proposed joint [counter-terrorism](83392.md) efforts alongside SDF. Apart from economic co-operation, the talks also involved negotiations on political arrangements, such as prospects for a joint HTS–SDF civil administration in the event that [SNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_National_Army) forces were expelled from Northwest Syria.[^cite_note-151]

### 2024



[Syrian Opposition offensives](78460726.md) that overthrew Assad's regime in 11 days

In November, the HTS launched the [Northwestern Aleppo offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Syria_offensive_\(2024\)), which it called Operation *Deterrence of Aggression* reportedly capturing 11 towns and villages in western [Aleppo Governorate](7444618.md), capturing the eponymous governorate's capital of [Aleppo](159244.md) four days into the offensive.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-\:2-153] By 4 December, HTS had captured most of Aleppo Governorate and [Idlib Governorate](7445043.md) and began to advance on [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama).[^cite_note-\:2-153]

According to [Abu Hassan al-Hamawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murhaf_Abu_Qasra), commander of HTS's military wing, the offensive resulted from year-long strategic planning and coordination between opposition groups.[^cite_note-guardian-plot-154] Prior to the operation, HTS had developed enhanced military capabilities, establishing a dedicated drone unit and indigenous weapons production. The group deployed its newly developed "Shahin" (Arabic for Falcon) suicide drone against regime forces for the first time during the offensive. HTS additionally facilitated the creation of a [unified operations room in southern Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Operations_Room), synchronizing military actions with approximately 25 rebel groups to encircle Damascus from multiple fronts.[^cite_note-guardian-plot-154][^cite_note-economist-2024-155]

On 5 December, HTS fighters had captured the city of Hama after two-days of fighting in neighboring villages. The group said it would advance on [Homs](77533.md) (40 km from Hama).[^cite_note-156] The next day, it was reported from a rebel commander and pro-rebel media that Tahir al-Sham forces continued to advance on Homs, reportedly reaching 10 km–5 km north of the city.[^cite_note-157]

On 7–8 December, Damascus [fell to Syrian opposition forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Damascus_\(2024\)), including HTS, the [Southern Operations Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Operations_Room), and the US-backed [Syrian Free Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Free_Army),[^cite_note-158] and Assad fled to Russia.[^cite_note-159] On 30 December, HTS commander and the *de facto* leader of Syria, [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md), announced that the organisation would be dissolved by 4–5 January 2025,[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] however, this announced deadline of the formal dissolution was delayed until the finalized process took place on 29 January.[^cite_note-Dissolution-16]

## Ideology and governance

[Abu Jaber Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Jaber_Sheikh), a prominent scholar and chief of the Shura Council of Tahrir al-Sham, was arrested several times by the [Ba'athist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_regime), which accused him of holding [Salafi jihadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_jihadism) beliefs. He was imprisoned at Sednaya Prison in 2005 and released in 2011 alongside several jihadist prisoners who later formed Salafi rebel groups during the Syrian civil war.[^cite_note-jaber-79] Abu Jaber advocates for "Popular Jihad," a grassroots approach that focuses on winning popular support before establishing [Islamic governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_governance), contrasting with IS's "Elite Jihad," which relies on a vanguard to lead from the top down.[^cite_note-al-Qaeda's_grand_plan-162] [Aymenn Jawad al-Tamimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymenn_Jawad_Al-Tamimi) of the [Combating Terrorism Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combating_Terrorism_Center) claimed in February 2017 that despite public statements by Tahrir al-Sham's leaders and partisans, the group was essentially [al-Qaeda](1921.md)-aligned.[^cite_note-formation-77]

On 18 June 2019, HTS released a statement offering condolences to [Egypt](8087628.md)'s former president [Muhammad Morsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Morsi) upon his death in Egyptian custody.[^cite_note-163] In an interview given to the [PBS Frontline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontline_\(American_TV_program\)) documentarian [Martin Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Smith_\(documentarian\)), [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) stated regarding the religious doctrines and political goals of Tahrir al-Sham:[^cite_note-Interview-Abu_Mohammad_al-Jolani-164]

> To limit the description of the HTS to only being a [Salafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement) or jihadist, I believe, needs a long discussion. And I don't want to comment on that now, because it would take a lot of research and study. We are trying today to talk about Islam in its real concept, the Islam that seeks to spread justice and aspires for building and for progress, and to protect [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Islam) and preserve their rights, and for education as well. So if we agree that there's an Islamic rule in the liberated areas, we say that there are universities, by Allah's grace, full of students, two-thirds being female students. There are more than 450,000 to 500,000 students enrolled in schools. There are fully functioning hospitals in the liberated areas, and there are people working to build towns and pave roads. Others are trying to establish an economic system for people to live securely and peacefully. And there's a judicial system that seeks to give people back their rights and not only to punish the wrongdoers in the way some people would think, when they hear it is an Islamic or a Salafi group.

&#x20;Being strongly supportive of the four traditional [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) *[madhahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhahib)* (schools of jurisprudence), HTS has promoted and enforced the [Shafi'i school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i_school) in territories under its control, thereby gaining popular support and influence amongst the [Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i_school)-majority population of [Idlib](2528679.md). One objective of the promotion and implementation of the *madh'hab* system by HTS has been to foster institutionalisation of governance within the framework of traditional [Islamic jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_jurisprudence).[^cite_note-165] Scholar Abu Abdullah al-Shami, the chief of HTS's religious council, has stated that "relying on the schools of jurisprudence" is a pathway towards "getting closer to people". According to Bassam Sihiouni, an influential member of the High Council of the Fatwa:&#x20;

> "The schools of jurisprudence are among the safest ways of preserving a correct and inventive intellectual orientation while applying the laws, ethics and morals of [Islam](6037917.md). On the contrary, abandoning these schools would result in a decline of jurisprudence on the basis of just and righteous thinking."[^cite_note-166]

Ines Barnard and Charlie Winter have described HTS as the "most complex and sophisticated Islamist group" in Syria, which integrates mobilisation of popular support and military operations through its [strategic communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_communication) networks.[^cite_note-167] HTS has also expressed its support for the right of [Palestinians](23267.md) to [reclaim their land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_the_river_to_the_sea).[^cite_note-168]

In 2024, the [BBC](19344654.md) reported that the goal of HTS since its break with al-Qaeda has been limited to trying to establish [Islamic rule](2646438.md) in Syria rather than a wider [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate). The report stated that the messaging of HTS after the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) was "one of inclusiveness and a rejection of violence or revenge".[^cite_note-169] According to Iranian foreign minister [Abbas Araghchi](21951274.md), HTS gave guarantees to protect [Shia](26961.md) religious sites in Syria ahead of the fall of the Assad regime.[^cite_note-170]

### Governance

Despite being a conservative [Islamist](15012.md) political organization, HTS was not socially draconian or extremist in its interpretation of Islamic governance. In contrast to organizations like the more hardline [Taliban](30635.md) or extremist [Islamic State](9087364.md), HTS did not impose the harsher aspects and interpretations of [Sharia](28840.md) over the populations of the regions it governed. Taxation in HTS-controlled areas was minimized due to high poverty levels and fear of antagonizing locals to its rule. The organization generally avoided interfering in the lives of women. It did not compel the use [hijabs](68301.md)/[niqabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niq%C4%81b) or discourage [women's access to education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_education). In 2020, the organization allowed the formation of a volunteer [religious police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_religious_police) but dismantled it within months, stating the concept had no place in a modern state. The organization was quick to respond to pressure from the local population to change course on policy or rhetoric to avoid public backlashes. The generally lenient approach adopted by HTS was the result of years of internal restructuring that left the group with an uncontested leadership that had adopted a pragmatic outlook.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKhalifa2023262–263-171]

&#x20;HTS largely refrained from controlling sectors of governance like education and health. After consolidating administrative control in [Idlib](2528679.md), the organization allowed the preexisting Education Directorate to continue its operations. The curricula adopted in schools under HTS control are a mix of [UNICEF](19698110.md) and Syrian government textbooks (omitting glorification of the [Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party) and the [al-Assad family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Assad_family)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKhalifa2023261-172] Educational curricula of Tahrir al-Sham teach the [Shafi'i school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i_school), the predominant *madhab* in Syria, and emphasize the importance of the four traditional [schools of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab) in ensuring Muslim unity. In theology, Tahrir al-Sham officially follows the [Athari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athari) school while also allowing [Ash'arite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash'ari) scholarship in educational institutes and co-operating with [mosques](19894.md) run by Ash'ari [imamss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imams). As it consolidated power over time, it also reduced its policy of [moral policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisbah), arguing that such duties were the role of "relevant ministries of the [SSG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government)".[^cite_note-173] Commenting on the changes since 2020, a female Islamist activist campaigning for women's issues stated:

> Pressure on individual behaviour fell with the creation of HTS. Before, it would have been inconceivable to imagine that women could talk about politics like this. The pressure to wear the full veil (*[niqab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niq%C4%81b)*) has also diminished... this situation is new. All the women wore the full veil after the [liberation of Idlib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Idlib_\(2015\)). The current transformation exasperates religious hardliners, but more pressure cannot be exerted on society, or it might get out of control.[^cite_note-174]

HTS also previously advocated for a centralised [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) and rejecting [federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism) in the process.[^cite_note-Unitary_and_centralisation-175]

### Protection of minorities

HTS, composed primarily of Jabhat al-Nusra (JaN), had for years—since its inception—viewed non-Muslims as second-class citizens to be tolerated. However, following a public rebranding after 2016, HTS publicly distanced itself from some of its earlier hardline positions.[^cite_note-\:4-65] For several years, HTS had worked to significantly improve relations with minority groups under its rule, such as the [Syrian Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Christianity) and [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) communities of [Idlib](2528679.md), notably returning property stolen during the civil war and permitting Christians living in its territories to reopen their churches.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKhalifa2023263-176]

Following the fall of the Assad regime, HTS military commander [Murhaf Abu Qasra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murhaf_Abu_Qasra) met with [Alawite](436616.md) leaders in [Latakia](195594.md) to provide assurances that the minority group would not face retribution, pointing to the group's treatment of Christians in Aleppo as evidence of their commitment to protecting minorities. The group also announced a general amnesty for former regime soldiers, with exceptions for those who committed war crimes or participated in torture.[^cite_note-economist-2024-155]

In June 2022, HTS leader [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) met with a delegation of Druze leaders and inaugurated a project that facilitated water supply to Druze-majority villages in the [Jabal al-Summaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harim_Mountains). At the event, al-Sharaa assured the Druze leaders that he was committed to improving economic conditions in the region and expanding public services provided by the [Syrian Salvation Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government). Various Syrian observers viewed the event as part of a public outreach programme of HTS with local Druze residents.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179]

In 2022, Tahrir al-Sham permitted the re-opening of churches in [Idlib](2528679.md), enabling the Christian residents to celebrate [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)). After a meeting with members of the Christian clergy and civil activists, al-Sharaa announced his policy to protect Christian participation in their religious rites and celebrations. He also promised the restoration of properties unjustly seized from Christian citizens. Al-Qaeda-aligned [Hurras al-Din](56718004.md) condemned the move, accusing the Salvation Government of changing Idlib to be "less Muslim". In response, Tahrir al-Sham leaders maintained that *[sharia](28840.md)* (Islamic law) safeguarded the rights of non-Muslim citizens (*[dhimmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhimmi)* or *[musta'min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musta'min)*) to observe and teach their religious rites within their communities, arguing for the need for tolerance and peaceful relations between religious communities living in an [Islamic government](2646438.md).[^cite_note-180]

Hanna Celluf, [Archpriest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archpriest) of a 1000-year-old church in [al-Quniyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quniyah) and a member of the [Franciscan Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan_Order), stated that Idlib residents have been free to practice their religious rites and expressed his pride in serving the Christians of Idlib.[^cite_note-181] In December 2024, following the swift overthrow of Assad, the group's spokesman, [Hassan Abdul Ghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Abdul_Ghani), stated that all religions will be free in the new Syrian state now that tyranny is gone forever.[^cite_note-182]

## Structure

### Member groups

The groups in *italic* were defectors from [Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham) which either left to join [Jabhat Fateh al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabhat_Fateh_al-Sham) in the last few days of its existence, or joined its successor group Tahrir al-Sham.

Military commander [Murhaf Abu Qasra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murhaf_Abu_Qasra) (Abu Hassan al-Hamawi) stated the unified armed forces would be 'tasked with protecting the nation on behalf of all Syrians.'[^cite_note-economist-2024-155]

### Leadership

Since October 2017 until 29 January 2025, the "general commander" or emir of Tahrir al-Sham was [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md), who was also Tahrir al-Sham's "military commander" and the emir of [Jabhat Fatah al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front), who also led its predecessor organisation [al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front), the Syrian branch of [al-Qaeda](1921.md) until 2016.[^cite_note-217] He would later abandon the use of his nom de guerre and became known by his birth name, [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md).

Abu Jaber then assumed the position of head of HTS's Shura council.[^cite_note-fate-1]

## Political relations

### Al-Qaeda

#### Initial Disputes (2017–2019)

During its foundation declaration, [Abu Jaber Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Jaber_Sheikh) had described the Levant Liberation Committee as "an independent entity" free from all the previous relations and allegiances as a result of the newly formed union with various Syrian Islamic militias, thereby disassociating itself from previously dissolved factions such as the al-Nusra Front.[^cite_note-226] Since officially disassociating from al-Qaeda in 2017, Tahrir al-Sham has formally established governance over many parts of North-West Syria.[^cite_note-Ioanes-227]

In November 2017, HTS launched a wide-scale crackdown on al-Qaeda elements in Idlib and arrested prominent leaders from [Af-Pak region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AfPak) and al-Nusra Front such as [Sami al-Oraydi](46871511.md). Emir [Ayman al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahiri) opposed the split of HTS from al-Qaeda, stating that it was a treasonous act done without his consent and further denounced the clampdown on foreign Jihadist fighters through an audio-statement.[^cite_note-228] Several al-Qaeda circles and supporters have also condemned Joulani and compared him to [Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_al-Baghdadi) because of the group's [conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2016_Idlib_Governorate_clashes) with other rebel groups, and have described him as an 'opportunist' as well as making claims that he is an agent of foreign powers.[^cite_note-229]

#### Conflict (since 2020)

After reaching a ceasefire deal brokered by Turkey, HTS turned its attention to destroying al-Qaeda cells and [Islamic State](9087364.md) remnants in [Idlib](2528679.md).[^cite_note-International_Crisis_Group-147] Rivalry between al-Qaeda aligned [Hurras al-Din](56718004.md) and Tahrir al-Sham had begun to escalate violently as early as December 2019. In February 2021, HTS intensified its fight against [al-Qaeda](1921.md) cells by launching a large-scale crackdown that saw many military commanders and leaders of Hurras al-Din incarcerated.[^cite_note-almcam-141] According to [Istanbul](3391396.md)-based academic Abbas Sharifeh, the measures were part of a strategy by HTS for governance consolidation: "Golani simply does not want any competitors in Idlib, especially from a jihadi current affiliated with al-Qaeda."[^cite_note-almcam-141] By 2023 Tahrir al-Sham had eliminated most of the clandestine networks of al-Qaeda and the Islamic State, disarmed the militias and established total control over Idlib.[^cite_note-International_Crisis_Group-147]

&#x20;In 2019, U.S. government alleged that Tahrir al-Sham was working with [al-Qaeda](1921.md)'s Syrian branch on a covert level, despite its self-identification as a distinct organisation.[^cite_note-eightyears-230] Some analysts assert that many of the group's senior figures, particularly Abu Jaber, held similarly extreme views.[^cite_note-al-Qaeda's_grand_plan-162][^cite_note-bbc-231] However, Tahrir al-Sham has officially denied being part of al-Qaeda and said in a statement that the group is "an independent entity and not an extension of previous organizations or factions".[^cite_note-jaber-79] In his 2021 interview to *PBS News*, [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) argued that financial co-operation with al-Qaeda was necessary to defend Syrians from the tyranny of the [Assad regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_regime), and stated that "even at that time when we were with Al Qaeda, we were against external attacks".[^cite_note-Interview-Abu_Mohammad_al-Jolani-164] Clarifying the reasons behind the termination of relations with al-Qaeda, al-Sharaa said:&#x20;

> "\[W]hen we saw that the interest of the revolution and the interest of the [people of Syria](8862873.md) was also to break up from \[the] Al Qaeda organization, we initiated this ourselves without pressure from anybody, without anybody talking to us about it or requesting anything. It was an individual, personal initiative based on what we thought was in the public interest that benefits the [Syrian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Syrian_Revolution)."[^cite_note-Interview-Abu_Mohammad_al-Jolani-164]

### Ahrar al-Sham

According to [Abdul Razzaq al-Mahdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Razzaq_al-Mahdi), who was a leading scholar in Tahrir al-Sham, the two groups do not particularly hate one another on the political or social battlefield. Certain members, however, believed that a war between the two was possible, since Ahrar al-Sham's attendance at the Astana talks labels it as a "moderate" faction, often seen as blasphemy within groups such as Tahrir al-Sham.[^cite_note-232]

In February 2018 [Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham) and the [Nour al-Din al-Zenki Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nour_al-Din_al-Zenki_Movement) merged and formed the [Syrian Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Liberation_Front) then launched an [offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlib_Governorate_clashes_\(February_2018\)) against Tahrir al-Sham seizing several villages and the city of [Maarrat al-Nu'man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maarrat_al-Nu'man).

HTS was designated a terrorist group by the United States, [European Union](9317.md), [Russia](25391.md), [Canada](5042916.md), and some other countries.[^cite_note-\:3-236] The US embassy in Syria, which had suspended operations in February 2012, confirmed in May 2017 that HTS had been [designated a terrorist organization](63578.md) in March 2017. A US State Department spokesperson further stated that a review of HTS's internal mechanisms was being conducted to analyse "the issue carefully".[^cite_note-Al-Araby-237] In May 2018, the [United States Department of State](31975.md) formally added HTS to its list of "[Foreign Terrorist Organizations](421835.md)" (FTO).[^cite_note-2017-2021.state.gov-238][^cite_note-Ioanes-227] The United States officially confirmed in June 2023 that HTS leader [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) had severed links to al-Qaeda in 2016, while sanctioning individuals known to finance HTS. These included Abu Ahmed Zakour, a member of HTS's [Majlis-ash-Shura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis-ash-Shura).[^cite_note-Y._Zelin-239]

On 7 November 2013, Canada designated its predecessor al-Nusra Front as a terrorist organization,[^cite_note-240] followed by Tahrir al-Sham itself on 23 May 2018.[^cite_note-Government_of_Canada-241] The designation was lifted on 5 November 2025.[^cite_note-242]

In August 2018, [Turkey](11125639.md) designated Tahrir al-Sham as a terrorist organization.[^cite_note-reuters.com-243] Despite this, the [Turkish army](30205.md) has pursued strategic co-operation with HTS, viewing them as allies in the fight against al-Qaeda and Islamic State remnants. Since the objective of Tahrir al-Sham was defending [Idlib](2528679.md) from the [Ba'athist regime](57134800.md) and consolidating governance, it became a party to several ceasefire agreements brokered by Turkey. Following the ceasefire deal in March 2020, HTS turned its focus to eradicating independent militias and hardline Jihadist factions in Idlib. Within a few weeks it was able to dismantle all the al-Qaeda networks and eventually succeeded in achieving undisputed control of Idlib by subduing or co-opting other rebel factions.[^cite_note-International_Crisis_Group-147]

Following the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) during the [2024 Syrian opposition offensives](78460726.md) spearheaded by Tahrir al-Sham, the [BBC News](1139893.md) reported that the UK government could remove HTS from its list of terrorist groups.[^cite_note-244] This was echoed by [Geir Otto Pedersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geir_Otto_Pedersen), the UN special envoy for Syria, who told [Financial Times](136566.md) that international powers would need to consider lifting the designation.[^cite_note-245]

Turkish Foreign Minister [Hakan Fidan](39996069.md) stated that HTS should be removed from the list of terrorist organizations and called on Western states.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247] A few days after this statement, Fidan went to Damascus and met with HTS leader [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md). Thus, Fidan became the first foreign minister to visit the transitional government established by HTS.[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249]

On 17 December 2024, the leader of [Chechnya](6095.md), [Ramzan Kadyrov](647880.md), called for the Russian government to remove the HTS from Moscow's list of banned terror groups.[^cite_note-250]

On 20 December 2024, the United States lifted a \$10-million reward it placed for the arrest of Sharaa following meetings between HTS officials and US diplomats in Damascus.[^cite_note-251]

Following a May 2025 meeting between U.S. President Trump and former HTS leader Ahmed al-Sharaa in Riyadh, in June 2025 Trump signed an executive order lifting most of the sanctions on Syria.[^cite_note-252]

On 7 July 2025, the United States revoked the [foreign terrorist organization](421835.md) designation for HTS.[^cite_note-\:6-253]

On 21 October 2025, the United Kingdom removed HTS from its list of proscribed terrorist organizations.[^cite_note-\:7-47]

On 5 December 2025, Canada removed Syria from list of foreign state supporters of terrorism and also removed Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham, from its list of terrorist entities under the Criminal Code.[^cite_note-254]

On 27 February 2026, the United Nations Security Council Committee concerning ISIL (Da’esh) and al-Qaeda removed Hay’at Tahrir al-Sham from its sanctions list, ending the asset freeze, travel ban and arms embargo previously applied to the group.[^cite_note-press.un.org-50]

On 27 February 2026, New Zealand removed Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham from its designated terrorist entities list following the United Nations decision.[^cite_note-\:1-255]

On 5 March 2026, Japan lifted asset-freezing measures and related restrictions on HTS following the United Nations decision.[^cite_note-arabnews.jp-49]

On 11 March 2026, the European Union removed Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham from its sanctions list.[^cite_note-\:8-51]

In April 2026, Indonesia removed HTS from its List of Suspected Terrorists and Terrorist Organizations (DTTOT), with the group no longer appearing as a designated entity in the revised list.[^cite_note-256]

As of the 2026 Malaysian Ministry of Home Affairs specified entities list, HTS no longer appears as a separately designated organization.[^cite_note-257]

As of August 2026, Argentina's Public Registry of Persons and Entities Linked to Acts of Terrorism and Terrorist Financing (RePET) no longer lists Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham as a designated entity.[^cite_note-258]

On 24 August 2026, the United States revoked the Specially Designated Global Terrorist (SDGT) designation of al-Nusrah Front, also known as Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham, and removed HTS from the Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons List.[^cite_note-259]

## Foreign support

### Turkey

In 2018, Turkey designated Tahrir al-Sham as a terrorist organization and al-Nusra Front in 2014. The Turkish government said it was opposed to Tahrir al-Sham (HTS), and had fought with it and declared it a terrorist organisation, and HTS's [Syrian Salvation Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government) was a direct challenge to the Turkish-backed [Syrian Interim Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Interim_Government).[^cite_note-Cling-260]

However, since 2017 there have been times where HTS, particularly its pragmatic faction around [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md), has fought alongside the [Turkish-backed Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish-backed_Free_Syrian_Army). HTS has not stopped Turkey from setting up several [observation posts in Idlib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_military_operation_in_Idlib_Governorate) and has joined joint operations rooms with Turkish-backed groups while preserving its autonomy. A 2024 peer-reviewed study by political scientist Jérôme Drevon found that HTS maintained security and military coordination with Turkey but retained substantially greater agency than factions of the [Syrian National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_National_Army), which received direct Turkish support.[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-\:4-65] The [Clingendael Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clingendael_Institute) in November 2019 has described the Turkish policy since 2018 as attempting to divide the pragmatic elements from the hardline elements within HTS.[^cite_note-Cling-260]

The head of the Turkish intelligence agency [MIT](575957.md), [İbrahim Kalın](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0brahim_Kal%C4%B1n), visited Damascus on 12 December 2024 and met with [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md), the leader of HTS. Kalın's visit was the first by a senior foreign official in Damascus since the rebel takeover.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264] Turkish Foreign Minister [Hakan Fidan](39996069.md) stated in an interview after the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md) that Turkey cooperated with HTS during his time as the head of [MIT](575957.md) and that HTS provided Turkey with information on many members of IS and Al Qaeda, including high-ranking officials.[^cite_note-265][^cite_note-266]

### European Union

In February 2025, the [European Union](9317.md) announced the suspension of select sanctions on [Syria](7515849.md), including in the energy and banking sectors, as part of an effort to support political transition and economic recovery. The decision was aimed at encouraging HTS to adopt a more inclusive governance approach and uphold human rights. However, the EU maintained key sanctions, particularly those related to [arms](608296.md), and warned that further measures could be imposed if HTS failed to meet its commitments.[^cite_note-267] Since then, the European Union has continued easing restrictions on Syria, including removing Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham from its sanctions list on 11 March 2026[^cite_note-\:8-51]

### GCC

In April 2025, [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and [Qatar](8238258.md) agreed to settle all of Syria's debts at the [World Bank](36752.md), approximately 15 million US dollars.[^cite_note-268] The move was done to enable Syria to resume access to the services of the World Bank after being suspended in 2011. The World Bank announced in May 2025 that Syria no longer had outstanding debts to the [International Development Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Development_Association) (IDA), its fund dedicated to low-income countries.[^cite_note-269]

## Human rights violations and war crimes

Despite HTS's efforts to publicly distance itself from al-Qaeda and present itself as a legitimate governing body through the Syrian Salvation Government, reports from the United Nations, United States, European Union and human rights organizations document its continued involvement in serious human rights violations and war crimes.[^cite_note-\:323-270][^cite_note-\:332-271][^cite_note-\:43-272][^cite_note-\:422-273][^cite_note-\:72-274]

### Extrajudicial executions, arbitrary arrests, and torture

HTS carried out extrajudicial killings, arbitrary arrests, and forced disappearances.[^cite_note-\:323-270][^cite_note-\:34-275] Political opponents, journalists, activists, and civilians perceived as critical of HTS are unlawfully detained.[^cite_note-\:34-275] Torture and mistreatment were widespread in detention facilities operated by HTS, with at least 22 documented methods of torture being employed by HTS, including physical and psychological abuse.[^cite_note-\:52-276] Confessions obtained under torture are admitted as evidence in HTS courts, while detainees have no legal recourse to challenge their imprisonment.[^cite_note-\:323-270][^cite_note-\:52-276][^cite_note-\:422-273] In 2019 a [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) report accused the group of torturing locals who were documenting their abuses in areas they controlled.[^cite_note-277]

### Violence against women and girls

HTS enforced strict, religiously motivated dress codes for women and girls, and significantly restricts their freedom of movement and access to education. Women are only allowed to travel when accompanied by a male relative ([mahram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahram)), with violations of religious decrees possibly leading to arrest and punishment.[^cite_note-\:323-270][^cite_note-\:52-276] A U.S. State Department report in 2023 asserted that women in HTS-controlled regions of Idlib faced widespread discrimination and violence, including arbitrary detention, sexual abuse in custody, and death sentences for charges such as "adultery" or "blasphemy".[^cite_note-\:323-270] It also alleged that women were also denied the right to file for divorce and are forbidden from wearing makeup in public and living alone.[^cite_note-\:323-270] US government agency [USCIRF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCIRF) alleged in 2022 that the education ministry of the [Syrian Salvation Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government) gave instructions to block married female students – including underage girls who were forced into [child marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_marriage) – from attending public schools and universities.[^cite_note-\:72-274]

Women activists, especially those working in humanitarian or media roles, have been particularly targeted.[^cite_note-\:323-270][^cite_note-\:43-272] Many are accused of high treason or other charges as a pretext to pressure them into ceasing activities critical of HTS.[^cite_note-\:43-272] HTS also arrested women with family ties to rival groups or opposition factions as a tactic to extract leverage or force cooperation.[^cite_note-\:43-272] Female detainees were subjected to degrading conditions, including isolation, threats, lack of medical care, and abuse.[^cite_note-\:323-270][^cite_note-\:43-272]

### Allegations of forced conversions and discrimination against religious minorities

A 2022 report issued by the [United States Commission on International Religious Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Commission_on_International_Religious_Freedom) (USCIRF) alleged that while HTS publicly claimed to be tolerant and made official overtures towards religious minorities in Idlib like Druze and Christians, it continued to commit widespread violations of religious freedom and human rights, targeting religious minorities and dissenting Sunni Muslims. According to the USCIRF report, the group forcibly converted Druze to Sunni Islam and that properties of Christians and Druze were systematically confiscated in HTS-controlled territories. Arbitrary arrests, torture, and executions under fabricated religious charges are routine in HTS-controlled territories, with detainees often subjected to abuse and denied legal recourse. The USCIRF concludes that HTS promoted an ideologically driven administration that undermines the region's religious diversity.[^cite_note-\:72-274] Since 2020, the Islamist organization has been less repressive toward religious minorities. Some churches are allowed to hold mass again, but they are prohibited from ringing bells or erecting crosses on their buildings.[^cite_note-278]

### Political repression

HTS systematically suppressed dissent through violent repression, arbitrary arrests and severe mistreatment of critics, including journalists, activists, and civilians.[^cite_note-\:323-270][^cite_note-\:34-275][^cite_note-\:422-273] Protests against HTS were rarely tolerated and often met with violent repression.[^cite_note-\:323-270][^cite_note-\:422-273] Journalists faced significant risks, including threats, imprisonment, and physical abuse, forcing many to flee the region.[^cite_note-\:323-270] HTS employed arbitrary detentions and torture to silence perceived political opponents.[^cite_note-\:323-270][^cite_note-\:34-275][^cite_note-\:422-273]

### Obstruction of humanitarian aid

According to a report issued by [US State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_State_Department) in 2023, HTS significantly obstructed humanitarian aid in areas under its control, severely hindering assistance to those in need, particularly [internally displaced persons](427408.md).[^cite_note-\:323-270] The group imposes arbitrary taxes on humanitarian shipments and interferes with the distribution of aid.[^cite_note-\:323-270] HTS regulates the flow of assistance, exerting control over the selection of beneficiaries and ensuring that aid is diverted for its own benefit.[^cite_note-\:323-270]

### Children as victims and forced participants of war

According to reports by the U.S. Department of State, HTS committed severe violations of [children's rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_rights), exploiting them in ways that contravene international law. These reports alleged that HTS used children as [human shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_shield), [suicide bombers](18835454.md), [child soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children_in_the_military), and [executioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executioner), forcing them into violent roles in the conflict.[^cite_note-\:332-271][^cite_note-\:62-279][^cite_note-\:5-280]

## Economic views

HTS has supported and advocated for [protectionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectionism)[^cite_note-auto4-281] and [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_markets) policies.[^cite_note-282] The group was pro-[tariffs](55551.md)[^cite_note-auto4-281] and pro-[price controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_controls).[^cite_note-283] HTS favored the [Turkish lira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_lira) over the [Syrian pound](456708.md) in order to reverse the economic damage of the pound's severe depreciation.[^cite_note-284] The economy of [Idlib](2528679.md) under the rule of HTS was described by [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) as a [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) model.[^cite_note-285] HTS weighted [Islamic capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_and_capitalism).[^cite_note-The_New_Arab-286] HTS's [Islamic economic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_economics) is seen as closer to [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) than [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism), but with a strong [social welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_welfare) system, some other describe it as economic system in some ways similar to the one of [Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdo%C4%9Fan).[^cite_note-The_New_Arab-286] The Islamic capitalism of HTS also got described as [pro-liberal economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberal) or even as [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism).[^cite_note-The_New_Arab-286][^cite_note-287]
