Between the [parliamentary election in October 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Iraqi_parliamentary_election) and October 2022, there was a political crisis in [Iraq](7515928.md), with members of the [Council of Representatives of Iraq](1445450.md) being unable to form a stable coalition government, or elect a new [President](209174.md).[^cite_note-\:0-4] Basic government services such as the civil service and military continued functioning, but the [national political system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Iraq) was in deadlock including in respect of almost all major spending and taxation issues.[^cite_note-5] On 27 October 2022, the government of Prime Minister [Mohammed Shia' Al Sudani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Shia'_Al_Sudani) was approved by the Council of Representatives.[^cite_note-reut-1][^cite_note-puk-2]

## Events

### 2021

[Violent clashes in Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Baghdad_clashes) following the election and the [attempted assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Mustafa_Al-Kadhimi) of Prime Minister [Mustafa Al-Kadhimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Al-Kadhimi) began the crisis.[^cite_note-6] On 18 November [Muqtada al-Sadr](351058.md) said he would like to form a [majority government](465640.md).[^cite_note-7]

### 2022

On 9 January, the newly elected parliament met for the first time in the [Green Zone](856542.md) to elect the parliament speaker and two deputies. This first parliamentary session resulted in senior interim parliament speaker [Mahmoud al-Mashahadani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_al-Mashhadani) falling ill and being taken to hospital.[^cite_note-8] [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) lawmaker and current parliament speaker [Mohamed al-Halbousi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_al-Halbousi) was re-elected for a second term,[^cite_note-9] with deputies [Shakhawan Abdulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakhawan_Abdulla) from the [Kurdistan Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party) and [Hakim al-Zamili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakim_al-Zamili) from the [Sadrist Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadrist_Movement).[^cite_note-R09012022-10] Thus, the [Kurdistan Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party), [Sadrist Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadrist_Movement) and [Progress Party](68979365.md) succeeded in filling those three positions due to the candidates of each bloc voting for each other.[^cite_note-R09012022-10] These nominations were not recognized as legitimate by the Shiite Pro-Iran factions in parliament, known as the [Coordination Framework](82383522.md) bloc, who claimed the session could not continue without the presence of the ill [al-Mashahadani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_al-Mashhadani).[^cite_note-11] Parliament was then temporarily suspended but later was able to resume again after a review by the [Iraqi Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Iraq).[^cite_note-12]

According to the [Constitution of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Iraq) a president must be selected within 30 days after the election of the parliament speaker.[^cite_note-BattleOpens-13] Incumbent [Barham Salih](1006308.md) was put forward by the [Patriotic Union of Kurdistan](70789.md) to run for a second term, while the [Kurdistan Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party) selected former foreign minister [Hoshyar Zebari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoshyar_Zebari) to run for the post as the party's second putting forward of a president to the required parliamentary vote.[^cite_note-BattleOpens-13]

Sadr amassed majority in parliament that included Kurdish [KDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party) and Sunni [Progress Party](68979365.md) and [Azem Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azem_Alliance).[^cite_note-14] The majority excluded pro-Iran [Coordination Framework](82383522.md). After that, [Supreme Court of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Supreme_Court_of_Iraq) ruled that parliament requires 2/3 quorum to elect the President, allowing Coordination Framework to block the process.[^cite_note-15] In protest, on 13 June 2022, 73 MPs from Sadr’s bloc resigned from [parliament](1445450.md).[^cite_note-16] On 23 June, the [Council of Representatives](1445450.md) swore in 73 new members in their place.[^cite_note-17] As a result, the [Coordination Framework](82383522.md) bloc, an alliance of [Iran](14653.md)-backed parties led by [Nouri al-Maliki](4723094.md), grew to 130 seats.[^cite_note-18]

On 17 July, secret recordings were leaked of [Nouri al-Maliki](4723094.md), the former Prime Minister, in which he was criticising al-Sadr. This was reported to have been controversial and a factor in deepening the crisis.[^cite_note-19]

On 25 July, the framework nominated former minister and governor of [Maysan Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maysan_Governorate) [Mohammed Shia' Al Sudani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Shia'_Al_Sudani) as prime minister.[^cite_note-20]

On 27 July, angry about the influence of Iran in Iraqi domestic governance, followers of al-Sadr [breached](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Iraq_Parliament_attack) the Green Zone and the [Iraqi Parliament](1445450.md) in Baghdad. After a public message by al-Sadr to "pray and go home", the crowd dispersed.[^cite_note-21] Thousands of supporters of Muqtada al-Sadr had been [camping in the parliament building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Iraq_parliament_attack) since July 27.[^cite_note-22] On 30 July followers of al-Sadr stormed the Iraqi parliament again.[^cite_note-23]

On 3 August, [Muqtada al-Sadr](351058.md) called for [snap elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Iraqi_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-24]

On 29 August, Sadr announced via a tweet his retirement from political life. Later that day, his supporters [stormed the presidential palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Baghdad_clashes) and armed clashes inside the Green Zone ensued, resulting in the deaths of at least 15 protesters.[^cite_note-AP-25] The Iraqi Army announced a nationwide [curfew](297681.md). Protests and clashes also erupted in [Basra](57580.md) and [Maysan Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maysan_Governorate) in southern Iraq.[^cite_note-AP-25]

On 30 August, fighting spread to [Karbala](210957.md) and escalated in Basra as demonstrators stormed the Iraqi parliamentary office in Karbala[^cite_note-26] and blocked the entrance to [Umm Qasr port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_Qasr_Port).[^cite_note-27]

On approximately 5 September the second round of negotiations ended, leaving further talks required to agree on any selection for the key ratificatory and head of state role of president and to agree a working coalition, key-issues confidence and supply arrangement between the parties or to the fresh elections the Prime Minister continues to seek.[^cite_note-28]

After more talks between the political parties, on 13 October, the Iraqi Parliament gathered once again and elected [Abdul Latif Rashid](440441.md) as the country's new president. He won after winning 230 votes in the parliament beating incumbent [Barham Salih](1006308.md). This election marked the beginning of the end of the deadlock as Rashid then tasked [Mohammed Shia' Al Sudani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Shia'_Al_Sudani) with forming the government to become prime minister.[^cite_note-29] Although offered by various figures, Sadr said he would not partake in this new government led by al-Sudani.[^cite_note-30]

On 27 October 2022, the government of Mohammed Shia' Al Sudani, from the [Coordination Framework](82383522.md), was approved by the Council of Representatives.[^cite_note-reut-1][^cite_note-puk-2] In his acceptance speech ahead of the final vote, Al Sudani stated that "[corruption](42043567.md)" had caused "many economic problems, (...) increasing poverty, unemployment, and poor public services".[^cite_note-reut-1]
