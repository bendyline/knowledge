The **Union of Democratic Forces of Guinea** (**UFDG**; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Union des forces démocratiques de Guinée*) was a [social-liberal](765430.md)[^cite_note-Freland-1] [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) in [Guinea](12176.md). It was the main opposition party of Guinea from 2010 to 2020. In 2026, the party was dissolved by the government of president [Mamady Doumbouya](68651894.md).[^cite_note-\:0-2]

## History

The UFDG was founded in 1991 by a number of opposition parties and groups. In October 2002, the party was joined by a faction of the [Union for Progress and Renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_Progress_and_Renewal) party under the leadership of [Bâ Mamadou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamadou_Boye_Bah), which unlike the majority of their party wished to boycott the [2002 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Guinean_parliamentary_election). Mamadou Ba was subsequently elected as president of UFDG. The party was affiliated with the Republican Front for Democratic Change alliance, which intended to field a candidate in the [2003 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Guinean_presidential_election).[^cite_note-3]

In 2007, [Cellou Dalein Diallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellou_Dalein_Diallo) became the new president of the party. Diallo stood for the party in the [2010 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Guinean_presidential_election), topping the poll in the first round before narrowly losing to [Alpha Condé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Cond%C3%A9) in the second round.

On 25 July 2015, Diallo was named as the UFDG's candidate for the [2015 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Guinean_presidential_election) at a party congress; he was also re-elected to lead the party for another five years.[^cite_note-4]

In 2026, the party was dissolved by the government of president [Mamady Doumbouya](68651894.md).[^cite_note-\:0-2]
