**Kalimantan** (Indonesian pronunciation: [\[kaliˈmantan\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Malay)) is the [Indonesian](14579.md) portion of the island of [Borneo](4518.md).[^cite_note-brit-2] It constitutes 73% of the island's area, and consists of the provinces of [Central Kalimantan](453643.md), [East Kalimantan](453651.md), [North Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kalimantan), [South Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kalimantan), and [West Kalimantan](453636.md). The non-Indonesian parts of Borneo are [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) and [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia). In [Indonesia](14579.md) the whole island of Borneo is also called "Kalimantan".[^cite_note-brit-2]

In 2019, President of Indonesia [Joko Widodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Widodo) proposed that [Indonesia's capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_Indonesia) be moved to Kalimantan. The [People's Consultative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Consultative_Assembly) approved the [Law on State Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_on_State_Capital) in January 2022.[^cite_note-3] The future capital, [Nusantara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusantara_\(city\)), is a [planned city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_city) that will be carved out of East Kalimantan. A government official said construction is expected to be fully complete by 2045,[^cite_note-4] but the unfinished capital officially celebrated [Indonesian Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Independence_Day) for the first time and it was scheduled to be inaugurated as the capital city on 17 August 2024,[^cite_note-Bloomberg822-5] but the move did not take place due to delays of construction.[^cite_note-6]

## Etymology

The name *Kalimantan* is derived from the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) word *Kalamanthana*, which means "burning weather island" or "very hot island", referring to its hot and humid [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical) climate. It consists of the two words *[kal\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%81la_\(time\))* ("time, season, period") and *manthan\[a]* ("boiling, churning, burning").[^cite_note-7] The native people of Indonesian Borneo referred to their island as *Pulu K'lemantan* or "Kalimantan" when the sixteenth century [Portuguese explorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_explorer) [Jorge de Menezes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_de_Menezes) made contact with them.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Due to Europeans encountering the [Bruneian Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruneian_Sultanate_\(1368%E2%80%931888\)) in the north part of the island during the [Age of Exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Exploration), the entire island has come to be called **Borneo** in English, with Kalimantan being known as **Indonesian Borneo**, but this name is not used in Indonesia itself.

In the early twentieth century, the British colonist [Charles Hose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hose) described Kalimantan as being home to a "[Klemantan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klemantan_people)", but this term is no longer in use as Kalimantan has always had many ethnic groups.

## Area



Map of Kalimantan (light colour) and its component provinces.

The Indonesian territory makes up 73% of the island by area, and 72.1% of its 2020 population of 23,053,723 (the population was 13,772,543 at the 2010 Census of Indonesia, and 16,625,796 at the 2020 Census).[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2021-10] The non-Indonesian parts of Borneo are of [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) (460,345 in 2020[^cite_note-11]) and [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia) (5,967,582 in 2020), the latter comprising the states of [Sabah](28678.md) (3,418,785) and [Sarawak](28258.md) (2,453,677), and the federal territory of [Labuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labuan) (95,120).

Kalimantan's total area is 534,698.27 square kilometres (206,448 sq mi).[^cite_note-geoh-12]

The widespread deforestation and other environmental destruction in Kalimantan and other parts of Indonesia has often been described by academics as an [ecocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecocide).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Administrative divisions

Kalimantan is now divided into five [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia). It was administered as one province between 1945 and 1956, but in 1956 it was split into three provinces – [East Kalimantan](453651.md), [South Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kalimantan) and [West Kalimantan](453636.md); then in 1957, the province of [Central Kalimantan](453643.md) was created when it was split away from the existing South Kalimantan. There remained four provinces until 25 October 2012, when [North Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kalimantan) was split off from [East Kalimantan](453651.md).
