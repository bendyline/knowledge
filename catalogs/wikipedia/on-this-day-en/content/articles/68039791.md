The **Ethiopian Army** ([Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic_language): የኢትዮጵያ ሰራዊት, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Amharic): *Ye-Ītyōṗṗyā serawīt*) is the [land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land) [service branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the [Ethiopian National Defense Force](9405.md). It is the senior of the three uniformed military branches. The force engages in [land warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_warfare) and [combined arms operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_arms), including [armored and mechanized operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_warfare) as well as [air assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_assault) operations.

## History

### 1990–1991 order of battle

Gebru Tareke listed the Ethiopian Army in 1990 as comprising four revolutionary armies organized as task forces, eleven corps, twenty-four infantry divisions, and four mountain divisions, reinforced by five mechanized divisions, two airborne divisions, and ninety-five brigades, including four mechanized brigades, three artillery brigades, four tank brigades, twelve special commandos and para commandos brigades – including the Spartakiad, which became operational in 1987 under the preparation and guidance of North Koreans – seven [BM-rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_rocket_launcher) battalions, and ten brigades of paramilitary forces.[^cite_note-4]

Forces underarms were estimated at 230,000 in early 1991.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOfcanskyBerry1993278-5] Mengistu's [People's Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Militia_\(Ethiopia\)) had also grown to about 200,000 members. The mechanized forces of the army comprised 1,200 [T-54/55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-54/55), 100 [T-62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-62) tanks, and 1,100 [armored personnel carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_personnel_carrier) (APCs), but readiness was estimated to be only about 30 percent operational, because of the withdrawal of financial support, lack of maintenance expertise and parts from the Soviet Union, Cuba, and other nations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOfcanskyBerry1993278–9-6]

### Twenty-first century structure

The [International Institute for Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies) estimated in the Military Balance 2009 that the army comprised 4 Military Regional Commands; (Northern (HQ [Mekele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekele).[^cite_note-10]), Western, Central, and Eastern) each acting as corps HQ,[^cite_note-11] there also being a Support Command and a strategic reserve of four divisions and six specialist brigades centred on Addis Ababa.

Each of the four corps comprises a headquarters, an estimated one mechanised division and between 4 and 6 infantry divisions.

In 2014 the regional commanders were listed by dissident sources as:

- Central Command, Major General [Yohannes Woldegiorgis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yohannes_Woldegiorgis?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- Northern Command, Major General [Gebrat Ayele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebrat_Ayele?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- Western Command, Major General [Birhanu Julla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birhanu_Julla?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- Eastern Command, Major General [Abraha Woldemariam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraha_Woldemariam?action=edit\&redlink=1)

  [^cite_note-12]

The modern ENDF has a wide mix of equipment. Many of its major weapons systems stem from the Communist era and are of Soviet and Eastern bloc design. The United States was Ethiopia's major [arms supplier](608296.md) from the end of the Second World War until 1977, when Ethiopia began receiving massive arms shipments from the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-13] These shipments, including armored patrol boats, transport and jet fighter aircraft, helicopters, tanks, trucks, missiles, artillery, and small arms have incurred an unserviced Ethiopian debt to the former Soviet Union estimated at more than \$3.5 billion.

Ethiopia made significant purchases of arms from Russia in late 1999 and early 2000 before the May 2000 United Nations arms embargo went into effect.[^cite_note-LOCEth2005-14] It is likely that much of that equipment suffered battle damage in the war with Eritrea. Thus, raw numbers alone will probably overstate the capacity of the ENDF.

## United Nations peacekeeping



Ethiopian Army soldiers at a ceremony marking Ethiopia joining AMISOM in Somalia.

Ethiopia has served in various [United Nations](31769.md) and [African Union](10634933.md) peacekeeping missions. These have included [Ivory Coast](57744.md),[^cite_note-Ethiopian_peacekeeping_in_Africa-15][^cite_note-Ethiopian_peacekeeping_missions-16] on the Burundi border,[^cite_note-Ethiopian_peacekeeping_in_Africa-15][^cite_note-17] and in Rwanda.

Two major previous Ethiopian missions were in Liberia and Darfur. The [United Nations Mission in Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mission_in_Liberia) (UNMIL) was established by [United Nations Security Council Resolution 1509](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1509), of 19 September 2003, to support the implementation of the ceasefire agreement and the peace process, protect United Nations staff, facilities and civilians, support humanitarian and human rights activities; as well as assist in national security reform, including national police training and formation of a new, restructured military.[^cite_note-18] In November 2007, nearly 1,800 Ethiopian troops serving with the United Nations Mission in Liberia (UNMIL) were presented with UN service medals for their "invaluable contribution to the peace process."[^cite_note-19] Up to three Ethiopian battalions used to constitute Sector 4 of the UN Mission, covering the southern part of the country. The mission ended in 2018.

Many thousands of Ethiopian peacekeepers were also involved in the hybrid [United Nations–African Union Mission in Darfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations%E2%80%93African_Union_Mission_in_Darfur) (UNAMID) in western Sudan. The Security Council authorized a force of about 26,000 uniformed personnel.[^cite_note-20] The Darfur mission was shut down in 2020–21.

Ethiopia also provided the entire force for the UN's Abyei mission, the [United Nations Interim Security Force for Abyei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Interim_Security_Force_for_Abyei), up until 2021. An Ethiopian officer commanded the force.

Many thousands of Ethiopian Army personnel were also part of the [African Union Mission in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_in_Somalia).

## Equipment

Main article: [List of equipment of the Ethiopian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_Ethiopian_Army)

The Military Balance 2012 estimated that about 450 BRDM, BMP-1, BTR-60, BTR-152, and Type 89 armoured fighting vehicles and armoured personnel carriers were in service.[^cite_note-IISS2012-22]

A total of 150 T-55 - 90 from Soviet Union, +40 from [Belarus](3457.md), +19 from [Bulgaria](3415.md), +50 from [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), +90 from [Ukraine](31750.md), and 150 T-54 (60 from [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany)) may have been in service over the years. Up to 150 M113 armoured personnel carriers may have been delivered from the United States.

16 M55 Quad quadruple anti-aircraft machine guns may have been in service from the United States. [M163 VADS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M163_VADS) self-propelled anti-aircraft guns may have been ordered but never delivered.
