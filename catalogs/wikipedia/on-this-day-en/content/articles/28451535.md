The **history of Turkey**, understood as the history of the area now forming the territory of the [Republic of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Turkey), includes the history of both [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) (the Asian part of Turkey) and [Eastern Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Thrace) (the European part of Turkey). These two previously politically distinct regions came under control of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in the second century BC, eventually becoming the core of the Roman [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire).[^cite_note-1] For times predating the Ottoman period, a distinction should also be made between the history of the [Turkic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_peoples), and the history of the territories now forming the Republic of Turkey.[^cite_note-US_Library_of_Congress-2][^cite_note-Howard-3] From the time when parts of what is now Turkey were conquered by the [Seljuq dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuq_dynasty), the history of Turkey spans the medieval history of the [Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire), the medieval to modern history of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), and the [history of the Republic of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Republic_of_Turkey) since the 1920s.[^cite_note-US_Library_of_Congress-2][^cite_note-Howard-3]

## Prehistory and ancient history

Main articles: [Prehistory of Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Anatolia) and [Prehistory of Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Southeast_Europe)

See also: [Hattians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattians), [Hittites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites), [Luwians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luwians), and [Pala (Anatolia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_\(Anatolia\))



Bronze Age Anatolia

Present-day Turkey has been inhabited by [modern humans](682482.md) since the [late Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic) period and contains some of the world's oldest [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) sites.[^cite_note-Howard_2016_24-4][^cite_note-MET-5] [Göbekli Tepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6bekli_Tepe) is close to 12,000 years old.[^cite_note-Howard_2016_24-4] Parts of [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) include the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent), an [origin of agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution).[^cite_note-6] Other important Anatolian Neolithic sites include [Çatalhöyük](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87atalh%C3%B6y%C3%BCk) and [Alaca Höyük](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaca_H%C3%B6y%C3%BCk).[^cite_note-Howard_2016-3-7] Neolithic Anatolian farmers differed genetically from farmers in [Iran](14653.md) and [Jordan Valley](12105764.md).[^cite_note-Bellwood_2022_229-8] These early Anatolian farmers also [migrated into Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_Farmers), starting around 9,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Bellwood_2022_229-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy)'s earliest layers go back to around 4500 BC.[^cite_note-Howard_2016-3-7] The [Urfa Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urfa_Man) statue is dated c. 9000 BC, to the period of the [Pre-Pottery Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Pottery_Neolithic), and is defined as "the oldest known naturalistic life-sized sculpture of a human".[^cite_note-RJC-11]

Anatolia's historical records start with [clay tablets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_tablets) from approximately around 2000 BC that were found in modern-day [Kültepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BCltepe).[^cite_note-Howard_2016-4-12] These tablets belonged to an [Assyrian trade colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karum_\(trade_post\)).[^cite_note-Howard_2016-4-12] The languages in Anatolia at that time included Hattian, Hurrian, [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_language), [Luwian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luwian_language), and [Palaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaic_language).[^cite_note-McMahon_Steadman_2012_p_234-13] [Hattian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattic_language) was a language indigenous to Anatolia, with no known modern-day connections.[^cite_note-McMahon_Steadman_2012_p_234-13][^cite_note-14] [Hurrian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurrian_language) was used in northern [Syria](7515849.md).[^cite_note-McMahon_Steadman_2012_p_234-13] Hittite, Luwian, and Palaic languages were in the [Anatolian sub-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_languages) of [Indo-European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages),[^cite_note-McMahon_Steadman_2012_p_522-15] with Hittite being the "oldest attested Indo-European language".[^cite_note-16] The origin of Indo-European languages is unknown.[^cite_note-17] [They may be native to Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_hypothesis)[^cite_note-18] or non-native.[^cite_note-19]

[Hattian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattians) rulers were gradually replaced by [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites) rulers.[^cite_note-Howard_2016-4-12] The Hittite kingdom was a large kingdom in Central Anatolia, with its capital of [Hattusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattusa).[^cite_note-Howard_2016-4-12] It co-existed in Anatolia with [Palaians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_\(Anatolia\)) and [Luwians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luwians), approximately between 1700 and 1200 BC.[^cite_note-Howard_2016-4-12] As the Hittite kingdom was disintegrating, further waves of Indo-European peoples migrated from southeastern Europe, which was followed by warfare.[^cite_note-20] The [Thracians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians) were also present in modern-day [Turkish Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Thrace).[^cite_note-21] It is not known if the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War) is based on historical events.[^cite_note-22] [Troy's Late Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age_Troy) layers matches most with *[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad)*'s story.[^cite_note-23]

Around 750 BC, [Phrygia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygia) had been established, with its two centers in [Gordium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordium) and modern-day [Kayseri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayseri).[^cite_note-Howard_2016-2-24] [Phrygians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygians) spoke an Indo-European language, but it was closer to [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), rather than Anatolian languages.[^cite_note-McMahon_Steadman_2012_p_522-15] Phrygians shared Anatolia with [Neo-Hittites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Hittite_states) and [Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu). Luwian-speakers were probably the majority in various Anatolian Neo-Hittite states.[^cite_note-25] Urartians spoke a non-Indo-European language and their capital was around [Lake Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Van).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Howard_2016-2-24] Urartu was often in conflict with [Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria),[^cite_note-27] but fell with the attacks of [Medes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes) and [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians) in seventh century BC.[^cite_note-Howard_2016-2-24] When [Cimmerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimmerians) attacked, Phrygia fell around 650 BC.[^cite_note-Howard_2016-28] They were replaced by [Carians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carians), [Lycians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycians) and [Lydians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydians).[^cite_note-Howard_2016-28] These three cultures "can be considered a reassertion of the ancient, indigenous culture of the Hattian cities of Anatolia".[^cite_note-Howard_2016-28]

## Anatolia and Thrace in classical antiquity

### Classical Anatolia

Main articles: [Classical Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Anatolia) and [History of Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Anatolia)

Further information: [Median Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_Empire), [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), [Macedonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Empire), [Ancient Regions of Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Regions_of_Anatolia), [Kingdom of Pontus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Pontus), [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire), [Byzantine Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Anatolia), and [Ancient Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Near_East)



Map showing events of the first phases of the Greco-Persian Wars

The classical history of [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) can be roughly subdivided into [the classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Anatolia) and [Hellenistic Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_Anatolia), ending with the conquest of the region by the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in the second century BC.

After the fall of the Hittites, the new states of Phrygia and Lydia stood strong on the western coast as [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) civilization began to flourish. They, and all the rest of Anatolia were relatively soon after incorporated into the [Achaemenid Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire).

As Persia grew in strength, their system of local government in Anatolia allowed many port cities to grow and to become wealthy. All of Anatolia got divided into various [satrapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrapies), ruled by [satraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satraps) (governors) appointed by the central Persian rulers. The first state that was called [Armenia](10918072.md) by neighbouring peoples was the state of the [Armenian](387816.md) [Orontid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontid_dynasty), which included parts of eastern Turkey beginning in the 6th century BC, which became the [Satrapy of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrapy_of_Armenia) under Achaemenid rule. Some of the satraps revolted periodically but did not pose a serious threat. In the 5th century BC, [Darius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_I) built the [Royal Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Road), which linked the principal city of [Susa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susa) with the west Anatolian city of [Sardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardis).[^cite_note-29]

Anatolia played a pivotal role in Achaemenid history. In the earliest 5th century BC, some of the Ionian cities under Persian rule revolted, which culminated into the [Ionian Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionian_Revolt). This revolt, after being easily suppressed by the Persian authority, laid the direct uplead for the [Greco-Persian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Persian_Wars), which turned out to be one of the most crucial wars in European history.



Regions of Roman Anatolia

Achaemenid Persian rule in Anatolia ended with the conquests of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), defeating [Darius III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_III) between 334 and 330 BC. Alexander wrested control of the whole region from Persia in successive battles. After Alexander's death, his conquests were split amongst several of his trusted generals, but were under constant threat of invasion from both the [Gauls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauls) and other powerful rulers in [Pergamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon), [Pontus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Pontus), and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ptolemaic_Egypt). The [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire), the largest of Alexander's territories, and which included Anatolia, became involved in a disastrous war with [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) culminating in the battles of [Thermopylae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Thermopylae_\(191_BC\)) and [Magnesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Magnesia). The resulting [Treaty of Apamea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Apamea) in (188 BC) saw the Seleucids retreat from Anatolia. The [Kingdom of Pergamum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attalid_dynasty) and the [Republic of Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_\(city\)), Rome's allies in the war, were granted the former Seleucid lands in Anatolia.

Roman control of Anatolia was strengthened by a 'hands off' approach by Rome, allowing local control to govern effectively and providing military protection. In the early 4th century, [Constantine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great) established a new administrative centre at [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), and by the end of the 4th century the Roman empire split into two parts, the Eastern part with Constantinople as its capital, referred to by historians as the Byzantine Empire from the original name, [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium).[^cite_note-30]

Thrace and the Thracian Odrysian kingdom in its maximum extent under Sitalces (431-424 BC)

The [Thracians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians) ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Θρᾷκες, [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Thraci*) were a group of [Indo-European tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans) inhabiting a large area in Central and Southeastern Europe.[^cite_note-31] They were bordered by the [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians) to the north, the [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) and the [Illyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrians) to the west, the [Ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks) to the south and the [Black Sea](3386.md) to the east. They spoke the [Thracian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracian_language) – a scarcely attested branch of the [Indo-European language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_language_family). The study of Thracians and Thracian culture is known as [Thracology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracology).

Starting around 1200 BC, the western coast of Anatolia was heavily settled by [Aeolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolians) and [Ionian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionians) [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece). Numerous important cities were founded by these colonists, such as [Miletus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miletus), [Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephesus), [Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna) and [Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium), the latter founded by [Greek](42056.md) colonists from [Megara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megara) in 657 BC. All of Thrace, and the native Thracian peoples were conquered by [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great) in the late 6th century BC, and were re-subjugated into the empire in 492 BC following [Mardonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardonius_\(general\))' campaign during the [First Persian invasion of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Persian_invasion_of_Greece).[^cite_note-32] The territory of Thrace later became unified by the [Odrysian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrysian_kingdom), founded by [Teres I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teres_I),[^cite_note-LewisBoardman1994-33] probably after the [Persian defeat in Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Persian_invasion_of_Greece).[^cite_note-34]

By the 5th century BC, the Thracian presence was pervasive enough to have made [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus)[^cite_note-35] call them the second-most numerous people in the part of the world known by him (after the [Indians](14533.md)), and potentially the most powerful, if not for their lack of unity. The Thracians in classical times were broken up into a large number of groups and tribes, though a number of powerful Thracian states were organized, such as the [Odrysian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrysian_kingdom) of Thrace and the [Dacian](100550.md) kingdom of [Burebista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burebista). A type of soldier of this period called the [Peltast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peltast) probably originated in Thrace.

Before the expansion of the Kingdom of [Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedon), Thrace was divided into three camps (East, Central, and West) after the withdrawal of the Persians following their eventual defeat in mainland Greece. [Cersobleptes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cersobleptes), a notable ruler of the East Thracians, attempted to expand his authority over many of the Thracian tribes but was eventually defeated by the [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Macedonians).

The Thracians were typically not city-builders. The largest [Thracian cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracian_cities) were in fact large villages[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] and the only [polis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polis) was [Seuthopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seuthopolis).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

## Byzantine Period

Main article: [Byzantine Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Anatolia)

See also: [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), [Sassanid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire), and [Byzantine-Sassanid Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine-Sassanid_Wars)



The empire in 555 under [Justinian the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_the_Great), at its greatest extent since the fall of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) (its [vassals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_state) in pink)

The Persian [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) fell to [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in 334 BC,[^cite_note-PersiansInAsiaMinor-40] which led to increasing cultural homogeneity and [Hellenization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenization) in the area.[^cite_note-SteadmanMcMahon2011-41] Following Alexander's death in 323 BC, Anatolia was subsequently divided into a number of small [Hellenistic kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_civilization), all of which became part of the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) by the mid-1st century BC.[^cite_note-AlexanderToRome-42] The process of [Hellenization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenization) that began with Alexander's conquest accelerated under Roman rule, and by the early centuries AD the local [Anatolian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_languages) and cultures had become extinct, being largely replaced by [ancient Greek language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language) and culture.[^cite_note-FreedmanMyers2000-43][^cite_note-Hout2011-44]

According to [Acts of the Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles), early Christian Church had significant growth in Anatolia because of [St Paul's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle) efforts. Letters from St. Paul in Anatolia comprise the [oldest Christian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity).[^cite_note-45] According to [extrabiblical traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Apostle#Extrabiblical_traditions), the [Assumption of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary) took place in Ephesus, where [Apostle John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Apostle) was also present. [Irenaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irenaeus) writes of "the church of Ephesus, founded by Paul, with John continuing with them until the times of [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan)."[^cite_note-46]



Originally a church, later a mosque, and now a Grand mosque, the [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia) in [Istanbul](3391396.md) was built by the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) in the 6th century.

Several [ecumenical councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_councils) of the early Church were held in cities located in present-day Turkey, including the [First Council of Nicaea](11118.md) ([Iznik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iznik)) in 325 (which resulted in the first uniform Christian [doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine), called the [Nicene Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Creed)), the [First Council of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Council_of_Constantinople) in 381, the [Council of Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ephesus) in 431, and the [Council of Chalcedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon) in 451.[^cite_note-47]

In 324, [Constantine I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I) chose Byzantium to be the new capital of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), renaming it [New Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rome). Following the death of [Theodosius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I) in 395 and the permanent division of the Roman Empire between his two sons, the city, which would popularly come to be known as [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) became the capital of the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire). This, which would later be branded by historians as the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), ruled most of the territory of what is today Turkey until the [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages),[^cite_note-48] while the other remaining territory remained in [Sassanid Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanian_Empire) hands.

Between the 3rd and 7th century AD, the Byzantines and the neighboring Sassanids [frequently clashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine-Sassanid_Wars) over possession of Anatolia, which significantly exhausted both empires, thus laying the way open for the eventual Muslim conquests from both empires' respective south.

The borders of the empire fluctuated through several cycles of decline and recovery. During the reign of [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) (r. 527–565), the empire reached its greatest extent after the fall of the west, re-conquering much of the historically Roman western [Mediterranean coast](19006.md), including [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exarchate_of_Africa), [Italy](14532.md) and [Rome](25458.md), which it held for two more centuries. The [Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Sasanian_War_of_602%E2%80%93628) exhausted the empire's resources, and during the [early Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests) of the 7th century, it lost its richest provinces, [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Egypt) and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Syria), to the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate). It then lost Africa to the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_dynasty) in 698, before the empire was rescued by the [Isaurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire_under_the_Isaurian_dynasty) dynasty.

The fall of Constantinople to the Ottoman Empire in 1453 marked the end of the Byzantine Empire. Refugees fleeing the city after its capture would settle in Italy and other parts of Europe, helping to ignite the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance). The [Empire of Trebizond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Trebizond) was conquered eight years later when its eponymous capital surrendered to Ottoman forces after it was [besieged in 1461](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Trebizond_\(1461\)). The last Byzantine [rump state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rump_state), the [Principality of Theodoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Theodoro), was conquered by the Ottomans in 1475.

Historians generally agree that the first Turkic people lived in a region extending from [Central Asia](6742.md) to [Siberia](29639.md). Historically they were established after the 6th century BC.[^cite_note-Peter_Zieme_2006,_p._64-49] The earliest separate Turkic peoples appeared on the peripheries of the late [Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiongnu) confederation about 200 BC[^cite_note-Peter_Zieme_2006,_p._64-49] (contemporaneous with the Chinese [Han Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Dynasty)).[^cite_note-Findley_2005,_p._29-50] The first mention of Turks was in a [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China#Imperial_China) text that mentioned trade of Turk tribes with the [Sogdians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sogdians) along the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road).[^cite_note-51]

It has often been suggested that the Xiongnu, mentioned in Han Dynasty records, were [Proto-Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Turkic_language) speakers.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

The [Hun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hun) hordes of [Attila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila), who invaded and conquered much of Europe in the 5th century AD, may have been Turkic and descendants of the Xiongnu.[^cite_note-Findley_2005,_p._29-50][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-pulleyblank-58] Some scholars argue that the Huns were one of the earlier Turkic tribes, while others argue that they were of [Mongolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Mongols) origin.[^cite_note-59]

In the 6th century, 400 years after the [collapse of northern Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han%E2%80%93Xiongnu_War) power in [Inner Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Asia), leadership of the Turkic peoples was taken over by the Göktürks. Formerly in the Xiongnu nomadic confederation, the Göktürks inherited their traditions and administrative experience. From 552 to 745, Göktürk leadership united the [nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) Turkic tribes into the [Göktürk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6kt%C3%BCrk_Empire). The name derives from *gok*, "blue" or "celestial". Unlike its Xiongnu predecessor, the Göktürk Khanate had its temporary *[khans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_\(title\))* from the [Ashina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashina_tribe) clan that were *subordinate* to a [sovereign](28791.md) authority controlled by a council of tribal chiefs. The [Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate) retained elements of its original [shamanistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamanism) religion, [Tengriism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengriism), although it received missionaries of [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) monks and practiced a syncretic religion. The Göktürks were the first Turkic people to write [Old Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Turkic) in a runic script, the [Orkhon script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orkhon_script). The Khanate was also the first state known as "Turk". Towards the end of the century, the Göktürks Khanate was split in two; i.e., [Eastern Turkic Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Turkic_Khaganate) and [Western Turkic Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Turkic_Khaganate). The [Tang Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_Empire) conquered the Eastern Turkic Khaganate in 630 and the Western Turkic Khaganate in 657 in a series of military campaigns. However, in 681 the khanate was revived. The Göktürks eventually collapsed due to a series of dynastic conflicts, but the name "Turk" was later taken by many states and peoples.&#x20;

Turkic peoples and related groups migrated west from [Turkestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkestan) and what is now [Mongolia](19271.md) towards [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), [Iranian plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_plateau) and Anatolia and modern Turkey in many waves. The date of the initial expansion remains unknown. After many battles, they established their own state and later created the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). The main migration occurred in medieval times, when they spread across most of Asia and into Europe and the Middle East.[^cite_note-Carter_V._Findley-60] They also participated in the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades).

## Seljuk Empire

Main article: [Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire)

See also: [Anatolian Beyliks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_Beyliks)

The [Seljuq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuq_dynasty) Turkmens created a [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) empire that controlled a vast area stretching from the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) to eastern Anatolia and from [Central Asia](6742.md) to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). From their homelands near the [Aral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aral_Sea), the Seljuqs advanced first into [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan) and then into mainland [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) before eventually conquering eastern Anatolia. [^cite_note-61]

The Seljuq/Seljuk empire was founded by [Tughril Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughril_Beg) (1016–1063) in 1037. Tughril was raised by his grandfather, [Seljuk-Beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuq_Dynasty) Seljuk gave his name to both the Seljuk empire and the Seljuk dynasty. The Seljuqs united the fractured political scene of the eastern [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world) and played a key role in the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade) and [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Crusade) crusades. Highly [Persianized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persianized) in culture and language, the Seljuqs also played an important role in the development of the [Turko-Persian tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turko-Persian_tradition), even exporting Persian culture to Anatolia.[^cite_note-62] A dynasty from Seljuks, the [Seljuks of Rum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuks_of_Rum), became the ruling power in Anatolia. After Mongol invasion of Anatolia, Seljuks of Rum collapsed,[^cite_note-63] paving the way for the [Anatolian Beyliks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_Beyliks).

## Ottoman Empire

Main article: [History of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Ottoman_Empire)



The Ottoman Empire in [1683](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vienna), at the start of the [Great Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Turkish_War) (1683–1699)

The [Ottoman beylik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_beylik)'s first capital was located in [Bursa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursa,_Turkey) in 1326. Edirne which was conquered in 1361[^cite_note-64] was the next capital city. After largely expanding to Europe and Anatolia, in 1453, the Ottomans nearly completed the conquest of the Byzantine Empire by capturing its capital, Constantinople during the reign of [Mehmed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_II). Constantinople was made the capital city of the Empire following Edirne. The Ottoman Empire would continue to expand into the Eastern Anatolia, Central Europe, the Caucasus, North and East Africa, the islands in the Mediterranean, Greater Syria, Mesopotamia, and the Arabian peninsula in the 15th, 16th and 17th centuries.

The Ottoman Empire's power and prestige peaked in the 16th and 17th centuries, particularly during the reign of [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent). The empire was often at odds with the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in its steady advance towards [Central Europe](5188.md) through the Balkans and the southern part of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth).[^cite_note-Ottoman_Turkey-65] In addition, the Ottomans were often at war with [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) over territorial disputes, which allowed them to inherit the [Timurid Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Renaissance). At sea, the empire contended with the Holy Leagues, composed of [Habsburg Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Spain), the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) and the [Knights of St. John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_of_St._John), for control of the [Mediterranean](19006.md). In the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), the Ottoman navy frequently confronted [Portuguese](23033.md) fleets in order to defend its traditional monopoly over the maritime trade routes between [East Asia](19605700.md) and [Western Europe](33800.md); these routes faced new competition with the Portuguese discovery of the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope) in 1488. The Ottomans even had influence in [Southeast Asia](28741.md) as the Ottomans sent soldiers to their most distant vassal, the [Sultanate of Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Aceh)[^cite_note-66] at Sumatra in Indonesia. Their forces in Aceh were opposed by the Portuguese that had crossed the Atlantic and Indian Oceans invaded the [Sultanate of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Malacca) and the Spaniards who had crossed from Latin America and invaded [formerly Muslim Manila in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines-Turkey_relations), as these Iberian powers waged a world war against the Ottoman Caliphate known as the [Ottoman–Habsburg wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Habsburg_wars).



[Mehmed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_II) enters Constantinople by [Fausto Zonaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fausto_Zonaro)

The Treaty of Karlowitz in 1699 marked the beginning of Ottoman territorial retreat; some territories were lost by the treaty: Austria received all of Hungary and Transylvania except the Banat; Venice obtained most of Dalmatia along with the Morea (the Peloponnesus peninsula in southern Greece); Poland recovered Podolia.[^cite_note-67] Throughout the 19th and early 20th centuries, the Ottoman Empire continued losing its territories, including [Greece](12108.md), [Algeria](358.md), [Tunisia](30188.md), [Libya](17633.md) and the [Balkans](4829.md) in the 1912–1913 [Balkan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Wars). Anatolia remained multi-ethnic until the early 20th century. Its inhabitants were of varied ethnicities, including [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people), [Armenians](387816.md), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people), [Kurds](17068.md), [Greeks](42056.md), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), and [Italians](385155.md) (particularly from [Genoa](47332321.md) and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice)). Following the loss of its outer territories and the expulsion of Muslims from former Ottoman Europe, [Ottomanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottomanism) pluralist ideas fell out of favor, replaced by anti-Christian sentiment.[^cite_note-suny-68] Following a coup led by the [Committee of Union and Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress), the Ottoman state pursued policies of [Turkification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkification),[^cite_note-bjornlund-69] including arbitrary violence against Greeks and Armenians in the Ottoman Empire.[^cite_note-Kaligian-70]



The sultan of the golden age, [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent).

Faced with territorial losses on all sides the Ottoman Empire under the rule of the [Three Pashas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Pashas) forged an alliance with Germany who supported it with troops and equipment. The Ottoman Empire entered [World War I](4764461.md) (1914–1918) on the side of the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers) and was ultimately defeated.[^cite_note-SchallerZimmerer-71] The Ottomans successfully defended the [Dardanelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardanelles) strait during the [Gallipoli campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallipoli_campaign) and achieved initial victories against British forces in the first two years of the [Mesopotamian campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_campaign), such as the [Siege of Kut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Kut); but the [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt) turned the tide against the Ottomans in the Middle East. In the [Caucasus campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_campaign), however, the Russian forces had the upper hand from the beginning, especially after the [Battle of Sarikamish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sarikamish). In the wake of this defeat, which War Minister [Enver Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enver_Pasha) blamed on Armenians siding with Russia, and with Ottoman military units already carrying out massacres against Armenian villages,[^cite_note-ungor-72] the CUP adopted a policy of eliminating Armenians[^cite_note-suny-68] in what is now broadly recognized by scholars as the [Armenian genocide](140376.md).[^cite_note-suny2-73]



Visit of [Kaiser Wilhelm II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_II) of Germany to Turkey in October 1917. Together with the [Mehmed V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_V) and [Enver Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enver_Pasha)

Russian forces advanced into northeastern Anatolia and controlled the major cities there until retreating from World War I with the [Treaty of Brest-Litovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk) following the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution). Following World War I, the huge conglomeration of territories and peoples that formerly comprised the Ottoman Empire was divided into [several new states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitioning_of_the_Ottoman_Empire).[^cite_note-74]

On October 30, 1918, the [Armistice of Mudros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros) was signed, followed by the imposition of [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) on August 10, 1920, by [Allied Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I), which was never ratified. The [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) would break up the Ottoman Empire and force large concessions on territories of the Empire in favour of [Greece](12108.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Britain](31717.md) and France.

## Republic of Turkey

Main article: [History of the Republic of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Republic_of_Turkey)



[Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) on the cover of the *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazine, Vol. I No. 4, March 24, 1923. Title: "Mustapha Kemal Pasha"

The occupation of some parts of the country by the Allies in the aftermath of World War I prompted the establishment of the [Turkish National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement).[^cite_note-Ottoman_Turkey-65] The [Turkish Provisional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Grand_National_Assembly) in [Ankara](802.md), which had declared itself the legitimate government of the country on [23 April 1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey), started to formalize the legal transition from the old Ottoman into the new Republican political system. The Ankara Government engaged in armed and diplomatic struggle. In 1921–1923, the Armenian, Greek, French, and British armies had been expelled:[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-18sep1922-78] The military advance and diplomatic success of the Ankara Government resulted in the signing of the [Armistice of Mudanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudanya) on 11 October 1922. The handling of the [Chanak Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanak_Crisis) (September–October 1922) between the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) and the Ankara Government caused the collapse of [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George)'s [Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_George_ministry) on 19 October 1922[^cite_note-79] and political autonomy of [Canada](5042916.md) from the UK.[^cite_note-80] On 1 November 1922, the Turkish Parliament in Ankara formally [abolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_Ottoman_sultanate) the [sultanate](22278.md), thus ending 623 years of [monarchical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy) Ottoman rule.

The [Treaty of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne) of 24 July 1923, which superseded the Treaty of Sèvres,[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-Atatürk-82] led to the international recognition of the sovereignty of the new Turkish state as the successor state of the Ottoman Empire. On 4 October 1923, the Allied occupation of Turkey ended with the withdrawal of the last Allied troops from [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Istanbul). The Turkish Republic was officially proclaimed on 29 October 1923 in Ankara, the country's new capital.[^cite_note-83] The [Lausanne Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_Concerning_the_Exchange_of_Greek_and_Turkish_Populations) stipulated a [population exchange between Greece and Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_exchange_between_Greece_and_Turkey).[^cite_note-Clogg-84]

Mustafa Kemal became the republic's first [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Turkey) and introduced [many reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk's_reforms). The reforms aimed to transform the old [religion-based and multi-communal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet_\(Ottoman_Empire\)) Ottoman monarchy into a Turkish [nation state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state) that would be governed as a [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic) under a [secular constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Constitution_of_1924).[^cite_note-BoweringCrone2012-85] The [fez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fez_\(hat\)) was banned, full rights for women politically were established, and new [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_alphabet) for [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) based upon the [Latin script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_script) was created.[^cite_note-86] Among the other things, economic privileges for foreigners were abolished and their means of production and railways were nationalised. Foreign schools were placed under state control. The [abolition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_Caliphate) of the [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Caliphate) followed on 3 March 1924. In the same year, Turkey abolished [sharia](28840.md) and in 1925, a clothing reform for men (the [Hat Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat_Revolution)) was enacted.

In the following years, entire legal systems were adopted from European countries and adapted to Turkish conditions. In 1926, Swiss civil law—and thus [monogamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogamy) with the equality of men and women—was adopted first (gender equality was only partially achieved in everyday life, however), so [polygamy](24475.md) was banned. This was followed by German commercial law and Italian criminal law. The Islamic [sectarian lodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zawiya_\(institution\)) in 1925 and the [Masonic lodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_lodge)[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] in 1935 were banned. The high [taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashar) imposed on farmers were reduced. In 1926, the [Arabic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar) was replaced by the [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar) and the [metric system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system) was introduced. In 1927, [co-education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-sex_education) were introduced. The law on industrial incentives was passed (1927) and the first five-year plan for industry came into force (1934). Secularisation was proclaimed in 1928. An [educational mobilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation's_school) was initiated to literate the rural population.

With the [Surname Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname_Law_\(Turkey\)) of 1934, the [Turkish Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey) bestowed upon Kemal the honorific surname "Atatürk" (*Father Turk*).[^cite_note-Atatürk-82] [Atatürk's reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk's_reforms) caused discontent in some [Kurdish](17068.md) and [Zaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazas) tribes leading to the [Sheikh Said rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Said_rebellion) in 1925[^cite_note-90] and the [Dersim rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dersim_rebellion) in 1937.[^cite_note-91]



[Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), [İnönü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0smet_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC) and [Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) at the [Second Cairo Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Cairo_Conference) which was held between 4–6 December 1943.

Turkey was neutral in World War II (1939–45) but signed a treaty with Britain in October 1939 that said Britain would defend Turkey if Germany attacked it. An invasion was threatened in 1941 but did not happen and Ankara refused German requests to allow troops to cross its borders into Syria or the USSR. Germany had been its largest trading partner before the war, and Turkey continued to do business with both sides. It purchased arms from both sides. The Allies tried to stop German purchases of chrome (used in making better steel). Starting in 1942 the Allies provided military aid. The Turkish leaders conferred with Roosevelt and Churchill at the [Cairo Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Conference) in November, 1943, and promised to enter the war. By August 1944, with Germany nearing defeat, Turkey broke off relations. In February 1945, it declared war on Germany and Japan, a symbolic move that allowed Turkey to join the nascent United Nations.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

Meanwhile, relations with Moscow worsened, setting stage for the start of the Cold War. The demands by the Soviet Union for military bases in the [Turkish Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Straits), prompted the United States to declare the [Truman Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Doctrine) in 1947. The doctrine enunciated American intentions to guarantee the security of Turkey and Greece, and resulted in large-scale [U.S. military and economic support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey-United_States_relations).[^cite_note-Truman_Doctrine-94]

After participating with the United Nations forces in the [Korean War](16772.md), Turkey joined [NATO](21133.md) in 1952, becoming a bulwark against Soviet expansion into the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin). Following a decade of [intercommunal violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypriot_intercommunal_violence) on the island of [Cyprus](5593.md) and the [Greek military coup of July 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EOKA-B), overthrowing President [Makarios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makarios_III) and installing [Nikos Sampson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikos_Sampson) as a dictator, Turkey [invaded the Republic of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Cyprus) in 1974. Nine years later the [Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Republic_of_Northern_Cyprus) (TRNC) was established. Turkey is the only country that recognises the TRNC[^cite_note-95]

The [one-party period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-party_period_of_the_Republic_of_Turkey) was followed by [multi-party democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_period_of_the_Republic_of_Turkey) after 1945. The Turkish democracy was interrupted by military [coups d'état](25914154.md) in [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-TRPoliticsandMilitary-96] In 1984, the [PKK](69680.md) began an insurgency against the Turkish government; the conflict, which has claimed over 40,000 lives, continues today.[^cite_note-97] Since the liberalization of the Turkish economy during the 1980s, the country has enjoyed stronger economic growth and greater political stability.[^cite_note-80sLiberalization-98]

The 90s saw a coalition government ruled by the first and only female prime minister, [Tansu Çiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tansu_%C3%87iller), and economic problems coupled with [devaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devaluation) and [inflation](38286.md). In March 1995, twenty-three people were killed and hundreds were injured in the incidents called [Gazi Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazi_Quarter_riots) in Istanbul. The events began with an armed attack on several coffee shops in the neighborhood, where an [Alevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alevism) religious leader was killed. Protests occurred both in [Gazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaziosmanpa%C5%9Fa) and [Ümraniye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cmraniye) district on the Asian side of İstanbul. Police responded with gunfire.[^cite_note-99]



[Istanbul LGBT Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_Pride) 2013 at [Taksim Square](631961.md) during [Gezi Park protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezi_Park_protests)

The year of 2002 marked a turning point, with [Justice and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Development_Party_\(Turkey\)) winning the [2002 Turkish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Turkish_general_election). The AKP party positioned itself as pro-liberal market economy, supporting [Turkish membership in the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Turkey_to_the_European_Union),[^cite_note-vote2007-100] though two decades later they would turn to the opposite direction. In late 2000s, concrete steps taken to phase out the [Headscarf controversy in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headscarf_controversy_in_Turkey). Accession negotiations between the EU and Turkey began in October 2005.[^cite_note-101] In 2013, countrywide [Gezi Park protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezi_Park_protests) took place.In August 2014, Turkish Prime Minister [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) won Turkey's first direct presidential [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Turkish_presidential_election).[^cite_note-102]

In July 2016, the Turkish [attempted coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) took place. A number of rogue government units took over and were only repelled after a few hours.[^cite_note-103] In December 2016, an off duty policeman Mevlut Altintas shot dead the Russian Ambassador inside an Art Gallery. He refused to surrender and was then shot dead by special police.[^cite_note-104] In April 2017, the constitutional amendments, which significantly increased the powers of Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, were narrowly accepted in the [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Turkish_constitutional_referendum).[^cite_note-105] In June 2018, President Erdoğan was re-elected for a new five-year term in the first round of the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Turkish_presidential_election). His [Justice and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Development_Party_\(Turkey\)) (AK Party) secured a majority in the separate [parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Turkish_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-106] In October 2018, Prince MBS of Saudi Arabia sent a group of government agents to murder prominent critic, [Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi), in the Saudi Arabian consulate in Istanbul. [His death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Jamal_Khashoggi) was just a few days before his sixtieth birthday.[^cite_note-107] In May 2022, Turkey blocked the start of [NATO accession negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO#Finland_and_Sweden) for [Finland](10577.md) and [Sweden](5058739.md).[^cite_note-108] In June 2022, Turkey decided to support the accession negotiations of the two countries.[^cite_note-109]

In July 2022, the Turkish government asked the international community to recognise Turkey by its Turkish name Türkiye, in part because of the [homonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homonym), [turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_\(bird\)) (bird), for the name of the country in the English language.[^cite_note-110] On 6 February 2023, [2023 Turkey–Syria earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Turkey%E2%80%93Syria_earthquakes) took place, which resulted in massive casualties. In May 2023, President Erdoğan won a new re-election and his AK Party with its allies held parliamentary majority in the [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Turkish_general_election).[^cite_note-111] As of May 2023, approximately 96,000 Ukrainian [refugees of the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) have sought refuge in Turkey.[^cite_note-112] In 2022, nearly 100 000 Russian citizens migrated to Turkey, becoming the first in the list of foreigners who moved to Turkey, meaning an increase of more than 218% from 2021.[^cite_note-113] As of August 2023, the number of [refugees of the Syrian civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_Syrian_civil_war) in Turkey was estimated to be 3.307.882 people. The number of Syrians had decreased by 205 894 people since the beginning of the year.[^cite_note-114] In March 2024, the opposition [Republican People's Party](404748.md) (CHP) gained a significicant victory in [local election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Turkish_local_elections), including mayoral victories in Turkey's five largest cities: Istanbul, Ankara, Izmir, Bursa, and Antalya.[^cite_note-115] [2025 Turkish protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Turkish_protests) began throughout Turkey on 19 March 2025 following [the detention and arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrest_of_Ekrem_%C4%B0mamo%C4%9Flu) of [Istanbul mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Istanbul) and the opposition's president candidate [Ekrem İmamoğlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekrem_%C4%B0mamo%C4%9Flu) and more than 100 other opposition members and protesters.
