The **Myanmar–Thailand border** is the international border between the territory of [Myanmar](19457.md) (formerly *Burma*) and [Thailand](30128.md). The border is 1,501 mi (1,304 nmi; 2,416 km) in length and runs from the [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) with [Laos](17752.md) in the north to the [Andaman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Sea) coast in the south.[^cite_note-1]

## Description

The border starts in the north at the tripoint with Laos at the confluence of the [Kok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kok_River) and [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong) rivers and, after briefly following the Kok and then the [Sai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_River_\(Thailand\)), then proceeds overland to the west via a series of irregular lines through the [Daen Lao Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daen_Lao_Range), before turning to the south-west and joining the [Salween River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salween_River). The border follows the Salween and then [Moei River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moei_River) southwards, before leaving it and proceeding southwards overland through the [Dawna Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawna_Range) and [Bilauktaung Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilauktaung) (collectively these mountains form the [Tenasserim Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenasserim_Hills)). The border continues southwards down the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula), almost cutting Thailand in two at [Prachuap Khiri Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prachuap_Khiri_Khan), before reaching the [Isthmus of Kra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kra_Isthmus) and the [Kraburi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraburi_River), which it then follows out via a wide estuary to the Andaman Sea. The maritime boundary then follows coordinates marked by the countries' continental shelf delimitation agreement towards the tripoint with India, which controls the [Andaman and Nicobar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_and_Nicobar_Islands).

## History



Map of the areas briefly annexed to Thailand during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War)

The border regions have historically been contested between the various Thai and Burmese states.[^cite_note-IBS-2] [Fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese%E2%80%93Siamese_wars) between Burma and the Siamese [Ayutthaya Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_Kingdom) over control of what is now Myanmar's [Tanintharyi Region](15162240.md) dominated the area in the 16th century, before Burma destroyed Ayutthaya and took control of the region in 1767.[^cite_note-IBS-2] Soon after, Siam extended suzerainty over the [Lanna Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Na) (now [Northern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thailand)), which had previously been under Burmese control. Burmese expansion in the 19th century brought it into conflict with the British in [India](14533.md), sparking a series of [conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Burmese_Wars) which ended with Burma being occupied and [incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma) into [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India).[^cite_note-IBS-2] With [France](5843419.md) occupying [French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina) in the same period, the two European states allowed the [Kingdom of Siam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattanakosin_Kingdom_\(1782%E2%80%931932\)) (the old name for Thailand) to retain its independence as a [buffer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_state).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]



Orange shows Trans-Salween territories relinquished by [Siam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattanakosin_Kingdom_\(1782%E2%80%931932\)) to [British Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma) in 1892, defining northern portions of Myanmar-Thailand border.

In 1868, Britain and Siam signed a treaty delimiting the Burma-Thai border from the Salween south to the Andaman Sea.[^cite_note-IBS-2] Following some confusion as to the border's location, on the ground demarcation of the border commenced from 1889 to 1892.[^cite_note-IBS-2] In 1892 the northern section of the border delimited and then demarcated on the ground from 1893 to 1894, with a final boundary treaty with maps signed on 17 October 1894.[^cite_note-IBS-2] The northern terminus of the border was fixed in 1896 when the British and French agreed that the Mekong would serve as the Burma–Laos border.[^cite_note-IBS-2] Some minor boundary adjustments occurred in 1929 and 1934.[^cite_note-IBS-2] In 1941, following [Japan](15573.md)'s [invasion of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Burma), parts of Burma were ceded to Siam as the [Saharat Thai Doem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharat_Thai_Doem) territory. These areas were returned to Burma in 1946 following Japan's defeat and since then the border has remained in place.[^cite_note-IBS-2][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] In 1937 Burma was separated from India and became a separate colony, gaining full independence in 1948, at which point the border became an international frontier between two sovereign states.[^cite_note-IBS-2]

At present, there is an outstanding dispute over the ownership of some small islands in the Andaman Sea.[^cite_note-BP-20200113-7]

From 2010 to 2012 there were [clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%932012_Myanmar_border_clashes) along the border between the Myanmar army and the [Karen National Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_National_Liberation_Army).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In 2021, the Myanmar military clashed with civilians in the border areas. Many of them fled and ended up in Thailand.[^cite_note-10]

In 2023 there were more clashes and the governments of both countries decided to form a humanitarian task force to facilitate assistance for people displaced by the fighting.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### Checkpoints for border trade

These border crossings are open for cross-border local trade only. There are 13 checkpoints for border trade officially recognized by the Ministry of Interior, located in [Chiang Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Rai_province), [Chiang Mai](211630.md), [Mae Hong Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Hong_Son_province) and [Ranong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranong_province) provinces. Entering the opposite country beyond these checkpoints and their associated markets is illegal.

### Special checkpoint for border trade

One special checkpoint for border trade is planned as a future permanent crossing. Entering the opposite country beyond these checkpoints and their associated markets is currently illegal.
