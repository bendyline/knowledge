***Leopardus*** is a [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) of [small cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felinae) native to the [Americas](29833.md).[^cite_note-catsg-3] This genus is considered the oldest branch of a [genetic lineage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_lineage) of small cats in the Americas whose [common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ancestor) crossed the [Bering land bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_land_bridge) from Asia to North America in the [late Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Miocene).[^cite_note-Johnson2006-4]

## Characteristics

*Leopardus* [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) have spotted [fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur), with ground colors ranging from pale [buff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buff_\(colour\)), [ochre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochre), [fulvous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulvous) and [tawny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawny_\(color\)) to light gray.[^cite_note-5] Their small [ears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ears) are rounded and white-spotted; their [rhinarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinarium) is prominent and naked above, and their [nostrils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostril) are widely separated.[^cite_note-6] They have 36 [chromosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome), whereas other felids have 38.[^cite_note-Trigo_al2008-7]

## Taxonomy

The [generic name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_name_\(biology\)) *Leopardus* was proposed by [John Edward Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Edward_Gray) in 1842, when he described two spotted cat skins from Central America and two from [India](14533.md) in the collection of the [Natural History Museum, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_History_Museum,_London).[^cite_note-8] Several genera were proposed in the 19th and early 20th centuries for small spotted cats in the Americas, including:

- *Dendrailurus*, *Lynchailurus*, *Noctifelis*, *Oncifelis* and *Oncoïdes* by [Nikolai Severtzov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Severtzov) in 1858;

  [^cite_note-9]

- *Margay*, *Pajeros*, *Pardalina* and *Pardalis* by Gray in 1867;

  [^cite_note-10]

- *Oncilla* by [Joel Asaph Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Asaph_Allen) in 1919;

  [^cite_note-11]

- *Oreailurus* by [Ángel Cabrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Cabrera_\(naturalist\)) in 1940;

  [^cite_note-12]

- *Colocolo* by [Reginald Innes Pocock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Innes_Pocock) in 1941.

  [^cite_note-13]

Analysis of skull [morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_\(biology\)) of these [taxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxa) revealed close similarities in their [base of skulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_of_skull) and [nasal bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_bone), their [masticatory muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masticatory_muscles), and dentition.[^cite_note-14] [Phylogenetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetic) analysis of tissue samples of these taxa and their ability to [hybridise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_\(biology\)) support the notion that they are members of the same genus.[^cite_note-Johnson2006-4][^cite_note-Trigo_al2008-7] The following eight extant *Leopardus* species have been recognized as [valid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valid_name_\(zoology\)) taxa by the [IUCN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_for_Conservation_of_Nature) since 2017:[^cite_note-catsg-3][^cite_note-\:1-15]

&#x20;\
&#x20;In 2017 and 2021, results of genetic analyses revealed a third tigrina-like species, *[Leopardus emiliae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_emiliae)*.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

A 2021 analysis of 142 pampas cat museum specimens collected across South America showed significant morphological differences between them. Therefore, it was proposed to split the historically contentious pampas cat [species complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_complex) into five species: *Leopardus colocolo*, *[Leopardus braccatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_braccatus)*, *[Leopardus garleppi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_garleppi)*, *Leopardus munoai*, and *[Leopardus pajeros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_pajeros)*.[^cite_note-33] Later that same year, it was noted that the oldest available name for pampas cats of the Uruguayan savannah region was *[Leopardus fasciatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_fasciatus)*, not *L. munoai*.[^cite_note-34]

Another new species, *[Leopardus narinensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_narinensis)* was described in 2023, based on a single dried skin collected in 1989 on the [Galeras Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galeras_Volcano) in the Nariño Department of Colombia. It differs from all other *Leopardus* species both morphologically and genetically.[^cite_note-narinensis-35]

In 2024, a detailed analysis of both the morphology and genetics of specimens assigned to *Leopardus tigrinus*, *Leopardus guttulus*, and *Leopardus emiliae* suggested *L. t. pardinoides* should be elevated to species status as *Leopardus pardinoides* due to significant differences in morphology, genetics, and ecology. The subspecies *L. t. oncilla* was assigned to be a subspecies of *L. pardinoides* as *L. p. oncilla*. Additionally, genetic analysis suggested that *L. emiliae* was not genetically distinct from *L. tigrinus*, and thus may be invalid. The study recommended the common names savannah tiger-cat for *L. tigrinus*, Atlantic Forest tiger-cat for *L. guttulus*, and clouded tiger-cat for *L. pardinoides*.[^cite_note-36]

In 2026, a cat found in Bolivia was described as a new species, *[Leopardus tilcayo](84257539.md)*.[^cite_note-Lescroart_al2026-37] The IUCN has yet to formally recognize this species.[^cite_note-\:1-15]

An expanded list of *Leopardus* species would be:[^cite_note-mdd-Leopardus-38]

- *Leopardus braccatus*, the [Pantanal cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantanal_cat) or Brazilian pampas cat

- *Leopardus colocola*, the colocolo or Central Chilean pampas cat

- *Leopardus emiliae*, the eastern tigrina or Snethlage's tigrina

- *Leopardus fasciatus*, Muñoa's pampas cat or Uruguayan pampas cat

- *Leopardus garleppi*, the northern pampas cat or Garlepp's pampas cat

- *Leopardus geoffroyi*, Geoffroy's cat

- *Leopardus guigna*, the kodkod

- *Leopardus guttulus*, the southern tigrina or Atlantic Forest tiger-cat

- *Leopardus jacobita*, the Andean mountain cat

- *Leopardus pajeros*, the southern pampas cat

- *Leopardus pardalis*, the ocelot

- *Leopardus pardinoides*, the [clouded tiger cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clouded_tiger_cat)

- *Leopardus tigrinus*, the oncilla, northern tigrina, or savannah tiger-cat

- *Leopardus weidii*, the margay

- *Leopardus narinensis*, the Nariño cat, Galeras cat, or red tigrina

- *Leopardus tilcayo*, the tilcayo

### Phylogeny

Phylogenetic analysis of the [nuclear DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_DNA) in tissue samples from all Felidae species revealed that the [evolutionary radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_radiation) of the Felidae began in Asia in the [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene) around [14.45 to 8.38](https://geoltime.github.io/?Ma=14.45%E2%80%938.38) million years ago.[^cite_note-Johnson2006-4] Analysis of [mitochondrial DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_DNA) of all Felidae species indicates a radiation at around [16.76 to 6.46](https://geoltime.github.io/?Ma=16.76%E2%80%936.46) million years ago.[^cite_note-Li_al2016-39]

The last [common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ancestor) of *Leopardus*, *Puma* and *Lynx* is estimated to have lived [10.95 to 6.3](https://geoltime.github.io/?Ma=10.95%E2%80%936.30) million years ago, based on analysis of nuclear DNA of cat species.[^cite_note-Johnson2006-4] Analysis of their mitochondrial DNA indicates that their last common ancestor lived [14.04 to 6.83](https://geoltime.github.io/?Ma=14.04%E2%80%936.83) million years ago.[^cite_note-Li_al2016-39] *Leopardus* forms an [evolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) [lineage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineage_\(evolution\)) that [genetically diverged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_divergence) between [4.25 to 2.02](https://geoltime.github.io/?Ma=4.25%E2%80%932.02) million years ago[^cite_note-Johnson2006-4] and [5.19 to 0.98](https://geoltime.github.io/?Ma=5.19%E2%80%930.98) million years ago.[^cite_note-Li_al2016-39] It crossed the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama) probably during the [Great American Biotic Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_American_Biotic_Interchange) in the [late Pliocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pliocene).[^cite_note-Johnson2006-4] *[Leopardus vorohuensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_vorohuensis)* is an extinct species of the genus, of which [fossils](10958.md) were found in the Argentinian [Vorohué Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorohu%C3%A9_Formation) dated to the [early Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Pleistocene); its [supraorbital foramen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supraorbital_foramen) and shape of teeth resemble those of the pampas cat.[^cite_note-Berta1983-1]

Within the genus, three distinct [clades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade) were identified: one comprising the ocelot and the margay, a second the Andean mountain cat and Pampas cat, and the third the kodkod, oncilla and Geoffroy's cat.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] The following [cladogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cladogram) shows estimated [divergence times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergence_times) in million years ago (mya).

More recent studies have indicated splitting the oncillas (tigrinas) into multiple species, and that they are paraphyletic to the Geoffroy’s cat and guigna (kodkod). The Andean cat *L. jacobi* was found to be related to the margay. The general grouping of three clades continues to be retrieved from analyses. Lescroart et al. (2023) proposed three subgenera: *Leopardus* (ocelot, margay, and Andean cat, type *L. pardalis*), *Lynchailurus* (Pampas cats, type *L. colocola*), and *Oncifelis* (tigrinas, guigna, and Geoffroy’s, type *L. geoffroyi*).[^cite_note-42]
