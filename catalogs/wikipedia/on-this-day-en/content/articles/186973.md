**USS *Nimitz* (CVN-68)** is an [aircraft carrier](2219.md) of the [United States Navy](20518076.md), and the [lead ship](855210.md) of [her class](181160.md). One of the largest [warships](263960.md) in the world, she was laid down, launched, and commissioned as **CVAN-68**, "aircraft carrier, attack, [nuclear powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_propulsion)", but she was later [redesignated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_classification_symbol) as **CVN-68**, "aircraft carrier, [multi-mission](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/multimission), nuclear-powered", on 30 June 1975, as part of a [fleet-wide realignment that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_1975_ship_reclassification).

The ship was named after [World War II](32927.md) Pacific fleet commander [Chester W. Nimitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_W._Nimitz), USN, (1885–1966), who was the Navy's third [fleet admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Admiral_\(United_States\)). She is the only *Nimitz*-class carrier whose official name is just the surname of the person for whom she is named. *Nimitz* had her homeport at [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md) until 1987, when she was relocated to [Naval Station Bremerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Bremerton) in [Washington](13015878.md) (now part of [Naval Base Kitsap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_Kitsap)). Following her [refueling and complex overhaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refueling_and_complex_overhaul) in 2001, her home port was changed to [Naval Air Station North Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_North_Island) in [San Diego County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County,_California). The home port of *Nimitz* was again moved to [Naval Station Everett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Everett) in Washington in 2012.

In January 2015, *Nimitz* changed home port from Everett back to [Naval Base Kitsap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_Kitsap).[^cite_note-6] With the inactivation of [USS *Enterprise*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Enterprise_\(CVN-65\)) in 2012 and decommissioning in 2017, *Nimitz* is now the oldest serving aircraft carrier in the world. On 10 March 2026, she began what was supposed to be her final voyage,[^cite_note-7] but on 14 March 2026, the Navy extended her active service through March 2027, to maintain the 11-carrier mandate.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

## Construction



Catherine Nimitz Lay christened *Nimitz* on 13 May 1972.

*Nimitz* was authorized by the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) in [fiscal year](5752099.md) 1967 and [Newport News Shipbuilding and Dry Dock Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News_Shipbuilding_and_Dry_Dock_Co.) in [Newport News, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News,_Virginia), was awarded the \$106.5 million contract (equivalent to \$1028.33 million today). Her [keel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keel) was [laid down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laid_down) on 22 June 1968. The vessel was christened on 13 May 1972, by Catherine Nimitz Lay, the daughter of the late [Admiral Nimitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Nimitz), six years after his death. *Nimitz* was delivered to the Navy in 1975, and was [commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_commissioning) at [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md) on 3 May 1975, by the 38th President of the United States, [Gerald R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford).[^cite_note-DANFS-9]

## Service history

### 1970s



*Nimitz* on her first deployment in 1976 alongside nuclear-powered cruisers [*California*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_California_\(CGN-36\)) and [*South Carolina*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_South_Carolina_\(CGN-37\))



*Nimitz* (*right*) alongside [HMS *Ark Royal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Ark_Royal_\(R09\)) at Norfolk Naval Station in August 1978

USS *Nimitz* first deployed to the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) on 7 July 1976, with [Carrier Air Wing 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Air_Wing_8) embarked in company with the nuclear-powered [cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser) [USS *South Carolina*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_South_Carolina_\(CGN-37\)) and [USS *California*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_California_\(CGN-36\)). In November 1976, *Nimitz* was awarded the [Battle "E"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_%22E%22) from [Commander, Naval Air Force U.S. Atlantic Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander,_Naval_Air_Force_U.S._Atlantic_Fleet), for being the most efficient and foremost aircraft carrier in the Atlantic Fleet. The cruise was uneventful, and the carrier returned to [Norfolk, Virginia](57898.md), on 7 February 1977.

A second uneventful Mediterranean cruise was conducted from 1 December 1977 to 20 July 1978. The third deployment began on 10 September 1979, to the Mediterranean. The ship moved to the Indian Ocean in response to the [Iran hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_hostage_crisis) in which the U.S. Embassy in [Tehran](57654.md), [Iran](14653.md), was overtaken and 52 hostages were held. Prior to this trip, the ship took part in the shooting of the 1980 film *[The Final Countdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Final_Countdown_\(film\))*, whose story was specifically set aboard the *Nimitz*. After four months on station, [Operation Evening Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Evening_Light) was launched from *Nimitz*'s decks in an attempt to rescue the U.S. Embassy staff. The mission was aborted after a helicopter crashed at a refueling point in the Iranian desert. The ship returned home 26 May 1980, having spent 144 days at sea.

### 1980s

On 26 May 1981, a [Marine Corps](17349325.md) [EA-6B Prowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA-6B_Prowler) from [Marine Tactical Electronic Warfare Squadron 2 (VMAQ-&#x32;**)**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VMAQ-2) assigned to [Carrier Air Wing 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Air_Wing_8) (CVW-8) crashed on the flight deck, killing 14 crewmen and injuring 45 others.[^cite_note-time-10][^cite_note-11] The Prowler was fuel-critical after a "bolter" (missed approach), and its crash and the subsequent fire and explosions destroyed or damaged 19 other aircraft.[^cite_note-USS_Nimitz_crash-12][^cite_note-13] Autopsies showed later that the pilot had had six times the normal level of the sedating antihistamine medication [brompheniramine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brompheniramine) in his blood.[^cite_note-14] Despite having no connection to the accident, the media focused on the autopsy results of several members of the *Nimitz*'s enlisted flight deck crew who were killed and who [tested positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_test) for [marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marijuana). An article by Robert Reinhold in the 17 June 1981 edition of *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported, "Experts at the National Institute on Drug Abuse say that it would probably be impossible to establish conclusively that any of the *Nimitz* crew had been smoking marijuana on the night of the crash because the test does not directly detect the component of marijuana smoke that acts on the brain. Because the metabolites may persist in the blood for many days, the test may detect marijuana that was used many days earlier, long after the effects have worn off".[^cite_note-15] As a result, President [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) instituted a ["Zero Tolerance" drug policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_Tolerance_drug_policy) across all of the U.S. armed services, which started the mandatory drug testing of all U.S. military personnel.[^cite_note-16]



Wreck of an EA-6B Prowler after it crashed during a night landing, 1981

*Nimitz* deployed again to the Mediterranean on 3 August 1981. The ship, in company with [USS *Forrestal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Forrestal), conducted a [Freedom of Navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Navigation) exercise in international waters in the [Gulf of Sidra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Sidra) near [Libya](17633.md) on 18 and 19 August 1981. On the morning of 19 August 1981, two [Grumman F-14 Tomcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_F-14_Tomcat) of [VF-41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VFA-41) were engaged by two Libyan [Su-22s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-22), resulting in the two Libyan aircraft being shot down in what became known as the [Gulf of Sidra incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Sidra_incident_\(1981\)).

*Nimitz*'s fourth deployment, from 10 November 1982 to 20 May 1983, was to the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) and the Mediterranean Sea. *Nimitz* deployed for a fifth time on 8 March 1985. On 14 June 1985, two Lebanese gunmen hijacked [TWA Flight 847](512123.md), which carried 153 passengers and crew and included Americans. In response, *Nimitz* was deployed to the coast of [Lebanon](17771.md), where the ship remained until August 1985.[^cite_note-17] The ship returned to Norfolk on 4 October 1985.

*Nimitz*, again with CVW-8 embarked, departed Norfolk for the sixth and final Mediterranean deployment on 30 December 1986. After four months and numerous Mediterranean port visits, the carrier crossed the equator en route to [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md). From Rio de Janeiro, she proceeded south around [Cape Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Horn) and into the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). After a brief stop in [San Diego](28504.md), to offload the East Coast air wing, *Nimitz* arrived at her new home port of [Bremerton, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremerton,_Washington), on 2 July 1987.

*Nimitz* deployed to the Western Pacific with [Carrier Air Wing 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Air_Wing_9) embarked on 2 September 1988. During the [1988 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics) in [Seoul](19159283.md), *Nimitz* provided security off the coast of [South Korea](27019.md), then in October, operated in the [North Arabian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Arabian_Sea), participating in [Operation Earnest Will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Earnest_Will), the protection of reflagged [Kuwaiti](7515890.md) tankers. On 30 November 1988, while in the Arabian Sea, a 20 mm cannon accidentally fired during maintenance, striking a [KA-6 Intruder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KA-6_Intruder). The ensuing fire spread to six other aircraft, and two sailors were killed. *Nimitz* returned to Bremerton on 2 March 1989.

### 1990s

On 25 February 1991, *Nimitz* departed Bremerton for the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) in relief of [USS *Ranger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Ranger_\(CV-61\)) in the aftermath of [Operation Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Storm), returning to Bremerton on 24 August 1991. *Nimitz* again deployed to the Persian Gulf on 1 February 1993, in support of [Operation Southern Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Southern_Watch), returning on 1 August 1993.



*Nimitz* (*left*) cruising with [*Independence*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Independence_\(CV-62\)) and [*Port Royal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Port_Royal_\(CG-73\)) in the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan) in September 1997

On 27 November 1995, *Nimitz* deployed to the Western Pacific, Indian Ocean, and Persian Gulf with Carrier Air Wing Nine (CVW-9). In March 1996, the ship patrolled the waters off [Taiwan](25734.md) amid [missile tests conducted by the Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Taiwan_Strait_Crisis) in the area, becoming the first American warship to pass through the [Taiwan Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Strait) since 1976. *Nimitz* also cruised the Persian Gulf in support of Southern Watch prior to returning from deployment on 20 May 1996.

Between 14 and 24 July 1997, *Nimitz* participated in Joint Task Force Exercise 97-2 (JTFEX 97–2) off the coast of Southern California, which also served as a "Revolution in Strike Warfare" demonstration. The latter event was designed to demonstrate the capability of an aircraft carrier and an embarked air wing to project carrier-based airpower into littoral warfare.[^cite_note-DANFS-9] On 20 July 1997, *Nimitz* and CVW-9 began a high-intensity strike campaign. When flight operations were completed four days later, *Nimitz* and CVW-9 had carried out 771 strike sorties while dropping 1,337 bombs on target. CVW-9 flew 975 fixed-wing sorties during this four-day surge operation. Almost 80% of the sorties flown were strike sorties, with strike support accounting for another 10%. [F/A-18 Hornet strike fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18) flew nearly 80% of the strike sorties. Of the 771 strike sorties, 727 were loaded with ordnance, while 44 were electronic support by [EA-6B Prowlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA-6B_Prowler). During this four-day period, only a portion of the medium-range interdiction strikes required tanking support. [KC-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-135) and [KC-130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-130) tanker aircraft provided most of this support. [S-3 Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-3_Viking) conducted recovery tanking and supplied more than one-third of the fuel passed to CVW-9 aircraft during this surge operation.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-USNIblog08272009-19] This surge had been preceded by a 16-hour preparation after undergoing four days that had generated about 700 fixed-winged sorties.[^cite_note-USNIblog08272009-19][^cite_note-20] A following study by the [Center for Naval Analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Naval_Analyses) determined that *Nimitz* and CVW-9 could have maintained this high-sortie operational tempo for another 12 to 24 hours before requiring equipment maintenance, with rest for the crews while ordnance and aviation fuel stocks were replenished.[^cite_note-21]

On 1 September 1997, *Nimitz* began an around-the-world cruise, again supporting Southern Watch, which ended in [Newport News, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News,_Virginia), on 2 March 1998. She spent the next three years undergoing a nuclear refueling and complex overhaul that ended on 25 June 2001.

### 2000s



Monitor showing data about *Nimitz*

On 21 September 2001,[^cite_note-UScarriers-22] after sea trials in the Virginia Capes, *Nimitz* began to transit around South America to her new home port of [NAS North Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_North_Island) in [San Diego](28504.md), [California](5407.md), arriving there on 13 November 2001. Aircraft from [Carrier Air Reserve Wing 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_Support_Wing) were embarked for the transit. From January to May 2002, a four-month post-shakedown maintenance availability was completed at North Island; during this time, an [advanced combat direction system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_combat_direction_system) was installed.



*Nimitz* with the ships [*Bridge*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USNS_Bridge) and [*Princeton*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Princeton_\(CG-59\)) during Operation Iraqi Freedom, 15 April 2003

*Nimitz*'s 11th operational deployment began on 3 March 2003.[^cite_note-23] The group relieved [USS *Abraham Lincoln*](199106.md) in the Persian Gulf in mid-April 2003, launching [Carrier Air Wing 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Air_Wing_11) aircraft sorties over Iraq in support of [Operation Iraqi Freedom](201936.md) (OIF) and [Afghanistan](737.md) in support of [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom). She returned to San Diego on 5 November 2003. *Nimitz* and CVW-11 were awarded the 2003 Battle "E"[^cite_note-24] and [Flatley Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatley_Award) in early 2004.[^cite_note-25]

In November 2004, *Nimitz* was contacted by [USS *Princeton*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Princeton_\(CG-59\)), which was tracking reported [unidentified flying objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidentified_flying_objects). *Princeton* subsequently contacted two Navy F/A-18F fighters from *Nimitz*, whose cockpit instrumentation recorded data and imagery that some pilots interpreted as [an object accelerating and maneuvering at extraordinary speeds](64109058.md). The incident was publicized in December 2017 along with details of the [Advanced Aviation Threat Identification Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Aviation_Threat_Identification_Program).[^cite_note-NYT-20171216-26]

*Nimitz*, again with CVW-11 embarked, deployed to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) on 7 May 2005, returning on 8 November 2005.[^cite_note-27] This deployment marked three decades of service, and was depicted in the [Emmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy) Award-winning 2008 [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) documentary series *[Carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_\(documentary\))*.[^cite_note-28] In June 2006, *Nimitz* was awarded the 2005 Battle "E".[^cite_note-29]



Lt. Cmdr. David Bynum, a Navy chaplain aboard *Nimitz*, passed out happy face sponge balls to the students of CSI High School for the Deaf in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), India during a community relations visit in July 2007.

The carrier departed North Island for her 13th deployment on 2 April 2007, to the Arabian Sea, relieving [USS *Dwight D. Eisenhower*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Dwight_D._Eisenhower) in support of OIF.[^cite_note-30] The carrier anchored off [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), India on 2 July 2007, as part of efforts to expand bilateral defense cooperation between India and the United States.[^cite_note-31] Sailors participated in community work in Chennai prior to departing, on 5 July 2007, along with the destroyer [USS *Pinckney*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Pinckney) towards the Persian Gulf, and then returned to North Island on 30 September 2007.[^cite_note-32]

On 24 January 2008, *Nimitz* deployed to the Pacific for a "surge"-deployment.[^cite_note-33] On 9 February 2008, two Russian [Tu-95 Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-95) bombers overflew the carrier in the Western Pacific.[^cite_note-msnbc-34] Four F/A-18C Hornets were launched when the bombers were 500 miles (800 km) away from the US ships, and intercepted the bombers 50 miles (80 km) south of *Nimitz*. Two F/A-18s trailed one of the bombers, which twice flew over the deck of the carrier at an altitude of 2,000 feet (610 m), while the other two F/A-18s trailed another Tu-95 circling about 50 miles (80 km) away from the carrier. Reportedly, no radio communication occurred between the American and Russian aircraft. According to the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense), one of the two aircraft was said to have flown above *Nimitz* at an altitude of 2,000 feet (610 m). On the same day, Russian aircraft entered Japanese airspace, which caused the Japanese to raise protest to the Russian ambassador in Tokyo.[^cite_note-35]

Again, on 5 March 2008, a Russian bomber came within 3 to 5 nautical miles (6 to 9 km) and flew 2,000 feet (610 m) above *Nimitz*, and the battle group. Two F/A-18 fighters intercepted the Russian aircraft and escorted it out of the area.[^cite_note-msnbc-34]

*Nimitz* was awarded the Navy Battle "E" for battle efficiency for 2007 along with the Ney award for food service excellence, and returned to her home port of San Diego on 3 June 2008.

The *Nimitz* Strike Group, including CVW-11, departed the US for a scheduled Western Pacific deployment on 31 July 2009,[^cite_note-36] and began to fly combat missions in support of Operation Enduring Freedom on 21 September.[^cite_note-37]

### 2010s

In January 2010, while in the Persian Gulf, the ship was awarded the [Meritorious Unit Commendation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritorious_Unit_Commendation) for back-to-back deployments in support of the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan in 2007 and 2008. The award was presented by Admiral [Gary Roughead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Roughead) in a ceremony on the ship on 6 January 2010.[^cite_note-38]



*Nimitz* sails in formation with [*Chosin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Chosin), [*Sampson*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Sampson_\(DDG-102\)), [*Pinckney*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Pinckney), and [*Rentz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Rentz), 15 February 2010.

*Nimitz* visited [Hong Kong](13404.md) for five days in February 2010 to allow the crew to rest and visit the city. The visit occurred despite China previously preventing a visit by the carrier [USS *Kitty Hawk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Kitty_Hawk_\(CV-63\)).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

On 9 December 2010, the Navy formally announced that [Everett, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everett,_Washington), was to be the new home port for *Nimitz*.[^cite_note-NNS101209-21-41] This move was expected to save the Navy \$100 million.[^cite_note-42] On 9 March 2012, *Nimitz* arrived at her new homeport of [Naval Station Everett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Everett) after spending nearly a week at sea conducting postoverhaul sea trials.[^cite_note-43]



An [MV-22 Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell-Boeing_V-22_Osprey) of [VMM-165](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VMM-165) lands on *Nimitz* in October 2012

In March 2012, *Nimitz* arrived at the new home port of Naval Station Everett in Washington after more than a year of maintenance work in Bremerton, replacing sister carrier *Abraham Lincoln*.[^cite_note-44] On 3 August 2012, *Nimitz* departed from Pearl Harbor after a two-day port call, arriving at NAS North Island on 9 August 2012 to begin [Fleet Replacement Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Replacement_Squadron) carrier qualifications.[^cite_note-UScarriers-22] On 6 October 2012, a [Bell Boeing V-22 Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Boeing_V-22_Osprey) tilt-rotor aircraft from squadron [VMM-165](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VMM-165) landed and refueled on board *Nimitz*. This operation was part of an evaluation of the feasibility of the MV-22 as a potential replacement for the [C-2 Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-2_Greyhound) [carrier onboard delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_onboard_delivery)  cargo transport aircraft.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]



Inflatable boat approaches *Nimitz* during a search-and-rescue drill, 7 September 2013.

The BBC reported that *Nimitz* was located in the Persian Gulf, ready to contribute to an operation against Syria when President Obama ordered a military strike. Two days later, it reported that the carrier task group had been rerouted westwards across the Arabian Sea.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

*Nimitz*, after eight months at sea, reportedly transited the Suez Canal on 20 October 2013, into the U.S. 6th Fleet area of responsibility, where the Navy intended to keep her for a few weeks, conducting joint training with allied nations before returning home.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] *Nimitz* returned to Everett on 16 December 2013.[^cite_note-51]

In late 2014, following the completion of work-up qualifications, *Nimitz* participated in her first deployment, a two-week multinational fleet exercise involving the [Third Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Fleet), as well as ships from the [Royal Canadian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Navy) and [JMSDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JMSDF).[^cite_note-52] Following the conclusion of the exercise on 3 November, the first [F-35C Lightning II](11812.md) to land on an aircraft carrier recovered aboard *Nimitz* to begin a two-week Development Testing I deployment. This had a pair of aircraft from [VX-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VX-23) undertaking carrier operations of launch, recovery, and handling aboard ship in both day and night conditions.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The initial deployment was completed on 14 November 2014.[^cite_note-55] In 2015, *Nimitz* transferred to Bremerton to undergo a 16-month maintenance cycle.[^cite_note-56]

On 1 June 2017, *Nimitz* left [Naval Base Kitsap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_Kitsap) for her next scheduled deployment.[^cite_note-57] This deployment was against [ISIS](9087364.md) in Iraq and Syria. Her F/A-18s played an important role in the [Battle of Tal Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tal_Afar_\(2017\)), providing precision air support for advancing Iraqi soldiers.[^cite_note-58]

On 1 March 2018, *Nimitz* entered [dry dock](213739.md) at [Puget Sound Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound_Naval_Shipyard) for 10 months of overhaul.[^cite_note-59]

### 2020s



*Nimitz* enters [Apra Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apra_Harbor), Guam, in June 2020

In April 2020, a [coronavirus](201983.md) (likely [COVID-19](63030231.md)) was reported to have spread to *Nimitz* when the first case was reported on 7 April. One sailor had received a positive result the previous week after exhibiting symptoms, and was subsequently placed in isolation and removed from the ship. Another crew member also tested positive, but was reported to have not been working on the ship.[^cite_note-politico-60][^cite_note-nimitz.military-61] On 27 April, *Nimitz* completed a 27-day quarantine and began [COMPTUEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COMPTUEX) training.[^cite_note-62]

On 5 July 2020, the ship was deployed in the [South China Sea](74209.md) along with [USS *Ronald Reagan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Ronald_Reagan). On 31 December, acting [Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_Defense) [Chris Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_C._Miller) ordered *Nimitz* to return directly to her home port following a nearly 10-month deployment in the Fifth Fleet area of operation.[^cite_note-63] The carrier was at the time supporting the withdrawal of U.S. troops in Somalia along with [USS *Makin Island*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Makin_Island_\(LHD-8\)) and her [amphibious ready group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_ready_group).[^cite_note-64] On 3 January 2021, in an abrupt reversal, acting Defense Secretary Miller ordered *Nimitz* to redeploy due to "Recent threats issued by Iranian leaders against President Trump and other U.S. government officials."[^cite_note-65]

In May 2022, *Nimitz* led [Carrier Strike Group 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_11) in the [Eastern Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Pacific_Ocean).[^cite_note-66] On 28 November 2022, *Nimitz* departed [Kitsap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_Kitsap) and sailed to [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_San_Diego) to pick up the carrier strike group leadership.[^cite_note-67] She left San Diego on 3 December for her Pacific deployment leading Carrier Strike Group 11 and embarking [Carrier Air Wing 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Air_Wing_17).[^cite_note-68] *Nimitz* logged her 350,000th arrested landing on 22 April 2023.[^cite_note-69] *Nimitz* returned from her deployment on 2 July 2023.



Flight deck of *Nimitz* in June 2025

*Nimitz* departed for her final global deployment on 21 March 2025. After the events of [Operation Rising Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rising_Lion) and [Operation True Promise 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_True_Promise_3), *Nimitz* and her [accompanying strike group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_11) were en route to the Middle East.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

On 17 June 2025 in response to the [Israel-Iran conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Iran%E2%80%93Israel_conflict), *Nimitz* moved south from the South China Sea through the Singapore Strait and the Malacca Strait before entering the Indian Ocean, likely heading for the Middle East to bolster US defenses in the region.[^cite_note-72] On 26 October, in separate incidents, an [MH-60R Seahawk](202031.md) helicopter and an [F/A-18F Super Hornet](845012.md) fighter jet operating from the aircraft carrier crashed in the South China Sea.[^cite_note-73] These two incidents occurred 30 minutes apart. Both crews were safely recovered. The cause of the crashes is still unknown and under investigation.[^cite_note-74] *Nimitz* returned from her final deployment on 16 December 2025.

On 10 March 2026, USS *Nimitz* departed [Naval Base Kitsap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_Kitsap) to prepare for her decommissioning at [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md) in [Virginia](32432.md). She is sailing from the Pacific Ocean to the Atlantic Ocean via [Cape Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Horn) at the tip of South America, after which she will begin heading toward Norfolk. This journey is expected to take about two to three weeks to complete. During her final voyage, she will also take part in the upcoming [Southern Seas 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Seas_2026?action=edit\&redlink=1) exercises. Though this voyage was intended to be her last, on March 14, the Navy extended her active service through March 2027, to maintain the 11-carrier mandate, while [USS *John F. Kennedy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_John_F._Kennedy_\(CVN-79\)) is being readied for active service.[^cite_note-\:0-8]



Argentine navy destroyer [ARA Sarandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARA_Sarand%C3%AD_\(D-13\)) steams alongside the carrier *Nimitz* during a bilateral maritime training in the southwestern [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) on April 29, 2026.

On 3 May 2026, *Nimitz* marked 51 years since its commissioning into the U.S. Navy, capping a week in which it steamed around the tip of [South America](26769.md) and held [joint exercises](1225714.md) with [Argentina’s navy](490409.md).[^cite_note-75]

On 20 May 2026, *Nimitz* along with her Carrier Strike Group, had arrived in the Caribbean to support [Operation Southern Spear](80987786.md#Cuban_crisis_and_further_buildup) as tensions with Cuba rise.[^cite_note-76]

On Friday July 3rd 2026 *Nimitz* arrived in the New York harbor ahead of 4th of July International Naval Review 250. *Nimitz* departed New York and arrived at her new home port at Norfolk on July 9th, 2026 .[^cite_note-77]

## Planned retirement

The *Nimitz*-class carriers have a lifespan around 50 years. Estimates on decommissioning for *Nimitz* herself were updated in April 2022, with the Navy Press Corps indicating, "USS *Nimitz* (CVN 68) was planned to be removed from the battle force in fiscal year (FY) 2025, when the Ship Terminal Off-load Program (STOP) begins, with inactivation scheduled to begin in 2027."[^cite_note-78]

In March 2025, the Navy announced that *Nimitz* would deploy one last time, then arrive at her new homeport of Norfolk Naval Station in Virginia no later than 12 April 2026. At that point, she will begin the one-year STOP process before shifting to Newport News Shipbuilding for initial defueling and deactivation.[^cite_note-79]

In March 2026, the Navy had announced that it had decided to prolong the service of Nimitz to March 2027 wanting to maintain its 11 carrier fleet while waiting for the commissioning of the second [*Ford*-class carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier), the [*John F. Kennedy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_John_F._Kennedy_\(CVN-79\)) which is expected to arrive in March of the same year.[^cite_note-80]

## In popular culture

*[The Final Countdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Final_Countdown_\(film\))*, a 1980 alternate history science fiction film about a contemporary aircraft carrier that travels through time to the day before the 1941 [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor), was set and filmed on board the real-life USS *Nimitz*.[^cite_note-NYTCanby-81]

*Nimitz* is the focus of *The Big Aircraft Carrier* in Little Mammoth Media's *BIG Adventure Series*. In it, they talk how the navy ship works for children including a complete tour of the carrier, how the sailors and pilots work and even all the training they undertake.[^cite_note-82]

The [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) series *[Carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_\(TV_series\))* followed the May–November 2005 deployment of *Nimitz* to the Persian Gulf, documenting the life and shipboard routines of the crew over ten episodes.[^cite_note-83]
