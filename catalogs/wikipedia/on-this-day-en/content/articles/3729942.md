**Danilovgrad** ([Montenegrin Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrin_Cyrillic_alphabet): Даниловград) is a town in central [Montenegro](20760.md). It has a population of 6,852, according to the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Montenegro). It is situated in the [Danilovgrad Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danilovgrad_Municipality) which lies along the main route between Montenegro's two largest cities, [Podgorica](172136.md) and [Nikšić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nik%C5%A1i%C4%87). Via villages, Danilovgrad forms part of a conurbation with Podgorica.

The town of Danilovgrad is located in the [Bjelopavlići](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjelopavli%C4%87i) plain, a fertile valley of the [Zeta River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_River). It is the centre of the Danilovgrad [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Montenegro), which has a population of 18,472.

In the surroundings of Danilovgrad, there are remains of Gradina (Martinići), dating back to the time of the [Serbian](59512.md) ruler [Petar Gojniković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petar_Gojnikovi%C4%87), from the [Vlastimirović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Vlastimirovi%C4%87) dynasty. The court was built by another member of the dynasty, prince [Mutimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutimir), who was also once buried in there. Gradina is also a prominent seat from the [Nemanjić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Nemanji%C4%87) period, believed to be the birthplace of [Rastko Nemanjić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sava), also known as Saint Sava, the founder of the [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church).

Danilovgrad was founded with the purpose of being the capital of Montenegro. Foundations for this [planned city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_town) were first laid by [King Nikola I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_I_of_Montenegro) in 1870. However, after the cities of [Nikšić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nik%C5%A1i%C4%87) and [Podgorica](172136.md) were liberated from Ottoman hands, during the [Congress of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Berlin), its significance diminished. It was named after Nicholas' predecessor, [Prince Danilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danilo_I,_Prince_of_Montenegro).

### World War II

On May 31, 1944, a [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF) [Consolidated B-24 Liberator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_B-24_Liberator) crashed in Danilovgrad.[^cite_note-JB-1] Although its entire crew of 10 ejected and survived, they were later caught and became [prisoners of war](25008.md).[^cite_note-JB-1] On July 23, 1944, at least 48 members of the [League of Communist Youth of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Communist_Youth_of_Yugoslavia) were killed in a mass execution by [Chetniks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetniks) in the village of Lazine.[^cite_note-Vijenac-2][^cite_note-ArhivZ-3]

### During the Yugoslav Wars

From 14 to 15 April 1995, a [pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom) drove out the [Romani population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) in one of Danilovgrad's neighborhoods, Božova Glavica.[^cite_note-FHP-4]

On 24 March 1999, the Milovan Šaranović barracks in Danilovgrad were [bombed by NATO aircraft](322473.md), killing a [Yugoslav Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Serbia_and_Montenegro) soldier named Saša Stojić. He was the first victim of the NATO bombing of Yugoslavia.[^cite_note-DW-5]

## Climate

Like many parts of Montenegro, Danilovgrad has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa* according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)) with cool winters and hot, drier summers. On 8 August 2012, Danilovgrad recorded a temperature of 44.8 °C (112.6 °F), which is the highest temperature to have ever been recorded in Montenegro.[^cite_note-JeffMasters2012HeatRecords-6]

## Sports

The local football team is [FK Iskra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Iskra_Danilovgrad), who have been playing in the country's [top tier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrin_First_League) since 2015. They host their games at the [Braća Velašević Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bra%C4%87a_Vela%C5%A1evi%C4%87_Stadium). The town's basketball team is [KK Danilovgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Danilovgrad) and [RK Danilovgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RK_Danilovgrad) is the handball club.

## Transport

Danilovgrad is situated approximately halfway between two largest Montenegrin cities, [Podgorica](172136.md) and Nikšić, on the main road that connects these two. It is also served by the [Nikšić–Podgorica railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nik%C5%A1i%C4%87%E2%80%93Podgorica_railway).

[Podgorica Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podgorica_Airport) is 30 km (19 mi) away, and has regular flights to [Belgrade](55904.md), [Budapest](36787.md), [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari), [Zagreb](55906.md), [Skopje](29618.md), [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich), [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main), [Ljubljana](42374.md), [Paris](22989.md), [Rome](25458.md) and [Vienna](55866.md).
