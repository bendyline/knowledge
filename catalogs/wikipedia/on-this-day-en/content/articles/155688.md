**Christie's** is a British [auction house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction_house) founded in 1766 by [James Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Christie_\(auctioneer\)). Its main premises are on [King Street, St James's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Street,_St_James's) in London, with additional salerooms in New York, Paris, Hong Kong, Milan, Geneva, Shanghai and Dubai.[^cite_note-Christie's_Locations-3] It is owned by [Groupe Artémis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupe_Art%C3%A9mis), the holding company of [François Pinault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Pinault).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] In 2022, Christie's sold US\$8.4 billion in art and luxury goods, an all-time high for any auction house.[^cite_note-All-Time_High-6] On 15 November 2017, the *[Salvator Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvator_Mundi_\(Leonardo\))* was sold at Christie's in New York for \$450 million to Saudi Prince [Badr bin Abdullah Al Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badr_bin_Abdullah_Al_Saud), [the highest price ever paid for a painting](235235.md).[^cite_note-7]

## History



In *A Peep at Christies* (1799), [James Gillray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gillray) caricatured actress [Elizabeth Farren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Farren) and huntsman [Lord Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Smith-Stanley,_12th_Earl_of_Derby) examining paintings appropriate to their tastes and heights.

### Founding



*[Portrait of James Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_James_Christie)* by [Thomas Gainsborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gainsborough), 1778

The official company literature states that founder [James Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Christie_\(auctioneer\)) (1730–1803) conducted the first sale in London on 5 December 1766,[^cite_note-8] and the earliest auction catalogue the company retains is from December 1766. However, other sources note that James Christie rented auction rooms from 1762, and newspaper advertisements for Christie's sales dating from 1759 have also been traced.[^cite_note-9] After his death, Christie's son, [James Christie the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Christie_the_Younger) (1773–1831) took over the business.[^cite_note-10]

### 20th century



*The Microcosm of London* (1808), an engraving of Christie's auction room

Christie's was a public company, listed on the [London Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stock_Exchange), from 1973 to 1999. In 1974, Jo Floyd was appointed chairman of Christie's. He served as chairman of Christie's International plc from 1976 to 1988, until handing over to [Lord Carrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Carington,_6th_Baron_Carrington), and later was a non-executive director until 1992.[^cite_note-11] Christie's International Inc. held its first sale in the United States in 1977. Christie's growth was slow but steady since 1989, when it had 42% of the auction market.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-12]



Clay tablet, a record of barley and emmer. Late Uruk period, 3300–3100 BCE. Purchased via Christie's in 1989, no provenance. British Museum

In 1990, the company reversed a long-standing policy and guaranteed a minimum price for a collection of artworks in its May auctions.[^cite_note-13] In 1996, sales exceeded those of [Sotheby's](541267.md) for the first time since 1954.[^cite_note-14] However, profits did not grow at the same pace;[^cite_note-Carol_Vogel_1998-2] from 1993 through 1997, Christie's annual pretax profits were about \$60M, whereas Sotheby's annual pretax profits were about \$265M for those years.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-15]

In 1993, Christie's paid \$10.9M for the London gallery [Spink & Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spink_&_Son), which specialised in Oriental art and British paintings; the gallery was run as a separate entity. The company bought Leger Gallery for \$3.3M in 1996, and merged it with Spink to become Spink-Leger.[^cite_note-16] Spink-Leger closed in 2002. To make itself competitive with Sotheby's in the property market, Christie's bought Great Estates in 1995, then the largest network of independent estate agents in North America, changing its name to Christie's Great Estates Inc.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-12]

In December 1997, under the chairmanship of [Lord Hindlip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Allsopp,_6th_Baron_Hindlip), Christie's put itself on the auction block, but after two months of negotiations with the consortium-led investment firm [SBC Warburg Dillon Read](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warburg_Dillon_Read) it did not attract a bid high enough to accept.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-15] In May 1998, [François Pinault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Pinault)'s holding company, Groupe Artémis S.A., first bought 29.1 per cent of the company for \$243.2M, and subsequently purchased the rest of it in a deal that valued the entire company at \$1.2bn.[^cite_note-Carol_Vogel_1998-2] The company has since not been reporting profits, though it gives sale totals twice a year. Its policy, in line with UK accounting standards, is to convert non-UK results using an average exchange rate weighted daily by sales throughout the year.[^cite_note-bloomberg.com-17]

### 21st century

In 2002, Christie's France held its first auction in Paris.[^cite_note-18]

Like Sotheby's, Christie's became increasingly involved in high-profile private transactions. In 2006, Christie's offered a reported \$21M guarantee to the [Donald Judd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Judd) Foundation and displayed the artist's works for five weeks in an exhibition that later won an [AICA award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Art_Critics) for "Best Installation in an Alternative Space".[^cite_note-19] In 2007 it brokered a \$68M deal that transferred [Thomas Eakins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Eakins)' *[The Gross Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gross_Clinic)* (1875) from the [Jefferson Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Medical_College) at the [Thomas Jefferson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson_University) in Philadelphia to joint ownership by the [Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Museum_of_Art) and the [Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Academy_of_the_Fine_Arts).[^cite_note-20] In the same year, the [Haunch of Venison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haunch_of_Venison) gallery[^cite_note-21] became a subsidiary of the company.[^cite_note-22]

On 28 December 2008, *[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Times)* reported that Pinault's debts left him "considering" the sale of Christie's and that a number of "[private equity](191280.md) groups" were thought to be interested in its acquisition.[^cite_note-23] In January 2009, the company employed 2,100 people worldwide, though an unspecified number of staff and consultants were soon to be cut due to a worldwide downturn in the art market;[^cite_note-downturn-24] later news reports said that 300 jobs would be cut.[^cite_note-downturn2-25] With sales for premier Impressionist, Modern, and contemporary artworks tallying only US\$248.8M in comparison to US\$739M just a year before, a second round of job cuts began after May 2009.[^cite_note-BloombergSecondCut-26]

In 2012, Impressionist works, which dominated the market during the 1980s boom, were replaced by contemporary art as Christie's top category. Asian art was the third most lucrative area.[^cite_note-bloomberg.com-17] With income from classic auctioneering falling, treaty sales made £413.4 million (\$665M) in the first half of 2012, an increase of 53% on the same period last year; they now represent more than 18% of turnover.[^cite_note-27] The company has since promoted curated events, centered on a theme rather than an art classification or time period.[^cite_note-28]

As part of a companywide review in 2017, Christie's announced the layoffs of 250 employees, or 12 per cent of the total work force, based mainly in Britain and Europe.[^cite_note-auto-29]

In June 2021, Christie's Paris held its first sale dedicated to women artists, most notably [Louise Moillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Moillon)'s *Nature morte aux raisins et pêches*.[^cite_note-30]

In 2022 Christie's sold \$8.4bn in art and luxury goods, an all-time high for any auction house.[^cite_note-All-Time_High-6][^cite_note-31]

Christie's agreed to acquire American [classic car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_car) auction house, [Gooding & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gooding_&_Company), in September 2024.[^cite_note-32]

## Commissions

From 2008 until 2013, Christie's charged 25 per cent for the first \$50,000; 20 per cent on the amount between \$50,001 and \$1M, and 12 per cent on the rest. From 2013, it charged 25 per cent for the first \$75,000; 20 per cent on the next \$75,001 to \$1.5M and 12 per cent on the rest.[^cite_note-33]

As of 2023, Christie's commission (buyer's premium) is 26 per cent of the hammer price of each lot up to £800,000/US\$1,000,000, plus 21 per cent of the hammer price from £800,001/US\$1,000,001 up to and including £4,500,000/US\$6,000,000, and 15 per cent on the rest.[^cite_note-34]

## Locations

As of 2023, Christie's has offices in 46 countries worldwide, with salerooms in London, New York, Paris, Geneva, Milan, Amsterdam, Dubai, Hong Kong, and Shanghai.[^cite_note-Christie's_Locations-3]

### Europe

Christie's flagship saleroom is in London on [King Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Street,_St_James's) in [St. James's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._James's), where it has been based since 1823. It had a second London saleroom in [South Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kensington) which opened in 1975 and primarily handled the middle market. Christie's permanently closed the South Kensington saleroom in July 2017 as part of their restructuring plans announced in March 2017. The closure was due in part to a considerable decrease in sales between 2015 and 2016 in addition to the company expanding its online sales presence.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

In early 2017, Christie's also announced plans to scale back its operation in Amsterdam.[^cite_note-auto-29]

### Americas

In 1977, led by then Chairman [Stephen Lash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Lash), the company opened its first international branch on [Park Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Avenue) in New York City in the [Delmonico's Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_Park_Avenue) grand ballroom on the second floor;[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] in 1997 it took a 30-year lease on a 28,000 m<sup>2</sup> (300,000 sq ft) space in [Rockefeller Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Center) for \$40M.[^cite_note-39]

Until 2001, Christie's East, a division that sold lower-priced art and objects, was located at 219 East 67th Street. In 1996, Christie's bought a townhouse on East 59th Street in Manhattan as a separate gallery where experts could show clients art in complete privacy to conduct private treaty sales.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-12]

Christie's opened a [Beverly Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills) saleroom in 1997.[^cite_note-40] In April 2017, in moved to a 4,500 sq ft (420 m<sup>2</sup>) two-story flagship space in Beverly Hills, designed by [wHY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulapat_Yantrasast).[^cite_note-41]

### Asia

Christie's has been operating a space in Hong Kong's [Alexandra House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_House) since 2014. In 2021, the company announced plans to move its Hong Kong headquarters to the [Zaha Hadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaha_Hadid)-designed luxury tower The Henderson in 2024, where it will launch year-round auctions. Measuring more than 50,000 sq ft (4,600 m<sup>2</sup>) over four storeys, the new space, which incorporates a permanent saleroom and galleries, is comparable in size to Christie's London headquarters.[^cite_note-42]

## Criticism

### Price-fixing scandal in 2000

In 2000, allegations surfaced of a price-fixing arrangement between Christie's and [Sotheby's](541267.md). Executives from Christie's subsequently alerted the Department of Justice of their suspicions of commission-fixing [collusion](162557.md).

Christie's gained immunity from prosecution in the United States as a longtime employee of Christie's confessed and cooperated with the US [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md). Numerous members of Sotheby's senior management were fired soon thereafter, and [A. Alfred Taubman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._Alfred_Taubman), the largest shareholder of Sotheby's at the time, took most of the blame; he and Dede Brooks (the CEO) were given jail sentences, and Christie's, Sotheby's and their owners also paid a civil lawsuit settlement of \$512M.[^cite_note-AR-113][^cite_note-CM-114][^cite_note-KW-115]

### Insufficient or invalid provenance for looted works

Christie's has been criticised for "an embarrassing history of a lack of transparency around [provenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provenance)".[^cite_note-\:0-84] In 2003, Christie's was criticised for its handling of two [Nazi-looted](3005707.md) artworks claimed by heirs of the original Jewish owners. In one case, it refused to divulge to the heirs the location of an Italian painting formerly owned by Jewish Viennese banker Heinrich Graf, looted by the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo).[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] Christie's eventually revealed the holder's name after the Jewish Community of Vienna filed a successful suit in the UK on behalf of Graf's American daughters in late 2004.[^cite_note-118] In the other 2003 case Christie's declined to inform the family that it had discovered that a painting consigned to it had been looted from Ulla and Moriz Rosenthal, a Jewish couple murdered in Auschwitz.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

On 19 May 2020, the craft supply company [Hobby Lobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobby_Lobby), who purchased material for loan or donation to [The Museum of the Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_Bible), filed a diversity action on the auction house regarding the sale and purchase of the [Gilgamesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgamesh) tablet by private sale agreement on 14 July 2014, allegedly while knowing the Iraqi-origin cuneiform object had a fake provenance.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] In June 2020, they were forced to withdraw four Greek and Roman antiquities from sale after it was discovered that they came from "sites linked to convicted antiquities traffickers".[^cite_note-\:0-84][^cite_note-123] The same month, they were criticised for putting up a [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_Bronzes) plaque and two [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people) [alusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alusi) figures for auction.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-\:2-125] The plaque was tied to similar plaques taken from Nigeria during the [Benin Expedition of 1897](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_Expedition_of_1897) and remained unsold after an auction was held.[^cite_note-\:2-125] The alusi figures are alleged to have been taken from Nigeria during the [Nigerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Civil_War) and were sold for €212,500 (after fees), below their low estimate of €250,000.[^cite_note-\:2-125][^cite_note-126] Christie's claims to require "verifiable documented provenance that the object was taken out of its source nation prior to the earlier date of 2000, or the date which is legally applicable between the country in which the sale takes place and the source nation".[^cite_note-\:2-125]

In November 2014, Christie's had to withdraw a prehistoric sculpture from [Sardinia](29376.md), valued at \$800,000–\$1.2m, put on auction by [Michael Steinhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Steinhardt), a US-billionaire, who was given a lifetime ban on acquiring further antiquities by the Manhattan district attorney's office in 2021.[^cite_note-127] After having acquired artworks with unverified [provenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provenance) for years, for example by convicted art dealer [Giacomo Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Medici_\(art_dealer\)), Steinhardt's collection had been subjected to search warrants and investigations since 2017. He finally surrendered 180 looted and illegally smuggled antiquities valued at \$70m. According to [The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian), the district attorney said: "For decades, Michael Steinhardt displayed a rapacious appetite for plundered artefacts without concern for the legality of his actions, the legitimacy of the pieces he bought and sold or the grievous cultural damage he wrought across the globe.[^cite_note-128]

In February 2023 a French court ordered Christie's to unconditionally restitute Dutch painting *The Penitent Magdalene*, signed [Adriaen van der Werff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriaen_van_der_Werff) (1707), looted in 1942 from Lionel Hauser in Paris and last sold by the auction house without any provenance in London in April 2005.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] Christie's had offered the Hauser heirs 50 per cent of the sale price; the heirs refused the offer and took the case to court.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

## Blockchain Technology at Auctions

In 2018, Christie's began offering "digital passports" stored on the [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain) to a select number of private collectors purchasing art. These "certificates of ownership" gave the buyer a clear and transparent [provenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provenance) record of the piece of art they were purchasing, a record that could never be erased or manipulated, and accessible worldwide.[^cite_note-133]

Six years later in October 2024, two Christie's auctions exhibited this block-chain technology in the most public events to date. In the first, on 2 October, Christie's New York showcased the work of [Diane Arbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Arbus) and [Cindy Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cindy_Sherman) in *An Eye Towards the Real: Photographs from the Collection of Ambassador [Trevor Traina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Traina)* – a former U.S. ambassador to Austria and founder of the [web3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web3) wallet provider *Kresus*. Eight days later, on 10 October, Christie's New York auctioned *Ascend*, a digital work created by Ryan Koopmans and Alice Wexell, marking the first time a piece inscribed on [Bitcoin's Ordinals protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_protocol) had been sold in a live auction at Christie’s. Christie's sees this "integration of physical and digital ownership" as the future of art auctioning, and the most efficient way for buyers to know they've purchased a work with as accurate and secure a provenance as possible.[^cite_note-134]

## Christie's Fine Art Storage Services (CFASS)

Christie's first ventured into storage services for outside clients in 1984, when it opened a 100,000 square feet brick warehouse in London that was granted "Exempted Status" by [HM Revenue and Customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Revenue_and_Customs),[^cite_note-online.wsj.com-135] meaning that property may be imported into the United Kingdom and stored without incurring import duties and [VAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAT). Christie's Fine Art Storage Services, or CFASS, is a wholly owned subsidiary that runs Christie's storage operation.

In September 2008, Christie's signed a 50-year lease on an early 1900s warehouse of the historic [N.Y.D. Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N.Y.D._Company)[^cite_note-An_exodus_from_Red_Hook-136] in [Red Hook, Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hook,_Brooklyn), and subsequently spent \$30M converting it into a six-storey, 250,000 square feet[^cite_note-137] art-storage facility.[^cite_note-online.wsj.com-135] The facility opened in 2010 and features high-tech security and climate controls that maintain a virtually constant 70° and 50% relative humidity.[^cite_note-138]

Located near the Upper Bay tidal waterway near the Atlantic Ocean, the Brooklyn facility was hit by at least one storm surge during [Hurricane Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Sandy) in 2012. CFASS subsequently faced client defections and complaints arising from damage to works of art.[^cite_note-An_exodus_from_Red_Hook-136] In 2013, [Axa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axa) Art Insurance filed a lawsuit in New York court alleging that CFASS' "gross negligence" during the hurricane damaged art collected by late cellist [Gregor Piatigorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Piatigorsky) and his wife [Jacqueline Rebecca Louise de Rothschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Piatigorsky).[^cite_note-139] Later that year, StarNet Insurance Co., the insurer for the [LeRoy Neiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeRoy_Neiman) Foundation and the artist's estate, also filed a lawsuit in [New York Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Supreme_Court) claiming that the storage company's negligence caused more than \$10M in damages to Neiman's art.[^cite_note-140]

## Educational and other ventures

Christie's Education previously offered master's degree programs in London and New York, but they were planned to be phased out in 2019. In 2020, in the aftermath of the [murder of George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd), Christie's noted that there was a lack of racial diversity in the art world, and admitted that Christie's degree programs only exacerbated these inequities.[^cite_note-141]

However, Christie's continue to offer non-degree programmes in London, New York, Hong Kong and Amsterdam as well as online.[^cite_note-142] In addition they offer an Art Business Masterclass Certificate and the Luxury Masterclass Certificate.[^cite_note-143]

With [Bonhams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonhams), Christie's is a shareholder in the London-based [Art Loss Register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Loss_Register), a privately owned database used by law enforcement services worldwide to trace and recover stolen art.[^cite_note-144]
