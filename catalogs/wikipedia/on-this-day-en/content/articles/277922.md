**New Year's Eve** in the [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar) refers to the evening—or commonly the entire day—of the last day of the year: [31 December](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_31). In many countries, New Year's Eve is celebrated with dancing, eating, drinking, and watching or lighting [fireworks](59493.md). Many [Christians](18337522.md) attend a [watchnight service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchnight_service) to mark the occasion. New Year's Eve celebrations generally continue into [New Year's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year's_Day), [1 January](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_1), past midnight.

The local [time zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_zone) determines the advent of the [New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year); the first places to welcome the New Year are west of the [International Date Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Date_Line): the [Line Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_Islands) (part of [Kiribati](16674.md)), [Samoa](27238.md) and [Tonga](30158.md), in the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). In contrast, [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa), [Baker Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Island) and [Howland Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howland_Island) (part of the [United States Minor Outlying Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Minor_Outlying_Islands)) are among the last.[^cite_note-1]

## By region

### Africa

#### Algeria

In [Algeria](358.md), New Year's Eve is usually celebrated with family and friends. In the largest cities, there are fireworks at midnight. The [Martyrs' Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrs'_Memorial,_Algiers) is the main attraction during the celebration, while some [Algerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerians) prefer celebrate outside the country, generally in [Tunis](57659.md) or [Paris](22989.md).

At 8 PM ([AST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Algeria)), the President's message of greetings is read on TV. The [EPTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tablissement_public_de_t%C3%A9l%C3%A9vision) network airs an entertainment show, with different hosts and guests.

#### Egypt

In [Egypt](8087628.md), New Year's Eve is celebrated with fireworks and often evening parties with friends and family.

#### Ghana

In [Ghana](12067.md), Ghanaians celebrate New Year's Eve by going to church; others go to nightclubs, pubs or take to the streets to celebrate. At midnight, [fireworks](59493.md) are displayed across various cities of Ghana, especially in [Accra](56824.md) and [Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tema).

#### Morocco



[Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca) fireworks display

In [Morocco](19291.md), New Year's Eve is celebrated by some in the company of family and friends. Moroccans get together to eat cake, dance, and laugh.

#### Nigeria



HKN's [Davido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davido) entertaining the crowd at the [Lagos Countdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Countdown) 2012 in [Nigeria](21383.md)&#x20;

In [Nigeria](21383.md), Nigerians often Celebrate the New Year's Eve by going to church; others go to nightclubs and parties organized by individuals, communities, and other organizations.

In [Lagos](85232.md), a year-end festival known as [Lagos Countdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Countdown) (later renamed One Lagos Fiesta)[^cite_note-2] was first held in 2012, as part of an effort to establish tourism-oriented New Year's festivities more in line with those of other major metropolitan areas.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

#### Rwanda

In [Rwanda](25645.md), Rwandans celebrate New Year's Eve by going to church, taking part in social gatherings and organizing family activities. The services usually start from 6 pm for the [Roman Catholic](606848.md) church and 10 pm for the [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant). At 00\:00, at midnight, the president delivers an end-of-year address which is broadcast live on many radio and television stations.[^cite_note-5] Fireworks were introduced in recent years, with the most significant displays happening at [Kigali Convention Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali_Convention_Centre), Rebero Hill, Mount Kigali.

#### South Africa



Fireworks in [Cape Town](6653.md) 2017.

In [South Africa](17416221.md), South Africans vote on a top ten music countdown before 31 December. When the countdown reaches number one, the song with the most votes plays on all the country's radio stations. Fireworks are lit all around South Africa. South Africans engage in occasional drinking and [braais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_variations_of_barbecue).

#### South Sudan

In [South Sudan](32350676.md), South Sudanese attend church services at many churches in [Juba](1514879.md). The service begins at 9 pm. At the stroke of midnight, the famous [carol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_carol), "[Hark! The Herald Angels Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hark!_The_Herald_Angels_Sing)" is sung to mark the end and beginning of the year with a blessing. The service ends at 12\:30 am.

#### Tunisia

In [Tunisia](30188.md), Tunisians celebrate by spending the evening in restaurants and hotels and exchange gifts and flowers, or travel outside Tunisia to spend New Year's Eve in a European country. But most Tunisians prefer to celebrate it at home in a family evening with relatives and friends.[^cite_note-\:tn-6]

Tunisians buy cakes or prepare them at home, in addition to holding dinner banquets, as [roast chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roast_chicken) remains the main dish for this occasion, and staying up until midnight to eat cake as the first moments of the new year arrive.[^cite_note-\:tn-6]

In recent years, the popular Tunisian film *[Choufli Hal, New Year's Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choufli_Hal,_New_Year's_Eve?action=edit\&redlink=1)&#x20;*&#x20;is broadcast every year, ending just minutes before the new year. This has become an annual tradition.[^cite_note-7]

### Asia



Some Asian countries where the main celebrations of the New Year are on a day other than 1 January.

#### Azerbaijan

The Gregorian calendar is still in force after Azerbaijan became an independent republic, and 1 January is celebrated as a day off. The day before, 31 December, is also marked as [International Solidarity Day of Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Solidarity_Day_of_Azerbaijanis), marking the double anniversary of that day in 1989 when the local residents took down the Soviet–Iranian border in then-[Nakhichevan ASSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhichevan_ASSR) to reunite with [Iranian Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Azerbaijanis) south of the border, as well as the Istanbul-held first World Congress of Azerbaijanis which tackled issues regarding the Azeri expat communities.[^cite_note-8]

Celebrations of the holiday are influenced from its Soviet history, at midnight the national anthem is played on all TV stations following the message of the President of Azerbaijan produced by state channel [AzTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AzTV).

#### Bangladesh



New Year celebration in [Dhaka](56656.md), Bangladesh.&#x20;

In Bangladesh, public celebrations of the Gregorian new year ([Pohela Boishakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pohela_Boishakh)—the new year of the [Bengali calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_calendar)—is celebrated in April),[^cite_note-sinha161-9] also known as *31st night*,[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-The_Business_Standard-2024-11][^cite_note-The_Financial_Express-Dec=2023-12] are centered upon major cities and beaches (including the capital city of [Dhaka](56656.md), and [Cox's Bazar Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_Beach)), and public events such as parties hosted by hotels.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In major cities such as Dhaka, there are restrictions placed on New Year's celebrations for safety, security, and environmental reasons, including bans on fireworks, the flying of lanterns, and organizing outdoor cultural events such as concerts.[^cite_note-The_Business_Standard-2024-11][^cite_note-The_Financial_Express-Dec=2023-12][^cite_note-15]

#### Hong Kong



Fireworks fired from [Two International Finance Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Finance_Centre_\(Hong_Kong\)) to mark the year 2010.

In [Hong Kong](13404.md), many gather in shopping districts like [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central,_Hong_Kong), [Causeway Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causeway_Bay) and [Tsim Sha Tsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsim_Sha_Tsui). A special edition of *[A Symphony of Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Symphony_of_Lights)* —the nightly [light and sound show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_and_sound_show) conducted across the buildings of Hong Kong's [Victoria Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Harbour)—is held on New Year's Eve, incorporating a fireworks show.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-Src6_2017-17]

From 1993 to 2014, the [Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_\(Hong_Kong\)) shopping centre in [Causeway Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causeway_Bay) hosted New Year's Eve festivities featuring the "[lowering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_objects_dropped_on_New_Year's_Eve)" of an apple (via 22 m (72 ft) of [signage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_signage)), in imitation of the [ball drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Ball) at [New York City](645042.md)'s [Times Square](31389.md). The countdown event was discontinued in 2015 in favor of other events over the holiday season.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

#### India

In India, New Year's Eve is a blend of modern celebrations and traditional festivities. Metropolitans like [Kochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochi), [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), [Mumbai](19189.md), [Delhi](37756.md), [Bengaluru](44275267.md), [Hyderabad](37534.md), [Pune](164634.md) and [Kolkata](47905.md) have parties, concerts, and colorful fireworks displays as part of the celebrations to attract crowds of people. Goa and Manali celebrate the day with beach parties and winter festivals. So, many people celebrate the festival by praying and performing rituals, since India is a culturally diverse country.

New Year's Eve celebrations are the biggest in large cities, and include [Goa](40010153.md)'s beaches and [Park Street, Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Street,_Kolkata). Other cities such as [New Delhi](51585.md) and [Mumbai](19189.md) also celebrate extravagantly.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

#### Israel

See also: [Rosh Hashanah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosh_Hashanah)

New Year's Eve has been observed in [Israel](9282173.md) since the introduction of the Gregorian calendar in 1918; it is referred to as *Silvester* to distinguish it from the Jewish New Year, [Rosh Hashanah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosh_Hashanah), which falls in either September or October on the Gregorian calendar. It is largely celebrated through social gatherings and parties.[^cite_note-i21c-22] The New Year's holiday has historically attracted a negative stigma among parts of the [Israeli Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Jews) population due to its connection to [Pope Sylvester I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_I)—who is widely considered to have been an [antisemite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemite). As a result, celebrations have historically been modest in comparison to other countries.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-Kershner-2018-24] In December 2014, [wearables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wearable_technology) manufacturer [Jawbone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawbone_\(company\)) published a report estimating that only 67.4% of Israelis were awake at midnight on New Year's Eve in 2013, and most people only stayed up as late as 12\:45 a.m. [IST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Standard_Time).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

During the era of [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) in the early-1930s, promotional material for formal New Year's Eve parties and [masquerade balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masquerade_ball) were targeted primarily towards [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)-speaking residents (by contrast, posters for [Hanukkah](7388.md) parties were written in [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language)). These parties also became popular among [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Germany) and [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Austria) Jews that had emigrated to avoid the rise of [Nazi Germany](21212.md).[^cite_note-i21c-22] The increasing popularity of Silvester faced criticism from the [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) population, including the [Hapoel HaMizrachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapoel_HaMizrachi), who considered them contrary to [Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) values.[^cite_note-i21c-22] In 1934, it was reported that the municipal council of [Tel Aviv](31453.md) had passed a resolution to ban Silvester parties, calling them "contrary to the spirit and traditions of the people of Israel". However, reported efforts to ban the holiday were unsuccessful or left unenforced, and it continued to increase in popularity—especially among secular populations.[^cite_note-i21c-22]

Following the [post-Soviet aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_post-Soviet_aliyah), [Novy God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novy_God) was imported into Israel by emigrants. The observance was initially obscure outside of Israel's [Russian Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Jews_in_Israel), and also faced stigma from those who mistook its traditions for being Christmas or Silvester. In the mid-2010s, a campaign was launched to promote awareness of the holiday among the [1.5 generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant_generations#1.5_generation) of immigrants, as well as non-Russian residents. By the 2010s, public awareness of Novy God had increased; Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) began to acknowledge Novy God in his holiday greetings, and it became more common for retailers to stock Novy God-related goods and hold promotions for the holiday.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-The_Jerusalem_Post-28][^cite_note-Kershner-2018-24][^cite_note-29] In a 2020 survey, 72% of Israelis surveyed stated they were familiar with the holiday, while 54% did not perceive Novy God to be part of the country's culture.[^cite_note-The_Jerusalem_Post-28][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

#### Japan

Main article: [Ōmisoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cmisoka)



Tokyo Tower on New Year's Eve, 2012

In [Japan](15573.md), New Year's Eve is used to prepare for and welcome [Toshigami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshigami) (年神), the New Year's god. Japanese clean their homes and prepare [Kadomatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadomatsu) or [Shimenawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimenawa) to welcome the god before New Year's Eve. Buddhist temples ring their bells 108 times at midnight in the traditional [Joya no Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joya_no_Kane) (除夜の鐘).[^cite_note-telegraph-32] The rings represent the 108 elements of [bonō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleshas_\(Buddhism\)) (煩悩), mental states that lead Japanese to take unwholesome actions.[^cite_note-33]

In most cities and urban areas across Japan, New Year's Eve celebrations are usually accompanied by concerts, countdowns, fireworks and other events. In [Tokyo](30057.md), revelers gather at the [Zojoji Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C5%8Dj%C5%8D-ji) in [Minato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minato,_Tokyo), release helium balloons with New Year's wishes, and watch the lighting of [Tokyo Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Tower) and [Tokyo Skytree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Skytree) with a year number displayed on the observatory at the stroke of midnight. [Shibuya Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibuya_Crossing) formerly hosted a gathering of revellers at midnight, but this ended in 2020 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Japan), as well as a subsequent crackdown on gatherings around Halloween and New Year's Eve due to issues with [overtourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtourism) and [public intoxication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_intoxication).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

Since 1951, [NHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK) has traditionally broadcast the *[Kōhaku Uta Gassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dhaku_Uta_Gassen)* ("Red and White Song Battle") on New Year's Eve, a music competition where two teams of popular musicians (the red and white teams, whose performances contain female and male vocalists respectively) perform songs. The winning team is determined by a panel of judges, audience members at the [NHK Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK_Hall) in Tokyo, and televotes. The special was previously one of Japan's most-watched television programs overall; its audience has since declined, and hit a record low of 34.3% in the [Video Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Research) ratings in 2021.[^cite_note-37]

From 1996 to 2022, the talent agency [Johnny & Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_&_Associates) organised *Johnny's Countdown*—a concert event at the [Tokyo Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Dome) televised by [Fuji Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_Television). In the wake of [sexual abuse allegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Kitagawa_sexual_abuse_scandal) surrounding the agency's founder, a new agency known as [Starto Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starto_Entertainment) was formed in 2023 to take on Johnny & Associates' roster: the new agency has yet to organise a successor to *Johnny's Countdown*.[^cite_note-38] The [Japan Record Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Record_Awards) were previously held on New Year's Eve from 1959 to 2006, but have since been held on 30 December.

A more recent tradition in Japan has been [combat sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_sports) [supercards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercard); the [Saitama Super Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Super_Arena) has hosted an MMA event on New Year's Eve since 2001, which was initially promoted by [Pride Fighting Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_Fighting_Championships). After its dissolution and sale to [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC), [Yarennoka!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarennoka!) was organized by former Pride executives in 2007,[^cite_note-Kotani-2007-39] while its successor [Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_\(mixed_martial_arts\)) held the event each year until its final event in 2012.[^cite_note-Gross-2012-40] These cards have also featured [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing) matches, promoted by groups such as [Glory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glory_\(kickboxing\)) and [K-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-1).[^cite_note-Gross-2012-40][^cite_note-Kotani-2007-39] [Rizin Fighting Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizin_Fighting_Federation) took over the tradition beginning in 2015 with its inaugural event.[^cite_note-41]

#### Kazakhstan

In Central Asia, such as Kazakhstan, New Year's Eve celebrations were inherited from Soviet traditions; thus they are similar to those of Russia. An example of such traditions would be the playing of the national anthem at midnight and the presidential address before it.

#### Korea

See also: [Korean New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_New_Year)

Image shows bell about three metres high, with celebrant alongside

Many South Korean gather at [Bosingak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosingak) in [Seoul](19159283.md) to celebrate the New Year.

Although the traditional [Korean New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_New_Year) *(Seollal)* is typically a more important holiday in both [North](21255.md) and [South Korea](27019.md), the 31 December New Year's Eve of the Gregorian calendar is also celebrated. Most cities and urban areas in both Koreas host New Year's Eve gatherings.

In South Korea, two of the biggest celebrations take place in the capital of [Seoul](19159283.md): the ringing of [Bosingak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosingak) bell 33 times at midnight and fireworks display at [Myeong-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myeong-dong), and an LED laser light show and fireworks display at the [Lotte World Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_World_Tower) in [Songpa-gu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songpa_District). Television networks [KBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Broadcasting_System) and [SBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Broadcasting_System) both broadcast award shows, the [KBS Drama Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KBS_Drama_Awards) and [SBS Drama Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SBS_Drama_Awards), to honor achievements in the [television dramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_dramas) aired by the networks. Until 2022 South Koreans calculated their age using the [East Asian age reckoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_age_reckoning) method, with all South Koreans adding a year to their age at midnight of the New Year (of the Gregorian, not the Korean calendar), the government finally ended the practice for 2023 and onwards.[^cite_note-42]

In [Pyongyang](47948.md), the capital of North Korea, the chimes of the clock at the Grand People's Study House and the national fireworks display along [Kim Il-sung Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il-sung_Square), [Juche Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juche_Tower) and the surrounding areas signal the start of the New Year. The celebration in Pyongyang, however, also marks the beginning of the [North Korean calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korean_calendar) or the Juche Year, which is based on 15 April 1912, [Kim Il Sung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il_Sung)'s date of birth, the celebrations are more recent in origin with the fireworks displays dating from 2013. For 2018–19, Kim Il Sung Square hosted a concert performance by the state [Moranbong Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moranbong_Band), midnight fireworks, and a [drone show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_display).[^cite_note-43] Since 2022, the national New Year's Eve concert and main fireworks display is held at the [Rungrado 1st of May Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rungrado_1st_of_May_Stadium).

#### Lebanon

In [Lebanon](17771.md). Lebanese people celebrate New Year's Eve with a dinner attended by family and friends. The dinner features traditional dishes such as tabouli, hummus, kibbi, and other Lebanese foods. These celebrations could also take place in restaurants and clubs. Game shows are also organized where contestants can try to win money. The countdown to New Year's is broadcast through the leading TV channel and the celebrations usually continue until sunrise. Fireworks are lit throughout the night.

#### Malaysia



Fireworks in [George Town, Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Town,_Penang) on 1 January 2018

#### Mongolia

Mongolians began celebrating the Gregorian New Year in the Socialist period, influenced by the former Soviet Union. It is common, just as in the former Soviet Union, for the [National Anthem of Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Anthem_of_Mongolia) to be played at midnight on television following the holiday address by the President of Mongolia.

#### Philippines



Fireworks in [Cebu City](285868.md) with main bridge of [CCLEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCLEX), venued at [Plaza Independencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Independencia_\(Cebu_City\)) 2025.

In the [Philippines](23440.md), New Year's Eve (*Bisperas ng Bagong Taon*) is a special non-working holiday (except for 2021 and 2022, in which it was a special working holiday),[^cite_note-46] and Filipinos usually celebrate in the company of family or close friends. Traditionally, most households would attend church for year-end services and afterwards, host or attend an abundant midnight feast called *Medianoche*. Typical dishes include *[pancit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancit)* (a noodle dish meant to symbolize having a long life) and *[hamón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jam%C3%B3n)* (dry-cured ham), while *[lechón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lechon)* (roasted pig) is usually prepared as is barbecued food and various desserts. Some refrain from serving chicken, as their scratching and pecking for food is said to be an unlucky idiom for a hand-to-mouth existence.

Many opt to wear new, bright, or colorful clothes with circular patterns, such as [polka dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polka_dot), or display sweets and twelve round fruits in the belief that circles attract money, while candies represent a sweeter year ahead. Several customs must be done exactly at midnight: scattering coins to increase wealth in the coming year, jumping to increase height, or the [Spanish custom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_influence_on_Filipino_culture) of eating twelve grapes, one for each month of the year. Many Filipinos also light [firecrackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firecrackers) and [fireworks](59493.md), and make loud noises by blowing on cardboard or plastic horns called *torotot*, banging on pots and pans, playing loud music, blowing car horns, or by firing [bamboo cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_cannon). This is an apotropaic ritual, as the din is believed to scare away bad luck and evil spirits.

Although many Filipinos typically spend their New Year's Eve at their family homes, in some urban areas, many New Year's Eve parties and countdown celebrations are also hosted by the private sector with the help of the local government. These parties, which include balls hosted by hotels, usually display their own fireworks and are also well-attended.

#### Saudi Arabia

Further information: [Islamic New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_New_Year)

Until 2016, Saudi Arabia used the [Umm al-Qura calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar#Saudi_Arabia's_Umm_al-Qura_calendar)—which is based on astronomical calculations—for administrative purposes. The [Committee for the Promotion of Virtue and the Prevention of Vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_the_Promotion_of_Virtue_and_the_Prevention_of_Vice_\(Saudi_Arabia\)) (CPVPV, the Saudi [religious police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_religious_police)) also enforced a ban on public celebrations of the Gregorian New Year as per a religious edict, and could fine shops for offering New Year's-related products and confiscate them. However, the religious police did not go after individual citizens holding private celebrations.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

The power of the CPVPV was curtailed by the [2016 reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_in_Saudi_Arabia) of [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] The country also began to base the salaries of public sector employees on the Gregorian calendar as a cost savings measure, while retaining the Islamic calendar for religious purposes.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] In 2019, the capital [Riyadh](51554.md) introduced a new winter entertainment festival known as [Riyadh Season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyadh_Season), in support of [Saudi Vision 2030](50299103.md).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] The inaugural festival included New Year's festivities centred upon [Boulevard Riyadh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_Riyadh_City), including fireworks and a concert at [Mohammed Abdo Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Abdo_Arena) featuring major [Arabic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_music) performers.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

#### Turkey



New Year's Eve decorations in [Kadıköy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y), [Istanbul](3391396.md).

Numerous decorations and customs traditionally associated with Christmas and [Bayrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayram_\(Turkey\)) are part of [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular) New Year's Eve celebrations in [Turkey](11125639.md). Homes and streets are lit in glittering lights. Small gifts are exchanged, and large family dinners are organized with family and friends, featuring a special [turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuffed_turkey) dish stuffed with a [zante currant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zante_currant), [pine nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_nuts), [pimiento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimiento) and [dill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dill) *[iç pilav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilaf)*, [dolma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolma), hot [börek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6rek), [baklava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baklava), and various other [Turkish dishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_cuisine); accompanied with [rakı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rak%C4%B1), [Turkish wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_wine), [boza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boza), [*şerbet*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharbat_\(beverage\)), [salep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salep), [Turkish tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_tea), or [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_coffee). Even though Turks generally do not celebrate [Christmas](6237.md), decorating [New Year trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year_tree) is an emerging tradition on New Year's Eve in Turkey and Turks associate [Santa Claus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Claus) with New Year's Eve.

Television and radio channels are known to continuously broadcast a variety of special New Year's Eve programs, while [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) all around the country organize fundraising events for the poor, in addition to celebratory public shows such as concerts and family-friendly events, as well as more traditional forms of entertainment such as the [Karagöz and Hacivat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karag%C3%B6z_and_Hacivat) [shadow-theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_theatre), and even performances by the [Mehter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehter)—the [Janissary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janissary) Band that was founded during the days of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).

Public and private parties with large public attendances are organized in a number of cities and towns, particularly in the largest metropolitan areas such as [Istanbul](3391396.md), [Ankara](802.md), [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir), [Adana](185243.md), [Bursa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursa) and [Antalya](361527.md), with the biggest celebrations taking place in Istanbul's [Taksim](631961.md), [Beyoğlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyo%C4%9Flu), [Nişantaşı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C5%9Fanta%C5%9F%C4%B1) and [Kadıköy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y) districts and Ankara's [Kızılay Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%B1z%C4%B1lay_Square), which generally feature dancing, concerts, laser and light shows as well as the traditional countdown and fireworks display.

In addition, on day, the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Turkey) addresses citizens in his New Year's message, congratulates them on the new year and talks about his goals for the new year.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

#### Albania

Preparations for New Year's Eve in [Albania](738.md) start with the Christmas tree, which in Albania is known as "New Year's Tree" or "New Year's Pine". At midnight, Albanians toast and greet each other and fireworks are lit.

#### Austria

In [Austria](26964606.md), New Year's Eve is usually celebrated with friends and family. At exactly midnight, all radio and television programmes operated by [ORF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ORF_\(broadcaster\)) broadcast the sound of the [Pummerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pummerin), the bell of [St. Stephen's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_Cathedral,_Vienna) in [Vienna](55866.md), followed by the *Donauwalzer* ("[The Blue Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blue_Danube)") by [Johann Strauss II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Strauss_II). Many Austrians dance to this at parties or in the street. Large crowds gather in the streets of Vienna, where the municipal government organizes a series of stages where bands and orchestras play. Fireworks are set off by both municipal governments and individuals.

#### Belgium

In [Belgium](3343.md), New Year's Eve ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Sint Sylvester Vooravond* \["Saint Sylvester's Eve"] or *Oudjaar* \["old year"]) is celebrated with family parties –  called *réveillons* \[let's wake up] in the [French speaking areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallonia). On television, a stand-up comedian reviews the past year after which a musical or variety show signals midnight, when Belgians kiss, exchange good luck greetings, and toast the New Year and absent relatives and friends with [champagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champagne_\(wine\)). Many Belgians light fireworks or go into the street to watch them. Most cities have their own fireworks display: the most famous is at [Mont des Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_des_Arts) in Brussels. Cities, cafés and restaurants are crowded. Free bus services and special New Year's Eve taxis (the *Responsible Young Drivers*) bring Belgians home afterwards.

On 1 January (Dutch: *Nieuwjaarsdag*) children read their "New Year's letter" and give holiday greeting cards of decorated paper featuring golden cherubs and angels, colored roses and ribbon-tied garlands to parents and godparents, on decorated paper.

#### Bosnia and Herzegovina

New Year is widely celebrated in [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md). Streets are decorated for New Year's Eve and there is a fireworks show and concerts in all of the larger cities. Restaurants, clubs, cafes and hotels are usually full of guests and they organize New Year's Eve parties.

In the capital [Sarajevo](26786.md), Bosnians gather in the Square of children of Sarajevo where a local rock band entertains them. Several trumpet and rock groups play until the early morning hours. At midnight there is a big fireworks show.

#### Czechia and Slovakia



[Prague](23844.md) New Year Fireworks

New Year's Eve (*Silvestr/Silvester*) celebrations and traditions in [Czech Republic](5321.md) and [Slovakia](26830.md) are very similar. New Year's Eve is the noisiest day of the year. Czechs and Slovaks generally gather with friends at parties, in pubs, clubs, in the streets, or city squares to eat, drink, and celebrate the new year. Fireworks are a popular tradition; in large cities such as [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava), or [Prague](23844.md), the fireworks start before noon and steadily increase until the clock strikes midnight. In the first minutes after midnight, Czechs and Slovaks toast with champagne, wish each other a happy new year, fortune and health, and go outside for the fireworks displays.

In both countries all major TV stations air entertainment shows before and after the midnight countdown, which is followed by the National anthem of each country. The Presidents of the republics gave their New Year speech in the morning – ex-Czech President [Miloš Zeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Zeman) renewed the tradition of Christmas speeches. In recent years however the [Czechoslovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) national anthem is played at midnight in some stations, in honor of the [shared history of both nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia).

On this day, Czechs and Slovaks traditionally make a sandwich-like food, the chlebíčky. The recipe is a bread, ham, cheese, egg and tomato.

The greeting is "Šťastný Nový rok!", but in Christany, they say "Všechno nejlepší do novího roku!" / "Všetko dobré do nového roku!" (All the best/goodness to the New Year!) followed by luck, health etc., similar to birthday wishes, but instead of saying "Thank you" they say "You too", because it is a holiday for everybody.

#### Denmark



New Year fireworks over [Copenhagen](5166.md)

Danes in [Denmark](76972.md) may go to parties or entertain guests at home. There is a special evening meal that concludes with [Kransekage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kransekage), a special dessert, along with champagne. Other traditional dishes are boiled cod, stewed kale and cured saddle of pork.[^cite_note-73] However, expensive cuts of beef as well as sushi have become increasingly popular.[^cite_note-74] Another well-known tradition is jumping off a chair at midnight, symbolizing a leap into the new year.[^cite_note-75]

Multiple significant traditional events are broadcast on television and radio on 31 December. This includes, but is not limited to:

The monarch's New Year message from [Amalienborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalienborg_Palace)[^cite_note-76] at 18\:00 and the Town Hall Clock in [Copenhagen](5166.md) striking midnight. Another reoccurring broadcast is the 1968 film [*The Party*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Party_\(1968_film\)), which is aired after midnight on 1 January.

#### Estonia

To celebrate New Year's Eve in [Estonia](28222445.md), Estonians decorate villages, visit friends and prepare lavish meals.

Some believe that Estonians should eat seven, nine, or twelve times on New Year's Eve. These are lucky numbers in Estonia; it is believed that for each meal consumed, the person gains the strength of that many men the following year. Meals should not be completely finished—some food should be left for ancestors and spirits who visit the house on New Year's Eve.

Traditional New Year food includes pork with sauerkraut or Estonian sauerkraut (*mulgikapsad*), baked potatoes and swedes with hog's head, and white and blood sausage. Vegetarians can eat potato salad with navy beet and pâté. Gingerbread and marzipan are very popular for dessert. Traditional New Year drinks include beer and mead, but mulled wine and champagne have become modern favourites.

#### Finland



Fireworks in the forest of [Ruka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rukatunturi) on New Year's Eve in [Kuusamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuusamo), Finland

In [Finland](10577.md), New Year's Eve is usually celebrated with family or friends. Late supper is served, often featuring wieners, [Janssons frestelse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janssons_frestelse), and potato salad. Some municipalities organize fireworks at midnight. Consumer fireworks are also very popular. A Finnish tradition is [molybdomancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdomancy) – to tell the fortunes of the New Year by melting "tin" (actually lead) in a tiny pan on the stove and throwing it quickly in a bucket of cold water. The resulting blob of metal is analyzed, for example by interpreting shadows it casts by candlelight. These predictions are however never taken seriously.

The principal broadcast is aired by [YLE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YLE) at [Helsinki Senate Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Senate_Square) featuring Finnish music stars. The countdown to the New Year is with the [Helsinki Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Cathedral) clock. Preceding this the German comedy sketch *[Dinner for One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinner_for_One)* is shown every year in the afternoon. On the radio, just before midnight, the poem *Hymyilevä Apollo* (Smiling Apollo) by [Eino Leino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eino_Leino) is read.[^cite_note-81]

#### France



Fireworks at [Arc de Triomphe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_de_Triomphe) in [Paris](22989.md) to welcome 2020.

In [France](5843419.md), New Year's Eve (*la Saint-Sylvestre*) is usually celebrated with a feast, *le Réveillon de la Saint-Sylvestre*[^cite_note-82] (*Cap d'Any* in [Northern Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Catalonia)). #### Germany

In [Germany](11867.md), parties are common on New Year's Eve (*Silvester*), and wishes of luck may be worded as "Guten Rutsch ins neue Jahr!", which literally translates into "Good slide into the new year!" or "Slide well into the new year!",[^cite_note-84] as well as "Prost Neujahr!" for "Cheers (to the) New Year!" or "Frohes Neues!" literally meaning "Happy new one!"[^cite_note-85] Fireworks are very popular, both with individuals and at large municipal displays. 31 December and the three days leading up to it are the only four days of the year on which fireworks (of class F2) may be sold to private citizens without a fireworks license in Germany.[^cite_note-86] Every year [Berlin](3354.md) hosts one of the largest New Year's Eve celebrations in all of Europe, attended by over a million Germans. The focal point is the [Brandenburg Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_Gate), where midnight fireworks are centered, with a live broadcast on [ZDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zweites_Deutsches_Fernsehen) under the name *[Willkommen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willkommen_20xx)* with musical guests beginning in 2011. Germans toast the New Year with a glass of [Sekt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparkling_wine#Sekt) (German sparkling wine) or champagne. [Molybdomancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdomancy) *(Bleigießen)* is another German New Year's Eve tradition, which involves telling fortunes by the shapes made by molten lead dropped into cold water. Other auspicious actions are to touch a chimney sweep or rub some ash on one's forehead for good luck and health. [Jam-filled doughnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berliner_\(doughnut\)) with or without alcoholic fillings are eaten. Finally a tiny [marzipan pig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marzipan_pig) is consumed for more good luck. In some northern regions of Germany (e.g. [East Frisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Frisia)) the making of *[Speckendicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speckendicken?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (also *Speckdicken*) is another tradition – Germans go door to door visiting their neighbors and partaking in this dish. It looks similar to a pancake, but the recipe calls for either dark molasses or dark syrup, topped with a few [mettwurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mettwurst) slices and bacon strips.[^cite_note-87]

Another notable tradition is watching the British comedy sketch *[Dinner for One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinner_for_One)*, which has traditionally been broadcast on German television on New Year's Eve since 1972. The version traditionally broadcast on German television was originally recorded in 1963, and was occasionally used as filler programming by [NDR](2917610.md) due to popular demand; in 1972, *Dinner for One* received its traditional New Year's Eve scheduling. The sketch, as well as its catchphrase "the same procedure as every year", are well known in German pop culture. *Dinner for One* is also broadcast on or around New Year's Eve in other European countries, although it is, ironically, relatively unknown in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-telegraph-32][^cite_note-88]

#### Ireland

New Year's Eve (*Oíche Chinn Bliana*, *Oíche na Coda Móire* or *Oíche Chaille*, the night of big portion)[^cite_note-Kinmonth-2006-96] when traditionally households would partake in a large feast that was believed to ensure a plentiful new year.[^cite_note-Mahon-1998-97] Beliefs around the day meant that no food or other goods would be taken from the house, to guard against lack or hunger in the year to come, believing that if anything was taken from the house on this day the house would remain empty for the year and have no luck. It was traditional for no water to be drawn from a well after sunset.[^cite_note-98] Even the homeless and those in need would not be offered food or alms on this day. In some parts of the country, a large [barmbrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barmbrack) would be baked during the day, with the man of the house taking three large bites of the cake in the evening, and throwing it against the inside of the front door as an offering to the [Holy Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity). An invocation accompanied this:

*Fógraímíd an gorta\
&#x20;Amach go tír na dTurcach;\
&#x20;Ó 'nocht go bliain ó 'nocht\
&#x20;Agus 'nocht féin amach*[^cite_note-Mahon-1998-97]

This translates as "We warn famine to retire, To the land of the Turks; From tonight to this night twelve months, And from this night itself." The bits of cake would be gathered, and eaten by the family.[^cite_note-Mahon-1998-97] Other variations include throwing the cake to someone outside the door, or conducting the ritual in the stables or other animal housing.[^cite_note-Kinmonth-2006-96] Church bells ringing, the lighting of bonfires, and singing would take place towards midnight.[^cite_note-Mahon-1998-97]

In modern times, celebrations in major cities are modest, with most Irish citizens favoring small parties in the home for family and friends.

#### Netherlands



The New Year's fireworks at the [Erasmusbrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmusbrug) in Rotterdam.



A group of people holding a [bonfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonfire) and *carbidschieten.*

New Year's Eve (*Oud en Nieuw* or *Oudejaarsavond*) in the [Netherlands](21148.md) is usually celebrated as a cozy evening with family or friends, although big organized parties can also be attended. Traditional snack foods are *[oliebollen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliebol)* (Dutch doughnuts)[^cite_note-104] and *appelbeignets* (apple slice [fritters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritter)).[^cite_note-105] A past tradition among [Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_churches) Protestant families was the reading of [Psalm 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalm_90) shortly before midnight.[^cite_note-106]

The Top 2000 usually begins on Christmas Day, and airs non-stop through New Year's Eve from a temporary studio at the [Netherlands Institute for Sound and Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Institute_for_Sound_and_Vision) in [Hilversum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilversum).[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

Fireworks displays are held in major cities such as [Rotterdam](26049.md) (which is the largest display in the country) and [Amsterdam](844.md).[^cite_note-Moltubak-2022-111] New Year's Eve is the only time of year where category F2 and F3 fireworks can be legally purchased for private use by the general public without training and/or certification; these fireworks may only be sold during the last three days of the year (excluding any Sundays), and may only be lit between 6 p.m. on 31 December and 2 a.m. on 1 January.[^cite_note-Vuurwerkbesluit-112][^cite_note-Moltubak-2022-111][^cite_note-RTVOost-113] A popular alternative or compliment to fireworks in rural areas is *carbidschieten* (carbide shooting), in which [calcium carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_carbide) explosions are used to shoot the lid (or, alternatively, a [ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball)) off the top of a [milk churn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_churn). In 2014, carbidschieten was named a part of the Netherlands' [intangible cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage).[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

Due to injuries and deaths caused by fireworks use, there have been increasing calls for banning the public sale of fireworks for New Year's Eve.[^cite_note-BBC_News-Apr-2025-116][^cite_note-nltimes.nl-2025a-117][^cite_note-nltimes.nl-2025b-118] As of 2024, 19 municipalities have banned fireworks outside of professional shows, and around 100 have enforced prohibitions on the use of fireworks in proximity to areas such as parks and hospitals. However, these policies have proven ineffective due to the wide use of illegal fireworks.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-BBC_News-Apr-2025-116] In April 2025, the [Tweede Kamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Netherlands\)) passed a motion seeking to completely ban consumer fireworks; any such ban will not take effect until New Year's 2026–27 at the earliest, due to the need to draft the required legislation, as well as address concerns from the pyrotechnics industry (which has demanded compensation from the government for the loss of business that a ban would create).[^cite_note-BBC_News-Apr-2025-116][^cite_note-nltimes.nl-2025a-117][^cite_note-nltimes.nl-2025b-118][^cite_note-120]

#### Norway

In [Norway](21241.md), New Year's Eve (*Nyttårsaften*) is the second biggest celebration of the year, after [Christmas Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Eve). While Christmas Eve is a family celebration, New Year's Eve is an opportunity to celebrate with friends.[^cite_note-121]

Traditionally, there is first a feast, commonly consisting of stuffed, roast turkey with potatoes, sprouts, gravy and [Waldorf salad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldorf_salad). The accompanying beverage is traditionally beer (commonly either Christmas beer or lager beer). Dessert will often be vanilla pudding or rice cream, and there will be cakes and coffee later in the evening – commonly accompanied by a glass of cognac. Then, at close to midnight on New Year's Day, Norwegians will go outside to send up fireworks. Fireworks are only permitted to be sold to the general public on the days leading up to New Year's Eve, and only to be launched that night.

Due to the general use of fireworks, more fires occur on New Year's Eve than on any other day of the year in Norway. Accordingly, most Norwegian cities, and many towns, host a large, public fireworks display in order to discourage private use of fireworks in built-up areas. Norwegians will then congregate in a central square or similar to watch and celebrate.

#### Portugal

Fireworks in Funchal, Madeira Islands

In [Portugal](23033.md), the New Year celebration is taken very seriously. The tradition is to drink champagne and eat twelve raisins – one for each month of the year, making a wish for each. Another Portuguese tradition is a special cake called *[Bolo-Rei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolo-Rei)* (literally: King Cake). *Bolo-Rei* is a round cake with a large hole in the centre, resembling a crown covered with crystallized and dried fruit. It is baked from a soft, white dough, with raisins, various nuts and crystallized fruit. Inside is hidden the characteristic *fava* ([broad bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_bean)). Tradition dictates that whoever finds the *fava* has to pay for the *Bolo-Rei* next year. Initially, a small prize (usually a small metal toy) was also included within the cake. However, the inclusion of the prize was forbidden by the European Union for safety reasons. The Portuguese brought the recipe of the *[Gateau des Rois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateau_des_Rois)* from France in the second half of the 19th century. To this day, this recipe is a very well kept secret.

In [Lisbon](18091.md), the New Year is celebrated with a grand concert. The New Year's Concert is held at the CCB ([Centro Cultural de Belém](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_Cultural_de_Bel%C3%A9m)) on the evening of 1 January, featuring the prestigious Lisbon Metropolitan Orchestra.

#### Russia

Main article: [Novy God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novy_God)

Midnight at Red Square in 2012.

The most prominent public celebration of the New Year is held at [Moscow](19004.md)'s [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square) under the [Kremlin Clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin_Clock)—whose chimes at midnight are traditionally followed by the playing of the [Russian national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_national_anthem), and a fireworks display.[^cite_note-124] The [President's](239646.md) [New Year's address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year_Address_by_the_President_of_Russia) is televised shortly before midnight in each [time zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Russia), reflecting on the previous year and the state of the country. In 1999, unpopular president [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin) famously announced his resignation during his New Year's address.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

Novy God ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Новый Год, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'New Year') is celebrated as a gift-giving holiday with similarities to [Christmas](6237.md); [New Year trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year_tree) (*yolka*) are decorated and displayed in homes and public spaces,[^cite_note-Father_Frost_and_the_Snow_Maiden_deliver_Russia's_winter_warmer,_by_Helen_Womack,_31_December_1996-128][^cite_note-Weber-2020-129][^cite_note-MIG-130] and [Ded Moroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ded_Moroz) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Дед Мороз, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Father Frost') is depicted as delivering presents to children on New Year's Eve.[^cite_note-Father_Frost_and_the_Snow_Maiden_deliver_Russia's_winter_warmer,_by_Helen_Womack,_31_December_1996-128][^cite_note-Weber-2020-129][^cite_note-MIG-130] with assistance from his granddaughter [Snegurochka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snegurochka) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Снегурочка, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'the Snow Maiden').[^cite_note-131]

The Novy God traditions were established under [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) rule, when the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union) abolished Christmas and other religious holidays in 1928 as part of [policies meant to curtail the practice of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_anti-religious_legislation). In 1935, Soviet officials, including politician [Pavel Postyshev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Postyshev), began promoting the New Year as a non-working holiday in the benefit of youth. Christmas traditions such as [trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_tree) and a [Santa Claus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Claus)-like figure were adapted in a [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularity) form.[^cite_note-Father_Frost_and_the_Snow_Maiden_deliver_Russia's_winter_warmer,_by_Helen_Womack,_31_December_1996-128][^cite_note-Weber-2020-129][^cite_note-MIG-130][^cite_note-132] Even after the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) and the reinstatement of religious holidays, Novy God has remained a popular celebration in modern Russia,[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-Father_Frost_and_the_Snow_Maiden_deliver_Russia's_winter_warmer,_by_Helen_Womack,_31_December_1996-128] and among Soviet and Russian expats living in other countries.[^cite_note-135]

Two Soviet comedy films set during the holiday—*[Carnival Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_Night)* (1956) and *[The Irony of Fate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irony_of_Fate)* (1976)—are often broadcast by Russian television channels on New Year's Eve,[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] with the latter having been compared to the traditional [Christmas Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Eve) airing of *[It's a Wonderful Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_a_Wonderful_Life)* in the United States.[^cite_note-138] The Soviet variety show *[Little Blue Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Blue_Light)* traditionally broadcast a New Year's special, which was revived by [Russia-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia-1) in 1997.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

#### Serbia

The Gregorian calendar was adopted by [Yugoslavia](34244.md) in 1919,[^cite_note-141] but the [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) continues to follow the Julian calendar, meaning that the new year is often celebrated twice. Prior to World War II, the New Year's holiday was celebrated more often by Serbs in urban regions, with large parties held on both 1 and [14 January](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_New_Year). By contrast, residents of rural regions rarely celebrated the new year, and placed a larger focus on [Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_in_Serbia).[^cite_note-Balkan_Insight-2013-142]

In 1945 after [World War II](32927.md), the [League of Communists of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Communists_of_Yugoslavia) came into power, and the [Kingdom of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) was succeeded by the [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) (SFR Yugoslavia). As in the Soviet Union, the communist government discouraged the observance of religious holidays, encouraged celebrations of the New Year on 1 January as a secular gift-giving holiday, and similarly adopted the figure of Ded Moroz as "Deda Mraz".[^cite_note-Nikolic-2015-143] Some residents (especially those in opposition to the communist government) continued to celebrate the Orthodox New Year, doing so quietly by candlelight in order to evade attention from authorities.[^cite_note-Balkan_Insight-2013-142]

After the end of communist rule and the [breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia), the Gregorian New Year (1 January) and the Serbian New Year (14 January) began to co-exist. Both holidays are marked by festivities in major Serbian cities, although festivities for the Serbian New Year (which, in 2013, was redesignated as a public holiday for the first time since 1918) are usually modest in comparison to their counterparts on 1 January.[^cite_note-Nikolic-2015-143][^cite_note-Balkan_Insight-2013-142]

#### Slovenia

As in the other constituents of SFR Yugoslavia, Christmas and other religious holidays were abolished by the communist government in the mid-1940s, with the New Year promoted as a secular holiday in the place of [Saint Nicholas Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas_Day) and Christmas. Ded Moroz is referred to in Slovenian as "Dedek Mraz", and was originally billed as having come from [Siberia](29639.md). After Yugoslavia [broke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tito%E2%80%93Stalin_split) from the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc), the character was stated to come from the [Triglav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triglav) mountain instead, and artist [Maksim Gaspari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksim_Gaspari) created a new depiction of Dedek Mraz in traditional Slovenian apparel.[^cite_note-Slovenia_Times-2005-144]

Saint Nicholas Day and Christmas were reinstated as holidays after the end of communist rule.[^cite_note-Slovenia_Times-2005-144]

#### Spain



[Madrid](41188263.md)'s [Puerta del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerta_del_Sol) on New Year's Eve, 2005.

In [Spain](26667.md), the main public celebration of New Year's Eve (*Nochevieja*, literally "Old Night", or *Fin de Año*) is held at [Puerta del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerta_del_Sol) in [Madrid](41188263.md), where revellers await the midnight chimes of the clock tower at the [Royal House of the Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_House_of_the_Post_Office). A notable Spanish tradition is to [eat twelve grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Grapes) at midnight—one for each chime of the clock—which is said to bring luck and prosperity. The tradition dates back as early as 1895[^cite_note-145] but first gained wider attention in 1909, when it was promoted by [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante) grape growers to help spur sales of that year's surplus harvest. In the lead-up to the holiday, grocery stores are usually stocked with large amounts of grapes.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-In_Spain-147] The tradition has also been adopted in other communities with cultural ties to Spain or [Latin America](18524.md), including [Hispanic and Latino Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_Americans).[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]

It is common to attend *cotillones de nochevieja* that last into the following morning,[^cite_note-150] including smaller parties at bars and larger-scale events at hotels. After midnight, Spaniards often drink sparkling wines such as [cava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cava_\(Spanish_wine\)) and [champagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champagne_\(wine\)).[^cite_note-In_Spain-147]

Several Spanish television personalities have become well known for their involvement in New Year's specials, including [Ramón García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Garc%C3%ADa_\(TV_host\))—who has hosted specials for [Televisión Española](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televisi%C3%B3n_Espa%C3%B1ola) (TVE) and [Antena 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antena_3_\(Spanish_TV_channel\)), and model [Cristina Pedroche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Pedroche)—who is known for the often-provocative dresses she has worn on-air.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

A 10 km (6.2 mi) race known as the [San Silvestre Vallecana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Silvestre_Vallecana) is also held in Madrid on the evening of New Year's Eve, which includes an amateur [fun run](8836052.md) and a competitive event for elite athletes. In 2012 the event hosted a record of around 40,000 runners.[^cite_note-156]

#### Sweden



[Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg) fireworks on New Year's Eve, 2008.

In [Sweden](5058739.md), New Year's Eve is usually celebrated with families or with friends. A few hours before and after midnight, Swedish citizens usually party and eat a special dinner, often three courses. New Year's Eve is celebrated with large fireworks displays throughout the country, especially in the cities, major ones in particular having distinguishing celebrations. Swedish citizens over the age of 18 are allowed to buy fireworks, which are sold by local stores or by private people. While watching or lighting fireworks at midnight, Swedish citizens usually drink champagne.

On television, the lottery show *[BingoLotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BingoLotto)* features a special New Year's Eve edition to commemorate the holiday with musical guests, four bingo games, and surprises.

The final of the [Spengler Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spengler_Cup) ice hockey tournament is traditionally held on New Year's Eve.

#### Ukraine



New Year celebration in [Kyiv](585629.md)

The main public celebration is held at [Maidan Nezalezhnosti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maidan_Nezalezhnosti) in Kyiv, including concerts and a fireworks display. For 2013–14, amid the [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) movement, it also included a world record attempt at the largest simultaneous singing of a [national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthem_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-157] New Year's Eve also coincides with [Malanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malanka), a folk custom that has similarities to [mummering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mummering).[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]

Under Soviet rule, Ukrainian New Year's celebrations were patterned off the secular [Novy God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novy_God) traditions, with Christmas (which, among those who practice [Eastern Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity), is held on 7 January) officially considered abolished—if not celebrated in secret by those opposed to the communist regime. Christmas regained prominence after the dissolution, with figures such as Grandfather Frost eventually being displaced by Western figures such as Saint Nicholas and Santa Claus,[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] and traditions associated with Novy God have been demonized by Ukrainians amid the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) and the [2014 annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation)—such as *yolka* ([New Year's trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year's_tree)) and the Soviet film *[The Irony of Fate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irony_of_Fate)* (which was pulled by [STB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STB_\(TV_channel\)) in 2015 as part of the [Boycott Russian Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott_Russian_Films) campaign, citing a lead actress who had been blacklisted by the Ukraine government for her support of the annexation).[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]

#### United Kingdom

##### England



The [London Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Eye) serves as a focal point for London's New Year's fireworks.

The most prominent New Year's Eve (Old Year's Night) celebration in England is that of [Central London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_London), where the arrival of midnight is greeted with the chimes of [Big Ben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ben). Since 2004, [a major fireworks display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year's_Eve_in_London) has also been held, with fireworks launched from the [South Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bank) of the [River Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames), and from the nearby [London Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Eye) observation wheel. ##### Scotland

Main article: [Hogmanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogmanay)

See also: [Edinburgh's Hogmanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh's_Hogmanay)



[Hogmanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogmanay) fireworks in [Edinburgh](9602.md).

In [Scotland](26994.md), the New Year—known as *[Hogmanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogmanay)*—is commonly celebrated through street parties and fireworks displays in major cities; both New Year's Day and 2 January are [bank holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_holiday) in Scotland, and Christmas is not usually considered to be as prominent of an observance in the country. Prominent festivities on 31 December in Scotland include those held in the capital of [Edinburgh](9602.md) (where several days of [organised festivities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh's_Hogmanay), held from 28 December through 2 or 3 January, culminate with the firing of [cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon) at [Edinburgh Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Castle) and fireworks at midnight), and the town of [Stonehaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehaven) near [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen) (where a procession of paraders swing "fireballs" around their heads made from [chicken wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_wire) and other flammable scrap at midnight, which are believed to ward off spirits).[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173]

"[Auld Lang Syne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auld_Lang_Syne)" is traditionally sung after midnight at gatherings, with participants traditionally forming a circle with other revellers, and joining hands during the final verse.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] Hogmanay is also associated with the custom of [first-footing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-foot)—in which the first person to enter someone's home after midnight is meant to bring a gift symbolizing prosperity for the new year (such as a loaf of [black bun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_bun) or [shortbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortbread), a lump of coal or salt, and/or [whisky](28896.md)).[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179]

From 1953 to 1985, Hogmanay had received exposure across the entire United Kingdom via the [BBC](19344654.md), who televised [BBC Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Scotland)-produced specials such as *[The White Heather Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_White_Heather_Club)* as part of their New Year's Eve programming. The specials were often lambasted by critics for their [stereotypical portrayal of Scottish culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartanry), but were popular enough to spawn competitors on [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV1). The practice ended after 1985, when the poor reception to that year's *[Live into 85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_into_85)* led to the BBC, among others, abandoning the concept altogether. [BBC One Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_One_Scotland) has since continued to air a [local Hogmanay special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Scotland's_Hogmanay) featuring coverage from Edinburgh as a [regional opt-out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_variation), with the rest of the network taking England-centric specials (albeit with exceptions in 1998–99, when BBC One networked an Edinburgh-based *New Year Live* special that would turn out to be equally-shambolic, and 2002–03, where BBC One carried a straight simulcast of *Hogmanay Live*).[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183]

##### Wales

Further information: [Calennig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calennig)



A [Mari Lwyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_Lwyd) c. 1910–1914. Traditionally carried from door to door during [Calennig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calennig) in [Wales](69894.md)

The [Welsh](69894.md) tradition of giving gifts and money on New Year's Day ([Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language): *[Calennig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calennig)*) is an ancient custom that survives in modern-day Wales.[^cite_note-184] Groups of singers, such as children or young people, go around the houses of their local area and sing a new years song to wish a happy new year.[^cite_note-Museum_Wales-185] The custom, similar to carol singing, leads to the group receiving money for their performance.[^cite_note-Museum_Wales-185] This custom is also coupled with the tradition [wassailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassail) in the presence of [Mari Lwyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_Lwyd) (a horse's head mounted on a pole).[^cite_note-186]

In [Cwm Gwaun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cwm_Gwaun), [Pembrokeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembrokeshire), the tradition of observing the [Old New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_New_Year) (on the 13 January) is still followed with similar festivities including *Calennig*.[^cite_note-187]

Thousands of Welsh citizens descend every year on [Cardiff](5882.md) to enjoy live music, catering, ice-skating, funfairs, and fireworks. Many of the celebrations take place at [Cardiff Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_Castle) and [Cardiff City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Hall,_Cardiff).

Every New Year's Eve, the [Nos Galan road race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nos_Galan_road_race) (*Rasys Nos Galan*), a five-kilometre (3.1 mi) running contest, is held in [Mountain Ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Ash,_Wales) in the [Cynon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynon_Valley), [Rhondda Cynon Taf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhondda_Cynon_Taf), [South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Wales). The race celebrates the life and achievements of Welsh runner [Guto Nyth Brân](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guto_Nyth_Br%C3%A2n).

Founded in 1958 by local runner Bernard Baldwin, it is run over the five-kilometre route of Guto's first competitive race. The main race starts with a church service at [Llanwynno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanwynno), and then a wreath is laid on Guto's grave in [Llanwynno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanwynno) graveyard. After lighting a torch, it is carried to the nearby town of Mountain Ash, where the main race takes place.

The race consists of a double circuit of the town Centre, starting in Henry Street and ending in Oxford Street, by the commemorative statue of Guto. Traditionally, the race was timed to end at midnight, but in recent times it was rescheduled for the convenience of family entertainment, now concluding at around 9 pm.

This has resulted in a growth in size and scale, and the proceedings now start with an afternoon of street entertainment, and fun run races for children, concluding with the church service, elite runners' race, and presentations.

#### Canada



Fireworks at [Banff, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banff,_Alberta) 2015.

New Year's Eve traditions and celebrations in [Canada](5042916.md) vary regionally, but are typically similar to those in the United States, with a focus on social gatherings and public celebrations (such as concerts and fireworks displays).[^cite_note-188] The [Canadian men's junior hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_men's_national_junior_ice_hockey_team) has usually played their [final preliminary round game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Canada_New_Year's_Eve_Game) at the [IIHF World Junior Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_World_Junior_Championship) on New Year's Eve.[^cite_note-189]

The [CBC's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation) English- and French-language television networks have been well known for airing [sketch comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sketch_comedy) specials on New Year's Eve, lampooning the major events and news stories of the year. From 1992 through 2019, [CBC Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_Television) aired *Year of the Farce*, an annual special produced by the radio comedy troupe [Royal Canadian Air Farce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Farce)*.* The special became part of a weekly [*Royal Canadian Air Farce* television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Farce_\(TV_series\)) beginning in 1993, while the 2008 edition doubled as the program's [series finale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_finale). The CBC's French network [Ici Radio-Canada Télé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ici_Radio-Canada_T%C3%A9l%C3%A9) airs a similar special, *[Bye Bye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bye_Bye_\(TV_series\))*, which has been presented by various comedians and troupes, Originally running from 1968 to 1998, it was revived in 2006 by the Québécois troupe [Rock et Belles Oreilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_et_Belles_Oreilles) to mark its 25th anniversary.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195] The program has continued to air annually since, although it was placed on hiatus for 2009 after the previous year's edition was criticized by viewers for sketches they deemed to be derogatory and racist.[^cite_note-196] Four out of the five [highest-rated television broadcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_watched_television_broadcasts_in_Canada) in Quebecois history have been editions of *Bye Bye*, with the 2021 edition being seen by a record 4.862 million viewers.[^cite_note-197]

Beginning in 2017 (with the inaugural edition marking the beginning of the country's [sesquicentennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/150th_anniversary_of_Canada) year),[^cite_note-198] CBC Television has broadcast a more traditional countdown special: a localized version was broadcast for each [time zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Canada), which features music performances, and coverage of midnight festivities from communities across the country. [Rick Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Mercer) hosted the majority of these specials.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-Friend-2024-193] The special was placed on hiatus in 2023 due to financial issues (with the network airing the premiere of a [Mae Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Martin) stand-up comedy special in its place),[^cite_note-200] but revived in 2024 across CBC and [CBC News Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_News_Network).[^cite_note-Friend-2024-193]

#### Costa Rica

In [Costa Rica](5551.md), families usually gather around 8 pm for parties that last until 1 or 2 am, the next day. There are several traditions among Costa Rican families, including eating 12 grapes representing 12 wishes for the new year, and running across the street with luggage to bring new trips and adventures in the upcoming year.

#### El Salvador

In [El Salvador](9356.md), New Year's Eve is spent with families. Family parties start around 5\:00 pm, and last until 1\:00 to 3\:00 am, the following day. Families eat dinner together and sing traditional New Year's Eve songs, such as "Cinco para las Doce". After the dinner, individuals light fireworks and continue partying outside. A radio station broadcasts a countdown to midnight. When the clock strikes midnight, fireworks are lit across the country. Salvadorans start exchanging hugs and wishes for the new year.

The main event takes place at midnight where fireworks are lit along with thousands of life-size effigies called "Año Viejo". Almost every local family will either make such an effigy from scraps of paper and old clothes or buy one ready-made. The effigy is placed just outside the front of their home. Such effigies represent the things people hated about the departing year and are fashioned to resemble celebrities, politicians, public servants, cartoon characters etc. They are burnt on the stroke of midnight to banish the old year and mark a fresh start in the new. Some of the braver Salvadorans jump through these burning effigies 12 times to represent a wish for every month.

#### Guatemala

In [Guatemala](17238567.md), banks close on New Year's Eve, and businesses close at noon.[^cite_note-201] In the town of [Antigua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua_Guatemala), Guatemalans usually gather at the Santa Catalina Clock Arch to celebrate New Year's Eve ([Spanish](26825.md): *Fin del Año*). In [Guatemala City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_City) the celebrations are centered on Plaza Mayor. Firecrackers are lit starting at sundown, continuing without interruption into the night. Guatemalans wear new clothes for good fortune and eat a grape with each of the twelve chimes of the bell during the New Year countdown, while making a wish with each one.

The celebrations include religious themes which may be either [Mayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_mythology) or [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic).[^cite_note-202] Catholic celebrations are similar to those at Christmas. Gifts are left under the tree on Christmas morning by the Christ Child for the children, but parents and adults do not exchange gifts until New Year's Day.[^cite_note-203]

#### Mexico



Fireworks at [Angel of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_of_Independence) in [Mexico City](18987.md) 2013.

Mexicans celebrate New Year's Eve (Spanish: "Fin de Año" or "Nochevieja") observing many traditions, including the Spanish tradition of eating a grape with each of the twelve chimes of a clock's bell during the midnight countdown, while making a wish with each one. Mexican families decorate homes and parties in colors that represent wishes for the upcoming year: red encourages an overall improvement of lifestyle and love, yellow encourages blessings of improved employment conditions, green for improved financial circumstances, and white for improved health. Mexican sweet bread is baked with a coin or charm hidden in the dough. When the bread is served, the recipient of the slice with the coin or charm is said to be blessed with good luck in the New Year . Another tradition is to make a list of all the bad or unhappy events over the past 12 months; before midnight, this list is thrown into a fire, symbolizing the removal of negative energy from the new year.[^cite_note-204] At the same time, they are expressed for all the good things during the year that is ending so that they will continue in the new year.[^cite_note-205]

Mexicans celebrate with a late-night dinner with their families, the traditional meal being turkey or pork loin. Afterwards many Mexicans attend parties outside the home, for example, in night clubs. In [Mexico City](18987.md) the national street festival on New Year's Eve takes place on the [Zocalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zocalo), the city's main square.[^cite_note-206] Though in recent years, the main public celebration has taken place in [Paseo de la Reforma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paseo_de_la_Reforma), near the [Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_of_Independence).[^cite_note-207] After the twelfth chime, Mexicans will shout and wish each other a "¡Feliz Año Nuevo!" (*transl.*  Happy New Year!) and, in many places, celebrations also include fireworks, firecrackers and sparklers. Celebrations there are either Spanish in origin or those adding influences of Aztec nature.

#### Panama

In [Panama](22997.md), Panamanians usually celebrate New Year's Eve with a dinner, followed by multiple individual fireworks celebrations. Fireworks begin around 11 pm for parties that last until 1 am, the next day. Many Panamanians leave the city and go to the rural towns across the country, to celebrate with families and friends.

#### Trinidad and Tobago

In [Port of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Spain) the tradition is to celebrate in one's yard with friends, families and neighbors, and eat and drink till sunrise. At midnight the city becomes festive with fireworks in every direction. The celebration only starts at midnight. Music is heard from all the houses and bars, nightclubs, street parties, and Soca raves. Trinidadians and Tobagonians celebrate not only the new year but the beginning of the carnival season as well.

#### United States



The [Times Square Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Ball) descending during the final seconds of 2024.



Fireworks on the [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip) in 2013.



Fireworks on the [Space Needle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Needle) in [Seattle](11388236.md) in 2011.

In the [United States](3434750.md), New Year's Eve is celebrated via a variety of social gatherings, and large-scale public events such as concerts, fireworks shows, and "[drops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_objects_dropped_on_New_Year's_Eve)"—an event inspired by [time balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_ball) where an item is lowered or raised over the course of the final minute of the year.[^cite_note-Barrett-2015-208][^cite_note-mentalfloss-weirditems-209]

Drop events are typically patterned after the annual "[ball drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Ball)" held at [New York City](645042.md)'s [Times Square](31389.md), where an illuminated ball is lowered down a 21-metre-high (70 ft) pole on the roof of [One Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Times_Square). The event has been held since 1907; the ball itself—which is adorned with [Waterford Crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterford_Crystal) panels and an LED lighting system—has been displayed atop the building year-round since 2008.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211][^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-Nov-2025-212] Its most recent iteration, which debuted in 2026, is 12.5 feet (3.8 m) in diameter, weighs 5,600 kilograms (12,300 lb), and is adorned with over 5,000 circular panels.[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-Nov-2025-212] Drop events often use either a ball in imitation of Times Square, or items that represent local culture or history (such as [Atlanta](3138.md)'s [Peach Drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peach_Drop), which reflects [Georgia](48830.md)'s identity as the "[Peach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peach) State").[^cite_note-Barrett-2015-208][^cite_note-mentalfloss-weirditems-209]

New York City and Times Square serve as the focal point for national media coverage of the holiday.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214] Bandleader [Guy Lombardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Lombardo) and his [band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_band)—The Royal Canadians—were well known for their annual [broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_band_remote) from New York City. Their signature performance of "[Auld Lang Syne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auld_Lang_Syne)" at midnight helped make the [standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_pop) synonymous with the holiday in North America. Beginning on radio in 1929, Lombardo moved to [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) television from 1956 to 1976, adding coverage of the ball drop.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217] Following Lombardo's death, *[Dick Clark's New Year's Rockin' Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Clark's_New_Year's_Rockin'_Eve)* (which premiered for 1973 on [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC), and moved to [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) for 1975) became the dominant New Year's Eve [special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_special) on U.S. television—especially among younger viewers—with [Dick Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Clark) having anchored New Year's coverage (including *New Year's Rockin' Eve* and the one-off *[ABC 2000 Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_2000_Today)*) for 32 straight years. After Clark suffered a [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke) in December 2004, [Regis Philbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regis_Philbin) guest hosted the 2005 edition. Due to a lingering [speech impediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_disorder) brought upon by the stroke, Clark retired as host and was succeeded by [Ryan Seacrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Seacrest) for 2006 (who later served as the executive producer of the special), but continued making limited appearances on the special until his death in 2012.[^cite_note-218]

Other notable New Year's events are held in New York besides those in Times Square; since 1984, the [Cathedral of St. John the Divine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._John_the_Divine) in Manhattan has hosted the annual "New Year's Eve Concert for Peace", which was founded in 1984 by composer [Leonard Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Bernstein).[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220] The [New York Road Runners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Road_Runners) hosts a "Midnight Run" event at [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park), which features a fireworks show and a footrace around the park that begins at midnight.[^cite_note-nyt-fourcities-221]

Other notable celebrations include the [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip)'s "[America's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Party)", which consists of a ticketed concert event at the [Fremont Street Experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Street_Experience), and a public fireworks show at midnight that is launched from various casino resorts on the Strip.[^cite_note-222][^cite_note-223][^cite_note-nyt-fourcities-221] [Nashville](22018.md) has typically held concerts featuring [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music) performers and a [music note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_note) drop; since 2021, the festivities have been televised by CBS as part of its special *[New Year's Eve Live: Nashville's Big Bash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year's_Eve_Live:_Nashville's_Big_Bash)*.[^cite_note-224] [Los Angeles](18110.md), a city long without a major public New Year celebration, held an inaugural gathering in [Downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles)'s newly completed [Grand Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Park) to celebrate the arrival of 2014. The event included food trucks, art installations, and culminated with a [projection mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projection_mapping) show on the side of [Los Angeles City Hall](1221425.md) near midnight. The inaugural event drew over 25,000 spectators and participants.[^cite_note-225] For 2016, [Chicago](6886.md) introduced an event known as Chi-Town Rising.[^cite_note-226] In [Miami](53846.md), major celebrations are centered around the downtown core, including the raising of the "Big Orange" on the side of the [InterContinental Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterContinental_Miami) hotel, and concerts at [Bayfront Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayfront_Park) (which were televised as a New Year's special on [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company), *[Pitbull's New Year's Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitbull's_New_Year's_Revolution)*, until 2017–18).[^cite_note-227][^cite_note-228]

Major [theme parks](137327.md) also hold New Year's celebrations. [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Experiences) and [Universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Destinations_&_Experiences) theme parks, such as [Walt Disney World Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_World) and [Universal Orlando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Orlando) in [Orlando, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida); as well as [Disneyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland) and [Universal Studios Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Studios_Hollywood) in [California](5407.md), are traditionally the busiest around the Christmas and New Year's holidays.[^cite_note-nyt-fourcities-221][^cite_note-229]

### Oceania

#### Australia



Sydney's New Year's fireworks show in 2009.

Falling in summer in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere), New Year's Eve is often celebrated via public events including fireworks shows, exhibitions, and concerts, Many are concentrated towards the country's [beaches](43347504.md), and attract tourism from other parts of the world.[^cite_note-ABC_News-Jan=2024-230]

The most prominent celebration in the country is [Sydney New Year's Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_New_Year's_Eve), which takes place at [Sydney Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Harbour) and consists of two fireworks shows — the evening [Calling Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Country) Fireworks (formerly the "Family Fireworks")[^cite_note-231] held at 9\:00 p.m., followed by the main show at midnight. [Sydney Harbour Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Harbour_Bridge) is a focal point of the show, via pyrotechnics launched from the bridge, as well as lighting displays that illuminate it during the show—colloquially known as the "bridge effect", and previously taking the form of a symbol on its [trusses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truss) that reflected an annual theme.[^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235] The event is televised on major Australian networks including [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Broadcasting_Corporation).

Other major Australian cities also host fireworks events, including [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane)'s Lord Mayor's New Year's Eve Fireworks on the [Brisbane River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bank,_Queensland),[^cite_note-236] and [Melbourne](17306237.md) (which are launched from various locations in the city's [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_central_business_district), and have attracted upwards of 400,000 spectators), [Perth](24355.md), [Semaphore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semaphore,_South_Australia), and [Hobart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart).[^cite_note-ABC_News-Jan=2024-230][^cite_note-237] Celebrations have also been held in remote locations in the [Outback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outback): at [Cameron Corner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Corner,_Queensland)—a location near the border between New South Wales, Queensland, and South Australia—a pub has traditionally celebrated for all three of their respective [time zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Australia) in succession.[^cite_note-ABC_News-Jan=2024-230]

#### Kiribati

The parts of the [Line Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_Islands) belonging to [Kiribati](16674.md), including [Kiritimati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiritimati), [Tabuaeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabuaeran) and [Teraina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teraina), are the first parts of the world to welcome the New Year as they are on the furthermost time zone at [UTC+14\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+14\:00). Other Kiribati islands follow at [UTC+13\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+13\:00) and [UTC+12\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+12\:00).

#### New Zealand

Fireworks in Auckland, New Zealand for the 2023 New Year taking place on the Sky Tower

Fireworks in Auckland, New Zealand for the 2023 New Year taking place on the Sky Tower

Many of New Zealand's cities and towns see in the new year with open-air concerts and fireworks displays.

[Auckland](18660332.md) regularly has a fireworks display at midnight from the top of the [Sky Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Tower_\(Auckland\)). In [Wellington](33804.md), [Frank Kitts Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Kitts_Park) is the venue for a festival including fireworks, music, and open-air film displays. Similar events occur in Hamilton, starting with a family-friendly event at Steele Park, followed by an adult-specific party at [SkyCity Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyCity_Hamilton). [Gisborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisborne,_New_Zealand), one of the first cities in the world to see sunrise at new year[^cite_note-238] also celebrates with a new year festival. The small town of [Whangamata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whangamata), on the [Coromandel Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coromandel_Peninsula), is a major party venue in the new year, especially for Aucklanders.

In the South Island, both [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch) and [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin) host free live music concerts culminating with a midnight fireworks display. These are held at [Hagley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagley_Park,_Christchurch) and [The Octagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Octagon,_Dunedin) respectively. The South Island's main resort town, [Queenstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queenstown,_New_Zealand) is also a major new year party venue, with music and fireworks.

#### Samoa

Samoa was the first country to receive the New Year as a whole from 2011 to 2021, when it used [UTC+14\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+14\:00) as its time zone during the Southern Hemisphere summer, sharing it with the [Line Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_Islands) of [Kiribati](16674.md).

### South America

#### Argentina



The burning of dolls is a local tradition in the city of [La Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Plata).

Traditional celebrations in [Argentina](18951905.md) include a family dinner of traditional dishes, including *[vitel tonné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitello_tonnato)*, *[asado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asado)*, *[sandwiches de miga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandwiches_de_miga)*, *[piononos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pionono)*. Like dessert: *[turrón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turr%C3%B3n)*, *[mantecol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantecol)*, *[budín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bud%C3%ADn)* and *[pan dulce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mexican_dishes#Desserts_and_sweets)*.[^cite_note-239]

Just at midnight signalling the first day of the New Year, Argentines wish each other their regards and share toasts with their families, sometimes with the neighbours, with cider, champagne or alcohol. After it, some flock to the streets to enjoy and light firecrackers and fireworks, although each year it has been gradually decreasing due to higher awareness of the danger of it and the economic crisis. Parties often continue until dawn or the early morning.

Citizens in [La Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Plata) have a long tradition of making giant dolls, mostly of paper and wood, although sometimes also incorporating fireworks, which are burnt after the stroke of midnight.[^cite_note-240]

The celebration is during the summer, like in many South American countries, so many families in the New Year are seen at tourist centers of the Argentine Atlantic coast ([Mar del Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_del_Plata), [Mar de Ajó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_de_Aj%C3%B3), [Villa Gesell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Gesell), [Miramar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miramar,_Buenos_Aires_Province), etc.).[^cite_note-241]

#### Brazil



[Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) hosts one of the world's largest fireworks displays on [Copacabana New Year's Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year's_Eve_in_Copacabana), attracting over a million spectators.

In Brazil, Brazilians typically celebrate New Year's Eve ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Ano Novo or Réveillon*) at large parties hosted by restaurants and clubs; local traditions determine who opens a bottle of [Champagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champagne_\(wine\)) at midnight. People often wear colors with religious symbolism on New Year's Eve, such as white for good luck, yellow for good energies, happiness and money, red for love. Rituals such as the consumption of grapes, lychees and lentils also take place due to this mixture.[^cite_note-242]

The most prominent public celebration in Brazil is a fireworks display on [Copacabana Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copacabana,_Rio_de_Janeiro) in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), which is one of the world's largest - justified as much of the nation is south of the Equator, the celebrations are held in summer time. In 2017, it was estimated that the fireworks would attract over three million spectators to welcome 2018.[^cite_note-243] Beaches in major cities and tourist areas are crowded all day long especially for nighttime events. On television, the most prominent New Year's Eve special is [TV Globo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Globo)'s *[Show da Virada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_da_Virada?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, which features pre-recorded concert performances (usually filmed from a different Brazilian city annually), and live coverage of New Year's celebrations across the country.[^cite_note-244]

[Brasília](4752.md) holds a public celebration on the [Monumental Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumental_Axis) or [Estádio Nacional Mané Garrincha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Nacional_Man%C3%A9_Garrincha). Celebrations in [Manaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaus) are centered upon a fireworks display on the [Rio Negro Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Negro_Bridge), while [Paulista Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulista_Avenue) hosts the main celebration in [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_\(city\)), Brazil's largest city.[^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251]

Another notable New Year's Eve tradition in São Paulo is the [Saint Silvester Road Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Silvester_Road_Race), a [15K run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15K_run) through the city's [Central Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Zone_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo). Held annually since 1925, its route incorporates several major streets and landmarks, including the [Viaduto do Chá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viaduto_do_Ch%C3%A1) and Paulista Avenue.[^cite_note-252]

#### Chile



Over one million visitors crowd the streets and beaches of [Valparaíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valpara%C3%ADso) each New Year's Eve.

New Year's Eve is celebrated in [Chile](5489.md) by the observation of various traditions, such as wearing yellow underwear and watching fireworks. Chileans who want to travel walk the streets with a suitcase in hand, others hold money in their hand or place coins at their door for good fortune in the new year. Celebrations include a family dinner with special dishes, usually lentils for good luck, and twelve grapes to symbolize wishes for each month of the coming year.[^cite_note-253] Family celebrations usually last until midnight, then some continue partying with friends until dawn. In Chile's capital [Santiago](51572.md), thousands of Chileans gather at the [Entel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entel_Tower) to watch the countdown to midnight and a fireworks display, which is televised on major Chilean networks including [Canal 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_13_\(Chile\)).[^cite_note-254]

There are several fireworks shows across the country, and over one million spectators attend the most popular, the "Año Nuevo en el Mar", in [Valparaíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valpara%C3%ADso).[^cite_note-255] Since 2000, the sale of fireworks to individuals has been illegal,[^cite_note-256] meaning fireworks can now only be observed at fireworks displays during major events.

As in much of South America, New Year's Eve is spent by Chileans in beach visits in major tourist areas of the nation as it falls in the southern hemisphere summer period.

#### Colombia

In [Colombia](5222.md) it is a traditional celebration. There are many traditions across the country, including a family dinner with special dishes, fireworks, popular music, wearing special or new clothes, eating empanadas and the giving of parties of various kinds.

#### Ecuador

A New Year's Eve tradition in [Ecuador](9334.md) is for men to dress in drag, representing the "widows" of the past year. They dance in the streets and ask for a toll from drivers to pass.[^cite_note-257]

There are also traditional family events, meals, and modern celebrations such as hosting parties and going to nightclubs. Ecuadorians usually eat grapes and drink Champagne with close family members and friends.

#### Suriname

In [Suriname](26828.md), Surinamese Citizens goes into cities' commercial districts to watch fireworks shows on New Year's Eve. It is a spectacle based on the famous red-firecracker-ribbons. The bigger stores invest in these firecrackers and display them in the streets. Every year the length of them is compared, and high praises are held for the company that has managed to import the largest ribbon. These celebrations start at 10 am and finish the next day. When the night starts, the big street parties are already at full capacity. The parties stop between 10 and 11 pm after which the people go home to light their *pagaras* (red-firecracker-ribbons) at midnight. After midnight, the parties continue and the streets fill again until daybreak.

#### Uruguay

In Uruguay, traditional celebrations begin at nightfall on New Year's Eve (31 December), with family gatherings in which *[asado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asado)* and *[lechon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lechon)* are usually eaten, as well as *[turrón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turr%C3%B3n)* and *[pan dulce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_dulce)* as desserts.[^cite_note-258] People usually wear white as it symbolizes optimism and purity.[^cite_note-259] At the stroke of midnight, Uruguayans flock to the streets to enjoy fireworks and light firecrackers, and to eat [Twelve Grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Grapes).[^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261]

Due to the fact that Uruguay lies in the Southern Hemisphere, the New Year is celebrated in summer, so resort cities such as [Punta del Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_del_Este) are filled with Uruguayans and foreign tourists, including celebrities from the region, to attend parties and festivals of music, fireworks, and light shows on the beach.[^cite_note-infobae-2018-262][^cite_note-263]

In the [Old City of Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Vieja,_Montevideo), a district where a large number of office buildings are concentrated, employees, prior to the end of the last working day of the year, throw torn daybooks and calendars through the windows, causing a "paper rain", which adds to the buckets of water that are thrown from the balconies.[^cite_note-264] In the [Mercado del Puerto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercado_del_Puerto) there is a street party with a massive "cider fight" accompanied by music.[^cite_note-infobae-2018-262] Every 30 December since the first half of the 20th century, the *Gordo de Fin de Año* ([Spanish](26825.md) for 'End-of-Year Lottery Jackpot')[^cite_note-265] is drawn, a special lottery draw run by Uruguay's state-owned National Lottery Directorate.[^cite_note-266]

#### Venezuela

Radio specials give a countdown and announce the New Year. In [Caracas](55289.md), the bells of the Cathedral of Caracas ring twelve times.[^cite_note-aljazeera-2017-123] During these special programs, is a tradition to broadcast songs about the end of the year. It is a non-working holiday. Popular songs include "Viejo año" ("Old year"), by [Gaita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaita_Zuliana) group [Maracaibo 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracaibo_15), and "Cinco pa' las 12" ("Five minutes before twelve"), which was versioned by several popular singers including Nestor Zavarce, Nancy Ramos and [José Luis Rodríguez El Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Rodr%C3%ADguez_El_Puma). The unofficial hymn for the first minutes of the New Year is "Año Nuevo, Vida Nueva" ("New Year, New Life"), by the band Billo's Caracas Boys. Venezuelans play the national anthem in their houses.

Traditions include wearing yellow underwear, eating [Pan de jamón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_de_jam%C3%B3n), and 12 grapes with sparkling wine.

Special holiday programs are broadcast on Venezuelan television stations including [Venevisión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venevisi%C3%B3n) and [Venezolana de Televisión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezolana_de_Televisi%C3%B3n), which airs the principal national event from Caracas' Bolivar Square featuring major stars.

## Religious observances



A [Watchnight Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchnight_service) at a Lutheran Christian church on New Year's Eve (2014)

Many Christian congregations have [watchnight services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchnight_service) on New Year's Eve, which is the seventh day of [Christmastide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmastide). The Watch Night service (or Watch Night Mass if Holy Communion is celebrated) is part of the tradition of various denominations in Christianity, especially [Moravians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Church) and [Methodists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist), as well as congregations populated by certain ethnic communities, such as in the Korean community and [African American community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_church), in which the faithful congregate in services continuing past midnight, giving thanks for the blessings of the outgoing year and praying for divine favor during the upcoming year. In the English-speaking world, Watch Night services can be traced back to [John Wesley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wesley), the founder of Methodism,[^cite_note-267] who learned the custom from the [Moravian Brethren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Brethren) who came to England in the 1730s. Moravian congregations still observe the Watch Night service on New Year's Eve. Watch Night services took on special significance to African Americans on New Year's Eve 1862, as slaves anticipated the arrival of 1 January 1863, when the [Emancipation Proclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_Proclamation) became effective.[^cite_note-268]

With Christianity, in the [Roman Catholic Church](606848.md), [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md), [Lutheran Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church), and the [Anglican Communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Communion), 1 January is observed as the [Feast of the Circumcision of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Circumcision_of_Christ), and specifically within Roman Catholicism, as the [Solemnity of Mary, Mother of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solemnity_of_Mary,_Mother_of_God); it is a [Holy Day of Obligation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Day_of_Obligation) in most countries ([Australia](4689264.md) being a notable exception), thus the Church requires the attendance of all Catholics in such countries for [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)) that day. However a vigil Mass may be held on the evening before a Holy Day; thus it has become customary to also have Mass on the night of New Year's Eve (which are often referred to as Watchnight Masses).[^cite_note-269] New Year's Eve is a [feast day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvester) honoring [Pope Sylvester I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_I) in the Roman Catholic calendar ([Saint Sylvester's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sylvester's_Day)).[^cite_note-270] The Catholic Church grants a plenary indulgence, under the usual conditions, to those who recite the [Te Deum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Deum) in public on New Year's Eve, which is usually done prior to the celebration of Mass.[^cite_note-271] In [Vatican City](32408.md), on 31 December, the [Pope](23056.md) usually performs a solemn service of Vespers with recitation of the Te Deum in [St. Peter's Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Basilica). After the service, he usually goes out from the basilica into [St. Peter's Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Square) to greet the faithful and visit the Nativity scene on the square.[^cite_note-272]
