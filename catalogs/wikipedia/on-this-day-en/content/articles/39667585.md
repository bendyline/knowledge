**Lebanese Maronite Christians** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): المسيحية المارونية في لبنان; [Classical Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Syriac_language): ܡܫܝܚܝ̈ܐ ܡܪ̈ܘܢܝܐ ܕܠܒܢܢ) refers to [Lebanese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people) who are members of the [Maronite Church](66391078.md) in [Lebanon](17771.md), the largest [Christian body](4796145.md) in the country.[^cite_note-freedom-3] The Lebanese Maronite population is concentrated mainly in [Mount Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon) and East [Beirut](37428.md).[^cite_note-\:102-4] They are believed to constitute about 22% of the total population of Lebanon.[^cite_note-freedom-3]

The [Maronites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronites) and the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) founded modern [Lebanon](17771.md) in the early eighteenth century through the ruling and social system known as the "[Maronite–Druze dualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_and_Druze)."[^cite_note-5] The [1860 Druze–Maronite conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1860_civil_conflict_in_Mount_Lebanon_and_Damascus) led to the establishment of [Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon_Mutasarrifate), an autonomous entity within the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) dominated by Maronites and protected by European powers. In the aftermath of the [First World War](4764461.md), the Maronites successfully campaigned for [Greater Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Lebanon) carved out from Mount Lebanon and neighboring areas. Under the [French Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon), and until the end of the [Second World War](32927.md), the Maronites gained substantial influence. Post-independence, they dominated Lebanese politics until the [1975–1990 civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War), which ended their supremacy. While the [Taif Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taif_Agreement) weakened Maronite influence, it endures alongside other dominant Lebanese communities, such as the [Shiites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims) and [Sunnis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Sunni_Muslims).[^cite_note-\:102-4]

Lebanon's constitution was intended to guarantee political representation for each of the nation's religious groups.[^cite_note-Incorporated2009-6] Under the terms of an unwritten agreement known as the [National Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Pact) between the various political and religious leaders of Lebanon, the [president of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Lebanon) must be a Maronite.[^cite_note-7]

## History

[^cite_note-Deeb_2013-8]

The cultural and linguistic heritage of the Lebanese people is a blend of both indigenous [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicianism) elements and the foreign cultures that have come to rule the land and its people over the course of thousands of years. In a 2013 interview the lead investigator of the [National Geographic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Society)'s [Genographic Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genographic_Project), [Pierre Zalloua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Zalloua), pointed out that genetic variation preceded religious variation and divisions: "Lebanon already had well-differentiated communities with their own genetic peculiarities, but not significant differences, and religions came as layers of paint on top. There is no distinct pattern that shows that one community carries significantly more [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) than another."[^cite_note-9]

The Maronite population in Lebanon has a rich history. Its foundation can be traced to early followers of [Maron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maron), who migrated from the region of [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch) to Mount Lebanon. Historically, Lebanese Maronites resided in remote mountain villages and were led by influential noble families.[^cite_note-\:102-4]

The followers of [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) first became known as "Christians" in the ancient Greek city of [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch) (Acts 11\:26), and the city became a center for Christianity [–](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_and_Druze) especially after the [destruction of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_\(70\)) in AD 70. According to Roman Catholic tradition, the first Bishop was [Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter) before his travels to Rome. The third Bishop was the [Apostolic Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Father) [Ignatius of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignatius_of_Antioch). Antioch became one of the five original [Patriarchates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate) (the [Pentarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentarchy)) after Roman Emperor [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I_\(emperor\)) recognized Christianity.

The Maronite Christianity derived its name and religious identity from Saint [Maron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maron) whose followers migrated to the area of [Mount Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon) (present day [Republic of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Lebanon)) from their previous location of residence around the area of [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch) (an ancient Greek city within present day [Hatay Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatay_Province), Turkey), establishing the nucleus of the [Maronite Church](66391078.md).[^cite_note-Mannheim-10]

More specifically, Maron, a fourth-century [monk](419369.md) and the contemporary and friend of [St. John Chrysostom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John_Chrysostom), left Antioch for the [Orontes River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontes_River) to lead an [ascetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascetic) life, following the traditions of [Anthony the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_the_Great) of the Desert and [Pachomius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachomius). Many of his followers also lived a monastic lifestyle. Following the death of Maron in 410, his disciples built a monastery in his memory and formed the nucleus of the Maronite Church.

The Maronites held fast to the beliefs of the [Council of Chalcedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon) in 451. When the [Monophysites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monophysites) of Antioch slew 350 monks, the Maronites sought refuge in the mountains of Lebanon. Correspondence concerning the event brought the Maronites papal and orthodox recognition, which was solidified by [Pope Hormisdas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Hormisdas) (514–523) on 10 February 518. A monastery was built around the shrine of St. Maron (Marun) after the [Council of Chalcedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon).[^cite_note-11]

The martyrdom of the [Patriarch of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch) in the first decade of the seventh century, either at the hands of [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) soldiers or local Jews,[^cite_note-12] left the Maronites without a leader, a situation which continued because of the final and most devastating [Byzantine–Sassanid War of 602–628](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Sassanid_War_of_602%E2%80%93628). In the aftermath of the war, the Roman Emperor [Heraclius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraclius) propagated a new Christological doctrine in an attempt to unify the various Christian churches of the east, who were divided over accepting the [Council of Chalcedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon). This doctrine, [monothelitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monothelitism), was meant as a compromise between supporters of Chalcedon, such as the Maronites, and opponents, such as the [Jacobites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Church). To win back the Monophysites, [Monoenergism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoenergism) was first advocated by [Patriarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople) [Sergius I of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergius_I_of_Constantinople). [Pope Honorius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Honorius_I) (625–638) of Rome naively called for an end to dispute and interpreted Sergius' view as true since Christ exhibited only one will insofar as His sinless human nature will never disagree with His divine will.

Instead, the [Patriarch of Constantinople's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople) doctrine and subsequent [Monothelitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monothelitism) caused greater controversy and was declared a heresy at the [Sixth Ecumenical Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Ecumenical_Council) in 680–681. Contemporary Greek, Latin and Arab sources indicate that the Maronites accepted monothelitism, rejected the sixth council, and continued to maintain a belief in the largely discredited monothelite doctrine for centuries, only moving away from monothelitism in the time of the crusades in order to avoid being branded heretics by the crusaders.[^cite_note-13] The modern Maronite Church, however, rejects the assertion that the Maronites were ever monothelites separated from the rest of the universal Church. The question remains a cause of significant offence to this day.[^cite_note-14]

In 687, the Roman Emperor [Justinian II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_II) agreed to evacuate many thousand Maronites from Lebanon and settle them elsewhere. The chaos and utter depression which followed led the Maronites to elect their first Patriarch, [John Maroun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maroun), that year. This, however, was seen as a usurpation by the then undivided Orthodox Catholic church. Thus, at a time when Islam was rising on the borders of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and a united front was necessary to keep out Islamic infiltration, the Maronites were focused on a struggle to retain their independence against imperial power. This situation was mirrored in other Christian communities in the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and helped facilitate the Muslim conquest of most of Eastern [Christendom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christendom) by the end of the century.

In 1858, there was a popular movement dubbed the "Kisrawan Uprising", aimed at reform of social order in a predominantly Maronite district, among Maronite Christians.[^cite_note-\:0-15] This uprising was spurred by the [Tanzimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzimat), wealth and material inequality and a unfair division of land between the notables and "common" people.[^cite_note-\:0-15]

The relationship between the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) and [Christians](18337522.md) has been characterized by [harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony) and peaceful [coexistence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurinationalism),[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] with amicable relations between the two groups prevailing throughout history, with the exception of some periods, including [1860 Mount Lebanon civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1860_Mount_Lebanon_civil_war).[^cite_note-google-20][^cite_note-21] The Maronite Catholics and the Druze founded modern Lebanon in the early eighteenth century, through a governing and social system known as the "[Maronite-Druze dualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_and_Druze)" in the [Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon_Mutasarrifate).[^cite_note-Deeb_2013-8]

## Religion

Main article: [Maronite Church](66391078.md)

The Maronites belong to the Maronite [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Christianity) Church of Antioch in [Hatay Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatay_Province), Turkey), an [Eastern Catholic](214903.md) Syriac Church that has affirmed its communion with [Rome](13393.md) since 1180. The official view of the Church is that it had never accepted either the [Monophysitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monophysitism) views held by their Syriac neighbours, which were condemned in the [Council of Chalcedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon), or the failed compromise doctrine of [Monothelitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monothelitism) (the latter claim being found in contemporary sources).[^cite_note-Moosa-22] The Maronite Patriarch is traditionally seated in [Bkerke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bkerke), north of Beirut.

## Geographic distribution within Lebanon

&#x20;Lebanese Maronite Christians are concentrated in the north of Beirut, northern part of [Mount Lebanon Governorate](3028437.md), southern part of [North Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Governorate), parts of [Beqaa Governorate](866204.md) and [South Governorate](1283570.md).[^cite_note-23]

The last Census in Lebanon in 1932 put the numbers of Maronites at 29%.[^cite_note-Central_Intelligence_Agency-24] A study done by the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) (CIA) in 1985 put the numbers of Maronites at 16% of the population.[^cite_note-Central_Intelligence_Agency-24] Given that the last census was in 1932, percentages are estimates only since it is difficult to have precise population estimates.

In 2012, Maronites constituted 31% of [Lebanon](17771.md)'s population, according to estimates.[^cite_note-2012Report-25] The Maronite Church's website claims 1,062,000 members were in Lebanon in 1994 which would have made them around 31% of Lebanon's population.[^cite_note-maronite-heritage-26] Maronite Catholics are the largest Christian group, followed by Greek Orthodox.[^cite_note-27]

In 2014, according to electoral data,[^cite_note-28] Maronites made up 19.97% of registered voters aged 21 and above. In 2022,[^cite_note-today.lorientlejour.com-29] based on the registered voters aged 21+ for the elections, Druze represented 19.94% of the global Lebanese population. The overall proportions remained stable between 2014 and 2022, with a slight decrease in this group.
