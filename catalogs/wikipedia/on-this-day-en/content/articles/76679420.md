[China](5405.md) is the world's largest exporter, a position it has maintained continuously since 2010. It is the largest trading partner of over 120 countries, as of at least early 2024. As a member of the [Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Comprehensive_Economic_Partnership) (RCEP), China is part of the world's largest trading bloc.&#x20;

China began promoting overseas investment through the "[Go Out policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_Out_policy)", which [General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Jiang Zemin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiang_Zemin) formally announced as a national strategy in 2000. After its 2001 entry into the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) (WTO), China focused on export-led growth and became a major link in global supply chains. Through this process, China developed large trade surpluses and foreign currency reserves.

## History

See also: [History of trade of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_trade_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China)

China resumed status as a member of the [General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Agreement_on_Tariffs_and_Trade) (GATT) in 1987.[^cite_note-\:025-1]<sup>: 163 </sup>

During [Jiang Zemin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiang_Zemin)'s tenure, China adopted policies to expand foreign trade and economic relations with other countries through the Maritime Commerce Law (1993), Anti-Subsidy Rules (1997), and 2001 revisions to the Foreign Investment Law.[^cite_note-\:025-1]<sup>: 190 </sup> In addition to Jiang, top officials like [Zhu Rongji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Rongji) also prioritized integrating China more deeply into the global trading system.[^cite_note-2]<sup>: 42 </sup>

In 1999, the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP) began promoting investment abroad through the [Go Out policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_Out_policy)[^cite_note-3] and CCP General Secretary Jiang Zemin formally announced it as a national strategy in March 2000.[^cite_note-\:32-4]<sup>: 56 </sup> The policy was implemented top-down from central government leadership.[^cite_note-\:13-5]<sup>: 123 </sup> During Jiang's tenure, the policy greatly expanded China's investment and influence in [global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_North_and_Global_South) countries, especially those in Africa and Asia.[^cite_note-\:13-5]<sup>: 124 </sup>

After its entry into the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) (WTO) in 2001, China began pursuing export-led growth and became a key link in global supply chains.[^cite_note-\:02-6]<sup>: 235–236 </sup> China formed the policy-based insurer [Sinosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinosure) to support the exports and overseas business of domestic companies.[^cite_note-\:Chen2-7]<sup>: 77–78 </sup> Chinese businesses were encouraged to trade directly with foreign companies (instead of working through SOEs as previously). with the exception of certain state monopoly sectors deemed critical to national security.[^cite_note-\:024-8]<sup>: 39 </sup> China's industrious and cheap labor also proved attractive to foreign investments.[^cite_note-\:02-6]<sup>: 235–236 </sup> China's share of the global trade surplus increased rapidly after it joined the WTO.[^cite_note-\:622-9]<sup>: 274 </sup> The country accumulated large trade surpluses and [foreign currency reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign-exchange_reserves_of_China), which greatly increased government resources.[^cite_note-\:02-6]<sup>: 235–236 </sup>

In 2016, China joined the Transports Internationaux Routiers Convention ([TIR Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TIR_Convention)), a multilateral treaty reducing administrative boundaries for international transportations of goods in customs-sealed containers.[^cite_note-\:0-10]<sup>: 149 </sup> The majority of TIR members also participate in China's [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative) and membership significantly facilitates China's trade.[^cite_note-\:0-10]<sup>: 149 </sup>

China is a member of the [Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Comprehensive_Economic_Partnership) (RCEP).[^cite_note-\:0-10]<sup>: 148 </sup> RCEP is the first trade agreement to include all three of China, Japan, and South Korea.[^cite_note-\:Ma\&Kang-11]<sup>: 176 </sup> In January 2022, RCEP became the world's largest [trade bloc](307255.md) (in economic terms) and continues to be the world's largest as of at least early 2024.[^cite_note-\:0-10]<sup>: 148 </sup>

China provides [foreign aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_foreign_aid) to advance both foreign trade objectives and [foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_China) objectives.[^cite_note-\:Chen-12]<sup>: 18 </sup> Since the 2018 creation of the [China International Development Cooperation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_International_Development_Cooperation_Agency) to coordinate aid, China has placed a lesser emphasis on aid to advance foreign trade and a greater emphasis on aid to advance foreign policy.[^cite_note-\:Chen-12]<sup>: 18 </sup>

In 2020, China signed major free trade agreements with the [European Union](9317.md) as well as fifteen different Asia-Pacific countries.[^cite_note-\:22-13]<sup>: 259 </sup> As of at least 2023, China is the world's largest exporter, a status it has maintained continuously since 2010.[^cite_note-\:102-14]<sup>: 88 </sup>

Effective 1 December 2024, China eliminated tariffs for goods imported from all of the countries that the [United Nations](31769.md) categorizes as [least developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_developed_countries) and with which China has [diplomatic relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_China).[^cite_note-\:1-15] Thirty-three of the countries benefiting from the agreement are in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa%E2%80%93China_economic_relations) and the non-African countries receiving zero tariff treatment are [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Yemen_relations), [Kiribati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Kiribati_relations), the [Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Solomon_Islands_relations), [Afghanistan](25396380.md), [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh%E2%80%93China_relations), [Cambodia](17997245.md), [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Laos_relations), [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Myanmar_relations), [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Nepal_relations), and [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93East_Timor_relations).[^cite_note-\:1-15]

## Impact

As of at least early 2024, China is the largest trading partner of over 120 countries.[^cite_note-\:0-10]<sup>: 147 </sup> Given the scale of China and its economy, the country's impact on international trade flow is major.[^cite_note-\:622-9]<sup>: 272 </sup> As of at least 2024, the global [current account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_account_\(balance_of_payments\)) surplus is largely composed of China, Europe, and the Middle East.[^cite_note-\:622-9]<sup>: 274 </sup>
