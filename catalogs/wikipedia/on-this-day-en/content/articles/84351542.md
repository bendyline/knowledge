On 27 September 2026, five men were arrested by [Gloucestershire Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire_Constabulary) in [Whelford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whelford), a village near [RAF Fairford](475013.md) in [Gloucestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire), England. RAF Fairford is a [Royal Air Force](25679.md) station used by the [United States Air Force](32090.md). The men were held on suspicion of offences under the [Explosives Act 1875](40883456.md) and the [Terrorism Act 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_Act_2006).

The police said the arrest followed a report of three suspicious vehicles travelling towards the base.[^cite_note-\:1-1] A [major incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_incident) was declared and about 85 households were evacuated while [British Army](4887.md) bomb disposal specialists examined the vehicles.[^cite_note-\:2-2] The next day, the head of [Counter Terrorism Policing](54350607.md) announced that the five suspects had all been released on bail.

## Background

RAF Fairford is a [Royal Air Force](25679.md) station that [hosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_in_the_United_Kingdom) [United States Air Force](32090.md) units, including heavy bombers.[^cite_note-\:5-3] During the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), the base was used by the US Forces for operations against Iran, with the UK government granting permission in March 2026 for strikes on Iranian missile sites.[^cite_note-\:6-4]

In the weeks prior to the incident there had been increased security at the airfield, including heavy vehicles and concrete blocks positioned across some airfield gates, and increased police activity around the airfield.[^cite_note-guardian-20260927-5][^cite_note-guardian-20260929a-6] *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)* reported that residents near RAF Fairford said they were told to stay indoors the evening before the incident, hours before police received the call about the alleged bomb plot, which the newspaper verified by viewing messages from a resident's [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp) group chat, including one resident who claimed armed police told them the police had intelligence of an imminent attack.[^cite_note-independent-20260928-7]

## Incident

At approximately 00\:45 [BST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Summer_Time) ([UTC+01\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+01\:00)) on 27 September 2026, police received a call concerning suspicious activity in [Whelford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whelford). Just after 01\:30 BST police received a second call from a member of the public concerning three vehicles and a group men acting suspiciously. Armed officers arrested five men in Whelford, about 2 miles (3.2 km) from the base, on suspicion of offences under the [Explosives Act 1875](40883456.md), and they were further arrested on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act under section 5 of the [Terrorism Act 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_Act_2006).[^cite_note-\:1-1][^cite_note-\:2-2][^cite_note-\:3-8]

Police established a 400-metre (1,300 ft) cordon around the vehicles, which were examined by [British Army](4887.md) [explosive ordnance disposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_ordnance_disposal#United_Kingdom) personnel.[^cite_note-\:5-3] Residents of about 85 homes were evacuated to a leisure centre in [Cirencester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirencester).[^cite_note-\:7-9] [CNN](62028.md) reporters near the perimeter fence heard what sounded like two controlled detonations from the direction of Whelford.[^cite_note-\:6-4]

## Investigation

On 27 September, Deputy Assistant Commissioner Vicki Evans of [Counter Terrorism Policing](54350607.md) said the investigation was at an early stage and that the five men remain in custody.[^cite_note-\:1-1] [ITV News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_News) reported that the operation was not intelligence-led, so investigators had not known of a potential plot in advance. One line of inquiry was whether representatives of the Iranian government had paid others to carry it out.[^cite_note-\:4-10][^cite_note-\:7-9][^cite_note-\:8-11]

On 28 September, the head of Counter Terrorism Policing, Assistant Commissioner [Laurence Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Taylor), announced that the five suspects had all been released on bail, saying "To be very clear, this does not mean the investigation is over, they are subject to stringent conditions on their movement and contact with others. They absolutely remain under investigation as we explore multiple lines of inquiry."[^cite_note-guardian-20260928-12][^cite_note-ctp-20260928-13] *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that the possible involvement of the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) was an active line of investigation.[^cite_note-14] *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* reported that no explosives had been found,[^cite_note-times-20260928-15] and *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported that the materials found inside the vans were not viable explosives.[^cite_note-guardian-20260929a-6]

The five men arrested were all [British nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_national) and London residents, aged 23–25.[^cite_note-CTP_update_28-16][^cite_note-17] [BBC News](1139893.md) reported the men had been held under the [Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_and_Criminal_Evidence_Act_1984), not the Terrorism Act, meaning they could not have been held for up to 14 days without charge.[^cite_note-bbc-20260929-18]

On 29 September, Taylor said no explosive devices had been found in the vans after extensive examination, though some petrol, described as an "accelerant", had been recovered.[^cite_note-telegraph-20260929-19][^cite_note-bbc-20260929-18] The vans were marked with a logo of a fuel transit company which denied ownership of the vehicles.[^cite_note-guardian-20260929b-20]

On 30 September, it was reported that one of the suspects had called the police himself at 00\:45 BST, almost an hour before the first member of the public reported having seen suspicious activity to the police.[^cite_note-bbc-20260930-21] A call to police was made by a local farmer, just after 01\:30 BST, after seeing three vans parked in a "weird formation" blocking the road, and "a large group of hooded and masked men" nearby who started to "panic" when they saw her.[^cite_note-22] *The Times* reported that authorities were already on the scene when the second call was made.[^cite_note-23]

On 1 October, counter-terror police said that two properties in [Westminster](37432.md), London had been searched and that a 25-year-old UK-Iranian dual national had been arrested on suspicion of preparation of terrorist acts.[^cite_note-24]

## Reactions

Prime Minister [Andy Burnham](414818.md) convened [COBRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Office_Briefing_Rooms) on 28 September,[^cite_note-guardian-20260928-12] said the incident would be deeply concerning for local residents, and urged the public not to speculate.[^cite_note-\:5-3] Defence Secretary [Wes Streeting](17169295.md) thanked emergency and specialist teams and said the situation was being dealt with.[^cite_note-\:7-9] US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) said American officials had been working with the British and that "We had them under investigation. They were looking to do big ⁠damage to our fort".[^cite_note-25] While he did not name Iran as the perpetrators, saying he has to be "careful" about what he says, [US Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_secretary_of_State) [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) did state on *[Fox News](11121.md)* that the incident "clearly involves the hand of a foreign actor".[^cite_note-Rubio-26]

## Timeline of events

Timeline of RAF Fairford incident and the investigation:[^cite_note-Timeline-27]

27 September

- 00\:45 [BST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Summer_Time) ([UTC+01\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+01\:00)) – First [999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/999_\(emergency_telephone_number\)) call, [Gloucestershire Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire_Constabulary) say they received a emergency call on Sunday 27 September about "three suspicious vehicles" that appeared to be travelling towards RAF Fairford.

  [^cite_note-Timeline-27]

  &#x20;The BBC reported that one of the five men who was later arrested had made the call himself.

  [^cite_note-28]

- 01\:36 BST – Second 999 call, a farmer living close to the base and returning home called 999 after seeing 3 vans parked in a "weird formation", she also reported seeing "a large group of hooded and masked men" near the vans who started to "panic" when they saw her. The woman told the BBC that she initially immediately called the [Ministry of Defence Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_Police) but had not received an answer.

  [^cite_note-29]

- Shortly afterwards (exact time is unknown) – Gloucestershire Constabulary arrested five men in the Whelford area. They were arrested on suspicion of committing offences under the [Explosives Act 1875](40883456.md).

- 08\:00 BST – Gloucestershire Constabulary declare a major incident, and set up a 400 metres (1,300 ft) cordon around the three vehicles. They also call the Army's Explosive Ordnance Disposal unit and evacuate 85 households in Whelford.

- 13\:50 BST – Counter Terrorism Policing take over the investigation from Gloucestershire Constabulary and further arrest the five men on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act.

- \~16\:00 BST – US President Donald Trump tells reporters the arrest of the five suspects was "fantastic" and "an amazing job" he went on to say "We had them under investigation. They were looking to ‌do big ⁠damage to our fort and working with the British worked out great" "We had them under view for a long time, and we got them."

28 September

- 12\:30 BST – Counter Terrorism Policing reveal that the five men were aged between 23 and 25 and all from various areas of London. They told media that they were being held under the [Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_and_Criminal_Evidence_Act_1984) and that a custody extension has been secured by a judge.

- 12\:30 BST – All five suspects were released on bail, Counter Terrorism Policing stated that the decision was "grounded in experience and careful consideration of policing powers".

- Monday afternoon – Prime Minister Andy Burnham called an emergency COBRA meeting.

  [^cite_note-guardian-20260928-12]

- Monday evening – US Secretary of State Marco Rubio told Fox News that the incident "clearly involves the hands of a foreign actor".

  [^cite_note-Rubio-26]

29 September

- Tuesday morning – It was learned by media that one of the men who had been arrested had made the first 999 call to police. It remains unclear why the man called police, or what he told them.

- 18\:00 BST – Counter Terrorism Policing gave a media updated saying that properties in London had been searched as part of the investigation. They also said that the three vehicles had undergone an "extensive examination" with the support of military and forensic experts. They said that no improvised explosive devices were found in the vans, but a "quantity of petrol" was recovered from them.

30 September

- Gloucestershire Police removed the cordon and reopened the scene.

  [^cite_note-Timeline-27]

- 17\:00 BST – Andy Burnham told the BBC that "There are strong indications that Iran played a part in what happened over the weekend at RAF Fairford",

  [^cite_note-30]

  &#x20;earlier in the week Iran's embassy in ‌London rejected what it described as "unfounded and malicious ​speculations" linking Tehran to the incident.

  [^cite_note-31]
