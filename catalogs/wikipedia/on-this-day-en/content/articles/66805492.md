A **member of Parliament** ([post-nominal letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-nominal_letters): **MP**; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *député*, [\[depyte\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)) is an elected politician who serves in the [House of Commons of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada), the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house) of the [Parliament of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada).

## Terminology

The term's primary use is in reference to the members of the House of Commons. In legislation, it can also refer to the members of the [Senate of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Canada), but in common usage, the title *senator* (French: *sénateur (masculine), sénatrice (feminine)*) is typically used. By contrast, no such alternate title exists for members of the House of Commons. A less ambiguous term for members of both chambers is *parliamentarian*.[^cite_note-1]

MPs each represent an individual [electoral district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_\(Canada\)), also known as a *constituency* or *riding*. MPs are elected using the [first-past-the-post system](261709.md) in a [general election or by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Canada), usually held every four years or less. In contrast, the 105 members of the Senate are appointed by the Crown on the advice of the [prime minister](24135.md).

## Representation

Seats are distributed among the [provinces](75763.md) in proportion to population, as determined by each decennial census, subject to the following exceptions made by the [Constitution of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada). Firstly, the "Senate floor" guarantees that each province will have at least as many elected MPs as senators.[^cite_note-\:0-2] Secondly, the "grandfather clause" guarantees each province has at least as many seats now as it had allocated in the 1985 *[Representation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_Act_\(Canada\))*.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

Following the [2022 Canadian federal electoral redistribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Canadian_federal_electoral_redistribution), the number of members is 343.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Oath and affirmation

&#x20;The oath for members of Parliament has stood the same since [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation); according to Section IX.128 of the *[Constitution Act, 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Act,_1867)*: "Every member of the Senate and the House of Commons of Canada shall before taking his Seat therein take and subscribe before the Governor General or some Person authorized by him, and every Member of a Legislative Council or Legislative Assembly of any Province shall before the Lieutenant Governor of the Province or some Person authorized by him, the Oath of Allegiance contained in the Fifth Schedule to the Act."[^cite_note-S128-5] The oath set out in said schedule is: *I, \[name], do swear, that I will be faithful and bear true Allegiance to Her Majesty Queen Victoria*, with the further instruction that "the name of the King or Queen of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland for the Time being is to be substituted from Time to Time, with Proper Terms of Reference thereto." The oath reads as follows:

> *I, \[name], do swear, that I will be faithful and bear true allegiance to His Majesty King Charles III.*[^cite_note-SV-6]

&#x20;Or in French:

> *Je, \[nom], jure que je serai fidèle et porterai une vraie allégeance à Sa Majesté le Roi Charles III.*[^cite_note-MLC-7]

For those parliamentarians whose religion prohibits the swearing of oaths, there exists a compromise affirmation, first instituted in 1905:

> *I, \[name], do solemnly, sincerely and truly affirm and declare the taking of an oath is according to my religious belief unlawful, and I do also solemnly, sincerely and truly affirm and declare that I will be faithful and bear true allegiance to His Majesty King Charles III.*[^cite_note-8]

## Privileges

Parliamentarians enjoy [parliamentary privilege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_privilege), as derived from [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law).[^cite_note-9]

In 2024, the annual salary of each MP was CA\$203,100. Members may receive additional sums by virtue of other positions or functions they hold, such as that of Prime Minister, [Speaker of the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_House_of_Commons_\(Canada\)) or a [minister of the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_the_Crown).[^cite_note-10]
