The **Iranian diaspora** (collectively known as **Iranian expats** or **expatriates**) is the global population of [Iranian citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Iran) or people of Iranian descent living outside [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-2]

While Iranian migration has occurred historically, a significant wave of Iranians fled Iran after the [1979 Islamic Revolution](347268.md) due to [political persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_executions_of_Iranian_political_prisoners) under the new government.[^cite_note-3] This trend has continued and intensified in the 2020s due to economic instability and political unrest.

In 2021, the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Iran](1166971.md) published statistics showing that 4,037,258 Iranians were living abroad. This figure includes people of Iranian ancestry in the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Kuwait](7515890.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), and [Bahrain](18933277.md) whose families may have left Iran decades prior to the 1979 revolution, as well as those with partial ancestry.[^cite_note-iranian.mfa.ir-4][^cite_note-gostaresh.news-5] In December 2025, [United Nations](31769.md) data indicated that there were 1.6 million Iranian asylum seekers worldwide, though most Iranians abroad are not asylum seekers.[^cite_note-6]

Over one million people of Iranian descent live in the [United States](3434750.md), with significant populations (between 100,000 and 500,000) in [Australia](4689264.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Sweden](5058739.md), Turkey, and the [United Kingdom](31717.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-migrationinformation.org-8] In early 2026, the [Iranian diaspora began holding rallies around the world](82164645.md) in support of the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md).[^cite_note-iranintl-english-9]

## Migration waves

The nation of Iran has experienced [waves of emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital_flight_from_Iran) since 1979. The government has proposed the creation of a ministry of immigration following reports indicating critical emigration statistics, largely driven by political instability and economic sanctions.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Socioeconomic status

Nearly 60 percent of Iranians abroad have earned at least an undergraduate degree. They have some of the highest rates of self-employment among immigrant groups. Many have founded their own companies, including [Isaac Larian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Larian), the founder of [MGA Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGA_Entertainment), and [Pierre Omidyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Omidyar), who founded [eBay](130495.md) in 1995 in [San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California), [California](5407.md). Iranian households in the [United States](3434750.md) earned on average \$87,288 annually as of 2018, and are [ranked ninth by income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_the_United_States_by_household_income).[^cite_note-28]

## Students abroad

See also: [Higher education in Iran](1273488.md) and [Iran's brain drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran's_brain_drain)

According to the [Iranian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_government), 55,686 Iranian students were studying abroad in 2013:[^cite_note-29] 8,883 studied in [Malaysia](3607937.md), 7,341 in the United States, 5,638 in Canada, 3,504 in Germany, 3,364 in Turkey, 3,228 in Britain, and the rest in other countries.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] The [Iranian Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Iran\)) estimated that between 350,000 and 500,000 Iranians were studying outside Iran as of 2014.[^cite_note-32]

## Religious affiliation

See also: [Religion in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Iran) and [Irreligion in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_Iran)

Members of the Iranian diaspora are considered to be mostly [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularity). The majority of them do not take fundamental Islamic rituals, such as daily prayers or fasting, and have largely embraced Western secularism.[^cite_note-50] According to a 2008 survey by the Public Affairs Alliance of Iranian Americans (PAAIA), 42% of Iranian Americans identified as [Muslim](19541.md), 9% as [Christian](18337522.md), 6% as [Jewish](25955086.md), 5% as [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism), 7% as [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith), and 31% as other or non-religious.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-Parade-52][^cite_note-irreligion-53] A 2012 national telephone survey of a sample of 400 Iranian-Americans, commissioned by the PAAIA and conducted by Zogby Research Services, asked the respondents what their religions were. The responses broke down as follows: [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) 31%, [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist)/[realist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_realism)/[humanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_humanism) 11%, [agnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnostic) 8%, Baháʼí 7%, [Jewish](25955086.md) 5%, [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) 5%, [Roman Catholic](606848.md) 2%, [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrian) 2%, "Other" 15%, and "No response" 15%.[^cite_note-paaia_survey-54] The survey had a cooperation rate of 31.2%.[^cite_note-paaia_survey-54] The [margin of error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margin_of_error) for the results was ± 5 percentage points, with higher margins of error for sub-groups.[^cite_note-paaia_survey-54] Notably, the number of Muslims decreased from 42% in 2008 to 31% in 2012.[^cite_note-paaia_survey-54][^cite_note-paaia1-55]

## Notes

In the period between 1961 and 2005, the United States became the main destination of Iranian emigrants. An estimated 378,995 Iranians immigrated to the United States in that period, with [California](5407.md) being the most common destination (158,613 Iran-born in 2000),[^cite_note-ReferenceA-56] [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_state) (17,323),[^cite_note-ReferenceA-56] [Texas](29810.md) (15,581),[^cite_note-ReferenceA-56] [Virginia](32432.md) (10,889),[^cite_note-ReferenceA-56] and [Maryland](18858.md) (9,733).[^cite_note-ReferenceA-56] The [Los Angeles Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metropolitan_Area) was estimated to be home to approximately 114,712 Iranian immigrants,[^cite_note-ReferenceA-56] earning the [Westwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westwood,_Los_Angeles,_California) area of Los Angeles the nickname [Tehrangeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrangeles).

The [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau)'s decennial census form does not offer a designation for individuals of Iranian descent, and therefore it is estimated that only a fraction of the total number of Iranians are writing in their ancestry. The [2000 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census) estimated that the Iranian American community (including the US-born children of the Iranian foreign-born) numbers around 330,000. Studies using alternative statistical methods have estimated the actual number of Iranian Americans in the range of 691,000 to 1.2 million.[^cite_note-migrationinformation.org-8][^cite_note-57]
