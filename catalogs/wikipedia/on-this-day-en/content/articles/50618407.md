**Kuching City Football Club**, simply known as **Kuching City**, is a professional [football](10568.md) club based in [Kuching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuching), [Sarawak](28258.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md) that currently plays in the [Malaysia Super League](42924474.md). It was the youngest club to join the highest level of the [Malaysian football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_football_league_system), having been founded since 2015.[^cite_note-2] It is the third professional football club based in the state of Sarawak, after former state football club [Sarawak FA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak_FA) and another defunct club [Sarawak United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak_United_F.C.).

## History

### As Kuching FA (2015–2020)

In 2015, with the commencement of the [People's Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Bolasepak_Rakyat) introduced by the [Football Association of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association_of_Malaysia), the purpose of the league was to transform lower leagues in Malaysia and promote amateur football in all districts and states across Malaysia.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Consequently, [Kuching F.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuching_Football_Association) was founded alongside it to represent the district of [Kuching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuching) in [Sarawak](28258.md).

#### FAM Cup

In the 2015–16 [Liga Bolasepak Rakyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Bolasepak_Rakyat), Kuching won the inaugural edition[^cite_note-5] after beating [Padang Besar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padang_Besar_F.C.?action=edit\&redlink=1) 3–1 at the [Selayang Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selayang_Stadium). With the championship, Kuching got an automatic promotion into the [2017 Piala FAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Malaysia_FAM_League).

In their first season in FAM League, Kuching achieved 4th place in Group B, qualifying the team for the play-offs. They were knocked out in the quarter-final stage by [UKM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKM_F.C.), which later gained promotion to [2018 Malaysia Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Malaysia_Premier_League) as finalist of the play-offs. Kuching's [second season in the FAM League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Malaysia_FAM_Cup) saw them in the 4th place in Group A, the same as previous season. Unfortunately, changes in the play-off qualifying saw them miss out on the knock-out stage as only top two teams of each group chosen. Kuching were registered to play in the [2019 Malaysia FAM Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Malaysia_FAM_Cup), but were also invited by FAM to play in the [2019 Malaysia Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Malaysia_Premier_League), as a number of teams pull out from the league.[^cite_note-6]

After announcing their promotion to the [2020 Malaysia Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Malaysia_Premier_League), Kuching changed their kit to a new one to kick start their new journey, the colours are green with white stripes and "City of Unity" is written in the middle of the kit. The following season, they added ''City'' to back of Kuching.



Kuching City players playing a friendly match against Bruneian club [DPMM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPMM) on 4 January 2025.

### As Kuching City (2021–*present*)

#### First cup final

Kuching City were promoted to the [2023 Malaysia Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Malaysia_Super_League) for the first time in history after their MFL License was approved for the 2023 season. On 2 August 2023, former Singaporean legend, [Aidil Sharin Sahak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidil_Sharin_Sahak) was announced as the new head coach for the club signing [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_national_football_team) captain, [Petrus Shitembi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrus_Shitembi) and Tajikistani midfielder [Nuriddin Davronov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuriddin_Davronov). Aidil Sharin guided the club to a great run in the [2023 MFL Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_MFL_Challenge_Cup) winning 4–0 on aggregate against his former club, Kedah Darul Aman in the quarter-finals, a 3–1 on aggregate against [Kelantan United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelantan_United_F.C.) in the semi-finals which saw the club first ever cup final in their history.

#### AFC Champions League Two debut

Kuching City then finished the [2025–26 Malaysia Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Malaysia_Super_League) as runners-up with 53 points thus seeing the club qualified for the [2026–27 ASEAN Club Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_ASEAN_Club_Championship) and the [2026–27 AFC Champions League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_AFC_Champions_League_Two) for the first time in the club history. In the club debut ASEAN Club Championship, they were draw in Group A alongside both Thai club [Buriram United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buriram_United_F.C.) and [Ratchaburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchaburi_F.C.), Vietnamese club [Công An Hồ Chí Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cong_An_Ho_Chi_Minh_City_FC), Singaporean club [Tampines Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampines_Rovers_FC) and Indonesian club [Borneo Samarinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_F.C._Samarinda). While in the club debut continental competition, Kuching City was then placed in Group H of the AFC Champions League Two alongside Korean club [Gangwon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangwon_FC), Hong Kong club [Kitchee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitchee_SC) and Cambodian club [Phnom Penh Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phnom_Penh_Crown_FC).

## Stadium



[Taha Ariffin Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taha_Ariffin_Stadium)

Kuching City has used [Taha Ariffin Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taha_Ariffin_Stadium) in Kuching as its home ground since 2015. Formerly known as Sarawak State Stadium, it is one of the major football venues in the city. The stadium has a seating capacity of approximately 26,000 spectators, making it one of the larger stadiums in Sarawak. The stadium also host Kuching City's league matches and other domestic football competitions. The stadium is also an important part of Kuching’s football culture, regularly attracting local supporters on match days.

The stadium was renamed Taha Ariffin Stadium in honour of Datuk Taha Ariffin, a prominent figure in the development of football in Sarawak. Taha Ariffin was involved in the administration of the Football Association of Sarawak (FAS) and played an important role in the development of Sarawak football during the late 20th century. Contemporary accounts describe him as a key figure in the establishment and development of the state's football administration.&#x20;
