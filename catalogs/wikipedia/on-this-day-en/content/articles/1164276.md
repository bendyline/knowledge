**Tombouctou Region** or **Timbuktu Region** ([Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language): ߕߎߡߎߕߎ ߘߌߣߋߖߊ, Tumutu Dineja) is one of the [administrative regions](690325.md) of [Mali](19127.md). For administrative purposes, the region is subdivided into five [cercles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercles_of_Mali).

The region is part of northern Mali that was separated and declared independent by the [National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad) (MNLA) during the [Tuareg rebellion of 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(2012\)). In the course of the conflict, the MNLA lost control of the territory to Islamist militias.

Tombouctou Region is world-famous for its capital, the ancient city [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Tombouctou*), synonymous to 19th-century Europeans with an elusive, hard-to-reach destination. The city gained fame in 1390 when its ruler, [Mansa Musa I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansa_Musa), went on a [pilgrimage to Mecca](23487999.md), stopping with his entourage in [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate) and dispensing enough [gold](12240.md) to [devalue](38286.md) the Egyptian currency. This started the legend of a city in the interior of Africa, where roads were said to be paved with gold and buildings topped with roofs of gold.

## History

The city is located at the southern edge of the [Sahara](325363.md), near the [Niger River](21437.md), which has headwaters in the highlands very near the [Atlantic](698.md) coast before its long 3,200 kilometres (2,000 mi) journey to the north east, before finally turning south to reach the Atlantic. The riches of the kingdom were due to Tombouctou's position as the southern terminus of the [trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade) in gold, salt, kola nuts, copper and slaves.

Timbuktu's decline began with the capture of the city by [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_Sultanate) in 1592. Many [Islamic scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulema) were dispersed, some to Morocco. Morocco had difficulty holding onto the city, as the supply lines were long compared to the closer kingdoms vying for dominance of the region. Furthermore, the Moroccans did not establish a proper means of which to govern Timbuktu, and their other holdings along the Niger bend. Ultimately, however, it was the rise of sea trade along the West Africa coast that doomed the overland routes that connected North Africa to [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md). The city lost its economic base and its fine university was not enough to save Timbuktu from decline.

Cut off from major trade routes, the city retained an aura of spectacular treasure. When French explorers rediscovered the city in 1815, they were disappointed to find a sand-blown city of low mud buildings.

The region was marginalized under [French colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) control, which ended in 1960. The French opened up shorter trade routes to the Atlantic, cutting into the trans-Sahara trading economy and people in the city.

In early 2012, the [National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad) and other militant groups opposed to the [government of Mali](19131.md) swept through the region, entering Timbuktu without a fight after making a deal with local [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_people) militias. On 6 April 2012, the region was declared independent from Mali as part of the new country of [Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawad). However, Mali refused to acknowledge the [Azawadi Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawadi_Declaration_of_Independence), and the international community continues to recognise [Bamako](56606.md)'s claim to the region. In 2013, Timbuktu was taken over by Islamist militants affiliated with the Tuareg but was retaken by the Malian government after a year. In 2023, Timbuktu came under siege from more Islamist militants.[^cite_note-4]

## Administrative subdivisions

The region is divided into five [*cercles*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercles_of_Mali):[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

The larger parts of Timbuktu and [Goundam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goundam) cercles (323,326 km² with about 134,000 inhabitants) were separated in 2016 to form the new [Taoudenit Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoud%C3%A9nit_Region).[^cite_note-8]

## Demographics

The Tombouctou Region is the seventh most populated region in Mali, with a population of 974,278 in 2022.[^cite_note-2022census-9] With a total fertility rate at 4.9 births per woman, Tombouctou has a lower TFR than the Malian national average of 6.1 births per woman.[^cite_note-censustfr-10]

### Religion

The 2022 census found that 99.67% of the population in Tombouctou was [Muslim](19541.md), 0.31% was [Christian](18337522.md), and 0.02% identified with no religion at all. [Islam](6037917.md) is the dominant religion among all of Tombouctou's ethnic groups, with the Tuareg, Songhai, Arab, Fula, and Bozo peoples being almost entirely Muslim.[^cite_note-censusculture-11]
