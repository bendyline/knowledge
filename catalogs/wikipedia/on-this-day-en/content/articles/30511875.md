# National Book Award for Fiction

The **National Book Award for Fiction** is one of five annual [National Book Awards](63097.md) that recognize outstanding literary work by United States citizens. Since 1987, the [National Book Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Foundation) has administered and presented the awards, but they are awards "by writers to writers".[^cite_note-history-1] The judges are five "writers who are known to be doing great work in their genre or field".[^cite_note-proccess-2]

General fiction was one of four categories when the awards were reestablished in 1950. For several years beginning in 1980, there were multiple fiction categories: hardcover, paperback, first novel or first work of fiction; from 1981 to 1983 hardcover and paperback [children's fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_literature); and in 1980 five awards for [mystery fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_fiction), [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction), and [western fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_fiction).[^cite_note-winners-3] When the Foundation celebrated the 60th postwar awards in 2009, all but three of the 77 previous winners in fiction categories were in print.[^cite_note-60thanniversary-4] The 77 included all eight 1980 winners but excluded the 1981 to 1983 children's fiction winners.[^cite_note-77fictionwinners-5]

The award recognizes one book written by a U.S. citizen and published in the U.S. from December 1 to November 30. The National Book Foundation accepts nominations from publishers until June 15, requires that nominated books be mailed to the judges by August 1, and announces five finalists in October. The winner is announced on the day of the final ceremony in November. The award is \$10,000 and a bronze sculpture; other finalists get \$1,000, a medal, and a citation written by the panel.[^cite_note-selection-6]

Authors who have won the award more than once include [William Faulkner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Faulkner), [John Updike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Updike), [William Gaddis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gaddis), [Jesmyn Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesmyn_Ward), and [Philip Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Roth), each having won twice along with numerous other nominations. [Saul Bellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Bellow) won the award in 1954, 1965, and 1971, and is the only author to have won three National Book Awards for Fiction.

## National Book Awards for Fiction

From [1935 to 1941](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Book_Award_winners#1935_to_1941), there were six annual awards for general fiction and the "Bookseller Discovery" or "Most Original Book" was sometimes a novel. From 1980 to 1985, there were six annual awards to first novels or [first works of fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_winners_of_the_National_Book_Award#Other_Fiction_1980_to_1985). In 1980 there were five awards for [mystery, western, or science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_winners_of_the_National_Book_Award#Other_Fiction_1980_to_1985). There have been many awards to fiction in the [Children's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_winners_of_the_National_Book_Award#Children's_books) or [Young People's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_winners_of_the_National_Book_Award#Young_People's_Literature) categories.[^cite_note-winners-3]

## Honorees, general fiction

This list covers only the postwar awards ([pre-war awards](#Early_awards_for_fiction) follow) to general fiction for adult readers: one annual winner from 1950 except two undifferentiated winners 1973 to 1975, dual hardcover and paperback winners 1980 to 1983.

For each award, the winner is listed first, followed by the finalists. Unless otherwise noted, the year represents the year the award was given for books published in the previous year. Thus, the award year 1950 is for books published in 1949.

### 1980s

For 1980 to 1983 this list covers the paired "Fiction (hardcover)" and "Fiction (paperback)" awards in that order. Hard and paper editions were distinguished only in these four years; none of the paperback winners were [original](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paperback#Paperback_originals); in their first editions all had been losing finalists in 1979 or 1981.

From 1980 to 1985 there was also one award for first novel or first work of fiction and in 1980 there were five more awards for mystery, western, and science fiction.[^cite_note-winners-3] None of those are covered here.

## Early awards for fiction

The National Book Awards for 1935 to 1940 annually recognized the "Most Distinguished Novel" (1935–1936) or "Favorite Fiction" (1937–1940). Furthermore, works of fiction were eligible for the "Bookseller Discovery" and "Most Original Book" awards; fiction winners are listed here.

There was only one National Book Award for 1941, the Bookseller Discovery, which recognized the novel *Hold Autumn In Your Hand* by George Perry;[^cite_note-nyt1942-160] then none until the 1950 revival in three categories including Fiction.
