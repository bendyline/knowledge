The **Afghanistan–Pakistan skirmishes** are a series of armed skirmishes between [Afghanistan](737.md) and the [Pakistan](23235.md) since 1949, along the [Afghanistan–Pakistan border](203570.md).[^cite_note-6]

The latest round of hostilities between the two countries began in April 2007.[^cite_note-start-7] Militants belonging to internationally designated terrorist groups Tehrik-i-Taliban/[Pakistani Taliban](18953699.md) (TTP)[^cite_note-8] and [Jamaat-ul-Ahrar](44284567.md)[^cite_note-9] also use Afghanistan's territory to target Pakistani security personnel deployed along the border.[^cite_note-va-3][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] [Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Sharif_Chaudhry), spokesperson for the [Pakistan Armed Forces](23369.md), said that the presence of terrorists belonging to the TTP on Afghan soil is the reason for sporadic shelling of, and airstrikes on, Afghanistan's territory by the Pakistan Armed Forces.[^cite_note-\:02-15] After de-escalation in March 2024, the conflict resurged in December 2024 with Pakistani airstrikes against Afghanistan, specifically in [Paktika Province](457294.md).[^cite_note-16] Conflict in December 2024 marked the third round of air strikes by Pakistan on the territory of [Afghanistan](737.md) in a period of less than two years. The first similar [Pakistani airstrikes on Afghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pakistani_airstrikes_in_Afghanistan) soil since Taliban takeover of Afghanistan were in 2022 and the second Pakistani airstrikes were in March 2024.[^cite_note-17]

Starting on 9 October 2025, [fighting between the two countries escalated](81304780.md), with a gunfight in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa,[^cite_note-\:9-18] followed by reported Pakistani airstrikes in Kabul,[^cite_note-\:10-19][^cite_note-\:11-20] Khost, Jalalabad,[^cite_note-Casualties2-21][^cite_note-Casualties3-22] and Paktika[^cite_note-Casualties4-23] targeting the TTP and its leader, [Noor Wali Mehsud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_Wali_Mehsud). In retaliation, the Taliban launched attacks on multiple Pakistani military posts along the Durand Line on 11–12 October,[^cite_note-\:14-24][^cite_note-\:15-25][^cite_note-26] triggering intense cross-border skirmishes and reported Pakistani drone strikes in Kandahar and Helmand.[^cite_note-27] Heavy fighting resumed around Spin Boldak on 15 October, after which Pakistan conducted further strikes in Kabul and Kandahar.[^cite_note-28] Both sides accused each other of violating the truce and targeting civilians. On 19 October, following mediation by Qatar and Turkey, Afghanistan and Pakistan agreed to a ceasefire, under which Kabul pledged to curb militant activity against Pakistan, while both nations vowed to refrain from further attacks.[^cite_note-cnn192-29]

During the same period, the TTP and the [Baloch Liberation Army](6010135.md) (BLA) carried out attacks inside Pakistan, while the [National Resistance Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Resistance_Front) of Afghanistan and the [Afghanistan Freedom Front](70895922.md) launched assaults against the Taliban within Afghanistan. Both countries accused each other of supporting armed opposition groups operating against them. On 21 February 2026, Pakistani airstrikes targeted TTP militant camps in Afghanistan,[^cite_note-30] which escalated into border skirmishes, and eventually, a [broader war](82482902.md) between the Taliban and Pakistani forces.

## Background

Hostilities existed between Afghanistan and the newly independent Pakistan since 1947,[^cite_note-Emadi-31] when Afghanistan became the only country to vote against the admission of Pakistan to the United Nations.[^cite_note-Hassan-32] Before Pakistan's [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Pakistan\)), Afghanistan advocated the independence of its north-west frontier,[^cite_note-FairWatson2015-33] although the region's predominant Pashtun population had voted overwhelmingly in favor of Pakistan over India in the referendum held in July 1947. 99.02% votes were cast in favor of Pakistan.[^cite_note-prr.hec.gov.pk-34][^cite_note-35] Though the proposed [Pashtunistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtunistan) state by Afghanistan fluctuated in size over time,[^cite_note-FairWatson20152-36] the Balochistan province of Pakistan was also frequently included in the Greater Pastunistan definition to gain access to the Arabian sea in case Pakistan failed as a state,[^cite_note-Emadi-31] as Afghanistan had expected, but the idea became unpopular.[^cite_note-Hassan-32]

The International border between [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Afghanistan) was established after the 1893 *Durand Line Agreement* between British [Mortimer Durand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortimer_Durand) of British India and [Amir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Afghanistan) [Abdur Rahman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdur_Rahman_Khan) of Afghanistan for fixing the limit of their respective [spheres of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spheres_of_influence). The single-page agreement, which contains seven short articles, was signed by Durand and Khan, agreeing not to exercise political interference beyond the [frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier) line between what was then the Emirate of Afghanistan and what was also then the [British Indian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Empire).[^cite_note-LoC-Smith-37] The [Durand Line](203570.md) was reaffirmed as the International Border between Afghanistan and British India in the [1919 Anglo-Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_Anglo-Afghan_War) after the Afghan independence. The Afghans undertook to stop interference on the British side of the line in the subsequent [Anglo-Afghan Treaty of 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Afghan_Treaty_of_1919) in Rawalpindi.[^cite_note-Sidebotham-38]

Pakistan inherited the Durand Line agreement after its [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Pakistan\)) in 1947, but the Afghan Government has always refused to accept the Durand Line Agreement. Afghanistan has several times tried to seize Pakistan's western provinces of [Balochistan](8431743.md) and [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md). The then Afghan Prime Minister, Muhammad Hashim, said: "... if an independent Pashtunistan cannot be set up, the frontier province should join Afghanistan. Our neighbor Pakistan will realize that our country, with its population and trade, needs an outlet to the sea, which is very essential", in an interview with the Statesman.[^cite_note-Emadi-31] In 1949, Pakistan Air Force [bombed the Afghan sponsored militant camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Mughalgai_raid) in border areas including an Afghan village to curb an unrest led by Ipi Faqir propagating independent Pashtunistan.[^cite_note-Pakistan_Defence_Journal-39] Border clashes were reported in 1949–50 for the first time.[^cite_note-Hassan-32]

### 1950s–1970s

On 30 September 1950, Pakistan claimed that Afghan troops and tribesmen had crossed into Pakistan's Balochistan, resulting in the Afghan invasion of Pakistan. The low-scale invasion was repelled after six days of fighting. The Afghan government denied its involvement and claimed that they were pro-Pashtunistan Pashtun tribesmen.[^cite_note-40]

Tensions soared with the Pakistani [One Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Unit) program, and both countries withdrew ambassadors and diplomatic staff in 1955. The Pakistani Embassy in Kabul and consulates in Kandhar and Jalalabad were attacked by mobs.[^cite_note-Emadi-31][^cite_note-Hassan-32] In 1960, major skirmishes broke with the Afghan Forces massing out on the Afghan side of the border with tanks. These skirmishes saw the Pakistan Air Force bombarding Afghan forces. This bombardment led to a brief hiatus in the skirmishes. On 6 September 1961, Kabul formally severed diplomatic relations with Pakistan.[^cite_note-FairWatson2015-33] In March 1961, months before the major battle broke out in the khyber pass area, Afghanistan reportedly provided weapons and ammunition to proxies under the leadership of Fazl Akbar, to incite an [uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uprising) in the Batmalai district of [Bajaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaur).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeake2017222-41] Pacha Gul was advised to go before an aerial counteroffensive would be triggered for serving as an agent for Afghanistan and providing resources worth of 170 million Afghanis, cash, and arms to the bajaur tribesmen which were to incite an uprising against Pakistan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaur1985110-42][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDupree2014539-540-43] The Pakistan air force acted by bombing the area where the ammunition dump was stored. The Pakistani air force then claimed to have destroyed a major ammunition dump during the raid.[^cite_note-\:0-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDupree2014539-540-43]

&#x20;In 1950 the [House of Commons of the United Kingdom](13828.md) held its view on the Afghan-Pakistan dispute over the Durand Line by stating:

> [His Majesty's Government in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Government_in_the_United_Kingdom) has seen with regret the disagreements between the Governments of Pakistan and Afghanistan about the status of the territories on the North West Frontier. It is His Majesty's Government's view that Pakistan is in international law the inheritor of the rights and duties of the old Government of India and of his Majesty's Government in the United Kingdom in these territories and that the Durand Line is the international frontier.[^cite_note-47]

— [Philip Noel-Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Noel-Baker), 30 June 1950

&#x20;At the 1956 SEATO ([Southeast Asia Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia_Treaty_Organization)) Ministerial Council Meeting held at [Karachi](17123.md), capital of Pakistan at the time, it was stated:

> The members of the Council declared that their governments recognised that the sovereignty of Pakistan extends up to the Durand Line, the international boundary between Pakistan and Afghanistan, and it was consequently affirmed that the Treaty area referred to in Articles IV and VIII of the Treaty includes the area up to that Line.[^cite_note-48]

— SEATO, 8 March 1956

The Afghan government, having secured a treaty in December 1978 that allowed them to call on Soviet forces, repeatedly requested the introduction of troops in Afghanistan in the spring and summer of 1979. The 1979 [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) forced millions of Afghans to take refuge inside [Pakistan](22663356.md). Pakistani officials feared that the Soviet Union began some kind of military show down and that Pakistan or at least its Balochistan province was next on the Soviet agenda. During the early 1980s, multi-national [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) forces (consisting of about 100,000 fighters from forty different Muslim countries in addition to 150,000 local fighters) found support from the United States, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Iran in the context of the [Cold War](325329.md). They were trained by Pakistani military in its frontier region around the Durand Line.[^cite_note-49]

### 1980s

On 29 September 1984, Pakistani officials stated that an [Afghan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force_\(1978-1992\)) aircraft flew across the [Durand Line](203570.md) and used its munitions on Pakistani border town [Teri Mangal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teri_Mangal), situated in [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md), used by the [Afghan mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen). The plane supposedly dropped two bombs and stayed in Pakistani airspace for 2–4 minutes before flying back to [Afghanistan](737.md). As a result of the bombing, 32 people were killed, 48 were injured and 200 local shops in the town were destroyed.[^cite_note-50] In the same year, observers reported that Soviet–Afghan forces made 43 airspace violations and 14 ground incursions into [Pakistan](23235.md), resulting in 300 casualties and heavy losses for each side. The [Afghan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Democratic_Party_of_Afghanistan) then accused the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) of attacking its garrisons after airstrikes spilled into Pakistani territory.[^cite_note-Seekins-51]

On 7 January 1986, the Afghan government submitted an official protest to the United Nations, accusing the Pakistani government of continued aggression and violations of Afghan sovereignty, despite repeated diplomatic complaints. The Afghan authorities additionally asserted that Pakistan was supporting anti-government elements (such as the Mujahideen) and enabling attacks on Afghan territory. The complaint cited numerous specific incidents:[^cite_note-52]

- On 2 January 1986, two Pakistan Air Force (PAF) helicopters penetrated 3 km into Afghan airspace near Gurpary (20 km southwest of Asadabad, Kunar). These helicopters allegedly coordinated artillery shelling on Pashad from nearby areas under Pakistan's jurisdiction, causing 8 civilian casualties, 6 injuries, and the destruction of 30 houses.

- On 30 December 1985, under Pakistani air cover, two missile launchers fired 150 rockets from Bandi Sarash and Karbaz villages into Afghan territory, killing 2 teachers, 5 civilians, and destroying 22 houses in Kunar.

- On 1 December 1985, a PAF jet reportedly entered 6 km into Afghan airspace over Pozdakah (from Torkham) and returned to Pakistan after 10 minutes.

### Present day

The Soviet Union decided to withdraw in 1989 and when aid dried up on Afghanistan in 1992, a [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1992%E2%80%931996\)) began. This was followed by the rise and fall of the [Taliban government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)). Since late 2001, as high as 140,000 [NATO](21133.md)-led troops were stationed in Afghanistan to [train Afghans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Training_Mission-Afghanistan) and rebuild their war-torn country. In the meantime, the [Taliban insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgency) began around 2004.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] To counter the insurgency and bring stability in Afghanistan, the United States built bases and garrisons for the [Afghan National Security Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Security_Forces), and is using [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md) to carry out [drone attacks in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_attacks_in_Pakistan), mainly the [Haqqani network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haqqani_network) in and around the [Federally Administered Tribal Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Tribal_Areas) (FATA).

In 2007, the [Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan (TTP)](18953699.md) militant group was established as a coalition of various hardline Sunni Islamist factions in Pakistan. This formation came in response to military actions against [Al-Qaida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaida)-affiliated terrorists in the [Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Tribal_Areas_\(FATA\)), an area in northwest Pakistan.[^cite_note-\:12-55][^cite_note-\:4-56] The group, led initially by [Baitullah Mehsud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baitullah_Mehsud), is primarily located along the border with Afghanistan and is estimated to have between 30,000 and 35,000 members. TTP aims to overthrow Pakistan's elected government to create an emirate governed by its interpretation of Islamic law. To achieve this goal, the TTP has attacked the Pakistani military and assassinated political figures. Their violent actions, including numerous suicide bombings, have resulted in the deaths of hundreds of military personnel, police, and civilians.[^cite_note-\:4-56]

TTP has been responsible for some of the most devastating attacks in Pakistan, targeting churches, schools, and notable figures like [Malala Yousafzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malala_Yousafzai), who survived an assassination attempt in 2012 for advocating women's education against Taliban restrictions.[^cite_note-\:4-56] Following the Taliban's takeover in Afghanistan in 2021, TTP has become more aggressive, as its leaders and fighters are based in Afghanistan. After ending a cease-fire with the Pakistani government in late 2022, TTP has intensified its attacks, causing casualties among Pakistani soldiers and police.[^cite_note-\:12-55]

In September 2017, [Brad Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Sherman), a US lawmaker, suggested conditioning US aid to Afghanistan to the recognition of Durand Line. He added:

> I realise that's tough. They'll say, oh, don't—but the fact is, as long as Afghanistan leaves open the idea that they're claiming Pakistani territory, it's going to be very hard to get the Pakistanis involved, as we need them involved, in controlling the Afghan Taliban.[^cite_note-57]

— [Brad Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Sherman), 7 September 2017

## Skirmishes since 2024

### March 2024

On 18 March 2024 Pakistan launched pre-dawn airstrikes targeting TTP hideouts in Afghanistan's [Khost](457249.md) and [Paktika](457294.md) province.[^cite_note-Dawn-124][^cite_note-Reuters01-125][^cite_note-126] The strikes were in retaliation to an earlier militant attack by TTP that resulted in death of seven Pakistani soldiers and six TTP militants. According to Afghan local sources, eight individuals, including five family members of a TTP commander, were killed in these airstrikes.[^cite_note-8am-127] Local sources in Afghanistan also report death of some members of TTP group in the airstrikes.[^cite_note-AP-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] However, the Taliban-led Afghan government claims the airstrikes had only killed eight civilians, which included five women and three children. Later, [Afghan Defense Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Defense_Ministry) also announced that Afghan army had targeted Pakistani troops at the border in response to the air strikes.[^cite_note-Reuters01-125] An officer of Pakistan army was killed, while three soldiers and four civilians were injured in the clash.[^cite_note-AlJazeera-131] Taliban officials did not disclose their losses, however, the local Afghans report death of a soldier of Afghan army in the clashes.[^cite_note-8am-127] Analyst believe that the pre-dawn airstrikes were meant to send a message to Afghan Taliban officials regarding militant attacks in Pakistan originating from Afghanistan.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133] Afghanistan led by the [Taliban](30635.md) denied that the perpetrators were from their territory.[^cite_note-Reuters-134] On 17 March 2024, [President of Pakistan](419460.md) [Asif Ali Zardari](993655.md) vowed strong retaliation against perpetrators of the attack.[^cite_note-135]

#### PAF airstrikes

On 18 March, in response to an attack originating from Afghanistan, the Pakistan Air Force carried out airstrikes in Afghanistan's eastern border provinces of Khost and Paktika. The Afghan government stated that the strikes killed five women and three children.[^cite_note-Reuters-134] An Afghan media outlet, citing local sources, also reported the deaths of eight individuals, including five family members of a TTP commander.[^cite_note-march_airstrike-136] Pakistan denied this claim, asserting that the operation was intelligence-based and targeted the [Hafiz Gul Bahadur militant group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz_Gul_Bahadur_militant_group)—a subgroup faction of the Pakistani Taliban. Pakistani officials said the strikes successfully eliminated several militants, including Sehra (known by the alias 'Janan'), a high-value target and senior commander in the Pakistani Taliban.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

In response to the airstrikes, [Afghanistan's Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Ministry_of_Defence) stated that its forces had targeted Pakistani positions across the border.[^cite_note-Reuters-134] Mortar fire from the Afghan side reportedly injured more than four civilians and three military personnel in Pakistan's Kurram District.[^cite_note-AlJazeera2-139] A Pakistani army captain was killed and two other soldiers were injured in the shelling.[^cite_note-Dawn2-140] Taliban officials did not disclose any casualties on their side; however, local sources in Afghanistan reported the death of one Afghan soldier during the skirmishes.[^cite_note-march_airstrike-136] Skirmishes were also reported in the hilly areas near the [Angoor Adda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angoor_Adda) border crossing in [South Waziristan](4175082.md), though no casualties were confirmed. In the vicinity of Wana, artillery fire reportedly struck near defensive positions on both sides of the border, but no losses were reported.[^cite_note-Relative_calm-141]

#### Subsequent attacks

On 20 March, separatist militants belonging to [Balochistan Liberation Army](6010135.md) attacked the [Gwadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar) port complex, which was repelled by Pakistani security forces. The clash killed eight BLA militants and two Pakistani soldiers. The [Chief Minister of Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Balochistan) claimed that the BLA attackers came from [Afghanistan](737.md) and were provided shelter by the Afghan government.[^cite_note-142]

On 22 March, a suicide bomber rammed his vehicle into a military convoy passing through [Dera Ismail Khan](696749.md); this attack resulted in the death of two Pakistani soldiers and wounding of 15 others.[^cite_note-143] In response to this attack Pakistan vowed a strong retaliation against terrorism.[^cite_note-144]

On 25 March, Balochistan Liberation Army's [Majeed Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majeed_Brigade) attacked Pakistan's second largest Naval Base [PNS Siddique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Siddique) in Turbat which houses American as well as Chinese aircraft.[^cite_note-navalbase-145] This attack was foiled by Pakistani Security forces.[^cite_note-146] In this attack, 6 BLA militants were killed by the [Frontier Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Corps) outside the perimeter of the base while one Pakistani soldier was killed.[^cite_note-147]

On 26 March, in [Shangla District](988627.md), [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md), Pakistan, a suicide bomber [attacked a bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Shangla_bombing) transporting five Chinese laborers and their Pakistani driver on their way to the [Dasu Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasu_Dam), killing all of them.[^cite_note-148] Pakistan police detained more than 12 people, including some Afghan nationals. But the [Taliban](30635.md) [government in Afghanistan](68866725.md) has repeatedly denied giving safe haven to militants.[^cite_note-149]

On 28 March, the National Resistance Front (NRF), claimed responsibility for a series of attacks in Kabul, reporting the deaths of three Taliban members and injuries to five others in two separate incidents. According to the NRF, the first attack targeted a Taliban checkpoint near the Sham-e-Paris Hotel in Kabul's 4th district, while the second occurred near the Lewa-e-Baba Jan area in the 11th district. The group stated that none of its fighters were harmed. The NRF also claimed to have killed a local Taliban commander, Mawlawi Siddiqullah, and his bodyguard in Baghlan Province on the same day, as well as three Taliban members in Kabul's Dahan-e-Bagh area on 27 March. The attacks marked renewed activity by the NRF and the Afghanistan Freedom Front (AFF), both of which had recently intensified operations against Taliban forces across several Afghan provinces.[^cite_note-kabulnow1-150]

Pakistan's [Defence Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Pakistan\)) [Khawaja Asif](16345137.md) in an interview to [BBC Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Urdu) stated that, "It's correct that we have been carrying out operations in Afghanistan, and we will continue to do so. We won't serve them with cake and pastries. If attacked, we'll attack back."[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152]

### June 2024

On 19 June, Abdul Manan, also known as Hakeemullah, a senior commander of Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan (TTP), was reportedly killed in Asadabad, the capital of Kunar province in eastern Afghanistan. According to Pakistani media, Hakeemullah had previously served with the TTP Malakand Shura and was involved in various militant activities, including targeted killings, checkpoint attacks, landmine explosions, and extortion, as well as training TTP commanders at a madrassa in Sirkanay district.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154]

On 25 June, the Afghanistan Freedom Front (AFF) and the National Resistance Front (NRF), reported multiple attacks on Taliban forces in Kabul, claiming at least 14 Taliban members were killed or injured. Taliban authorities did not comment on the incidents. Since the Taliban's return to power, armed opposition groups, largely composed of former security personnel, have increasingly carried out guerrilla operations, particularly in Kabul and northern provinces. According to a recent UN report, the AFF conducted 14 attacks, all in Kabul, while the NRF carried out 29 attacks across multiple provinces, highlighting the continued activity of anti-Taliban resistance groups.[^cite_note-155]

### August 2024

On 12 August, clashes erupted between Taliban and Pakistani forces near the Torkham border crossing in eastern Nangarhar province, leading to the temporary closure of the border.[^cite_note-august_clash-156] According to Taliban spokesperson Abdul Matin Qani, Pakistani forces initiated the confrontation by firing on Taliban border personnel, prompting a Taliban response. The exchange involved light and heavy weapons, including artillery, and reportedly resulted in the deaths of three Afghan civilians, including a woman and two children. Pakistani reports suggested the clash began after objections to the Taliban's construction of a checkpoint near the border. The incident led to the evacuation of nearby areas and the temporary suspension of border crossings, marketplaces, and government offices.[^cite_note-august_clash-156]

### September 2024

On 7 September, intense clashes occurred between Taliban forces and Pakistani border troops along the Afghanistan–Pakistan border, particularly near Khost province and the adjoining Kurram district. The fighting reportedly began after Taliban forces attempted to construct a security outpost on the Afghan side of the [Pakistan-Afghanistan border barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan%E2%80%93Pakistan_border_barrier), prompting Pakistani troops to open fire to force the other side to stop the activity. Multiple sources reported that ongoing heavy clashes had injured at least five Pakistani soldiers, including an officer while in Pakistan's retaliation at least eight [Afghan Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Taliban) fighters were killed including two [Taliban Army](212681.md) commanders and 16 others injured in the early hours of Saturday (7 September). The skirmishes involved heavy weaponry and lasted several hours, resulting in the deaths of at least two Taliban fighters according to pro-Taliban sources, multiple injuries among Taliban and Pakistani forces, and five civilians wounded. The clashes caused damage to homes and infrastructure in the area.

Open Source Intelligence also claimed one Soviet-era [T-62 tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-62_tank) operated by Afghan Taliban destroyed by the Pakistani forces. A spokesman for Afghanistan's Information Ministry refused to provide any information about the clashes.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] This incident marked the second border confrontation in less than a week, following a previous skirmish in Paktia and Khost provinces.[^cite_note-160] Heavy clashes had injured at least five Pakistani soldiers, including an officer while in Pakistan's retaliation at least eight [Afghan Taliban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Taliban) fighters were killed including two [Taliban Army](212681.md) commanders (Khalil and Jan Muhammad), and 16 others injured in the early hours of 7 September. One Soviet-era T-62 tank operated by Afghan Taliban was destroyed by the Pakistani forces.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]

On 20 September, militant attacks targeted Pakistani security outposts in the northwestern border regions of South and North Waziristan, near the Afghan border. According to Pakistani officials, the predawn clashes resulted in the deaths of at least six Pakistani soldiers and injuries to 14 others, including four described as seriously wounded. Pakistani forces responded, killing 12 militants during the engagements, including seven who were reportedly attempting to infiltrate from the Afghan side. The Pakistani officials state that they recovered a significant quantity of weapons, ammunition, and explosives from the assailants.[^cite_note-164]

On 29 September, a clash occurred between Taliban fighters and Pakistani border forces in Nangarhar province, near the Durand Line, resulting in two Taliban fighters killed and three wounded. #### 25 December 2024

On 15 October, in Spin Boldak, at least seven civilians were killed and 30 wounded. The youngest victim was three months old. In Kabul's PD4 district, two explosions struck a residential area, killing at least nine people and injuring at least 37 others. Witnesses stated that rockets struck an oil tanker and nearby homes, causing widespread destruction. Residents reported hearing aircraft or drones overhead.[^cite_note-\:2-261]

On 30 October, Qari Amjad, the deputy leader of the Pakistani Taliban, was killed while attempting to cross into the [Bajaur District](4461891.md) of Pakistan from Afghanistan, as per Pakistani officials. Three other members of the group, including a commander, were also killed in the operation.[^cite_note-263] The Pakistani Taliban confirmed his death but did not provide details about the circumstances of the killing.[^cite_note-264]

### November 2025

On 7 November, a clash broke out between Afghan Taliban forces and Pakistani border forces at the Chaman-Spin Boldak crossing, with both sides accusing each other of initiating the conflict amid peace negotiations between both sides.[^cite_note-\:3-265] The Afghan Taliban spokesman, while blaming Pakistani border forces for firing first, stated that Taliban fighters refrained from responding to the gunfire to respect the ongoing talks in Istanbul.[^cite_note-266] At least five people were killed and six others were injured on the Afghan side, while no casualties were reported from the Pakistani side. Some eyewitnesses in Afghanistan claimed that Pakistani fighter jets bombed areas inside Afghan territory during the clash.[^cite_note-267] Following the clashes, the negotiations between the two sides reportedly deadlocked.[^cite_note-\:3-265]

The [Pakistani](23235.md) [interior minister](26905116.md) [Mohsin Naqvi](72818268.md) said that [Afghan nationals](2235.md) had carried out the [November 2025 suicide bombing in Islamabad](81571429.md), which killed 12 people and injured many others, further adding that the attack was allegedly planned and directed from [Afghanistan](737.md) by [Jamaat-ul-Ahrar](44284567.md).[^cite_note-268]

On 25 November, Afghan authorities reported that at least nine children and a woman were killed in [Khost province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khost_province) after what the Taliban administration described as a Pakistani air strike on a civilian home in the [Gurbuz district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurbuz_District). Taliban spokesman [Zabihullah Mujahid](18935255.md) stated that the attack, reported at midnight, destroyed the residence of a local man, Waliat Khan, and that additional strikes in [Kunar](2159600.md) and [Paktika](457294.md) provinces wounded at least four civilians.[^cite_note-269] Taliban vowed "appropriate response" in the future.[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271]

### December 2025

On 4 December, Pakistani border forces shot dead three laborers who were crossing the border around the Paktika province.[^cite_note-\:2-261]

On 5 December, a clash broke out between border troops of Pakistan and Afghanistan along Spin Boldak–Chaman border crossing, with both countries accusing each other of initiating the clash. According to local Afghan sources, the clash left 2 Taliban fighters and 4 Afghan civilians dead. Taliban officials confirmed death of Afghan civilians but did not comment on the death of its fighters.[^cite_note-272] The clash between the two countries also injured 4 Afghan and 3 Pakistani civilians.[^cite_note-273][^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275] The clash also forced many Afghan civilians to flee Spin Boldak overnight and seek refugee in other areas.[^cite_note-276]

According to [UNAMA](1234277.md), at least 70 civilians were killed and 478 injured in Afghanistan between October and December 2025. The Taliban administration stated that the findings were "close to reality."[^cite_note-\:2-261]

### February 2026

Further information: [2026 Afghanistan–Pakistan war](82482902.md)

On 21 February 2026, Pakistan carried out airstrikes on alleged training camps operated by [Pakistani Taliban](18953699.md) and [ISIS–K](48029263.md) in eastern Afghanistan.[^cite_note-277] On 26 February, Pakistani authorities reported that Afghan forces fired into northwestern Pakistan for two hours during the evening. Both sides claimed to have conducted cross-border raids to capture or destroy each other's outposts.[^cite_note-278] Afghan Taliban officials confirmed that the assault was in response to the Pakistani airstrikes.[^cite_note-279]
