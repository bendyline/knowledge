On 28 February 2026, the first day of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), the [United States](3434750.md) (US) [Armed Forces](32212.md) struck the Shajareh Tayyebe&#x68;**[^cite_note-3]** Elementary School in [Minab](2392946.md), [Hormozgan province](448064.md) in [southern Iran](36042664.md) with a [Tomahawk missile](31238.md), before [following up with others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-tap_strike) in close proximity, directly killing 156 [civilians](145804.md), including 120 schoolchildren, 35 adults, and a [fetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetus).[^cite_note-Asriran-4][^cite_note-AI_23march-1][^cite_note-Reuters-20260317-5][^cite_note-\:3-6] As of 22 September 2026, the attack was the [deadliest strike in terms of civilian casualties](82723923.md#Civilians) in the ongoing war; it was also the deadliest US attack on civilians in the [21st century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_century),[^cite_note-\:10-7] being the deadliest since the [Amiriyah shelter bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiriyah_shelter_bombing) in 1991.[^cite_note-\:11-8]

Independent investigations, [satellite-based](1184557.md) analyses, and [witness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witness) accounts concluded that the school was hit by a US military strike.[^cite_note-BBC050326-9][^cite_note-Da_Silva-2026-10] Those which concluded the US' responsibility in the attack include the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN),[^cite_note-\:1-11] *[Amnesty International](18947898.md)*,[^cite_note-AI_23march-1] *[Forensic Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_Architecture)* (FA)[^cite_note-\:2-12] and *[Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News)*,[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-\:3-6] *[Bellingcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellingcat)*,[^cite_note-Bellingcat-20260308-14] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* (NYT),[^cite_note-NYT_US_Strike-15] the *[Associated Press](18935732.md)* (AP),[^cite_note-\:5-16] and others. Sources involved with the [US military's internal investigation](#US_Department_of_Defense) corroborated that the strike was perpetrated by the US—being aware within hours of the strike—despite the probe not being concluded.[^cite_note-\:10-7][^cite_note-\:13-17]

The attack was condemned by the [Iranian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_government),[^cite_note-SNN-2026-18][^cite_note-Kashmir_Observer-20260301-19][^cite_note-\:20-20] the [UN Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization](21786641.md) (UNESCO),[^cite_note-SNN-2026-18] the [UN Secretary-General](162415.md) (UNSG),[^cite_note-United_Nations-2026-21] some of the [US Congress](31756.md),[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Schatz-20262-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-\:112-25] and [other international human rights organizations and activists](#Human_rights_activists_and_organizations) as a [violation](#Legality) of [international humanitarian law](1093604.md) (IHL). A UN fact-finding mission concluded that there was reasonable grounds to believe the US committed [war crimes](48626.md).[^cite_note-\:1-11] US congresspersons and international media outlets condemned the use of [AI warfare against Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_warfare_against_Iran), including the [Department of Defense](7279897.md)'s (DoD) [Project Maven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Maven)—a target tagging system integrating elements of [Palantir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palantir)'s Maven Smart System and [Anthropic](6206236.md)'s [Claude](75879512.md) [large language model](73248112.md).

## Background

### Minab elementary school and civilian elements

The Shajareh Tayyebeh Elementary School in southern [Minab](2392946.md) was attended by both boys and girls taught on separate floors; girls were taught on the upper floor and boys on the lower.[^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26] According to locals,[^cite_note-Schneid-2026-27] the school was previously a military facility during the [Iran-Iraq war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War)—ending in 1988—before being converted into a school.[^cite_note-\:2-12] Its location was near[^cite_note-31] the Sayyid al-Shuhada military complex which included the headquarters of the Asif Brigade of the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy](4676345.md) (IRGCN),[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-2026b-32] whose operations were "likely administrative in nature" according to FA.[^cite_note-\:2-12]



Painted walls included murals

Investigations confirmed that the school had existed as a civilian institution for more than a [decade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decade), located close to, but separate from the IRGCN compound.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-2026b-32] According to [satellite imagery](1184557.md), the building housing the school was walled within the IRGCN compound in 2013 and walled off from the rest of the compound by October 2016 at the latest.[^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-Browne-2026-30][^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33] Its new external wall had three entrances separate from the rest of the compound, allowing entry without having to pass through the compound, and all were without a military security checkpoint.[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33][^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26] Imagery of a [football pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_pitch) and assembly areas were visible in August 2017.[^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33][^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-20260311-34] By 2018, the school compound's walls were painted pink and blue, distinguishing the girls' and boys' yards respectively.[^cite_note-\:2-12]

*[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* determined there was no indication that the building served a military purpose and found that the adjacent compound buildings were a medical clinic and a pharmacy,[^cite_note-McClure-2026-35] the former of which was walled off from the compound between 2022 and 2023.[^cite_note-Brumfiel-2026-29] Other buildings in the immediate surroundings—some of which were also hit—were also civilian, including a clinic, cultural center, supermarket, car wash, and café.[^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-\:5-16] The clinic had been inaugurated in January 2025 by then IRGC commander-in-chief, Major General [Hossein Salami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Salami).[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33] Furthermore, the compound's four military security posts had been removed by 2016.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26] According to Minab's mayor, the compound had been closed for about 15 years and all military personnel had moved out.[^cite_note-Da_Silva-2026-10] The school and adjacent civilian facilities also had a rich online presence through its website and social media.[^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-Reuters-20260312-36]

### US–Iran nuclear negotiations, US military buildup, and use of artificial intelligence

This section is an excerpt from [Prelude to the 2026 Iran war](82272270.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Prelude_to_the_2026_Iran_war\&action=edit)]

Tension between [Iran](14653.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) during [negotiations](79685207.md) over [Iran's nuclear program](721807.md) intensified in January 2026 when [Iranian security forces massacred](82036668.md) [anti-government protesters](81976180.md).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

The US began its greatest [buildup of military assets in the region](82251337.md) since the [Iraq War in 2003](201936.md). The US accused Iran of building up its nuclear and missile programs to threaten the United States and its allies. Iran accused the US of [disinformation](142690.md). [Gulf states](3088555.md) like [Qatar](8238258.md) predicted that escalations could severely harm the [region](19323.md). [Negotiations regarding the Iranian nuclear program](79685207.md) were held in February until the US and Israel commenced [military strikes in Iran](82533582.md) on 28 February 2026.

In the preparation and planning of Operation Epic Fury, the DoD utilized [Project Maven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Maven), an artificial intelligence software which uses [machine learning algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning) to [analyze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_analysis) and [fuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_fusion) vast amounts of [surveillance data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance)—designed to streamline the [targeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeting_\(warfare\)) process and greatly reduce the amount of personnel involved in it.[^cite_note-\:4-39][^cite_note-Peterson20192-40][^cite_note-manson20242-41] These systems rely on data integration software produced by [Palantir Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palantir_Technologies).[^cite_note-Peterson20192-40][^cite_note-manson20242-41] Capable of producing 1,000 target packages in one hour, the US military later claimed to have struck 6,000 targets in Iran during the first two weeks of the war.[^cite_note-\:4-39][^cite_note-\:15-42]

### Pentagon shutting of program to maintain civilian casualty policy

Trump's administration reportedly made deep cuts to the Pentagon’s civilian harm mitigation and response (CHMR) program. A report by the [Inspector General of the US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Inspector_General_of_the_United_States_Army), "the US military no longer has the people, tools or infrastructure needed to comply with two federal statutes requiring it to maintain a functioning civilian casualty policy, and operate a Civilian Protection Center of Excellence (CP CoE)."[^cite_note-43]

## Prelude

### US and Israel begin striking Iran

Further information: [2026 Iran war § First three days of war (27 February – 1 March)](82533582.md#First_three_days_of_war_\(27_February_%E2%80%93_1_March\))

On the morning of 28 February 2026, [the US and Israel jointly launched attacks against Iran](82533582.md).[^cite_note-44] Airstrikes began to land around 9\:45 a.m. [IST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_Standard_Time), coinciding with the time at which Iranians usually send their children to school, as Saturday is a [working day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_day) in Iran.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-Solomon-2026-46][^cite_note-McClure-2026-35]

### Attendance and closing of the school

Human rights organization *[Hengaw](72014392.md)* stated that around 170 students were present in the school at the time while the [Iranian Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Iran\)) said that 264 students were present.[^cite_note-Solomon-2026-46][^cite_note-Da_Silva-2026-10]

Between 10\:00 and 10\:15 a.m.—prior to the nationwide announcement closing schools—one teacher at the school saw that [Tehran](57654.md) [had been attacked](82533582.md#First_three_days_of_war_\(27_February_%E2%80%93_1_March\)).[^cite_note-\:3-6] Believing that the neither the school nor the rest of the country would be attacked, the boys' head teacher, Pouran Gholipour, called an emergency announcement to close the school around 11\:00 a.m., with the girls' section following suit shortly after. Teachers and administrators began to make calls to parents to have children picked up.[^cite_note-\:3-6] Due to congestion and distance, many parents were not able to reach the school before the airstrike landed on the compound.[^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26][^cite_note-Schneid-2026-27]

&#x20;At approximately 11\:22 a.m.,[^cite_note-47] strikes on the area began.[^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-\:2-12] Of the initial nine strikes, taking place over a minute and a half, the school was the fourth building hit.[^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-BBC_News-20260309-48][^cite_note-Parent-2026-49] The impact, landing in the southwesterly half of the building, instantaneously killed dozens inside.[^cite_note-McClure-2026-35] The impact affected over half of the structure, destroying the walls of the building and causing its roof to collapse, burying people underneath;[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33][^cite_note-Solomon-2026-46][^cite_note-Middle_East_Eye-2026-50] graphic footage shows some bodies partly trapped,[^cite_note-McClure-2026-35] and others of body parts scattered around the site, some being as far as 147 feet (45 m) meters away.[^cite_note-\:32-51] Some of those buried would die of suffocation.[^cite_note-\:17-52]



Collapsed second floor of structure

The explosion destroyed at least half of the two-story school building, collapsing much of the upper floor with the lower.[^cite_note-Browne-2026-30][^cite_note-53] Furthermore, according to eyewitness testimony by Hossein, father of schoolgirl victim Zahra: "The boys’ section was still partly standing. But the area where the girls took their lessons had been levelled."[^cite_note-54] Footage taken on the school's brightly painted soccer and volleyball pitches shows smoke coming out of the school's windows, whose muraled walls were adorned with paintings of crayons, children, and an apple.[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33][^cite_note-CBS_News-2026-55]

Immediately following the strike, witnesses recount the principal, teachers, and other staff running towards the building to bring out students who had survived the first strike.[^cite_note-\:22-56][^cite_note-Amnesty_International-202603162-57][^cite_note-Schneid-20262-58] Videos, recorded between 12\:30 and 1\:15 p.m., show civilians and aid workers searching through the rubble, during which a number of children and teachers were rescued.[^cite_note-\:2-12] From 1\:47 p.m. onwards, multiple news outlets in Iran began to report about the attack, calling the building a girls' school and announcing an initial death toll of five, expecting that it would rise.[^cite_note-\:2-12]

### Second wave of strikes





Civilian and volunteer recovery efforts in the immediate aftermath of the attack

According to testimony given to the *[Middle East Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Eye)* by two [Red Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Crescent) medics and a victim's parent, the initial strike to the school was followed by a second, "[double tap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_tap_strike)" strike.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-mee-double-tap-60] One of the medics recounted that, following the first strike, the school's principal moved a group of students to a prayer room and called parents, asking them to come pick up their children; that area was then hit by a second strike, killing most that had taken shelter.[^cite_note-mee-double-tap-60] The parent corroborates this story, relating he received a call from the school informing him of the first strike, which his daughter had survived; but before he could arrive, the school was hit again, and she was killed.[^cite_note-mee-double-tap-60] According to Minab's mayor and the Iranian Ministry of Education, the school was triple tapped, being struck three times in total.[^cite_note-Da_Silva-2026-10] Although satellite-base analysis by BBC Verify suggest the area of the school was hit with multiple missiles,[^cite_note-BBC050326-9] later investigations confirmed only two missiles had impacted the school.[^cite_note-\:1-11]

Twelve structures within the IRGC compound were significantly impacted by airstrikes sometime after 10\:23 a.m., including the Shahid Absalan Specialist Clinic shortly after the airstrike on the school, which had begun treating those injured in the prior airstrike;[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33][^cite_note-Brumfiel-2026-29][^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26] it was one of five damaged sites whose roofs were punctured by munitions before the munitions' subsequent detonation.[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33] [The Daily Telegraph](23797577.md) characterized the strike on the clinic as "what appeared to be a second attack on the same location" and by authorities as a "follow-up attack".[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-Kashmir_Observer-20260301-19] The NYT corroborated that smoke was billowing from two buildings.[^cite_note-Browne-2026-30]

## Death toll

See also: [Makan Nasiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makan_Nasiri)



The body of a girl is trapped under the rubble, adjacent to [disemboweled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disembowelment), dust-covered [internal organs](152776.md)

156 people were killed in the strike; the victims included 120 students (73 boys and 47 girls), 26 teachers (all of whom were women), seven parents of students (four men and three women), a school bus driver, a pharmacy technician from a nearby clinic, and a six-month-old fetus.[^cite_note-Asriran-4][^cite_note-Browne-2026-30][^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-20260311-34][^cite_note-\:12-62] Three more were presumed dead.[^cite_note-\:12-62][^cite_note-AlJazeera-63][^cite_note-Tehran_Times2-64] Of the teachers killed,[^cite_note-Schneid-2026-27] the school's principal was included.[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33] According to a local official, the fatalities included multiple parents who had come to pick up their children after the [school's closure was ordered](#Attendance_and_closing_of_the_school).[^cite_note-McClure-2026-35][^cite_note-Middle_East_Eye-2026-50][^cite_note-Aslan-2026-65] This resulted in the death of three members of a four-member family.[^cite_note-66] Fatalities rose from initial counts after the conclusion of recovery efforts on 1 March,[^cite_note-71] with reported numbers fluctuating as more bodies of those killed were identified.



The first trace of Nasiri found during recovery efforts was his sneaker

Of the three presumed to be dead,[^cite_note-\:12-62][^cite_note-AlJazeera-63][^cite_note-Tehran_Times2-64] all were children. Despite their [bodies nor any trace thereof having been found](#Search_and_identification_of_remains), they were found to include [Makan Nasiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makan_Nasiri),[^cite_note-\:17-52][^cite_note-AlJazeera-63][^cite_note-\:18-72] [Masiha Salari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masiha_Salari?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-\:19-73] and [Amirali Jedavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amirali_Jedavi?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:18-72] Nasiri's remains included only a blood-stained sweater and pair of sneakers.[^cite_note-\:17-52] On 22 September, during a visit to the school, the [judiciary spokesperson of Hormozgan](66501437.md) officially announced that no remains of the three students had been found.[^cite_note-\:19-73][^cite_note-\:18-72][^cite_note-74] All became [symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr) of civilian suffering during the conflict in Iran.[^cite_note-\:12-62][^cite_note-AlJazeera-63][^cite_note-Tehran_Times2-64]

### Fluctuation of death toll during aftermath

During rescue efforts, numerous sources reported fatality numbers other than the final official death toll. On 1 March, following the conclusion of search operations, Shiva Amelirad, representing the Coordinating Council of Iranian Teachers’ Trade Associations, told [*Time* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\)) that 108 or more children had been killed in the attack, according to sources within Minab she was in contact with.[^cite_note-Schneid-2026-27] Minab's [public prosecutor](440936.md) office reported later that day, through the state-run [Islamic Republic News Agency](841314.md), that 150 "innocent school girls" were killed, with some still trapped under the rubble.[^cite_note-Schneid-2026-27] The judicial head of Hormozgan stated that 140 of the deceased had been identified up to that point, while efforts continued to identify another 25 people, and that the remains of the bombs used in the attack had been located, seized, and transferred for analysis in preparation for an investigation.[^cite_note-75] On 3 March, *[Mizan News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizan_News_Agency)* claimed to have confirmed that 66 boys, 54 girls, 26 teachers, and four parents had been killed.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26] A majority of those reportedly killed were schoolchildren.[^cite_note-Middle_East_Eye-68][^cite_note-toll-69][^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-2026a-76]

## Aftermath

Immediate official statements in Iran included the Iranian government stating the missile was a US-Israeli airstrike, whereas Vice Governor of [Hormozgan](448064.md) Ahmad Nafisi said the school was struck amidst US-Israeli air raids on Minab.[^cite_note-77] Videos taken of the destroyed school immediately following the attack were verified by the NY&#x54;*,*[^cite_note-Solomon-2026-46] *[The Washington Post](102226.md)*,[^cite_note-wapo-78] *[Reuters](18998750.md)*, and Iranian fact-checking organization Factnameh, as authentic; these videos were compared against existing imagery of the school.[^cite_note-Parent-2026-49] *[Drop Site News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_Site_News)* published accounts of the incident from several parents whose children were among those killed.[^cite_note-Aslan-2026-65]

### Emergency and volunteer response

People search through rubble after the school airstrike, via [*Mehr News*](2707394.md)&#x20;

As reports emerged that the school had been hit, many panicked locals, including family members of victims, rushed to the scene while security forces attempted to push families back, fearing the area would be targeted again, and sealed off the building.[^cite_note-Aslan-2026-65] Soon after, recovery efforts began—some informal—as civilians, [Iranian Red Crescent Society](20826914.md) emergency workers, and individuals donning uniforms resembling the [Basij](2892082.md) searched through the debris.[^cite_note-Browne-2026-30][^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26] Although initially using just their hands,[^cite_note-Aslan-2026-65] rescue services used construction cranes and shovels to save people trapped by rubble,[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33][^cite_note-Solomon-2026-46] while black smoke scorching the remaining walls continued to pour from the building's windows.[^cite_note-Schneid-2026-27][^cite_note-Bosch-2026-79] Footage reveals items including severed arms,[^cite_note-Browne-2026-30] bodies, and school bags being recovered.[^cite_note-Bosch-2026-79][^cite_note-80] Other imagery confirmed that areas struck within the school were used for schooling.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26] One video shows a man, previously digging through the rubble, waving dust-covered textbooks and worksheets; in a quote translated by *The Guardian*, he yelled:[^cite_note-McClure-2026-35]

> These are the schoolbooks of the children who are under these ruins, under this rubble here. You can see the blood of these children on these books. These are civilians, who are not in the military. This was a school and they came to study.

During recovery efforts, an opening was created between the IRGC and school's compounds, allowing recovery teams to move rubble out of the way.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26] Corpses were collected in body bags and injured victims taken away in ambulances,[^cite_note-Browne-2026-30] with most initially being sent to the Hazrat Abolfazl located 2 km away from the school.[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33] Later in the day of the strike, bodies of the victims were transferred to their relatives gathering at a nearby designated collection area.[^cite_note-Aslan-2026-65] The attack flooded Minab's [morgues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgue), however, forcing some of the bodies of victims to be held in [refrigerated trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigerated_trucks).[^cite_note-Schneid-2026-27] Identifications efforts took time as some bodies were only able to be identified through the clothes victims were wearing.[^cite_note-\:12-62] Authorities announced the end of search operations for survivors on 1 March,[^cite_note-81] despite some bodies still needing identification.[^cite_note-\:14-82]

#### Search and identification of remains



The strike left some victims so [mutilated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilation), they were only identifiable from their clothing and belongings

Recovery operations at the site of the attack continued for approximately 7 months, led by a local forensic medicine department.[^cite_note-\:17-52][^cite_note-AlJazeera-63] In July 2026, Iranian officials in Hormozgan Province said that weeks of search operations at Shajareh Tayyebeh School and surrounding areas had uncovered 62 body fragments, which DNA testing identified as belonging to 32 student victims. The remains were buried on 22 July in a ceremony attended by the victims’ families and local residents.[^cite_note-83]

Seven-year-old [Makan Nasiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makan_Nasiri) was one of three children from the school whose remains were never recovered. Despite extensive searches by specialized teams and forensic examinations, no part of his remains was found. According to Iranian officials, the search for Nasiri was eventually closed, while a grave was prepared for him and his personal belongings, including a bloodstained sweater and a single shoe, were preserved in a glass display case at a nearby mosque.[^cite_note-AlJazeera-63] On 13 September, the head of Iran's Legal Medicine Organization, Abbas Masjedi, announced that all the bodies of the victims from Minab had been identified, except for one, which could not be identified due to its condition and the circumstances of the incident.[^cite_note-84]

### Mass funeral and burial

Mourners are gathered, some praying, in Minab's central square surrounding some of the caskets of the victims of the attack; a billboard of Ali Khamenei can be seen in the background.

Mourners are gathered around rows of graves, amounting to a hundred or more pits, waiting to be filled with the caskets of victims of the attack

The funeral and mass burial of victims of the attack, both attended by thousands of mourners

On 3 March, Iran held a mass funeral for the children killed in the airstrike in a public square in Minab, attended by thousands of mourners. Those in attendance held up imagery of the attack, faces of the [children killed](#Death_toll), and phrases condemning what one victim's mother called "a document of [American crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_war_crimes)".[^cite_note-\:14-82][^cite_note-Al_Jazeera,_Associated_Press,_Reuters-2026-85][^cite_note-Dunbar-2026-86] At least 165 [coffins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffin) draped with the [Iranian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Iran) were carried through Minab's streets on a truck.[^cite_note-\:14-82][^cite_note-87] Some were arranged together in the square, where mourners gathered to be led in a prayer service.[^cite_note-CBS_News-2026-55]

Later that day, an image shared by Iran's foreign minister, [Abbas Araghchi](21951274.md), show excavators preparing 100 or more graves at a mass burial site at Minab's Hermud cemetery—demarcated first in small white chalk rectangles[^cite_note-\:16-88]—whose grounds were subsequently filled with the mourners which held a procession bringing the caskets of victims to the site.[^cite_note-Da_Silva-2026-10][^cite_note-CBS_News-2026-55][^cite_note-Al_Jazeera,_Associated_Press,_Reuters-2026-85][^cite_note-\:16-88] Further imagery shows bodies wrapped in white [shrouds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shroud) being lowered into the prepared graves before being filled in.[^cite_note-CBS_News-2026-55] Some victims were buried at other sites outside Minab.[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33] The imagery was mired in uncertainty over its verifiability—promoted in part by AI models misattributing the image to other disasters—but was later confirmed by fact-checkers.[^cite_note-\:16-88][^cite_note-89]

## Investigations

### US Department of Defense

A week after the strike, on 5 March, *Reuters* reported that two American military personnel involved in an internal investigation believed the attack was likely perpetrated by the US, although a final conclusion had not yet been reached.[^cite_note-reutersinvestigation-91] On 11 March, the NYT reported that the preliminary findings of the investigation determined that the US was responsible for the strike. The inquiry suggested that the school was likely targeted due to outdated coordinates provided by the [Defense Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Intelligence_Agency).[^cite_note-\:13-17][^cite_note-92] CNN reported on 7 July that military intelligence systems had warned users that the information about the target was outdated, but the warnings were ignored.[^cite_note-93] In response to the attention to the NYT article, on 13 March, US secretary of defense [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md) promised a thorough probe into the strike, in what the *Washington Post* described as a tacit acknowledgement of US responsibility for the attack.[^cite_note-94] The DoD also said it elevated the investigation. Three US officials told *Reuters* that the administrative probe was known internally as a "15-6" \[United States Army Regulation 15-6], which could be used as basis for disciplinary action.[^cite_note-95]

As of 21 September 2026, the Pentagon's probe has not been released and remains under investigation. However, information divulged by officials revealed that within hours of the attack, Pentagon personnel knew the US was responsible. *[Bloomberg News](3057255.md)* reported on 18 September that officials describe it as a "cascade of preventable failures." According to officials, the strike was an accumulation of smaller bad decisions. Pressed by Trump in the final days of February, days before the strikes on Iran, officials said he requested an overwhelming aerial assault, compressing the time frame for compiling targets. Despite this, it was proven that there were "gaps in underlying information about the site, cuts in civilian-protection personnel, and an overreliance on \[AI] technology."[^cite_note-\:10-7]

FA—a multidisciplinary research group based out of [Goldsmiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldsmiths,_University_of_London), University of London—partially worked together with British news outlet *[Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News)*—whose *Full Story Films* commissioned FA's work—to independently investigate the strike. Conducted over the four and a half months following the strike, the investigation sought to reconstruct a chronology of events including geolocating, timing strikes, and stories of survivors.[^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-\:2-12] Techniques FA employed included [3D modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_modeling), image-data complex, [open-source intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_intelligence) (OSINT), [photogrammetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photogrammetry), and [cartographic regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map_regression).[^cite_note-\:2-12]

FA and *Sky News* published the results of their investigations on their separate platforms on 16 July 2026.[^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-\:2-12] FA said that "following the initial media reporting \[after the first strike], US forces would have had an opportunity to reassess their initial misidentification, before conducting subsequent strikes"—inherently blaming the US for the strikes.[^cite_note-\:2-12] *Sky News* corroborated this fact, concluding that "all evidence points to \[the] US being responsible".[^cite_note-\:3-6]

## Misinformation

Main article: [Misinformation during the 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misinformation_during_the_2026_Iran_war)

Following the attack, Israeli and Iranian [pro-monarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchism_in_Iran) opposition pages began disseminating [disinformation](142690.md) that the airstrike was a failed [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) interception. These claims, originating from associated [Telegram](41501305.md) channels, were debunked by news organizations,[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-2026b-32] with photographs used as supposed evidence of an IRGC misfire having actually been taken in the city of [Zanjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanjan,_Iran), 1,600 kilometres (990 mi) away from Minab.[^cite_note-McClure-2026-35][^cite_note-144] Other social media accounts claimed the footage was supposedly older footage shot in Pakistan, which has been debunked.[^cite_note-McClure-2026-35] The NYT stated that "a single errant missile wouldn't have caused such precise and targeted damage to several buildings across the naval base".[^cite_note-NYT_US_Strike-15] It was also falsely claimed on the social media website [X](9988187.md) that the IRGC had admitted to mistakenly destroying the school in a missile airstrike, despite the fact that no such statement admitting responsibility had been made by the Iranian state.[^cite_note-145]

U.S. president Trump claimed, without providing evidence, that Iran possessed Tomahawk missiles, and suggested that Iranian Tomahawk missiles may have been responsible for the attack on the school. *[PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS)* fact checkers rated Trump's claim as false, noting that the U.S. is the only party in the conflict known to possess Tomahawk missiles. They also cited video evidence released by CENTCOM, showing that the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) had launched several Tomahawks on 28 February, the same day the school was struck.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] A preliminary investigation by the US military further contradicted Trump's assertions, concluding that the attack was likely the result of a U.S. airstrike attacking the elementary school.[^cite_note-148]

## Analysis

### Targeting process and precision



Parallels have been drawn between Israel's [attacks on schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacks_on_schools_during_the_Gaza_war) during the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md) with the strike on the Minab school

An initial [*Al Jazeera*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English) investigation into the airstrike concluded that the attack was either based on "outdated intelligence" from before 2013, and would thus "constitute grave negligence," or that the attack was intentional and done "to inflict maximum societal shock and undermine popular support for Iran's military establishment,"[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-2026b-32] The investigation also linked the attack and subsequent misinformation to similar attacks targeting civilian facilities by the US and Israel, such as the [Bahr El-Baqar primary school bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahr_El-Baqar_primary_school_bombing) (1970), [Amiriyah shelter bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiriyah_shelter_bombing) (1991), [Qana massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qana_massacre) (1996), [Kunduz hospital airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz_hospital_airstrike) (2015), and [Israeli attacks on schools during the Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacks_on_schools_during_the_Gaza_war) (since 2023).[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-2026b-32] A *Reuters* investigation called into question the vetting processes for reviewing strike locations given reports of the US' potential use of outdated information, visibility from satellite imagery, and online presence.[^cite_note-Reuters-20260312-36]

Experts speaking to [NPR](29697232.md) corroborated the airstrike's precision. Researchers at [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University)'s Conflict Ecology Laboratory, Corey Scher and Jamon Van Den Hoek, noted that the detonation [centroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centroid) were "pretty clean." Satellite imagery specialist [Jeffrey Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Lewis_\(academic\)) agreed that the airstrikes were precision airstrikes but added that the strike being an error was more likely.[^cite_note-Brumfiel-2026-29] A later HRW report published satellite imagery showing damaged roofs of buildings within the compound, where "relatively small circular" entry points through the roofs demonstrated the munitions' precise nature.[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33]

#### Tagging, verification, and use of artificial intelligence



An example of Project Maven's analyst view for reviewing targets

Two people familiar with the logistics of the strike told *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* that intelligence had tagged the school as either a factory or arms depot before approving it as an airstrike target.[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-20260311-34] Numerous reports, outlets, and US senators raised concerns over the use of [artificial intelligence](1164.md) (AI) tools, including [Palantir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palantir)'s Maven Smart System and [Claude](75879512.md) systems.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26][^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-20260311-34][^cite_note-Schatz-2026-115][^cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post-20260313-149] One US-based source familiar with the process revealed that these systems were used to process vast amounts of data during the US's operations during the Iran war.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26][^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-20260311-34][^cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post-20260313-149] Furthermore, these AI tools are able to "evaluate a strike after it is initiated".[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-20260311-34] This opened the possibility for the strike being caused by these AI tools, either in part or without any further verification.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-20260316-26][^cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post-20260313-149] Many of these sources also raised concerns over who or what should be held responsible in cases like the one against the school.[^cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post-20260313-149]

### Responsibility, intentionality, and weaponry



Map presented by the DoD of the "First 100 Hours" of its strikes in Iran includes a circle centered on Minab

Shortly after the strike, satellite imagery specialist, Jeffrey Lewis, said the US was more likely than Israel to be responsible for the strike based on Minab's location as related to where the US had been striking.[^cite_note-Brumfiel-2026-29] After the first day of strikes, the US published a map of its strikes in Iran, which included a point on Minab;[^cite_note-\:5-16] the US and Israel had divided their strikes geographically, with the US responsible for striking targets in southern Iran where the school was located.[^cite_note-reutersinvestigation-91] Investigations by the [CBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation) and NPR corroborated Lewis' analysis, finding that the area where the school is located was the target of more than one precision strike, with imagery showing multiple craters and plumes of smoke present. Both investigations suggested that the strike was likely the result of a failure in intelligence gathering.[^cite_note-Brumfiel-2026-29][^cite_note-Angelovski-2026-150] An investigation by the NYT also concluded that the school was likely hit by a US precision strike.[^cite_note-NYT_US_Strike-15]



An American [destroyer](8779.md) firing a TLAM as part of Operation Epic Fury on the first day of strikes, 28 February

The CBC and NPR investigation also found that the school and surrounding complex fell under the area of operations of the US military, with verified US airstrikes having taken place nearby.[^cite_note-Brumfiel-2026-29][^cite_note-Angelovski-2026-150] Later investigations by *Bellingcat* and [BBC Verify](74154160.md) geolocated footage released by *Mehr*, showing a [subsonic cruise missile](6590.md) identified by both as an American [RGM/UGM-109 Tomahawk Land Attack Missile](31238.md) (TLAM) striking the area near the school shortly after it hit. The missile being a Tomahawk was confirmed by numerous other munition experts,[^cite_note-Bellingcat-20260308-14][^cite_note-BBC_News-20260309-48] including Sam Lair of the [James Martin Center for Nonproliferation Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Martin_Center_for_Nonproliferation_Studies) (CNS),[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-CNN-20260310-152][^cite_note-153] Director of [Armament Research Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armament_Research_Services) (ARES) N.R. Jenzen-Jones,[^cite_note-BBC050326-9][^cite_note-reutersinvestigation-91][^cite_note-\:6-154] U.S. Air Force Special Operations targeting expert Wes Bryant,[^cite_note-BBC_News-20260309-48] an anonymous former Navy official who helped develop the Tomahawk program,[^cite_note-\:6-154] among others.[^cite_note-\:5-16][^cite_note-CNN-20260310-152] Furthermore, the US was the only participant in the conflict to use the Tomahawk missile—with Lewis,[^cite_note-Brumfiel-2026-29] Jenzen-Jones,[^cite_note-\:6-154] and others saying the missile in the footage did not match any known weapons possessed by Iran.[^cite_note-Bellingcat-20260308-14][^cite_note-BBC_News-20260309-48][^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] Jenzen-Jones expanded that "despite various claims circulating online, the munition in question is clearly not an Iranian [Soumar missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soumar_\(missile\))."[^cite_note-157] Many of these sources, including *Bellingcat*, added that the footage having confirmed the US' striking of areas within the vicinity of the school contradicted Trump's claim that an Iranian missile hit the school.[^cite_note-CNN-20260310-152][^cite_note-Bellingcat-20260308-14] Despite being preprogrammed with target locations and flight path auto-adjustment capabilities, TLAMs are equipped with cameras which send images to operators. Operators can change its target and direction mid-flight.[^cite_note-\:6-154]



Since 2002, operators are able to reprogram the missile while in-flight, including the capability to loiter over the target area for some hours

A CBC investigation concluded that the school was bombed as part of a precision airstrike against the military complex adjacent to the targeted building and that it was not a mistake. It said the impact on the school demonstrated that it was either a weapons system failure or a serious CENTCOM intelligence gathering error, adding that the US primarily targeted military bases and missile launchers in [southern](36042664.md) and [central Iran](36063822.md). Israel, on the other hand, was focusing its operations in the [north](8825402.md) saying that "Minab's location in the south, near the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), places it within the US military's primary area of operations and lines up with other US strikes on the [Bandar Abbas](464348.md) Naval Base, about 80 kilometres west of Minab, and facilities in [Konarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konarak,_Iran), which is 400 kilometres to the southeast."[^cite_note-Angelovski-2026-150]

Photographs of missile fragments released by [Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting](819903.md) alongside independent video, satellite and debris analysis has consistently identified the munition as a Tomahawk cruise missile.[^cite_note-Byline_Times,_Overton,_Osman_(2026)-158][^cite_note-AOAV\:Osman-159] A satellite data link antenna was recovered from the site which was imprinted with the name of [Colorado](5399.md)-based manufacturer, [Ball Aerospace & Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_Aerospace_&_Technologies), owned by British arms-manufacturer [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems).[^cite_note-160] Further analysis of the debris connects it to a contract issued by the United States [Naval Air Systems Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Systems_Command) to contractor [Raytheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raytheon) on 24 September 2014 for the production of 231 Tomahawk Block IV missiles for \$251,133,201.[^cite_note-AOAV\:Osman-159]

### Legality

Shannon Bosch, an associate professor of law at [Edith Cowan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Cowan_University), analyzed the strike from the perspective of [international humanitarian law](1093604.md) (IHL). Without drawing firm conclusions, Bosch noted that schools and children under 18 are especially protected under IHL, concluding that "the legality of that strike turns on whether the expected harm to children and the school was excessive compared to the military advantage gained by striking the target".[^cite_note-Bosch-2026-79] UN human rights experts characterized the strike as a potential [war crime](48626.md) under the [Rome Statute](68762.md).[^cite_note-161] Regarding the attack, international humanitarian law expert [Janina Dill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janina_Dill) said that attackers are required to "verify the status" of targets to avoid harming civilians. [Beth Van Schaack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Van_Schaack) said that the US "should have known that a school was in the vicinity".[^cite_note-NYT_US_Strike-15] HRW said the same, adding that beyond the laws of war requiring precise intelligence, the strike's proximity to civilian infrastructure required advanced notice.[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-20260307-33]

Oona Hathaway, a legal scholar at [Yale University](34273.md) and then president-elect of the [American Society of International Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_International_Law), said that the attack on Iran "appeared to violate international law" because it lacked UN approval and was not necessary for immediate self-defense. Regarding the school strike specifically, Hathaway said that it would not necessarily be a war crime if it was an honest mistake, but that if old targeting data reflected "a reckless lack of care", it might rise to one.[^cite_note-162]

### Media coverage

Polish journalist [Mariusz Zawadzki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariusz_Zawadzki?action=edit\&redlink=1), writing for *[Gazeta Wyborcza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazeta_Wyborcza)*, stated that in the week after the attack, the event was being mostly ignored by [Western media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_media).[^cite_note-163] Following the attack, the Iranian Revayat-e Fath Institute created an AI-generated [Lego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego)-styled video of the Iranian military attacking neighboring countries and Israeli and American military outposts. The video was declared to be in remembrance of the victims of the Minab school attack, and it was redistributed by various government-run outlets and the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](1166971.md).[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]
