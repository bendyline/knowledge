**Lagos** ([Pronunciation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/6a/Ta-Lagos.ogg/Ta-Lagos.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [/ˈleɪɡɒs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*LAY-goss*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-\:14-13][^cite_note-m-w-14][^cite_note-15] [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language): *Èkó* [\[èkó\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Yoruba)), or **Lagos City**, is a large metropolitan port city in southwestern [Nigeria](21383.md). As of November 2025, the size of the city's population has been estimated to be between 17 and 21 million residents, making Lagos the largest city in Nigeria, the most populous urban area on the African continent,[^cite_note-\:16-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] and one of the fastest-growing [megacities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacity) in the world.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

The lands are separated by creeks, fringing the southwest mouth of [Lagos Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Lagoon), while being protected from the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) by [barrier islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_island) and long sand spits such as [Bar Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Beach,_Lagos), which stretch up to 100 kilometres (62 mi) east and west of the mouth.&#x20;

With its [international airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtala_Muhammed_International_Airport), three ports ([Apapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apapa_Port_Complex), Tin Can Island and [Lekki deep-sea port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Port)), four expressways (to [Badagry](39680858.md), [Abeokuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos%E2%80%93Abeokuta_Expressway), [Ibadan](46727447.md) and [Calabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos-Calabar_Coastal_Highway), the latter under construction since 2023 and partially operational in 2026), and the [standard-gauge rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway) link[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-Lagos-Ibadan_train_Experience-32] to [Abeokuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeokuta) and [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan)[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34], the city is well connected in terms of transport and handles 88% of Nigeria’s exports.[^cite_note-35] The [urban rail transit system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Rail_Mass_Transit) in Lagos is regarded as one of the most modern of its kind in Africa.[^cite_note-LAMATA_rail_service_expands_to_94_daily_trips-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

Lagos is best known as the leading financial centre of West and Central Africa, with headquarters of financial institutions like [Access Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Access_Bank_Group), the [First Bank of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bank_of_Nigeria) or [Zenith Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith_Bank). The [Nigerian Exchange Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Exchange_Group), the main stock exchange group in Nigeria, is also located here. Other major employers include two breweries, three major shipyards and the headquarters of several African [airlines](1942.md). Lagos is also home to numerous pharmaceutical companies[^cite_note-39]; the city is also an exhibition city: [Art X Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ART_X_Lagos) is West Africa’s largest art fair[^cite_note-West_Africa's_Capital_Of_Culture-40], whilst [Lagos Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Fashion_Week) is Africa’s largest fashion fair[^cite_note-SystemMagazineInterviewOmoyemiAkerele-41]. It has more than 20 data centres in operation and branches of [Microsoft](19001.md), [Equinix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equinix) and [Google](1092923.md).[^cite_note-42] Five out of seven African high tech start-ups with a value above US\$ 1 [billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billion) ("[unicorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicorn_\(finance\))") are located in Lagos.[^cite_note-43] The employer with the most staff is the [Dangote Refinery](58744636.md), which, once expanded, will be the world’s largest oil refinery.[^cite_note-44]

Lagos houses four public universities ([LASU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_State_University), [UNILAG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lagos), [LASUSTECH](https://lasustech.edu.ng/), and [LASUED](https://lasued.edu.ng/web/)) and six private universities as well as scientific facilities like the [Institute for Oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Institute_for_Oceanography_and_Marine_Research) and the [Institute of Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Institute_of_Medical_Research). Lagos is also a cultural hub. The [National Arts Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre,_Nigeria) in [Surulere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surulere),[^cite_note-45] the National Gallery of Modern Art (in the same building), the [Centre For Black And African Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Black_and_African_Arts_and_Civilization) on Broad Street, the [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_National_Museum) and the [John Randle Centre for Yoruba Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Randle_Centre_for_Yoruba_Culture_and_History)[^cite_note-46] in Onikan and privately run galleries like [Nike Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Art_Gallery) (West Africa's biggest art collection[^cite_note-47]) display Nigerias culture. The conservatoire [MUSON Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muson_Centre), many local orchestras[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] and choirs (like the Lagos City Chorale[^cite_note-50] or the Cathedral Church of Christ Choir[^cite_note-51]) as well as writers like [Sefi Atta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefi_Atta), [Oyinkan Braithwaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyinkan_Braithwaite) and [Ayòbámi Adébáyò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayobami_Adebayo) and artists like [Dotun Popoola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotun_Popoola), [Peju Alatise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peju_Alatise), [Idowu Sonaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idowu_Sonaya?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Collins Abinoro Akporode represent Lagos' creative scene. Furthermore, Lagos is the centre of media outlets and of Nigerian cinema, "[Nollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nollywood)". The lagoon city also is a hot spot for West African music ("[Afrobeats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrobeats)"); in its recording studios Nigerian stars like [Wizkid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizkid), [Tems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tems), [Davido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davido), and [Asake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asake) have started their careers. International stars like [Paul McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney), [James Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brown), [Ed Sheeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Sheeran) and [Beyoncé](83688.md) have recorded albums there.

## Etymology

*Lagos* is derived from the Portuguese word for "lakes". The pronunciation [/ˈleɪɡɒs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) ([*LAY-goss*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is typically standard in [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) and [Nigerian English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_English).[^cite_note-wells-52][^cite_note-hd-53] The native [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language) name *Èkó* is also used by [Yoruba people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people). Lagos was most likely named after [Lagos, Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos,_Portugal), as it was the main centre of Portuguese maritime expeditions down the African coast in the 15th century.[^cite_note-54]

## History

Main article: [History of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Lagos)

Further information: [Lagos Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Colony)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Lagos).

Lagos emerged as a home to the [Awori people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awori_people), a subgroup of the [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people) of [West Africa](67393.md) in the 15th century. They lived in the area which is centered on the present-day Local Government Areas ([LGAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lagos_State_local_government_areas_by_population)) of [Lagos Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Island), [Eti-Osa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eti-Osa), [Amuwo-Odofin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amuwo-Odofin), and [Apapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apapa). Before the 15th century, the Awori settled on a farmstead along the coastal line, in and around which they worked and lived. "Farmstead" or "hamlet" translates to *Ereko* in Yoruba,[^cite_note-55] from which comes the indigenous name of Lagos; *Eko*, which it was named by the natives from its earliest days as a farm before it was eventually permanently settled by the Aworis of nearby [Iddo Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iddo_Island).[^cite_note-56]<sup>: 43–44 </sup>

Due to rapid urbanisation, the city expanded to the west of the lagoon to include areas in the present-day [Lagos Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Mainland), [Ajeromi-Ifelodun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajeromi-Ifelodun), and [Surulere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surulere). This led to the classification of Lagos into two main areas: the [Island](#Island), which was the original city of Lagos, and the [Mainland](#Mainland), which it has since expanded into.[^cite_note-Lagos_Case_Study-57] This city area was governed directly by the Federal Government through the Lagos City Council until the creation of Lagos State in 1967, which led to the splitting of Lagos city into the present-day seven Local Government Areas (LGAs), and the addition of other towns (which now make up 13 LGAs) from the then [Western Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Region,_Nigeria) to form the state.[^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-58] Present-day Lagos consists of 16 LGAs, including Ikeja, the state capital of Lagos State.[^cite_note-metropolitan_Lagos-5][^cite_note-59] While this conurbation encompasses 37% of Lagos State's total land area, it accommodates approximately 85% of the state's total population.[^cite_note-metropolitan_Lagos-5][^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-58][^cite_note-60]

Lagos was the previous national capital of Nigeria, however, in February 1976, [the government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Nigeria) made the decision to relocate the capital, and initiated its removal to the inland city of [Abuja](56823.md) in the centre of the country. After its designation, construction of the new national capital started in earnest, and on December 12 1991, the seat of government officially relocated.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

## Administration

Lagos was formerly the capital city of [Nigeria](21383.md), but it has since been replaced by [Abuja](56823.md). Abuja officially became the capital of Nigeria on 12 December 1991, although the decision to move the federal capital had been made fifteen years earlier, in Act No. 6 of 1976. Lagos is also home to the High Court of the [Lagos State Judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_State_Judiciary), housed in an old colonial building on Lagos Island.[^cite_note-64]



A map showing the 16 [LGAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Areas_in_Nigeria) making up the Lagos Metropolitan Area

In terms of administration, Lagos is not a single [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) and therefore has no overall city administration.[^cite_note-65] The geographical city limits of Metropolitan Lagos comprise 16 of the 20 Local Government Areas of Lagos State. The latter entity provides overall government for the metropolitan region. The former Municipality of Lagos, which covered Lagos Island, Ikoyi, and Victoria Island as well as some mainland territory, was managed by the Lagos City Council (LCC), but it was disbanded in 1976 and divided into several [Local Government Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Areas_in_Nigeria) (most notably Lagos Island LGA, [Lagos Mainland LGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Mainland), and [Eti-Osa LGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eti-Osa)).[^cite_note-66]

The mainland beyond the Municipality of Lagos, on the other hand, included several separate towns and settlements such as [Mushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushin,_Nigeria), Ikeja, and Agege. In the wake of the 1970s Nigerian oil boom, Lagos experienced a population explosion, untamed economic growth, and unmitigated rural migration. This caused the outlying towns and settlements to develop rapidly, thus forming the present-day "Lagos Metropolitan Area", also known as "Metropolitan Lagos". The history of Lagos is still evidenced in the layout of the LGAs that display the unique identities of the cultures that created them.[^cite_note-67]

By 2006, the metro area around Lagos had extended beyond Lagos State's boundaries and attained a [megacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacity) status. This much larger area is referred to as "Greater Metropolitan Lagos" or "Lagos Megacity Region", which is a continuously built-up land area of an additional 1,535.4 square kilometres (592.8 [square miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_mile)), in LGAs situated next to Lagos's eastern and western city limits in Lagos State, and also beyond its northern limits, spilling into some LGAs in adjoining [Ogun State](1436438.md). Ogun State LGAs that have become part of Greater Metropolitan Lagos include [Obafemi Owode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obafemi_Owode), [Sagamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagamu), [Ifo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifo,_Ogun_State), [Ado-Odo/Ota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ado-Odo/Ota), and part of [Ewekoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewekoro).[^cite_note-The_sustainable_city-68]

Today, the word *Lagos* most often refers to the urban area, called "Metropolitan Lagos" in Nigeria, which includes both the islands of the former municipality of Lagos and the mainland suburbs. The Lagos State government is responsible for some of the utilities, including roads and transportation, power, water, health, and education. Metropolitan Lagos extends over 16 of the 20 LGAs of [Lagos State](626168.md) and contains about 85% of the population of Lagos State, including some semi-rural areas.[^cite_note-METRO-69] Lagos has a considerable number of high-rise buildings that dominate its skyline. Most of the tall buildings are located in the downtown Central Business District.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

## Demography

The population of Metropolitan Lagos is disputed.[^cite_note-72] In the 2006 federal census data, the conurbation had a population of about 9 million people.[^cite_note-Metropolitan_Lagos_population-73] However, the figure was disputed by the [Lagos State Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_State_Government), which later released its own population data, putting the population of [Lagos Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Metropolitan_Area_Transport_Authority) at approximately 16 million.[^cite_note-LGA-6] Daily, the Lagos area is growing by some 3,000 people or around 1.1 million annually, so the true population figure of the greater Lagos area in 2022 is roughly 28 million (up from some 23.5 million in 2018).&#x20;

Lagos may therefore have overtaken [Kinshasa](45773.md) as Africa's most populous city.[^cite_note-metrolagospop-4][^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-58][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-The_sustainable_city-68] The Lagos conurbation is part of an emerging transnational [megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalopolis) on the coast of West Africa that includes areas in five [sovereign states](23604120.md), the [Abidjan–Lagos Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abidjan%E2%80%93Lagos_Corridor).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]&#x20;

Although the 2006 National Population Census of [Nigeria](21383.md) credited the metropolitan area with a population figure of 7,937,932, the figure is at variance with some projections by the United Nations and other population agencies and groups worldwide. The population figure of [Lagos State](626168.md) given by the Lagos State Government is 17,553,924. That figure was based on claimed conducted enumeration for social planning by the Lagos State Government's "parallel census" and it believes that since the inhabitants of the metropolitan area of Lagos constitute 88% of the Lagos State population, the population of metropolitan Lagos is about 15.5 million.[^cite_note-79]

A rejoinder to Lagos State Government views[^cite_note-80] concluded that Lagos State concealed the fact that the UN agencies' population projection for Lagos Urban Agglomeration had been revised downwards substantially as early as 2003. It failed to interpret the two most important and fairly representative and reliable secondary data sets already in the public domain, the National Identity Card Scheme and the 2003 Voters Registration figures from INEC. The figures for 2007 voter registration by INEC were an act sequel to the release of the provisional census results and comprehensively corroborated, vindicated, and validated the population figures. According to the official results of the 2006 census, there were 8,048,430 inhabitants in Metropolitan Lagos.[^cite_note-Metropolitan_Lagos_population-73] This figure was lower than anticipated and has created controversy in Nigeria. Lagos Island, the central Local Government Area and historic center of Metropolitan Lagos, had a population of 212,700 at the 2006 Census.[^cite_note-81]

Authorities of Lagos State have disputed the results of the 2006 census, accusing the Nigerian National Population Commission of undercounting the population of the state. This accusation is denied by the National Population Commission.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] A study found that research carried out by Africapolis (the African subsidiary of e-Geopolis backed by the [Agence française de développement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Development_Agency)), in addition to the cross-referencing of official figures with more scientific independent research, concluded that the 2006 census figures for Lagos State of about 9 million were valid and that the state's own assessments are inflated.[^cite_note-84]

Lagos is indigenous to the [Yoruba people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people), and the [Yoruba language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language) is widely spoken. It is, by most estimates, one of the fastest-growing cities in the world.[^cite_note-africareport-85] Lagos is experiencing a population increase of about 275,000 persons per annum due to the influx of people from other regions for economic purposes. In 1999, the [United Nations](31769.md) predicted that the city's metropolitan area, which had only about 290,000 inhabitants in 1950, would exceed 20 million by 2010 and thus become one of the ten most populous cities in the world.[^cite_note-86]

### Cityscape

Lagos is loosely classified into two main geographical areas—"Lagos Island" and the "Mainland".[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

The historic city centre is situated in the western half of Lagos Island, within the Ring Road (E1), with Marina Road, Broad Street, Campbell Street, Igbosere Road and Bamgbose Street/Nnamdi Azikiwe Street running parallel to the coastline towards the Commodore Canal, and Balogun Street, Martin Street, Custom Street, Kakawa Street, Odunlami Street, Campos Street, Joseph Harden Street and Brook Street running perpendicular to it. As in the historic centres of other cities, this area is characterised by social hotspots, chaotic traffic conditions, unaffordable property prices and historic buildings, the proper maintenance of which the city can only afford to a limited extent.

Outside this area, there is a marked disparity in prosperity within the city[^cite_note-VIvsIkoyi-90], with the south-eastern part – comprising neighbourhoods such as [Ikoyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikoyi), [Victoria Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Island) and [Lekki](44625841.md), home to bank headquarters and fintech start-ups, the Free (Trade) Zone, the deep-sea port, the Dangote Refinery, hotels and tourist attractions – being described as affluent.[^cite_note-VIvsIkoyi-90] By contrast, the western and northern outskirts of the city, with neighbourhoods such as Surelere, Oshodi and Agege, have been able to keep up only to a limited extent with the city’s rapid development since 2010.[^cite_note-91]

#### Lagos Island

Main article: [Lagos Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Island)

Lagos Island is surrounded by the lagoon (from which it takes its name), the Commodore Channel (which separates the island from the mainland and Apapa) and Five Cowrie Creek (which lies between it and Victoria Island respectively the Lekki Peninsula). During the colonial era, the eastern half of Lagos Island – Ikoyi – was home mainly to light-skinned residents (including a secondary school and a hospital for ‘whites’). To enforce racial segregation, an artificial canal – the McGregor Canal – was dug between the two halves of the island around 1900. This effectively made Lagos Island and Ikoyi two separate islands.[^cite_note-\:21-92] In the 1970s, the canal was filled in to allow for the construction of the Ring Road (E1) around the old town centre. As a result, Ikoyi is, geographically speaking, once again part of Lagos Island – yet both halves of the island continue to exhibit completely different social and architectural structures to this day and can therefore still be regarded as distinct neighbourhoods.&#x20;

&#x20;Three major bridges join this Island to the Mainland. They are the [Carter Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Bridge), which starts from Iddo; the [Eko Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eko_Bridge) (formerly called the Second Mainland Bridge); and the [Third Mainland Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Mainland_Bridge), which passes through densely populated mainland suburbs to the Lagos Lagoon.



Lagos Island, view from Eko Atlantic (2026)

The western part of Lagos Island, together with [Calabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabar), was one of the two starting points for British colonisation. From this strategically situated island, the British were able to control the entire wider region with little effort. Locals and slaves returning from [Sierra Leone](27308.md), [Brazil](3383.md) and [Cuba](5042481.md) were able to conduct business under the protection of the [Royal Navy](26061.md), and turned the marshy island into what it remains today: a pre-eminent financial centre.[^cite_note-\:21-92]

Lagos Island contains a [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district).[^cite_note-93] This district is characterized by high-rise buildings. The Island also contains many of the city's largest wholesale marketplaces (such as the popular [Idumota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idumota_Market) and [Balogun Markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balogun_Market)).[^cite_note-94] It also has the National Museum of Nigeria, the Central Mosque, the [Glover Memorial Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glover_Memorial_Hall), [Christ's Church Cathedral (CMS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_Church_of_Christ,_Lagos), and the [Oba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oba_of_Lagos)'s [Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iga_Idunganran) ([Iga Idunganran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iga_Idunganran)).[^cite_note-95] Another major part of Lagos Island is Marina. It borders the Idumota and Balogun markets and houses major Banking institutions. Though formerly in a derelict condition, Lagos Island's [Tinubu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinubu_Square) is a site of historical importance; it was here that the Amalgamation Ceremony that unified the North and South [protectorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) to form Nigeria took place in 1914.[^cite_note-96]

##### Ikoyi

Main article: [Ikoyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikoyi)



Aerial view of Ikoyi (Victoria Island in the background)

[Ikoyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikoyi) is situated on the eastern half of Lagos Island.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] Ikoyi is connected to Victoria Island by Falomo bridge, which carries the main road over Five Cowrie creek.[^cite_note-99] The Ikoyi link bridge links Ikoyi and Lekki Phase 1.[^cite_note-100]

Since colonial times, Ikoyi has been a neighbourhood for the more affluent, although since independence in 1960 it has no longer been exclusively for light-skinned people. Wide, well-maintained avenues with bicycle lanes and ostentatious buildings belonging to the nouveau riche characterise this neighbourhood[^cite_note-101] – named after the British governors who administered Nigeria from Lagos (such as Bourdillon). Alfred Rewane Road is home to the offices of international companies such as [Microsoft](19001.md), [Google](1092923.md), [Dangote](22094689.md), [Lufthansa](97733.md), [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung), [BAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_American_Tobacco) and others. The Ikoyi Club 1938, which was also originally an exclusively ‘white’ establishment[^cite_note-102], remains exclusive and retains its status as one of the largest and prestigious golf courses in Africa[^cite_note-103] despite the astronomical property prices in the surrounding area. The [Dodan Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodan_Barracks) (originally built in Ikoyi by the colonial rulers to protect them against uprisings from other parts of the city) served as Nigeria’s centre of power during the military dictatorships until the 1990s, when Abuja became the new capital.[^cite_note-104] Military ruler [Murtala Muhammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtala_Muhammed) was assassinated by coup plotters in 1976 near the barracks on Ikoyi Road – a memorial commemorates him. [Radio Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Radio_Corporation_of_Nigeria) has been broadcasting from Ikoyi since colonial times and was for a long time the country’s only mass medium.[^cite_note-105] The Federal High Court of Nigeria also resides in Ikoyi.[^cite_note-106]

##### Victoria Island

Main article: [Victoria Island (Nigeria)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Island_\(Nigeria\))



[Eko Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eko_Atlantic), a project at the mouth of [Lagos Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Lagoon) under construction, extending and further developing Victoria Island (as seen from [Tarkwa Bay Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarkwa_Bay_Beach))

Victoria Island with its annex is situated to the south of Lagos Island and known with a zip code of 101241[^cite_note-107] as assigned by NIPOST.[^cite_note-Conference-108] It has expensive real estate properties and for that reason, many new luxury condos and apartments. Along with Ikoyi, Victoria Island occupies a major area in Lagos that boasts several shopping districts. On its seashore along the Atlantic front, there is an environmentally reconstructed [Bar Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Beach,_Lagos).[^cite_note-109]

##### Ajah/Lekki

The Lekki Peninsula shares some prestige with its Ikoyi and Victoria Island neighbours. Development has stretched the piece of land further such that the Ibeju axis, though closer to Epe (which is on the outskirts of Lagos) is almost always described as part of Lekki. The expanse of land starts from the Lekki toll gate, which was the focal stage of the famous #EndSars protest in October 2020, and ends in Ibeju-Lekki and boasts of communities slowly inching their way to suburb status such as [Ajah](68796572.md), Awoyaya, Sangotedo, Abijo, and Eputu. There is quite a bit of place to see – the [Lekki Conservation Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Conservation_Centre); The Novare Mall; The [Lekki Free Trade Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Free_Trade_Zone) – Dangote, Africa's richest man is building his refinery in this FTZ; [Lagos Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Business_School); [Eleko Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleko_Beach); [Elegushi Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elegushi_Beach); [La Campagne Tropicana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Campagne_Tropicana) – a beach/tourist getaway, [Pan-Atlantic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Atlantic_University). The area has a Catholic monastery.[^cite_note-110]

##### Iddo

Across the main channel of the lagoon from Lagos Island, there is a smaller settlement called [Iddo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iddo_Island). Iddo is also [a railroad terminus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Terminus_railway_station) and it is now situated in the Lagos Mainland Local Government Area after it was connected to the Mainland like a peninsula.[^cite_note-111]

#### Mainland

A huge population of Lagosians also live on the Lagos Mainland, and most industries are located there. The Mainland is known for its music and nightlife, which used to be located in areas around [Yaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaba,_Lagos), Ikeja, and [Surulere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surulere). However, in recent years more nightclubs have sprung up on the Island, making the Island (particularly Victoria Island, Ikate, and Lekki Phase 1) the main nightlife attraction. Mainland LGAs include Surulere, [Apapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apapa), and [Lagos Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Mainland). Metropolitan Lagos suburban LGAs include: [Agege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agege), [Amuwo Odofin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amuwo_Odofin), [Mushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushin,_Lagos), [Oshodi-Isolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshodi-Isolo) and Ikeja (site of [Murtala Muhammed International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtala_Muhammed_International_Airport) and the capital of Lagos State).[^cite_note-112]

Major areas on the Mainland include [Ebute Metta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebute_Metta), [Yaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaba,_Lagos) and [Ejigbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejigbo,_Lagos). Some rivers, like [Badagry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badagry) Creek, flow parallel to the coast for some distance before exiting through the sand bars to the sea.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

## Climate

Lagos experiences a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) (*Aw*) according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), as there are three months under 60 millimetres or 2.4 inches of rain, and annual rainfall is not nearly high enough for [tropical monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) classification. The wet season starts in May and ends in October, while the dry season starts in November and ends in April. There is also a short dry season from July to August. The wettest month is June with precipitation total 316 millimetres or 12.44 inches, while the driest month is January with precipitation total 13 millimetres or 0.51 inches.

Located near the equator, Lagos has only a slight seasonal temperature variation, with mean high temperatures ranging from 28 to 32 °C (82 to 90 °F). Lagos has the highest temperatures in March with a daily range from 32 to 26 °C (90 to 79 °F), and least hot temperatures in August ranging from 28 to 24 °C (82 to 75 °F), not unlike the seasons of coastal locations in the Southern Hemisphere. Summer is the coolest season, with June, July and August are the coldest and rainiest months, while spring (March - May) is the hottest season.&#x20;

### Climate change

A 2019 paper published in [PLOS One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_One) estimated that under [Representative Concentration Pathway 4.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_Concentration_Pathway#4.5), a "moderate" scenario of [climate change](5042951.md) where global warming reaches \~2.5–3 °C (4.5–5.4 °F) by 2100, the climate of Lagos in the year 2050 would most closely resemble the current climate of [Panama City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_City). The annual temperature would increase by 1.6 °C (2.9 °F) and the temperature of the warmest month by 1.5 °C (2.7 °F), while the temperature of the coldest month would be 2.9 °C (5.2 °F) higher.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] According to [Climate Action Tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Action_Tracker), the current warming trajectory appears consistent with 2.7 °C (4.9 °F), which closely matches RCP 4.5.[^cite_note-121]

Moreover, according to the 2022 [IPCC Sixth Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Sixth_Assessment_Report), Lagos is one of 12 major African cities ([Abidjan](21371767.md), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), [Algiers](1644.md), [Cape Town](6653.md), [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), [Dakar](44251.md), [Dar es Salaam](8500.md), [Durban](313650.md), Lagos, [Lomé](57067.md), [Luanda](18402.md) and [Maputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo)) that would be the most severely affected by [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise). It estimates that they would collectively sustain cumulative damage of US\$65 billion under RCP 4.5 and US\$86.5 billion in the high-emission scenario RCP 8.5 by the year 2050. Additionally, RCP 8.5 combined with the hypothetical impact from [marine ice sheet instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ice_sheet_instability) at high levels of warming would involve up to US\$137.5 billion in damage, while the additional accounting for the "low-probability, high-damage events" may increase aggregate risks to US\$187 billion for the "moderate" RCP4.5, US\$206 billion for RCP8.5 and US\$397 billion under the high-end ice sheet instability scenario.[^cite_note-122] Since sea level rise would continue for about 10,000 years under every scenario of climate change, future costs of sea level rise would only increase, especially without adaptation measures.[^cite_note-123] Sea level rise is being exacerbated by [subsidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidence), which is occurring at up to 87 millimetres (3.4 in) per year.[^cite_note-124]

## Economy

The city of Lagos is a major economic focal point in Nigeria, generating around 30-35% of the country's GDP. Most commercial and financial businesses are carried out in the [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_districts) situated on the island. This is also where most of the country's commercial banks, financial institutions, and major corporations are headquartered. Lagos is also the major information communications and telecommunications (ICT) hub of West Africa.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] Lagos is developing a [24-hour economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24/7_service).[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-nightlife-128] Yves Bellinghausen summed up the city’s economy as follows: 'Lagos is Africa's [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles), [Manhattan](45470.md), and [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley) all rolled into one.'[^cite_note-129]

The globalisation of Lagos's economy is rated "beta minus" by the GaWC.[^cite_note-130] This is equivalent to [Manchester](20206.md) or [Edinburgh](9602.md) in the [UK](31717.md). Lagos is thus the most "globalised" city in West and Central Africa.[^cite_note-131] 5 out of 7 African tech "[unicorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicorn_\(finance\))" operate out of Lagos (see below).[^cite_note-\:1-132] Lagos is home to more tech centres than any other city in Africa.[^cite_note-\:0-133]

### Financial institutions



Financial district of Lagos

Lagos is a major financial and banking centre. The four largest banks in West and Central Africa are headquartered in Lagos,[^cite_note-\:5-134] and another nine banks in Lagos are among the 20 largest banks in the region. [Zenith Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith_Bank), [Access Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Access_Bank_plc), [Guaranty Trust Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaranty_Trust_Holding_Company_PLC) and [First Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bank_of_Nigeria) have capital of more than US\$2 billion each.[^cite_note-\:5-134] Banking headquarters are located on Victoria Island and Lagos Island.

The national stock exchange, the [Nigerian Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Exchange_Group), is also located here (in the background on the right in the adjacent picture). Furthermore, the NASD OTC Securities Exchange (on the left, in the light-coloured building) and the FMDQ Group are based here on [Victoria Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Island,_Lagos).

The insurance industry in Nigeria is comparatively and modestly developed, with an industry turnover of around US\$1 billion per year.[^cite_note-135] As with the banks, the headquarters of the insurance companies are predominantly located in Lagos.

### Ports, shipyards

Main articles: [Apapa Port Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apapa_Port_Complex), [Tin Can Island Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_Can_Island_Port), and [Lekki Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Port)



Entrance of the Lekki Deep Sea Port

&#x20;\
&#x20;The Port of Lagos, formally known as the Lagos-Elbert Mathews Memorial Port, is Nigeria's leading [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) and one of the largest and busiest Ports in Africa. Due to the large urban population, Lagos is categorized as a medium-port megacity using the Southampton System for port-city classification.[^cite_note-136] It is administered by the [Nigerian Ports Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Ports_Authority).&#x20;

The *Port of Lagos / Apapa* is the oldest and largest port in the country, both in terms of land area and cargo volume handled. More than half of Nigeria's maritime trade is handled here, and the port also acts as a transhipment point for landlocked countries such as Chad and Niger. Around 1,000 ships with 5,700,000 tonnes of cargo call at the Lagos port complex annually.[^cite_note-\:4-137] *Tin Can Island Port* is located west of Apapa near the Lagos Port Complex. It was established in 1975. In early 2023, the [*deep sea port of Lekki*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Port) was commissioned 50 km east of Lagos. This thus does not belong to the urban area of Lagos but to the [state of the same name](626168.md). In 2025, a decision was taken to modernise the ports of Apapa and Tin Can Island at a cost of one billion US dollars.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] The fourth (and easternmost) port in Lagos worth mentioning is the port of the [Dangote Refinery](58744636.md), which supplies the refinery with crude oil from the Niger Delta or, for example, from [Angola](701.md), and ships out products such as petrol and [kerosene](16992.md).

Three of Nigeria’s four largest shipyards are located in Lagos: [Nigerdock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerdock) (Snake Island), Naval Dockyard Ltd. (Victoria Island) and [Samsung Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Heavy_Industries) Mega Construction Yard (Tarkwa Bay). Nigerdock is the largest shipyard in West Africa. It is situated in the Snake Island Integrated Free Zone and boasts the country’s largest dry dock (25,000 DWT).[^cite_note-140] It is a major hub for shipbuilding, ship repair and oil and gas production. Naval Dockyard Limited is owned by the Nigerian Navy but serves customers from the merchant shipping and oil and gas sectors. It is one of the most efficient and best-equipped technical facilities in West Africa.[^cite_note-141] Samsung Heavy Industries Mega Construction Yard is a huge integrated shipyard built primarily for the assembly of extremely large floating production, storage and offloading units (FPSOs) and for heavy shipbuilding.

### Oil refinery

Main article: [Dangote Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangote_Refinery)

For decades, there was no oil processing industry in Nigeria, apart from illegal refineries in the Niger Delta (which are very polluting due to the lack of [cracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cracking_\(chemistry\))). Nigeria therefore had to have the end products of domestic crude oil such as [fuels](21188370.md), [bitumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitumen), [paraffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraffin_\(fuel\)), [motor oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_oil), [polypropylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polypropylene) etc. produced in US or European refineries, with transport costs over thousands of nautical miles and margins for middlemen. The oil refinery in Lekki went into operation in December 2023[^cite_note-149] and presently produces 650,000 barrels of oil per day, with plans to expand capacity to 14 million barrels per day.[^cite_note-150]

In October 2025, Dangote announced plans to more than double the daily capacity of the Lekki refinery to 1.4 million barrels at a cost of USD 350 million. This would make Lekki the largest refinery in the world.[^cite_note-151] During the same period, the neighbouring states of [Ogun](1436438.md) and [Ondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondo_State) announced their own plans to build oil refineries: Ogun on Tongeji Island near [Ipokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipokia) (close to [Badagry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badagry)) and Ondo in the ‘Sunshine Free Trade Zone Igotiri’ in [Ilaje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaje), 50 km east of the Lekki refinery.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]

## Culture

Lagos is a cultural centre of Nigeria. As a [port city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) and the starting point of [British colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), the Western influence is stronger here than in probably any other Nigerian city. All Nigerian ethnic groups can be found in the [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot) of this [metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis), with the [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people) predominating. The [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Nigeria) and [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Nigeria) industries in the city are dynamic centres of the country with international acclaim.

### Architecture

Main article: [Architecture of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Lagos)

The city of Lagos has the tallest skyline in Nigeria. The architectural styles in Lagos are diverse, ranging from tropical and vernacular to colonial European and ultramodern buildings, or a mixture. Brazilian-style architecture brought by the creoles is evident in buildings such as Water House and Shitta Bey Mosque.[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199] Skyscrapers and most high-rise buildings are centered on the islands, while the mainland has some high-rise buildings.[^cite_note-200]

Lagos adopted a tropical modernist style (a climate-adapted international style), using materials like reinforced concrete, featuring clean lines, louvres, and a sun-screen facade to manage heat and environmental conditions. This style was practised widely by [Onafowokan Michael Olutusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onafowokan_Michael_Olutusen) and [Oluwole Olumuyiwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oluwole_Olumuyiwa) First generation architects in Nigeria. These included paying attention to simplicity rather than ornamentation in the building.

The spatial logic of Lagos between the early 1960s and 1980s followed a clear core periphery model.[^cite_note-201] Lagos Island, the historic colonial seat, served as the administrative and commercial nucleus, while areas such as Yaba, Apapa, and Surulere developed as peripheral growth zones.[^cite_note-202] This structure reflected a metabolic approach to city-building in Lagos, where the core’s functions were redistributed outward to manage congestion and promote economic decentralization.[^cite_note-203] Zoning plans often emphasized the separation of functions, echoing the CIAM-influenced ideals of modernist planning.

### Secular buildings



[National Arts Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Arts_Theatre), Iganmu, Lagos

&#x20;The tallest building in Lagos (and in West Africa) is the *NECOM House* in the Marina commercial centre, the former headquarters of the ex-telecoms monopoly NITEL. The communications mast at the top of the tower also serves as a lighthouse beacon for the Port of Lagos.[^cite_note-204]



*Bookshop House (2024), statue of [Herbert Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Macaulay)*

A very striking building in Lagos is the *National Arts Theatre* (officially "Wole Soyinka Center for Culture and Creative Arts") with its oval base, its spaceship-like and military hat form echoing Japan’s Metabolist movement, where structure mirrored function and symbolic nationhood. The renovation of the National Arts Theatre was completed in March 2023.[^cite_note-205] With the new "blue line" of the [Lagos light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Rail_Mass_Transit), the National Theatre is recently easily accessible – the station "National theatre" is at a stone's throw distance from the theatre building.

*Tafawa Balewa Square*, near the National Museum, is a stadium with a capacity of 50,000 people, which served as a racecourse under British rule and where independence was proclaimed in 1960. At the entrance to the square stand sculptures of four white horses and seven red eagles.[^cite_note-206]

The [Oba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oba_of_Lagos)'s (King's) [Palace at Iga Idunganran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iga_Idunganran) is an ancestral palace for the [Oba of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oba_of_Lagos) – the custodian on the traditions and customs of the people of Eko.

Another frequently photographed structure in Lagos is the [Lekki-Ikoyi Link Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki-Ikoyi_Link_Bridge) – or more simply: Lekki bridge.

The [Bookshop House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookshop_House). It was built by G. Cappa in 1973 and designed by architects [John Godwin and Gillian Hopwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Godwin_and_Gillian_Hopwood). It was designed with special sun screening and windows having a sunbreaker facade, which reduced the heat load on air conditioners by 75% on the office floors.

### Churches and mosques



The [Cathedral Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_Church_of_Christ,_Lagos) at the central Marina on Lagos Island

The [Cathedral Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_Church_of_Christ,_Lagos) is the centre of the oldest part of Lagos. It is better known to Lagosians by the acronym CMS (the [Church Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Mission_Society), CMS, is the Anglican organisation that runs this church). The Anglican church was built between 1867 and 1869, shortly after the establishment of British colonial rule. The present building, however, was constructed between 1924 and 1946. The remains of the first bishop of African descent in the Anglican Church and translator of the Bible into the Yoruba language, [Samuel Ajayi Crowther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Ajayi_Crowther), were transferred to this cathedral in 1976. A memorial stone commemorates him.[^cite_note-207]

The *African Church Cathedral Bethel* is situated at 59 Broad Street. The [African Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_African_Church) was founded around 1900 under the leadership of the cocoa farmer (and founder of the first agricultural co-operative in West Africa) Jacob Kehinde Coker as a breakaway from the [Anglican Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism), which rejected the use of local musical instruments in worship and the practice of [polygamy](24475.md). It was part of the growing Nigerian sense of self-identity during the early colonial period.[^cite_note-208] Opposite the cathedral stands the cenotaph in memory of [Taiwo Olowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwo_Olowo) (‘Taiwo the Rich’), who is considered one of the founders of Lagos as a financial centre.

The Catholic *Cathedral of the Holy Cross* on Campbell Street is the heart of the ‘Brazilian quarter’. Following the abolition of [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) in [Brazil](3383.md) in 1888, many former slaves returned to Nigeria and settled in Lagos. These ‘Aguda’ people brought about a significant surge in development in the port city.

The *Church of the Trinity*, part of the [Methodist](20119.md) [Wesleyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_theology) movement, stands on Tinubu Square. The building replaced the original church, built in 1862, in the 1950s, as it could no longer cope with the influx of worshippers. It was constructed in an architectural style that was considered ‘modern’ in 1950.

The *[Synagogue Church of All Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagogue_Church_of_All_Nations)* was built in 2004. It is part of the Nigerian [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism) movement, one of Nigeria’s charismatic religious movements.



Alli Balogun Mosque

The *[Lagos Central Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Central_Mosque)* on Nnamdi Azikiwe Street in downtown Lagos, with a capacity for 10,000 worshippers, is one of the six largest mosques in Nigeria. It is associated with the (moderate) Jum’at movement. It was inaugurated in 1988 and replaced an older mosque dating from 1905. A few metres further on stands the *Alli Balogun Mosque*, renovated in 2026, which, stylistically, could easily be mistaken for a church. It was built by the shipowner Alli Balogun (a supplier to the German merchants G. L. Gaiser and Bausch\&Witt), who was dissatisfied with the Central Mosque. – About 100 metres away is the oldest mosque in Lagos, the *Shitta Bey Mosque* on Martins Street, which was inaugurated in 1894 and has stood the test of time as a building in the Brazilian Baroque style.

### Art

Lagos is a hotbed for renowned sculptors, including [Ben Enwonwu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Enwonwu), who is internationally renowned for his works in wood and bronze, and the contemporary scrap metal artist [Dotun Popoola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotun_Popoola). Other prominent artists include [Peju Alatise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peju_Alatise), Idowu Sonaya (known for his public busts) and experimental artists such as Collins Abinoro Akporode, who are shaping a dynamic, modern art scene.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210]

It was created in 1964 by [Ben Enwonwu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Enwonwu), arguably Nigeria’s most famous sculptor.

In Yaba, near Sabo Market, Umeh Bede’s sculpture ‘*Youth Empowerment*’ stands on a traffic island. It depicts eleven books and three people in poses of learning or triumph.[^cite_note-216]

A roundabout in the Ojodu Berger neighbourhood is not actually a park, as it is not accessible. However, there is a huge black foot there – a sculpture by Hamza Atta.[^cite_note-217] Consequently, the traffic circle is known as the ‘*Foot Statue Roundabout*’.[^cite_note-218]

In the Marwa district, on the edge of a roundabout on the Lekki-Epe expressway, visitors can admire the sculpture ‘*Virtues of Life*’ by Segun Aiyesan.[^cite_note-219] It is a cube made of scrap metal, with each side representing a virtue such as ‘love’, ‘faith’ and so on.[^cite_note-220]

### Museums



Interactive exhibition of Yoruba art at the Randle centre

The [*National Museum*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_National_Museum) in Onikan on Lagos Island houses archaeological and ethnographic collections as well as traditional art. There is an opportunity to purchase Nigerian arts and crafts at the adjoining craft centre. Haggling is allowed at the island's Jankara market. Spices, printed cotton and hand-woven fabrics as well as leather articles are offered here.

The [*John K. Randle Centre*](https://lucas.leeds.ac.uk/research/projects/the-john-k-randle-yoruba-heritage-centre/) houses an exhibition on Yoruba culture inaugurated in 2023.[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222] The curators actively work with artists, writers, craftspeople, historians and storytellers to share the rich Yoruba culture. The John K. Randle Centre is a new, partly interactive kind of museum. It adapts modern Western museum practices to present new forms of storytelling inspired by Yoruba traditions.[^cite_note-223] It celebrates tangible and intangible culture by preserving, enhancing and promoting the cultural heritage of the Yoruba people. The centre actively collects a wide range of items that distinguish it from a traditional museum. The John K. Randle Centre plays a leading role in the repatriation of Yoruba artifacts from European institutions.

The *[National Gallery of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Modern_Art,_Lagos)* (NGMA) is situated within the [National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre,_Nigeria) [Arts Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre,_Nigeria) and can be accessed via Entrance B. Spread over two floors, it displays colourful abstract paintings by [Bruce Onobrakpeya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Onobrakpeya) and bronze busts by Ben Osawe. The ‘Portrait Gallery’ section features portraits of prominent figures, including all heads of state. These include works by artists such as [Aina Onabolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aina_Onabolu), [Hubert Ogunde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Ogunde), [Chinua Achebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinua_Achebe), [Wole Soyinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wole_Soyinka) and [Ben Enwonwu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Enwonwu). Other paintings are by Akinola Lasekan, [Erhabor Emokpae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhabor_Emokpae), [Solomon Wangboje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Wangboje), Haig David West and [Gani Odutokun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gani_Odutokun). There is also a section dedicated to modern sculptures.[^cite_note-224]

The *[Kalakuta Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalakuta_Republic)* is a museum honouring late musician [Fela Kuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fela_Kuti).[^cite_note-225]

Furthermore, the *[Mindscape Children Museum](https://mindscapesmuseums.org/)* deserves mentioning. Since 2015 it has been notable as Nigeria's premiere Children's museum. It aims at social interaction and improves their natural curiosity.[^cite_note-226]

### Performance



Conservatoire MUSON Centre

The [Muson Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muson_Centre) (Musical Society of Nigeria) is a theatre/performance hall. MUSON regularly organizes concerts of both Nigerian and Western genres. Its choir has performed since 1995, and the symphony orchestra, Nigeria's only professional symphony orchestra at the time, since 2005. Both perform regularly at the annual MUSON Festival and during the Society's concert season.[^cite_note-227]

### Festivals

In Lagos, festivals take place in different months. These are the *Lagos Carnival* in January,[^cite_note-228] the *Eko International Film Festival*[^cite_note-229] in March, the *Lagos Black Heritage Carnival* in April,[^cite_note-230] the *Lagos Photo Festival*[^cite_note-231] in November, the *Book & Art Festival*[^cite_note-232] in November and the *Lagos Food Festival*[^cite_note-233] in December. The *Lagos Jazz Festival* features music of all genres with a focus on jazz.[^cite_note-234] *The Experience* is a decibel-rich gospel concert hosted by the [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) House of the Rock Church in packed [Talewa Balewa Square](#Parks_and_gardens) on the first Friday in December.[^cite_note-235] [Ear protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_protection_device) and a certain tolerance for overly dedicated worshippers are recommended.

The *Eyo Carnival* is an irregular festival that originated in Iperu Remo, Ogun State.[^cite_note-236]

### Cuisine

Main articles: [Nigerian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_cuisine) and [African cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_cuisine)



Arewa Traditional Kitchen

Some of the famous dishes in Lagos include Indigenous delicacies such as eba and egusi; [amala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amala_\(food\)) and [ewedu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewedu_soup); [jollof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jollof_rice) (the go-to party dish); [ofada rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofada_rice); plantains (locally called dodo); beans; [suya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suya) (spicy [shish kebab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shish_kebab) or spiced roasted beef), which is consumed in local clubs and bars with a bottle of cold beer; and [eba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eba), made from [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) and eaten with soups prepared with vegetables and mixture of spices and herbs. Other dishes range from local ones like *Iyan* (pounded yam) made from yam flour, amala; [asaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asaro_\(food\)), which is usually eaten with various kinds of vegetables; and [Egusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egusi) (melon soup) to European, Middle-Eastern, and Asian cuisine.[^cite_note-post-237][^cite_note-238]

### Film



[Silverbird Galleria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverbird_Galleria) cinema in Lagos

The Surulere district is the centre of the Nigerian film industry, commonly referred to as Nollywood. Lagos itself is the location and setting for many films. The city is featured in domestic and foreign feature film productions. Many films are shot in the Festac area of Lagos, which also hosted the World Festival of Black Arts.[^cite_note-NOLLYWOOD-251] The 2016 film *[Captain America: Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_America:_Civil_War)* contains a scene set in Lagos.[^cite_note-252] The Spanish police series *La unidad* (2020–2023), the British drama *The last tree* (2019) and the US-Spanish drama *The Way, Chapter 2* with [Martin Sheen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Sheen) (2023 still in development) also use Lagos as a filming location. The film *[93 Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/93_Days)* with [Danny Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Glover) is a somewhat melodramatic but fact-based account of the [Ebola outbreak in Lagos in 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_in_Nigeria) and was filmed at original locations.

Since the success of the Nigerian thriller *The Figurine*, Nigerian film has increasingly focused on high-quality productions that are also commercially successful. This, in turn, has led to ever-new records in box office takings in Nigeria (2009: "The Figurine", 2013: "Half of a Yellow Sun", 2016: "The Wedding Party").[^cite_note-253] .

## Education

See also: [List of schools in Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Lagos)



Lagos Business School



Lagos Business School's Cafeteria

The Lagos State Government operates state schools.[^cite_note-258] The education system is the 6-3-3-4 system, which is practiced throughout the country (as well as by many other members of the [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States)). The levels are Primary, Junior Secondary School (JSS), Senior Secondary School (SSS), and university. All children are offered basic education, with a special focus now on the first nine years. Many of the schools in Nigeria are federally funded and usually are boarding schools. A few examples are the Federal government college Odogbolu (FGCOdogbolu), the Federal government girls' college Sagamu (FGGCSagamu), and the Federal government college Kano (FGCKano). The state of Lagos has its own federally funded high schools namely Federal government college Ijanikin also known as FGC Lagos, King's College Lagos, and Queen's College Lagos.[^cite_note-259]

Lagos is home to postsecondary schools, universities, and other vocational institutions that are either operated by the government or private entities.[^cite_note-260]

## Crime

Accurate statistics on crime are not available for Lagos. Residents may not report crimes to the police due to lack of access, distrust, fear of retaliation, or the belief that the police will simply be unable to help. The police, in turn, suffer from chronic underfunding, poor equipment, inadequate training, and corruption, limiting their ability to investigate crimes.[^cite_note-263]

The [Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_on_Foreign_Relations)' Nigeria Security Tracker[^cite_note-\:13-264] tracked [homicides](123451.md) ([murder](20171.md), [manslaughter](7397035.md), etc.) by criminals, religious zealots (such as Boko Haram) and police attacks in Nigeria for each state from 2012 to 2023.[^cite_note-265]

Strikingly, according to the NST, Nigeria's two largest cities, Lagos and [Kano](508782.md), are significantly safer than rural areas when it comes to homicides. For Lagos State, the NST shows 135 such violent deaths in the past 24 months (as of July 2023), or 5.6 annually for every million inhabitants (for comparison, the [United Kingdom](31717.md)'s crime statistics in 2022 counted 10 homicides per million inhabitants[^cite_note-266] or [Turkey](11125639.md)'s 24[^cite_note-267]). For Nigeria as a whole, this figure is 93, with 62% of these cases attributable to [police violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality) (mainly [vigilante justice](58325.md)). [Boko Haram](23769768.md) plays a negligible role (as of 2023) in Lagos.[^cite_note-\:13-264]

## Parks and recreation

#### Parks and gardens



Freedom Park (2026)

*[Freedom Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Park_\(Lagos\))* is a memorial and recreational park in the middle of Lagos city centre on Lagos Island, Nigeria, which used to be Her Majesty's Broad Street Prison. It was designed by architect Theo Lawson. The park was built to preserve the history and cultural heritage of Nigerians. The monuments in the park commemorate the colonial heritage of Lagos and the history of Her Majesty's Broad Street Prison. It was erected to mark the 50th Independence Anniversary celebrations in October 2010. The park is a national memorial, a historical landmark, a cultural site and an arts and recreation centre. Now a tranquil place for individuals, visitors and collective contemplation, the park is open to the public daily. Today, Freedom Park has become a gathering place for various social events and recreational entertainment.[^cite_note-268]

*[Muri Okunola Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muri_Okunola_Park)* on [Victoria Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Island,_Lagos) is adorned with three works of art: sculpture *MA´MI* by Abolore Sobayo (2017), *Undressed Identity* by Dudu Emmanuel (2018) and *"The King's Court"* by artist collective *Inscribe* (2026). It is often used by photographers and content creators as a backdrop.[^cite_note-269]

*[Ndubuisi Kanu Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndubuisi_Kanu_Park)* and *[Johnson Jakande Tinubu (JJT) Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Jakande_Tinubu_Park)* are public green spaces in the district of [Ikeja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikeja), situated in the immediate vicinity of the [Lagos State House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_State_House_of_Assembly), the official residence of the State Governor and other government buildings (Ikeja is the administrative centre of [Lagos State](626168.md)). They are separated from one another by Mobolaji Johnson Avenue. – Johnson Jakande Tinubu Park is characterised by a sculpture in which the heads of the three (former) governors after whom the park is named merge into one another.

The *Jhalobia Recreation Park and Gardens* is a botanical garden run by Veronica Adepoju and her two daughters – three qualified landscape designers who showcase the full range of their artistry on just under a hectare of land. Situated in the immediate vicinity of the airport, the site is very photogenic and is therefore regularly booked for weddings and other celebrations, but it is also ideal for tourists who have arrived too early for their return flight.[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271]

*[MKO Abiola Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKO_Abiola_Park)* and the adjacent *Peace Park* are public green spaces in the Ketu district with sculptures. The former is named after [Moshood Abiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshood_Abiola), the presumed winner of the 1993 elections, who died as a political prisoner under the dictator [Abacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sani_Abacha); there is a statue of him in the park.

#### Nature parks

The *Lekki Conservation Centre* is located in Lekki Peninsula II. It consists of a fenced-off strip of vegetation that attempts to replicate rainforest, mangroves and savannah. There are monkeys, numerous birds, snakes and crocodiles. There is a small museum with stuffed animals. On Sundays however, it is used for lengthy religious service. According to a tourism website, the LCC is the second-most popular site[^cite_note-272] in Lagos to see (after the Nike Gallery, see [chapter "Art"](#Art)).

*[LUFASI Nature Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUFASI_Park)* is the Lekki Urban Forestry and Animal Shelter Initiative in Sangotedo.[^cite_note-273] It preserves nature and protects wildlife and endangered species. Nollywood film crews often use this park as a set.

## Transportation

Main article: [Transport in Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Lagos)

Lagos has one of the largest and most extensive road networks in West Africa.[^cite_note-274][^cite_note-BRT-275] It also has suburban trains and some ferry services. Highways are usually [congested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) in peak hours owing to the geography of the city and to its explosive population growth.[^cite_note-africareport-85][^cite_note-276] Lagos is also linked by many highways and bridges.[^cite_note-277]

Expressway A1 at Lagos-Onipanu with bus lane

Lagos's importance as a commercial centre and port and its strategic location have led to it being the end-point of three [Trans-African Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-African_Highway) routes using Nigeria's national roads.[^cite_note-286] The [Trans–West African Coastal Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans%E2%80%93West_African_Coastal_Highway) leaves the city as the Badagry Expressway to [Benin](3459.md) and beyond as far as [Dakar](44251.md) and [Nouakchott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouakchott); the [Trans-Sahara Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Sahara_Highway) to [Algiers](1644.md), which is close to completion, leaves the city as the Lagos-Ibadan Expressway.[^cite_note-287] – However, the term ‘[Trans-African Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-African_Highway_network)’ (according to the Wikipedia entry) is ‘not in wide common usage outside of planning and development circles, and as of 2014 one does not see them signposted as such or labelled on maps’. ‘Trans-African Highway’ therefore really only represents a planning proposal dating from 2007. Instead, the term ‘Trans-African *corridors*’ is therefore more commonly used. – Infrastructure planning in Africa that spans several states faces challenges given the political instability of some of the countries involved. Furthermore, due to existing disparities in prosperity, the [Economic Community of West African States, ECOWAS](36848.md), is unable to fulfil the role in coordinating international transport projects that the [European Union](9317.md), for example, plays.

### Intercity railway line



Central station "Mobolaji Johnson" (2026)

Since 2021, a railway line has linked Lagos to [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan) (via [Abeokuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeokuta)), a city situated 120 km further north-east, deep in the interior of the country (Ibadan is the country’s third-largest city after Lagos and [Kano](508782.md)). The journey takes two and a half hours and costs 9,000 naira (6 euros) in first class. The locomotives, carriages and station buildings are new and modern[^cite_note-Lagos-Ibadan_train_Experience-32][^cite_note-288]. Six trains run daily on this line[^cite_note-289].

The railway line, built to [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway) (1,436 mm), replaced a route dating from the colonial era and built to [‘Cape’ gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_ft_6_in_gauge_railways) (1,056 mm between the rails). This allows for higher speeds. This new rail link is stimulating local economic growth[^cite_note-290].

The Chinese state-owned enterprises [CCECC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Civil_Engineering_Construction_Corporation) and [CRRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRRC) played a decisive role in the construction and are evidence of the growing influence of the [People’s Republic of China](5405.md) in [Africa](5334607.md).

The rail link is managed by the [Nigerian Railway Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Railway_Corporation), which reports to the Nigerian federal government.

### Air traffic



[Murtala Muhammed International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtala_Muhammed_International_Airport)

Lagos is served by [Murtala Muhammed International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtala_Muhammed_International_Airport), one of the largest and [busiest airports in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Africa). The MMIA is Nigeria's premier international air gateway. The airport's history dates back to colonial times, around the time of the Second World War. The international airport terminal was built and commissioned over 40 years ago, in 1978. The terminal opened officially on 15 March 1979. The airport had been known simply as the Lagos International Airport. It was, however, renamed for the late Nigerian Head of State, General Murtala Muhammed, who died in 1976.[^cite_note-291]

The airport terminal has been renovated several times since the 1970s but its most radical makeover began in 2013, following the launch of the Federal government's multi-billion naira Remodelling/ Rehabilitation Programme for its airports nationwide. Under the re-modeling work there, by late in 2014, the MMA lounge area had been expanded to four times its previous size and new passenger handling conveyor systems were installed which can handle over 1,000 passengers per hour.

A second airport, [Lekki-Epe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki-Epe_International_Airport) has been approved by the Federal Government in April 2023.[^cite_note-292]

### Local public transport

The Lagos Metropolitan Area Transport Authority (LAMATA) is responsible for public transport.[^cite_note-293][^cite_note-LAMATA_rail_service_expands_to_94_daily_trips-36][^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295]

Since 2021, using a bus or the light rail system is paid for with a public transport card without cash.[^cite_note-296][^cite_note-297] This card can be used equally on BRT and LBSL buses.[^cite_note-Cowry_contactless_card_for_transit-298] One can purchase a public transportation card at any of the ticketing booths at the bus terminals scattered across Lagos State.[^cite_note-299][^cite_note-Cowry_contactless_card_for_transit-298]

#### City buses



BRT bus in Lagos

There are two city bus companies in Lagos: BRT ([Lagos Bus Rapid Transit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Bus_Rapid_Transit_System)) and LBSL (Lagos Bus Services).[^cite_note-300][^cite_note-301] The city buses are air-conditioned. (However, during the Covid epidemic, the AC had to be switched off at all times.)[^cite_note-302][^cite_note-303]

BRT was inaugurated in 2008. BRT offers e-payment with bank cards.[^cite_note-304] On two arterial roads (Ikorodu Road and Funsho Williams Avenue), a dedicated bus lane has been established for BRT buses.[^cite_note-305] BRT uses diverse brands of buses, like Ashok Leyland and Yutong. Primero Transport Services (PTS) Ltd. is the sole operator of the BRT buses.[^cite_note-306]

Before the new big bus automated system, Lagos used to depend on the popular big bus called **"Molue"** which only very few can still be found plying the Lagos road especially within Oshodi - Iyana Ipaja to Sango route.

LBSL was inaugurated in 2019. LBSL uses Brazilian-built Marcopolo buses.[^cite_note-307]

The central hub for city buses and long-distance buses is the [Oshodi Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshodi_Bus_Terminal),[^cite_note-308] which is visible from afar. It is the largest bus station in [West Africa](67393.md) and commenced operation in 2019.[^cite_note-309]

The Lagos Transportation Department, [LAMATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Metropolitan_Area_Transport_Authority), introduced electric buses in Lagos in May 2023.[^cite_note-310][^cite_note-311]

#### Metro rail



Station "Mile 2", current terminus station of the blue line (2026)

The rapid transit system, the [Lagos Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Light_Rail), has been operational since February 2023.[^cite_note-312][^cite_note-313] The "[Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Lagos_Transit\))" runs between Mile 2 and Marina (East-west axis). The ''[Red Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_\(Lagos_Transit\))'' runs between [Agbado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifako-Ijaiye) and Oyingbo (North–south axis). The Lagos light rail system has been in operation since 2023 and, since 2024, has comprised two lines: the blue and the red lines. Both lines run above ground and, in the city centre, on stilts, i.e. a few metres above the ground.

Here, too, passengers use the ‘Cowry’ prepaid card, as is already the case on the city buses.

On 4 September 2026, the operator LAMATA announced on [Facebook](7529378.md) that the blue line had carried 9 million passengers over the past 36 months across 70,000 scheduled train runs[^cite_note-314][^cite_note-315]. A train runs every 10 minutes on the Blue Line. The network claims that 99.5 per cent of trains run on time.

&#x20;In 2026, work will be completed on extending the Blue Line to Okokomaiko[^cite_note-316], and the construction of a ‘Green Line’ is planned[^cite_note-317] (which is also to run partly on piles). The three lines are to be connected at Marina station (the [French Development Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Development_Agency), AFD, is involved in this project).

#### Shared cabs

A popular means of transportation are yellow minibuses called **"Danfo" or "Faragan"** as popularly called. The yellow buses, most of the VW T3 or LT type, characterize the appearance of the city. They run on fixed routes but without a timetable, according to the principle of shared cabs.[^cite_note-318]

#### Ferries

According to residents, getting to work by car in Lagos can take six times longer than by ferry. About two million passengers were recorded by the Ferry Authority per month in 2021.[^cite_note-319]

Five Cowries Terminal is the central terminal for ferry operations in Lagos.[^cite_note-\:7-320] The terminal is located on the lagoon between Lagos Island and Victoria Island and was commissioned on 30 August 2018.[^cite_note-321][^cite_note-322] Five Cowries also serves as the headquarters of the Lagos State Waterways Authority (LASWA). It has a jetty, restaurant, bar, administrative offices, ticket offices, waiting area, toilets, lift, conference room, and ATMs. Boats from private individuals and other operators also dock at the pier. The terminal also has a multi-storey car park with space for over 800 vehicles. The multi-storey car park is located behind the terminal (as seen from the lagoon) and is directly connected to the terminal. The terminal was built without thresholds and there is a toilet for wheelchair users.[^cite_note-\:7-320] Five Cowries is open seven days a week, from 8.00 am to 5.00 pm. The terminal is located next to the Falomo Bridge. The destinations of LASWA ferries are Marina, [Ikoyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikoyi), [Victoria Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Island), Lekki, [Apapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apapa), [Ikorodu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikorodu) and [Badagry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badagry).[^cite_note-323]

## Quality of life

Opinions on the quality of life in Lagos vary widely.

In 2023, Lagos ranked second – behind [Manila](184334.md) – in a global survey by the Statista Research Department of cities with the *lowest* quality of life.[^cite_note-334] – By contrast, from 2024 through 2026, Lagos has featured each year in [Time Out magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Out_magazine)'s list of the "50 Best Cities in the World", based on the Time Out Index web survey of locals.[^cite_note-TimeOut-2024-335][^cite_note-TimeOut-2025-336][^cite_note-TimeOut-2026-337] In the 2024 survey, 96 percent of respondents said they were happy,[^cite_note-TimeOut-2024-335] and in every year, over two-thirds of respondents have said that it's easy to make friends in Lagos.[^cite_note-TimeOut-2024-335][^cite_note-TimeOut-2025-336][^cite_note-TimeOut-2026-337] "[Business Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BusinessDay_\(Nigeria\))" adds that Lagos remains a major cultural and economic centre characterised by its vibrancy, diversity and population density. The city has also made a name for itself through its music and creative industries.[^cite_note-338] In 2023, Brand Finance ranked Lagos fifth among the most desirable cities in Africa, behind [Cape Town](6653.md) and [Johannesburg](58893.md), [Cairo](6293.md) and [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), but ahead of [Nairobi](21482.md). The survey involved 15,000 respondents across 20 countries.[^cite_note-\:17-339][^cite_note-\:18-340][^cite_note-\:19-341]

By way of qualification, it must be made clear that index lists of countries or cities are usually not based on verifiable or locally collected data,[^cite_note-\:10-342] but often on subjective assessments by Europeans or North Americans. The [CPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index) of [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International), for example, is, according to its own definition, *"based on the perceived assessment of lay people and experts and is not reduced to actual experience and its analysis"*. There is criticism that the indices determined simply reflect the prejudices of Western countries[^cite_note-343] numerically.[^cite_note-344][^cite_note-345]

## Social situation, informal economy

There is a huge spectrum of wealth distribution among the people that reside in Lagos. It ranges from the very wealthy to the very poor. Lagos has attracted many young people and families seeking a better life from all other parts of Nigeria and beyond.[^cite_note-first-346][^cite_note-347]

In some parts of Lagos, residents have one of the highest standards of living in Nigeria and in Africa.[^cite_note-348][^cite_note-349]

Reliable data on [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment), [income below the subsistence level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_threshold), etc. are hardly available for Lagos – as for the whole region – and must be taken with skepticism where they are provided, e.g. in other articles (see also the previous chapter). The reason for this is the widespread "*[informal economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_economy)*" (not to be confused with "[shadow economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market)") in West Africa. Insufficient jobs in traditional wage labour force people to look for work elsewhere. This benefits the informal sector of the economy, where there is no minimum wage and workers pay no taxes, have no holiday or labour rights and often work in unsafe conditions.[^cite_note-\:11-350]

According to the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), about 5.5 million people are employed in the informal economy in Lagos State alone – about three-quarters of Lagos's workforce.[^cite_note-\:12-351] Throughout Lagos, one can observe street vendors, artisans, sellers, small and micro enterprises, shared taxis, tricycles and motorbikes (okada drivers), domestic workers, market traders[^cite_note-\:11-350] and others engaged in the informal sector.

Activities in the informal economy are not included in economic statistics. As a result, the wealth of the population, but also e.g. unemployment, is significantly underestimated if the massive informal economy is not taken into account.[^cite_note-\:12-351]

## Recycling, sewage and water supply

### Recycling/waste management

In Lagos, only 40 percent of waste is collected and only 13 percent is recycled.[^cite_note-\:9-352] 13,000 tonnes of waste are generated daily in the metropolis.[^cite_note-353] Some residents burn their waste, which exacerbates pollution.[^cite_note-\:9-352]

### Water supply

Tap water in Lagos is not suitable for drinking, but can be used for other purposes such as cooking and showering.[^cite_note-354] The water in the distribution network is often contaminated. Since the raw water in the lagoon is too polluted, the city draws its water from the Ogun and Owo rivers. There has been debate about the poor water quality in Lagos for years.[^cite_note-355][^cite_note-356] At the same time, a sizable proportion of the residents live in slums without access to piped water and sanitation.[^cite_note-357][^cite_note-358][^cite_note-359][^cite_note-360]

### Sewage

An efficient sewage system is lacking. Sewage is flushed into the open sewerage system by rainwater. This water then carries pollutants into rivers and the lagoon. Sewage also enters groundwater through leaking septic tanks and latrines. The contaminants can then contaminate the water in wells and boreholes. Water sold by street vendors can also be affected, as it comes from the same sources.[^cite_note-361]
