**Valencia**,[^cite_note-5] known officially in [Valencian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language) as ***València***,[^cite_note-6] is the capital and largest city of the [Valencian Community](20514168.md) and the [province of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Valencia) in Spain. It is located on the banks of the [Turia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turia_\(river\)), on the east coast of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) on the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md). With a population of 824,340, it is the [third most-populous city in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_lists_of_Spanish_municipalities#By_population). The urban area of Valencia has 1.6 million people while the metropolitan region has 2.5 million.

Valencia was founded as a [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) colony in 138 BC as "[Valentia Edetanorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentia_Edetanorum)". As an autonomous city in late antiquity, its militarization followed the onset of the threat posed by the [Byzantine presence to the South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spania), together with effective integration to the [Visigothic Kingdom of Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom_of_Toledo) in the late 6th century.[^cite_note-7] [Islamic rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) and acculturation ensued in the 8th century, together with the introduction of new irrigation systems and crops. With the [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) Christian conquest in 1238, the city became the capital of the [Kingdom of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Valencia).

Due to trade with the rest of the Iberian Peninsula, Italian ports, and other Mediterranean locations, the city thrived in the 15th century and Valencia had become one of the largest European cities by the end of the century. The emergence of the [Atlantic World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_World) affected Mediterranean trade in the global trade networks and, along with insecurity created by [Barbary piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates) throughout the 16th century. The 16th century had been notable for the large number of religious foundations which, according to one estimate, suggested that one third of its area had been occupied by religious buildings. The city's economic activity suffered a crisis following the [expulsion of the Moriscos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Moriscos) in 1609.

The city became a major [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk) manufacturing centre in the 18th century. During the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), the city served as the provisional seat of the [Spanish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic) from 1936 to 1937.

The [Port of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Valencia) is one of the busiest container ports in Europe and the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean). The city is ranked as a Gamma-level [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network). Valencia has numerous celebrations and traditions, such as the *[Falles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Fallas)* (or *Fallas*), which were declared a [Fiesta of National Tourist Interest of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiestas_of_National_Tourist_Interest_of_Spain) in 1965 and an [intangible cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in November 2016. The city was selected as the [World Design Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Design_Capital) 2022 and the [European Green Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Capital_Award) 2024.

## Name



Roman [cornucopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornucopia), symbol of Valentia, found on the floor of a Roman building excavated in the Plaça de la Mare de Déu

The [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) name of the city was "Valēntia" ([\[waˈleːntia\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Latin)), meaning "strength" or "valour", due to the Roman practice of recognising the valour of former Roman soldiers after a war. The Roman historian [Livy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livy) explains that the founding of Valentia in the 2nd century BC was due to the settling of the Roman soldiers who fought against a [Lusitanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusitania) rebel, [Viriatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viriatus), during the Third Raid of the [Lusitanian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusitanian_War).[^cite_note-Astin1989-8]

During the period of Islamic rule, the city was called *Medina at-Tarab* ('City of Joy') according to one transliteration, or *Medina at-Turab* ('City of Sands') according to another, since it was located on the banks of the River Turia. It is not clear if an Arabised variant of the Latin name ("Balansiyya") referred to the greater [Taifa of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Valencia), or just the city itself.[^cite_note-Galbis2009-9]

Via gradual phonetic changes, "Valentia" became "Valencia" [\[baˈlenθja\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) in [Spanish](26825.md) and "València" [\[vaˈlensia\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Catalan) in [Valencian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language). In Valencian, an "e" with a grave accent ("è") indicates [\[ɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Catalan) in contrast to [\[e\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Catalan), but the word "València" is an exception to this rule, since "è" is pronounced [\[e\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Catalan). The spelling "València" was approved by the [Acadèmia Valenciana de la Llengua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acad%C3%A8mia_Valenciana_de_la_Llengua) (AVL) based on tradition after a debate on the matter. The name "València" has been the only official name of the city since 2017.[^cite_note-10] In 2023, the Commission of Culture of the municipal corporation agreed in principle on a dual official denomination *Valencia*/*Valéncia*, with the [far-right](62493456.md) insisting on a non-standard acute accent in the "e" of the Valencian-language name.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## History

Main article: [History of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Valencia)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Valencia).

### Roman colony



Detail of 2nd-century Roman mosaic found in the city

Valencia is one of the oldest cities in [Spain](26667.md), founded in the Roman period c. 138 BC as "Valentia Edetanorum".[^cite_note-Law1999-13] A few centuries later, with the power vacuum left by the demise of the Roman imperial administration, the [Catholic Church](606848.md) assumed power in the city, coinciding with the first waves of the invading Germanic peoples ([Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebi), [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals), [Alans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alans), and later [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths)).

### Middle Ages

After the fall of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire), Valencia became part of the [Visigothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom) from 546 to 711 AD.[^cite_note-14] The city surrendered to the invading [Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors) about 714 AD.[^cite_note-Sanz2003-15] [Abd al-Rahman I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Rahman_I) laid waste to old Valencia by 788–789.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009159-16] From then on, the name of Valencia (Arabised as "Balansiya") appears more related to the wider area than to the city, which is primarily cited as "Madînat al-Turâb" ('city of earth' or 'sand') and presumably had diminished importance throughout the period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009160-17] During the emiral period, the surrounding territory, under the ascendancy of Berber chieftains, was prone to unruliness.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009161-18] In the wake of the start of the [fitna of al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitna_of_al-Andalus), Valencia became the head of an independent emirate, the [Taifa of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Valencia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009163-19] It was initially controlled by [eunuchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunuch),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009163-19] and then, after 1021, by Abd al-Azîz (a grandson of [Almanzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almanzor)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009164–165-20] Valencia experienced notable urban development in this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009165-21] Many Jews lived in Valencia, including the accomplished Jewish poet and philosopher [Solomon ibn Gabirol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_ibn_Gabirol), who spent his last years in the city.[^cite_note-Angel-22] After a damaging offensive by Castilian–Leonese forces towards 1065, the territory became a satellite of the [Taifa of Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Toledo), and following the fall of the latter in 1085, a protectorate of "[El Cid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cid)". A revolt erupted in 1092, handing the city to the [Almoravids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravid_dynasty) and forcing El Cid to [take the city by force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Valencia_\(1092%E2%80%931094\)) in 1094, henceforth establishing [his own principality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordship_of_Valencia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009166–167-23]

Following the evacuation of the city in 1102, the Almoravids took control. As the Almoravid empire crumbled in the mid 12th-century, [ibn Mardanīsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ab%C5%AB_%CA%BFAbd_All%C4%81h_Mu%E1%B8%A5ammad_ibn_Sa%CA%BFd_ibn_Mardan%C4%ABsh) took control of eastern al-Andalus, creating a Murcia-centred independent emirate to which Valencia belonged, resisting the [Almohads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate) until 1172.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009168-24] During the Almohad rule, the city perhaps had a population of about 20,000.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETorró2009169-25] When the city fell to [James I of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon), the Jewish population constituted about 7 per cent of the total population.[^cite_note-Angel-22]



12th-century Arab dish

In 1238,[^cite_note-Guichard2001-26] King James I of Aragon, with an army composed of [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragonese_people), [Catalans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalans), Navarrese, and crusaders from the [Order of Calatrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Calatrava), laid siege to Valencia and on 28 September [obtained a surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Valencia_\(1238\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-27] Fifty thousand Moors were forced to leave. The Jews of Valencia were afforded a quarter for residence in 1239, which was surrounded by a high wall in 1390. The quarter had three gates, all of which were closed at night; the Jewish cemetery was permitted just beyond 'the Jew's Gate', the Portal dels Jueues.[^cite_note-28]

Valencia endured serious troubles in the mid-14th century, including the decimation of the population by the [Black Death of 1348](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death) and subsequent years of epidemics—as well as the series of wars and riots that followed.

In 1391, a pogrom struck the Jewish quarter of Valencia, part of a wave of [anti-Jewish attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_1391) that began earlier that year in Seville and spread across [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile) and into the Crown of Aragon. The assault in Valencia was sparked when a procession of youths marched on the Jewish quarter, shouting that "the [Archdeacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdeacon) of Castile is coming with his cross, and that all the Jews should be baptized or die."[^cite_note-29] Despite efforts by royal guards to intervene, thousands of Jews were murdered, and the survivors were forced to convert. The Jewish quarter was destroyed.[^cite_note-Angel-22]

By the late 14th century, [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) traders promoted the expansion of the cultivation of [white mulberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morus_alba) in the area and later introduced innovative [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk) manufacturing techniques. Valencia became a centre of mulberry production and was, for a time, a major silk-producing centre.[^cite_note-silk-30] The Genoese community in Valencia—merchants, artisans and workers—became, along with Seville's, one of the most important in the Iberian Peninsula.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENavarro_Espinach1994223–224-31]

In 1407, following the model of the Barcelona institution created some years before, a *[Taula de canvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taula_de_canvi)* (a municipal public bank) was created in Valencia, although its first iteration yielded limited success.[^cite_note-32]

The 15th century was a time of economic expansion, known as the Valencian Golden Age, during which culture and the arts flourished. Concurrent population growth made Valencia the most populous city in the Crown of Aragon. Some of the city's landmark buildings were built during the Late Middle Ages, including the [Serranos Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_de_Serranos), the [Silk Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llotja_de_la_Seda), the [Miguelete Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguelete_Tower), and the Chapel of the Kings of the Convent of Sant Domènec. In painting and sculpture, [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people) and Italian trends had an influence on Valencian artists.

Valencia became a major [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery) centre in the 15th century, second only to [Lisbon](18091.md) in the West,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGonzález_Arévalo201917-33] prompting a Lisbon–[Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville)–Valencia axis by the second half of the century powered by the incipient Portuguese slave trade originating in [West Africa](67393.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGonzález_Arévalo201919-34] By the end of the 15th century Valencia was one of the largest European cities, being the most populated city in the Hispanic Monarchy and second to Lisbon in the Iberian Peninsula.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESantamaría1992373-35]

### Modern history

Following the death of [Ferdinand II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon) in 1516, the nobiliary estate challenged the Crown amid the relative void of power.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPérez_García2019-36] In 1519, the Taula de Canvis was recreated again, known as "Nova Taula".[^cite_note-37] The nobles earned the rejection from the people of Valencia, and the whole kingdom was plunged into the [Revolt of the Brotherhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt_of_the_Brotherhoods) and full-blown civil war between 1521 and 1522.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPérez_García2019-36] Muslim vassals were forced to convert in 1526 at the behest of [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPérez_García2019-36]

The 16th century had been notable for the large number of religious foundations which, according to one estimate, suggested that one third of its area had been occupied by religious buildings.[^cite_note-38] Urban and rural delinquency—linked to phenomena such as [vagrancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagrancy), [gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling), [larceny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larceny), [pimping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procuring_\(prostitution\)) and false begging—as well as the nobiliary banditry consisting of the revenges and rivalries between the aristocratic families flourished in Valencia during the 16th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Martínez197285–86-39] Furthermore, [North African piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates) targeted the whole coastline of the [kingdom of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Valencia), forcing the fortification of sites.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Martínez197288-40] By the late 1520s, the intensification of Barbary [corsair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsairs_of_Algiers) activity along with domestic conflicts and the emergence of the Atlantic Ocean in detriment of the Mediterranean in global trade networks put an end to the economic splendor of the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFranch_Benavent200884-41] This piracy also paved the way for the ensuing development of Christian piracy, that had Valencia as one of its main bases in the Iberian Mediterranean.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Martínez197288-40] The Berber threat—initially with [Ottoman](22278.md) support—generated great insecurity on the coast, and it would not be substantially reduced until the 1580s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarcía_Martínez197288-40]



View of Valencia by [Anton van den Wyngaerde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_van_den_Wyngaerde) (1563)



Expulsion of the Moriscos from Valencia Grau by Pere Oromig (1616)

The crisis deepened during the 17th century with the 1609 expulsion of the [Moriscos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morisco), descendants of the Muslim population that had converted to Christianity. The Spanish government systematically forced Moriscos to leave the kingdom for Muslim North Africa. They were concentrated in the former [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon), and in the [Kingdom of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Valencia) specifically, and constituted roughly a third of the total population.[^cite_note-42] The expulsion caused the financial ruin of some of the Valencian nobility and the bankruptcy of the Taula de canvi in 1613.

The decline of the city reached its nadir with the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession) (1702–1709), marking the end of the political and legal independence of the [Kingdom of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Valencia). During the war, Valencia sided with the [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) ruler of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), [Charles of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). King Charles vowed to protect the laws ("[Furs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furs_of_Valencia)") of the [Kingdom of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Valencia), which gained him the sympathy of a wide sector of the Valencian population. On 24 January 1706, after having ridden south from Barcelona, [Charles Mordaunt, 3rd Earl of Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mordaunt,_3rd_Earl_of_Peterborough), led a handful of English cavalrymen into the city and captured the nearby fortress at [Sagunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagunto), bluffing the Spanish Bourbon army into withdrawal.

The English held the city for 16 months, defeating several attempts to expel them. After the victory of the Bourbons at the [Battle of Almansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Almansa) on 25 April 1707, the English army evacuated Valencia and [Philip V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Spain) ordered the repeal of the Furs of Valencia as punishment for the kingdom's support of Charles of Austria.[^cite_note-43] By the [Nueva Planta decrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Planta_decrees), the ancient [Charters of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furs_of_Valencia) were abolished and the city was governed by the Castilian Charter, similarly to other places in the Crown of Aragon.

The Valencian economy recovered during the 18th century with the rising manufacture of woven silk and ceramic tiles. The silk industry boomed during this century, with Valencia replacing [Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Spain) as the centre of silk-manufacturing in Spain.[^cite_note-silk-30] The Palau de Justícia is an example of the affluence manifested in the most prosperous times of Bourbon rule (1758–1802) during the rule of Charles III. The 18th century was the [Age of Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) in Europe and, in Valencia, its humanistic ideals influenced men such as [Gregorio Mayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorio_Mayans) and [Francisco Pérez Bayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_P%C3%A9rez_Bayer), who maintained correspondence with the leading French and German thinkers of the time.

#### Peninsular War

Main article: [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War)

The 19th century began with Spain embroiled in wars with France, Portugal, and England—but the Peninsular War (also known as the Spanish War of Independence) affected the Valencian territories and the capital city. The repercussions of the French Revolution were still being felt when Napoleon's armies invaded the Iberian Peninsula. The Valencian people rose up in arms against them on 23 May 1808, inspired by leaders such as Vicent Doménech el Palleter.

The mutineers seized the Citadel, the [Supreme Junta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Junta) government took over, and on 26–28 June, Napoleon's Marshal [Moncey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon-Adrien_Jeannot_de_Moncey) attacked the city with a column of 9,000 French [Grande Armée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Arm%C3%A9e) troops at the first [Battle of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Valencia_\(1808\)). He failed to take the city in two assaults and retreated to Madrid. Marshal [Suchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis-Gabriel_Suchet) began a long siege of the city in October 1811, and after intense bombardment forced it to surrender on 8 January 1812. After the capitulation, the French instituted reforms in Valencia, which became the capital of Spain when the [Bonapartist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonapartism) king, [José I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bonaparte) (Napoleon's elder brother), moved the Court there in the middle of 1812. The disaster of the [Battle of Vitoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vitoria) on 21 June 1813 obliged Suchet to quit Valencia, and the French troops withdrew in July.

#### Post-war



Valencia in 1832 by French lithographer [Alfred Guesdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guesdon)

[Ferdinand VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_VII) returned to the throne at end of the Peninsular War, which freed Spain from Napoleonic domination. On his return, on 24 March 1814 from exile in France, the Cortes requested that he respect the liberal Constitution of 1812, which significantly limited royal powers. Ferdinand refused and went to Valencia instead of Madrid. Here, on 17 April, [General Elio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Javier_de_El%C3%ADo) invited the King to reclaim his absolute rights and put his troops at the King's disposition. The king abolished the [Constitution of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Constitution_of_1812) and dissolved the two chambers of the Spanish Parliament on 10 May. Thus began six years (1814–1820) of absolutist rule, but the constitution was reinstated during the [Trienio Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trienio_Liberal), a period of three years of liberal government in Spain from 1820 to 1823.

On King Ferdinand VII's death in 1833, [Baldomero Espartero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldomero_Espartero) became one of the most ardent defenders of the hereditary rights of the king's daughter, the future [Isabella II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_II). During the regency of [Maria Cristina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Christina_of_the_Two_Sicilies), Espartero ruled Spain for two years as its 18th prime minister from 16 September 1840 to 21 May 1841. City life in Valencia carried on in a revolutionary climate, with frequent clashes between liberals and republicans.[^cite_note-44]



The start of demolition work on the walls of the city in 1865

The reign of Isabella II as an adult (1843–1868) was a period of relative stability and growth for Valencia. During the second half of the 19th century the bourgeoisie encouraged the development of the city and its environs; landowners were enriched by the introduction of the orange crop and the expansion of vineyards and other crops. This economic boom corresponded with a revival of local traditions and of the [Valencian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language) language, which had been ruthlessly suppressed from the time of Philip V.

Work to demolish the walls of the old city started on 20 February 1865.[^cite_note-murallas-45] The demolition of the citadel ended after the [1868 Glorious Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glorious_Revolution_\(Spain\)).[^cite_note-murallas-45]

During the [Cantonal rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonal_rebellion) in 1873, Valencia was the capital of the short-lived [Valencian Canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_Canton).[^cite_note-46]

Following the introduction of [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) (for men) in the late 19th century, the political landscape in Valencia—until then consisting of the bipartisanship characteristic of the early [Restoration period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_\(Spain\))—experienced a change, leading to a growth of [republican forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_Spain), gathered around the emerging figure of [Vicente Blasco Ibáñez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Blasco_Ib%C3%A1%C3%B1ez).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAguiló_Lúcia199261–62-47] Not unlike the equally republican [Lerrouxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lerrouxism), the Populist [Blasquism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasquism?action=edit\&redlink=1) came to mobilize the Valencian masses by promoting [anticlericalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-clericalism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuárez_Cortina201128-48] Meanwhile, in reaction, the right-wing coalesced around several initiatives such as the Catholic League or the reformulation of Valencian [Carlism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlism), and [Valencianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencianism) did similarly with organizations such as Valencia Nova or the Unió Valencianista.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAguiló_Lúcia199262-49]

### 20th century



Women working at the Albufera (c. 1915)



Bombing of the city by the Italian [Aviazione Legionaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviazione_Legionaria) (1937) during the Spanish Civil War

In the early 20th century, Valencia was an industrialised city. Although the silk industry had disappeared, there was a large production of hides and skins, wood, metals, and foodstuffs, the latter with substantial exports, particularly of wine and citrus. Small businesses predominated, but with the rapid mechanisation of the industry, larger companies were being formed. The best expression of this dynamic was in regional exhibitions, including that of 1909 held next to the L'Albereda (Paseo de la Alameda), which depicted the progress of agriculture and industry. Among the most architecturally successful buildings of the era were those designed in the [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) style, such as the [Estació del Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estaci%C3%B3_del_Nord_\(Valencia\)) and the Central and Columbus markets.

[World War I](4764461.md) (1914–1918) greatly affected the Valencian economy, causing the collapse of its citrus exports. The [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic) (1931–1939) opened the way for democratic participation and the increased politicisation of citizens, especially in response to the rise of Conservative Front power in 1933. The [initial coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_coup_of_July_1936) by [Nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_faction_\(Spanish_Civil_War\)) forces was preempted when, on 11 July 1936, [Falangists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falange_Espa%C3%B1ola_de_las_JONS_\(1976\)) took over the city's radio station, announcing that " the national syndicalist movement will soon begin in the streets", which led to rioting by those sympathetic to the Republic.[^cite_note-50] When the coup was launched nationally a week after this, stalling and indecision led the abortive rising in Valencia to fail.[^cite_note-51] The inevitable march toward civil war and combat in Madrid resulted in the relocation of the capital of the Republic to Valencia.[^cite_note-Vázquez-52]

After the continuous unsuccessful [Francoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain) offensive on besieged Madrid during the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), Valencia temporarily became the capital of Republican Spain on 6 November 1936. It hosted the government until 31 October 1937.[^cite_note-Vázquez-52]

#### Francoist Spain

During the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), Valencia was heavily bombarded by air and sea, mainly by the [Fascist Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy_\(1922%E2%80%931943\)) air force, as well as the Francoist air force with [Nazi German](21212.md) support. By the end of the war, the city had survived 442 bombardments, leaving 2,831 dead and 847 wounded, although it is estimated that the death toll was higher. The Nationalist push to capture Valencia was halted along the [XYZ Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XYZ_Line) of fortifications in July 1938.[^cite_note-53] The Republican government moved to Barcelona on 31 October of that year. On 30 March 1939, Valencia surrendered and Nationalist Spanish troops entered the city.

The postwar years were a time of hardship for Valencians. During Franco's regime, speaking or teaching [Valencian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language) was prohibited; in a significant reversal, it is now compulsory for every schoolchild in Valencia. Franco's dictatorship forbade political parties and began a harsh ideological and cultural repression countenanced and sometimes led by the Catholic Church. During Franco's regime some leading Valencian intellectuals, such as [Juan Peset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Peset), [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_\(academia\)) of [University of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Valencia), were executed and many of them, including [Josep Renau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Renau) and [Max Aub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Aub), went into exile.



1957 flood



Valencian houses following the Valencia flood of 1957

In 1943, Franco decreed the exclusivity of Valencia and Barcelona for the celebration of international [fairs](504805.md) in Spain.[^cite_note-ventoso-54] These two cities held the monopoly on international fairs for more than three decades, until the rule's abolishment in 1979 by the government of [Adolfo Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolfo_Su%C3%A1rez).[^cite_note-ventoso-54] In October 1957, [a flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Valencia_flood) from the Turia river resulted in 81 deaths and extensive property damage.[^cite_note-levante-55] The disaster led to the remodelling of the city and the creation of a new river bed for the Turia, with the old one becoming one of the city's "green lungs".[^cite_note-levante-55] The economy began to recover in the early 1960s, and the city experienced explosive population growth through immigration spurred by jobs created with the implementation of major urban projects and infrastructure improvements.

#### Post-Franco

With the [advent of democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy) in Spain, the ancient kingdom of Valencia was established as a new autonomous entity, the [Valencian Community](20514168.md), the Statute of Autonomy of 1982 designating Valencia as its capital. Valencia has since then experienced a surge in its cultural development, exemplified by exhibitions and performances at such iconic institutions as the Palau de la Música, the Palacio de Congresos, the Metro, the [City of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (Ciutat de les Arts i les Ciències), the Valencian Museum of Enlightenment and Modernity (Museo Valenciano de la Ilustración y la Modernidad), and the Institute of Modern Art (Institut Valencià d'Art Modern). The various productions of [Santiago Calatrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Calatrava), a renowned structural engineer, architect, and sculptor and of the architect [Félix Candela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Candela) have contributed to Valencia's international reputation. In 1983 the statue of Francisco Franco in Plaza del Caudillo was torn down and the plaza renamed "[Plaza del Ayuntamiento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_del_Ayuntamiento)". Some time after, the bronze statue *La Paz y la Concordancia* by José Puche was erected in [Plaza de la Reina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_la_Reina) and dedicated to victims of terrorism. These public works and the ongoing rehabilitation of the "Old City" (Ciutat Vella) have helped improve the city's livability, and tourism is continually increasing.

### 21st century



Modern developments in the Penya-Roja neighborhood

On 3 July 2006, a major mass transit disaster, the [Valencia Metro derailment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Metro_derailment), left 43 dead and 47 wounded.[^cite_note-56] Days later, on 9 July, the World Day of Families, during Mass at Valencia's Cathedral, Our Lady of the Forsaken Basilica, [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) used the *Sant Calze*, a 1st-century Middle-Eastern artifact that some Catholics believe is the [Holy Grail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Grail).[^cite_note-58]

Valencia was selected in 2003 to host the historic [America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Cup) yacht race, the first European city ever to do so. The [2007 America's Cup matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_America's_Cup) took place from April to July. On 3 July 2007, [Alinghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alinghi) defeated [Team New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_New_Zealand) to retain the America's Cup. Twenty-two days later, on 25 July 2007, the leaders of the Alinghi syndicate, holder of the America's Cup, officially announced that Valencia would be the host city for the 33rd America's Cup, held in June 2009.[^cite_note-59]

The results of the [Valencia municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_City_Council_elections) from 1991 to 2011 delivered a 24-year uninterrupted rule (1991–2015) by the [People's Party](404727.md) (PP) and Mayor [Rita Barberá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Barber%C3%A1), with support from the [Valencian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_Union). Barberá's rule was ousted by left-leaning forces after the [2015 municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Valencia_City_Council_election), with [Joan Ribó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Rib%C3%B3) of [Compromís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalici%C3%B3_Comprom%C3%ADs) becoming the new mayor.

## Geography



Valencia and surroundings as seen by the [ESA](10363.md)'s [Sentinel-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel-2) satellite

Located on the eastern coast of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) and the western part of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), fronting the [Gulf of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Valencia), Valencia lies on the highly fertile alluvial silts accumulated on the floodplain formed in the lower course of the [Turia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turia_\(river\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPuncel_Chornet1999-60] At its founding by the Romans in 138 BC, it stood on an alluvial plain of the Turia River several kilometers from the sea.[^cite_note-Monrabal2011-61]

The [Albufera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albufera) lagoon, located about 12 km (7 mi) south of the city proper (and part of the municipality),[^cite_note-62] was originally a saltwater lagoon, but since the severing of links to the sea, it has eventually become a freshwater lagoon, progressively decreasing in size.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClemente_Meoro20081-63] The lagoon and its environment are used for the cultivation of [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) in [paddy fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_field), and for hunting and fishing purposes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClemente_Meoro20081-63]

The Valencia City Council bought the lake from the [Crown of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Spain) for 1,072,980 [pesetas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_peseta) in 1911,[^cite_note-Gonzálbez1960-64] and today it forms the main portion of the [Parc Natural de l'Albufera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_Natural_de_l'Albufera) (Albufera Nature Reserve), with a surface area of 21,120 hectares (52,200 acres). Because of its cultural, historical, and ecological value, it was declared a natural park in 1976.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Valencia)

Valencia and its metropolitan area has a [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics)[^cite_note-65] [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSh*) bordering on a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*)[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] with mild winters and hot, dry summers.[^cite_note-KGcc-68][^cite_note-69]

The average annual temperature of Valencia is 18.6 °C (65.5 °F); 23 °C (73 °F) during the day and 14.2 °C (57.6 °F) at night. In the coldest month, January, the maximum daily temperature typically ranges from 15 to 20 °C (59 to 68 °F), the minimum temperature typically at night ranges from 6 to 10 °C (43 to 50 °F). December, January and February are the coldest months, with average temperatures around 17 °C (63 °F) during the day and 8 °C (46 °F) at night. March is transitional, the temperature often exceeds 20 °C (68 °F), with an average temperature of 19.3 °C (66.7 °F) during the day and 10 °C (50 °F) at night. During the warmest months – July and August, the maximum temperature during the day typically ranges from 28 to 32 °C (82 to 90 °F), about 21 to 24 °C (70 to 75 °F) at night.[^cite_note-AEMET-70] The highest and lowest temperatures recorded in the city since 1937 were 44.5 °C (112.1 °F) on 10 August 2023 and −7.2 °C (19.0 °F) on 11 February 1956, respectively.[^cite_note-aemet8416-71] Valencia has one of the mildest winters in Europe, owing to its southern location on the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) and the [Foehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foehn_wind) phenomenon, locally known as *ponentà*.[^cite_note-72]

Peak precipitation occurs in autumn, coinciding with the time of the year when [cold drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_drop) (*gota fría*) episodes of heavy rainfall—associated with [cut-off low pressure systems at high altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_\(meteorology\)#Cut-off_highs_and_lows)—[^cite_note-73] are common along the Western mediterranean coast.[^cite_note-climate-74] The year-on-year variability in precipitation may be, however, considerable,[^cite_note-climate-74] as exemplified by large floods in [1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Valencia_flood) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2024_Spain_floods), which both occurred in the month of October. Snowfall almost does not occur at all; the most recent occasion snow accumulated on the ground was on 11 January 1960.[^cite_note-75]

Valencia, on average, has around 2,733 sunshine hours per year, from 158 in December (average of 5 hours of sunshine duration a day) to 313 in July (average around 10 hours of sunshine duration a day). The average temperature of the sea is 14–15 °C (57–59 °F) in winter and 25–26 °C (77–79 °F) in summer.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] Average annual [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) is around 66%.[^cite_note-78]

## Economy



[Bank of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_de_Valencia)

Valencia enjoyed strong economic growth before the [Great Recession of 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession), much of it spurred by tourism and [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction), with concurrent development and expansion of telecommunications and transport. The city's economy is service-oriented, as nearly 84% of the working population is employed in service sector occupations. However, the city still maintains an important industrial base, with 8.5% of the population employed in this sector. Growth has recently improved in the manufacturing sector, mainly automobile assembly; [Ford Valencia Body and Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Valencia_Body_and_Assembly) lies in the municipality of [Almussafes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almussafes).[^cite_note-93] Agricultural activity still occurs but is of relatively minor importance, with only 1.9% of the working population working in agriculture and 3,973 ha (9,820 acres) of farmland (mostly orchards and citrus groves).

Since the onset of the Great Recession, Valencia had experienced a growing unemployment rate, increased government debt, and other issues, and severe spending cuts were introduced by the city government. However, in 2009, Valencia was designated "the 29th fastest-improving European city".[^cite_note-94] Its influence in commerce, education, entertainment, media, fashion, science and the arts contributes to its status as one of the world's "Gamma" rank [global cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city).[^cite_note-GAWC-95] According to *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*, Valencia is among the most livable cities in the world.[^cite_note-96]

The city is the seat of one of the four [stock exchanges](28168.md) in Spain, the [Bolsa de Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsa_de_Valencia?action=edit\&redlink=1), part of [Bolsas y Mercados Españoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsas_y_Mercados_Espa%C3%B1oles) (BME), owned by [SIX Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIX_Group).[^cite_note-97]

The Valencia metropolitan area had a [GDP amounting to \$52.7 billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP), and \$28,141 per capita.[^cite_note-Brookings-98]

In 2021, the [European Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Investment_Bank) (EIB) provided a €27 million loan to Sociedad Anónima Municipal Actuaciones Urbanas de Valencia (AUMSA) to support affordable public rental housing projects, which included building 323 new units and renovating four existing ones, expanding AUMSA's housing stock by more than 50%.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

In 2022, the city was voted the world's top destination for [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate), based on criteria such as [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life) and affordability.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

### Tourism



Malvarrosa Beach

Starting in the mid-1990s, Valencia, formerly an industrial centre, saw rapid development that expanded its cultural and tourism possibilities, and transformed it into a newly vibrant city. Many local landmarks were restored, including the medieval [Torres de Serranos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_de_Serranos) and [Torres de Quart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_de_Quart) and the [Monasterio de San Miguel de los Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monasterio_de_San_Miguel_de_los_Reyes?action=edit\&redlink=1), which now holds a conservation library. Whole sections of the old city, for example the Carmen Quarter, have been extensively renovated. The Passeig Marítim, a 4 km (2 mi) long palm tree-lined beach promenade, was constructed along the [beaches](40630.md) of the north side of the port (Platja de Les Arenes, Platja del Cabanyal and Platja de la Malva-rosa).

Valencia boasts a highly active and diverse [nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife), with [bars](272207.md), dance bars, [beach bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_bar) and [nightclubs](19321330.md) staying open well past midnight.[^cite_note-103] The city has numerous [convention centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_center) and venues for trade events, among them the Institución Ferial de Valencia and the Palau de Congressos (Conference Palace), and several five-star hotels to accommodate business travelers.



Locals and tourists watching the traditional "[mascletà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masclet%C3%A0)" during *[Falles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falles)*

In its long history, Valencia has acquired many local traditions and festivals, among them the *[Falles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Fallas)*, which was declared a Celebration of International Tourist Interest *(Festes d'Interés Turístic Internacional)* on 25 January 1965[^cite_note-104] and an [intangible cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage) by UNESCO on 30 November 2016, and the [Water Tribunal of the plain of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Tribunal_of_the_plain_of_Valencia) called to order each Thursday in the [Plaza of the Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_of_the_Virgin), which was declared an intangible cultural heritage in 2009. In addition to these, Valencia has hosted world-class events that helped shape the city's reputation and put it in the international spotlight, such as the Regional Exhibition of 1909, the 32nd and the 33rd [America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Cup) competitions, the [European Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Grand_Prix) of [Formula One](10854.md) auto racing, the former [Valencia Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Open) tennis tournament, and the Global Champions Tour of equestrian sports. The final round of the [MotoGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motorcycle_racing) Championship is held annually at the [Circuit de la Comunitat Valenciana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Ricardo_Tormo).

The [2007 America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_America's_Cup) yachting races were held at Valencia in June and July 2007 and attracted huge crowds. The Louis Vuitton stage drew 1,044,373 visitors and the America's Cup match drew 466,010 visitors to the event.[^cite_note-105] The city was selected as the [European Capital of Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Sport?action=edit\&redlink=1) 2011.

In October 2021, Valencia was shortlisted for the [European Commission](9974.md)'s 2022 European Capital of Smart Tourism award, along with [Bordeaux](4097.md), [Copenhagen](5166.md), [Dublin](8504.md), [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), [Ljubljana](42374.md), and [Palma de Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca).[^cite_note-106] The city was selected as the [World Design Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Design_Capital) 2022 and the [European Green Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Capital_Award) 2024.[^cite_note-107]

Valencia boasts an Open Air Museum as a walking tour.[^cite_note-108]

Valencia is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Spain), the basic local administrative division in Spain. The *[ayuntamiento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayuntamiento_\(Spain\))* (*Ajuntament* in [Valencian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language)) is formally known as the *Consell Municipal de València* and is the body charged with the municipal government and administration,[^cite_note-109] and is formed by 33 elected municipal councillors. In 2015, [Joan Ribó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Rib%C3%B3) became the first mayor who did not belong to the [People's Party](404727.md) (PP) since 1991, renewing his term for a second mandate following the 2019 election.[^cite_note-110] The [last municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Valencia_City_Council_election) took place on 28 May 2023. ## Culture



Traditional preparation of paella.



A glass of [orxata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orxata) and [fartons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fartons)

Valencia is known internationally for the [Falles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falles) (*Les Falles*), a local festival held in March, as well as for *[paella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paella) valenciana*, traditional Valencian [ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramics), craftsmanship in traditional dress, and the architecture of the [City of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciutat_de_les_Arts_i_les_Ci%C3%A8ncies), designed by [Santiago Calatrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Calatrava) and [Félix Candela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Candela).

There are also a number of well-preserved traditional Catholic festivities throughout the year, like those of the [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semana_Santa), which are considered some of the most colourful [in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_Spain).[^cite_note-111]

Since 1886, the City of Valencia International Band Contest ([Spanish](26825.md): *Certamen Internacional de Bandas de Música Ciudad de Valencia*), a music contest for [wind orchestras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_orchestra) and [concert bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_band), is held each year in the town.[^cite_note-112]

Valencia used to be the place of the [Formula One](10854.md) [European Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Grand_Prix), first hosting the event on 24 August 2008, but was dropped later at the beginning of the [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Formula_One_World_Championship) Formula 1 season. However, Valencia still holds the annual Moto GP race at the [Circuit Ricardo Tormo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Ricardo_Tormo), usually the last race of the season, in November.

### Food

Main article: [Valencian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_cuisine)

Valencia is known for its gastronomic culture. The *[paella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paella)*, a simmered rice dish with meat (usually chicken or rabbit) or seafood, was born in Valencia. Other traditional dishes of Valencian gastronomy include [fideuà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fideu%C3%A0), [arròs a banda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arr%C3%B2s_a_banda), [arròs negre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arr%C3%B2s_negre) (black rice), [fartons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fartons), [bunyols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bu%C3%B1uelos), [Spanish omelette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortilla_de_patatas), [pinchos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pincho) or [tapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapas) and [calamares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squid_\(food\)) (squid).

Valencia was also the birthplace of the cold *[xufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyperus_esculentus)* beverage known as *[orxata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orxata)*, popular in many parts of the world, including the Americas.

### Languages



[Carrer de la Pau](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrer%20de%20la%20Pau) street sign

[Valencian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language) and [Spanish](26825.md) are the [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language). Spanish is currently the predominant language in the city proper.[^cite_note-113] Valencia proper and its surrounding metropolitan area are—along with the [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante) area—the traditionally Valencian-speaking territories of the Valencian Community where the Valencian language is less spoken and read.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBriz2004120-114] According to a 2019 survey commissioned by the local government, 76% of the population use only Spanish in their daily life, 1.3% use only the Valencian language, while 17.6% of the population use both languages.[^cite_note-115] However, vis-à-vis the education system and according to the 1983 regional Law on the Use and Teaching of the Valencian Language, the municipality of Valencia is included within the territory of Valencian linguistic predominance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZabaltza201769-116] In 1993, the municipal government agreed to use exclusively Valencian for the plaques that serve as street signs.[^cite_note-117] The variety of Valencian spoken in and around the city of Valencia is known as *[apitxat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Valencian)*.[^cite_note-118]

### Falles

Main article: [Falles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falles)



*La cremà* (burning of the *ninots*) during the 2015 falles

Every year, the five days and nights from 15 to 19 March, called [Falles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falles), are a continual festival in Valencia; beginning on 1 March, the popular pyrotechnic events called *[mascletàes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masclet%C3%A0)* start every day at 2\:00 pm. The [Falles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falles) (*Fallas* in Spanish) is an enduring tradition in Valencia and other towns in the [Valencian Community](20514168.md),[^cite_note-fiestas-119] where it has become an important tourist attraction. The festival started being celebrated in the 18th century,[^cite_note-fallasvalencia.es-120] and came to be celebrated on the night of the feast day of [Saint Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph), the patron saint of carpenters, with the burning of waste planks of wood from their workshops, as well as worn-out wooden objects brought by people in the neighborhood.[^cite_note-RodgersRodgers2002-121]

This tradition continued to evolve, and eventually the *parots* were dressed with clothing to look like people—these were the first *ninots*, with features identifiable as being those of a well-known person from the neighborhood often added as well. In 1901 the city inaugurated the awarding of prizes for the best Falles monuments,[^cite_note-fallasvalencia.es-120] and neighborhood groups still rival with each other to make the most impressive creations.[^cite_note-Villarroya1992-122] Their intricate assemblages, on top of pedestals for better visibility, depict famous personalities and topical subjects of the past year, presenting humorous and often satirical commentary on them.

The 19 March, St Joseph's Day, is a regional public holiday. On the night of 19 March, Valencians burn all the Falles in an event called "La Cremà".[^cite_note-123]

### Holy Week

The *Setmana* or *[Semana Santa Marinera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_Valencia?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, as the [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week) is known in the city, was declared "Festival of National Tourist Interest" by 2012.[^cite_note-124]

## Main sights

Its historic centre is one of the largest in Spain, spanning approximately 169 hectares (420 acres).[^cite_note-125] Major monuments include [Valencia Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Cathedral), the [Torres de Serrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_de_Serranos), the [Torres de Quart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_de_Quart), the [Lonja de la Seda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonja_de_la_Seda) (declared a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in 1996). The [Museu de Belles Arts de València](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museu_de_Belles_Arts_de_Val%C3%A8ncia) houses a large collection of paintings from the 14th to the 18th centuries, including works by [Velázquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez), [El Greco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Greco), and [Goya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Goya), as well as an important series of engravings by [Piranesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Piranesi).[^cite_note-126] The [Institut Valencià d'Art Modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Valenci%C3%A0_d'Art_Modern) (Valencian Institute of Modern Art) houses both permanent collections and temporary exhibitions of contemporary art and photography.[^cite_note-127]

### Architecture

The ancient winding streets of the Barrio del Carmen contain buildings dating to [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) times. The [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mary_of_Valencia_Cathedral) and its bell tower [El Miguelete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguelete_Tower), built between the 13th and 15th centuries, are primarily of [Valencian Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_Gothic) style but contains elements of [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) and [Romanesque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture). Beside the cathedral is the [Basilica of Our Lady of the Forsaken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_the_Forsaken) (Basílica De La Mare de Déu dels Desamparats). The 15th-century Serrans and Quart towers are part of what was once the wall surrounding the city.

[UNESCO](21786641.md) has recognised the Silk Exchange market ([La Llotja de la Seda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonja_de_la_Seda)), erected in early Valencian Gothic style, as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-128] The Central Market ([Mercat Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercado_Central,_Valencia)) in [Valencian Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_Art_Nouveau) style, is one of the largest in Europe. The main railway station [Estació Del Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estaci%C3%B3_del_Nord_\(Valencia\)) is built in [Valencian Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_Art_Nouveau) (a Spanish version of [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau)) style.

The [Ciutat de les Arts i les Ciències](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciutat_de_les_Arts_i_les_Ci%C3%A8ncies) (City of Arts and Sciences) is an entertainment, cultural and architectural complex designed by [Santiago Calatrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Calatrava) and [Félix Candela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Candela).[^cite_note-CAC-129] It contains an opera house/performing [arts centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_centre), a [science museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_museum), an [IMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAX) cinema/planetarium, an oceanographic park and other structures such as a long covered walkway and restaurants. Calatrava is also responsible for the bridge named after him in the centre of the city. The [Palau de la Música de València](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_de_la_M%C3%BAsica_de_Val%C3%A8ncia) (Music Palace) is another noteworthy example of modern architecture in Valencia.

### Cathedral

Main article: [Valencia Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Cathedral)



Northern view of the cathedral: dome, apse, and the Basilica of Our Lady

The [Valencia Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Cathedral) was called "Iglesia Major" in the early days of the *Reconquista*, then "Iglesia de la Seu" ("Seu" is from the Latin "sedes", i.e., (archiepiscopal) See), and by virtue of the papal concession of 16 October 1866, it was called the "Basílica Metropolitana". It is situated in the centre of the ancient Roman city where some believe the temple of Diana stood. In Gothic times, it seems to have been dedicated to the Holy Saviour; [the Cid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cid) dedicated it to the Blessed Virgin; King [James I of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon) did likewise, leaving in the main chapel the image of the Blessed Virgin, which he carried with him and is reputed to be the one now preserved in the sacristy. The Moorish mosque, which had been converted into a [Christian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church) by the conqueror, was deemed unworthy of the title of the cathedral of Valencia, and in 1262 Bishop Andrés de Albalat laid the cornerstone of the new Gothic building, with three naves; these reach only to the choir of the present building. Bishop Vidal de Blanes built the chapter hall, and James I added the tower, called [El Miguelete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguelete_Tower) in [Castilian Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilian_Spanish) or Torre del Micalet in the [Valencian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language) because it was blessed on St. Michael's day in 1418. The tower is about 58 metres (190 feet) high and is topped with a [belfry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_tower) (1660–1736).

In the 15th century the dome was added and the naves extended back of the choir, uniting the building to the tower and forming a main entrance. Archbishop Luis Alfonso de los Cameros began the building of the main chapel in 1674; the walls were decorated with marbles and bronzes in the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) style of that period. At the beginning of the 18th century, the German Conrad Rudolphus built the façade of the main entrance. The other two doors lead into the transept; one, that of the Apostles in pure pointed Gothic, dates from the 14th century, the other is that of the Palau. The additions made to the back of the cathedral detract from its height. The 18th-century restoration rounded the pointed arches, covered the Gothic columns with [Corinthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinthian_order) pillars, and redecorated the walls.

The dome has no lantern, its plain ceiling being pierced by two large side windows. There are four chapels on either side, besides that at the end and those that open into the choir, the transept, and the sanctuary. It contains many paintings by eminent artists. A silver [reredos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reredos), which was behind the altar, was carried away in the war of 1808, and converted into coin to meet the expenses of the campaign. There are two paintings by [Francisco de Goya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Goya) in the San Francesco chapel. Behind the Chapel of the [Blessed Sacrament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament) is a small [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) chapel built by [Calixtus III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Calixtus_III). Beside the cathedral is the [Basilica of Our Lady of the Forsaken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_the_Forsaken) dedicated to [Our Lady of the Forsaken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgen_de_los_Desamparados).

The Tribunal de les Aigües (Water Court), a court dating from Moorish times that hears and mediates in matters relating to irrigation water, sits at noon every Thursday outside the Porta dels Apostols (Portal of the Apostles) in the [Plaza of the Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_of_the_Virgin).[^cite_note-132]

### Hospital



[Palau de la Generalitat Valenciana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Generalitat_Valenciana)

In 1409, a hospital was founded and placed under the [patronage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronage) of [Santa Maria dels Innocents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_de_los_Inocentes?action=edit\&redlink=1). The building now houses the [Public Library of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Library_of_Valencia). To this was attached a confraternity devoted to recovering the bodies of the unfriended dead in the city and within a radius of 5 km (3.1 mi) around it. At the end of the 15th century this confraternity separated from the hospital, and continued its work under the name of "Cofradia para el ámparo de los desamparados". King [Philip IV of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_IV_of_Spain) and the [Duke of Arcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Arcos) suggested the building of the new chapel, and in 1647 the [Viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy), [Conde de Oropesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conde_de_Oropesa?action=edit\&redlink=1), who had been preserved from [the bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague), insisted on carrying out their project. The Blessed Virgin was proclaimed patroness of the city under the title of *Virgen de los desamparados* (Virgin of the Forsaken), and Archbishop Pedro de Urbina, on 31 June 1652, laid the cornerstone of the new chapel of this name. The archiepiscopal palace, a [grain market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_trade) in the time of the Moors, is simple in design, with an inside cloister and achapel. In 1357, the arch that connects it with the cathedral was built. Inside the council chamber are preserved the portraits of all the prelates of Valencia.

### Squares and gardens



[Turia Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turia_Garden)

The [largest plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_city_squares_by_size) in Valencia is the Plaça del Ajuntament; it is home to the [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_City_Hall) *(Ajuntament)* on its western side and the central post office (Edifici de Correus) on its eastern side, a cinema that shows classic movies, and many restaurants and bars. It serves as ground zero during the [Les Falles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falles) when the fireworks of the *[Mascletà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masclet%C3%A0)* can be heard every afternoon. There is a large fountain at the northern end.

Around the corner is the [Plaza de la Reina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_la_Reina), with the cathedral, orange trees, and many bars and restaurants.

### Football

Valencia is also internationally famous for its football club, [Valencia CF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_CF), one of the most successful clubs in Europe and La Liga, winning the [Spanish league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) a total of six times including in 2002 and 2004 (the year it also won the [UEFA Cup](232175.md)), and was a [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) [runner-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runner-up) in 2000 and 2001. The club is currently owned by [Peter Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Lim), a Singaporean businessman who bought the club in 2014. The team's stadium is the [Mestalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestalla_Stadium), which can host up to 49,000 fans. The club's city rival, [Levante UD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levante_UD), plays its home games at [Estadi Ciutat de València](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadi_Ciutat_de_Val%C3%A8ncia).

### American football

Valencia is the only city in Spain with two American football teams in [LNFA Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LNFA), the national first division: [Valencia Firebats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Firebats) and [Valencia Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Giants). The Firebats have been national champions four times and have represented Valencia and Spain in the European playoffs since 2005. Both teams share the Jardín del Turia stadium.

### Motor sports



[Valencia Street Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Street_Circuit)

Once a year between 2008 and 2012 the European [Formula One](10854.md) Grand Prix took place in the [Valencia Street Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Street_Circuit). Valencia is among (with [Barcelona](4443.md), [Porto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto) and [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo)) the only European cities ever to host Formula One World Championship Grands Prix on public roads in the middle of cities. The final race in [2012 European Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_European_Grand_Prix) saw home driver [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso) win for [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari), in spite of starting halfway down the field. The [Valencian Community motorcycle Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_Community_motorcycle_Grand_Prix) *(Gran Premi de la Comunitat Valenciana de motociclisme)* is part of the Grand Prix motorcycle racing season at the [Circuit Ricardo Tormo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Ricardo_Tormo) (also known as *Circuit de València*), held in November in the nearby town of [Cheste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheste). Periodically the Spanish round of the [Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Tourenwagen_Masters) touring car racing Championship (DTM) is held in Valencia.

### Rugby League

Valencia is also the home of the [Asociación Española de Rugby League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asociaci%C3%B3n_Espa%C3%B1ola_de_Rugby_League), who are the governing body for [Rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) in Spain. The city plays host to a number of clubs playing the sport and to date has hosted all of the country's international home matches.[^cite_note-137] In 2015, Valencia hosted their first match in the [Rugby league European Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league_European_Federation?action=edit\&redlink=1) C competition, which was a qualifier for the [2017 Rugby League World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Rugby_League_World_Cup). Spain won the fixture 40–30.[^cite_note-138]

## Infrastructure

### Port

Main article: [Port of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Valencia)



View of the port of Valencia

Valencia's port is the biggest on the Mediterranean western coast,[^cite_note-139] the first in Spain in container traffic as of 2008[^cite_note-140] and the second in Spain[^cite_note-141] in total traffic, handling 20% of Spain's exports.[^cite_note-142] The main exports are foodstuffs and beverages. Other exports include oranges, furniture, ceramic tiles, fans, textiles and iron products. Valencia's [manufacturing sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy) focuses on metallurgy, chemicals, textiles, shipbuilding and brewing. Small and medium-sized industries are an important part of the local economy.

Valencia's port underwent radical changes to accommodate the 32nd [America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Cup) in 2007. It was divided into two parts—one was unchanged while the other section was modified for America's Cup festivities. The two sections remain divided by a wall that projects far into the water to maintain clean water for America's Cup events.

### Transport



Metrovalencia, rapid transit

Public transport is provided by [Ferrocarrils de la Generalitat Valenciana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocarrils_de_la_Generalitat_Valenciana) (FGV), which operates the [Metrovalencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrovalencia) (rapid transit, tram) and other rail and bus services. The [Estació del Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estaci%C3%B3_del_Nord_\(Valencia\)) (North Station) is the major railway terminus in Valencia. A second station, the [Estació de València-Joaquín Sorolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia-Joaqu%C3%ADn_Sorolla_railway_station), has been built on land adjacent to this terminus to accommodate high speed [AVE](523382.md) trains to and from Madrid and Alicante. [Valencia Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Airport) is situated 9 km (5.6 mi) west of the city centre, and [Alicante–Elche Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante%E2%80%93Elche_Miguel_Hern%C3%A1ndez_Airport) is approximately 133 km (83 mi) south of the city centre.

A [bicycle-sharing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle-sharing_system) named [Valenbisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valenbisi) is available to both visitors and residents. As of 13 October 2012, the system had 2750 bikes distributed over 250 stations throughout the city.[^cite_note-143]

The average amount of time people spend on public transit in Valencia on a weekday is 44 minutes. 6% of public transit riders ride for more than 2 hours every day. The average amount of time people wait at a stop or station for public transit is 10 minutes, while 9% of riders wait for over 20 minutes on average every day. The average distance people usually ride in a single trip with public transit is 5.9 km (3.7 mi), while 8% travel for over 12 km (7.5 mi) in a single direction.[^cite_note-144]

## Education

The [University of Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Valencia) (Universitat de València Estudi General), founded in 1499, is one of the oldest surviving universities in Spain and the oldest in the Valencian Community. It was listed as one of the four leading Spanish universities in the 2011 [Shanghai Academic Ranking of World Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities).

In 2012, Boston's [Berklee College of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berklee_College_of_Music) opened a satellite campus at the [Palau de les Arts Reina Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_de_les_Arts_Reina_Sofia), their first and only international campus.[^cite_note-145] Since 2003, Valencia has also hosted the music courses of [Musikeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musikeon?action=edit\&redlink=1), the leading musical institution in the Spanish-speaking world.
