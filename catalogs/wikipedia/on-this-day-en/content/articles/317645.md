**State-sponsored terrorism** is [terrorist](30636.md) violence carried out with the active support of national governments provided to [violent non-state actors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_non-state_actor). It contrasts with *[state terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_terrorism)*, which is carried out directly by state actors.

States can sponsor terrorist groups in several ways, including but not limited to funding terrorist organizations, providing training, supplying weapons, providing other logistical and intelligence assistance, and hosting groups within their borders. Because of the pejorative nature of the word, the identification of particular examples are often subject to political dispute and different [definitions of terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definitions_of_terrorism).

A wide variety of states in both [developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) and [developing](78449.md) areas of the world have engaged in sponsoring terrorism. During the 1970s and 1980s, state sponsorship of terrorism was a frequent feature of international conflict. From that time to the 2010s there was a steady pattern of decline in the prevalence and magnitude of state support. Nevertheless, because of the increasing consequent level of violence that it could potentially facilitate, it remains an issue of highly salient international concern.[^cite_note-1]

## Definition

&#x20;There are at least 250 definitions of "terrorism" available in academic literature and government and intergovernmental sources, several of which include mention of state sponsorship.[^cite_note-2] In a review of primary documents on international law governing armed conflict, Reisman and Antoniou identify that:[^cite_note-3]

> Terrorism has come to mean the intentional use of violence against civilian and military targets generally outside of an acknowledged war zone by private groups or groups that appear to be private but have some measure of covert state sponsorship.

&#x20;The [Gilmore Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilmore_Commission)[^cite_note-4] of the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) gave the following definition of state-sponsored terrorism:[^cite_note-5]

> the active involvement of a foreign government in training, arming, and providing other logistical and intelligence assistance as well as sanctuary to an otherwise autonomous terrorist group for the purpose of carrying out violent acts on behalf of that government against its enemies.

The [U.S. Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Government), which has repeatedly engaged in sponsorship of terrorism as a feature of its foreign policy,[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-uscuba-7][^cite_note-8] provides its own definition in the U.S. State Department's [list of state sponsors of terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Sponsors_of_Terrorism_\(U.S._list\)).[^cite_note-grant-9] Authorities and scholars of terrorism and conflict, such as [Alex P. Schmid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_P._Schmid) (former Officer-in-Charge of Terrorism Prevention at the [United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_on_Drugs_and_Crime)), [Daniel Byman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Byman), Richard Chasdi, and Frank Shanty, have pointed to problems in the U.S.' definition, including that it is politicized, analytically unclear,[^cite_note-10] and inherently self-serving.[^cite_note-11]

## Background

The use of terrorist organizations as proxies in armed conflicts between state actors became more attractive in the mid-20th century as a result of post [World War II](32927.md) developments like the increasing costs of traditional warfare and the risk of [nuclear war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_war). Speaking about the effect of nuclear capability on traditional military conflict [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) agent Alexander Sakharovsky said that "In today's world, when nuclear arms have made military force obsolete, terrorism should become our main weapon." Though state-sponsored terrorism persists in the post-9/11 era, some scholars have argued that it has become less significant in the age of global jihadism. On the other hand, [Daniel Byman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Byman) believes its importance has increased. Organizations like [Hamas](13913.md), [Hezbollah](13919.md) and [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md) are heavily dependent on state support. According to the US Counter-Terrorism Coordinator's Office this support can include "funds, weapons, materials and the secure areas" that organizations use for "planning and conducting operations".[^cite_note-ganor-12]

The *Max Planck Encyclopedia of Public International Law* notes that international legal institutions currently lack a mechanism to prosecute terrorist leaders who "instruct, support or succour" terrorism. At the conclusion of the [Lockerbie trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockerbie_trial), some commentators continued to harbor doubts about the legitimacy of the only conviction secured during the trial, and thus also about [Libya](17633.md)'s involvement. The domestic trial proved to be insufficient to identify those who had given the instructions.[^cite_note-grant-9]

## By country

### Afghanistan

See also: [KHAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHAD) and [Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrik-i-Taliban_Pakistan)

The United States and Pakistan have accused [Afghanistan's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) KhAD agency of being responsible for numerous terrorist attacks on Pakistani soil in the 1980s and early 1990s.

According to a report by the US Department of Defense, approximately 90% of the estimated 777 acts of international terrorism committed worldwide in 1987 took place in Pakistan.[^cite_note-13] By 1988, [KhAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHAD) and [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) agents were able to penetrate deep inside Pakistan and carry out attacks on mujahideen sanctuaries and guerrilla bases.[^cite_note-14] There was strong circumstantial evidence implicating Moscow-Kabul in the August 1988 assassination of [Zia ul-Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Zia-ul-Haq), as the Soviets perceived that Zia wanted to adversely affect the [Geneva process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Accords_\(1988\)).[^cite_note-15] WAD/KhAD has also been suspected behind the assassination of [Palestinian](23267.md) jihadist [Abdullah Yusuf Azzam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Yusuf_Azzam) alongside his son in 1989.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-\:02-17]

Afghanistan's KHAD was one of four secret service agencies accused of perpetrating terrorist bombings in multiple Pakistani cities including [Islamabad](51153.md), [Lahore](125315.md), [Karachi](17123.md), and [Rawalpindi](343524.md) during the early 1980s resulting in hundreds of civilian casualties.[^cite_note-18] By the late 1980s, the US State Department blamed WAD for the perpetration of terrorist bombings in Pakistani cities.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Between the late 1970s and the early 1990s, Afghanistan security agencies supported the terrorist organization called Al-[Zulfiqar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Zulfikar), the group that hijacked a Pakistan International Airlines plane from Karachi to Kabul in 1981.[^cite_note-21] Notable attacks include the [Karachi Car bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Karachi_car_bombing) and an attempted car bombing on the [US Consulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_consulate) in [Peshawar](66230.md) which ended up killing over 30 people in 1987.[^cite_note-\:12-22] KhAD has also been accused of being behind the Hathora Murders which terrorized Karachi for 2 years in the mid-1980s.[^cite_note-23]

On 24 June 2017, Pakistani army chief [Qamar Javed Bajwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qamar_Javed_Bajwa) chaired a high-level meeting in Rawalpindi and called on Afghanistan to "do more" in the fight against terrorism. According to the [ISPR](7718433.md), the [attacks in Quetta and Parachinar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2017_Pakistan_bombings) were linked to terrorist sanctuaries in Afghanistan which enjoyed the "patronage of Afghanistan's [National Directorate of Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Directorate_of_Security) (NDS)"[^cite_note-DAWN6-24][^cite_note-25]

### China

See also: [Naxalite–Maoist insurgency](21636599.md) and [Insurgency in Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_Northeast_India)

[India](14533.md) has accused China of supporting the [Naxalites](4760079.md) in the [Naxalite–Maoist insurgency](21636599.md).[^cite_note-26] In 2011, Indian police accused the Chinese government of providing sanctuary to the movement's leaders, and accused Pakistani [ISI](2159021.md) of providing financial support.[^cite_note-China_Paki_link-27] India has also reported of China supporting militant groups in its [Northeast states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India) of [Manipur](422642.md), [Nagaland](408677.md) and [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram).[^cite_note-IPCS-28][^cite_note-The_Quint-29]

The Chinese government has blocked [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council) [Sanctions Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaida_Sanctions_Committee) listing of [Masood Azhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masood_Azhar), the founder and leader of the Pakistan-based organization [Jaish-e-Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaish-e-Mohammed) as a terrorist.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Since 2009, there have been 4 attempts to put Masood Azhar in the UN Security Council's counter-terrorism sanctions list. All the attempts were vetoed by China, citing 'lack of evidence'. In 2016 China again blocked India's appeal to the United Nations to label Azhar as a terrorist.[^cite_note-32] China also blocked a US move to get Azhar banned by the UN in February 2017.[^cite_note-33] The most recent attempt was on 13 March 2019.[^cite_note-Firstpost14-34] However, in May 2019, China backed listing of Masood Azhar as a global terrorist.[^cite_note-UNSC-35]

In mid-2020, [Myanmar](19457.md) accused China of arming the [Arakan Army](38106209.md), which was legally considered a terrorist organisation by the Myanmar government from 2019 to 2021. China has allegedly given the Arakan Army assault rifles, machine guns and [FN-6 Chinese Manpads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN-6) capable of shooting down helicopters, drones and combat aircraft.[^cite_note-EFSAS-36][^cite_note-The_Irrawaddy-37][^cite_note-LICAS_News-38]

### India

India has been accused by Pakistan[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] of supporting terrorism and carrying out "economic sabotage".[^cite_note-tribune.com.pk1-41]

In 2017, [Kulbhushan Jadhav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulbhushan_Jadhav), an Indian naval officer arrested in March 2016 in [Balochistan](4687.md) and charged with espionage and sabotage was [sentenced to death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentenced_to_death). He was accused of operating a [covert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covert) terror network within Balochistan.[^cite_note-Spy-42] Jadhav had confessed that he was tasked by India's intelligence agency, the [Research and Analysis Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_Analysis_Wing) (RAW), "to plan and organise espionage and sabotage activities" in Balochistan and Karachi.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-Spy-42]

In November 2020, the [Foreign Office of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Office_of_Pakistan) made public a dossier containing 'irrefutable proofs' of the alleged Indian sponsorship of [terrorism in Pakistan](2816470.md).[^cite_note-44] It contained proof of India's alleged financial and material sponsorship of multiple terrorist organisations, including UN-designated terrorist organisations [Balochistan Liberation Army](6010135.md), [Jamaat-ul-Ahrar](44284567.md), and [Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrik-i-Taliban_Pakistan).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] The dossier was shared with the [United Nations Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General) [António Guterres](471806.md).[^cite_note-48]

Pakistan has also accused Indian consulates in Kandahar and Jalalabad, Afghanistan, of providing arms, training and financial aid to the Balochistan Liberation Army (BLA) in an attempt to destabilize Pakistan.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-outlook-50] [Brahamdagh Bugti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahamdagh_Bugti) stated in a 2008 interview that he would accept help from India.[^cite_note-51] Pakistan has repeatedly accused India of supporting Baloch rebels,[^cite_note-talks-52] and [David Wright-Neville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Wright-Neville) writes that outside Pakistan, some Western observers also believe that India secretly funds the BLA.[^cite_note-Wright-Neville-53] In August 2013, US Special Representative [James Dobbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dobbins_\(diplomat\)) said Pakistan's fears over India's role in Afghanistan were “not groundless".[^cite_note-54] A [leaked diplomatic cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak) sent on December 31, 2009, from the U.S. consulate in Karachi said it was "plausible" that Indian intelligence was helping the Baluch insurgents. An earlier 2008 cable, discussing the Mumbai attacks reported fears by British officials that "intense domestic pressure would force Delhi to respond, at the minimum, by ramping up covert support to nationalist insurgents fighting the Pakistani army in Baluchistan."[^cite_note-55]

#### Operation Pawan

Main article: [Operation Pawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pawan)

India's Research and Analysis Wing trained and armed the Sri Lankan Tamil group [LTTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTTE) which want an independent country for Tamils of Sri Lanka, due to the continuous [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_Only_Act) and [violent persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_attacks_on_civilians_attributed_to_Sri_Lankan_government_forces) against [Sri Lankan Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamils) by the [Sinhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people) dominated [Sri Lankan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_government) during the 1970s, but it later withdrew its support in the late 1980s when the terrorist activities of LTTE became serious and it formed alliances with separatist groups in the southern Indian state of Tamil Nadu.[^cite_note-\:3-56][^cite_note-57] From August 1983 to May 1987, India, through its intelligence agency Research and Analysis Wing (R\&AW), provided arms, training and monetary support to six Sri Lankan Tamil insurgent groups including the LTTE. During that period, 32 terror training camps were set up in India to train these 495 LTTE insurgents,[^cite_note-sndayt-58] including 90 women who were trained in 10 batches.[^cite_note-59] The first batch of Tigers were trained in [Establishment 22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Frontier_Force) based in [Chakrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakrata), Uttarakhand. The second batch, including LTTE intelligence chief [Pottu Amman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottu_Amman_\(Tamil_militant\)),[^cite_note-60] trained in [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md). Prabakaran visited the first and the second batch of Tamil Tigers to see them training. Eight other batches of LTTE were trained in Tamil Nadu.[^cite_note-61] [Thenmozhi Rajaratnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thenmozhi_Rajaratnam) *alias* Dhanu, who carried out the [assassination of Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Rajiv_Gandhi) and Sivarasan—the key conspirator were among the militants trained by R\&AW, in [Nainital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nainital), India.[^cite_note-62] In April 1984, the LTTE formally joined a common militant front, the [Eelam National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eelam_National_Liberation_Front) (ENLF), a union between LTTE, the [Tamil Eelam Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Eelam_Liberation_Organization) (TELO), the [Eelam Revolutionary Organisation of Students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eelam_Revolutionary_Organisation_of_Students) (EROS), the [People's Liberation Organisation of Tamil Eelam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Organisation_of_Tamil_Eelam) (PLOTE) and the [Eelam People's Revolutionary Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eelam_People's_Revolutionary_Liberation_Front) (EPRLF). On [4 June 1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Poomalai), when the Tamil Tiger-held Jaffna Peninsula was under siege by the Sri Lankan army, India provided airdrop of relief supplies to LTTE.[^cite_note-63]

### Iran

Main article: [Iran and state-sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_and_state-sponsored_terrorism)

See also: [Hezbollah–Iran relations](75617077.md) and [2024 Iranian operations inside Australia](80881934.md)

Former United States President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) accused the Iranian government of being the "world's primary state sponsor of terror."[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

Iran's [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) was instrumental in founding, training, and supplying [Hezbollah](13919.md), a group designated a "Foreign Terrorist Organization" by the United States Department of State,[^cite_note-2009-2017.state.gov-67] and likewise labeled a terrorist organization by Israel's Ministry of Foreign Affairs[^cite_note-68] and the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md).[^cite_note-69] This view is not universal, however; for example, the [European Union](9317.md) differentiates between the political, social, and military wings of Hezbollah, designating only its military wing as a terrorist organization,[^cite_note-70] while various other countries maintain relations with Hezbollah.

Iran conducted terrorism in Australia through the [2024 Iranian operations inside Australia](80881934.md).[^cite_note-71] On 27 November 2025, the [Parliament of Australia](302298.md) formally listed the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps as a State Sponsor of Terrorism, under the framework of the Criminal Code Amendment (State Sponsors of Terrorism) Act 2025.[^cite_note-\:03-72][^cite_note-\:13-73] The IRGC was the first entity to be designated as such, after the Minister of Home Affairs determined that it met the criteria specified in Division 110 of the code, thereby following the recommendations of Australian Government intelligence, security and policy agencies.[^cite_note-\:03-72][^cite_note-\:13-73] According to the listing, it is "illegal to direct the activities of, be a member of, associate with members of, recruit for, train with, get funds to, from or for, or provide support to, the Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corp",[^cite_note-\:03-72][^cite_note-\:13-73] with a penalty of 25 years of imprisonment.[^cite_note-\:03-72][^cite_note-\:13-73]

### Iraq

See also: [State Sponsors of Terrorism (U.S. list) § Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Sponsors_of_Terrorism_\(U.S._list\)#Iraq)

[Iraq](7515928.md) was added to the list on December 29, 1979, but was removed in February 1982 to allow US aid to Iraq while it was fighting Iran in the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] It was re-added on September 13, 1990, following its [invasion of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Kuwait),[^cite_note-76] and again removed from the list on 25 October 2004.[^cite_note-77]

### Israel

Main article: [Israel and state-sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_and_state-sponsored_terrorism)

In the 21st-century, the [State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Israel) has been accused of sponsoring and supporting opposition groups as part of [its proxy conflict with Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Israel_proxy_conflict), as well as being a state-sponsor of terrorism,[^cite_note-78] and also committing acts of [state terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_terrorism).[^cite_note-79]

Several sovereign countries have at some point officially alleged that Israel is a proponent of state-sponsored terrorism, including [Iran](14653.md), [Lebanon](17771.md),[^cite_note-80] [Saudi Arabia](349303.md),[^cite_note-81] [Syria](7515849.md),[^cite_note-82] [Turkey](11125639.md),[^cite_note-83] and [Yemen](350939.md).[^cite_note-84]

An early example of Israeli state-sponsored terrorism was the 1954 [Lavon Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavon_Affair), a botched bomb plot in Egypt that led to the resignation of the Israeli defense minister at the time. In the 1970s and 1980s, Israel was also a major supplier of arms to dictatorial regimes in South America, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Asia. A report by [Brian Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Ross_\(journalist\)) and [Christopher Isham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Isham) of [ABC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_\(United_States\)) in April 2007 alleged that [Jundallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jundallah_\(Iran\)) "had been secretly encouraged and advised by American officials" to destabilize the government in Iran.[^cite_note-85] Israel additionally trained, and armed, far right drug trafficking paramilitary organizations in Latin America, such as the "[United Self-Defense Forces of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Self-Defense_Forces_of_Colombia)" during the 80s.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) was also himself accused of [championing a policy of empowering Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_support_for_Hamas) in Gaza by allowing suitcases of Qatari money to be given to the Israeli-designated terrorist group as part of a strategy to sabotage a [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution) by confining the Palestinian Authority to the West Bank and weakening it, and to demonstrate to the Israeli public and western governments that Israel has no partner for peace.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-CBS11-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

### Libya

Main article: [Libya and state-sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_and_state-sponsored_terrorism)

### Pakistan

Main article: [Pakistan and state-sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_and_state-sponsored_terrorism)

Pakistan has been accused by [India](14533.md), [Afghanistan](737.md), [Israel](9282173.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and the [United States](3434750.md)[^cite_note-US-105][^cite_note-state-106][^cite_note-Pakdt-107] of involvement in [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\)) as well as Afghanistan.[^cite_note-NYT2006-108] [Poland](22936.md) has also alleged that terrorists have "friends in Pakistani government structures".[^cite_note-NYT2009-109] In July 2009, the then [President of Pakistan](419460.md) [Asif Ali Zardari](993655.md) admitted that the [Pakistani government](1897833.md) had "created and nurtured" terrorist groups to achieve its short-term foreign policy goals in the 1980s under Zia.[^cite_note-Zardari-110] According to an analysis published by the [Saban Center for Middle East Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saban_Center_for_Middle_East_Policy) at [Brookings Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookings_Institution) in 2008, Pakistan was the worlds 'most active' state sponsor of terrorism including aiding groups which were considered a direct threat to the United States.[^cite_note-111]

The [Jammu Kashmir Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_Kashmir_Liberation_Front) (JKLF) has stated that it was training more than 3,000 militants from various nationalities.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] According to some reports published by the [Council of Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Foreign_Relations), the Pakistan military and the [Inter-Services Intelligence](2159021.md) (ISI) have provided covert support to terrorist groups active in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir), including [Jaish-e-Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaish-e-Mohammed).[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] Pakistan has denied any involvement in terrorist activities in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir), arguing that it only provides political and moral support to the [secessionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secessionist) groups who wish to escape Indian rule. Many Kashmiri militant groups also maintain their headquarters in [Pakistan-administered Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azad_Jammu_and_Kashmir), which is cited as further proof by the [Indian government](553883.md). Many of the terrorist organizations are banned by the UN and Pakistan, but continue to operate under different names.[^cite_note-116]

The [United Nations](31769.md) organization has publicly increased pressure on Pakistan on its inability to control its Afghanistan border and not restricting the activities of [Taliban](30635.md) leaders who have been designated by the UN as terrorists.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] Many consider that Pakistan has been playing both sides in the US "[war on terror](13425800.md)".[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

[Ahmed Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Rashid), a noted Pakistani journalist, has accused Pakistan's ISI of providing help to the Taliban.[^cite_note-121] Author [Ted Galen Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Galen_Carpenter?action=edit\&redlink=1) echoed that statement, stating that Pakistan "... assisted rebel forces in Kashmir even though those groups have committed terrorist acts against civilians"[^cite_note-122] Author [Gordon Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Thomas_\(author\)) stated that whilst aiding in the capture of Al-Qaeda members, Pakistan "still sponsored terrorist groups in the disputed state of Kashmir, funding, training and arming them in their war on attrition against India."[^cite_note-123] Journalist [Stephen Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Schwartz_\(journalist\)) notes that several militant and criminal groups are "backed by senior officers in the Pakistani army, the country's ISI intelligence establishment and other armed bodies of the state."[^cite_note-124] According to one author, [Daniel Byman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Byman), "Pakistan is probably today's most active sponsor of terrorism."[^cite_note-125]

The Inter-Services Intelligence has often been accused of playing a role in major [terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_attacks) across the world including the [September 11, 2001 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001_attacks) in the United States,[^cite_note-126] [terrorism in Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_in_Jammu_and_Kashmir),[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] [Mumbai Train Bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_July_2006_Mumbai_train_bombings),[^cite_note-130] [Indian Parliament Attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Indian_Parliament_attack),[^cite_note-131] [Varanasi bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Varanasi_bombings),[^cite_note-132] [Hyderabad bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25_August_2007_Hyderabad_bombings)[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] and [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] The ISI is also accused of supporting Taliban forces[^cite_note-autogenerated2-137] and recruiting and training [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen)[^cite_note-autogenerated2-137][^cite_note-138] to fight in Afghanistan[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-autogenerated1-140] and Kashmir.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-140] Based on communication intercepts US intelligence agencies concluded Pakistan's ISI was behind the [attack on the Indian embassy in Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Indian_embassy_bombing_in_Kabul) on July 7, 2008, a charge that the governments of India and Afghanistan had laid previously.[^cite_note-141] Afghan President [Hamid Karzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Karzai), who has constantly reiterated allegations that militants operating training camps in Pakistan have used it as a launch platform to attack targets in Afghanistan, urged western military allies to target extremist hideouts in neighbouring Pakistan.[^cite_note-142] When the United States, during the [Clinton administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_administration), targeted Al-Qaeda camps in Afghanistan with [cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missiles), *[Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_\(magazine\))* reported that two officers of the ISI were killed.[^cite_note-143]

Pakistan is accused of sheltering and training the Taliban as strategic asset[^cite_note-144] in operations "which include soliciting funding for the Taliban, bankrolling Taliban operations, providing diplomatic support as the Taliban's virtual emissaries abroad, arranging training for Taliban fighters, recruiting skilled and unskilled manpower to serve in Taliban armies, planning and directing offensives, providing and facilitating shipments of ammunition and fuel, and on several occasions apparently directly providing combat support," as reported by [Human Rights Watch](46530.md).

Pakistan was accused of evacuating of about 5,000 of the top leadership of the Taliban and Al-Qaeda who were encircled by NATO forces in the 2001 invasion of Afghanistan. This alleged event, often referred as the Kunduz airlift, which was also popularly called the "Airlift of Evil", allegedly involved several [Pakistani Air Force](377433.md) transport planes flying multiple sorties over a number of days. The Pakistani Government, Armed Forces, as well as the US government along with coalition forces, officially deny that such airlift had even occurred.

Former President of Afghanistan, [Hamid Karzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Karzai) accused Pakistan of supporting ISIS during interview with [ANI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_News_International) that Afghanistan has evidence of Pakistan's support to [ISIS](9087364.md). He added that there is no doubt to the above statement.[^cite_note-145] On 10 January 2025, Abbas Stanekzai, the Taliban's Deputy Foreign Minister, via a post on [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_\(Twitter\)) (formerly [Twitter](9988187.md)), accused the Pakistani Army of training and supplying weapons to ISKP.[^cite_note-146] This accusation came despite the fact that Pakistan was officially against the Islamic State, having supported Iraqi forces against it in Iraq and also conducting multiple military operations against it and having arrested many of its members, while also having been targeted by the group in its attacks, including suicide bombings.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]

On May 1, 2011 [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) was killed in Pakistan, he was living in a [safe house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden's_compound_in_Abbottabad) in [Abbottabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbottabad), less than a mile away from, a [Pakistan Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Military_Academy). This had given rise to numerous allegations of an extensive support system for Osama Bin Laden was in place by the Government and Military of Pakistan.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]

During the raid on the compound, [US Navy SEALs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEALs) were able to recover some 470,000 computer files from a trove of ten hard drives, five computers and around one hundred thumb drives and disks.[^cite_note-last_days-152] The documents recovered from the compound contained nothing to support the idea that bin Laden was protected by Pakistani officials or that he was in communication with them. Instead, the documents contained criticism of Pakistani military and future plans for attacks against Pakistani military targets.[^cite_note-last_days-152] [Steve Coll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Coll) confirmed that as of 2019 no direct evidence showing Pakistani knowledge of bin Laden's presence in Abbottabad had been found, and that captured documents from the Abbottabad compound suggest bin Laden was wary of contact with Pakistani intelligence and police, especially in light of Pakistan's role in the arrest of [Khalid Sheikh Mohammed](190884.md).[^cite_note-153]

[Pervez Musharraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervez_Musharraf), former Pakistan President, had admitted in 2016 that Pakistan supported and trained terrorist groups like [Lashkar-e-Taiba](649718.md) in the 1990s to carry out militancy in Kashmir and Pakistan was in favour of religious militancy in 1979. He said that [Zakiur Rehman Lakhvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakiur_Rehman_Lakhvi) and [Hafiz Saeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz_Muhammad_Saeed) were seen as heroes in Pakistan during the 1990s. He added that later on this religious militancy turned into terrorism and they started killing their own people. He also stated that Pakistan trained the [Taliban](30635.md) to fight against Russia, saying that the Taliban, [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden), [Jalaluddin Haqqani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalaluddin_Haqqani) and [Ayman Al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahiri) were heroes for Pakistan, however they later became villains.[^cite_note-154]

In an April 2025 interview, when Pakistan's defence minister [Khawaja Asif](16345137.md) was asked about Pakistan’s response and stance on terrorism in the aftermath of the [Pahalgam Attack](79778862.md) that killed twenty six people, he framed it as "dirty work" that Pakistan had been doing for the "West" for about "three decades."[^cite_note-\:4-155] When further pressed on the attack's links to [The Resistance Front](74821509.md), an offshoot of [Lashkar-e-Taiba](649718.md), he claimed that the group was extinct and did not exist anymore.[^cite_note-\:4-155] However, later it was revealed that one of the terrorist camps that India targeted during its [Operation Sindoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sindoor) on 07 May was related to Lashkar-e-Taiba. Markaz-e-Taiba was a camp located in [Muridke](19493547.md), and according to Indian sources, an attacker behind a terrorist attack on [Indian Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Science) was trained here.[^cite_note-156] Videos of supporters of Lashkar-e-Taiba and the Al-Qaeda-linked 313 Brigade promoting jihad, found on TikTok, YouTube, and Google, originated from this camp located in Pakistan. Forensically verified, the footage showed armed men and children in martial arts training, with captions and hashtags like "#313" and "mujahid" indicating ties to banned groups.[^cite_note-157]

### Qatar

In 2011 the *[Washington Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Times)* reported that [Qatar](8238258.md) was providing weapons and funding to [Abdelhakim Belhadj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelhakim_Belhadj), leader of the formerly U.S. designated terrorist group, [Libyan Islamic Fighting Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Islamic_Fighting_Group) (LIFG) and then leader of the [conservative](6675.md) [Islamist](15012.md) Al-[Watan Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Watan_Party_\(Libya\)).[^cite_note-158]

In December 2012 the *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* published an editorial accusing the Qatari regime of funding the Al-[Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front), a U.S. government [designated terrorist organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_designated_terrorist_organizations).[^cite_note-159] The *[Financial Times](136566.md)* noted [Emir Hamad's](295929.md) visit to Gaza and meeting with [Hamas](13913.md), another internationally designated terrorist organization.[^cite_note-160] Spanish football club [FC Barcelona](68187.md) were coming under increasing pressure to tear up their £125m shirt sponsorship contract with the [Qatar Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Foundation) after claims the so-called charitable trust finances Hamas. The fresh controversy follows claims made by the Spanish newspaper *[El Mundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Mundo_\(Spain\))* that the Qatar Foundation had given money to cleric [Yusuf al Qaradawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_al_Qaradawi) who is alleged to be an advocate of terrorism, [wife beating](18978563.md) and [antisemitism](1078.md).

In January 2013 French politicians again accused the Qatari Government of giving material support to [Islamist](15012.md) groups in [Mali](19127.md) and the French newspaper *[Le Canard enchaîné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Canard_encha%C3%AEn%C3%A9)* quoted an unnamed source in French military intelligence saying that "The [MNLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad) \[secular Tuareg separatists], al Qaeda-linked [Ansar Dine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_Dine) and [Movement for Unity and Jihad in West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_Oneness_and_Jihad_in_West_Africa) have all received cash from Doha."[^cite_note-161]

In March 2014, the then Iraqi Prime Minister [Nouri Al-Maliki](4723094.md) has accused the Qatari government of sponsoring Sunni insurgents fighting against Iraqi soldiers in western Anbar province.[^cite_note-162]

In October 2014, it was revealed that a former Qatari Interior Ministry official, Salim Hasan Khalifa Rashid Al-Kuwari, had been named by the [U.S. Department of the Treasury](53667.md) as an al Qaeda financier, with allegations that he gave hundreds of thousands of dollars to the terrorist group. Kuwari worked for the civil defense department of the Interior Ministry in 2009, two years before he was designated for his support of al Qaeda.[^cite_note-163]

A number of wealthy Qataris are accused of sponsoring the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md).[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] In response to public criticism over Qatari connections to ISIL, the government has pushed back and denied supporting the group.[^cite_note-166]

### Russia and the Soviet Union&#x20;

Further information: [Terrorism and the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_and_the_Soviet_Union)

See also: [Terrorism in Russia](8743696.md)

The Soviet (and later Russian) secret services worked to establish a network of terrorist [front organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_organization) and had been described as the primary promoters of terrorism worldwide.[^cite_note-Lunev0-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-Spetsnaz-169] According to defector [Ion Mihai Pacepa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Mihai_Pacepa), General [Aleksandr Sakharovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Sakharovsky) from the [First Chief Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Chief_Directorate) of the [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) once said: "In today’s world, when [nuclear arms](21785.md) have made military force obsolete, terrorism should become our main weapon."[^cite_note-Pacep2-170] Pacepa further claims Sakharovsky stated that "Airplane hijacking is my own invention" and that [George Habash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Habash), who worked under the KGB's guidance,[^cite_note-Mitrokhin-171] explained: "Killing one Jew far away from the field of battle is more effective than killing a hundred Jews on the field of battle, because it attracts more attention."[^cite_note-Pacep2-170]

Pacepa described an alleged operation "SIG" ("[Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist) Governments") that was devised in 1972, to turn the whole Islamic world against [Israel](9282173.md) and the [United States](3434750.md). KGB chairman [Yury Andropov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yury_Andropov) allegedly explained to Pacepa that "a billion adversaries could inflict far greater damage on America than could a few millions. We needed to instill a [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi)-style hatred for the [Jews](25955086.md) throughout the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world), and to turn this weapon of the emotions into a terrorist bloodbath against Israel and its main supporter, the United States."[^cite_note-Pacep2-170]

The following organizations have been allegedly established with assistance from [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) security services:[^cite_note-World-172]

- the PLO,

- the [National Liberation Army of Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberation_Army_\(Bolivia\)) (created in 1964 with help from [Ernesto Che Guevara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Che_Guevara)),

- the [National Liberation Army of Colombia](822032.md) (created in 1965 with help from [Cuba](5042481.md)),

Advance notice of this operation "was almost certainly" given to the KGB.[^cite_note-Third_World_pages_250-253-173]

### Saudi Arabia

See also: [Alleged Saudi government role in the September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alleged_Saudi_government_role_in_the_September_11_attacks)

While Saudi Arabia is often a secondary source of funds and support for terror movements who can find more motivated and ideologically invested benefactors, Saudi Arabia arguably remains the most prolific sponsor of international [Islamist terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamist_terrorism), allegedly supporting groups as disparate as the Afghanistan [Taliban](30635.md), [Al Qaeda](1921.md), [Lashkar-e-Taiba](649718.md) (LeT) and the Al-[Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front).[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-Guardian-WikiLeaks-200]

Saudi Arabia is said to be the world's largest source of funds and promoter of [Salafist jihadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafist_jihadism),[^cite_note-201] which forms the ideological basis of terrorist groups such as Al-[Qaeda](1921.md), [Taliban](30635.md), [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) and others. In a December 2009 diplomatic cable to U.S. State Department staff (made public in the [diplomatic cable leaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak) the following year), [U.S. Secretary of State](32293.md) [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) urged U.S. diplomats to increase efforts to block money from [Gulf Arab states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Arab_states) from going to terrorists in Pakistan and Afghanistan, writing that "Donors in Saudi Arabia constitute the most significant source of funding to Sunni terrorist groups worldwide" and that "More needs to be done since Saudi Arabia remains a critical financial support base for Al-[Qaeda](1921.md), the [Taliban](30635.md), [LeT](649718.md) and other terrorist groups."[^cite_note-Guardian-WikiLeaks-200] An August 2009 State Department cable also said that the Pakistan-based [Lashkar-e-Taiba](649718.md), which carried out the [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks), used a Saudi-based front company to fund its activities in 2005.[^cite_note-Guardian-WikiLeaks-200][^cite_note-202]

The violence in [Afghanistan](737.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md) is partly bankrolled by wealthy, conservative donors across the Arabian Sea whose governments do little to stop them.[^cite_note-Guardian-WikiLeaks-200] Three other Arab countries which are listed as sources of militant money are [Qatar](8238258.md), [Kuwait](7515890.md), and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), all neighbors of Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-Guardian-WikiLeaks-200][^cite_note-US_embassy-203]

According to two studies published in 2007 (one by [Mohammed Hafez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Hafez_\(academic\)) of the [University of Missouri in Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri_in_Kansas_City) and the other by [Robert Pape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pape) of the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago)), most of the suicide bombers in [Iraq](7515928.md) are Saudis.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206]

Fifteen of the 19 hijackers of the four airliners who were responsible for [9/11](5058690.md) originated from Saudi Arabia, two from the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), one from Egypt, and one from Lebanon.[^cite_note-207] [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) was born and educated in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).

Starting in the mid-1970s the [Islamic resurgence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_resurgence) was funded by an abundance of money from Saudi Arabian oil exports.[^cite_note-208] The tens of billions of dollars in "[petro-Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro-Islam)" largess obtained from the recently heightened price of oil funded an estimated "90% of the expenses of the entire faith."[^cite_note-209]

Throughout the Sunni Muslim world, religious institutions for people both young and old, from children's [madrassas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasah) to high-level scholarships received Saudi funding,[^cite_note-210] "books, scholarships, fellowships, and mosques" (for example, "more than 1500 [mosques](19894.md) were built and paid for with money obtained from public Saudi funds over the last 50 years"),[^cite_note-211] along with training in the Kingdom for the preachers and teachers who went on to teach and work at these universities, schools, mosques, etc.[^cite_note-212] The funding was also used to reward journalists and academics who followed the Saudis' strict interpretation of Islam; and satellite campuses were built around Egypt for [Al Azhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Azhar_University), the world's oldest and most influential Islamic university.[^cite_note-213]

The interpretation of Islam promoted by this funding was the strict, conservative Saudi-based [Wahhabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism) or [Salafism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafism). In its harshest form it preached that Muslims should not only "always oppose" infidels "in every way", but "hate them for their religion ... for Allah's sake", that [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) "is responsible for all the horrible wars of the 20th century", that [Shia](26961.md) and other non-Wahhabi Muslims were "[infidels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy_in_Islam)", etc.[^cite_note-214] According to former [Prime Minister of Singapore](350647.md) [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew), while this effort has by no means converted all, or even most, Muslims to the Wahhabist interpretation of Islam, it has done much to overwhelm more moderate local interpretations of Islam in [Southeast Asia](28741.md), and to pitch the Saudi-interpretation of Islam as the "gold standard" of religion in minds of Muslims across the globe.[^cite_note-215]

[Patrick Cockburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Cockburn) accused Saudi Arabia of supporting extremist [Islamist](15012.md) groups in the [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md), writing: "In Syria, in early 2015, it supported the creation of the [Army of Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Conquest), primarily made up of the Al-[Qaeda](1921.md) affiliate the Al-[Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front) and the ideologically similar [Ahrar Al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham), which won a series of victories against the [Syrian Army](3423927.md) in [Idlib](2528679.md) province."[^cite_note-216]

While the Saudi government denies claims that it exports religious or cultural extremism, it is argued that by its nature, Wahhabism encourages intolerance and promotes terrorism.[^cite_note-217] Former [CIA](5183633.md) director [James Woolsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Woolsey) described it as "the soil in which Al-[Qaeda](1921.md) and its sister terrorist organizations are flourishing."[^cite_note-iags.org-218] In 2015, [Sigmar Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmar_Gabriel), [Vice-Chancellor of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Chancellor_of_Germany), accused Saudi Arabia of supporting intolerance and extremism, saying: "Wahhabi mosques are financed all over the world by Saudi Arabia. In [Germany](11867.md), many dangerous Islamists come from these communities."[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220] In May 2016, The New York Times editorialised that the kingdom allied to the U.S. had "spent untold millions promoting Wahhabism, the radical form of Sunni Islam that inspired the [9/11](5058690.md) hijackers and that now inflames the Islamic State".[^cite_note-inflames-221] Iranian [Hamidreza Taraghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamidreza_Taraghi), a hard-line analyst with ties to [Iran’s supreme leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%99s_supreme_leader), [Ayatollah Ali Khamenei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayatollah_Ali_Khamenei), said, “ISIS ideologically, financially and logistically is fully supported and sponsored by Saudi Arabia...They are one and the same”.[^cite_note-222]

In 2014, former [Prime Minister of Iraq](208879.md) [Nouri Al-Maliki](4723094.md) stated that Saudi Arabia and Qatar started the civil wars [in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%932017\)) and [Syria](30741795.md), and incited and encouraged terrorist movements, like [ISIL](9087364.md) and Al-[Qaeda](1921.md), supporting them politically and in the media, with money and by buying weapons for them. Saudi Arabia denied the accusations which were criticised by the country, the [Carnegie Middle East Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Middle_East_Center) and the [Royal United Services Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_United_Services_Institute).[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224]

One of the leaked [Podesta emails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podesta_emails) from August 2014, addressed to [John Podesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Podesta), identifies [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and [Qatar](8238258.md) as providing clandestine financial and logistic aid to [ISIL](9087364.md) and other "radical Sunni groups." The email outlines a plan of action against [ISIL](9087364.md), and urges putting pressure on [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and [Qatar](8238258.md) to end their alleged support for the group.[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226] Whether the email was originally written by [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton), her advisor [Sidney Blumenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Blumenthal), or another person is unclear.[^cite_note-227]

Following the [2017 Tehran attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Tehran_attacks), Iranian authorities such as members of the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) and the [Iranian Minister of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ministers_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_Iran) [Javad Zarif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javad_Zarif), have accused Saudi Arabia of being behind the attacks.[^cite_note-\:0-228][^cite_note-\:1-229] In a Twitter post, Zarif wrote, "Terror-sponsoring despots threaten to bring the fight to our homeland. Proxies attack what their masters despise most: the seat of democracy". His statements referred to the Saudi deputy crown prince [Mohammad bin Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_bin_Salman_Al_Saud)'s threats against the country about a month earlier, in which bin Salman revealed their policy to drag the regional conflict into Iranian borders.[^cite_note-\:0-228][^cite_note-\:1-229][^cite_note-230] [Adel Al-Jubeir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adel_al-Jubeir), Saudi Arabia's foreign minister, denied his country's involvement in the attacks and said Riyadh had no knowledge of who was responsible for them.[^cite_note-SA-Rea-231] He condemned terrorist attacks and killing of the innocent "anywhere it occurs".[^cite_note-SA-Rea-231]

In 2017 [Bob Corker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Corker), then-chairman of the [US Senate Committee on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Foreign_Relations), stated that the Saudi support for terrorism "dwarfs what Qatar is doing"; the statement was made after Saudi Arabia cut ties with Qatar, citing alleged support of terrorism by the latter.[^cite_note-232]

According to *[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek)*, the [United Kingdom](31717.md) government may decide to keep secret the results of an official inquiry into the supporters of the Islamist militant groups in the country. The findings are believed to have references to Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-233]

Following various accusations relating to sponsoring terrorism, Saudi Arabia became eager to join the [Financial Action Task Force on Money Laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force_on_Money_Laundering) (FATF). However, a review conducted by the FATF on Saudi’s anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing system, pointed that the kingdom has not been able to tackle the risk of [terrorism financing](4308598.md) by third-party and facilitators, as well as individuals financing international terrorist organizations.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235]

In 2019, Saudi Arabia has been granted a full membership of the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) becoming the first Arab country awarded this full membership. This was following the group’s Annual General Meeting in Orlando. The group is responsible for designing and issuing standards and policies that face money laundering and terrorist financing.[^cite_note-236]

Attorneys who defended [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) in the 9/11 lawsuits, are reported to be representing crown prince [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md) in the alleged targeting and assassination of an ex-intelligence official from Saudi Arabia. The cases filed in August accused the prince of committing human rights violations, murder, and torture.[^cite_note-237]

### Sudan

[Sudan](27421.md) was considered a state sponsor of terrorism by the US government from 1993 to 2020, and was targeted by United Nations sanctions in 1996 for its role in sheltering suspects of an attempted assassination of [Hosni Mubarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosni_Mubarak), president of Egypt. Sudan has been suspected of harboring members of the militant organizations Al-Qaeda, Hezbollah, Hamas, the Palestinian Islamic Jihad, the Abu Nidal Organization, Jamaat Al-Islamiyya, and the [Egyptian Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Islamic_Jihad), as well as supporting insurgencies in Uganda, Tunisia, Kenya, Ethiopia, and Eritrea.[^cite_note-238] Voice of America News reported that Sudan is suspected by US officials of allowing the [Lord's Resistance Army](17873743.md) to operate within its borders.[^cite_note-239]

In December 1994, [Eritrea](17238590.md) broke [diplomatic relations with Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan-Eritrea_relations) after a long period of increasing tension between the two countries due to a series of cross-border incidents involving the [Eritrean Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Islamic_Jihad) (EIJ). Although the attacks did not pose a threat to the stability of the [Government of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Eritrea) (the infiltrators have generally been killed or captured by government forces), the Eritreans believe the [National Islamic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Islamic_Front) (NIF) in [Khartoum](17320.md) supported, trained, and armed the insurgents. After many months of negotiations with the Sudanese to try to end the incursions, the Government of Eritrea concluded that the NIF did not intend to change its policy and broke relations. Subsequently, the Government of Eritrea hosted a conference of Sudanese opposition leaders in June 1995 in an effort to help the opposition unite and to provide a credible alternative to the present government in Khartoum. Eritrea resumed diplomatic relations with Sudan on December 10, 2005.[^cite_note-240] Since then, Sudan has accused Eritrea, along with [Chad](5488.md), of supporting rebels.[^cite_note-241] The undemarcated border with Sudan previously posed a problem for Eritrean external relations.[^cite_note-242]

Sudan was accused of allowing members of Hamas to travel to and live in the country, as well as raise funds,[^cite_note-243] though the presence of terrorists in Sudan has largely been a secondary concern in terms of Sudanese sponsorship of terror to the facilitation of material supplies to terrorist groups[^cite_note-244] and the use of Sudan by Palestine-based terrorist organizations has declined in recent years.[^cite_note-245] The [Allied Democratic Forces](1258240.md), designated as a terrorist organization by Uganda, is said to be supported by Sudan and suspected of affiliation with widely designated terrorist group Al-[Shabaab](8726463.md).

Osama Bin Laden and Al Qaeda are said to have been formerly based in Sudan during the early 1990s. The US and Israel have conducted operations against Sudanese targets affiliated with terrorist groups as recently as 2012.[^cite_note-246]

Following the fall of Omar Al Bashir as the president of Sudan and the visit of the newly appointed Sudanese Prime Minister [Abdalla Hamdok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdalla_Hamdok) to Washington, the United States agreed to exchange ambassadors and said it would consider dropping Sudan from its list of countries of state sponsored terrorism.[^cite_note-247]

On December 14, 2020, the United States officially removed Sudan from the list after it [agreed to establish relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Sudan_normalization_agreement) with [Israel](9282173.md) and pay \$335m to US victims of terror attacks.[^cite_note-248]

### Syria

Further information: [Terrorism in Syria § Alleged Syrian state-sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Syria#Alleged_Syrian_state-sponsored_terrorism)

After his seizure of power in 1970, [Hafez Al-Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez_al-Assad) allied Ba'athist Syria closely to the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) and adopted an [anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism), [anti-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Americanism) strategy in the region by militarizing the Syrian state.[^cite_note-worldpoliticsreview\.com-249] The Ba'athist Syrian government itself was accused of engaging in [state sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_sponsored_terrorism) by U.S. President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) and by the [U.S. State Department](31975.md) since 1979.[^cite_note-250] Syria was designated as a "[State Sponsor of Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Sponsors_of_Terrorism_\(U.S._list\))" by the United States in 1979 for Hafez's [occupation policy in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_occupation_of_Lebanon) and financing of numerous militant groups like [PKK](69680.md), [Hezbollah](13919.md), and several Iranian-backed terrorist groups.[^cite_note-Ker-Lindsay-251]

After the [fall of Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), the Syrian government lost its primary military supplier and geo-political ally; and became a [pariah state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pariah_state), isolated in the international arena for its destabilizing policies and severe domestic repression.[^cite_note-worldpoliticsreview\.com-249] The 30-year rule of [Hafez Al-Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez_al-Assad) was widely viewed as a force of destabilization in the region due to the Syrian military's occupation of Lebanon and the Assad government's policy of facilitating Iran-aligned terrorist groups.[^cite_note-Ker-Lindsay-251] The European Community met on 10 November 1986 to discuss the [Hindawi affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindawi_affair), an attempt to bomb an [El Al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Al) flight out of [London](17867.md), and the subsequent arrest and trial in the UK of Nizar Hindawi, who allegedly received Syrian government support after the bombing, and possibly beforehand.[^cite_note-252] The European response was to impose sanctions against Syria and state that these measures were intended "to send Syria the clearest possible message that what has happened is absolutely unacceptable."[^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254]

After his succession in 2000, Bashar maintained core aspects of his father's foreign policy.[^cite_note-Ker-Lindsay-251] On 14 February 2005, [Rafic Hariri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafic_Hariri), the former prime minister of Lebanon, was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Rafic_Hariri) in a massive truck-bomb explosion in [Beirut](37428.md), killing 22 people and injuring 220 more. Syrian government was widely blamed for perpetrating the terrorist attack.[^cite_note-255] [Bashar Al-Assad](364813.md) is widely regarded to have ordered the launch of the terrorist operation that targeted Rafic Hariri. International investigations revealed direct participation of members in the highest echelons of the Syrian government.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257] A UN investigation commission's report, published on 20 October 2005, revealed that high-ranking members of [Syrian intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_intelligence) and the ruling [Assad family](27334130.md) had directly supervised the killing.[^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260]

After the [U.S. invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._invasion_of_Iraq) in 2003, Bashar supported the [Iraqi insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_insurgency_\(2003%E2%80%932011\)) against the United States and the [Iraqi interim government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Interim_Government).[^cite_note-261] Syrian government allowed numerous fighters to pass through Syrian borders to fight the [American occupation forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._occupation_of_Iraq).[^cite_note-262] [Izzat Ibrahim Al-Douri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izzat_Ibrahim_al-Douri), Secretary-General of the [Iraqi Ba'ath party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Ba'ath_Party), had close relations with Ba'athist Syria. After the [American invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_invasion_of_Iraq) in 2003, Al-Douri reportedly fled to [Damascus](8914.md), from where he co-ordinated with several [anti-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Americanism) militant groups during the [Iraqi insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_insurgency_\(2003%E2%80%932011\)).[^cite_note-263] Throughout the years of the anti-American insurgency in Iraq, thousands of Al-[Qaeda](1921.md) fighters entered Iraq through Syria.[^cite_note-264] According to several sources, [Assef Shawkat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assef_Shawkat), then-chief of Syrian military intelligence and Bashar Al-Assad's nephew, was reportedly a key Syrian facilitator of the logistic networks of Al-[Qaeda in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_Iraq) (AQI).[^cite_note-Joscelyn-265] Leaked cables of [U.S. State Department](31975.md) contained remarks by American general [David Petraeus](3223944.md) which stated that "Bashar Al-Asad was well aware that his brother-in-law 'Asif Shawqat, Director of [Syrian Military Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_Directorate_\(Syria\)), had detailed knowledge of the activities of AQI facilitator [Abu Ghadiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ghadiya), who was using Syrian territory to bring foreign fighters and suicide bombers into Iraq".[^cite_note-Joscelyn-265]

In 2016, the [US district court of Columbia](1786384.md) declared that the financial and logistical support of the Syrian government was crucial for establishing a well-structured pathway for the fighters of Al-Qaeda in Iraq in carrying out anti-American combat operations throughout the [Iraqi insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_insurgency_\(2003%E2%80%932011\)). The court further stated that Syria "became a crucial base for AQI", by hosting several associates of [Abu Mus'ab Al-Zarqawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Musab_al-Zarqawi) and leading commanders of the insurgency, and stated that Syria's policies "led to the deaths of hundreds of Americans in Iraq". The district court also found evidence of [Syrian military intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_Directorate_\(Syria\)) assisting Al-[Qaeda in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_Iraq) and giving "crucial material support" to AQI militants who carried out the [2005 Amman bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Amman_bombings).[^cite_note-266]

### Turkey

See also: [Turkish involvement in the Syrian Civil War § Related criticism of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_involvement_in_the_Syrian_Civil_War#Related_criticism_of_Turkey), and [Turkish support for Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_support_for_Hamas)

[Francis Ricciardone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ricciardone), [United States Ambassador to Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Ambassador_to_Turkey) from 2011 to 2014, claims that Turkey had directly supported and worked with Al-[Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front) and [Ahrar Al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham) in the Syrian conflict for a period of time.[^cite_note-267] [Syria](7515849.md), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md),[^cite_note-terror_groups_by_UAE-268] [Russia](25391.md),[^cite_note-269] [Iran](14653.md) and [Egypt](8087628.md) have designated Ahrar Al-Sham a terrorist organization[^cite_note-al-Masdar_Saudi_Arabia_blocks_peace_talks-270] but the U.S. has not.[^cite_note-U.S._Department_of_State_Ahrar_al-Sham_not_a_terrorist_organization-271] The [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) and many countries including the US class Al-Nusra as a terrorist organisation;[^cite_note-272] it was the official Syrian branch of Al-[Qaeda](1921.md) until July 2016, when it ostensibly split.[^cite_note-Rebrand-273][^cite_note-glob.post8-11-13-274]

[Al-Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Monitor) claimed in 2013 that Turkey was reconsidering its support for Nusra, and Turkey's designation of the Nusra Front as a terrorist group since June 2014 was seen as an indication of it giving up on the group.[^cite_note-\:1-229][^cite_note-275]

Turkey, Qatar and Saudi Arabia supported the [Army of Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Conquest), a coalition of [Salafist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafist) and [Islamist](15012.md) Syrian rebel groups formed in March 2015[^cite_note-276] that included the Al-Nusra Front and Ahrar Al-Sham, but that also included non-al-Qaeda-linked Islamist factions, such as the [Sham Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_Legion), that have received covert arms support from the United States.[^cite_note-277] According to *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)*, some Turkish officials admitted giving logistical and intelligence support to the command center of the coalition, but denied giving direct help to Al-Nusra, while acknowledging that the group would be beneficiaries. It also reported that some rebels and officials claim that material support in the form of money and weapons was given to the coalition by Saudis with Turkey facilitating its passage.[^cite_note-278]

The [2014 National Intelligence Organisation scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_National_Intelligence_Organisation_scandal_in_Turkey) caused a major controversy in Turkey. The critiques of the government claimed that the Turkish government has been providing arms to ISIL,[^cite_note-279][^cite_note-samanyoluhaber.com-280][^cite_note-t24.com.tr-281][^cite_note-belge-282] while the Turkish government has maintained that the trucks were bound for the [Bayırbucak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay%C4%B1rbucak) Turkmens, who are opposed to the Syrian government.[^cite_note-283] According to later academic study the arms were bound for the [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) and rebel Syrian Turkmen.[^cite_note-Yavuz_&_Balcı-284]

In 2014, [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News) reported that the Turkish government had stamped passports of foreigners seeking to cross the border and join ISIL.[^cite_note-285] However, it was also reported by Sky News that ISIL members use fake passports in order to get to Syria and Turkish officials can not easily identify the authenticity of these documents.[^cite_note-286]

[YPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Protection_Units) commander [Meysa Abdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meysa_Abdo) in an op-ed written for *[NY Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NY_Times)* on October 28 claimed there is evidence that Turkish forces have allowed the Islamic State's men and equipment to move back and forth across the border.[^cite_note-287] On November 29, Nawaf Khalil, a spokesman for Syria's Kurdish [Democratic Union Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Union_Party_\(Syria\)) (PYD), reportedly said that ISIL started to attack them from all four sides for the first time.[^cite_note-288] Turkey's hesitation to help [YPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YPG) and [PYD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Union_Party_\(Syria\)) in the fight against ISIL was reportedly caused by their affiliation with the [PKK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKK), which is designated as a terrorist organization by the UN, EU and many countries including US, but Turkey later gave support to the Kurdish [Peshmerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshmerga) from [northern Iraq](679693.md) instead of the YPG, allowing 155 peshmerga to pass through Turkey with their arms who, President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) told, would initially be about 2000 but PYD was reluctant to accept.[^cite_note-\:0-228] Ahmet Gerdi, a Peshmerga general, told the Turkish press that they appreciate Turkey's help in their fight against ISIL.

Russian Prime Minister [Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) and President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) accused Turkish officials of helping the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) in the aftermath of [shootdown of a Russian Sukhoi Su-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Russian_Sukhoi_Su-24_shootdown) on 25 November. These accusations were rejected by Turkish President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan).[^cite_note-289] In October 2015, the office of the Turkish Prime Minister had stated that while smuggling of oil between Turkey and Syria had taken place, the nation had been successful in effectively stopping it.[^cite_note-290] In December 2015, the [Russian ministry of defence](7875884.md) claimed it had evidence regarding the Turkish president and his relatives being involved in oil trade with Islamic State. It also published pictures purporting to show trucks carrying oil travelling from oil installations under ISIL control into Turkey. [Mark Toner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Toner), the deputy spokesperson for the [United States Department of State](31975.md), rejected these claims stating there was no proof to back up the claims of Turkish government being involved in oil trade with ISIL who was selling oil in Turkey through middlemen. Russia also accused Turkey of allowing weapons trade with ISIL. White House Press Secretary [Josh Earnest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Earnest) meanwhile stated they had intelligence that most of the terror group's oil was being sold to the Syrian government of [Bashar Al-Assad](364813.md).[^cite_note-291]

Several analysts meanwhile, have also claimed Russia's accusations of Turkey's cooperation as baseless, while also stating that a small amount of oil might end up in Turkey with cooperation from some middlemen and corrupt officials but much of it is actually sold in Syria.[^cite_note-292][^cite_note-293][^cite_note-294] American officials meanwhile stated that the smuggling of oil by ISIL into Turkey was low.[^cite_note-295] [Adam Szubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Szubin), the acting [Under Secretary of the Treasury for Terrorism and Financial Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_the_Treasury_for_Terrorism_and_Financial_Intelligence), also stated that most of the oil was being sold in areas under Syrian government's control, with only some going towards Turkey.[^cite_note-296] Israel's Minister of Defence [Moshe Ya'alon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Ya'alon) also accused Turkey of purchasing oil from the terror group in January 2016.[^cite_note-297] In December, [WikiLeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks) released 57,000 emails of Turkish Minister of Energy and Natural Resources [Berat Albayrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berat_Albayrak) stolen by [RedHack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RedHack), a hacktivist group. 32 of them included him directing business affairs of [Powertrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powertrans), which has been accused by Turkish media of transporting ISIL oil in past and whom Albayrak had denied having links with. *The Independent* however had stated in past that the reports of Powertrans smuggling ISIL oil had no concrete proof.[^cite_note-298]

Some Arab and Syrian media agencies claimed that the village of Az-Zanbaqi (الزنبقي) in [Jisr Al-Shughur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jisr_al-Shughur)'s countryside has become a base for a massive amount of [Uyghur](31783.md) [Turkistan Islamic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkistan_Islamic_Party) militants and their families in Syria, estimated at 3,500. They further accused the Turkish intelligence of being involved in transporting these Uyghurs via Turkey to Syria, with the aim of using them first in Syria to help [Jabhat Al-Nusra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabhat_Al-Nusra) and gain combat experience fighting against the Syrian Army before sending them back to [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) to fight against [China](5405.md) if they manage to survive.[^cite_note-299][^cite_note-300]

In 2016, [Jordan](7515964.md)'s king accused Turkey of helping Islamist militias in [Libya](17633.md) and [Somalia](27358.md).[^cite_note-301]

In 2019, the [Libyan National Army](35457665.md) accused the Turkish authorities of supporting terrorist groups in Libya for many years. They added that the Turkish support has evolved from just logistic support to a direct interference using military aircraft to transport mercenaries, as well as ships carrying weapons, armored vehicles and ammunition to support terrorism in Libya.[^cite_note-302]

### Ukraine

In 2024, [Mali](19127.md) and [Niger](21373.md) severed relations with [Ukraine](31750.md) after declaring it a sponsor of terrorism, claiming it supported terrorist organizations in Ukraine's acknowledged involvement in an [act of aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tinzaouaten_\(2024\)) against Mali.[^cite_note-303][^cite_note-304]

### United Arab Emirates

No official connection to state sponsored terrorism was found between the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) government to terrorists,[^cite_note-305][^cite_note-306] however the UAE has been listed as a place used by investors to raise funds to support militants in [Afghanistan](737.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-Guardian-WikiLeaks-200] Taliban and their militant partners the [Haqqani network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haqqani_network) has been reported to raise funds through [UAE](69328.md)-based businesses.[^cite_note-US_embassy-203]

The United States [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress) Research Division in its 2007 report reported the UAE to be a major transit point for terrorists, stating that more than half of the 9/11 hijackers directly flew out of [Dubai International Airport](154742.md) to the United States. The report also indicated that UAE based banks were utilized by the hijackers.[^cite_note-307]

The United Arab Emirates has been fighting alongside General [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md)'s army in the Libya war. As mentioned in a December, 2019 [International Peace Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Institute) report, the army led by Haftar comprises militias.[^cite_note-308] Meanwhile, according to another report, UAE has been accused by the United Nations of breaching its 1970 arms embargo imposed on Libya, in a 376-page report. Weapons obtained by the Haftar army, were [Pantsir S-1](8645085.md) surface-to-air missile system, which is "a configuration only the United Arab Emirates uses".[^cite_note-309] In the airstrikes led by the United Arab Emirates, more than 100 civilians have been reportedly killed and injured, while 100,000 have been reported to be displaced.[^cite_note-310]

On 30 April 2020, [Financial Action Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Action_Task_Force) said that the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE)'s actions to combat terrorist financing and [money laundering](19390.md) were not enough. The watchdog acclaimed that it will now put region's financial centre [Dubai](211583.md) under a year-long observation and monitor 10 of 11 missing pointers required to improve laundering along with the financing of terrorism and weapons of mass destruction.[^cite_note-311]

[OFAC](231045.md) sanctioned 16 entities and individuals, in businesses spreading across the Horn of Africa, the UAE and Cyprus. This business network was alleged of raising and laundering millions of dollars for Al-[Shabaab](8726463.md). The US Treasury Department stated that Al-Shabaab's key financial facilitator is Dubai-based Haleel Commodities L.L.C., along with its subsidiaries and branches in Somalia, Kenya, Uganda, and Cyprus. The influential businesspersons serving Al-Shabaab's financial facilitators included, UAE-based Qemat Al Najah General Trading and Mohamed Artan Robel; Kenya-based Faysal Yusuf Dini and Mohamed Jumale Ali Awale; Finland-based Somali citizen Hassan Abdirahman Mahamed; and Somalia-based Abdikarin Farah Mohamed and Farhan Hussein Hayder.[^cite_note-312]

### United Kingdom

The [United Kingdom](31717.md) supported [Ulster loyalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_loyalism) [paramilitaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_loyalism#Paramilitary_and_vigilante_groups) during [The Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles) in [Northern Ireland](21265.md).[^cite_note-Stevens-313] During the 1970s, a group of loyalists known as the "[Glenanne gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenanne_gang)" carried out numerous shootings and bombings against [Irish Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Catholic) and [Irish nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationalism) in an area of Northern Ireland known as the "murder triangle".[^cite_note-ConflictArchive-314] It also carried out some cross-border attacks in the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md). The group included members of the [Ulster Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Volunteer_Force) (UVF) paramilitary group as well as [Ulster Defence Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Defence_Regiment) (UDR) soldiers and [Royal Ulster Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Ulster_Constabulary) (RUC) police officers.[^cite_note-315][^cite_note-\:2-316] It was allegedly commanded by the [Intelligence Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Corps_\(United_Kingdom\)) and [RUC Special Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RUC_Special_Branch).[^cite_note-\:2-316][^cite_note-317] Evidence suggests that the group was responsible for the deaths of about 120 civilians.[^cite_note-318] The *Cassel Report* investigated 76 killings attributed to the group and found evidence that UDR soldiers and RUC policemen were involved in 74 of those.[^cite_note-ConflictArchive-314]<sup>: 4 </sup> One former member, RUC officer [John Weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Weir_\(loyalist\)), claimed his superiors knew of the group's activities but allowed it to continue.[^cite_note-ConflictArchive-314]<sup>: 63 </sup>[^cite_note-Village-319] Attacks attributed to the group include the [Dublin and Monaghan bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_and_Monaghan_bombings) (which killed 34 civilians), the [Miami Showband killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Showband_killings) and the [Reavey and O'Dowd killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reavey_and_O'Dowd_killings).[^cite_note-\:2-316][^cite_note-ConflictArchive-314]<sup>: 8 </sup> The UK has also been accused of providing intelligence material, training, firearms, explosives and lists of people that members of the security forces wanted to have killed to Loyalist paramilitaries.[^cite_note-320]

The [Stevens Inquiries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevens_Inquiries) concluded that the [Force Research Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Research_Unit) (FRU), a covert unit of the Intelligence Corps, helped loyalists to kill people, including civilians.[^cite_note-guardianscandal-321][^cite_note-forcesaidedloyalists-322] FRU commanders say their plan was to make loyalist groups "more professional" by helping them target IRA activists and prevent them killing civilians.[^cite_note-stevenspeople-323] The Stevens Inquiries found evidence only two lives were saved and that FRU was involved with at least 30 loyalist killings and many other attacks – many of the victims uninvolved civilians.[^cite_note-guardianscandal-321] One of the most prominent killings was that of the Republican solicitor [Pat Finucane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Finucane). A FRU double-agent also helped ship weapons to loyalists from South Africa.[^cite_note-nelsonobituary-324] Stevens would later claim that members of the security forces attempted to obstruct his team's investigation.[^cite_note-forcesaidedloyalists-322]

Starting in 1979, the UK worked alongside the US and Saudi Arabia to fund and arm the [Mujahedeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) under [Operation Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cyclone), which arguably contributed to the creation of the [Taliban](30635.md) and Al-[Qaeda](1921.md) (more information here on [United Kingdom in the Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_in_the_Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War)).[^cite_note-HOW-THE-CIA-CREATED-OSAMA-BIN-LADEN-325][^cite_note-1986-1992-CIA-AND-BRITISH-RECRUIT-AND-TRAIN-MILITANTS-WORLDWIDE-TO-HELP-FIGHT-AFGHAN-WAR-326]

The UK has also been accused by Iran of supporting Arab separatist terrorism in the southern city of [Ahvaz](599079.md) in 2006.[^cite_note-327]

### United States

Main article: [United States and state-sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_and_state-sponsored_terrorism)

#### Operation Mongoose

See also: [Operation 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_40) and [Operation Mongoose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mongoose)

&#x20;Starting in 1959, under the [Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) administration, the US government had the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) (CIA) recruit operatives in [Cuba](5042481.md) to carry out terrorism and sabotage, kill civilians, and cause economic damage.[^cite_note-Yaffe20-328][^cite_note-DomYaf17-329][^cite_note-Franklin16-330] Following the failed invasion at the [Bay of Pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Pigs_Invasion), the US massively [escalated its sponsorship of terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mongoose) against [Cuba](5042481.md). In late 1961, using the [military](32212.md) and the Central Intelligence Agency, the US government engaged in an extensive campaign of state-sponsored terrorism against civilian and military targets in Cuba. The terrorist attacks killed significant numbers of civilians. The US armed, trained, funded and directed the terrorists, most of whom were Cuban expatriates. Terrorist attacks were planned at the direction and with the participation of US government employees and launched from US territory.[^cite_note-uscuba-7] The terrorist attacks directed by the CIA continued through at least 1965,[^cite_note-Brenner90-336] and the CIA was ordered to intensify the campaign in 1969.[^cite_note-Garthoff11-337] [Andrew Bacevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Bacevich), Professor of International Relations and History at Boston University, wrote of the campaign:[^cite_note-Bacevich10-338]

> In its determination to destroy the Cuban Revolution, the Kennedy administration heedlessly embarked upon what was, in effect, a program of state-sponsored terrorism... the actions of the United States toward Cuba during the early 1960s bear comparison with Iranian and Syrian support for proxies engaging in terrorist activities against Israel

The United States had trained militant Cuban exiles [Luis Posada Carriles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Posada_Carriles) and [Orlando Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Bosch) as part of this state-sponsored terrorism campaign. They are widely understood to be responsible for the [Cubana 455 bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubana_de_Aviaci%C3%B3n_Flight_455), the deadliest terrorist attack in the western hemisphere prior to the [attacks of September 2001](5058690.md) in New York and Washington. The [US Justice Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Justice_Department) recorded Bosch as having participated in at least thirty terrorist attacks, and sought to deport him when he entered the US illegally. Bosch was released by the US Government without charges at the instruction of [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush), and Bosch was granted residency in the country.[^cite_note-339][^cite_note-BBC05-340][^cite_note-NYTPosada05-341][^cite_note-NSArchive06-342]

#### Operation Cyclone

Starting in 1979, the US worked alongside the UK and Saudi Arabia to fund and arm the [Mujahedeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) under [Operation Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cyclone) as part of the [Reagan Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_Doctrine), which arguably contributed to the creation of the [Taliban](30635.md) and Al-[Qaeda](1921.md).[^cite_note-HOW-THE-CIA-CREATED-OSAMA-BIN-LADEN-325][^cite_note-1986-1992-CIA-AND-BRITISH-RECRUIT-AND-TRAIN-MILITANTS-WORLDWIDE-TO-HELP-FIGHT-AFGHAN-WAR-326] However, scholars such as [Jason Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Burke), [Steve Coll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Coll), [Peter Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bergen), [Christopher Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Andrew_\(historian\)), and [Vasily Mitrokhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Mitrokhin) have argued that [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) was "outside of [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) eyesight" and that support from reliable sources are lacking for "the claim that the CIA funded bin Laden or any of the other Arab volunteers who came to support the mujahideen."[^cite_note-343][^cite_note-344][^cite_note-345][^cite_note-346][^cite_note-347][^cite_note-348]

#### Other US operations

The US has been accused of arming and training a political and fighting force of some [Kurds](17068.md) in Syria, the [People's Protection Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Protection_Units) (YPG), which is a sister organization of Turkey's [Kurdistan Workers Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Workers_Party) (PKK).[^cite_note-349][^cite_note-350][^cite_note-351] The PKK is listed in the [US Department of State](31975.md)'s Foreign Terrorist Organizations list,[^cite_note-2009-2017.state.gov-67] and described as "a US-designated terrorist organization" in the CIA's World Factbook,[^cite_note-352][^cite_note-353] but the YPG is not.

### Venezuela

Main article: [Venezuela and state-sponsored terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_and_state-sponsored_terrorism)

In 2019, the [National Assembly of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Venezuela\)) designated the [colectivos](42254804.md) (irregular, leftist Venezuelan community organizations that support [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md), the Bolivarian government and the [Great Patriotic Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Patriotic_Pole)) as terrorist groups due to their "violence, paramilitary actions, intimidation, murders and other crimes", declaring their acts as state-sponsored terrorism.[^cite_note-354]
