This article lists **[political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) in [Guinea](12176.md)**.

Although Guinea has held nominally democratic multi-party elections since [1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Guinean_Territorial_Assembly_election), the country has had a history of being governed under either a [dominant-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant-party_system) or [single-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-party_system).

- The [Democratic Party of Guinea – African Democratic Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_of_Guinea_%E2%80%93_African_Democratic_Rally) (PDG-RDA) was the sole legal party of Guinea from 1960 to 1984, led by president [Ahmed Sékou Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_S%C3%A9kou_Tour%C3%A9) until his death.

  [^cite_note-1]

- The [Unity and Progress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_and_Progress_Party) (PUP) was the dominant ruling party of Guinea from 1992 to 2008, led by president [Lansana Conté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansana_Cont%C3%A9) until his death.

- The [Rally of the Guinean People](2726867.md) (RPG) was the dominant ruling party of Guinea from 2010 to 2021, led by president [Alpha Condé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Cond%C3%A9) until [his overthrow in 2021](68651369.md).

Currently, Guinea has a [dominant-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant-party_system), with the [Generation for Modernity and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_for_Modernity_and_Development) (GMD) becoming the largest party in the [National Assembly](3623664.md) following the [2026 Guinean parliamentary election](82416708.md). The GMD is led by president [Mamady Doumbouya](68651894.md), a military leader who came to power following the 2021 coup d'état. In 2026, several opposition parties were forcibly dissolved by the government.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

Guinea's various [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Guinea) have played significant roles in Guinean politics. For example, president Alpha Conde's party, the RPG, derived most of its support from the [Malinke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malinke), Guinea's second-largest ethnic group.[^cite_note-4] Guinea's opposition was backed by the [Fula](102383.md) ethnic group, who account for around 40% of Guinea's population.[^cite_note-5]
