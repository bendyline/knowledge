The **Iraqi insurgency** is an ongoing [low-intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-intensity_conflict) [insurgency](589841.md) that began in 2017 after the [Islamic State](9087364.md) (IS) lost its [territorial control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_the_Islamic_State) in the [War in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Iraq_\(2013%E2%80%932017\)), during which IS and allied [White Flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Flags) fought the [Iraqi military](4627429.md) (largely backed by the [United States](3434750.md), [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [other countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_military_intervention_against_ISIL) conducting airstrikes against IS) and [allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_militias_in_Iraq) [paramilitary](146730.md) forces (largely backed by [Iran](14653.md)).

## Context

The insurgency is a direct continuation of the [War in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Iraq_\(2013%E2%80%932017\)) from 2013 to 2017, with IS continuing armed opposition against the Shia-led [Iraqi Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Government). Along with the Islamic State, other insurgents fighting the government include a group known as the [White Flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Flags) which is reportedly composed of former IS members and Kurdish rebels and is believed by the government of Iraq to be part of [Ansar al-Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Islam_in_Kurdistan) and possibly affiliated with [al-Qaeda](1921.md).[^cite_note-businessinsider.com-16] The group operates mostly in the [Kirkuk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkuk_Governorate) and has used an assortment of [guerilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerilla) tactics against government forces. In September 2017, [Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_al-Baghdadi), the leader of IS, called on IS supporters around the world to launch attacks on Western news media and continued in his message the IS must focus on combating the two-pronged attack on the Muslim [Ummah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummah); these statements marked a departure from previous rhetoric which was focused on the state building of IS and heralded a shift in IS's strategy toward a classical insurgency.[^cite_note-17]

## Course of the insurgency

Since IS's [loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Western_Iraq_campaign) of all territory in Iraq in late 2017 which was declared as Iraq's victory over IS and widely seen as an end to the war, and declared as such by Iraq's Prime Minister [Haidar al-Abadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haidar_al-Abadi),[^cite_note-18] multiple incidents of violence have occurred being carried out by the conflicting sides, in spite of Iraq's declaration of victory over IS the group is widely seen as far from gone and continues to retain a presence throughout Iraq, and still capable of carrying out attacks and skirmishes with pro-government forces.[^cite_note-19] IS has been waging a [guerrilla war](12720.md) with a strong presence in the governorates of [Kirkuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkuk_Governorate), [Diyala](377806.md), [Saladin](200537.md), and [Sulaymaniyah](752619.md), with local forces largely ill-equipped and inexperienced, IS has also taken advantage of the areas' rough terrain to carry out operations. IS has also made a notable presence in the cities of [Kirkuk](199292.md), [Hawija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawija) and [Tuz Khurmato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuz_Khurmato) and has carried out attacks at night in rural areas.

IS fighters also reportedly move through villages during the day without interference from security forces, and locals have been asked by IS to give fighters food and give information on the whereabouts of Iraqi personnel, locals have also stated that IS fighters will frequently enter into Mosques and ask for [Zakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakat) to fund the insurgency. IS's operations are reported to include assassinations, kidnappings, raids and ambushes.[^cite_note-20]

As of 2021, U.S. officials warned that IS "remains capable of waging a prolonged insurgency” but also described IS in Iraq as "diminished”. Iraqi intelligence estimated that IS has 2,000–3,000 fighters in Iraq.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-auto3-22]

Following IS's defeat in December 2017, they have been greatly weakened and violence in Iraq has been sharply reduced. 20 civilians lost their lives from violence-related incidents during October 2024, the lowest figure in 21 years.[^cite_note-23] By 2024, attacks claimed by IS have gone down by 94% compared to 2019.[^cite_note-24] In 2025, a UN report submitted to the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md), assessed that the status of ISIS in Iraq is “at its weakest”, with fewer than five claimed attacks.[^cite_note-25]

## Timeline

### 2018

Main article: [Timeline of the Iraqi insurgency (2018)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Iraqi_insurgency_\(2018\))

In 2018, 3,643 IS militants, 937 Iraqi security forces, 11 American service members, 1 British service member were killed. The death toll in this year was noted to be the lowest since 2003, when the United States invaded the country.[^cite_note-auto1-11]

Notable[^cite_note-Notability-26] events this year include:

- [2018 Baghdad bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Baghdad_bombings): On 15 January 2018, two [suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bombings) took place at al-Tayaran Square of [Baghdad](4492.md), killing 36 people and injuring more than 105 others.

- [2018 Asdira funeral bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Asdira_funeral_bombing): On 12 April 2018, a bombing killed 25 people and wounded 18 others. The attack took place at a funeral for Iraqi fighters who had been killed by ISIS.

### 2019

Main article: [Timeline of the Iraqi insurgency (2019)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Iraqi_insurgency_\(2019\))

In 2019, 1,129 IS militants and 387 Iraqi security forces were killed.[^cite_note-27]

Notable[^cite_note-Notability-26] events this year include:

- [2019 Karbala bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Karbala_bombing): On 20 September, 2019, a bomb exploded on a minibus near [Karbala](210957.md), Iraq, killing 12 civilians and injuring at least five others. The bombing was one of the worst attacks against civilians since the end of the [War in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Iraq_\(2013%E2%80%932017\)) of 2013 to 2017.

- [2019 K-1 Air Base attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_K-1_Air_Base_attack): A rocket attack was carried out on the [K-1 Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-1_Air_Base) in [Kirkuk province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkuk_Governorate) in Iraq on 27 December 2019. The air base was one of many Iraqi military bases that host [Operation Inherent Resolve](44120613.md) (OIR) personnel and, according to the coalition, was attacked by more than 30 rockets. The identity of the perpetrators is disputed.

### 2020

Main article: [Timeline of the Iraqi insurgency (2020)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Iraqi_insurgency_\(2020\))

In 2020, 777 IS militants and 412 Iraqi security forces were killed.[^cite_note-28]

Notable[^cite_note-Notability-26] events this year include:

- [Withdrawal of United States troops from Iraq (2020–2021)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_United_States_troops_from_Iraq_\(2020%E2%80%932021\)): On 20 March 2020, CJTF-OIR confirmed that certain troops would be withdrawing from Iraq due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Iraq).

### 2021

Main article: [Timeline of the Iraqi insurgency (2021)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Iraqi_insurgency_\(2021\))

In 2021, 487 IS militants and 409 Iraqi security forces were killed.[^cite_note-29]

Notable[^cite_note-Notability-26] events this year include:

- [January 2021 Baghdad bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2021_Baghdad_bombings): Two [suicide bombers](18835454.md) attacked an [open-air market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketplace) in central [Baghdad](4492.md), [Iraq](7515928.md). They killed at least 32 people and injured another 110. This was the Iraqi capital's first terrorist attack since 2019.

### 2022

Main article: [Timeline of the Iraqi insurgency (2022)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Iraqi_insurgency_\(2022\))

In 2022, 564 IS militants and 183 Iraqi security forces were killed. Furthermore, one US Marine died of non-combat related causes.[^cite_note-auto2-12]

Notable[^cite_note-Notability-26] events this year include:

- [2022 Diyala massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Diyala_massacre): On January 21, 2022, several [Islamic State](9087364.md) gunmen raided an [Iraqi Army](4627429.md) [base](843566.md) in rural al-Azim district, [Diyala Governorate](377806.md). At least 11 Iraqi soldiers were killed in their sleep.

- [2022 Kirkuk bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kirkuk_bombing): At least nine police officers were killed when a convoy hit a roadside bomb near [Kirkuk](199292.md), [Iraq](7515928.md). The bombing was followed by a gun fight. One Islamic State militant was reported killed.

### 2023

Main article: [Timeline of the Iraqi insurgency (2023)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Iraqi_insurgency_\(2023\))

In 2023, 281 IS militants, 61 Iraqi security forces and 3 French soldiers were killed.[^cite_note-auto-14]

### 2024

Main articles: [Timeline of the Iraqi insurgency (2024)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Iraqi_insurgency_\(2024\)) and [2024 in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_in_Iraq)

In 2024, 235 IS militants, 58 Iraqi security personnel and 42 civilians were killed. A further 166 IS militants were executed in prisons across Iraq.[^cite_note-30]

Notable[^cite_note-Notability-26] events this year include:

- [2024 Anbar raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Anbar_raid): On 31 August 2024, a raid occurred in [Al Anbar Governorate](467036.md) where at least fifteen [Islamic State](9087364.md) militants were killed and six U.S. soldiers were injured.

In 2025, at least 90 IS fighters were killed in Iraqi security operations against the group.[^cite_note-31]

91 ISIS Fighters were also arrested during 2025. [^cite_note-32]
