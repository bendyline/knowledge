**Ahmedabad** ([/ˈɑːmədəbæd, -bɑːd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*AH-mə-də-ba(h)d*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), Hindustani: [\[ɛʱ(ɛ).m(ə).d̪ɑː.bɑːd̪\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu)), also spelt **Amdavad** (Gujarati: [\[ˈəmdɑʋɑd\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Gujarati)) and historically known as **Karnavati** (Sanskrit: [\[kɐrɳaːʋɐt̪iː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Sanskrit)), is the most populous city in the Indian [state](375986.md) of [Gujarat](53707.md). It is the administrative headquarters of the [Ahmedabad district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_district) and the seat of the [Gujarat High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_High_Court). Ahmedabad's population of 5,570,585 (per the 2011 population census) makes it the [sixth-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_India_by_population) in India,[^cite_note-15] and the encompassing [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) population estimated at 8,854,444 (as of 2024) is the [seventh-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_million-plus_urban_agglomerations_in_India) in India.[^cite_note-16] Ahmedabad is located near the banks of the [Sabarmati River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_River),[^cite_note-citypop_India_aggs-17] 25 km (16 mi)[^cite_note-citypop_world_aggs-18] from the capital of [Gujarat](53707.md), [Gandhinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhinagar), also known as its twin city.[^cite_note-19]

Ahmedabad has emerged as an important economic, industrial and cultural hub in India. It is the second-largest producer of [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) in India, due to which it was known as the "[Manchester](20206.md) of India" along with [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur). The [Ahmedabad Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Stock_Exchange) (before it was shut down in 2018) was the country's second-oldest. [Cricket](25675557.md) is a popular sport in Ahmedabad; the [Narendra Modi Stadium](80201506.md) at [Motera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motera) can accommodate 132,000 spectators, making it the [largest stadium in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stadiums_by_capacity). The [Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel Sports Enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel_Sports_Enclave) located near the stadium will be one of the biggest in the country once complete. Ahmedabad will host the [2030 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2030_Commonwealth_Games).[^cite_note-20]

The effects of the [liberalisation of the Indian economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalisation_in_India) have energised the city's economy towards [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector) activities such as commerce, communication and construction.[^cite_note-jnnurm-21] Ahmedabad's increasing population has increased demand in the construction and housing industries, resulting in the development of skyscrapers.[^cite_note-Ahmadabad_joins_ITES_hot_spots-22]

In 2010, Ahmedabad was ranked third in *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*'s list of fastest growing cities of the decade.[^cite_note-23] In 2012, *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India)* chose Ahmedabad as India's best city to live in.[^cite_note-best_city_to_in_which_to_live_in_India-24] The gross domestic product of Ahmedabad metro was estimated at \$136.1 billion in 2023.[^cite_note-auto-14][^cite_note-25] In 2020, Ahmedabad was ranked as the third-best city in India to live in by the Ease of Living Index.[^cite_note-26] In July 2022, *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazine included Ahmedabad in its list of the world's 50 greatest places of 2022.[^cite_note-27]

Ahmedabad has been selected as one of the hundred Indian cities to be developed as a [smart city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_city) under the [Government of India](553883.md)'s flagship [Smart Cities Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Cities_Mission).[^cite_note-28] In July 2017, the [historic city of Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_City_of_Ahmadabad), or Old Ahmedabad, was declared a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-29]

## History

Main article: [History of Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ahmedabad)

### Toponymy

Based on relics found in several neighbourhoods of the old city and on writings of the Persian historian [al-Biruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Biruni), it is surmised that an early [Bhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhil) tribal group settlement was known as [Ashaval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashaval).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMichellShah198817-30][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBobbio2015164-31]

According to [Merutunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merutunga), [Karna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karna_I), the [Chaulukya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaulukya_dynasty) (Solanki) ruler of [Anhilwada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhilwada) (modern [Patan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patan,_Gujarat)), successfully launched a military campaign against [Ashaval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashaval) and founded a city nearby called Karnavati.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMichellShah198817-30] The location of Karnavati is not definitively known.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBobbio2015164-31] References from the 14th and 15th centuries mention Ashaval but do not mention Karnavati.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBobbio2015164-31]

[Ahmad Shah I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_I) of the [Gujarat Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Sultanate) transferred its capital from Anhilvada to Ashaval in 1411 CE; as was customary, the city was subsequently renamed Ahmedabad after the Sultan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMichellShah198818-32]

### Early history

The area around Ahmedabad has been inhabited since the 11th century, when it was known as *[Ashaval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashaval)*.[^cite_note-33] At that time, [Karna I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karna_I), the [Chaulukya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaulukya_dynasty) (Solanki) ruler of Anhilwara (modern [Patan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patan,_Gujarat)), waged a successful war against the [Bhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhil) king of Ashaval,[^cite_note-34] and established a city called *Karnavati* on the banks of the Sabarmati.[^cite_note-Madan-35] Solanki rule lasted until the 13th century, when Gujarat came under the control of the [Vaghela dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaghela_dynasty) of [Dholka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dholka). Gujarat subsequently came under the control of the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) in the 14th century. However, by the early 15th century, the local Muslim governor Zafar Khan Muzaffar established his independence from the Delhi Sultanate and crowned himself [Sultan of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Sultanate) as [Muzaffar Shah I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffar_Shah_I), thereby founding the [Muzaffarid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarids_\(Gujarat\)).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-\:2-37][^cite_note-sen2-38] In 1411, the area came under the control of his grandson, Sultan [Ahmed Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_I), who selected the forested area along the banks of the Sabarmati river for his new capital. He laid the foundation of a new walled city near Karnavati and named it Ahmedabad after himself.[^cite_note-baba-39][^cite_note-40] According to other versions, he named the city after four Muslim saints in the area who all had the name Ahmed.[^cite_note-AMC_History-41] Ahmed Shah I laid the foundation of the city on 26 February 1411[^cite_note-Pandya_2010-42] (at 1.20 pm, Thursday, the second day of [Dhu'l-Qa'da](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhu'l-Qa'da), [Hijri era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijri_era) 813[^cite_note-43]) at [Manek Burj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manek_Burj). Manek Burj is named after the legendary 15th-century Hindu saint, [Maneknath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maneknath), who intervened to help [Ahmad Shah I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_I) build [Bhadra Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhadra_Fort) in 1411.[^cite_note-baba-39][^cite_note-igg-44][^cite_note-mnk-45][^cite_note-tam-46] Ahmed Shah I chose it as the new capital on 4 March 1411.[^cite_note-47] Chandan and Rajesh Nath, 13th generation descendants of Saint Maneknath, perform *[puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puja_\(Hinduism\))* and hoist the flag on Manek Burj on Ahmedabad's foundation day and for the [Vijayadashami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami) festival every year.[^cite_note-baba-39][^cite_note-mnk-45][^cite_note-India_2015-48][^cite_note-dnax2011-49]



City walls of Ahmedabad, 1866

In 1487, [Mahmud Begada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Begada), the great-great-grandson of Ahmed Shah, fortified the city with an outer wall 10 km (6.2 mi) in circumference and consisting of [twelve gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gates_of_Ahmedabad), 189 bastions, and over 6,000 battlements.[^cite_note-50] In 1535 [Humayun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayun) briefly occupied Ahmedabad after capturing [Champaner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaner) when the ruler of Gujarat, [Bahadur Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahadur_Shah_of_Gujarat), fled to [Diu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diu,_India).[^cite_note-51] Ahmedabad was then reoccupied by the Muzaffarid dynasty until 1573 when Gujarat was conquered by the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) emperor [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar). During the Mughal reign, Ahmedabad became one of the Empire's thriving centres of trade, mainly in textiles, which were exported as far as Europe. The Mughal ruler [Shah Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jahan) spent the prime of his life in the city, sponsoring the construction of the [Moti Shahi Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel_National_Memorial) in [Shahibaug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahibaug). The [Deccan famine of 1630–1632](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_famine_of_1630%E2%80%931632) affected the city, as did famines in 1650 and 1686.[^cite_note-52] Ahmedabad remained the provincial headquarters of the Mughals until 1758, when they surrendered the city to the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire).[^cite_note-53]

### Modern history



A market scene in Ahmedabad, 1901

During the period of Maratha governance, the city became the centre of a conflict between the [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) of [Poona](164634.md) and the [Gaekwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaekwad_dynasty) of [Baroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara).[^cite_note-GNHistory-54] In 1780, during the [First Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Maratha_War), a British force under [James Hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hartley_\(East_India_Company_officer\)) stormed and captured Ahmedabad, but it was handed back to the Marathas at the end of the war. The [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) took over the city in 1818 during the [Third Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Maratha_War).[^cite_note-AMC_History-41] A military cantonment was established in 1824, and a municipal government in 1858.[^cite_note-AMC_History-41] Incorporated into the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency) during [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), Ahmedabad became one of the most important cities in the Gujarat region. In 1864, a railway link between Ahmedabad and [Mumbai](19189.md) (then Bombay) was established by the [Bombay, Baroda and Central India Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay,_Baroda_and_Central_India_Railway) (BB\&CI), enabling traffic and trade between [northern](1552939.md) and [southern India](678583.md) via the city.[^cite_note-AMC_History-41] Over time, the city established itself as the home of a developing textile industry, which earned it the nickname "Manchester of India".[^cite_note-BordenFraser2014-55]



Ahmedabad and its environs, ca 1914

The [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) developed roots in the city when [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) established two [ashrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashram) – the [Kochrab Ashram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochrab_Ashram) near [Paldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paldi_\(Ahmedabad\)) in 1915 and the Satyagraha Ashram (now [Sabarmati Ashram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Ashram)) on the banks of the Sabarmati in 1917 – which would become centres of nationalist activities.[^cite_note-AMC_History-41][^cite_note-manchester-56] During the [mass protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-cooperation_movement_\(1919%E2%80%931922\)) against the [Rowlatt Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchical_and_Revolutionary_Crimes_Act,_1919) in 1919, textile workers burned down 51 government buildings across the city in protest against a British attempt to extend wartime regulations after the [World War I](4764461.md). In the 1920s, textile workers and teachers went on strike, demanding civil rights and better pay and working conditions. In 1930, Gandhi initiated the [Salt March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_March) from Ahmedabad by embarking from his ashram on the Dandi March. The city's administration and economic institutions were rendered inoperative in the early 1930s by the large numbers of people who took to the streets in peaceful protests, and again in 1942 during the [Quit India Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quit_India_Movement).

#### Post-independence

Following independence and the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947, the city was scarred by the intense communal violence that broke out between [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) and [Muslims](19541.md) in 1947. Ahmedabad was the focus of settlement by Hindu migrants from Pakistan,[^cite_note-GillyGilinskiy2009-57] who expanded the city's population and transformed its demographics and economy.

&#x20;By 1960, Ahmedabad had become a metropolis with a population of over one million people, with classical and colonial European-style buildings lining the city's thoroughfares.[^cite_note-Ghurye1962-58] It was chosen as the capital of Gujarat after the partition of the [Bombay State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_State) on 1 May 1960.[^cite_note-YājñikaSheth2005-59] During this period, a large number of educational and research institutions were founded in the city, making it a centre for [higher education](293121.md), science, and technology.[^cite_note-Political_Science-60] Ahmedabad's economic base became more diverse with the establishment of heavy and chemical industry during the same period.&#x20;



[Sabarmati Ashram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Ashram), established by [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi)

In the late 1970s, the capital shifted to the newly built city of [Gandhinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhinagar). This marked the start of a long period of decline in Ahmedabad, marked by a lack of development. The 1974 *[Navnirman Andolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navnirman_Andolan)* agitation, a protest against a 20% hike in the hostel food fees at the [Lalbhai Dalpatbhai College of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalbhai_Dalpatbhai_College_of_Engineering) in Ahmedabad, snowballed into a movement to remove [Chimanbhai Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimanbhai_Patel), then [Chief Minister of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Gujarat).[^cite_note-61] In the 1980s, a [reservation policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservation_in_India) was introduced in the country, which led to anti-reservation protests in 1981 and 1985. The protests witnessed violent clashes between people belonging to various [castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste_system_in_India).[^cite_note-62] The city was considerably impacted by the [2001 Gujarat earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Gujarat_earthquake); up to 50 multi-storey buildings collapsed, killing 752 people and causing significant damage.[^cite_note-63] The following year, three days of violence between Hindus and Muslims in the western Indian state of Gujarat, known as the [2002 Gujarat violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Gujarat_violence), spread to Ahmedabad; in eastern [Chamanpura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamanpura), 69 people were killed in the [Gulbarg Society massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulbarg_Society_massacre) on 28 February 2002.[^cite_note-Gulbarg-64] Refugee camps were set up around the city, housing 50,000 Muslims, as well as some small Hindu camps.[^cite_note-65]

The [2008 Ahmedabad bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Ahmedabad_bombings), a series of seventeen bomb blasts, killed and injured many people.[^cite_note-66] The terrorist group [Harkat-ul-Jihad al-Islami](5592225.md) claimed responsibility for the attacks.[^cite_note-67] In 2020, the city hosted the *[Namaste Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namaste_Trump)* event at the newly built [Narendra Modi Stadium](80201506.md), the [world's largest cricket stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cricket_grounds_by_capacity) as well as the [largest overall stadium in the world by capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stadiums_by_capacity).

City population increased by 23.43% from 4,519,000 as of the [2001 census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_census_of_India) to 5,577,940 (2,938,985 males and 2,638,955 females resulting in a sex ratio of 898 females per 1,000 males) as of the [2011 census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India) making Ahmedabad the [fifth most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_India_by_population) in India.[^cite_note-\:3-68][^cite_note-\:4-69][^cite_note-70] The [Urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) centred upon Ahmedabad had a population of 6,352,254 and was the [seventh most populous urban agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_million-plus_urban_agglomerations_in_India) in India as of the [2011 census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India).[^cite_note-\:4-69][^cite_note-71] The population of children aged 0 to 6 was 621,034 (336,063 males and 284,971 females resulting in a child sex ratio of 848 females per 1,000 males) as of the [2011 census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India).[^cite_note-\:3-68] The city had an average [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 88.29%, a male literacy rate of 92.30%, and a female literacy rate of 83.85%.[^cite_note-\:3-68]

### Poverty

In the mid-1970s and early 1980s, the textile mills that were responsible for much of Ahmedabad's wealth faced competition from automation and domestic speciality looms. Several mills closed down, leaving between 40,000 and 50,000 people without a source of income, and many moved into informal settlements in the city centre. The [Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Municipal_Corporation) (AMC), the governing and administrative body of the city, simultaneously lost much of its tax base and saw an increased demand for services. In the 1990s, newly emerging pharmaceutical, chemical, and automobile manufacturing industries required skilled labour, so many migrants seeking work ended up in the informal sector and settled in slums.[^cite_note-\:0-73]

Ahmedabad has made efforts to reduce poverty and improve the living conditions of poor residents. The urban poverty rate has declined from 28% in 1993–1994 to 10% in 2011–2012.[^cite_note-\:0-73] This is partly due to the strengthening of the AMC and its partnership with several civil society organisations (CSOs) representing poor residents. Through projects and programs, the AMC has provided utilities and basic services to slums. However, some challenges remain, and there are still many residents who lack access to sanitation, clean running water, and electricity. Riots, often rooted in religious tensions, threaten the stability of neighbourhoods and have caused spatial segregation across religious and caste lines. There has yet to be a concerted effort to balance pro-poor, inclusive development with national initiatives that aim to create 'global cities' that are the focus of capital investment and technological innovation.

#### Informal housing and slums

As of 2011, about 66% of the population lives in formal housing, with the other 34% living in slums or *chawls*, which are tenements for industrial workers. There are approximately 700 slum settlements in Ahmedabad, and 11% of the total housing stock is public housing. The population of Ahmedabad has increased while the housing stock has remained generally constant, and this has led to a rise in the density of both formal and informal housing and a more economical usage of existing space. The Indian census estimates that the Ahmedabad slum population was 25.6% of the total population in 1991 and had decreased to 4.5% in 2011, but these numbers are contested, and local entities maintain that the census underestimates informal populations. There is a consensus that there has been a reduction in the percentage of the population that lives in slum settlements, and that there has also been a general improvement in living conditions for slum residents.[^cite_note-\:0-73]

#### Slum Networking Project



Photograph of slum neighborhood in Ahmedabad, 1979.

In the 1990s, the AMC faced increased slum populations. They found that residents were willing and able to pay for legal connections to water, sewage, and electricity, but because of tenure issues, they were paying higher prices for low-quality, informal connections. To address this, beginning in 1995, the AMC partnered with civil society organisations to create the Slum Networking Project (SNP) to improve basic services in 60 slums, benefitting approximately 13,000 households.[^cite_note-\:0-73] This project, also known as *Parivartan* (lit. 'Change'; i.e., the said word is a [tatsama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatsama) in [Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_literature), [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) and [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) alike), involved [participatory planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participatory_planning) in which slum residents were partners alongside [AMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Municipal_Corporation), private institutions, microfinance lenders, and local NGOs. The goal of the program was to provide both physical infrastructure (including water supply, sewers, individual toilets, paved roads, storm drainage, and tree planting) and community development (i.e. the formation of resident associations, women's groups, community health interventions, and vocational training).[^cite_note-\:1-74] In addition, participating households were granted a minimum de facto tenure of ten years. The project cost a total of ₹4,350 million. Community members and the private sector each contributed ₹600 million, NGOs provided ₹90 million, and the AMC paid for the rest of the project.[^cite_note-\:1-74] Each slum household was responsible for no more than 12% of the cost of upgrading their home.[^cite_note-\:0-73]

This project has generally been regarded as a success. Having access to basic services increased the residents' working hours, since most work out of their homes. It also reduced the incidence of illness, particularly water-borne illness, and increased children's rates of school attendance.[^cite_note-75] The SNP received the 2006 [UN-Habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN-Habitat_Scroll_of_Honour_Award) Dubai International Award for Best Practice to Improve the Living Environment.[^cite_note-76] However, concerns remain about the community's responsibility and capacity for the maintenance of the new infrastructure. Additionally, trust was weakened when the AMC demolished two of the slums that were upgraded as part of SNP to create recreational parks.[^cite_note-\:0-73]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Ahmedabad)



19th-century painted cloth map of Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad lies in [Western India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_India) at 53 metres (174 feet) above sea level on the banks of the [Sabarmati River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_River), in north-central Gujarat. It covers an area of 505 km<sup>2</sup> (195 sq mi).[^cite_note-Municipal_Extensions-88][^cite_note-Municipalities_have_extension_in_Gujarat-89][^cite_note-AMC_Expands-90][^cite_note-AMC-91] The Sabarmati frequently dried up in the summer, leaving only a small stream of water, and the city is in a sandy and dry area. However, with the execution of the [Sabarmati River Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Riverfront) Project and Embankment, the waters from the [Narmada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) river have been diverted to the Sabarmati to keep the river flowing throughout the year, thereby eliminating Ahmedabad's water problems. The steady expansion of the [Rann of Kutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rann_of_Kutch) threatened to increase desertification around the city area and much of the state; however, the Narmada Canal network is expected to alleviate this problem. Except for the small hills of [Thaltej-Jodhpur Tekra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaltej-Jodhpur_Tekra), the city is almost flat. Three lakes lie within the city's limits—[Kankaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankaria_Lake), [Vastrapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vastrapur_Lake) and Chandola. Kankaria, in the neighbourhood of [Maninagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maninagar), is an artificial lake developed by the Sultan of Gujarat, Qutb-ud-din, in 1451.[^cite_note-kankaria-92]

According to the [Bureau of Indian Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Indian_Standards), the town falls under [seismic zone 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_hazard_zoning_of_India), in a scale of 2 to 5 (in order of increasing vulnerability to earthquakes).[^cite_note-hazardprofile-93]

Ahmedabad is divided by the Sabarmati into two physically distinct eastern and western regions. The eastern bank of the river houses the old city, which includes the central town of [Bhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhadra_Fort). This part of Ahmedabad is characterised by packed [bazaars](556904.md), the *[pol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol_\(housing\))* system of closely clustered buildings, and numerous places of worship.[^cite_note-94] A pol (pronounced as pole) is a housing cluster which comprises many families of a particular group, linked by [caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste), [profession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profession), or religion.[^cite_note-Others-95][^cite_note-96] [This is a list of pols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_pols_in_Ahmedabad) in the [old walled city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Ahmedabad)[^cite_note-Others-95] of Ahmedabad in [Gujarat](53707.md), India. Heritage of these pols[^cite_note-97] has helped Ahmedabad gain a place in [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s Tentative Lists, in [selection criteria](44940.md#Cultural_criteria) II, III and IV.[^cite_note-98] The secretary-general of EuroIndia Centre quoted that if 12,000 [homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havelis) of Ahmedabad are restored they could be very helpful in promoting heritage tourism and its allied businesses.[^cite_note-99] The *Art Reverie* in *Moto Sutharvado* is [Res Artis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Res_Artis) center. The first pol in Ahmedabad was named *Mahurat Pol*.[^cite_note-100] The old city also houses the main railway station, the main post office, and some buildings of the Muzaffarid and British eras. The colonial period saw the expansion of the city to the western side of the Sabarmati river, facilitated by the construction of [Ellis Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_Bridge) in 1875 (and later the modern [Nehru Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nehru_Bridge)). The western part of the city houses educational institutions, modern buildings, residential areas, shopping malls, multiplexes and new business districts centred around roads such as [Ashram Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashram_Road), [Chimanlal Girdharlal Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimanlal_Girdharlal_Road), and [Sarkhej–Gandhinagar Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarkhej%E2%80%93Gandhinagar_Highway).[^cite_note-101]



There are nine bridges on the river Sabarmati that connect the eastern and western regions.

The [Sabarmati Riverfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Riverfront) is a waterfront area being developed along the banks of the Sabarmati River in Ahmedabad, India. Proposed in the 1960s, its construction began in 2005, and it opened in 2012.[^cite_note-102]

### Climate

Ahmedabad has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): [BSh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate)), with marginally less rain than required for a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate). There are three main seasons: summer, monsoon, and winter. Aside from the monsoon season, the climate is extremely dry. The weather is hot from March to June; the average summer maximum is 43 °C (109 °F), and the average minimum is 24 °C (75 °F). From November to February, the average maximum temperature is 30 °C (86 °F), and the average minimum is 13 °C (55 °F). Cold winds from the north are responsible for a mild chill in January. The southwest monsoon brings a humid climate from mid-June to mid-September. The average annual rainfall is about 800 millimetres (31 in), but infrequent heavy torrential rains cause local rivers to flood, and it is not uncommon for droughts to occur when the monsoon does not extend as far west as usual. The highest temperature in the city was recorded on 20 May 2016, with it reaching 48 °C (118 °F).[^cite_note-103]

### Cityscape

Early in Ahmedabad's history, under [Ahmed Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Shah_of_Gujarat), builders fused Hindu craftsmanship with [Persian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_architecture), giving rise to the [Indo-Saracenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Saracenic) style.[^cite_note-KishoreSharma-119] Many mosques in the city were built in this fashion.[^cite_note-KishoreSharma-119] [Sidi Saiyyed Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidi_Saiyyed_Mosque) was built in the last year of the Sultanate of Gujarat. It is entirely arched and has ten stone latticework windows or [jali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jali) on the side and rear arches. Private mansions or [haveli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haveli) from this era have carvings.[^cite_note-Others-95] A [pol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol_\(housing\)) is a typical housing cluster of [Old Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Ahmedabad).

After independence, modern buildings appeared in Ahmedabad. Architects given commissions in the city included [Louis Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kahn), who designed the [IIM-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Management_Ahmedabad); [Le Corbusier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Corbusier), who designed the [Shodhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Shodhan) and [Sarabhai Villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Sarabhai), the [Sanskar Kendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskar_Kendra) and the [Mill Owners' Association Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Owners'_Association_Building), and [Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lloyd_Wright), who designed the administrative building of [Calico Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calico_Mills) and the [Calico Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calico_Dome).[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] [B. V. Doshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._V._Doshi) came to the city from Paris to supervise Le Corbusier's works and later set up the [School of Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEPT_University) (now CEPT). His local works include Sangath, [Amdavad ni Gufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdavad_ni_Gufa), [Tagore Memorial Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagore_Memorial_Hall) and the School of Architecture. [Charles Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Correa), who became a partner of Doshi's, designed the [Gandhi Ashram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Ashram) and [Achyut Kanvinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achyut_Kanvinde), and the [Ahmedabad Textile Industry's Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Textile_Industry's_Research_Association) complex.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-GansCorbusier2006-123][^cite_note-GargianiRosellini2011-124] [Christopher Charles Benninger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Charles_Benninger)'s first work, the Alliance Française, is located in the Ellis Bridge area.[^cite_note-125] [Anant Raje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anant_Raje) designed major additions to [Louis Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kahn)'s [IIM-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Management,_Ahmedabad) campus, namely the Ravi Mathai Auditorium and KLMD.[^cite_note-126]

Some of the most visited gardens in the city include [Law Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Garden), Victoria Garden, and Bal Vatika. Law Garden was named after the College of Law located nearby. Victoria Garden is located at the southern edge of the Bhadra Fort and contains a statue of [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria). Bal Vatika is a children's park situated on the grounds of [Kankaria Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankaria_Lake) and houses an amusement park. Other gardens in the city include [Parimal Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parimal_Garden), Usmanpura Garden, Prahlad Nagar Garden, and Lal Darwaja Garden.[^cite_note-Law_garden-127] Ahmedabad's Kamla Nehru Zoological Park houses a number of endangered species including flamingoes, [caracals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracal), [Asiatic wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhole), and [chinkara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinkara).[^cite_note-cza-128]

The [Kankaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankaria) Lake, built in 1451 CE, is one of the biggest lakes in Ahmedabad.[^cite_note-129] In earlier days, it was known by the name *Qutub Hoj* or *Hauj-e-Kutub*.[^cite_note-kndfg-130] Lal Bahadur Shastri lake in Bapunagar is almost 136,000 square metres. In 2010, another 34 lakes were planned in and around Ahmedabad of which five lakes will be developed by AMC; the other 29 will be developed by the [Ahmedabad Urban Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Urban_Development_Authority) (AUDA).[^cite_note-131] [Vastrapur Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vastrapur_lake) is a small artificial lake located in the western part of Ahmedabad. Beautified by local authorities in 2002, it is surrounded by greenery and paved walkways and has become a popular leisure spot for the citizens.[^cite_note-132] [Chandola Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandola_Lake) covers an area of 1200 hectares. It is home to [cormorants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cormorants), [painted storks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painted_stork), and [spoonbills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoonbill).[^cite_note-133] During the evening, many people visit this place and take a stroll.[^cite_note-134] There is a recently developed lake in [Naroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naroda),[^cite_note-135] and there is also the world's largest collection of antique cars in Kathwada at IB farm (Dastan Farm).[^cite_note-136] AMC has also developed the [Sabarmati Riverfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Riverfront).[^cite_note-Mahadevia-137]

Looking at the health of traffic police staff deployed near the Pirana dump site, the [Ahmedabad City Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_City_Police) is going to install outdoor [air purifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_purifiers) at traffic points so that the deployed staff can breathe fresh air.[^cite_note-138]

## Civic administration



[Gujarat High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_High_Court) in Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad is the administrative headquarters of [Ahmedabad district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_district) and is administered by the [Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Municipal_Corporation) (AMC). The AMC was established in July 1950 under the Bombay Provincial Corporation Act of 1949. The AMC commissioner is an [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) (IAS) officer appointed by the [state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Gujarat) who reserves the administrative executive powers, whereas the corporation is headed by the [mayor of Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Ahmedabad). The city residents elect the 192 [municipal councillors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Councillor) by popular vote, and the elected councillors select the deputy mayor and mayor of the city. The mayor, Bijal Patel, was appointed on 14 June 2018.[^cite_note-AhmMir-15Jun-139] The administrative responsibilities of the AMC are water and sewerage services, primary education, health services, fire services, public transport and the city's infrastructure.[^cite_note-AMC-91] AMC was ranked 9th out of 21 cities for "the best governance & administrative practices in India in 2014. It scored 3.4 out of 10 compared to the national average of 3.3."[^cite_note-140] Ahmedabad registers two accidents per hour.[^cite_note-141]

The city is divided into seven zones, constituting 48 wards.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] The city's urban and suburban areas are administered by the [Ahmedabad Urban Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Urban_Development_Authority) (AUDA).

- The city is represented by two elected members of parliament in the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) (the lower house of the Indian Parliament) and 21 [members of the Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly) at the Gujarat [Vidhan Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidhan_Sabha) (state legislative assembly).

- The [Gujarat High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_High_Court) is located in Ahmedabad, making the city the judicial capital of Gujarat.

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Ahmedabad)



[Navaratri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navaratri) celebrations in Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad is known for its rich architecture, traditional housing designs, community-oriented settlement patterns, urban structure, as well as its unique crafts and mercantile culture.[^cite_note-149] The people of Ahmedabad celebrate a vast range of festivals. Celebrations and observances include [Uttarayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttarayana), a harvest festival which involves kite-flying on 14 and 15 January. The nine nights of [Navratri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navratri) are celebrated with people performing [Garba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garba_\(dance\)), the most popular folk dance of Gujarat, at venues across the city. The annual [Rath Yatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rath_Yatra) procession takes place on the *Ashadh-sud-bij* date of the [Hindu calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar) at the [Jagannath Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagannath_Temple,_Ahmedabad). Festivals like Diwali, Holi, Christmas, and Muharram (pan-Indian festivals) are also celebrated.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]

### Cuisine

One of the most popular dishes in Ahmedabad is the Gujarati *[thali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thali)*, which was first served commercially by Chandvilas Hotel in 1900.[^cite_note-152] It consists of [roti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti) (chapati), [dal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dal), rice, and *shaak* (cooked vegetables, sometimes with [curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry)), with accompaniments of [pickles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_pickle) and roasted *[papads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papad)*. Sweet dishes include [laddoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laddu), [mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), and vedhmi. *Dhoklas*, *theplas*, and *dhebras* are other popularly consumed dishes in Ahmedabad.[^cite_note-153] Beverages include [buttermilk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttermilk) and tea. Drinking alcohol is legally banned in Ahmedabad, as Gujarat is a 'dry' state.[^cite_note-154]

Many restaurants serve Indian and international cuisines. Most of the food outlets serve only vegetarian food, as there exists a strong tradition of vegetarianism that has been maintained by the city's Jain and Hindu communities for centuries.[^cite_note-155] The first all-vegetarian [Pizza Hut](37561.md) in the world opened in Ahmedabad.[^cite_note-156] KFC has a separate staff uniform for serving vegetarian items and prepares vegetarian food in a separate kitchen,[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] as does McDonald's.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160] Ahmedabad has a number of restaurants serving typical [Mughlai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughlai_cuisine) non-vegetarian food in older areas like Bhatiyar Gali, [Kalupur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalupur) and Jamalpur.[^cite_note-161] [Manek Chowk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manek_Chowk_\(Ahmedabad\)) is an open square near the centre of the city that functions as a vegetable market in the morning and a jewellery market in the afternoon. However, it is best known for becoming a vast congregation of food stalls in the evening, which sell local [street food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_food). It is named after the Hindu saint Baba [Maneknath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maneknath).[^cite_note-Desai2007-162]

The city has diverse food options for the vegan community as well. PETA India has named Ahmedabad as India's most vegan-friendly city of 2024.[^cite_note-163]

### Art and crafts

Parts of Ahmedabad are known for their [folk art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_art). The artisans of Rangeela 'pol' make [tie-dyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tie-dye) *[bandhinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerchief)*, while the cobbler shops of Madhupura sell traditional *mojdi* (also known as *mojri*) footwear. Idols of the Hindu deity [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha) and other religious icons are made in large numbers by artisans in the Gulbai Tekra area. In 2019, there was a surge in demand for eco-friendly idols due to increased awareness surrounding the effects of submerging the traditional plaster-of-paris idols in the Sabarmati River.[^cite_note-164] The shops at the [Law Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Garden) sell mirrorwork handicrafts.[^cite_note-Law_garden-127]



The gate of [Swaminarayan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaminarayan_Temple,_Ahmedabad) in Kalupur, Ahmedabad

Three main literary institutions were established in Ahmedabad for the promotion of [Gujarati literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_literature): [Gujarat Vidhya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Vidhya_Sabha), [Gujarati Sahitya Parishad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_Sahitya_Parishad) and [Gujarat Sahitya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Sahitya_Sabha). [Saptak School of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saptak_School_of_Music) festival is held in the first week of the new year. This event was inaugurated by [Ravi Shankar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_Shankar).[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

The [Sanskar Kendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskar_Kendra), one of the several buildings in Ahmedabad designed by [Le Corbusier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Corbusier), is a museum displaying the city's history, art, culture, and architecture. The [Gandhi Smarak Sangrahalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhi_Smarak_Sangrahalaya) and the [Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel National Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel_National_Memorial) have permanent displays of photographs, documents, and other articles relating to the Gujarat-born Indian independence movement leaders [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) and [Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Patel). The [Calico Museum of Textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calico_Museum_of_Textiles) has a large collection of Indian and international fabrics, garments, and textiles.[^cite_note-167] The [Hazrat Pir Mohammad Shah Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazrat_Pir_Mohammad_Shah_Library) has a collection of rare original manuscripts in Arabic, [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_language), and [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language).[^cite_note-168] The Vechaar Utensils Museum has stainless steel, glass, brass, copper, bronze, zinc, and German silver tools on display.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] The [Conflictorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflictorium) is an interactive installation space that explores conflict in society through art.

The Shreyas Foundation has four museums on its campus. The Shreyas Folk Museum (Lokayatan Museum) has art forms and artefacts from various Gujarati communities. The [Kalpana Mangaldas Children's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_children's_museums_in_India) has a collection of toys, puppets, dance and drama costumes, coins, and a repository of recorded music from traditional shows from all over the world. Kahani houses photographs of fairs and festivals of [Gujarat](53707.md). Sangeeta Vadyakhand is a gallery of musical instruments from India and other countries.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173]

The L. D. Institute of Indology houses 76,000 hand-written Jain manuscripts with 500 illustrated versions and 45,000 printed books, making it the largest collection of Jain scripts, Indian sculptures, terracottas, miniature paintings, cloth paintings, painted scrolls, bronzes, woodwork, Indian coins, textiles and decorative art, paintings of [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore), and art of Nepal and Tibet.[^cite_note-174] The N. C. Mehta Gallery of Miniature Paintings has a collection of ornate miniature paintings and manuscripts from all over India.[^cite_note-175]

In 1949, the [Darpana Academy of Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darpana_Academy_of_Performing_Arts) was established by the scientist [Dr. Vikram Sarabhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Vikram_Sarabhai) and his wife, Bharat Natyam dancer Mrinalini Sarabhai. Its influence has led Ahmedabad to become a centre of [Indian classical dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_dance).[^cite_note-176]

### Primary and secondary education

Schools in Ahmedabad are either run publicly by the AMC or privately by entities, trusts, and corporations. ### Higher education and research organisations



Gujarat College, established in 1860.

Several institutions of higher education with a focus on engineering, management, and design are located in Ahmedabad.[^cite_note-177] The oldest higher educational institution is [Gujarat College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_College).[^cite_note-178] Among the [universities in Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Universities_and_colleges_in_Ahmedabad), [Gujarat University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_University) is a [collegiate university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_university) established in 1949[^cite_note-179] and has 286 affiliated colleges, 22 recognized institutions, and 36 postgraduate departments.[^cite_note-180] [Indira Gandhi National Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi_National_Open_University), commonly known as IGNOU is a public university in India and having an active regional centre in Ahmedabad region to offer 290 ODL programs and 40+ online programs to the students lives in the city.[^cite_note-181] Other state universities in the city include [Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_Open_University?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-182] [Gujarat Technological University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Technological_University),[^cite_note-183] and [Kaushalya Skill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaushalya_Skill_University).[^cite_note-184] [Gujarat Vidyapith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Vidyapith), located near the [Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel_Stadium,_Navrangpura), was founded by [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) in 1920 and became a [deemed university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deemed_university) in 1963.[^cite_note-185]

Private universities located in the city include [Ahmedabad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_University),[^cite_note-186] [CEPT University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEPT_University) (formerly Centre for Environmental Planning and Technology),[^cite_note-187] Indus University,[^cite_note-188] [Nirma University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirma_University),[^cite_note-189] [GLS University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLS_University),[^cite_note-190] and [Silver Oak University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Oak_University).[^cite_note-191] Two [Institutes of National Importance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutes_of_National_Importance) are located in the city—[Indian Institute of Management, Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Management_Ahmedabad)[^cite_note-192] and [National Institute of Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Design,_Ahmedabad).[^cite_note-193]

Other institutions located in the city include the [Physical Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_Research_Laboratory), which was established in 1947 by the physicist and astronomer [Vikram Sarabhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram_Sarabhai).[^cite_note-\:5-194] It is an autonomous research institute under the [Department of Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Space) with a focus on research in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), experimental and [theoretical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physics), and [earth sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_science).[^cite_note-\:5-194] The [Ahmedabad Textile Industry's Research Association (ATIRA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Textile_Industry's_Research_Association), registered in 1947, is an autonomous, non-profit association engaged in operational and applied research in the textile industry.[^cite_note-195]

The state-owned television broadcaster [Doordarshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doordarshan) provides free terrestrial channels, while three [multi system operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi_system_operator)—InCablenet, [Siti Cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siti_Cable), and GTPL—provide a mix of Gujarati, Hindi, English, and other regional channels via [cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television).[^cite_note-201] Telephone services are provided by landline and mobile operators such as [Jio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jio), [BSNL Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSNL_Mobile), [Airtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharti_Airtel), and [Vodafone Idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone_Idea_Limited).[^cite_note-202]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Ahmedabad)



[Torrent Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrent_Power) thermal power station at Sabarmati, Ahmedabad

The gross domestic product of Ahmedabad was estimated at \$64 billion in 2014.[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204] The RBI ranked Ahmedabad as the seventh largest deposit centre and seventh largest credit centre nationwide as of June 2012.[^cite_note-205] In the 19th century, the textile and garments industry received strong capital investment. On 30 May 1861 [Ranchhodlal Chhotalal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchhodlal_Chhotalal) founded the first Indian textile mill, the [Ahmedabad Spinning and Weaving Company Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Spinning_and_Weaving_Company_Limited),[^cite_note-Textile_industry_of_Ahmedabad-206] followed by the establishment of a series of textile mills such as [Calico Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calico_Mills), Bagicha Mills and [Arvind Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvind_Mills). By 1905 there were about 33 textile mills in the city.[^cite_note-History_of_textile_industry_in_Ahmedabad-207] The textile industry underwent rapid expansion during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) and benefited from the influence of Mahatma Gandhi's [Swadeshi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swadeshi_movement), which promoted the purchase of Indian-made goods.[^cite_note-208] Ahmedabad was known as the "Manchester of the East" for its textile industry.[^cite_note-manchester-56] The city is the largest supplier of denim and one of the largest exporters of [gemstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemstone) and jewellery in India.[^cite_note-jnnurm-21] The [automobile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_India) is also important to the city; after [Tata's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Group) [Nano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Nano) project, [Ford](30433662.md), [Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki) and [Peugeot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot) have established engine and vehicle manufacturing plants near Ahmedabad.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211]



The defunct Ahmedabad Stock Exchange

The [Ahmedabad Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Stock_Exchange), located in the Ambavadi area of the city, was India's second-oldest stock exchange. It is now defunct.[^cite_note-212] Two of the biggest [pharmaceutical companies of India](3373039.md)—[Zydus Lifesciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zydus_Cadila) and [Torrent Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrent_Pharmaceuticals)—are based in the city. The [Nirma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirma) group of industries, which runs detergent and chemical industrial units, has its corporate headquarters in the city. The city houses the corporate headquarters of the [Adani Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adani_Group), a multinational trading and infrastructure development company.[^cite_note-213] The [Sardar Sarovar Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Sarovar_Project) of dams and canals has improved the supply of potable water and electricity for the city.[^cite_note-214] The [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology_in_India) industry has developed significantly in Ahmedabad, with companies such as [Tata Consultancy Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Consultancy_Services) opening offices in the city.[^cite_note-Ahmedabad,_Kolkata_among_new_global_hotspots-215] A [NASSCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASSCOM) survey in 2002 on the "Super Nine Indian Destinations" for IT-enabled services ranked Ahmedabad fifth among the top nine most competitive cities in the country.[^cite_note-Ahmedabad_joins_ITES_hot_spots-216] The city's educational and industrial institutions have attracted students and young skilled workers from the rest of India.[^cite_note-217] Ahmedabad houses other major Indian corporates such as [Zydus Lifesciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zydus_Lifesciences), [Rasna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasna_\(drink\)), [Wagh Bakri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Tea_Processors_&_Packers_Ltd), [Cadila Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadila_Pharmaceuticals), and [Intas Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intas_Pharmaceuticals). Ahmedabad is the second largest cotton textile centre in India after Mumbai and the largest in Gujarat.[^cite_note-218] Many cotton manufacturing units operate in and around Ahmedabad.[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222][^cite_note-223] Textiles are one of the major industries of the city.[^cite_note-224] Gujarat Industrial Development Corporation has acquired land in [Sanand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanand) taluka of Ahmedabad to set up three new industrial estates.[^cite_note-225]

### Transportation

[Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Airport), located in Hansol and operated by the [Adani Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adani_Group), is Ahmedabad's principal airport.[^cite_note-226] The [Dholera International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dholera_International_Airport), located 110 km (68 mi) southwest of central Ahmedabad in Navagam village, is under construction and expects completion of its first phase by 2025.[^cite_note-227]

The [Ahmedabad railway division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_railway_division), an operating division under the [Western Railway zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Railway_zone) of [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways), is headquartered in the city.[^cite_note-228] [Ahmedabad Junction railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Junction_railway_station), locally known as Kalupur railway station,[^cite_note-229] is Ahmedabad's primary and Gujarat's busiest railway hub.[^cite_note-230] Other major railway stations that service the city include [Chandlodiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandlodiya_railway_station),[^cite_note-231] [Gandhigram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhigram_railway_station),[^cite_note-232] [Maninagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maninagar_railway_station),[^cite_note-233] and [Sabarmati Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Junction_railway_station).[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235]

Public transit includes the [Ahmedabad Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Metro), a [rapid transit system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) inaugurated in March 2019 with 40 km (25 mi) of track on two lines (East-West and North-South) and a daily ridership of 90,000.[^cite_note-236] Phase 2 of the Ahmedabad Metro—connecting Motera Stadium northwards to Mahatma Mandir in Gandhinagar—began construction in February 2021 and is expected to be complete by 2026.[^cite_note-237] Other public transit options include the [Ahmedabad BRTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Bus_Rapid_Transit_System), also known as *Janmarg* (people's way), a bus rapid transit system inaugurated in October 2009 with a total fleet of 325 buses over 19 routes and a daily ridership of 190,000.[^cite_note-\:6-238] Bus transportation is also provided by [Ahmedabad Municipal Transport Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Municipal_Transport_Service) (AMTS) with 700 buses over 149 routes.[^cite_note-\:6-238] Both the Ahmedabad BRTS and the AMTS are overseen by the Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation.[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240] Ahmedabad also has self drive car rental service provided by private companies like Just Drive Self Drive Cars.

The Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation introduced "AmdaBike", a public bicycle sharing system, in December 2019 to improve last mile connectivity.[^cite_note-\:7-241] [MYBYK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MYBYK) is the main service provider for AmdaBike with 300 bicycle stations—including at Ahmedabad BRTS stations—and 4,000 bicycles.[^cite_note-\:7-241]

### Road



Ahmedabad BRT

[National Highway 48](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_48_\(India\)) passes through Ahmedabad, connecting it with [New Delhi](51585.md) and [Mumbai](19189.md). The [National Highway 147](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_147_\(India\)) also links Ahmedabad to Gandhinagar. The city is connected to [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara) through [National Expressway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Vadodara_Expressway), a 94 km (58 mi) expressway with two exits. This expressway is part of the [Golden Quadrilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Quadrilateral) project.[^cite_note-242]

In 2001, the Central Pollution Control Board ranked Ahmedabad as the most polluted city in India out of 85 surveyed. The [Gujarat Pollution Control Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Pollution_Control_Board) provided autorickshaw drivers a ₹10,000 incentive to convert all 37,733 vehicles to cleaner-burning [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas) to reduce pollution. Consequently, by 2008, Ahmedabad was ranked as the 50th most polluted city in India.[^cite_note-243]

## Sports



[EKA Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EKA_Arena) is a [multi-purpose stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_stadium) in the city



[Narendra Modi Stadium](80201506.md)

[Cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_India) is the most popular sports in the city.[^cite_note-244] The [Narendra Modi Stadium](80201506.md)—originally the [Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Patel_Stadium,_Motera) (built in 1982) and also known as the Motera Stadium—hosts both [one day internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_day_international) and [test matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket). With a seating capacity of 132,000, it is the [largest stadium in the world by capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stadiums_by_capacity).[^cite_note-245] The venue hosted matches during the 1987, 1996, 2011, and 2023 [Cricket World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup), including the 2023 final.[^cite_note-Indian_Grounds-246][^cite_note-247] It serves as the home ground for the [Gujarat cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_cricket_team), a [first-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_cricket) team, which competes in domestic tournaments. Ahmedabad has a second cricket stadium at the Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation's [Sports Club of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel_Stadium,_Navrangpura).[^cite_note-Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel_Stadium-248] Ahmedabad is also home to the IPL team Gujarat Titans, which won their first title in 2022 in front of its home crowd.[^cite_note-249]

Other popular sports include [field hockey](10886.md), [badminton](3956.md), [tennis](29773.md), [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\)), and [golf](19568112.md). Ahmedabad has nine [golf courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_course).[^cite_note-Five_more_golf_courses_to_tee_off_in_state-250] The [Mithakhali Multi Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithakhali_Multi_Sports_Complex) is being developed by the Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation to promote various indoor sports.[^cite_note-Multi-crore_sports_complex_in_city-251] The city has also hosted national-level games for [roller skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roller_skating) and table tennis.[^cite_note-Looking_to_Feature-252] [Kart racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kart_racing) is gaining popularity in the city following the introduction of a 380 metre track based on [Formula One](10854.md) design concepts.[^cite_note-No_sense_of_adventure-253][^cite_note-Karting-254]



Participants in the [Sabarmati Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Marathon)

The [Sabarmati Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Marathon) has been held annually since 2011; its categories include full and half marathons, a 7 km (4.3 mi) "dream run", a 5 km (3.1 mi) run for the visually disabled, and a 5 km (3.1 mi) wheelchair run.[^cite_note-255] In 2007, Ahmedabad hosted the 51st national-level [shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_sports) games.[^cite_note-National_Shooting_C'ships_to_be_held_at_Ahmedabad-256] The [2016 Kabaddi World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Kabaddi_World_Cup_\(International_Kabaddi_Federation\)) was held at [The Arena by Transtadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Arena_\(Ahmedabad\)) (a renovated Kankaria football ground). [Geet Sethi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geet_Sethi), a five-time winner of the [World Professional Billiards Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Professional_Billiards_Championship) and a recipient of India's highest sporting award, the [Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi_Khel_Ratna), was raised in the city.[^cite_note-257]

The [Adani Ahmedabad Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Marathon) has been organised annually by the [Adani Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adani_Group) since 2017. The inaugural event attracted 8,000 participants and the 2020 event was held virtually in compliance with [COVID-19](62750956.md) guidelines.[^cite_note-258]

In the 2020s, Ahmedabad has been proposed as India's host city for the [2036 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2036_Summer_Olympics) and other multi-sport events. The [Gujarat government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_government) has identified 33 sites in and around Ahmedabad for the development of infrastructure to support the Olympic bid.[^cite_note-259] The bid is being developed with international experts, including consultants from Australia.[^cite_note-260] The Gujarat government is setting up a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) to manage the bid.[^cite_note-261] Ahead of the city's staging of the [2030 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2030_Commonwealth_Games), the [Veer Savarkar Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veer_Savarkar_Sports_Complex) in Naranpura and the planned [Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel Sports Enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel_Sports_Enclave) in Motera are slated to host events.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263]
