**[Rail transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) in [Poland](22936.md)** primarily utilises [standard-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway) railway lines of over 19,083 kilometres (11,858 mi), of which 98% is managed by the state-owned [PKP Polskie Linie Kolejowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKP_Polskie_Linie_Kolejowe).[^cite_note-UTK-lines-2] In addition, there are 578.9 kilometres (359.7 mi) of [broad-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad-gauge_railway) railway lines, most notably the [Broad Gauge Metallurgy Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Gauge_Metallurgy_Line),[^cite_note-UTK-lines-2] and 29 disconnected local [narrow-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge_railway) railways with the combined length of lines at around 423 kilometres (263 mi) as of 2025.[^cite_note-3]

Poland is a member of the [International Union of Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Railways) (UIC), its [UIC Country Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UIC_Country_Code) is 51.

Rail services are operated by a range of public and private rail operators. The state-owned [PKP Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKP_Group) operates the majority of rail services. In addition to PKP owned companies, there are a number of private cargo operators, as well as a number of independent passenger operators, with the latter owned predominantly by [Voivodeship provincial governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland).

## Overview



Max speeds in Poland

The vast majority of the network was built before [World War II](32927.md) by various railway companies, including by the German [Deutsche Reichsbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Reichsbahn) and by the [Russian Imperial State Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Imperial_State_Railways), and a minor component was built from 1946 onwards by the Communist authorities of the [Polish People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Republic). During the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) at the beginning of [World War II](32927.md) the Polish railway network was crippled by the [Luftwaffe](17885.md) [bombing campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_II).[^cite_note-4] Due to the average age of the network and lack of maintenance, many sections are limited to speeds below 160 km/h (99 mph) even on trunk lines. 2,813 km (1,748 mi) allow 160 km/h (99 mph) or more.[^cite_note-5]

Since Poland's entry into the [European Union](9317.md) in 2004, major financing has been made available by European financing institutions to improve both the Polish rail network and the rolling stock fleet. Up to June 2014, the [European Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Investment_Bank) had provided loans totalling €1.9 billion for rail modernization projects in Poland.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] An additional €578 million had been provided through December 2013 to modernize 70% of [PKP Intercity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKP_Intercity) rolling stock.[^cite_note-8] The €665 million purchase of 20 [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom) [Pendolino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendolino) high-speed trains delivered in 2014 was financed in part by €342 million from the European Investment Bank.[^cite_note-9]

Poland currently has no *[high-speed lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Poland)* operated at speeds above 200 km/h (124 mph). The [Central Rail Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Rail_Line_\(Poland\)), *centralna magistrala kolejowa*, 'CMK', which links [Warsaw](32908.md) to [Katowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katowice) and [Kraków](16815.md), was designed with an alignment to permit 250 km/h (155 mph), but for over 30 years after its construction Poland possessed no rolling stock capable of speeds above 160 km/h (99 mph). Since 2008, the CMK has been upgraded to allow higher speeds, including installation of [European Train Control System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Train_Control_System) Level 1 which provides the [Cab signalling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cab_signalling) required by high speed trains. Most trains on the CMK still operate at speeds up to 160 km/h (99 mph), but since 14 December 2014 new [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom) [Pendolino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendolino) ED250 trains operate on a 90 km section of the CMK at 200 km/h (124 mph), and improvements under way should raise the authorized speed to 200 km/h (124 mph) on most of the line. In test runs on the CMK in November 2013 a new [Pendolino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendolino) ED250 train set a new Polish speed record of 293 km/h (182 mph).[^cite_note-10]

New PKP Intercity [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens) [EuroSprinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroSprinter) departing from [Berlin](3354.md)

In 2011–2015, the [Warsaw–Gdańsk railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw%E2%80%93Gda%C5%84sk_railway) has undergone a major upgrading costing \$3 billion, partly funded by the [European Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Investment_Bank), including track replacement, realignment of curves and relocation of sections of track to allow speeds up to 200 km/h (124 mph), modernization of stations, and installation of the most modern ETCS Level 2 signalling system, which is to be completed in June 2015. In December 2014 new [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom) [Pendolino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendolino) high-speed trains were put into service between [Gdańsk](12099.md), [Warsaw](32908.md) and [Kraków](16815.md) reducing the rail travel time from Gdańsk to Warsaw to 2 hours 58 minutes,[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] to be reduced in late 2015 to 2 hours 37 minutes.[^cite_note-13]

In 2008, the government announced the construction of a [dedicated high speed line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Poland) based on the French [TGV](57212.md) model and possibly using TGV style trainsets, by 2020. The Y-shaped line would link [Warsaw](32908.md) to [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA), [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84) and [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw) at speeds of up to 320 km/h (199 mph). The plans included an upgrade of the [Central Rail Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Rail_Line_\(Poland\)) to 250 km/h (155 mph) or more, as this line has an [LGV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignes_%C3%A0_Grande_Vitesse)-like profile. In December 2011 plans to build the high speed 'Y' line were postponed until 2030, due to the high cost.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

See also: [High-speed rail in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Poland)

As of 2008, foreign services include [EuroCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroCity) and [EuroNight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroNight) trains between Western and Eastern Europe. Most notably, the now defunct [EN Jan Kiepura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EN_Jan_Kiepura?action=edit\&redlink=1). This train carried direct sleeping cars between [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) and [Warsaw](32908.md). Though formerly its Western terminuses have included [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Brussels), [Amsterdam](844.md), [Basel](4911.md) and [Munich](19058.md), while the Eastern terminus for some through carriages reached as far as [Moscow](19004.md) and [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk).

### Broad-gauge railways



LHS links southern Poland with [broad gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_gauge) railways in Ukraine and other eastern countries

The network is [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge) except for the [Linia Hutnicza Szerokotorowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linia_Hutnicza_Szerokotorowa) (LHS) lit. 'Broad Gauge Metallurgy Line' and a few short stretches near [border crossings](288329.md). LHS to [Sławków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82awk%C3%B3w) is the longest [broad-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad-gauge) line, single track, almost 400 km (250 mi) long, from the [Ukrainian border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland%E2%80%93Ukraine_border) just east of [Hrubieszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrubiesz%C3%B3w). It is the westernmost broad gauge line connected to the system of the [former Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states).

Since the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), there are proposed to build more broad-gauge lines in and around Poland.[^cite_note-17] LHS westward extension from Slawkow via Gliwice, Gorlitz, Jena, Paderborn and Oberhausen to Amsterdam and Rotterdam, and branch to Hamburg, and new broad-gauge link from Ukrainian border via Chelm, Lublin, Bialystok and Suwalki to Mockai (Lithuania) and branch to Gdansk Port.

### PKP Group

[Polish State Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_State_Railways) (PKP), a state-owned [corporate group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_group) and part of the [PKP Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKP_Group) conglomerate, is the main provider of railway services, holding an almost complete monopoly on long-distance passenger services. It is both supported and partly funded by the government.

There are three main PKP companies:

- [PKP PLK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKP_PLK) – owns and maintains infrastructure including lines and stations.

- [PKP Intercity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKP_Intercity) – provides long-distance connections on the most popular routes. Trains are divided into the [categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_categories_in_Europe): EuroNight (EN), EuroCity (EC), Express InterCity (EIC), Express InterCity Premium (EIP) – generally faster and more expensive, InterCity (IC) and (TLK) (interregional fast trains, slower than EN/EC/EIC but cheaper) and international fast trains.

  [PKP Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKP_Cargo) – provides cargo rail transport.

#### Rapid transit
