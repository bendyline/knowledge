**Al-Mahrah** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): ٱلْمَهْرَة *_al-Mahrah_*), or simply **Mahra**, is the easternmost [governorate](690277.md) (*[muhafazah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhafazah)*) of [Yemen](350939.md), situated in the [southern part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia) of the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md). Bordered by [Oman](22316.md) to the east and the [Rub' al Khali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub'_al_Khali) desert to the north, it is often referred to as the country's "Eastern Gateway."[^cite_note-NIC-2] Its capital is [Al Ghaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ghaydah), and it remains the least populated governorate in Yemen, accounting for only 0.5% of the national population.[^cite_note-NIC-2]

Al-Mahrah has a 500-kilometer coastline along the [Arabian Sea](2747.md), a rugged central mountain plateau reaching elevations of 1,300 metres (4,300 ft), and the Rub' al Khali desert dunes to the north. The region is ecologically unique to the rest of the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) due to the seasonal [monsoon](57630.md), or *[Khareef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khareef)* season, which transforms the coastal mountains of the [Hawf District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawf_District) into lush, verdant forests and valleys.[^cite_note-NIC-2][^cite_note-Cavendish2006-3]

A significant portion of the [Mahris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahris) speak the [Mehri language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehri_language), a [modern South Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_South_Arabian_languages) language, in addition to Arabic as their primary tongue.[^cite_note-GeoCurrents-4] The Mahri people are traditionally regarded as descendants of the ancient [ʿĀd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BF%C4%80d) kingdom.[^cite_note-Caton2013-5] Historically, the region was a center for the [frankincense trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incense_trade_route) and played a pivotal role in early Islamic military history, contributing elite cavalry to the [Muslim conquest of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Egypt) and the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb).[^cite_note-Yale_University_Press-6]

Al-Mahrah relies heavily on fishing, livestock breeding, and cross-border trade through the [Port of Nishtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Nishtun) and various land crossings into Oman.[^cite_note-NIC-2] While the governorate has remained relatively stable and largely isolated from the direct violence of the [Yemeni Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_\(2015%E2%80%93present\)), it has seen an increased Saudi Arabian military and political presence since late 2017.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

## History

See also: [Mahra Sultanate § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahra_Sultanate#History)

### Ancient history

The ancient history of the Mahra region begins with the formation of the [ʿĀd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BF%C4%80d) kingdom by an Arabian tribe called ʿĀd which settled in South Arabia. The Mehri people are traditionally considered descendants of the ʿĀd Kingdom and blood relatives of the [Thamud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thamud).[^cite_note-8] According to [Islamic](6037917.md) genealogies, the forefather of the Mehri people was [Ya'rub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya'rub), the son of [Qahtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qahtanite), grandson of the Islamic prophet [Hud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hud_\(prophet\)), and ancestor of the [Himyarite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himyarite_Kingdom), [Qataban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qataban) and [Sabaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaeans) kingdoms.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Ya'rub (or, by alternate accounts, Ya'rub's son), is sometimes credited with the invention of the [Arabic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

During ancient times, the ʿĀd Kingdom was a transshipment point for the [frankincense trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incense_trade_route). It was exported mostly to ancient Europe. It has been suggested the ʿĀd Kingdom, and the current location of Mahra Sultanate, were the first places in the world where the [camel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel) was domesticated.[^cite_note-16]

### Islamic period

During the first decade of the [Islamic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar) (the [620s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/620s) in the [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar)), a large delegation from Mahra under the leadership of [Mehri bin Abyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehri_bin_Abyad?action=edit\&redlink=1) went to Medina to meet the Islamic prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad), and during that meeting the entire Mehri tribe decided to embrace [Islam](6037917.md). Before embracing Islam, the tribe was polytheist and worshiped multiple deities. After the meeting in Medina, Muhammad issued an injunction, stating that the members of the Mehri tribe are true Muslims and no war should be waged against them, and that any violator of the injunction shall be considered to be waging war against [Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allah).[^cite_note-17]

The entire Mehri tribe became some of the earliest adopters of Islam. Their action had an added bonus as becoming Muslims secured them a political alliance and stable relations with the Muslim leadership in Medina. Prior to embracing [Islam](6037917.md), Al-Mahra was a vassal state of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) and had been subjected to Persian control for many years. Siding with Medina enabled the Mehri people to break away from Persian control and regain their liberty.

#### Ridda Wars

Main article: [Ridda Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridda_Wars)

When Muhammad died in the year 632 CE, many Arab tribes, including the Mehri, interpreted his death as the end of Islam, and they abandoned the religion by either reverting to paganism or following certain individuals who claimed prophethood.[^cite_note-SUNY_Press-18] In 634 CE, the Mehri and other tribes rebelled against Caliph [Abu Bakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakar) who became the new leader of the Muslims. In response, he launched a new military campaign against the rebels.

There were not many records about the power structure within the Mehris, however, during the [Ridda Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridda_wars) information regarding the intra-tribal affair was revealed by [al-Tabari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tabari). According to al-Tabari,[^cite_note-SUNY_Press-18] before the death of Muhammad, there was an intra-tribal rivalry within the Mehri tribe, which consisted of two competing factions: the Bani Shakhrah faction and their larger rival, the Bani Muharib. The Bani Muharib, who hailed from Al-Mahra's mountain regions, had the upper hand against their smaller rival.

A Muslim army under the command of [Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikrimah_ibn_Abi_Jahl) was sent to Al-Mahra to face the Mehri who had turned their back on Islam like many Arab tribes. The Muslim army was too weak to confront the Mehri tribe in battle, and this situation forced Ikrimah to engage in political activity rather than initiating war in Mahra. Ikrimah met with the leadership of the Bani Muharib faction and convinced them to return to Islam. After this event, the army under Ikrimah's command, and the Bani Muharib faction, formed a military alliance against the Bani Shakhrah. The Ridda War in Al-Mahra ended quickly as the newly formed alliance subdued the Bani Shakhrah faction without bloodshed. Islam was once again the only religion in Al-Mahra.

#### The military legacy of al-Mahra



[Bin Yasir Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bin_Yasir_Fort?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the [Hawf District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawf_District)

The people of al-Mahra played a role in the history of Islam and the Arab world's military achievements during the early years of Islam. The Mehri army participated in the first [Muslim conquest of the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb). The Mehri tribe's achievements have been well-documented by historian [Ibn 'Abd al-Hakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_'Abd_al-Hakam)[^cite_note-19] in his book titled *The History of the Conquests of Egypt and North Africa and Spain*.

At the beginning of the first [Muslim conquest of the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb), the Al-Mahri tribe mostly contributed cavalry to the army. They played a crucial role in the Arab army under the command of ['Amr ibn al-'As](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Amr_ibn_al-'As), who was a well-known Arab military commander and one of the Sahaba [Companions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad's_companions). The Al-Mahri army fought alongside him during the Arab conquest of North Africa, which began with the defeat of the Byzantine imperial forces at the [Battle of Heliopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Heliopolis), and later at the [Battle of Nikiou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nikiou) in Egypt in the year 646. The Mehri army were highly skilled [cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry) which rode horses and a special camel breed called the Mehri originating from Al-Mahra which was renowned for its speed, agility and toughness.[^cite_note-Yale_University_Press-6] The Al-Mahra contingent even spearheaded the army during the conquest of the city of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria).[^cite_note-Yale_University_Press-6]

The Al-Mahra army was nicknamed "the people who kill without being killed" by ['Amr ibn al-'As](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Amr_ibn_al-'As).[^cite_note-Yale_University_Press-6] Commander 'Amr ibn al-'As was amazed by Mehri army's ruthlessly fighting skill and efficient warfare.[^cite_note-Yale_University_Press-6]

As a result of Al-Mahri's success in the [Muslim conquest of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Egypt), its commander named Abd al-sallam ibn Habira al-Mahri was promoted and he was ordered by 'Amr ibn al-'As to lead the entire Muslim army during the Arab conquest of [Libya](17633.md) which at the time was a [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) territory.[^cite_note-Yale_University_Press-6] The army under the command Abd al-sallam ibn Habira Al-Mahri defeated the Byzantine imperial army in Libya, and this campaign headed by Commander Al-Mahri brought a permanent end to Byzantine rule of Libya. After the Muslim conquest of Egypt, Abd al-sallam ibn Habira Al-Mahri was once again promoted as a result of his success as a temporary commander of the entire Muslim army, and subsequently he was appointed the first Muslim leader of [Libya](17633.md).

During the [Second Fitna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Fitna), more than 600 soldiers carrying the Al-Mahra flag were sent to North Africa to fight the Byzantines and Berbers.[^cite_note-Yale_University_Press-6]

Throughout the first [Muslim conquest of the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb) the army from Al-Mahra were awarded lands in the newly conquered territories. Initially the Mehri tribe were awarded lands in the region of Jabal Yashkar by the Muslim leadership. This region was located east of the town of [Al-Askar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Askar) which at that time was the capital of Egypt.[^cite_note-20] After the end of Muslim conquest of Egypt in year 641, the Muslim commander 'Amr ibn al-'As established the town of [Fustat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fustat) which became the new capital of [Egypt](8087628.md), and the Mehri tribe were given additional land in [Fustat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fustat) which then became known as Khittat Mahra or the Mahra quarter in English. This land was used by the Mahra forces as a garrison.[^cite_note-Yale_University_Press-6] The Mahra quarter was named after the residents from Al-Mahra as they were the sole residents and owners of the land. Other Arab tribes which were part of the Muslim conquest of Egypt had to share lands which is the reason why their lands bore a non-tribal name.[^cite_note-Yale_University_Press-6] The Mahra tribe also shared the al-Raya quarter in Fustat with various tribes who were closely associated with the Muhammad and, according to historical accounts, the Mahra forces used the al-Raya quarter as a residence and stable for their precious horses.[^cite_note-21] The Mahra quarter was located close to the Al-Raya quarter was which the absolute centre of the new capital of Fustat. Later, the Mahra neighbourhood was renamed and lost its historic name.

Main article: [Muhammad ibn Ammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Ammar)

Several centuries later, another Mehri man called [Abu Bekr Mohammed Ibn Ammar Al-Mahri Ash-shilbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Ammar), who was a politician from modern-day [Silves, Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silves,_Portugal), became a prime minister of the [Taifa of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Seville) in Islamic Iberia,[^cite_note-22] and served King [Al-Mu'tamid ibn Abbad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mu'tamid_ibn_Abbad), who was member of Muslim dynasties of Spain. Abu Bekr was highly competent as prime minister, but later he crowned himself king of the annexed [Taifa of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Murcia) and led a failed rebellion against the Mohammedan dynasties of Spain. In year 1084, Abu Bekr Mohammed Ibn Ammar Al-Mahri Ash-shilbi was caught and executed by the forces of the [Kingdom of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Seville).

### Yemeni Civil War

The governorate has remained relatively untouched by the [civil war in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Civil_War_\(2015%E2%80%93present\)) that began in 2015.[^cite_note-23]

On 10 September 2016, three militants in al-Mahrah Governorate declared on social media the formation of a new *wilayat*, or state, belonging to the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL). However, official ISIL media did not acknowledge the declaration.[^cite_note-24]

From 2015 to late 2017, al-Mahrah was under the control of the Yemeni 123rd and 137th Mechanized Brigades.[^cite_note-25] From mid-November 2017 onwards, Saudi Arabia began increasing its presence in the governorate, taking control of facilities, Nishtun port, the Sarfit and Shehen border crossings, and al-Gaydah Airport, while establishing military outposts around key infrastructure and coastal areas.[^cite_note-\:0-7] On 27 November 2017, Mohammed Abdullah Kuddah, the governor of al-Mahrah, was replaced by Rajeh Said Bakrit, following the former's objections to Saudi influence.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

## Demographics



Children playing in the town of al-Hawf in the [Hawf District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawf_District)

A sizeable part of the Mahrah population does not speak Arabic as their primary language. Non-Arabic speakers primarily speak [Mehri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehri_language), a [modern South Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_South_Arabian_languages) language.[^cite_note-26] The people that speak Mahri call themselves 'Mahris', and are presumed to be descendants of the ancient people of ['Ad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BF%C4%80d).[^cite_note-Caton2013-5]

### Population

Al Mahrah is the least populated governorate in the Republic of Yemen, accounting for approximately 0.5% of the total national population.[^cite_note-auto-27] According to the 2004 General Census of Population, Housing, and Establishments, the total population was 88,594 people, with an annual growth rate of 4.51%.[^cite_note-NIC-2]

The population is primarily concentrated in the southern coastal plain, which contains most of the major urban centers, including the provincial capital, [Al Ghaydah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ghaydah).[^cite_note-NIC-2] This concentration is due to the region's economic reliance on fishing and trade. In contrast, the northern districts bordering the [Rub' al Khali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub'_al_Khali) desert, such as [Hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat_district) and [Shahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahan_district), are extremely sparsely populated.[^cite_note-NIC-2]

## Geography



The [Hawf district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawf_District) in Al Mahrah during the annual *[Khareef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khareef)* season.

Al Mahrah is situated in the easternmost part of Yemen, approximately 1,318 kilometres (819 mi) from Sana'a, and is often referred to as the country's eastern gateway. It covers an area of roughly 67,297 square kilometres (25,984 sq mi), bordering the [Rub' al Khali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub'_al_Khali) to the north, the Sultanate of Oman to the east, and [Hadramaut Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadramaut_Governorate) to the west.[^cite_note-auto-27]

The topography is characterized by three primary regions: a southern coastal plain, a central mountain plateau, and the northern desert. The coastal plain stretches for about 500 kilometres (310 mi) along the [Arabian Sea](2747.md), with the specific segment from the Hadramaut border to the Omani border extending 375 kilometres (233 mi).[^cite_note-NIC-2] This plain rises to a maximum elevation of approximately 250 metres (820 ft) and is where the majority of the population and urban centers are located.[^cite_note-NIC-2] Moving inland, the central plateau consists of rugged mountain ranges and valleys, including peaks such as Jabal al-Habshiya, Jabal al-Ghart, and the Jabal Bani Kashit range.[^cite_note-NIC-2] These mountains, sometimes referred to as the Mahrat or part of the broader [Hadhramaut Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadhramaut_Mountains), reach heights of around 1,300 metres (4,300 ft).[^cite_note-Cavendish2006-3][^cite_note-Scoville1979-29] Other notable geological features in the governorate include the [Well of Barhout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_of_Barhout) sinkhole and the lush valleys of the [Hawf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawf_District) region.[^cite_note-AtlO-30]

The climate is generally hot during the summer and moderate in the winter, though it is significantly influenced by the seasonal [monsoon](57630.md), known as the *[Khareef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khareef)*.[^cite_note-NIC-2] During this period, the coastal mountains near the border with Oman become enveloped in fog and moisture, transforming the typically barren landscape into verdant forests and valleys.[^cite_note-NIC-2] While water scarcity is common across the governorate, rainfall is primarily concentrated in the summer and winter months within these mountainous areas, with annual precipitation recorded at 982 millimetres (38.7 in) in 2004.[^cite_note-NIC-2]

The biological diversity of the region is shaped by its arid and semi-arid environments. Vegetation predominantly consists of desert shrubs and herbs that appear following rainfall, alongside perennial trees such as the frankincense tree (*[Boswellia sacra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boswellia_sacra)*), [sidr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziziphus_spina-christi), and various acacia species like the samar.[^cite_note-NIC-2] The remote valleys and mountains provide a habitat for wildlife including foxes, rabbits, and hedgehogs, as well as rarer species such as the [Arabian leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_leopard) and gazelles.[^cite_note-NIC-2] The avian population is equally diverse, featuring birds of prey like falcons and owls alongside various smaller species found in the more densely vegetated wadis.[^cite_note-NIC-2]
