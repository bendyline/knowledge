"**Golden**" is a song performed by [Ejae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejae), [Audrey Nuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audrey_Nuna), and [Rei Ami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rei_Ami) as the [fictional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_band) K-pop girl group [Huntrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntrix) in the 2025 animated musical [urban fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_fantasy) film *[KPop Demon Hunters](67104864.md)*. It was released on July 4, 2025, through [Republic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Records), as the second single from [the film's soundtrack album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPop_Demon_Hunters_\(soundtrack\)).

Critically acclaimed and an immediate hit, the song reached number one on the [*Billboard* Global 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Global_200) and topped the [charts](1795886.md) in more than 30 countries, including South Korea, the United Kingdom, and the United States. It reached the top ten of charts in more than 20 other countries, and was [certified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_recording_certification) 2x Platinum or higher in eight countries.&#x20;

Ejae, Nuna, and Ami promoted "Golden" with performances on *[The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_Starring_Jimmy_Fallon)*, *[Jimmy Kimmel Live!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel_Live!)*, and at [Macy's Thanksgiving Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy's_Thanksgiving_Day_Parade) in New York. The song received several accolades, including the [Critics' Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics'_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Song), [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Original_Song), and [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song) for Best Original Song. At the [68th Grammy Awards](79128292.md), the song received four nominations (including [Song of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Song_of_the_Year)) and won [Best Song Written for Visual Media](415037.md); it was the first K-pop song to win a Grammy Award and the first to win an Academy Award.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## Background and release

The animated musical fantasy film *[KPop Demon Hunters](67104864.md)* was released on [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) on June 20, 2025. The film follows the fictional K-pop girl group Huntrix, consisting of Rumi, Mira, and Zoey, whose singing voices are performed by [Ejae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejae), [Audrey Nuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audrey_Nuna), and [Rei Ami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rei_Ami), respectively.[^cite_note-7] The film's [soundtrack album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPop_Demon_Hunters_\(soundtrack\)) was released on the same day, with "Golden" as the fourth track.[^cite_note-8]

[Republic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Records) released "Golden" as a single on July 4, 2025, along with [instrumental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumental) and [a cappella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_cappella) versions, and announced that the song would be submitted for awards consideration.[^cite_note-Variety_Golden_single_announcement-9] *[Billboard](18309966.md)* noted that "following the soundtrack's second-week streaming explosion, Republic hustled to have 'Golden' impact [top 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_40) radio stations" on July 8.[^cite_note-Billboard_Lipshutz-2025b-10] Republic Records Chairman Jim Roppo said that they were also in discussions on "potential remixes of" the song "with some 'A-list remixers' in play".[^cite_note-Billboard_Lipshutz-2025b-10] A remix of "Golden" by [David Guetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Guetta) was released on July 25.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

"Golden" was performed live in full for the first time by Ejae, Nuna, and Rei Ami on *[The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_Starring_Jimmy_Fallon)* on October 7, 2025.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] A few days earlier, on October 4, an abbreviated version was performed live for the first time on the [*Saturday Night Live* season 51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live_season_51) premiere as part of a sketch in which host [Bad Bunny](55943877.md)'s character expressed his love for the movie.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] On January 8, 2026, Ejae, Nuna, and Rei Ami performed on *[Jimmy Kimmel Live!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel_Live!)* with the debut of a new [symphonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony) version of "Golden". This version, "Golden (Glowin' Version)", was released the following day.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] In January 2026, it was announced that "Golden" would be one of two live musical performances at the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md) (March 2026), following its nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song).[^cite_note-20] In January 2026, the song’s official lyric video achieved 1 billion views on [YouTube](3524766.md).[^cite_note-21]

## Lyrics and composition

Within the narrative of *KPop Demon Hunters*, "Golden" is the latest musical release by Huntrix, intended to bring about the "Golden Honmoon", a permanent magical barrier against the demons that Huntrix fight.[^cite_note-Tudum_Song_Details-22][^cite_note-THS-23][^cite_note-24] As the song plays in the film, the audience learns that Rumi is secretly half-demon.[^cite_note-Tudum_Song_Details-22][^cite_note-THS-23] Ian Eisendrath, executive producer for the soundtrack album, described "Golden" as an ["I Want" song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%22I_Want%22_song) that explores the characters' purpose while also dwelling on Rumi's inner thoughts during the song's [bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_\(music\)), which shifts from "inspirational pop" to a slightly darker "[sotto voce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotto_voce_\(music\))".[^cite_note-Tudum_Song_Details-22] *[The Chosun Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chosun_Daily)* wrote that "while over 90% of the lyrics are in English, key moments feature Korean words such as 'eoduwajin' ('darkened') and 'yeongwonhi ggaejil su eomneun' ('unbreakable forever'), preserving its K-pop identity".[^cite_note-The_Chosun_Daily-2025-25]

Eisendrath said that they initially "had five to six songs written for that slot" but kept developing "until it was the right energy. The directors are tough customers, in the best possible way, and they had a real vision for what this needed to be".[^cite_note-26] The song had the working title "Gold" during production.[^cite_note-ChungTudum-27] According to Danny Chung, who worked and performed on the film's soundtrack, "Golden" was "the last \[song] to be really locked in". It went "through a few iterations of production, meaning the instrumental changed a few times before it landed where it would be."[^cite_note-ChungTudum-27] The song was co-written by [Ejae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejae) and [Mark Sonnenblick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Sonnenblick) "under the guidance" of Eisendrath.[^cite_note-The_Korea_Herald-2025-28]

The song is performed in [G major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_major),[^cite_note-29] covering a three-octave range up to a high A at A5.[^cite_note-The_Chosun_Daily-2025-25][^cite_note-Ars_Technica-30] Music critic Lim Hee-yoon's wrote that "the powerful high note reaching up to a [high A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_\(musical_note\)) in the third [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave) is rarely used in idol songs", adding "freshness".[^cite_note-The_Chosun_Daily-2025-25] *[Ars Technica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Technica)* wrote that the song's three-octave range was "impressive", saying A5 was "usually reserved for classically trained [operatic sopranos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operatic_soprano)".[^cite_note-Ars_Technica-30] Ejae told *[Korea JoongAng Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_JoongAng_Daily)* that she "intentionally wrote a challenging vocal line" to "reflect both Rumi's vocal prowess and her fierce determination to seal the magical barrier of \[the] Honmoon", and said that she "just followed where the melody took me, and suddenly I was hitting an A5 note".[^cite_note-31] Co-director [Maggie Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_Kang) said that they "had Ejae sing the highest notes that she could possibly sing in a lot of the songs," including "Golden".[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-2025-32] *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* wrote that "it was important to Kang that the song embody 'the feeling that you get when you hear an artist, like, just put all of her talent and effort and that physical struggle into hitting a note'".[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-2025-32]

Co-director [Chris Appelhans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Appelhans) said the "I Want" song followed "the conventions of a traditional musical" while also "making it a legitimately great pop song" which "is probably why it's \[in the Top 10] on the worldwide [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify) charts", adding that "a good pop song also tells a story" which is what "Golden" is doing.[^cite_note-Slate_Interview-2025-33] In a [Reddit](3829005.md) "Ask Me Anything" interview, Appelhans said "Golden" was inspired by songs such as [Biggie's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G.) "[Juicy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juicy_\(The_Notorious_B.I.G._song\))" and "[Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forever_\(Drake,_Kanye_West,_Lil_Wayne,_and_Eminem_song\))" by [Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_\(musician\)), [Kanye West](523032.md), [Lil Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lil_Wayne), and [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem) that helped the writers "understand how a pop song could be a biography about starting as a nobody and finding yourself".[^cite_note-34]

## Critical reception

The song received critical acclaim. Debashree Dutta of *[Rolling Stone India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone_India)* called the song "an infectious [electropop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electropop) track", noting how it focuses on the purpose of the characters "while also reflecting Rumi's thoughts and the complexity of their role. Thematically, 'Golden' portrays their growth as guardians of humanity".[^cite_note-RollingStone-1] Similarly, Angela Garcia of *[SLUG Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLUG_Magazine)* noted the song, along with "[Your Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Your_Idol)", as energetic tracks with "infectious melodies".[^cite_note-SLUG_Mag-2025-35] Sarah Carey of *That Hashtag Show* described it as a passionately emotional song that listeners "play when \[they] need to remind \[themselves] who \[they] are."[^cite_note-36] Anne Branigin of *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* commented that its "popularity can be measured not just in its streaming numbers but also in the scores of covers (dance, vocal and instrumental) and passionate [lip-synching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lip-synching) videos that it has inspired on [TikTok](56822861.md) and [Instagram](31591547.md)", noting that "even real-life K-pop idols are fans, sharing their own interpretations of the dances and songs".[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-2025-32] Branigin also highlighted that the song has accomplished "a rare feat for a K-pop song" by "even being played on [American radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_radio)"; during August 1–7, its radio airplay audience impressions "surged more than 70 percent".[^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-2025-32][^cite_note-BillboardAug16-37]

In a *Billboard* discussion on the success of "Golden" compared to other songs on the soundtrack, Jason Lipshutz felt that the song's trajectory as the "standout hit from the soundtrack" was "understandable", since it is the "unifying anthem for \[Huntrix] with soaring melodies and chest-thumping inspiration".[^cite_note-Billboard_5_Burning_Questions-38] Lipshutz further explained that it "has become a single-song representation of the *KPop Demon Hunters* phenomenon, as a highly positive, musically undeniable piece of pop songwriting; it was designed as a smash in the fictional world of the movie, but was strong enough to become one in the real world, too".[^cite_note-Billboard_5_Burning_Questions-38] Abby Webster commented that as the film's "I Want" song, "Golden" is the clear "centerpiece of the soundtrack, but it also does a great job threading the needle between pop and narrative songwriting". She stated that the song is "a really emotionally satisfying song" regardless of having seeing the associated film, though it is "heightened if you have".[^cite_note-Billboard_5_Burning_Questions-38]

Chris Molanphy of *[Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_\(magazine\))* called the song "well-crafted inspirational pop" and felt that while "soaring" was a "cliché" description, "soaring is quite literally what 'Golden' is about, in its musical structure as well as in the lyrics of its chorus".[^cite_note-Slate-2025-39] Molanphy also described "Golden" as "an *[American Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Idol)* coronation song or a Disney 'I want ...' song run through the K-pop machine, an empowerment anthem tailored for fans to relate to it both individually and parasocially—a big theme running through \[*KPop Demon Hunters*], the movie, wherein pop stars' fans are their most precious asset".[^cite_note-Slate-2025-39] He opined that "Golden" and the rest of the film's soundtrack saved us "from the summer of our discontent" by injecting a pop surprise into an otherwise stagnant 2025 musical landscape "clogged with aging holdovers from 2024" and the chart reign of "[Alex Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Warren)'s snoozy, churchy ballad '[Ordinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_\(Alex_Warren_song\))'".[^cite_note-Slate-2025-39] Mackenzie Schmidt of *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_\(magazine\))* similarly commented that "Golden" has been "ticking every box required of a '[song of the summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_the_summer)'", noting the song "sparks that rare giddy, new music feeling, where you know you're going to put it on repeat and the first notes in your headphones are an instant hit of serotonin".[^cite_note-SS-40] Schmidt also highlighted that it has "captivated a massive and incredibly diverse fandom".[^cite_note-SS-40] Aimee Hart of *[Polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon_\(website\))* commented that compared to the original version, the "Glowin' Version" is "a much softer, melodic performance, and the harmonies shared between Ejae, Nuna, and Ami feel far more distinct and almost ethereal".[^cite_note-41]

## Commercial performance

See also: [List of K-pop songs on the Billboard charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_K-pop_songs_on_the_Billboard_charts), [List of Billboard Global 200 number ones of 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Billboard_Global_200_number_ones_of_2025), and [List of Billboard Hot 100 number ones of 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Billboard_Hot_100_number_ones_of_2025)

In addition to the film's successful soundtrack, "Golden" attained individual commercial success worldwide, charting on various national record charts and garnering millions of streams. The song reached number one on both the [*Billboard* Global 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Global_200) and the [*Billboard* Global Excl. US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Global_Excl._US) charts during the week of July 19,[^cite_note-Global200NumOne-108] holding the top position for a total of eighteen non-consecutive weeks between July 19 – December 13.[^cite_note-109]

### North America

According to *Billboard*, "Golden" garnered seven million U.S. streams from June 27 to June 30, a 272% increase from the same period during the previous week.[^cite_note-Billboard_Lipshutz-2025a-110] In the first week of July 2025, the song debuted at number 81 on the U.S. [*Billboard* Hot 100](423161.md) and number 63 on the [Canadian Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Hot_100).[^cite_note-Billboard_Global_200_Jul_1-111][^cite_note-112] Two weeks later, the song entered the top ten of both charts.[^cite_note-BillboardNumSix-113][^cite_note-Canada819-114] The song later rose to number one in both countries.[^cite_note-CanadaHot100-115][^cite_note-BillboardAug16-37][^cite_note-116] *Billboard* noted that this was a milestone in multiple categories, as Huntrix is the first in the U.S. to reach number one "for all-women collectives of three or more members" since [Destiny's Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destiny's_Child) with "[Bootylicious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bootylicious)" in 2001, the first fictional act to reach number one since "[We Don't Talk About Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Don't_Talk_About_Bruno)" in 2022, and "'Golden' is the ninth song associated with Korean pop to conquer the Hot 100" as well as "the first by female lead vocalists";[^cite_note-BillboardAug16-37] *[Vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_\(website\))* highlighted that those eight other K-pop songs had either been [BTS](39862325.md) songs or solos by BTS members.[^cite_note-117] In its fifth non-consecutive week at number one on the Hot 100, the song surpassed [The Chipmunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chipmunks)' "[The Chipmunk Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chipmunk_Song)" and [The Archies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Archies)' "[Sugar, Sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar,_Sugar)" (both of which spent four weeks atop in 1958 and 1969 respectively) to become the longest-leading hit by an animated act.[^cite_note-118]

On July 3, the song reached number two on the US Spotify daily chart, with the [BBC](19344654.md) highlighting how it "surpass\[ed] Blackpink as the highest-charting female K-pop group".[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] Five days later, on July 8, "Golden" topped the US Spotify daily chart, causing Huntrix to become the first female K-pop group to have a song do so.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] The song debuted at number 7 on the US [Streaming Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_Songs) chart – "Golden" is the first song "credited to a fictional act (Huntr/x) to reach the top 10 in the Streaming Songs tally's 12-year history" and "also the first song from an animated film to reach the Streaming Songs top 10 since tracks from *[Encanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encanto_\(soundtrack\))*" (2021) were on the list.[^cite_note-Billboard_on_Hot_100_Jul-12-124]

### Asia

In Asia, "Golden" peaked at number one on the [International Federation of the Phonographic Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_the_Phonographic_Industry)'s (IFPI) [Official Malaysia Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Malaysia_Chart),[^cite_note-Malaysia-125] and Singapore's [Official Singapore Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_Singapore#Charts).[^cite_note-RIAS-126] In the Philippines, the song peaked at number two on *[Billboard Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Philippines)*' [Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_Hot_100).[^cite_note-Billboard_Philippines-127] In South Korea, "Golden" charted at number one on the [Digital Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_Digital_Chart) and debuted at number two on the weekly Global K-pop Chart, both published by [Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_Chart).[^cite_note-Circle_Chart-128][^cite_note-129] The song also became the third to achieve a 2025 [perfect all-kill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_all-kill) in South Korea,[^cite_note-130] and later broke the record for the most hourly perfect all-kills of all time.[^cite_note-PAK-songs-131] In Japan, the song peaked at number seven on the [Japan Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Hot_100).[^cite_note-JapanHot100-132] According to [Oricon Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oricon_Chart), 51,073 digital copies of the song were sold as of April 5, 2026.[^cite_note-133]

### Europe

In Germany, "Golden" debuted at number 27 on the [GfK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GfK_Entertainment_charts)'s Top 100 Singles chart. The following week, it entered the top ten at number nine, and later topped the chart in the week of August 8.[^cite_note-GfK-134] In Austria, the song debuted at number 30 on the [Ö3 Austria Top 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%963_Austria_Top_40). It later held the runner-up spot for five consecutive weeks before then peaking at number one for seven weeks.[^cite_note-Austria-135] In Switzerland, the song debuted at number 39 on the [Swiss Hitparade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Hitparade). It later reached number two for seven consecutive weeks before dethroning [Alex Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Warren)'s "[Ordinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_\(Alex_Warren_song\))", ending its 25-week run at number one, where it remained for three weeks.[^cite_note-HitparadeSwiss-136] In Ireland, the song peaked at number two on the [Irish Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Singles_Chart).[^cite_note-Ireland4-137] It also appeared on the IFPI chart in Norway, reaching number one.[^cite_note-IFPI_Norge-138] The song additionally topped Sweden's [Sverigetopplistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverigetopplistan),[^cite_note-Sverigetopplistan-139] the [Single Top 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_Top_100) in the Netherlands,[^cite_note-Dutch100-140] and the [Top 100 Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Productores_de_M%C3%BAsica_de_Espa%C3%B1a#Charts) chart in Spain.[^cite_note-Spain100-141]

In the United Kingdom, the song reached number one on the [UK Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart) the week beginning August 1, becoming the second K-pop song in history to achieve this feat after [Psy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psy)'s "[Gangnam Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangnam_Style)", as well as the first song by a fictional band since "[The Official BBC Children in Need Medley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Official_BBC_Children_in_Need_Medley)" in 2009 to reach number one.[^cite_note-142] The song subsequently spent ten non-consecutive weeks atop the chart, surpassing [The Archies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Archies)' "[Sugar, Sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar,_Sugar)" as the longest-running number one single by a fictional act.[^cite_note-143]

### Oceania

In Australia, "Golden" debuted on the [Australian Recording Industry Association](308882.md)'s (ARIA) Top 50 Singles Chart at number eight.[^cite_note-144] The song also debuted at number five on New Zealand's [Top 40 Singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Aotearoa_Music_Charts),[^cite_note-145] later rising to number one in both countries.[^cite_note-Australian_Recording_Industry_Association-2025-146][^cite_note-Recorded_Music_NZ-2025-147]
