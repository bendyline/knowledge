**Reformists Front** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): جبهه اصلاح‌طلبان), formerly called **Democracy Front** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): جبهه مردمسالاری)[^cite_note-4] and **Front for Consolidation of Democracy** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): جبهه تحکیم دموکراسی),[^cite_note-RFU-2] is an Iranian [reformist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_reform_movement) [political alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_alliance) of "little-known parties" formerly pivoted by [Democracy Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Party_\(Iran\)).[^cite_note-Iran_newspaper-5]

## History

### 2008 parliamentary election

| --- | --- |

| [Spokesperson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_spokesperson) | [Fatemeh Karroubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatemeh_Karroubi)[^cite_note-ISNA-6] |
| Vice President | Ghodratali Heshmatian[^cite_note-Mehr-7] |
| Slogan | [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): همکاری ملی و اصلاحات مردمی برای بهبود زندگی "*National Cooperation and Popular Reforms to Improve Life*"[^cite_note-ISNA-6] |

In [2008 Iranian legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Iranian_legislative_election), they formed "Popular Coalition of Reforms", consisting of 14 groups.[^cite_note-Mehr-7] [Mostafa Kavakebian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Kavakebian) and [Fatemeh Karroubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatemeh_Karroubi) were key figures of the alliance.[^cite_note-ISNA-6] Other than [Democracy Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Party_\(Iran\)), [Islamic Assembly of Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Assembly_of_Women) and some [Islamic Iran Solidarity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Iran_Solidarity_Party) politicians—not the party itself—were notable members of the coalition.[^cite_note-Mehr-7] The coalition was allied with [National Trust Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trust_Party_\(Iran\)) and [Moderation and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderation_and_Development_Party) in the election and competed with [Council for Coordinating the Reforms Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_Coordinating_the_Reforms_Front)'s "Reformists Coalition: Friends of Khatami" list, as well as conservative [United Front of Principlists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Front_of_Principlists) and [Principlists Pervasive Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principlists_Pervasive_Coalition).[^cite_note-Iran_newspaper-5][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

### 2012 parliamentary election

Led by [Mostafa Kavakebian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Kavakebian), in the [2012 Iranian legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Iranian_legislative_election) they were renamed to "Reformists Front", a name similar to the "[Reforms Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_coordinating_the_Reforms_Front)"[^cite_note-BBC_Persian-10]—which decided not to participate in the election.[^cite_note-11] Some reformist groups ruled out any cooperation with the Reformists Front, and some even labeled them as “fake reformists” ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): اصلاح‌طلبان بدلی).[^cite_note-12] In [Tehran, Rey, Shemiranat and Eslamshahr electoral district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran,_Rey,_Shemiranat_and_Eslamshahr_\(electoral_district\)) they were allied with the [Worker House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worker_House) and endorsed 15 shared candidates. The two groups were the most notable reformist parties competing in the elections.[^cite_note-Iran_newspaper-5][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

### Democracy Party departure and front split

In early summer 2014, [Democracy Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Party_\(Iran\)) left the front in order to be able to remain in the [Council for Coordinating the Reforms Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_Coordinating_the_Reforms_Front). Following the exit, a split occurred in the front. One faction believed the Deputy Secretary-General [Mohammad-Javad Haghshenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad-Javad_Haghshenas) takes the secretary-general office, and the other faction elected Ali Faezi as the new secretary-general and retained Majid Mohtashami as spokesperson. On 26 October 2014, one faction tried to register the front in the [Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_\(Iran\)) and become the legitimate faction.[^cite_note-year-1][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] In October 2014, former leader [Mostafa Kavakebian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Kavakebian) criticized their decision to form a parallel reformist alliance in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Iranian_legislative_election), but defended their participation in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Iranian_legislative_election).[^cite_note-18]

### 2016 parliamentary election

The front issued a list for the [2016 Iranian legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Iranian_legislative_election) which was 100% overlapping with the [List of Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pervasive_Coalition_of_Reformists).[^cite_note-19]

### 2025-2026 Iranian protests

According to Iran International, amid concerns over the collapse of the Islamic Republic and the fall of Ali Khamenei, in addition to the [increasing number of protesters killed by security forces](82036668.md) during the [2025-2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md), the Central Council of the [Iranian Reform Front](50063989.md) held an emergency meeting behind closed doors, at the conclusion of which a statement was drafted, calling for the resignation of Khamenei and the transmission of state control to a "transitional council".[^cite_note-\:10-20] However, upon learning of the statement, security forces issued "heavy and blatant" threats to the leaders of the alliance as well as warnings of widespread arrests, and the statement was withdrawn.[^cite_note-\:10-20] On 25 January, Front spokesman Mohammad Javad Emam stated: "Today, more than ever, reformists must stand with the people, because the status quo can no longer be approved or justified."[^cite_note-21] On 30 January, the Front threatened to suspend its activities if the authorities refused to listen to the demands of protesters.[^cite_note-22] On 8 February 2026, Front leader [Azar Mansouri](54632737.md), along with fellow reformists [Ebrahim Asgharzadeh](7756323.md) and [Mohsen Aminzadeh](23312237.md) were arrested by Iranian authorities on charges of "targeting national unity, taking a stance against the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Iran), coordination with enemy propaganda, promoting surrender, diverting political groups and creating secret subversive mechanisms".[^cite_note-23]
