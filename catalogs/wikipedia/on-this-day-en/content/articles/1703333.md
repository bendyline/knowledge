**Al-Qassam Brigades**, also known as the **Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigades** (**EQB**;[^cite_note-Ezzedeen-15][^cite_note-18] [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): كتائب الشهيد عز الدين القسام, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Katāʾib ash-Shahīd ʿIzz ad-Dīn al-Qassām*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'The Battalions of the [Martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid) [Izz ad-Din al-Qassam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izz_ad-Din_al-Qassam)'), are the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) wing[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] of the [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [Islamist](15012.md) [Palestinian nationalist](47246228.md) organization, [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-ECFR-16][^cite_note-AA20210510-22][^cite_note-BBC20210513-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Led by [Mohammed Deif](92534.md) until his [death on 13 July 2024](77347017.md), Al-Qassam Brigades are the largest and best-equipped militant organization operating within the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) in recent years.[^cite_note-ECFR-16]

Created in mid-1991,[^cite_note-About_Us-27] they were at the time concerned with blocking the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) negotiations.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] From 1994 to 2000, Al-Qassam Brigades have claimed responsibility for carrying out a number of attacks against [Israelis](181296.md).[^cite_note-ECFR-16]

At the beginning of the [Second Intifada](71717.md), the group became a central target of [Israel](9282173.md). Al-Qassam Brigades operated several cells in the [West Bank](33209.md). Hamas retained a forceful presence in the Gaza Strip, generally considered its stronghold. [Yahya Sinwar](53195047.md), Hamas political leader in the Gaza Strip from February 2017 to October 2024, was the main military leader in the Brigades in Gaza during the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] After [his killing](78140031.md), Sinwar was succeeded by his brother [Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Sinwar). Mohammed was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Gaza_European_Hospital_strikes) in May 2025 and was succeeded by [Izz al-Din al-Haddad](79997859.md), who was assassinated in May 2026. He was succeeded by [Mohammed Odeh](83210587.md), who was assassinated that same month.

Al-Qassam Brigades are explicitly listed as a terrorist organization by the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine%E2%80%93European_Union_relations),[^cite_note-councild-32] [Australia](67539.md),[^cite_note-AU-33] [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_New_Zealand),[^cite_note-NZ-r1373-terrorlist-34] [Egypt](31125607.md),[^cite_note-RTS20150131-35] and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_relations).[^cite_note-Legislation20000720-36][^cite_note-proscribed-terrorist-groups-or-organisations-37] Though not explicitly mentioning EQB, the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine%E2%80%93United_States_relations)[^cite_note-foreign-terrorist-organizations-38][^cite_note-State2005-39] and [Canada](5042916.md)[^cite_note-MEForum-40] have designated its parent entity, Hamas, as a terrorist organization;[^cite_note-PublicSafety2018-41] former Brigade leader Mohammed Deif was classified as a [Specially Designated Global Terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specially_Designated_Global_Terrorist) by the US under [Executive Order 13224](1296167.md).[^cite_note-Treasury2021-42][^cite_note-executive-order-13224-43]

In July 2026, US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) announced that his [Board of Peace](81274522.md) had reached an agreement to dissolve the Brigades.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

## Overview



[Izz ad-Din al-Qassam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izz_ad-Din_al-Qassam), Syrian Muslim preacher and leader in Arab nationalist resistance to British and French rule, a militant opponent of Zionism in the 1920s and 1930s

The Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades are the military wing of the [Palestinian](47246228.md) organization [Hamas](13913.md), operating in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md).[^cite_note-ECFR-16][^cite_note-AA20210510-22] They were led by [Mohammed Deif](92534.md) and, before his death on 10 March 2024, his deputy, [Marwan Issa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwan_Issa).[^cite_note-ECFR-16]

Al-Qassam Brigades were named after [Izz ad-Din al-Qassam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izz_ad-Din_al-Qassam), a [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) preacher and *[mujahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahid)* who fought in [Syria](7515849.md), [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_resistance_movement), and [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine).[^cite_note-About_Us-27][^cite_note-segev-46] In 1930, al-Qassam organised and established the [Black Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hand_\(Mandatory_Palestine\)), a militant organisation that was opposed to [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) and British and French rule in the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant).[^cite_note-segev-46] Before dying in a shootout with the [Palestine Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Police_Force) in 1935, al-Qassam exhorted his followers to embrace martyrdom and fight until the last bullet, which turned him into a role model for Palestinian nationalists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlumenthal201529-47]

## Relation to political wing; commanders

The Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigades are an integral part of Hamas. While they are subordinate to Hamas's broad political goals and its ideological objectives, they have a significant level of independence in decision making.[^cite_note-AU-33]

In 1997, political scientists Ilana Kass and Bard O'Neill described Hamas' relationship with the Brigades as reminiscent of [Sinn Féin](28175.md)'s relationship to the [Provisional Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army) (IRA) and quoted a senior Hamas official: "The Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigade is a separate armed military wing, which has its own leaders who do not take their orders \[from Hamas] and do not tell us of their plans in advance."[^cite_note-48]

Carrying the IRA analogy further, Kass and O'Neill argued that the separation of the political and military wings shielded Hamas' political leaders from responsibility for terrorism with the [plausible deniability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plausible_deniability) provided made Hamas an eligible representative for peace negotiations as had happened with Sinn Féin politician [Gerry Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Adams).[^cite_note-49]

The fighters' identities and positions in the group often remain secret until their death. Even when they fight against Israeli incursions, all the militiamen wear a characteristic black hood on which the group's green headband is attached. The Brigades operate on a model of independent cells. Even high-ranking members are often unaware of the activities of other cells. This allows the group to constantly regenerate after member deaths.[^cite_note-Asser2002BBC-50]

During the [Second Intifada](71717.md), the leaders of the group were targeted by numerous airstrikes that killed many members, including [Salah Shehade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_Shehade) and [Adnan al-Ghoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adnan_al-Ghoul). The former leader of the Brigades, [Mohammed Deif](92534.md), survived at least seven assassination attempts.[^cite_note-Asser2002BBC-50]

## History

### Background

In 1984, Sheikh [Ahmed Yassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Yassin), [Ibrahim al-Makhadmeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_al-Makadmeh), Sheikh [Salah Shehada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_Shehade), and others began preparing for the establishment of an armed organization to resist [Israeli control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Occupation_of_Palestine), with a focus on acquiring weapons for future [resistance activities](2646043.md). Members of the group were, however, arrested and the weapons were confiscated.[^cite_note-About_Us-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHussein202184-54]

In 1986, Shehada formed a network of resistance cells, called *al-Mujahidun al-Filastiniun* ('Palestinian fighters'), who targeted Israeli troops and "traitors." This network operated until 1989, with their most famous operation being the 1989 kidnapping and killing of two Israeli soldiers: [Avi Sasportas and Ilan Saadon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Avi_Sasportas_and_Ilan_Saadon).[^cite_note-About_Us-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENajibFriedrich2007105-55]

Hamas was officially established on 14 December 1987, forming other similar networks as *al-Mujahidun al-Filastiniun*, such as the [Abdullah Azzam Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Azzam_Brigades).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENajibFriedrich2007105-55] In the summer of 1991, during the [First Palestinian Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada) (1987–1994), the Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades were established, with their first act being the assassination of the rabbi of [Kfar Darom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kfar_Darom).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENajibFriedrich2007105-55]

### Contemporary operations and activities

The EQB's transition to a recognised militant organisation began during the establishment of the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) to assist [Hamas](13913.md) efforts in blocking them.[^cite_note-56]

The year 2004 was pivotal in the development of Al-Qassam Brigades from a loosely-formed militia, into a structured organization with a defined chain of command.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAviad20094-57] The [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF)'s assassinations of local leaders [Ahmed Yassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Yassin) and [Abdel Aziz al-Rantisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Aziz_al-Rantisi) resulted in decision-making power being transferred to leaders exiled in Damascus, which ultimately led to greater influence and funding from Syria, Iran, and Hezbollah.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHussein202191-58][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAviad20094-57]

The Gaza strip was divided into six or seven regional divisions, headed by a division commander with responsibility over defined sectors of territory.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAviad20094-57] Each division commander oversaw regiment commanders and company commanders, who were responsible for small areas such as neighborhoods.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAviad20094-57] A focus [on tunnel warfare](43413500.md) was selected as a primary means of combating the IDF.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAviad20094-57]

On 3 August 2004, the first [Yasin missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasin_\(RPG\))–a homebrew anti-tank [rocket-propelled grenade](26286.md)–was launched.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHussein202193-59] The group developed other homemade weapons, such as rocket launchers ([al-Bana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bana), [Batar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batar)) and the [Qassam rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qassam_rocket).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStrazzariTholens2010126-60][^cite_note-61]



Qassam rockets launched from Gaza, on display at an Israeli police station at Sderot, 2009.

In 2003 and 2004, the Brigades in Gaza resisted incursions by the Israel Defense Forces (IDF), including the [siege of Jabalya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_days_of_penitence) in October 2004.

In 2005, as President [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md) had taken direct control of the [PA security forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Security_Services), which were loyal to the president's [Fatah](11104.md) movement, the Hamas government in the Gaza Strip formed a separate 3,000-strong paramilitary police force, called the [Executive Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Security_Services#Executive_Force),[^cite_note-62] consisting of Al-Qassam Brigades members.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

In June 2006, Al-Qassam Brigades were involved in the operation which led to the capture of Israeli soldier [Gilad Shalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilad_Shalit).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChehab200767-67] Al-Qassam Brigades engaged in heavy fighting in the Gaza Strip during [Operation Summer Rains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Summer_Rains), launched by the IDF. It was the first time in over 18 months that the brigades were actively involved in fighting against Israeli soldiers. In May 2007, the brigades acknowledged they lost 192 fighters during the operation.[^cite_note-68]

In January 2007, Abbas outlawed the Executive Force and ordered that its then-6,000 members be incorporated into the PA security forces under his command. The order was resisted by the Hamas government,[^cite_note-69] which instead announced plans to double the size of the force to 12,000 men.[^cite_note-70] Al-Qassam Brigades and the Executive Force took part in the [Hamas takeover of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gaza_\(2007\)) in June 2007.

In June 2008, Egypt brokered a ceasefire, which lasted until 4 November when Israeli forces crossed into Gaza and killed six Hamas fighters. This resulted in an increase in rocket attacks on Israel, going from two in September and October to 190 in November 2008. Both sides said the other had broken the truce.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeurat201953-71][^cite_note-72]

In July 2026, US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) said that his [Board of Peace](81274522.md) had reached an agreement to disarm Hamas and [other militant organisations in Gaza](75192468.md). Hamas leader [Ghazi Hamad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_Hamad) declared that "draft agreement" announced by Trump calls for the transfer of heavy weaponry in Gaza to a Palestinian administration following Israeli withdrawal from Gaza.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

## Organization

Al-Qassam Brigades are organized into formal military structures with established command hierarchies. They organize themselves from the [squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squad), all the way to the [brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade) level, similar to conventional militaries. Strategies centered on targeted killings to remove key Hamas leaders are often ineffective, as Hamas is capable of promoting low-ranking members to replace those killed.[^cite_note-ISW_ORBAT-76]

The forces are mainly divided into five brigades, divided geographically. Each brigade is divided into multiple battalions, with 30 total battalions. Each battalion is associated with a major settlement. They may be relocated and change their areas of responsibility during conflicts.[^cite_note-ISW_ORBAT-76]

### Recruitment

Hamas fighters are largely recruited from unemployed minors, aged under 18. About 50,000 Gazan youths under 18 registered for "security" training.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Recruitment is likely driven by the highest unemployment in the world, where 45% of Gazans are unemployed.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] Al-Qassam spokesman [Abu Obaida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Obaida) stated in a public speech in 2023 during the [Gaza–Israel conflict](16346266.md) that 85% of their recruits are orphans desiring revenge whose parents were killed by the Israeli Defense Forces.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

## Strength and armament

Since its establishment in December 1987, the military capability of the Brigades has increased markedly, from rifles to [Qassam rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qassam_rocket) and more.[^cite_note-washington-87] Although the Brigades manufacture their own weapons,[^cite_note-CNN:_Weapons-88] an [Associated Press](18935732.md) analysis found that the majority of their arms come from [China](5405.md), [Iran](14653.md), and [Russia](25391.md), as well as [North Korea](21255.md) and former [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) countries. The Associated Press was unable to determine whether Hamas obtains weapons directly from those nations or through [arms trafficking](42210.md).[^cite_note-89] China and North Korea have denied directly arming the Brigades,[^cite_note-APNKorea-90][^cite_note-91] but American, Israeli, and South Korean intelligence suggests that North Korea has,[^cite_note-APNKorea-90] while a Hamas official has stated the group received permission from Russia to copy its weapons. Iran is believed to smuggle weapons to the Brigades via [underground tunnels in Gaza](43413500.md).[^cite_note-CNN:_Weapons-88]

The Brigades run their own intelligence division.[^cite_note-ToI23-92]

The Brigades have a substantial inventory of light automatic weapons and [grenades](20353360.md), improvised rockets, [mortars](324499.md), [bombs](47628.md), [suicide belts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_belt), and [explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosives). The group engages in [military-style training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_training), including training that takes place in Gaza, on a range of weapons designed to inflict significant casualties on civilian and military targets.[^cite_note-93]



Al Qassam militants [rappelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappelling) during a training exercise in Gaza, January 2013

The Brigades have a variety of [anti-tank guided missiles](37517.md), including the [Kornet-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9M133_Kornet), [Konkurs-M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9M113_Konkurs), [Bulsae-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K111_Fagot) (North Korean version of Fagot), [9K11 Malyutka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K11_Malyutka) and [MILAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MILAN) missiles. They possess [shoulder-launched anti-aircraft missiles](1389714.md) (MANPADS), such as the [SA-7B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strela_2), [SA-18 Igla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K38_Igla) missiles, and it is believed a number of [SA-24 Igla-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K38_Igla) that it received from Libya.

While the number of members is known only to the Brigades leadership, in 2011, Israel estimated that the Brigades have a cadre of several hundred members who receive military-style training, including training in Iran and [Syria](7515849.md).[^cite_note-94] Additionally, the Brigades have an estimated 30,000 operatives "of varying degrees of skill and professionalism" who are members of the internal security forces, Hamas, and their supporters. These operatives can be expected to reinforce the Brigades in an "emergency situation."[^cite_note-95] Other sources estimate their strength at 30,000–50,000.[^cite_note-michelgoya-96][^cite_note-97] An October 2023 estimate provides a figure of 40,000 fighters, with expertise in cyber security, naval warfare, and other specializations.[^cite_note-98]

According to a statement by CIA director [George Tenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Tenet) in 2000, possibly referring to the Brigades, Hamas has pursued a capability to conduct [attacks with toxic chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_terrorism).[^cite_note-washington-87] There have been reports of Hamas operatives planning and preparing attacks incorporating chemicals. In one case, nails and bolts packed into explosives detonated by a Hamas suicide bomber in a December 2001 attack in [Ben-Yehuda Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Yehuda_Street) in [Jerusalem](16043.md) were soaked in [rat poison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat_poison).[^cite_note-washington-87] In 2014, they launched the first [Palestinian](241405.md) reconnaissance ([UAV](58900.md)) aircraft, called [Ababeel1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ababeel1).

### Gaza forces

During the Gaza war, the IDF published its intelligence about the Hamas military in the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-ToI23-92] They put the strength of the Qassam Brigades there at the start of the war at 30,000 fighters, organised by area in five brigades, consisting in total of 24 battalions and c. 140 companies. Each regional brigade had a number of strongholds and outposts, and included specialised arrays for rocket firing, anti-tank missiles, air defenses, snipers, and engineering.[^cite_note-ToI23-92]

On 8 January 2024, Israel discovered the largest known weapons factory of Hamas in Bureij. The site was opened for reporters by the IDF which contained, metal tubes and components as well as shell casings were stacked in an overground workshop area and long metal racks holding missiles could be seen. An elevator lead into a tunnel where rockets were stored and is connected to a tunnel network which allowed Hamas to transport rockets underground to launch sites.[^cite_note-99] The same month, Israel reported that it discovered a "massive" stockpile of Chinese weaponry used by Hamas.[^cite_note-100]

## Killed leaders

On 3 September 2005, after Israel's withdrawal from settlements in the Gaza Strip, Al-Qassam Brigades revealed for the first time the names and functions of its commanders on its website as well as in a printed bulletin distributed to Palestinians.[^cite_note-119] On 12 July 2006, the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) bombed a house in the [Sheikh Radwan](21912219.md) neighborhood of Gaza City, where Mohammed Deif, Ahmad al-Ghandur, and Raid Said were meeting. The three-story house was completely leveled, killing Hamas official Nabil al-Salmiah, his wife, their five children and two other children. Two of the three brigades leaders present escaped with moderate wounds. Deif received a spinal injury that required four hours of surgery.[^cite_note-120]

On 1 January 2009, [Nizar Rayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizar_Rayan), a top Hamas leader who served as a liaison between the Palestinian organization's political leadership and its military wing, was killed in an Israeli Air Force strike during [Operation Cast Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cast_Lead).[^cite_note-121] The day before the attack, Rayan had advocated renewal of [suicide attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palestinian_suicide_attacks) on Israel, declaring, "Our only language with the Jew is through the gun".[^cite_note-122] A 2,000-pound bomb was dropped on his house, also killing his 4 wives (Hiam 'Abdul Rahman Rayan, 46; Iman Khalil Rayan, 46; Nawal Isma'il Rayan, 40; and Sherine Sa'id Rayan, 25) and 11 of their children (As'ad, 2; Usama Ibn Zaid, 3; 'Aisha, 3; Reem, 4; Miriam, 5; Halima, 5; 'Abdul Rahman, 6; Abdul Qader, 12; Aaya, 12; Zainab, 15; and Ghassan, 16).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] On 3 January 2009, Israeli aircraft attacked the car in which Abu Zakaria al-Jamal, a leader of Izz ad-Din al-Qassam armed wing, was traveling. He died of the wounds suffered in the bombing.[^cite_note-127] The following day, the Israeli Air Force struck and killed in Khan Yunis two senior Brigrade leaders, Hussam Hamdan and Muhammad Hilo, both of whom the Israelis blamed for attacks against Israel. According to Israeli authorities Hamdan was in charge of rocket attacks against [Beersheba](312755.md) and [Ofakim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofakim), while Hilo was reportedly behind Hamas' special forces in Khan Yunis.[^cite_note-128] On 15 January 2009, the Israeli Air Force bombed a house in [Jabaliya](453723.md), killing a prominent Brigades commander named Mohammed Watfa. The strike targeted the Palestinian Interior Minister [Said Seyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_Seyam), who was also killed.[^cite_note-129]

On 30 July 2010, one of the leaders [Issa Abdul-Hadi Al-Batran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issa_Abdul-Hadi_Al-Batran), aged 40, was killed at the [Nuseirat refugee camp](18168164.md) in the central Gaza Strip by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-130] On 14 November 2012, [Ahmed Jaabari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Jaabari), the head of Al-Qassam Brigade, was killed along with seven others in Gaza, marking the beginning of Israel's "[Operation Pillar of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pillar_of_Defense)".[^cite_note-BBC20210513-23][^cite_note-131] On 21 August 2014, an Israeli air strike killed Muhammad Abu Shamala, the sub-commander of Southern Gaza Strip; [Raed al Atar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raed_al_Atar), the commander of the [Rafah](584373.md) company and member of the Hamas high military council; and Mohammed Barhoum.[^cite_note-132] On 30 January 2018, [Imad Al-Alami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imad_Al-Alami?action=edit\&redlink=1) died as a result of injuries sustained while he was inspecting his personal weapon in Gaza City.[^cite_note-133]

Hamas confirmed in November 2023 that Israeli airstrikes had killed [Ahmed Ghandour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Ghandour_\(militant\)), the commander of Al-Qassam Brigade in northern Gaza; Ayman Siam, head of the rocket-firing unit; and [Fursan Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fursan_Khalifa), a senior commander in the [West Bank](33209.md), as well as Ghandour's deputy Wael Rajab.[^cite_note-134] [Marwan Issa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwan_Issa), deputy leader of Al-Qassam Brigades and second-in-command to Mohammed Deif, was [killed by Israeli forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Marwan_Issa) on 10 March 2024.[^cite_note-135]

On 13 May 2025, the IDF and [Shin Bet](311752.md) said that [Mohammed Sinwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Sinwar), commander of the Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigades, had been targeted in an Israeli airstrike on a bunker under the [Gaza European Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_European_Hospital) in [Khan Yunis](101330.md). The strike killed 26 people, according to the [Gaza Health Ministry](52186562.md), but Sinwar's fate was unclear.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] According to the Saudi channel [Al-Hadath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlHadath), the bodies of Sinwar and [Muhammad Shabana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Shabana) were recovered from the tunnel. Israeli defense minister [Israel Katz](19041378.md) said that "according to all the indications Muhammad Sinwar was eliminated."[^cite_note-139] Senior Hamas official [Osama Hamdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_Hamdan) said that Hamas members in the Gaza Strip had told him that Sinwar was still alive.[^cite_note-140] On 28 May 2025, Israeli Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) said that Sinwar had been killed.[^cite_note-141] On 31 May 2025, the IDF and Shin Bet confirmed that Sinwar had been killed.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] On 8 June 2025, the IDF said they found Sinwar's body in an "underground passageway beneath the European Hospital" in Khan Yunis.[^cite_note-144]

On 15 May 2026, [Izz ad-Din al-Haddad](79997859.md), successor of Mohammed Sinwar as the leader and commander of the Al-Qassam Brigades, was killed in an Israeli air strike in Gaza City.[^cite_note-145] On 26 May, his successor, [Mohammed Odeh](83210587.md), was killed just days after his predecessor's death in a similar attack.[^cite_note-146]

## International response

The international community, and more specifically the [United Nations](13444990.md), considers the practice of war combatants to turn civilians into [human shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_shields) as a violation of the [Geneva Conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Conventions) standards of war,[^cite_note-a7-doubleWarCrime-147][^cite_note-CNN-doubleWarCrime-148][^cite_note-JP-dobleWarCrime-149] and considers indiscriminate attacks (e.g., by [rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qassam_rocket) or [suicide bombers](18835454.md))[^cite_note-rwkckb07-150] on civilian populations as [illegal under international law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_conventions#Protocols).[^cite_note-ICRC-humanShieldsArt51-151]

Al-Qassam Brigades are explicitly listed as a terrorist organization by the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine%E2%80%93European_Union_relations),[^cite_note-councild-32] [Australia](67539.md),[^cite_note-AU-33] [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_New_Zealand),[^cite_note-NZ-r1373-terrorlist-34] [Egypt](31125607.md),[^cite_note-RTS20150131-35] [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93Palestine_relations),[^cite_note-152] and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_relations).[^cite_note-Legislation20000720-36][^cite_note-proscribed-terrorist-groups-or-organisations-37] Though not explicitly mentioning EQB, [Argentina](18951905.md),[^cite_note-153] [Canada](5042916.md),[^cite_note-MEForum-40] the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine%E2%80%93United_States_relations),[^cite_note-foreign-terrorist-organizations-38][^cite_note-State2005-39] and [Paraguay](23420.md)[^cite_note-154] have designated its parent entity, Hamas, as a terrorist organization;[^cite_note-PublicSafety2018-41] Brigade leader Mohammed Deif has also been classified as a [Specially Designated Global Terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specially_Designated_Global_Terrorist) by the US under [Executive Order 13224](1296167.md).[^cite_note-Treasury2021-42][^cite_note-executive-order-13224-43]

## Military media



An [inverted red triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_triangle_\(Palestinian_symbol\)) has been used as a "signature" by the Qassam Brigades to highlight combat vehicles and ground forces that are about to be targeted

After Israel launched the ground invasion of Gaza on 27 October 2023, the Qassam Brigades publicized many of their ambushes against Israeli vehicles on their military media for the battle of al-Aqsa Flood. Most of these videos, shot mainly from the fighters' point of view through a [GoPro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GoPro) camera involve the ambush of vehicles, targeted by rockets before the militiamen retreat to their bases, though footage of sniper operations and targeting ground forces has been published as well.

Targets were highlighted with a flashing [inverted red triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_triangle_\(Palestinian_symbol\)).[^cite_note-155] Due to the nature of these attacks, being hit and run and militiamen turning away immediately after the round has been shot, as well as the Israeli vehicles [Trophy APS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trophy_APS), it has been called into question how effective these attacks were and how many tanks were successful hit instead of the rockets being intercepted. In spite of this, the Qassam brigades have publicised videos on their military media showing successful hits where plates are seen being torn off tanks after being hit by rockets, or the aftermath of their ambushes showing ignited vehicles,[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] as well as captured uniforms and weapons from the IDF. videos in July 2024 also show the Qassam Brigades destroying and igniting a Namer and a Merkava, which were both equipped with a trophy APS.[^cite_note-158]
