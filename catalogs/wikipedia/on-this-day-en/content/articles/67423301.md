[General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_\(United_Kingdom\)) **Sir Charles Roland Vincent Walker** (born 14 May 1970) is a senior [British Army](4887.md) officer, who has served as [Chief of the General Staff](691314.md), the professional head of the British Army, since 15 June 2024.[^cite_note-1] Walker previously served as [Deputy Chief of the Defence Staff (Military Strategy and Operations)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Chief_of_the_Defence_Staff#Deputy_Chief_of_the_Defence_Staff_\(Military_Strategy_and_Operations\)) from April 2021 until June 2024.[^cite_note-2]

## Early life and education

Walker was born on 14 May 1970 in [Nairobi](21482.md), Kenya, to Patrick Walker and Daphne Walker (née Armour).[^cite_note-WW_24-3] His mother later married [Frederick Conyngham, 7th Marquess Conyngham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Conyngham,_7th_Marquess_Conyngham), in 1980.[^cite_note-WW_24-3] He was educated at the [Dragon School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_School), a [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_schools_in_the_United_Kingdom) [preparatory school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparatory_school_\(United_Kingdom\)) in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), and then at [Harrow School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow_School), an all-boys independent [boarding school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boarding_school) in London. Sponsored by the [British Army](4887.md) as a [university cadetship officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadet),[^cite_note-LG_52323-4] he studied at the [Royal Agricultural College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Agricultural_College), graduating with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree.[^cite_note-WW_21-5] He later studied at [Cranfield University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranfield_University), graduating with a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degree.[^cite_note-WW_24-3]

## Military career

Walker was commissioned into the [Irish Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Guards) as a [second lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_lieutenant) (on probation) on 9 September 1990 as part of his undergraduate cadetship.[^cite_note-LG_52323-4] In September 1993, his commission was confirmed and he was promoted to [lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_\(British_Army_and_Royal_Marines\)) back-dated to 11 August 1992.[^cite_note-6] He began active service in the Irish Guards in 1993, and saw operational tours in Northern Ireland and Iraq.[^cite_note-WW_21-5] He was promoted to [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(British_Army_and_Royal_Marines\)) on 11 August 1995.[^cite_note-7] In 1997, he joined [22 Special Air Service Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22_Special_Air_Service_Regiment).[^cite_note-8] He was promoted to [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_\(United_Kingdom\)) on 30 September 2000.[^cite_note-9] He attended the [Advanced Command and Staff Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Command_and_Staff_Course) from 2001 to 2003.[^cite_note-WW_24-3] Between 2003 and 2007, he undertook multiple [operational tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_tour) in Iraq.[^cite_note-WW_24-3] He was promoted to [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel_\(United_Kingdom\)) on 30 June 2008.[^cite_note-10]

Following his time with Special Forces, Walker transferred from the Irish Guards to the [Grenadier Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenadier_Guards) on 11 November 2008.[^cite_note-LG_8_September_2009-11] He served as [commanding officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanding_officer) of the 1st Battalion, Grenadier Guards from 2008 to 2010.[^cite_note-WW_21-5][^cite_note-Portraits-12] With the Grenadiers, he served a tour in Afghanistan, during which the [Ridgeback PPV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar_\(MRAP\)) in which he was travelling was blown up by an [improvised explosive device](265112.md) (IED): all six soldiers inside escaped uninjured, but the vehicle was thrown into the air, had its wheels blown off and its armour shredded.[^cite_note-Portraits-12] Walker was appointed a [Companion of the Distinguished Service Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_the_Distinguished_Service_Order) (DSO) in September 2010 in recognition of gallant and distinguished services in Afghanistan during the period 1 October 2009 to 31 March 2010.[^cite_note-dso-13] He was promoted to [colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_\(United_Kingdom\)) on 30 June 2012.[^cite_note-LG_3_July_2012-14]

Walker commanded the [12th Armoured Infantry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Armoured_Infantry_Brigade_\(United_Kingdom\)) from 2013 to 2015,[^cite_note-WW_21-5] and was promoted to [brigadier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier_\(United_Kingdom\)) on 30 June 2014.[^cite_note-15] He has since held staff appointments at [Army Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Headquarters_\(United_Kingdom\)) and the [Ministry of Defence](203793.md).[^cite_note-WW_21-5] He was promoted to [major general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major-general_\(United_Kingdom\)) on 8 March 2018,[^cite_note-16] and appointed [Director Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_Special_Forces).[^cite_note-17] He was promoted to [lieutenant general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-general_\(United_Kingdom\)) and became [Deputy Chief of Defence Staff (Military Strategy and Operations)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Chief_of_the_Defence_Staff) on 16 April 2021.[^cite_note-18] He was appointed a [Knight Commander of the Order of the Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_Bath) (KCB) in the [2023 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_New_Year_Honours), thereby granted the title [sir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir).[^cite_note-kcb-19]



Walker (left) on a visit to the United States in 2023

Walker served as [Colonel Commandant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_Commandant) of the [Royal Army Veterinary Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Army_Veterinary_Corps) from 1 March 2017 to 1 June 2022,[^cite_note-20] and Regimental Lieutenant Colonel of the Grenadier Guards from 18 June 2017 to 18 June 2022; both ceremonial appointments.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] He was Honorary Colonel of the Cambridge University Officers' Training Corps until June 2024.[^cite_note-23] He was appointed Colonel Commandant of the [Honourable Artillery Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honourable_Artillery_Company) on 30 May 2024.[^cite_note-24]

### Chief of the General Staff

It was announced in December 2023 that Walker had been selected to be the next [Chief of the General Staff](691314.md), the professional head of the British Army, and would take up the post in June 2024.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] He took up the post on 16 June 2024. On the same day, he was promoted to [general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_\(United_Kingdom\)) and appointed [Aide-de-camp general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aide-de-camp_general) to the King.[^cite_note-27]

On 23 July 2024, Walker warned of the threat posed by [Russia](25391.md), [China](5405.md), [Iran](14653.md) and [North Korea](21255.md).[^cite_note-28] He said the UK must be prepared for a possible [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_III) with Russia by 2027.[^cite_note-29]

Walker said there were "no winners" in Russia's [invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), adding that "it is an utter devastation for both sides and lost generations."[^cite_note-30] He said that with the current way of fighting, it would take Russia five years to control the four regions of Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson and Zaporizhia that [Russia claims as its own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts), and it would cost Russia more than 1.5 million [casualties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casualties_of_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War).[^cite_note-31] He said: "It doesn’t matter how it ends. I think Russia will emerge from it probably weaker objectively – or absolutely – but still very, very dangerous and wanting some form of retribution for what we have done to help Ukraine."[^cite_note-32]

In 2025, he was considered for the appointment of [Chief of the Defence Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Defence_Staff_\(United_Kingdom\)), the professional head of the British Armed Forces, but came runner up to [Sir Rich Knighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Rich_Knighton).[^cite_note-33]

## Personal life

In 1998, Walker married Kate White-Thomson. They have three daughters and live in [Herefordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herefordshire).[^cite_note-WW_21-5]
