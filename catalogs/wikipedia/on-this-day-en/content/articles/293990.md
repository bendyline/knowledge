A **seaside resort** is a [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_town), village, or hotel that serves its purpose as a [vacation resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort) and is located on a [coast](5236.md). Sometimes the concept includes an aspect of an official accreditation based on the satisfaction of certain requirements such as in the German *Seebad*.[^cite_note-1] Where a [beach](40630.md) is the primary focus for [tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist), it may be referred to as a **beach resort**.

## History



*Brighton, The Front and the Chain Pier Seen in the Distance*, an early 19th century watercolour painting of [Brighton](49796.md), a seaside resort in [East Sussex](67923.md), England



[Barcola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcola) in [Northeast Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Italy), a holiday seaside resort



A c. 1841 illustration [Heiligendamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiligendamm) in [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg), [Germany](11867.md), established in 1793, the oldest seaside resort in [continental Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Europe)

Seaside resorts have existed since antiquity. In [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) times, the town of [Baiae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiae) by the [Tyrrhenian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrrhenian_Sea) in [Italy](14532.md) was a resort for those who were sufficiently prosperous.[^cite_note-2] [Barcola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcola) by the [Adriatic Sea](23275478.md) in northern Italy with its Roman luxury villas is considered a special example of ancient leisure culture by the sea.[^cite_note-3] [Mersea Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersea_Island) in [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex), [England](9316.md) was a seaside holiday destination for wealthy ancient Romans living in [Colchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchester).[^cite_note-4]

The development of the beach as a popular leisure resort from the mid-19th century was the first manifestation of what is now the global tourist industry. The first seaside resorts were opened in the 18th century for the aristocracy, who began to frequent the seaside as well as the then fashionable spa towns, for recreation and health.[^cite_note-The_business_of_tourism-5] One of the earliest such seaside resorts was [Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_North_Yorkshire) in [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire) during the 1720s; it had been a popular spa town since a stream of acidic water was discovered running from one of the cliffs to the south of the town in the 17th century.[^cite_note-The_business_of_tourism-5] The first rolling [bathing machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathing_machine) were introduced by 1735.

In 1793, [Heiligendamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiligendamm) in [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg), [Germany](11867.md) was founded as the first seaside resort of the European continent, which successfully attracted Europe's aristocracy to the [Baltic Sea](3335.md).[^cite_note-nyt-6]

The opening of the resort in [Brighton](49796.md) and its reception of [royal patronage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronage) from King [George IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_IV) extended the seaside as a resort for health and pleasure to the much larger [London](17867.md) market, and the beach became a centre for upper-class pleasure and frivolity. This trend was praised and artistically elevated by the new [romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism) ideal of the picturesque landscape; [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen)'s unfinished novel *[Sanditon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanditon)* is an example of that. Later, [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria)'s long-standing patronage of the [Isle of Wight](15102.md) and [Ramsgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsgate) in [Kent](16766.md) ensured that a seaside residence was considered a highly fashionable possession for those wealthy enough to afford more than one home.

### Seaside resorts for the middle and working classes



The [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool) promenade in [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire), [England](9316.md), c. 1898

The extension of this form of leisure to the middle and working classes began with the development of the railways in the 1840s; they offered cheap travel to fast-growing resort towns. In particular, the branch line to the small seaside town of [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool) from [Poulton-le-Fylde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poulton-le-Fylde) led to a sustained economic and demographic boom. A sudden influx of visitors arriving by rail motivated entrepreneurs to build accommodation and create new attractions, leading to more visitors and rapid growth throughout the 1850s and 1860s.[^cite_note-7]

The growth was intensified by the practice among the Lancashire [cotton mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_mill) owners of closing the factories for a week every year to service and repair machinery. These became known as [wakes weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakes_week). Each town's mills would close for a different week, allowing Blackpool to manage a steady and reliable stream of visitors over a prolonged period in the summer. A prominent feature of the resort was the [promenade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promenade) and the [pleasure piers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleasure_pier), where an eclectic variety of performances vied for the people's attention. In 1863, the [North Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pier,_Blackpool) in Blackpool was completed, rapidly becoming a centre of attraction for elite visitors. [Central Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Pier,_Blackpool) was completed in 1868, with a theatre and a large open-air dance floor.[^cite_note-Rough597-8]

Many popular beach resorts were equipped with [bathing machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathing_machine), because even the all-covering [beachwear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimsuit) of the period was considered immodest.

By the end of the century the English coastline had over 100 large resort towns, some with populations exceeding 50,000.[^cite_note-9]

### Expansion around the world



A seaside façade in [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo) in [Monaco](19261.md) in the 1870s



The [strandkorb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strandkorb) became a symbol of seaside tourism by the end of the 19th century, especially on the southern [Baltic Sea](3335.md) coast

The development of the seaside resort abroad was stimulated by the well-developed [English](18803164.md) love of the beach. The [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera) on the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) had already become a destination for the British upper class by the end of the 18th century. In 1864, the first railway to [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) was completed, making the Riviera accessible to visitors from all over Europe. By 1874, foreign residents in Nice, mostly British, numbered 25,000. The coastline became renowned for attracting the royalty of Europe, including [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) and King [Edward VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VII).[^cite_note-10]

In the [United States](3434750.md), early seaside resorts in the late 1800s catered to the wealthy, including city businessmen. [Cape May, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_May,_New_Jersey) became one of the first coastal resorts in the United States, when regular steamboat traffic on the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) began after the War of 1812. Early visitors to Cape May included [Henry Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Clay) in 1847, and [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) in 1849. By 1880, [Henry Flagler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Flagler) had extended several rail lines southward down the US Atlantic coastline, enticing northern upper-class families south to subtropical Florida. The [Florida East Coast Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_East_Coast_Railway) brought northern tourists to [St. Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida) in greater numbers, and by 1887 Flagler began to build two large ornate hotels in St. Augustine, the 540-room [Ponce de Leon Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponce_de_Leon_Hotel) and the [Hotel Alcazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Alcazar), and bought the [Casa Monica Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Monica_Hotel) the next year.

Continental European attitudes towards gambling and nudity tended to be more lax than in Britain, and British and French entrepreneurs were quick to exploit the possibilities. In 1863, the Prince of [Monaco](19261.md), [Charles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_III,_Prince_of_Monaco) and [François Blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Blanc), a French businessman, arranged for [steamships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamship) and carriages to take visitors from Nice to Monaco, where large luxury hotels, gardens and casinos were built. The place was renamed [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo). Commercial seabathing also spread to other areas of the [United States](3434750.md) and parts of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) such as [Australia](4689264.md), where [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing) became popular in the early 20th century. By the 1970s cheap and affordable air travel was the catalyst for the growth of a global tourism market.

Since the late 20th century, [recreational fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_fishing) and leisure boat pursuits have become very lucrative, and traditional [fishing villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_village) are often well positioned to take advantage of this. [Destin, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destin,_Florida), for instance, has evolved from an [artisanal fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisanal_fishing) village into a seaside resort dedicated to tourism with a large fishing fleet of recreational charter boats.[^cite_note-11]

[Germany](11867.md) is known for its traditional seaside resorts on the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) and the [North Sea](21179.md) coasts, mainly established in the 19th century. In [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) they are called *Seebad* ("Sea Spa") or *Seeheilbad*, sometimes with *Ostsee-* or *Nordsee-* as prefixes for the respective coastline.

The most prestigious resorts can be found along the Baltic coastline, including the islands of [Rugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCgen) and [Usedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usedom). They often feature a unique architectural style called [resort architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_architecture). The coast of [Mecklenburg and Western Pomerania](38168.md) alone has an overall length of 2000 km[^cite_note-12] and is nicknamed *German [Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riviera)*.[^cite_note-13] [Heiligendamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiligendamm) in [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg), established in 1793, is the oldest seaside resort in Germany and [continental Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Europe).[^cite_note-14]

Most important coastal areas with seaside resorts in Germany:

- [Baltic Sea](3335.md): islands of [Fehmarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fehmarn), [Hiddensee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiddensee), [Rügen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCgen), [Usedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usedom); [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg) coast, [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warnem%C3%BCnde), peninsula of [Fischland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischland), [Darss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darss) and [Zingst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zingst)

- [North Sea](21179.md): [East Frisian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Frisian_Islands) and [North Frisian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Frisian_Islands)

### [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Ireland)



The seafront in [Bray, County Wicklow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bray,_County_Wicklow), Ireland



[Kilkee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilkee) on the west coast of Ireland

The 'Irish Riviera' on the South Coast of Ireland features the seaside resorts of [Youghal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youghal), [Ardmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardmore,_County_Waterford), [Dungarvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungarvan), [Cóbh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3bh) and [Ballycotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballycotton), all set close to the south coast of [Ireland](147575.md). Youghal has been a favoured holiday destination for over 100 years, situated on the banks of the [River Blackwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_Blackwater) as it reaches the sea. Dungarvan is a seaside market town beneath the mountains in the centre of the Irish south coast. [Kinsale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsale) is often described as a yachting town, with a diverse range of restaurants.

Seaside resorts in the East of Ireland developed after the introduction of rail travel. The [Dublin and Kingstown Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_and_Kingstown_Railway) introduced [day-trippers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day-tripper) from [Dublin](8504.md) to Kingstown (now [Dún Laoghaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BAn_Laoghaire)) in [South Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dublin), and the coastal town became Ireland's first seaside resort. Other South Dublin towns and villages such as [Sandycove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandycove), [Dalkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalkey) and [Killiney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killiney) grew as seaside resorts when the rail network was expanded. Since the opening of [Bray Daly Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bray_Daly_Station) in 1852, the [County Wicklow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Wicklow) coastal town of [Bray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bray,_County_Wicklow) has become the largest seaside resort on the East Coast of Ireland. The town of [Greystones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greystones), five miles south of Bray, also grew as a seaside resort when the railway line was extended in 1855. Other seaside resorts include [Courtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtown) and [Rosslare Strand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosslare_Strand) in [County Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Wexford).

[Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster) has a number of seaside resorts, such as [Portrush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrush), situated on the north coast, with its two beaches and a world-famous golf course, [Royal Portrush Golf Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Portrush_Golf_Club).[^cite_note-15] Other Ulster seaside resorts are [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle,_County_Down), located on the east coast at the foot of the Mourne Mountains; [Ballycastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballycastle,_County_Antrim); [Portstewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portstewart); [Rathmullan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rathmullan); [Bundoran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundoran) and [Bangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down). [Bangor Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor_Marina) is one of the largest in Ireland and the marina has on occasion been awarded the [Blue Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Flag_beach) for attention to environmental issues.

The main seaside towns in the west of Ireland are in [County Clare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Clare); the largest are [Lahinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahinch) and [Kilkee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilkee). Lahinch is a popular [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing) location.

Like British resorts, many seaside towns in Ireland have turned to other entertainment industries. Larger resorts such as Bray or [Portrush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrush) host [air shows](2790.md), while most resorts host summer festivals.

[Margate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margate) in [Kent](16766.md), the first seaside resort of England, established in the 1750s



[Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_North_Yorkshire)'s South Bay

Long walkway supported by metal legs arising from the sand, leading to a white painted building. In the foreground are donkeys on sand.

The Grand Pier and donkey rides at [Weston-super-Mare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston-super-Mare)

The United Kingdom saw the popularisation of [seaside resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_seaside_resorts_in_the_UK), and nowhere was this more seen than in [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool). Blackpool catered for workers from across industrial [Northern England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_England), who packed its beaches and [promenade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promenade). Other northern seaside towns (for example [Bridlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridlington), [Cleethorpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleethorpes), [Morecambe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morecambe), [Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_North_Yorkshire), [Skegness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skegness), and [Southport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport)) shared in the success of this new concept, especially from trade during [wakes weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakes_week). The concept spread rapidly to other British coastal towns, including several on the coast of [North Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wales), notably [Rhyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyl), and [Llandudno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llandudno), the largest resort in [Wales](69894.md) and known as "The Queen of the Welsh Resorts", from as early as 1864.[^cite_note-20] As the 19th century progressed, British [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) [day-trippers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day-tripper) travelled on organised trips such as railway excursions, or by [steamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat), for which long [piers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier) were erected so that the ships bringing the trade could berth.

Another area notable for its seaside resorts was (and is) the [Firth of Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firth_of_Clyde), outside Glasgow. Glaswegians would take a ferry "doon the watter" from the city, down the [River Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Clyde), to the Firth's islands and peninsulas and beyond, such as [Cowal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowal), [Bute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Bute), [Arran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Arran), and [Kintyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintyre). Resorts include [Rothesay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothesay,_Argyll_and_Bute), [Lamlash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamlash), [Whiting Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiting_Bay), [Dunoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunoon), [Tighnabruaich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tighnabruaich), [Carrick Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrick_Castle), [Helensburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helensburgh), [Largs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largs), [Millport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millport,_Cumbrae) and [Campbeltown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbeltown). In contrast to many resorts, some on the Firth of Clyde have continued to prosper as middle-class [commuter towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_town).

Some resorts, especially those more southerly such as [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings), [Worthing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worthing), [Eastbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne), [Bournemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth), and [Brighton](49796.md) were built as new towns or extended by local landowners to appeal to wealthier holidaymakers. Others came about due to their proximity to large urban areas of population, such as [Southend-on-Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southend-on-Sea), which became increasingly popular with residents of London once rail links were established to it allowing day trips from London. The sunshine and sea air were seen by Victorians as beneficial for health,[^cite_note-21] and resorts such as [Ventnor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventnor) owed their growth to a visit being considered as treatment for chest complaints. Owing to its generally better climate, the south coast has many seaside towns, the most being in [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex).

In the later 20th century, the popularity of the British seaside resort declined for the same reason that it first flourished: advances in transport. The greater accessibility of foreign holiday destinations, through [package holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Package_holiday) and, more recently, European [low-cost airlines](428422.md), makes it easier to holiday abroad. Despite the loyalty of returning holidaymakers, resorts such as Blackpool have struggled to compete against the hotter weather of [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe) and the [sunbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunbelt) in the [United States](3434750.md). Now, many symbols of the traditional British resort ([holiday camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_camp), [end-of-the-pier shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier#Pleasure_piers) and saucy [postcards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaside_postcard#British_seaside_postcards)) are regarded by some as drab and outdated; the skies are imagined to be overcast and the beach windswept. This is not always true; for example [Broadstairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadstairs) in [Kent](16766.md) has retained much of its old world charm with [Punch and Judy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_and_Judy) and [donkey rides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_rides) and still remains popular, being only one hour from the [M25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M25_motorway). Brighton has also seen a fall in visitor numbers in recent years.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] The city has also experienced a rise in homelessness, especially noticeable on the city streets and in green spaces where tents have been erected.[^cite_note-24]

Many people can now afford "second holidays" and short breaks, resulting in increased tourism in British seaside towns. Many seaside towns have large shopping centres which also attract people from a wide area. Day trippers still come to the coastal towns, but on a more local scale than during the 19th century.

Many coastal towns are also popular retirement hotspots where older people reside permanently or take short breaks in the autumn months. Other English coastal towns have successfully sought to project a sense of their unique character. In particular, [Southwold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwold) on the [Suffolk](27886.md) coast is an active yet peaceful retirement haven with an emphasis on calmness, quiet countryside and jazz. [Weymouth, Dorset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weymouth,_Dorset) offers itself as "the gateway to the Jurassic Coast", Britain's only natural World Heritage Site. [Newquay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newquay) in [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall) offers itself as the 'surfing capital of Britain', hosting international surfing events on its shores.

[Torbay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torbay) in South [Devon](8166.md) is also known as the [English Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Riviera). Consisting of the towns of [Torquay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torquay), [Paignton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paignton) with its pier and [Brixham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brixham), the bay has 20 beaches and coves along its 22-mile (35 km) coastline, ranging from small secluded coves to the larger promenade-style seafronts of Torquay's Torre Abbey Sands and Paignton Sands.

However, British seaside resorts have faced increasingly stiff competition from sunnier resorts overseas since the 1970s. Largely due to the falling price of air travel under the [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) government of [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher) ([elected in 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_United_Kingdom_general_election)), the number of British families who took holidays abroad rose significantly in the 1980s.[^cite_note-25] The decline of British seaside resorts was discussed in the [Morrissey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrissey) song "[Everyday Is Like Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everyday_Is_Like_Sunday)" where daily life in the resort is likened to the emptiness of streets once associated with the shop closures on Sunday.

### [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_United_States)

Fort Lauderdale harbor

Fort Lauderdale harbor



[Hotel Del Coronado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Del_Coronado) in [Coronado, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronado,_California) in 1908

With 3,800 miles (6100 km) of coastline, the US mainland has hundreds of seaside resorts on three coasts, [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), and [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). Unlike in many smaller countries, the seaside resorts in the US are located in various climate zones, with great differences in topography and environment. Many American seaside resorts are popular destination across the world, known for their climates, culture, and entertainment opportunities.

Seaside resorts in the United States first developed near the nation's largest industrial cities on the upper [East Coast](89126.md), including [New York City](645042.md), [Philadelphia](50585.md), and [Boston](24437894.md). [Cape May, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_May,_New_Jersey), part of the [Philadelphia metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_metropolitan_area), and [Provincetown, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincetown,_Massachusetts), part of the [Boston metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Boston) were two of the nation's first seaside resorts, developed in the 19th century and catering to city workers. Cape May is often called Americas "first seaside resort". The early emergence of Cape May as a summer resort was due to easy transport by water from Philadelphia to the Atlantic Ocean. Early Cape May vacationers were carried to the town on sloops from Philadelphia, and water transport was also easy from New York, Baltimore, Washington, D.C., and points south. The resort business in Cape May began to thrive when regular steamboat traffic on the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) began after the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812). Early visitors to Cape May included [Henry Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Clay) in 1847, and [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) in 1849. Today, the [Cape May Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_May_Historic_District) is one of the largest and well preserved examples of Victorian architecture in the United States.

On the southern Atlantic coast, [Henry Flagler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Flagler) had the idea to make [St. Augustine, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida) a winter resort. He built several rail lines south, and combined them with existing lines to create the [Florida East Coast Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_East_Coast_Railway) in 1885. He built a railroad bridge over the [St. Johns River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Johns_River) in 1888, opening up the Atlantic coast of Florida to development. In 1887 Flagler began construction of two large ornate hotels in St. Augustine, the 540-room [Ponce de Leon Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponce_de_Leon_Hotel) and the [Hotel Alcazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Alcazar), and bought the [Casa Monica Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Monica_Hotel) the next year.

In [Miami, Florida](53846.md), the community of [Cocoanut (now Coconut) Grove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_Grove,_Miami) began development as a resort town in the 1880s with the building of the Bayview House (aka Peacock Inn) which closed in 1902. Visitors to the greater Miami area then flocked to Camp Biscayne (in Coconut Grove), the [Royal Palm Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palm_Hotel_\(Miami\)) in [Downtown Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Downtown_Miami), and other resort hotels in Miami, as well as in smaller numbers to the [Florida Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Keys). In 1894, the lavish [Royal Poinciana Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Poinciana_Hotel) opened in Palm Beach, Florida, with rave reviews from wealthy New York tourists who picked oranges in January to their delight. On the Gulf of Mexico, the City of Galveston was emerging as a booming city, and in 1882, architect [Nicholas J. Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_J._Clayton) designed the [Beach Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_Hotel_\(Galveston\)). By 1888, Galveston, TX was a wealthy city and booming seaside playground for wealthy [New Orleans](53842.md) businessmen.

On the Pacific coast in California, in April 1886, Babcock and Story created the Coronado Beach Company, which sought to develop Coronado as a seaside resort. In the mid-1880s, the San Diego region was in the midst of one of its first real estate booms. The [Hotel del Coronado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_del_Coronado) was built in March 1887, with Babcock's visions for the hotel built around a courtyard of tropical trees, shrubs and flowers, with a dining wing to give full value to the view of the ocean, bay and city. By 1915, more hotels were built along the Los Angeles coastline to serve the wealthy tourists and Hollywood film makers. In May 1926, brothers E.A. "Jack" Harter and T.D. "Til" Harter built the [Hotel Casa del Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Casa_del_Mar) in Santa Monica, at a cost of \$2 million, creating one of the most successful beach clubs in Southern California, popular with socialites and Hollywood celebrities.

In the 1920s, Carl Fisher was the main promoter of [Miami Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Beach), and helped to develop the city as a seaside resort. To accommodate the wealthy tourists, several grand hotels were built, among them the [Flamingo Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flamingo_Hotel). In 1926, the massive [The Breakers hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Breakers_hotel) in Palm Beach had been rebuilt, and there was a large northern tourist industry in coastal southern Florida. By the 1950s with increasing auto travel, more seaside resorts grew along the Atlantic and Pacific coasts, while small, declining industrial ports were being rebuilt. In 1954, the [Fontainebleau Miami Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontainebleau_Miami_Beach), and was considered, (at that time) the most lavish seaside hotel in the world.

In the modern era, hundreds of seaside resorts now string the Gulf, Atlantic, and Pacific coasts of the United States. Many Americans move with the seasons when they visit seaside resorts, vacationing in northern seaside areas in the warm season (April through October), and then moving to southern areas in the cold season (November through March). Many seaside resorts in Florida and California however, see travelers all year.
