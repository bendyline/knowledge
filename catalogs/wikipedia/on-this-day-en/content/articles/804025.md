The **Paraguay national football team** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Selección de fútbol de Paraguay*; [Guarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarani_language): *Poravo Paraguáigua Vakapipopo pegua*), nicknamed ***La Albirroja***, represents [Paraguay](23420.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) competitions, and are controlled by the [Paraguayan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_Football_Association). Paraguay is a member of [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL).

The team has qualified for nine [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) competitions ([1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup), [1986](157231.md), [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), [2010](168079.md), [2026](36581929.md)), with their best performance coming in 2010 when they reached the quarter-finals. A regular participant at the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), Paraguay have been crowned champions of the competition on two occasions (in [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_South_American_Championship) and [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica)). Paraguay's highest [FIFA World Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Ranking) was 8th in March 2001 and their lowest was 103rd in May 1995. Paraguay was awarded second place with [Best Move of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings#Best_Mover_of_the_Year) in 1996 for their rise in the FIFA World Ranking.

The national team's most successful period was under the coaching of Argentine [Gerardo Martino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardo_Martino), who was awarded with the [South American Coach of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_Coach_of_the_Year) in 2007 and took Paraguay to the quarter-finals stage of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) competition for the first time in history (in [2010](168079.md)) and also to the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Final) of the [2011 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), where Paraguay finished as runners-up. In Paraguay's entire history at the FIFA World Cup, only [Carlos Gamarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Gamarra) and [José Luis Chilavert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Chilavert) hold the distinction of being selected as part of the [All-Star Team](3482503.md#All-Star_Team), for their performances in the [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) edition. [Paulo da Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_da_Silva) holds the most appearances for the national team with 148 matches and [Roque Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Santa_Cruz) is the all-time leading goalscorer with 32 goals. [Denis Caniza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Caniza), who was part of the national team from 1996 to 2010, is the only player to have represented Paraguay in four consecutive FIFA World Cup competitions (1998, 2002, 2006, 2010).

## History

Main article: [History of the Paraguay national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Paraguay_national_football_team)

### The beginning (1900–1930)



Paraguay at the [1929 South American Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_South_American_Championship)

Soon after the introduction of football in Paraguay by [Williams Paats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Paats), the **Liga Paraguaya de Fútbol** (today [Asociación Paraguaya de Fútbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asociaci%C3%B3n_Paraguaya_de_F%C3%BAtbol)) was created in 1906. The first national football team was organized in 1910 when an invitation by the Argentine club Hércules of [Corrientes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrientes) was received to play a friendly match that ended in a 0–0 draw.[^cite_note-3]

Because of the increasing number of invitations to play matches and international tournaments, the Asociación Paraguaya de Fútbol decided to officially create the national team and select the striped red and white jerseys that, to this day, remain as the official colours (taken from the [Paraguayan flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Paraguay)). In late 1919, Paraguay accepted an invitation to play the [1921 South American Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921_South_American_Championship); in order to prepare for that event, a number of friendly matches were played between 1919 and 1921. The first of those friendly matches was a 5–1 loss against [Argentina](454699.md), the first international match played by Paraguay. When the 1921 South American Championship began, Paraguay defeated three-time South American champions [Uruguay](679782.md) 2–1, with this being the first match in an official competition for the Paraguayan football team. Paraguay eventually finished fourth in the tournament and became a regular participant of the tournament for the next editions.

### First taste of success (1930–1970)

In 1930, Paraguay participated in the first [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), organized by Uruguay. In the first round, Paraguay lost to the [United States](582488.md) 0–3, before defeating [Belgium](679716.md) 1–0 with a goal by [Luis Vargas Peña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Vargas_Pe%C3%B1a). Only one team per group advanced from the first round, and the U.S. left Paraguay behind.

After participating in the [1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_South_American_Championship), [1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_South_American_Championship) and [1949 South American Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_South_American_Championship) (where Paraguay finished in second place), Paraguay returned to the World Cup in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), facing [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) and [Italy](362466.md) in Group 3. Paraguay failed to advance to the next round after a 2–2 draw against Sweden and a 2–0 loss against Italy. Paraguay won their first [South American Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) in 1953, played in [Peru](170691.md), after winning against [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) (3–0), [Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivia_national_football_team) (2–1) and [Brazil](149286.md) (2–1), and drawing [Ecuador](1016797.md) (0–0), [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_national_football_team) (2–2) and Uruguay (2–2). Since Paraguay and Brazil were tied in points at the end of the tournament, a final playoff match was played between them, with Paraguay winning the final 3–2.

For the [1958 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup), Paraguay qualified ahead of Uruguay (beating them 5–0 in the decisive game) with a team that contained a formidable attacking lineup with stars such as [Juan Bautista Agüero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Bautista_Ag%C3%BCero), [José Parodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Parodi), [Jorge Lino Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Lino_Romero), [Cayetano Ré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayetano_R%C3%A9) and [Florencio Amarilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florencio_Amarilla). In their first game in Sweden, Paraguay were 3–2 up against [France](250197.md) in a game they lost 7–3. A 3–2 win over [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) and a 3–3 draw with [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) saw Paraguay finish third in their group.

The departure of several of their stars for European football (mainly Spain) resulted in a weakening of Paraguay's football fortunes somewhat, but they were only edged out by [Mexico](808402.md) in the [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup) qualifiers.

### More continental success (1970–1990)

With players such as [Romerito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_C%C3%A9sar_Romero), [Carlos Alberto Kiese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alberto_Kiese_Wiesner), [Alicio Solalinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicio_Solalinde), [Roberto Paredes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Paredes), [Hugo Ricardo Talavera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ricardo_Talavera) and [Eugenio Morel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Morel) and manager Ranulfo Miranda,[^cite_note-4] Paraguay won the [1979 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) after finishing first in Group C (which consisted of Uruguay and Ecuador as well) with two wins and two draws. In the semi-finals, Paraguay defeated Brazil by an aggregate score of 4–3. In the final, Paraguay defeated Chile by an aggregate score of 3–1 to claim its second continental crown.

Paraguay ended a 28-year absence from the World Cup in [1986](157231.md); during their time in Mexico, the team defeated [Iraq](1039652.md) 1–0, and drew the hosts 1–1 and Belgium 2–2. They reached the second round where they were beaten 3–0 by [England](9904.md).[^cite_note-5]

### The golden generation (1998–2011)

A drought followed once again, as Paraguay failed to reach the [1990](157232.md) and [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) World Cups.

In 1992, Paraguay won the South American pre-Olympic tournament, which guaranteed a spot in the [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics) football competition. In the Olympics, Paraguay finished second in its group and were eliminated by [Ghana](1029620.md) in the quarter-finals.[^cite_note-Olympics_1992--6] Despite this, new players such as [Carlos Gamarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Gamarra), [Celso Ayala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celso_Ayala), [José Luis Chilavert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Chilavert), [Francisco Arce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Arce) and [José Cardozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Cardozo) emerged, becoming part of the "golden generation" that led Paraguay to three straight World Cups and noteworthy performances in continental competitions.

Coached by the Brazilian [Paulo César Carpegiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_C%C3%A9sar_Carpegiani), the *Albirroja* returned to the World Cup finals for the first time since 1986. Paraguay were hosts of the [1999 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), played in four cities throughout the country. Many of the 1998 World Cup players returned, to be coached by [Ever Hugo Almeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ever_Hugo_Almeida). Grouped with Bolivia, [Japan](993546.md) and Peru, the Albirroja played their first match of the competition, drawing 0–0 against Bolivia. On 2 July, Paraguay faced Japan and sealed a 4–0 victory. In Paraguay's third and last group stage fixture against Peru, Paraguay won 1–0. The *Albirroja* topped the group with seven points. Paraguay were drawn against Uruguay at the quarter-final stage. The match was decided via a penalty shootout, which saw Paraguay lose 5–3. Following the conclusion of the competition, striker Roque Santa Cruz was awarded with the 1999 [Paraguayan Footballer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_Footballer_of_the_Year) award.[^cite_note-9]

In the [2001 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), head coach [Sergio Markarián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Markari%C3%A1n) selected a squad of mostly domestic based players. Paraguay were drawn against Peru, Mexico and Brazil. Their 12 July match against Peru ended 3–3. On 15 July, Paraguay drew 0–0 with Mexico, before Brazil's 3–1 win three days later in Paraguay's last group stage fixture.

#### 2004 Copa América

Coach [Carlos Jara Saguier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Jara_Saguier) took a relatively young squad to the 2004 Copa América, with the majority of players tied to clubs of the [Primera División Paraguaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_Paraguaya). Having been drawn in Group C with Brazil, Costa Rica and Chile, a penalty sealed Paraguay's 1–0 victory in their first group stage match against Costa Rica. Paraguay then drew 1–1 with Chile, then earned a 2–1 victory against Brazil, finishing undefeated in their group with seven points. Paraguay were drawn against Uruguay in the quarter-finals, being eliminated after losing 3–1.

Group C included Paraguay, [Argentina](454699.md), [Colombia](680175.md), and the [United States](582488.md). In Paraguay's first fixture, they would defeat Colombia 5–0 after a hat-trick from Roque Santa Cruz and a double from [Salvador Cabañas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Caba%C3%B1as).[^cite_note-15] In Paraguay's second fixture against the United States, Édgar Barreto opened the scoring in the 29th minute just before the USA's [Ricardo Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Clark) equalized in the 35th minute. Paraguay would win the match 3–1 after a goal from Óscar Cardozo and a 92nd minute free kick from Salvador Cabañas.[^cite_note-16]

With both Paraguay and Argentina having obtained six points and qualifying from Group C, the two teams faced in their last group stage fixture with a second-string team. A 79th minute [Javier Mascherano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Mascherano) goal was enough to seal a 1–0 victory for Argentina,[^cite_note-17] as Paraguay advanced to the knockout stages to face [Mexico](808402.md). Mexico had already beaten [Brazil](149286.md) in the group stage and had finished in first place in Group B. After Paraguayan goalkeeper Aldo Bobadilla was sent off in the third minute, Paraguay conceded a penalty in the fifth minute and eventually found themselves down 3–0 at half-time. Mexico would score another three more goals, thrashing Paraguay 6–0 and ending their Copa América campaign.[^cite_note-18]

The 2010 World Cup saw Paraguay drawn into [Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F) alongside defending champions [Italy](362466.md), [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team) and [New Zealand](1023035.md), with the team facing Italy in their opening match and drawing 1–1, after taking the lead in the 39th minute.[^cite_note-26] They then beat Slovakia 2–0[^cite_note-27] and drew New Zealand 0–0, to finish first in the group.[^cite_note-28]

Paraguay then eliminated [Japan](993546.md) in the round of 16, as they won a penalty shoot-out 5–3 after a 0–0 draw.[^cite_note-29] Paraguay advanced to the quarter-finals for the first time.[^cite_note-30] The Albirroja were drawn against [Spain](609825.md) at the quarter-final stage. Paraguayan goalkeeper [Justo Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justo_Villar) saved a penalty kick, but Spain scored in the 83rd minute to win 1–0, and went on to win the tournament.[^cite_note-31] After the match, Gerardo Martino stated that he would be leaving his position at the end of his contract.

#### 2011 Copa América



Paraguay's [Justo Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justo_Villar) (centre, in yellow) was voted best goalkeeper of the 2011 Copa América.

At the [2011 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), Paraguay were paired with [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Group_B) with [Brazil](149286.md), [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_national_football_team) and [Ecuador](1016797.md), drawing all three matches to end up as the group's third-place team.

As one of the best third-placed teams that advanced, Paraguay won the rematch against Brazil. The match was decided via a penalty shoot out, with Paraguay winning in order to meet Venezuela in the semi-final. With another penalty shoot out, Paraguay won 5–3 to advance to their first final since [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), where they lost to [Uruguay](679782.md). Despite the loss, Paraguayan goalkeeper [Justo Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justo_Villar) was awarded as the [best goalkeeper of the tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica#Awards). Gerardo Martino resigned soon afterwards as coach of the Albirroja.

[Francisco Arce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Arce) was in charge of the Albirroja in 2011, following the departure of Gerardo Martino.

Throughout the duration of the 2014 World Cup qualifiers, Paraguay changed coaches three times, only to end up [last in qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL\)#Standings). The [2015 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) eventually saw Paraguay reach the semi-finals, after defeating Brazil in the quarter-finals on penalties. They were eliminated by Argentina by a score of 6–1.[^cite_note-32]

Paraguay finished last in their group in a special [Copa América Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario).

Paraguay qualified for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), their first since 2010. In the group stage, they lost 4–1 to hosts United States, before defeating Turkey 1–0.[^cite_note-33] Paraguay advanced to the knockout stages and faced Germany in the Round of 32 at [Gillette Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillette_Stadium). After a 1–1 draw in regular and extra time—with [Julio Enciso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Enciso_\(footballer,_born_2004\)) scoring for Paraguay and [Kai Havertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Havertz) evening it up for Germany—Paraguay secured a historic upset by defeating the Germans 4–3 in a dramatic penalty shootout to advance to the Round of 16.[^cite_note-34]
