The **Cessna 172 Skyhawk** is an American four-seat, single-engine, [high wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoplane#High), [fixed-wing aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-wing_aircraft) made by the [Cessna Aircraft Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Aircraft_Company).[^cite_note-SkyhawkOverview-2] First flown in 1955,[^cite_note-SkyhawkOverview-2] more 172s have been built than [any other aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-produced_aircraft).[^cite_note-AvWeb1-3] It was developed from the [Cessna 170](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_170), which was first manufactured in 1948, but with [tricycle landing gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricycle_landing_gear) rather than [conventional landing gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_landing_gear). The Skyhawk name was originally used for a trim package, but was later applied to all standard-production 172 aircraft; in the 1980s, the 172 was briefly marketed as the **Cutlass**. The 172 was also produced under license in France by [Reims Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims_Aviation). The 172 was developed into the more powerful but less successful [Cessna 175](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_175); after that aircraft's discontinuation, Cessna and Reims used the 175 [type certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) to develop the [R172](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_R172) series, which includes several aircraft marketed as 172 variants.

Measured by its longevity and popularity, the Cessna 172 is the most successful aircraft in history. Cessna delivered the first production model in 1956, and as of 2015, the company and its partners had built more than 44,000 units.[^cite_note-auto-1][^cite_note-FlyingMag1-4][^cite_note-5] With a break from 1986 to 1996, the aircraft remains in production today.

A [light](2643987.md) [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) airplane, the Skyhawk's main competitors throughout much of its history were the [Beechcraft Musketeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_Musketeer) and [Grumman American AA-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_American_AA-5) series, though neither are currently in production. Other prominent competitors still in production include the [Piper PA-28 Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper_PA-28_Cherokee),[^cite_note-Plane_and_Pilot-6] and, more recently, the [Diamond DA40 Diamond Star](7234644.md) and [Cirrus SR20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirrus_SR20).

## Design and development



Early Cessna 172s, like this 1957 model, had a "[fastback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastback)" rear cabin with no rear window and featured a "square" fin design.

The Cessna 172 started as a tricycle landing gear variant of the [taildragger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taildragger) [Cessna 170](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_170), with a basic level of standard equipment. In January 1955, Cessna flew an improved variant of the Cessna 170, a [Continental O-300-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_O-300)-powered Cessna 170C with larger elevators and a more angular tailfin.[^cite_note-simpson-7] Although the variant was tested and certified, Cessna decided to modify it with a tricycle landing gear, and the modified Cessna 170C flew again on June 12, 1955.[^cite_note-simpson-7] To reduce the time and cost of certification, the type was added to the Cessna 170 [type certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) as the Model 172.[^cite_note-simpson-7] Later, the 172 was given its own type certificate.[^cite_note-3A12-8][^cite_note-A799-9] The 172 became an overnight sales success, and over 1,400 were built in 1956, its first full year of production.[^cite_note-10]

Early 172s were similar in appearance to the 170s, with the same straight aft fuselage and tall landing gear legs, although the 172 had a straight tailfin while the 170 had a rounded fin and rudder. In 1960, the 172A incorporated revised landing gear and the swept-back tailfin, which is still in use today.[^cite_note-simpson-7] Starting with the 172B, Cessna introduced the stepped firewall and enlarged, relocated instrument panel from the related [Cessna 175](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_175); from this point forward, the fuselages of the two types became largely identical.[^cite_note-Kinney-11]

The final aesthetic development, found in the 1963 172D and all later 172 models, was a lowered rear deck allowing an aft window. Cessna advertised this added rear visibility as "[Omni-Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna#Marketing_initiatives)".[^cite_note-Clarke-12]

Production halted in 1986 because of [product liability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_liability) costs, but resumed in 1996 at Cessna's new factory at [Independence, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence,_Kansas), with the Cessna 172R Skyhawk.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESimpsonLongleySwan202221-13] Cessna supplemented this in 1998 with the 180 hp (134 kW) Cessna 172S Skyhawk SP.[^cite_note-JAWA03_p588-14]

### Modifications

The Cessna 172 may be modified through a wide array of [supplemental type certificates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplemental_type_certificate) (STCs), including increased engine power, higher gross weight limits, and certain aerobatic conversions. Available STC engine modifications increase power from 180 to 210 hp (134 to 157 kW), add [constant-speed propellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constant-speed_propeller), or allow the use of [automobile gasoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avgas#Automotive_gasoline). Other modifications include additional fuel tank capacity in the [wing tips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_tip), added baggage compartment tanks, added [wheel pants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel_pants) to reduce drag, or enhanced landing and takeoff performance and safety with a [STOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOL) kit.[^cite_note-15] The 172 has also been equipped with the 180 hp (134 kW) [fuel injected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_injected) [Superior Air Parts Vantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Air_Parts_Vantage) engine.[^cite_note-16]

## Operational history

The Cessna 172 has been used extensively for flight training, personal transportation, and aerial patrol work. Various operators, including civil flying schools and government agencies, have flown the type for tasks such as search-and-rescue, pipeline inspection, and law-enforcement observation.



The record-setting 1958-built Cessna 172

### World records

From December 4, 1958, to February 7, 1959, Robert Timm and John Cook set the [world record for (refueled) flight endurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_endurance_record#Refueled,_crewed) in a Cessna 172, registration number N9172B. They took off from McCarran Field (now [Harry Reid International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Reid_International_Airport)) in Las Vegas, Nevada, and landed back at McCarran Field after 64 days, 22 hours, 19 minutes and 5 seconds in a flight covering an estimated 150,000 miles (240,000 km), over 6 times farther than flying around the world at the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator). The flight was part of a fund-raising effort for the [Damon Runyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Runyon) Cancer Fund.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Ruffin-18] The aircraft is now on display at the airport.[^cite_note-19]

## Variants

For variants marketed under the Cessna 172 name but certified under the type certificate of the [Cessna 175](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_175_Skylark), see [Cessna R172](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_R172).

*Cessna has historically used [model years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_year) similar to U.S. auto manufacturers, with sales of new models typically starting a few months prior to the actual calendar year.*

172

Introduced in November 1955 for the 1956 model year as a development of the [Cessna 170B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_170) with tricycle landing gear, dubbed "[Land-O-Matic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna#Company_terminology)" by Cessna. The 172 also featured a redesigned tail similar to the experimental 170C, "Para-Lift" flaps, and a maximum gross weight of 2,200 lb (998 kg) while retaining the 170B's 145 hp (108 kW) [Continental O-300-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_O-300) six-cylinder, air-cooled engine. The 1957 and 1959 model years brought only minor changes, while 1959 introduced a new cowling for improved engine cooling. The prototype 172, c/n 612, was modified from 170 c/n 27053, which previously served as the prototype of the 170B. A total of 3,757 were constructed over the four model years; 1,178 (1956), 1,041 (1957), 750 (1958), 788 (1959).[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp16-20][^cite_note-\:0-21]

172A



A 1960 Cessna 172A

1960 model year with a swept-back vertical tail and rudder and powered by a 145 hp (108 kW) O-300-C engine. It was also the first 172 to be certified for [floatplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floatplane) operation. 994 built.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp17-22][^cite_note-\:0-21]

172B

1961 model year with shorter landing gear, engine mounts lengthened by three inches (76 mm), a reshaped cowling, a pointed propeller spinner, and an increased gross weight of 2,250 lb (1,021 kg). The stepped firewall introduced in the closely related [Cessna 175](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_175) was adopted in the 172, along with the 175's wider, rearranged instrument panel located further aft in the fuselage. For the first time, the **Skyhawk** name was applied to an available deluxe option package that included optional wheel fairings, avionics, and a cargo door along with full exterior paint rather than partial paint stripes. The Skyhawk was also powered by an O-300-D in place of the O-300-C of the standard model. 989 built.[^cite_note-Kinney-11][^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp17-22][^cite_note-\:0-21]

172C

1962 model year with fiberglass wingtips, redesigned wheel fairings, a key starter to replace the previous pull-starter, and an optional autopilot. The seats were redesigned to be six-way adjustable, and a child seat was made optional to allow two children to be carried in the baggage area. 810 built.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp17-22][^cite_note-\:0-21]

172D



1963 Cessna 172D

1963 model year with a cut down rear fuselage with a wraparound [Omni-Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna#Marketing_initiatives) rear window, a one-piece windshield, increased horizontal stabilizer span, and a folding hat shelf in the rear cabin. Gross weight was increased to 2,300 lb (1,043 kg), where it would stay until the 172P. New rudder and brake pedals were also added. 1,011 were built by Cessna, while a further 18 were produced by [Reims Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims_Aviation) in France as the **F172D**.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp17-22][^cite_note-\:0-21]

172E

1964 model year with a redesigned instrument panel with center-mounted avionics and [circuit breakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_breaker) replacing the electrical [fuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuse_\(electrical\)) of previous models. 1,209 built, 67 built by Reims as the **F172E**.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp178-23][^cite_note-\:0-21]

172F

1965 model year with electrically operated flaps to replace the previous lever-operated system and improved instrument lighting. 1,400 built, plus 94 by Reims as the **F172F**.[^cite_note-3A12-8][^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp178-23][^cite_note-\:0-21]

The 172F formed the basis for the [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force)'s [T-41A Mescalero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_T-41_Mescalero) primary trainer, which was used during the 1960s and early 1970s as initial flight screening aircraft in USAF Undergraduate Pilot Training (UPT). Following their removal from the UPT program, some extant USAF T-41s were assigned to the [U.S. Air Force Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force_Academy) for the cadet pilot indoctrination program, while others were distributed to Air Force aero clubs.[^cite_note-24]

172G



1966 Reims F172G

1966 model year with a longer, more pointed spinner and sold for US\$12,450 in its basic 172 version and US\$13,300 in the upgraded Skyhawk version. 1,474 built (including 26 as the T-41A), plus 140 by Reims as the **F172G**.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp178-23][^cite_note-\:0-21]

172H

1967 model year with a 60A alternator replacing the generator, a rotating beacon replacing the flashing unit, redesigned wheel fairings, and a shorter-stroke [nose gear oleo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleo_strut) to reduce drag and improve the appearance of the aircraft in flight. A new cowling was used, introducing shock-mounts that transmitted lower noise levels to the cockpit and reduced cowl cracking. The electric stall warning horn was replaced by a pneumatic one. 1,586 built (including 34 as the T-41A), plus 435 by Reims as the **F172H** for both the 1967 and 1968 model years.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp178-23][^cite_note-\:0-21]

172I



The 1968-built Cessna 172I introduced the [Lycoming O-320-E2D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_O-320) engine of 150 hp (112 kW).

The 1968 model year marked the beginning of the [Lycoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_Engines)-powered 172s, with the 172I introduced with a [Lycoming O-320-E2D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_O-320) engine of 150 hp (112 kW), an increase of 5 hp (3.7 kW) over the Continental powerplant. The increased power resulted in an increase in optimal cruise from 130 mph (209 km/h) [true airspeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_airspeed) (TAS) to 131 mph (211 km/h) TAS. There was no change in the sea level rate of climb at 645 ft (197 m) per minute. Starting with this model, the standard and deluxe Skyhawk models were no longer powered by different engines.[^cite_note-Phillipsp178-23] The 172I also introduced the first standard "T" instrument arrangement. 649 built.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp178-23][^cite_note-\:1-25]

172J

Main article: [Cessna 177 Cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_177_Cardinal)

For 1968, Cessna planned to replace the 172 with a newly designed aircraft called the 172J, featuring the same general configuration but with a more sloping windshield, a strutless [cantilever wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantilever_wing), a more stylish interior, and various other improvements. A single 172J prototype, registered N3765C (c/n 660), was built. However, the popularity of the previous 172 with Cessna dealers and flight schools prompted the cancellation of the replacement plan, and the 172J was redesignated as the [177](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_177_Cardinal) from the second prototype onward and sold alongside the 172.[^cite_note-Phillipsp25-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

172K



1969 model-year Cessna 172K, built in 1968

Introduced for the 1969 model year with a redesigned tailfin cap and reshaped rear windows enlarged by 16 square inches (103 cm<sup>2</sup>). Optional long-range 52 US gal (43 imp gal; 197 L) wing fuel tanks were also offered. The 1970 model year featured fiberglass, downward-shaped, conical camber wingtips and optional fully articulated seats. 2,055 built for both model years, plus 50 by Reims as the **F172K**.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp18-29][^cite_note-\:1-25]

172L

Introduced for the 1971 model year with tapered, tubular steel landing gear legs replacing the original flat spring steel legs, increasing landing gear width by 12 in (30 cm). The new landing gear was lighter, but required aerodynamic fairings to maintain the same speed and climb performance as experienced with the flat steel design. 172L also had a nose-mounted landing light, a bonded baggage door, and optional cabin [skylights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylight). The 1972 model year introduced a plastic fairing between the dorsal fin and vertical fin to introduce a greater family resemblance to the [182](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_182)'s vertical fin. 1972 also introduced a reduced-diameter propeller, bonded cabin doors, and improved instrument panel controls. 1,535 built for both model years, plus 100 by Reims as the **F172L**.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp18-29][^cite_note-\:1-25]

172M



1975 Cessna 172M

Introduced for the 1973 model year with a "Camber-Lift" wing with a drooped leading edge for improved low-speed handling, a key-locking baggage door, and new lighting switches. The 1974 model year introduced the **Skyhawk II**, which was sold alongside the baseline 172M and Skyhawk models with higher standard equipment, including a second nav/comm radio, an [ADF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_direction_finder) and [transponder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transponder_\(aviation\)), a larger baggage compartment, and nose-mounted dual landing lights. 1975 introduced inertia-reel shoulder harnesses and an improved instrument panel and door seals. Beginning in 1976, Cessna stopped marketing the aircraft as the 172 and began exclusively using the "Skyhawk" designation. This model year also saw a redesigned instrument panel to hold more avionics. Among other changes, the fuel and other small gauges were relocated to the left side for improved pilot readability compared with the earlier 172 panel designs. 6,826 built; 4,926 (1973–75) and 1,900 (1976), plus 610 by Reims as the **F172M**.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp18-29][^cite_note-\:1-25]

172N Skyhawk/100



1979 Cessna 172N Skyhawk in 2019

1977 model year powered by a 160 horsepower (119 kW) [Lycoming O-320-H2AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_O-320-H2AD) engine designed to run on 100-octane fuel (hence the "Skyhawk/100" name), whereas all previous engines used 80/87 fuel. Other changes included pre-select flap control and optional rudder trim. The 1978 model year brought a 28-volt electrical system to replace the previous 14-volt system as well as optional air conditioning. The 1979 model year increased the flap-extension speed to 110 knots (127 mph; 204 km/h). 6,425 total built; 1,725 (1977), 1,725 (1978), 1,850 (1979), and 1,125 (1980), plus 525 by Reims as the **F172N**.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp189-30][^cite_note-\:1-25]

172O

There was no "O" model 172, to avoid confusion with the number zero.[^cite_note-Clarke-12]

172P Skyhawk P



Cessna 172P in October 2012

Introduced for the 1981 model year with a [Lycoming O-320-D2J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_O-320) engine replacing the O-320-H2AD of the 172N, which had proven unreliable.[^cite_note-31] Other changes included a decreased maximum flap deflection from 40 degrees to 30 to allow a gross weight increase from 2,300 lb (1,043 kg) to 2,400 lb (1,089 kg). A 62 US gal (235 L) [wet wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_wing) and air conditioning were optional. The 1982 model year moved the landing lights from the nose to the wing to increase bulb life, while 1983 added some minor soundproofing improvements and thicker windows. 1984 introduced a second door latch pin, a thicker windshield and side windows, additional avionics capacity, and low-vacuum warning lights. 2,664 total built; 1,052 (1981), 724 (1982), 319 (1983), 179 (1984), 256 (1985), and 134 (1986), plus 215 by Reims as the **F172P**. Following the end of 172P production in 1986, Cessna ceased production of the Skyhawk for ten years.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp189-30][^cite_note-\:1-25]

172Q Cutlass

Introduced for the 1983 model year, the 172Q was given the name "Cutlass" to create an affiliation with the 172RG Cutlass RG, although it was actually a 172P with a [Lycoming O-360-A4N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_O-360) engine of 180 horsepower (134 kW). The aircraft had a gross weight of 2,550 lb (1,157 kg) and an optimal cruise speed of 122 knots (140 mph; 226 km/h) compared to the 172P's cruise speed of 120 knots (138 mph; 222 km/h) on 20 hp (15 kW) less. It had a useful load that was about 100 lb (45 kg) more than the Skyhawk P and a rate of climb that was actually 20 feet (6 m) per minute lower, due to the higher gross weight. The **Cutlass II** was offered as a deluxe model of the 172Q, as was the **Cutlass II/Nav-Pac** with [IFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_flight_rules) equipment. The 172Q was produced alongside the 172P for the 1983 and 1984 model years before being discontinued. Sources disagree on the exact number of 172Q aircraft built,[^cite_note-first-33] and the construction numbers listed on the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) [type certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) overlap with those of the 1983 and 1984 172P.[^cite_note-Clarke-12][^cite_note-Phillipsp19-32][^cite_note-\:1-25]

172R Skyhawk R

The Skyhawk R was introduced in 1996 and is powered by a derated [Lycoming IO-360-L2A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_O-360) producing a maximum of 160 horsepower (120 kW) at just 2,400 rpm. This is the first Cessna 172 to have a factory-fitted fuel-injected engine.

The 172R's maximum takeoff weight is 2,450 lb (1,111 kg). This model year introduced many improvements, including a new interior with soundproofing, an all new multi-level ventilation system, a standard four point intercom, contoured, energy absorbing, 26g front seats with vertical and reclining adjustments and inertia reel harnesses.

172S Skyhawk SP



Cessna 172S Skyhawk SP

The Cessna 172S was introduced in 1998 and is powered by a [Lycoming IO-360-L2A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_O-360) producing 180 horsepower (134 kW). The maximum engine rpm was increased from 2,400 rpm to 2,700 rpm resulting in a 20 hp (15 kW) increase over the "R" model. As a result, the maximum takeoff weight was increased to 2,550 lb (1,157 kg). This model is marketed under the name Skyhawk SP, although the Type Certification data sheet specifies it is a 172S.[^cite_note-3A12-8][^cite_note-CessnaSkyhawkSP1-34]

The 172S was built primarily for the private owner-operator and is, in the early 2000s, was offered with the [Garmin G1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmin_G1000) avionics package and leather seats as standard equipment.[^cite_note-CessnaSkyhawkSP2-35]

As of 2009, the 172S model was the only Skyhawk model in production.[^cite_note-CessnaSE-36]

As of 2026, the 172S remains in production, with a variety of interiors suited to private owner-pilots and flight schools.[^cite_note-37]

### Special versions



Cessna J172T displayed at [AERO Friedrichshafen 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AERO_Friedrichshafen)

J172T Turbo Skyhawk JT-A

Model introduced in July 2014 for 2015 customer deliveries, powered by a 155 hp (116 kW) [Continental CD-155](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_CD-155) [diesel engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine) installed by the factory under a [supplemental type certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplemental_type_certificate).[^cite_note-\:2-38] Initial retail price in 2014 was \$435,000 (\~\$566,105 in 2024).[^cite_note-39] The model has a top speed of 131 kn (243 km/h; 151 mph) and burns 3 U.S. gallons (11 L; 2.5 imp gal) per hour less fuel than the standard 172.[^cite_note-Niles28Jul14-40] As a result, the model has an 885 nmi (1,639 km; 1,018 mi) range, an increase of more than 38% over the standard 172.[^cite_note-41] This model is a development of the proposed and then canceled [Skyhawk TD](#Canceled_model).[^cite_note-Bertorelli30Jul14-42] Cessna has indicated that the JT-A will be made available in 2016.[^cite_note-avw2015-05-43]

In reviewing this new model Paul Bertorelli of AVweb said: "I'm sure Cessna will find some sales for the Skyhawk JT-A, but at \$420,000, it's hard to see how it will ignite much market expansion just because it's a Cessna. It gives away \$170,000 to the near-new Redbird Redhawk conversion which is a lot of change to pay merely for the smell of a new airplane. Diesel engines cost more than twice as much to manufacture as gasoline engines do and although their [fuel efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_efficiency) gains back some of that investment, if the complete aircraft package is too pricey, the debt service will eat up any savings, making a new aircraft not just unattractive, but unaffordable. I haven't run the numbers on the JT-A yet, but I can tell from previous analysis that there are definite limits."[^cite_note-Bertorelli30Jul14-42]

The model was certified by both EASA and the FAA in June 2017.[^cite_note-Grady06Jun17-44] It was discontinued in May 2018, due to poor sales as a result of the aircraft's high price, which was twice the price of the same aircraft as a diesel conversion. The aircraft remains available as an [STC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplemental_Type_Certificate) conversion from [Continental Motors, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Motors,_Inc.)[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

Electric-powered 172

In July 2010, Cessna announced it was developing an [electrically powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_aircraft) 172 as a proof-of-concept in partnership with [Bye Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bye_Energy). In July 2011, Bye Energy, whose name had been changed to [Beyond Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_Aviation), announced the prototype had commenced taxi tests on 22 July 2011 and a first flight would follow soon.[^cite_note-AvWeb18Oct10-47][^cite_note-AVweb28Jul11-48] In 2012, the prototype, using Panacis batteries, engaged in multiple successful test flights.[^cite_note-49] The R\&D project was not pursued for production.

### Canceled model

172TD Skyhawk TD

On October 4, 2007, Cessna announced its plan to build a diesel-powered model, to be designated the 172 Skyhawk TD ("Turbo Diesel") starting in mid-2008.[^cite_note-\:2-38] The planned engine was to be a [Thielert Centurion 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thielert_Centurion_2.0), liquid-cooled, two-liter displacement, dual overhead cam, four-cylinder, in-line, turbo-diesel with [full authority digital engine control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FADEC) with an output of 155 hp (116 kW) and burning [Jet-A](1205435.md) fuel. In July 2013, the 172TD model was canceled due to Thielert's bankruptcy. The aircraft was later refined into the [Turbo Skyhawk JT-A](#Special_versions), which was certified in June 2014 and discontinued in May 2018.[^cite_note-AvWeb1-3][^cite_note-Niles28Jul14-40][^cite_note-GoyerApr2008-50][^cite_note-AOPA01May08-51]

Simulator company [Redbird Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redbird_Flight_Simulations) uses the same engine and reconditioned 172 airframes to produce a similar model, the Redbird Redhawk.[^cite_note-Bertorelli28Jul13-52][^cite_note-Bertorelli09Sep13-53]

Premier Aircraft Sales also announced in February 2014 that it would offer refurbished 172 airframes equipped with the [Continental/Thielert Centurion 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thielert_Centurion) diesel engine.[^cite_note-Niles20Feb14-54]

## Military operators

A variant of the 172, the [T-41 Mescalero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-41_Mescalero) was used as a trainer with the [United States Air Force](32090.md) and [Army](32087.md). In addition, the [United States Border Patrol](567453.md) uses a fleet of 172s for aerial surveillance along the Mexico-US border.

From 1972 to 2019 the [Irish Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Air_Corps) used the Reims version for aerial surveillance and monitoring of cash, prisoner and explosive escorts, in addition to army cooperation and pilot training roles.[^cite_note-IAC-55]

## Accidents and incidents



[Mathias Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathias_Rust)'s Cessna F172P, used in his flight from Helsinki to Moscow, on display at the [German Museum of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Museum_of_Technology), [Berlin](3354.md)

- On February 13, 1964, [Ken Hubbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Hubbs), second baseman for the [Chicago Cubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Cubs) and winner of the [Rookie of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_Rookie_of_the_Year_Award) and the [Gold Glove Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Glove_Award), was killed when the Cessna 172 he was flying crashed near Bird Island in [Utah Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Lake).

  [^cite_note-78]

- On October 23, 1964, [David Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Box), lead singer for [The Crickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crickets) on their 1960 release version of "[Peggy Sue Got Married](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peggy_Sue_Got_Married_\(song\))" and "Don't Cha Know" and later a solo artist, was killed when the Cessna 172 he was aboard crashed in northwest [Harris County, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_County,_Texas), while en route to a performance. Box was the second lead vocalist for The Crickets to die in a plane crash, following [Buddy Holly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Holly).

  [^cite_note-HubStuff-79]

  [^cite_note-buddyhollyonline-80]

- On August 31, 1969, American professional boxer [Rocky Marciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Marciano) was killed when [the Cessna 172 in which he was a passenger crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Newton_Cessna_172_crash) on approach to an airfield outside [Newton, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton,_Iowa).

  [^cite_note-81]

- On September 25, 1978, a Cessna 172, N7711G, and [Pacific Southwest Airlines Flight 182](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Southwest_Airlines_Flight_182), a [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727), collided over [San Diego](28504.md), [California](5407.md). There were 144 fatalities, 2 in the Cessna 172, 135 on the PSA Flight 182 and 7 on the ground.

  [^cite_note-AAR795-82]

- On May 28, 1987, a rented Reims Cessna F172P, [registered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_registration) D-ECJB, was used by German teenage pilot [Mathias Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathias_Rust) in an unauthorized flight from [Helsinki-Malmi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki-Malmi_Airport) through [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) airspace to land near the [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square) in [Moscow](19004.md) without being intercepted by Soviet [air defense](146640.md).

  [^cite_note-83]

- On April 9, 1990, [Atlantic Southeast Airlines Flight 2254](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Southeast_Airlines_Flight_2254), an [Embraer EMB 120 Brasilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_120_Brasilia), collided head-on with a [Civil Air Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Air_Patrol) Cessna 172, N99501, while en route from [Gadsden Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadsden_Municipal_Airport) to [Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartsfield%E2%80%93Jackson_Atlanta_International_Airport). The Cessna crashed, killing two occupants, but the Brasilia made a safe emergency landing.

  [^cite_note-ASN1990-84]

- On January 5, 2002, high school student Charles J. Bishop [stole a Cessna 172](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Tampa_Cessna_172_crash), N2371N, and intentionally crashed it into the side of the [Bank of America Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_America_Plaza_\(Tampa\)) in downtown [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida), killing only himself and otherwise causing very little damage.

  [^cite_note-cnn-85]

  [^cite_note-cn-86]

- On April 6, 2009, a Cessna 172N, C-GFJH, belonging to [Confederation College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_College) in [Thunder Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_Bay), [Ontario](22218.md), Canada, was stolen by a student who flew it into United States airspace over [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior). The 172 was intercepted and followed by [NORAD](42905.md) [F-16s](11642.md), finally landing on Highway 60 in [Ellsinore, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellsinore,_Missouri), after a seven-hour flight. The student pilot, a Canadian citizen born in Turkey, Adam Dylan Leon, formerly known as Yavuz Berke, suffered from [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(mood\)) and was attempting to commit suicide by being shot down, but was instead arrested shortly after landing. On November 3, 2009, he was sentenced to two years in a US federal prison after pleading guilty to all three charges against him: interstate transportation of a stolen aircraft, importation of a stolen aircraft, and illegal entry into the US. College procedures at the time allowed easy access to aircraft and keys were routinely left in them.
