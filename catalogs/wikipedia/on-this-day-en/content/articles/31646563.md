# Norway at the FIFA World Cup

For the women's team, see [Norway at the FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_at_the_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup).



Norway against [Italy](362466.md), during the qualification phase of the 2026 World Cup in June 2025.

[Norway](203277.md) has participated in the [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) for 18 [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) tournaments and have appeared in the main tournament four times, in [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), and [2026](36581929.md). In 2026, the team underwent their deepest run at a World Cup, advancing to the quarter-finals for the first time in their history at the 2026 World Cup.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

## Background

Norway's two appearances in the 1990s were built on the direct style of manager [Egil Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egil_Olsen), who favoured long passes, compact defending and rapid counter-attacks. Drawing on the goal-scoring analysis of statistician [Charles Reep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Reep), the approach carried Norway to second in the [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings) in the mid-1990s.[^cite_note-3]

The return in 2026 ended a 28-year absence, the country's first appearance since 1998. Led by [Erling Haaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erling_Haaland), who scored 16 goals in [UEFA qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), Norway secured their place with a 4–1 win over Italy in Milan.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]
