The [Tony Award](54741.md) for **Best Musical** is given annually to the best new Broadway [musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_play), as determined by Tony Award voters. The award is one of the ceremony's longest-standing awards, having been presented each year since [1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Tony_Awards). The award goes to the producers of the winning musical. A musical is eligible for consideration in a given year if it has not previously been produced on Broadway and is not "determined... to be a 'classic' or in the historical or popular repertoire", otherwise it may be considered for [Best Revival of a Musical](253054.md).[^cite_note-1]

Best Musical is the final award presented at the Tony Awards ceremony. Excerpts from the musicals that are nominated for this award are usually performed during the ceremony before this award is presented.

This is a list of winners and nominees for the Tony Award for Best Musical.

## Records

Accumulated records as of 2026:[^cite_note-72]

- *[The Producers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Producers_\(musical\))* has won the most Tonys, winning in 12 categories, including Best Musical.

- *[Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_\(musical\))* is the most-nominated production in Tony history, with 16 nominations.

- *[The Sound of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music)* and *[Fiorello!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorello!)* are the only two musicals to date to have ever tied for the Best Musical award (in 1960).

- *[Passion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passion_\(musical\))*, with 280 performances, is the Best Musical winner with the shortest official run. (If preview performances are included in the tally, then the shortest-run distinction belongs to *[A Strange Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Strange_Loop)*.)

- *[The Phantom of the Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phantom_of_the_Opera_\(1986_musical\))* is the longest-running Best Musical winner, with 16 previews and over 13,981 performances.

  [^cite_note-73]

- *[Hallelujah, Baby!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallelujah,_Baby!)* is the only show thus far to have won the Tony Award for Best Musical after closing.

- *[Kiss Me, Kate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_Me,_Kate)* and *[Titanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic_\(musical\))* are the only two shows to win the Best Musical award without any Tony nominations in the acting categories. (In the case of *[Kiss Me, Kate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_Me,_Kate)*, only winners were announced that year, and only in the lead performance categories.)

- *[Two Gentlemen of Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Gentlemen_of_Verona_\(musical\))* (1972), *[Raisin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raisin_\(musical\))* (1974), *[42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_\(musical\))* (1981), and *[A Strange Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Strange_Loop)* (2022) won Best Musical and only one other Tony Award.

- What is now the [Richard Rodgers Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Rodgers_Theatre) has housed more Best Musical winners than any other Broadway venue: *[Guys and Dolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guys_and_Dolls)* (1951), *[Damn Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damn_Yankees)* (1956), *[Redhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redhead_\(musical\))* (1959), *[How to Succeed in Business Without Really Trying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Succeed_in_Business_Without_Really_Trying_\(musical\))* (1962), *[1776](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1776_\(musical\))* (1969), *[Raisin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raisin_\(musical\))* (1974), *[Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_\(musical\))* (1982), *[In the Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Heights)* (2008), and *[Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_\(musical\))* (2016).

- *[The Mystery of Edwin Drood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mystery_of_Edwin_Drood_\(musical\))* (1986) was the first winner of the Tony Award for Best Musical to be entirely written by one person ([Rupert Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Holmes)), a feat unmatched until *[Rent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_\(musical\))* (1996, by [Jonathan Larson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Larson)), then recurring with *[Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_\(musical\))* (2016, by [Lin-Manuel Miranda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin-Manuel_Miranda)), *[Hadestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadestown)* (2019, by [Anaïs Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana%C3%AFs_Mitchell), the first woman), *[A Strange Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Strange_Loop)* (2022, by [Michael R. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_R._Jackson)), and *[Schmigadoon!](76848716.md)* (2026, by [Cinco Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinco_Paul)).

- *[Fun Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fun_Home_\(musical\))* (2015) was the first Best Musical winner written entirely by a team of women.

- The [74th Tony Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/74th_Tony_Awards) (2020) marked the first ceremony when all the Best Musical nominees were [jukebox musicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jukebox_musical).
