**Eritrea**,[^cite_note-19] officially the **State of Eritrea**,[^cite_note-21] is a country in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md) region of [East Africa](260987.md). Its capital and largest city is [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara). The country is bordered by [Ethiopia](187749.md) to the [south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea%E2%80%93Ethiopia_border), [Sudan](27421.md) to the west, and [Djibouti](17207794.md) to the southeast. The northeastern and eastern parts of Eritrea have an extensive coastline along the [Red Sea](26191.md). The country has a total area of approximately 117,600 km<sup>2</sup> (45,406 sq mi),[^cite_note-Eritrea-4][^cite_note-Eritrea_country_profile-5] and includes the [Dahlak Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlak_Archipelago) and several of the [Hanish Islands](3542308.md).

Hominid remains found in Eritrea have been dated to 1 million years old and anthropological research indicates that the area may contain significant records related to human evolution. The [Kingdom of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum), centred in Eritrea and [northern Ethiopia](357350.md), was established during the first or second century AD and was considered one of the four great powers of the world by the Persian prophet [Mani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mani_\(prophet\)), the founder of [Manichaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manichaeism).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Munro-Hay57-23][^cite_note-Paul_B._Henze_2005-24] It adopted [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Orthodox_Church) around the middle of the fourth century. Beginning in the 12th century, the [Zagwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagwe_dynasty) and [Solomonid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire) dynasties held fluctuating control over the entire plateau and the Red Sea coast. Eritrea's central highlands, known as [Mereb Melash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mereb_Melash) ("Beyond the [Mereb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mareb_River)"), were the northern frontier region of the Ethiopian kingdoms and were ruled by a governor titled the *[Bahr Negus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_aristocratic_and_court_titles#Important_regional_offices)* ("King of the Sea").

In the 16th century, the decades-long [attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Ethiopian_War_\(1557%E2%80%931589\)) to conquer the [Ethiopian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands) by the [Ottomans](22278.md) would result in failure, forcing them to offer a peace treaty where they were limited to the Eritrean coastline. In May 1865, much of the coastal lowlands came under the rule of the [Khedivate of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khedivate_of_Egypt), until it was transferred to [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) in February 1885. Beginning in 1885–1890, Italian troops systematically spread out from [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa) toward the highlands, eventually resulting in the formation of the colony of [Italian Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea) in 1889, establishing the present-day boundaries of the country. Italian rule continued until 1942, when Eritrea was placed under [British Military Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Military_Administration_\(Eritrea\)) during World War II; following a UN General Assembly decision in 1952, [Eritrea would govern itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea) with a local Eritrean parliament, but for foreign affairs and defense, it would enter into federal status with Ethiopia for ten years. However, in 1962, the government of Ethiopia annulled the Eritrean parliament and [formally annexed Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea_Province). The Eritrean secessionist movement organized the [Eritrean Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Liberation_Front) in 1961 and fought the [Eritrean War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence) until Eritrea gained *de facto* independence in 1991. Eritrea gained *de jure* independence in 1993 after [an independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Eritrean_independence_referendum).[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-25]

Modern Eritrea is a multi-ethnic country with nine recognized ethnic groups, each of which has a distinct language. The most widely spoken language is [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language). The others are [Tigre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigre_language), [Saho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saho_language), [Kunama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunama_language), [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_language), [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_language), [Beja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_language), [Bilen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilen_language) and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language).[^cite_note-26] [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) serve as the three working languages.[^cite_note-MOI_Eritrea-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Most residents speak languages from the [Afroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages) family, either from the [Ethiopian Semitic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Semitic_languages) or [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_languages) branches. In Eritrea, ethnic [Tigrinyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrigni) make up about 50% of the population, with the [Tigre people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigre_people) constituting around 30% of inhabitants. In addition, there are several [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan_languages)-speaking [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic) ethnic groups. Most people in the country adhere to [Christianity](5211.md) or [Islam](6037917.md), with a small minority adhering to [traditional faiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions).[^cite_note-CIA-31]

Eritrea is a unitary [one-party presidential republic](102459.md) and a *de facto* [totalitarian dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian_dictatorship), in which [national legislative and presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Eritrea) have never been held.[^cite_note-gi-32][^cite_note-Eritrea's_Silent_Totalitarianism_-_McGill_Journal_of_Political_Studies-33] [Isaias Afwerki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaias_Afwerki) has served as president since its official independence in 1993. The country's [human rights record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Eritrea) is among the worst in the world.[^cite_note-hrw-34] The Eritrean government has dismissed these allegations as politically motivated.[^cite_note-Tesf-35] Eritrea is a member of the [African Union](10634933.md), the [United Nations](31769.md), and is an [observer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_Arab_League#List_of_current_observer_states) in the [Arab League](52625.md) alongside Brazil and [Venezuela](32374.md).[^cite_note-CNN-36] It was part of the [Intergovernmental Authority on Development](1921406.md) until withdrawing in December 2025, "accusing the organisation of 'becoming a tool against' countries like itself."[^cite_note-37]

## Etymology

The name *Eritrea* is derived from the ancient (originally Greek) name for the [Red Sea](26191.md), the [Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythraean_Sea) ([Ἐρυθρὰ Θάλασσα](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythraean_Sea) *Erythra Thalassa*, based on the adjective ἐρυθρός *erythros* "red"). It was first formally adopted in 1890, with the formation of [Italian Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea) (*Colonia Eritrea*).[^cite_note-ConnellKillion2010-38] The name persisted throughout subsequent [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Military_Administration_\(Eritrea\)) and [Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea) occupation, and was reaffirmed by the [1993 independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_independence_referendum,_1993) and [1997 constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Eritrea).[^cite_note-39]

## History

Main article: [History of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Eritrea)

### Prehistory



*[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus)* skull cast from [Buya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buya,_Eritrea)

[Madam Buya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madam_Buya), a fossil found at an Eritrean archaeological site by Italian anthropologists, has been identified as among the oldest [hominid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominid) fossils found to date. This fossil has been said to reveal significant stages in human evolution and to represent a possible link between the earlier *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus)* and an archaic *[Homo sapiens](682482.md)*. Her remains have been dated to 1 million years old, making her the oldest skeletal find of her kind.[^cite_note-40] It is believed that the [Danakil Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Depression) in Eritrea was a major site of human evolution and may contain other archaeological links between *Homo erectus* hominids and [anatomically modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomically_modern_humans).[^cite_note-41]

During the last interglacial period (roughly 130,000 to 115,000 years ago), the [Red Sea](26191.md) coast of Eritrea was occupied by early anatomically modern humans.[^cite_note-pmid10811218-42] It is believed that the area was on the route out of Africa that some scholars suggest was used by early humans to colonize the rest of the Old World.[^cite_note-pmid10811218-42] In 1999, the Eritrean Research Project Team (which was composed of Eritrean, Canadian, American, Dutch, and French scientists) discovered a [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) site with stone and obsidian tools dated to more than 125,000 years old, near the [Gulf of Zula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Zula), south of [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa), along the Red Sea [littoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_zone). The tools are believed to have been used by early humans to harvest marine resources such as clams and oysters.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-Blench143144-46]

### Antiquity

Main articles: [Gash Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gash_Group), [Land of Punt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Punt), and [Dʿmt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%CA%BFmt)



Pre-Axumite monolithic columns in [Qohaito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qohaito)

Tools found in the [Barka Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barka_River), dating from 8,000 BC, appear to offer the first concrete evidence of human settlement in the area.[^cite_note-47] Research also shows that many of the ethnic groups of Eritrea were the first to inhabit these areas.[^cite_note-48]

Excavations in and near [Agordat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agordat), in central Eritrea, yielded the remains of an ancient pre-Aksumite civilization known as the [Gash Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gash_Group).[^cite_note-Leclant402-49] This included ceramics dated to between 2,500 and 1,500 BC.[^cite_note-Cole-50]

Around 2,000 BC, parts of Eritrea were likely part of the [Land of Punt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Punt), a kingdom first mentioned in the twenty-fifth century BC.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-independent.co.uk-52][^cite_note-53] It was known for producing and exporting [gold](12240.md), aromatic [resins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resin), [blackwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalbergia_melanoxylon), [ebony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebony), [ivory](26744317.md), and wild animals. The region is known from [ancient Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian) records of trade expeditions, especially a well-documented expedition to Punt in approximately 1,469 BC, during the reestablishment of disrupted trade routes by [Hatshepsut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatshepsut) and shortly after the beginning of her rule as the pharaoh of ancient Egypt.[^cite_note-Shaw_&_Nicholson,_p.231-54][^cite_note-\:210-55][^cite_note-\:1-56][^cite_note-\:2-57]

Excavations at [Sembel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sembel) found evidence of an ancient, pre-[Aksumite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum) civilization in greater Asmara. This culture is believed to have been among the oldest pastoral and agricultural communities in [East Africa](260987.md). Artifacts at the site have been dated to between 800 BC and 400 BC, contemporaneous with other pre-Aksumite settlements in the Eritrean and Ethiopian highlands during the mid-first millennium BC.[^cite_note-Schmidt-58][^cite_note-Avanzini-59][^cite_note-60]

#### D'mt

Main article: [Dʿmt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%CA%BFmt)



Bronze oil lamp excavated at [Matara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matara,_Eritrea), dating from the [Kingdom of Dʿmt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%CA%BFmt) (first century BC or earlier)

[Dʿmt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%CA%BFmt) was a kingdom that existed from the tenth to fifth centuries BC in what is now Eritrea and northern Ethiopia. Evidence of a massive temple complex at [Yeha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeha) suggests that it was most likely Dʿmt's capital. [Qohaito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qohaito), often identified as the town of Koloe in the *[Periplus of the Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea)*,[^cite_note-61] and [Matara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matara,_Eritrea) were important Dʿmt cities located in southern Eritrea.

The realm developed [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) schemes, used [plows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plow), grew [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), and made [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) tools and weapons. After Dʿmt fell in the fifth century BC, the plateau was dominated by smaller successor kingdoms. This lasted until the first century, when one of these polities, the [Kingdom of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum), was able to reunite the area.[^cite_note-62]

#### Kingdom of Aksum

Main article: [Kingdom of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum)



Some of the buildings of the [Christian](5211.md) monastery complex of [Debre Bizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debre_Bizen) built during as early as 4th century



The [Mosque of the Companions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque_of_the_Companions) in Massawa is believed by some to be the first mosque in Africa, built by the [companions of Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companions_of_Muhammad) in the 7th century.

[^cite_note-Reid2012Massawa-63]

The [Kingdom of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum) (or Axum) was a trading empire centered in Eritrea and northern Ethiopia.[^cite_note-64] It existed from approximately 100–940 AD, growing from the proto-Aksumite [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) period around the fourth century BC and achieving prominence by the first century AD.

According to the medieval *Liber Axumae* ([Book of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Aksum)), Aksum's first capital, Mazaber, was built by Itiyopis, son of Cush.[^cite_note-Agrvt-65] The capital was later moved to [Axum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axum) in northern Ethiopia. The kingdom used the name "Ethiopia" as early as the fourth century.[^cite_note-Munro-Hay57-23][^cite_note-Paul_B._Henze_2005-24]

The Aksumites erected a number of large [stelae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stele), which served a religious purpose in pre-[Christian](5211.md) times. One of these granite columns, the [Obelisk of Aksum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obelisk_of_Aksum), is the largest such structure in the world, standing at 90 feet (27 metres).[^cite_note-Eospvo-66] Under [Ezana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezana_of_Axum) ([fl.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floruit) 320–360), Aksum later adopted Christianity.[^cite_note-Munro-Hay-67]

Christianity was the first [world religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_religion) adopted in modern Eritrea. The oldest monastery, [Debre Sina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debre_Sina_\(monastery\)), dates back to the fourth century, [Debre Libanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debre_Libanos_\(Eritrea\)) was built in the late fifth or early sixth century. Originally located in the village of Ham, it was moved to an inaccessible location on the edge of a cliff below the Ham plateau. Its church contains the Golden Gospel, a metal-covered bible dating to the thirteenth century, during which Debre Libanos was a seat of religious power.[^cite_note-68]

In the seventh century AD, early [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) from [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca), at least [companions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahaba) of the [Islamic prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophets_and_messengers_in_Islam) [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad), sought refuge from [Qurayshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quraysh) [persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Muslims_by_the_Meccans) by travelling to the kingdom, a journey known in [Islamic history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_history) as the [First Hijrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_to_Abyssinia). They reportedly built the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fatah_Mosque,_Kigali) African [mosque](19894.md): the [Mosque of the Companions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque_of_the_Companions) in Massawa.[^cite_note-69]

The kingdom is mentioned in the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* as an important market place for [ivory](26744317.md), which was exported throughout the ancient world. At the time, Aksum was ruled by [Zoskales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoskales), who also governed the port of [Adulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adulis).[^cite_note-Periplusme-70] The Aksumite rulers facilitated trade by minting their own [Aksumite currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksumite_currency).[^cite_note-Raffaele2007-71]

### Early Modern Period

Main articles: [Medri Bahri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medri_Bahri) and [Habesh Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habesh_Eyalet)



Map of Eritrea by [James Bruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bruce), c. 1770

Pre-colonial Eritrea had four distinct regions, divided by geography and in limited contact with each other. The [Abyssinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinian_people), [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language)-speaking Christians controlled the highlands, the nomadic [Tigre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigre_people) and [Beni Amer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beni-Amer_people) clans the western lowlands, the Arabic Muslims the regions [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa) and Dahlak, and the [pastoralist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism) [Afars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people) the [Dankalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Red_Sea_Region) region.[^cite_note-72]

After the decline of Aksum, the Eritrean highlands fell under the domain of the [Christian](5211.md) [Zagwe dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagwe_dynasty), and later the control of the [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire).[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-25] Its coastal territories were also under the [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate) during the reign of Sultan [Badlay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badlay_ibn_Sa'ad_ad-Din).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] The area was first known as *Ma'ikele Bahri* ("between the seas/rivers", i.e. the land between the [Red Sea](26191.md) and the [Mereb river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mareb_River)), and later renamed the Medri Bahri ("Sea land" in Tigrinya).[^cite_note-75] The region, ruled by a local governor called the *[Bahr Negash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_aristocratic_and_court_titles#Important_regional_offices)*, was first documented in an obscure land grant of the 11th-century Zagwe king [Tatadim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatadim). He considered the unnamed Bahr Negash one of his *seyyuman* or "appointed ones".[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Ethiopian Emperor [Zara Yaqob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zara_Yaqob) strengthened imperial presence in the area by increasing the power of the Bahr Negash and placing him above other local chiefs, establishing a military colony of settlers from [Shewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shewa), and forcing the Muslims on the coast to pay tribute.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

The first Westerner to document a visit to Eritrea was Portuguese explorer [Francisco Álvares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_%C3%81lvares) in 1520. He recounted his journey through the principality ruled by the Bahr Negash, highlighting three key cities, with [Debarwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debarwa) as the capital. He then detailed the border demarcation at the [Mereb River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mereb_River) with the province of [Tigray](357350.md) and recounted the difficulties in transporting certain goods across the border. His books have the first description of the local powers of Tigray and the Bahr Negash.[^cite_note-81]



1690 map of Ethiopia (Abyssinia) by [Vincenzo Coronelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Coronelli) illustrating *Midrabahr* (Midri Bahri) in the northern part of [Abyssinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinia)

The contemporary coast of Eritrea formed a route to the region of Tigray, where the Portuguese had a small colony, and to the interior [Ethiopian](187749.md) allies of the Portuguese. Massawa was also the stage for the 1541 landing of troops by [Cristóvão da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crist%C3%B3v%C3%A3o_da_Gama) in the military campaign that eventually defeated the [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate) in the [battle of Wayna Daga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wayna_Daga) in 1543.[^cite_note-82]

By 1557, the [Ottomans](22278.md) had succeeded in occupying all of northeastern present-day Eritrea for the following two decades, an area that stretched from Massawa to [Swakin](1124174.md) in Sudan.[^cite_note-MB01-83] The territory became an Ottoman governorate, known as the [Habesh Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habesh_Eyalet), with a capital at Massawa. When the city became of secondary economic importance, the administrative capital moved across the Red Sea to [Jeddah](277718.md).[^cite_note-encaet-84] The Turks tried to occupy the highlands of Eritrea in 1559, but withdrew after they encountered resistance, pushed back by the Bahri Negash and highland forces. In 1578 they tried to expand into the highlands with the help of Bahri Negash Yisehaq, who had switched alliances due to a power struggle. Ethiopian Emperor [Sarsa Dengel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarsa_Dengel) made a punitive expedition against the Turks in 1588 in response to their raids in the northern provinces, and apparently, by 1589, they were once again compelled to withdraw to the coast. The Ottomans were eventually driven out in the last quarter of the sixteenth century. However, they retained control over the seaboard until the establishment of [Italian Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea) in the late 1800s.[^cite_note-MB01-83][^cite_note-A10-4-85][^cite_note-A10-5-86]

In 1734, the [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people) leader Kedafu established the [Mudaito Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudaito_Dynasty) in Ethiopia, which later also came to include the southern Denkel lowlands of Eritrea, thus incorporating the southern Denkel lowlands into the [Sultanate of Aussa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Aussa).[^cite_note-MB01-83][^cite_note-Pankhurstteb-87] The northern coastline of Denkel was dominated by a number of smaller Afar sultanates, such as the Sultanate of [Rahayta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahayta), the Sultanate of [Beylul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beylul) and the Sultanate of [Bidu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidu_\(woreda\)).[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-Aesna-89][^cite_note-90]

### Italian Eritrea

Main article: [Italian Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea)



[Eritrean Ascaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Ascari), colonial troops of the Italian Army, in an 1898 wood engraving

The boundaries of present-day Eritrea were established during the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa). On 15 November 1869, the ruling local chief sold lands surrounding the Bay of [Assab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assab) to the Italian missionary [Giuseppe Sapeto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Sapeto), on behalf of the [Rubattino Shipping Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Societ%C3%A0_di_Navigazione_Rubattino).[^cite_note-EBAb-91] The area served as a [coaling station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaling_station) along the [shipping lanes](2018420.md) introduced by the recently completed [Suez Canal](29323.md). In 1882, the Italian government formally took possession of the Assab colony from its commercial owners and expanded their control to include [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa) (and most of the Eritrean coastal lowlands) after the Egyptians withdrew from Eritrea in February 1885.[^cite_note-Ully-92]

In the [power vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_vacuum) that followed the [1889 death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gallabat) of [Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Ethiopia) [Yohannes IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yohannes_IV), Gen. [Oreste Baratieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oreste_Baratieri) occupied the highlands along the Eritrean coast and Italy proclaimed the establishment of [Italian Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea), a colony of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy). In the [Treaty of Wuchale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Wuchale) (It. *Uccialli*) signed the same year, [Menelik II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelik_II) of [Shewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shewa), a southern Ethiopian kingdom, recognized the Italian occupation of his rivals' lands of [Bogos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogos), [Hamasien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamasien), [Akkele Guzay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkele_Guzay), and [Serae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serae) in exchange for guarantees of financial assistance and continuing access to European arms and ammunition. His subsequent victory over rival kings and enthronement as Emperor Menelik II (r. 1889–1913) made the treaty formally binding upon the entire territory.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-25]



View of Italian Asmara

In 1888, the Italian administration launched its first development projects in the new colony. The [Eritrean Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Railway) was completed to Saati in 1888,[^cite_note-94] and reached [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara) in the highlands in 1911.[^cite_note-f1-95] The [Asmara–Massawa Cableway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara-Massawa_Cableway) was the longest line in the world during its time but was later dismantled by the British in World War II. Besides major infrastructural projects, the colonial authorities invested significantly in the agricultural sector. They also oversaw the provision of urban amenities in Asmara and Massawa, and employed many Eritreans in public service, particularly in the police and public works departments.[^cite_note-f1-95] Thousands of Eritreans were concurrently enlisted in the Italian army, serving during the [Italo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Turkish_War) in Libya as well as the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Italo-Ethiopian_War) and [Second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Italo-Ethiopian_War) Italo-Abyssinian Wars.

Additionally, the Italian Eritrea administration opened many new factories that produced buttons, cooking oil, pasta, construction materials, packing meat, tobacco, hide, and other household commodities. In 1939, there were approximately 2,198 factories and most of the employees were Eritrean citizens. The establishment of industry also increased the populations of Italians and Eritreans residing in cities. The number of Italians in the territory increased from 4,600 to 75,000 in five years; and with the involvement of Eritreans in the industry, trade and fruit plantations were expanded across the nation. Some plantations were owned by Eritreans.[^cite_note-96]

In 1922, [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini)'s rise to power in Italy brought profound changes to the colonial government in Italian Eritrea. After *[il Duce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Duce)* declared the birth of the [Italian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Empire) in May 1936, Italian Eritrea (enlarged with northern Ethiopia's regions) and [Italian Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland) were merged with the just-conquered Ethiopia into the new [Italian East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_East_Africa) (*Africa Orientale Italiana*). This [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascists) period was characterized by imperial expansion in the name of a "new Roman Empire". Eritrea was chosen by the Italian government to be the industrial center of Italian East Africa.[^cite_note-97]

After 1935, [art deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_deco) architecture was widely employed in Asmara. The Italians designed more than 400 buildings in a construction boom that only halted with Italy's involvement in [World War II](32927.md). These included the [Fiat Tagliero Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Tagliero_Building) and [Cinema Impero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_Impero).[^cite_note-98] In 2017, the city was declared a [World Heritage Site](44940.md), described by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as featuring *eclectic and rationalist built forms, well-defined open spaces, and public and private buildings, including cinemas, shops, banks, religious structures, public and private offices, industrial facilities, and residences*.[^cite_note-99])

### British administration



Port of [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa) in the late 1940s

Through the 1941 [Battle of Keren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Keren), the British expelled the Italians and took over the administration of the country.[^cite_note-100] The decade of British administration saw significant restructuring of the Eritrean economy. Until 1945, the British and Americans relied on Italian equipment and skilled labor for wartime needs and Allied support in the Middle East. This economic boom, fueled by substantial Italian involvement, lasted until the end of the war, when the Eritrean economy faced a combination of recession and depression. War factories that had employed thousands shut down, and Italians began to be repatriated. Additionally, many small manufacturing plants established between 1936 and 1945 were forced to close due to intense competition from factories in Europe and the Middle East.[^cite_note-101]

The British placed Eritrea under British military administration until [Allied forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) could decide its fate. In the absence of agreement amongst the Allies concerning Eritrea's status, the British administration continued until 1950. During the immediate postwar years, the British proposed that Eritrea be divided along religious community lines and annexed partly to the British colony of Sudan and partly to Ethiopia. After the peace treaty with Italy was signed in 1947, the [United Nations](31769.md) sent a Commission of Enquiry to decide the fate of the colony.[^cite_note-auto5-102]

### Federation with Ethiopia

Main article: [Federation of Ethiopia and Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea)



[Eritrean War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence) against Ethiopia 1961–1991

In the 1950s, the Ethiopian semi-feudal state under Emperor [Haile Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Selassie_of_Ethiopia) sought to annex Eritrea and Italian Somaliland. Selassie laid claim to both territories in a letter to [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) at the Paris Peace Conference and the First Session of the United Nations.[^cite_note-un-103] In the United Nations, the debate over the former Italian colonies continued. The British and Americans preferred to cede all of Eritrea except the Western province to the Ethiopians, as a reward for their support during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-104] The Independence Bloc of Eritrean parties consistently requested for the United Nations General Assembly to hold an immediate referendum to settle the question of Eritrean sovereignty.

The United Nations Commission of Enquiry arrived in Eritrea in early 1950, and after about six weeks, returned to New York to submit its report. Two reports were presented. The minority report, presented by Pakistan and Guatemala, proposed that Eritrea be independent after a period of trusteeship. The majority report, presented by Burma, Norway, and the Union of South Africa, called for Eritrea to be incorporated into Ethiopia.[^cite_note-auto5-102]

Following the adoption of [U.N. Resolution 390A(V)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/United%20Nations%20General%20Assembly%20Resolution%20390) in December 1950, Eritrea was [federated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) with Ethiopia under the prompting of the United States.[^cite_note-UNGA390-105] The resolution called for Eritrea and Ethiopia to be linked through a loose federal structure under the sovereignty of the emperor. Eritrea was to have its own administrative and judicial structure, its own new flag, and control over its domestic affairs, including police, local administration, and taxation.[^cite_note-un-103] The federal government, which for all practical purposes was the existing imperial government, was to control foreign affairs (including commerce), defense, finance, and transportation. The resolution ignored the wishes of Eritreans for independence, but did guarantee the population democratic rights and a measure of autonomy.[^cite_note-auto5-102]

### Independence

Main articles: [Eritrean War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence) and [Eritrea under Isaias Afwerki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea_under_Isaias_Afwerki)



Fighters of the [Eritrean People's Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_People's_Liberation_Front) during the [Eritrean War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence) (1961–1991)



A view over [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara). Because of the Italian architecture of the city, it was called "[Little Rome](25458.md)".

[^cite_note-106]

In 1958, a group of Eritreans founded the Eritrean Liberation Movement (ELM). The organization mainly consisted of Eritrean students, professionals, and intellectuals. It engaged in clandestine political activities intended to cultivate resistance to the centralizing policies of the imperial Ethiopian state.[^cite_note-107] On 1 September 1961, the [Eritrean Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Liberation_Front) (ELF), under the leadership of [Hamid Idris Awate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Idris_Awate), waged an armed struggle for independence. In 1962, Emperor [Haile Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Selassie) unilaterally dissolved the Eritrean parliament and [annexed](18950961.md) the territory. The ensuing [Eritrean War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence) went on for 30 years against successive Ethiopian governments until 1991, when the [Eritrean People's Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_People's_Liberation_Front) (EPLF), a successor of the ELF, defeated the Ethiopian forces in Eritrea and helped a coalition of [Ethiopian rebel forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_People's_Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) take control of the Ethiopian capital [Addis Ababa](52593.md).

In the 1980s, a non-government organization called the Eritrea Inter-Agency Consortium (EIAC) aided in the development projects for the Eritrean Liberation movement.[^cite_note-108]

Following a [referendum in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Eritrean_independence_referendum) supervised by the United Nations (dubbed UNOVER) in which the Eritrean people overwhelmingly voted for independence, Eritrea declared its independence and gained international recognition in 1993.[^cite_note-109] The EPLF seized power, established a one-party state along nationalist lines and banned further political activity. As of 2024, there have been no elections.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-Voice_of_America-111][^cite_note-Bloomberg-112][^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-113][^cite_note-114] On May 28, 1993, Eritrea was admitted into the [United Nations](31769.md) as the 182nd member state.[^cite_note-115]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Eritrea)



Topography of Eritrea



Satellite image of Eritrea

Eritrea is located in [East Africa](260987.md). It is bordered to the northeast and east by the [Red Sea](26191.md), [Sudan](27421.md) to the west, [Ethiopia](187749.md) to the south, and [Djibouti](17207794.md) to the southeast. Eritrea lies between latitudes 12° and 18°N, and longitudes 36° and 44°E.

The country is virtually bisected by a branch of the [East African Rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Rift). Eritrea, at the southern end of the Red Sea, holds the fork in the rift. The [Dahlak Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlak_Archipelago) and its fishing grounds are situated off the sandy, arid coastline.

Eritrea is split into three [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion). Extending along the coast, a hot, arid plain is narrow in the west and widens towards the east. These coastal lowlands are part of the [Djibouti xeric shrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti_xeric_shrublands) ecoregion. The cooler, more fertile [highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Highlands) reach up to 3,000 m (9,800 ft) and are a northern extension of the [Ethiopian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands), home to [montane grasslands and woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_montane_grasslands_and_woodlands).[^cite_note-Friis-116] Habitats here vary, from the [sub-tropical rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_moist_broadleaf_forests) at Filfil Solomona to the precipitous cliffs and [canyons](78478.md) of the southern highlands.[^cite_note-117] [Filfil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filfil) receives over 1,100 mm of rainfall annually.[^cite_note-118] There is a steep escarpment along the eastern side of the highlands, which is the western wall of the East African Rift. The western slope of the highlands is more gradual, descending to interior lowlands. Southwestern Eritrea is drained by the [Atbara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atbara_River), which flows northwestwards to join the [Nile](21244.md). The northwestern slope of the highlands is drained by the [Barka River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barka_River), which flows northwards into Sudan to empty into the Red Sea.[^cite_note-119] Western Eritrea is part of the [Sahelian Acacia savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahelian_Acacia_savanna), which extends across Africa south of the [Sahara](325363.md) from Eritrea to Senegal.[^cite_note-120]

The [Afar Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_Triangle) or Danakil Depression of Eritrea is the probable location of a [triple junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_junction) where three tectonic plates are pulling away from one another. The highest point of the country, [Emba Soira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emba_Soira), is located in the center of Eritrea, at 3,018 m (9,902 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level). Eritrea has volcanic activity in the southeastern parts of the country. In 2011, [Nabro Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabro_Volcano) erupted.

Eritrea's major urban areas include its capital city [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara), the port town of [Asseb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asseb) in the southeast, the towns of [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa) to the east, the town of [Keren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keren,_Eritrea) to the north, and the central town [Mendefera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendefera).

Local variability in rainfall patterns and reduced precipitation are known to occur, which may precipitate soil [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion), floods, droughts, [land degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_degradation), and [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification).[^cite_note-121]

Eritrea is part of a 14-nation constituency within the [Global Environment Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Environment_Facility), which partners with international institutions, civil society organizations, and the private sector to address global environmental issues while supporting national sustainable development initiatives.[^cite_note-122]

In 2006, Eritrea announced that it would become the world's first country to make its entire coast an environmentally protected zone. The 1,347 km (837 mi) coastline, along with another 1,946 km (1,209 mi) of coast around its hundreds of islands, will come under governmental protection.

### Climate

Eritrea can be broadly divided into three major climate zones: the [temperate zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_zone), [subtropical climate zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate), and [tropical climate zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate_zone).[^cite_note-123]



Eritrea's climate according to [Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)

The climate of Eritrea is shaped by its diverse topographical features and its location within the tropics. The variation of landscape and topography across the highlands and lowlands of Eritrea creates a diversity of climate. The highlands have a temperate climate throughout the year. The climate of most lowland zones is arid and semi-arid. Distribution of rainfall and vegetation types varies markedly throughout the country. Eritrean climate also varies based on seasonal and altitudinal differences.

Due to its physical diversity, Eritrea is one of the few countries where one can experience "four seasons in a day".[^cite_note-124] In the highlands (up to 3,000 [m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) or 9,800 [ft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_\(unit\)) above sea level), the hottest month is usually May, with temperatures reaching 30 °C (86 °F), whereas winter occurs during December to February when temperatures can be as low as 10 °C (50 °F) at night. The capital, Asmara, is temperate year-round.

In the lowlands and the coastal areas, summer occurs from June to September, when temperatures can reach 40 °C (104 °F). Winter in the lowlands occurs from February to April, when temperatures are between 21 and 35 °C (70 and 95 °F).[^cite_note-125]

A 2022 analysis found that the expected costs for Eritrea to adapt to and avert the environmental consequences of [climate change](5042951.md) are high.[^cite_note-126]

### Biodiversity

Main article: [Wildlife of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Eritrea)

See also: [List of mammals in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_in_Eritrea) and [List of birds of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_birds_of_Eritrea)



[Pelicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelicans) in May sirwa near [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara)

Eritrea has a rich avifauna of 560 species of birds[^cite_note-127] and is home to a large number of mammals; 126 species of mammals, 90 species of reptiles, and 19 species of amphibians have been recorded.[^cite_note-Main_Details-128] Enforced regulations have helped in steadily increasing wildlife numbers throughout Eritrea.[^cite_note-129] Mammals commonly seen today include the [Abyssinian hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyssinian_hare), [African wild cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_wild_cat), [Black-backed jackal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-backed_jackal), [African golden wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_golden_wolf), [genet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genet_\(animal\)), [ground squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_squirrel), [pale fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_fox), [Soemmerring's gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soemmerring's_gazelle), and [warthog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warthog). [Dorcas gazelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorcas_gazelle) are common on the coastal plains and in [Gash-Barka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gash-Barka_Region).

[Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lions) are said to inhabit the mountains of the [Gash-Barka region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gash-Barka_region). [Dik-diks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dik-diks) may be found in many areas. The endangered [African wild ass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_wild_ass) may be seen in the [Denakalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denkalia) Region. Other local wildlife include [bushbuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_bushbuck), [duikers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duikers), [greater kudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_kudu), [Klipspringer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klipspringer), [African leopards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_leopards), [oryx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oryx), and [crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodiles).[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-ibis.atwebpages.com-131] The [spotted hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_hyena) is widespread and fairly common.



Habitat near road to Massawa



Madote Island, one of Eritrea's 200 islands, part of [Dahlak Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlak_Archipelago) Marine National Park

Historically, a small population of [African bush elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_bush_elephant) roamed some parts of the country. Between 1955 and 2001, there were no reported sightings of elephant herds, however, and they were thought to have fallen victim to the [War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence). In December 2001, a herd of approximately 30 elephants, including 10 juveniles, was observed near the [Gash River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gash_River). The elephants seemed to have formed a [symbiotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiosis) relationship with [olive baboons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_baboon): the baboons use water holes dug by the elephants, and the elephants use baboon vocalizations from the tree tops as an early warning system. It is estimated that there are approximately 100 [African bush elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephants) left in Eritrea.[^cite_note-132]

The endangered [African wild dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_wild_dog) (*[Lycaon pictus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycaon_pictus)*) was previously found in Eritrea but is now deemed [extirpated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extirpated) from the entire country.[^cite_note-133] In Gash-Barka, snakes such as the [saw-scaled viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echis_pyramidum) are common. [Puff adders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitis_arietans) and [red spitting cobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_spitting_cobra) are widespread and may be found in the highlands. In coastal areas, common marine species include [dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin), [dugongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugong), [whale sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_shark), [turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtles), [marlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlin), [swordfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swordfish), and [manta rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manta_ray).[^cite_note-ibis.atwebpages.com-131] 500 fish species, 5 marine turtles, and at least 8 cetaceans have been recorded in the country.[^cite_note-Main_Details-128]

Eritrea also harbours many [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) species, including insects, amphibians, mammals, reptiles, and plants.[^cite_note-134]

Over 700 plants have been recorded in Eritrea, including marine plants and seagrass.[^cite_note-Main_Details-128][^cite_note-135] 26% of Eritrea is [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land).[^cite_note-136] Eritrea has many diverse habitats, including tropical and subtropical grasslands, [savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna), shrublands, [xeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserts_and_xeric_shrublands) shrublands, deserts, tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests and [mangrove forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove_forest).[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

All of Eritrea's national parks are protected, which include Dahlak Marine National Park, Nakfa Wildlife Reserve, Gash-Setit Wildlife Refuge, Semenawi Bahri National Park, and Yob Wildlife Reserve.[^cite_note-139]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Eritrea)

See also: [G-15 (Eritrea)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-15_\(Eritrea\)) and [National Assembly (Eritrea)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Eritrea\))



[Isaias Afwerki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaias_Afwerki)\
[President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Eritrea)&#x20;



President [Isaias Afwerki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaias_Afwerki) with U.S. Secretary of Defense [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld), December 2002

The [People's Front for Democracy and Justice](404603.md) (PFDJ) is the [only legal party](102459.md) in Eritrea.[^cite_note-BBC-140] Other political groups are not allowed to organize, though the unimplemented [Constitution of 1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Eritrea) provides for the existence of [multi-party politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). The National Assembly has 150 seats. National elections have been periodically scheduled and cancelled; as of 2025, none have ever been held in the country.[^cite_note-CIA-31] President [Isaias Afwerki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaias_Afwerki) has been in office since independence in 1993.

In May 2001, a group of senior government officials and prominent members of the ruling party ([PFDJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFDJ)), known as the [G-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-15_\(Eritrea\)), wrote an open letter to President Isaias Afwerki. This letter called the president to implement the ratified [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Eritrea), enact democratic reforms, and establish the rule of law. In September 2001, the Eritrean government arrested 11 of the signatories and subjected them to [incommunicado detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incommunicado_detention). The crackdown also targeted independent media outlets and multiple independent journalists, including [Dawit Isaac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawit_Isaak).[^cite_note-141] To this day, they remain imprisoned without charge or trial.[^cite_note-guardian-142][^cite_note-Eritrean_G-15_advocacy_site-143]

In 1993, 75 representatives were elected to the National Assembly; the rest were appointed. As the report by the United Nations Human Rights Council explained: "No national elections have taken place since that time, and no presidential elections have ever taken place. Local or regional elections have not been held since 2003–2004. The National Assembly elected independent Eritrea's first president, Isaias Afwerki, in 1993. Following his election, Afwerki consolidated his control of the Eritrean government." President Isaias Afwerki has regularly expressed his disdain for what he refers to as "Western-style" democracy. In a 2008 interview with [Al Jazeera](30204052.md), for example, the president stated that "Eritrea will wait three or four decades, maybe more, before it holds elections. Who knows?"[^cite_note-144] According to 2023 [V-Dem Democracy indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-Dem_Democracy_indices), Eritrea is 2nd lowest ranked worldwide and the lowest ranked [electoral democracy in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Africa).[^cite_note-vdem_dataset-145]

However, local elections were held for a time in Eritrea. The most recent round of local government elections were in 2010 and 2011.[^cite_note-146]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Eritrea)



The 23d ISCOE [East Africa](260987.md) Conference in [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara) in 2019



[Isaias Afwerki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaias_Afwerki) with Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) at the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin) in [Moscow](19004.md) on 31 May 2023

Eritrea is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md) and the [African Union](10634933.md). It is an [observing member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab%E2%80%93Eritrean_relations) of the [Arab League](52625.md), alongside Brazil and [Venezuela](32374.md).[^cite_note-CNN-36] The nation holds a seat on the United Nations Advisory Committee on Administrative and Budgetary Questions (ACABQ). Eritrea also holds memberships in the [International Bank for Reconstruction and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bank_for_Reconstruction_and_Development), [International Finance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Finance_Corporation), [International Criminal Police Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Police_Organization) (INTERPOL), [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement), [Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons](36832.md), [Permanent Court of Arbitration](38058.md), [Port Management Association of Eastern and Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Management_Association_of_Eastern_and_Southern_Africa), and the [World Customs Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Customs_Organization).

The Eritrean government previously withdrew its representative to the African Union to protest the AU's alleged lack of leadership in facilitating the implementation of a binding decision demarcating the border between Eritrea and Ethiopia. Since January 2011, the Eritrean government has appointed an envoy, Tesfa-Alem Tekle, to the AU.[^cite_note-147]

Its relations with Djibouti and Yemen are tense, due to territorial disputes over the [Doumeira Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doumeira_Islands) and [Hanish Islands](3542308.md) respectively.

On 28 May 2019, the United States removed Eritrea from the "Counterterror Non-Cooperation List" which also includes [Iran](14653.md), [North Korea](21255.md), [Syria](7515849.md), and [Venezuela](32374.md).[^cite_note-148] Moreover, Eritrea was visited two months earlier by a U.S. congressional delegation for the first time in 14 years.[^cite_note-149]

Along with [Belarus](3457.md), Syria, and North Korea, Eritrea was one of only four countries not including Russia to vote against a [United Nations General Assembly resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_emergency_special_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly) condemning Russia's [2022 invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-150]

#### Relations with Ethiopia

See also: [Eritrea–Ethiopia relations](57858711.md) and [Eritrean–Ethiopian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean%E2%80%93Ethiopian_War)

Further information: [Eritrean War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence) and [1993 Eritrean independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Eritrean_independence_referendum)



[Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Eritrea\)) is one of the most important public holidays in Eritrea.



[Commemoration & celebration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Eritrea) during [Martyrs' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrs'_Day) in Asmara

After the 30-year war for Eritrean independence, Eritrea and Ethiopia entered a relationship of cautious mutual tolerance which lasted until May 1998. The [Eritrean–Ethiopian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean%E2%80%93Ethiopian_War) lasted from 1998 to 2000, claimed approximately 70,000 lives from both sides[^cite_note-151] and cost hundreds of millions of dollars.[^cite_note-152] The impetus involved a major border conflict, notably around [Badme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badme) and [Zalambessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalambessa), and was not resolved until 2018.[^cite_note-153]

Disagreements following the war have resulted in stalemates punctuated by periods of elevated tension and renewed threats of war.[^cite_note-horntension1-154][^cite_note-155][^cite_note-horntension3-156] The stalemate led the president of Eritrea to urge the UN to take action on Ethiopia, with the [Eleven Letters](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Eleven%20Letters) penned by the president to the United Nations Security Council. The situation has been further escalated by Eritrean and Ethiopian leaders' continued support to the opposition in each other's countries.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] In 2011, the [United Nations](31769.md) released a report which stated that Eritrean agents planted bombs at an [African Union](10634933.md) summit in [Addis Ababa](52593.md). Eritrea denied the claims.[^cite_note-aubomb-159][^cite_note-160]

A peace treaty between both nations was signed on 9 July 2018.[^cite_note-161] The next day, they signed a joint declaration that formally ended the [Eritrean–Ethiopian border conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean%E2%80%93Ethiopian_border_conflict).[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]

In 2020, Eritrean troops intervened in the [Tigray War](65760352.md) on the side of the Ethiopian government.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-Voice_of_America-111][^cite_note-Bloomberg-112][^cite_note-The_Washington_Post-113] In April 2021, Eritrea confirmed its troops were fighting in Ethiopia.[^cite_note-165]

### Military

Main article: [Eritrean Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Defence_Forces)



[Eritrean Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Navy), branch of the [Eritrean Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Defence_Forces) during ceremony

The [Eritrean Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Defence_Forces) are the official [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_forces) of the State of Eritrea, which are one of the largest in Africa.[^cite_note-166]

Compulsory military service was instituted in 1995. Officially, [conscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_Eritrea), male and female, must serve for 18 months minimum, which includes six months of military training and 12 months during the regular school year to complete their last year of high school. Since the 2000s, national service has been open-ended, and the practice has received significant international criticism.[^cite_note-167]

The National Service Proclamation of 1995 does not recognize the right to conscientious objection to military service. According to the 1957 Ethiopian penal code Eritrea adopted during independence, failure to enlist in the military or refusal to perform military service are punishable with imprisonment terms of six months to five years and up to ten years, respectively.[^cite_note-168] National service enlistment times may be extended during times of "national crisis"; since 1998, everyone under the age of 50 is enlisted in national service for an indefinite period until released, which may depend on the arbitrary decision of a commander. In a study of 200 escaped conscripts, the average service was 6.5 years, and some had served more than 12 years.[^cite_note-National_service_in_Eritrea-169]

### Legal profession

According to the [NYU School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYU_School_of_Law), the Legal Committee of the Ministry of Justice oversees the admission and requirements to practice law in Eritrea. Although the establishment of an independent bar association is not proscribed under Proclamation 88/96, among other domestic laws, there is no bar association. The community electorate in the local jurisdiction of the Community Court chooses the court judges. The Community Court's standing on women in the legal profession is unclear, but elected women judges have reserved seats.[^cite_note-170]

### Human rights

Main article: [Human rights in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Eritrea)



Building of regional administration in [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara)

Eritrea is a [one-party state](102459.md) in which national legislative elections have been repeatedly postponed.[^cite_note-gi-32] According to [Human Rights Watch](46530.md), the government's [human rights](13831.md) record is considered among the worst in the world.[^cite_note-hrw-34] Most countries have accused the Eritrean authorities of [arbitrary arrest and detentions](13435705.md), and of detaining an unknown number of people without charge for their political activism. According to the [US Department of State](31975.md), male and female [same-sex sexual activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Eritrea) are illegal in Eritrea, although the Eritrean government does not actively enforce this law.[^cite_note-171]

The nation's human rights record has been criticized by the United Nations.[^cite_note-Guardian-172] Human rights violations are allegedly often committed by the government or on behalf of the government. [Freedom of speech](21401843.md), [press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media), [assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_assembly), and [association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpersonal_relationship) are limited. Those who practice "unregistered" religions, try to flee the nation, or escape military duty are arrested and put into [prison](19008450.md).[^cite_note-Guardian-172] By 2009, the number of political prisoners was in the range of 10,000–30,000, there was widespread and systematic [torture](47702.md) and [extrajudicial killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_killing), with "anyone" for "any or no reason", including children of eight years old, people more than 80 years old, and ill people, and Eritrea was "one of the world's most totalitarian and human rights-abusing regimes".[^cite_note-EHREA_Lasting_Struggle-173] During the Eritrean independence struggle and 1998 [Eritrean-Ethiopian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean-Ethiopian_War), many atrocities were committed by the Ethiopian authorities against unarmed Eritrean civilians.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175]



[Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara), Eritrea in 2015



Traditional Eritrean agudo/tukul huts in a village near [Barentu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barentu,_Eritrea)

In June 2016, a 500-page [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md) report accused the Eritrean government of extrajudicial executions, torture, indefinitely prolonged national service (6.5 years on average), and forced labour, and it indicated that among state officials, sexual harassment, rape, and sexual servitude practices are widespread.[^cite_note-UNHRC-3][^cite_note-guard-176] [Barbara Lochbihler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Lochbihler) of the [European Parliament Subcommittee on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament_Subcommittee_on_Human_Rights) said the report detailed 'very serious human rights violations', and asserted that EU funding for development would not continue as at present without change in Eritrea.[^cite_note-DWelle-177] The Eritrean Foreign Ministry responded by describing the commission's report as being "wild allegations" that were "totally unfounded and devoid of all merit".[^cite_note-178] Representatives of the United States and China disputed the report's language and accuracy.[^cite_note-179]

All Eritreans between 18 and 40 years of age must complete mandatory national service, which includes military service. This requirement was implemented after Eritrea gained [independence](62450.md) from Ethiopia, as a means to protect Eritrea's [sovereignty](28791.md), to instill national pride, and to create a disciplined populace.[^cite_note-National_service_in_Eritrea-169] Eritrea's national service requires long, indefinite conscription (6.5 years on average), which some Eritreans leave the country to avoid.[^cite_note-National_service_in_Eritrea-169][^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

In an attempt at reform, Eritrean government officials and NGO representatives in 2006 participated in many public meetings and dialogues. In these sessions, they answered questions as fundamental as, "What are human rights?", "Who determines what are human rights?", and "What should take precedence, human or communal rights?".[^cite_note-182]

In 2007, the Eritrean government banned [female genital mutilation](11408.md).[^cite_note-183] In Regional Assemblies and religious circles, Eritreans speak out continuously against the use of female circumcision, citing health concerns and individual freedom as primary concerns. Furthermore, they implore rural peoples to cast away this ancient cultural practice.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185]

In 2009, a movement called Citizens for Democratic Rights in Eritrea was formed to create dialogue between the government and political opposition. The group consists of ordinary citizens and some people close to the government.[^cite_note-186] Since the movement's creation, no significant effort has been made by the Eritrean government to improve its record on human rights.

In July 2019, UN ambassadors of 37 countries, including Eritrea, signed a joint letter to the [UNHRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHRC) defending China's treatment of [Uyghurs](31783.md) and other Muslim minority groups in the [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) region.[^cite_note-187] Eritrea continued this support in 2020.[^cite_note-188]

Eritrea claims [Western media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_media) stories of the country are decontextualized, sometimes fabricated, and usually deployed to build a regime change narrative.[^cite_note-auto-189] It claims it's being targeted for not conforming to the West's agenda for African countries,[^cite_note-190] for instance by refusing to accept humanitarian foreign aid. Eritrea aspires to be self-reliant and, since 2005, has rejected foreign aid because it sees aid as a hindrance to true economic development. In 2006 alone, Eritrea walked away from US\$200 million in foreign aid and refused a US\$100 million loan from the [World Bank](36752.md).[^cite_note-191] Besides accusing the [West](21208200.md) of demonization through [smear campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smear_campaign), it also sees itself as targeted by sanctions and [Western-supported war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxy_war) against Eritrea through the Ethiopian [TPLF](1276759.md).[^cite_note-192] It also accuses the West of luring Eritreans abroad by granting many Eritreans political asylum.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195][^cite_note-auto-189]

### Media freedom

Main article: [Freedom of press in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_press_in_Eritrea)

See also: [Mass media in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Eritrea)

Eritrea lacks media freedom, as all independent media has been banned since September 2001. No foreign or national media are authorised to operate in the country.[^cite_note-censored-196][^cite_note-197] All media outlets in Eritrea are from the government's Ministry of Information. The state-owned news agency censors news about external events.[^cite_note-censored-196] In 2026, [Press Freedom Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Freedom_Index) and [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md) rated Eritrea's press freedom the lowest out of 180 countries. Eritrea also received the last place in 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2021, 2024, 2025, and 2026.[^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199] According to the [BBC](19344654.md), "Eritrea is the only African country to have no privately owned news media",[^cite_note-200] and Reporters Without Borders said of the public media, "\[They] do nothing but relay the regime's belligerent and ultra-nationalist discourse... Not a single \[foreign correspondent] now lives in Asmara."[^cite_note-201] In December 2024, the [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists) found Eritrea to be the worst jailer of journalists in Sub-Saharan Africa, keeping 16 journalists in prison without trial.[^cite_note-202]

The 2024 [Edelstam Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edelstam_Prize) was awarded to journalist [Dawit Isaak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawit_Isaak), whom Eritrean authorities have imprisoned since 2001 without [legal process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_process).[^cite_note-Edelstam-203]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Eritrea)

In 2020, the IMF estimated Eritrea's [GDP](12594.md) at \$2.1 billion, or \$6.4 billion on a [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) basis.[^cite_note-204] Between 2016 and 2019, Eritrea had a GDP growth between 7.6% and 10.2%, down from the peak at 30.9% in 2014. In 2023, the GDP growth is expected to be 2.8%, a decrease due to factors such as the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and the effects of COVID-19 on value chains. However, the country's economy is expecting a steady growth in coming years.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207]

In 2021, mining and agriculture each accounted for 20% of the GDP. As of 2020, [remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance) from abroad were estimated to account for 12% of GDP.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-\:3-209]

### Mining



Mine site, Bisha Eritrea

In 2021, mining accounted for about 20% of GDP.[^cite_note-\:3-209] In 2013, the pickup in growth had been attributed to the commencement of full operations in the gold and silver [Bisha Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisha_Mine) by Canadian Nevsun Resources (now Chinese [Zijin Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zijin_Mining)[^cite_note-210]), the production of cement from the cement factory in [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa),[^cite_note-afdb-211] and investment in Eritrea's [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), and [Colluli potash mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colluli_mine) operations by Australian[^cite_note-Mining_Technology_2019-212] and Chinese[^cite_note-Bloomberg_2018-213] mining companies.

### Agriculture



Gerset dam (20 million cubic meters) in Eritrea, one of the hundreds of dams and micro-dams built since independence

[^cite_note-auto4-214]

This section is an excerpt from [Agriculture in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Eritrea).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agriculture_in_Eritrea\&action=edit)]

[Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) is one of the main economic activities in Eritrea. Agriculture made up 20% of the country its GDP in 2021.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Eritrea_autogenerated1-216] Eritrea has 565,000 hectares (1,396,000 acres) of [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land) and permanent crops. 70% of the Eritrean workforce is employed in agriculture,[^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Eritrea_huffingtonpost.com-217] accounting for roughly one-third of the economy.[^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Eritrea_Eritrea_World_Bank_2019-218] Eritrea's main agricultural products include [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum), [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [legumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legume), [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable), [fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit), [sesame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame), [linseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linseed), [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep), [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat), and [camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel).[^cite_note-219]

Since independence, Eritrea has constructed 187 dams, each with a capacity of over 50,000 m3 and the biggest ones with a capacity of 350 million m3 in size. These have been built to combat drought and for agriculture, fishing, and energy purposes. In addition, 600 micro-dams have been built.[^cite_note-auto4-214][^cite_note-shabait-220]

### Energy

Annual consumption of petroleum in 2001 was estimated at 370,000 tons. Eritrea has no domestic petroleum production; the Eritrean Petroleum Corporation conducts purchases through international competitive tender. According to the U.S. Department of Commerce, opportunities exist for both on- and offshore oil and natural gas exploration; however, these prospects have yet to come to fruition. The use of [wind energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_energy), [solar power](13690575.md), and [hydropower](14073.md) has slightly increased, due to the growth of solar power manufacturing companies in the country. The Eritrean government has expressed interest in developing alternative energy sources, including geothermal, solar, and wind power.[^cite_note-cp-221]

### Tourism



Tourists on a steam railway excursion from [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara)



Steam train above the clouds, between Asmara and Massawa, on the [Eritrean Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Railway)

Tourism made up 2% of [Eritrea's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Eritrea) up to 1997. After 1998, revenue from the industry fell to one-quarter of 1997 levels. In 2006, it made up less than 1% of the country's GDP.[^cite_note-monitor-222]

Eritrea is a member of [World Tourism Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tourism_Organization) which calculated that the country's international tourism receipts in 2002 were US\$73 million.[^cite_note-gov-223] Sources from 2015 state that most tourists are members of the Eritrean diaspora. Overall visitors have steadily increased in recent years and annual visitors were 142,000 as of 2016.[^cite_note-224]

Tourism in Eritrea has seen increased attention in later years. For instance, in 2019, the country was added to *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic)*'s Cool List. Highlighted areas included the capital, Asmara, known for its art deco architecture; the [Dahlak Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlak_Islands); and the country's wilderness areas.[^cite_note-225] [Lonely Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonely_Planet) also lists the capital Asmara, the Dahlak Islands, the city of Massawa and archeological sites as top attractions.[^cite_note-226]

The nation's flagship carrier, [Eritrean Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Airlines), had no scheduled service as of July 2023. International visitors rely on alternatives such as [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md) and [Turkish Airlines](167925.md) to get to the country.[^cite_note-227]

The government has started a twenty-year tourism development plan entitled "the 2020 Eritrea Tourism Development Plan" to develop the country's tourist industry, aiming to enhance the rich cultural and natural resources of the country. The country participates in many trade fairs to promote its tourist industry. [^cite_note-monitor-222]

### Transportation

Main article: [Transport in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Eritrea)



Filfil road in Eritrea

Transport in Eritrea includes highways, airports, railways, and seaports, in addition to various forms of public and private vehicular, maritime, and aerial transportation.

The Eritrean highway system is named according to the road classification. The three levels of classification are primary (P), secondary (S), and tertiary (T). The lowest road level is tertiary, which serves local interests. Typically, tertiary roads are improved earth roads that are occasionally paved. During the wet seasons, these roads typically become impassable. The middle road level is secondary. These are typically single-layered asphalt roads that connect district capitals and regional capitals. Primary roads are paved with asphalt throughout their entire length and carry traffic between major cities and towns in Eritrea.



Steam train outside [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara) on the [Eritrean Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Railway)

As of 1999, there is a total of 317 kilometres of 950 mm (3 ft 1+3⁄8 in) (narrow gauge) rail line in Eritrea. The [Eritrean Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Railway) was built between 1887 and 1932.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229] Badly damaged during [World War II](32927.md) and in later fighting, it was closed section by section, with the final section closed in 1978.[^cite_note-230] After independence, rebuilding efforts commenced, and the first section was reopened in 2003. As of 2009, the section from [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa) to [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara) was fully rebuilt and available for service.

Rehabilitation of the remainder and rolling stock has occurred in recent years. Current service is very limited due to the extreme age of most of the railway equipment and its limited availability. Further rebuilding is planned. The railway linking Agordat and Asmara with the port of Massawa has been inoperative since 1978, except for an approximately 5-kilometre stretch in Massawa reopened in 1994. A railway formerly ran from Massawa to Bishia via Asmara and is under reconstruction.

During the war, Eritrea continued to develop its [transportation infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Eritrea) by asphalting new roads, improving its ports, and repairing war-damaged roads and bridges as a part of the [Wefri Warsay Yika'alo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wefri_Warsay_Yika'alo) program. The most significant of these projects were the construction of a coastal highway of more than 500 km connecting [Massawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massawa) with [Asseb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asseb) and the rehabilitation of the [Eritrean Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Railway). The rail line has been restored between the port of Massawa and the capital Asmara, although services are sporadic. Steam locomotives are sometimes used for groups of enthusiasts.

### Ethnic composition



Ethno-Demography of Eritrea

There are nine recognized ethnic groups according to the government of Eritrea.[^cite_note-CIA-31][^cite_note-238] No independent census has been conducted, but the [Tigrinya people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_people) make up approximately 55% and [Tigre people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigre_people) make up approximately 30% of the population. A majority of the remaining ethnic groups belong to [Afroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages)-speaking communities of the [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_languages) branch, such as the [Saho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saho_people), [Hedareb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedareb_people), [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people), and [Bilen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilen_people). There are also several [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic_peoples) ethnic groups, some of which are represented in Eritrea by the [Kunama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunama_people) and [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_people). Each ethnicity speaks a different native tongue, and many minorities speak more than one language.

The [Arabic](2185.md) [Rashaida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashaida_people) people represent approximately 2% of Eritrea's population.[^cite_note-Ciaethn-239] They reside in the northern coastal lowlands of Eritrea as well as the eastern coasts of Sudan. The Rashaida first came to Eritrea in the nineteenth century, from the [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz) region.[^cite_note-240]

In addition, there exist [Italian Eritrean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritreans) (concentrated in Asmara) and Ethiopian Tigrayan communities. Neither is generally given citizenship except through marriage or, more rarely, by having it conferred upon them by the state. According to 1931 Italian census, of the 600,573 inhabitants in Eritrea, 4,188 were Italians, making up about 0.7% of the population.[^cite_note-1931census-233] In 1941, Eritrea had approximately 760,000 inhabitants, including 70,000 Italians.[^cite_note-241] Most Italians left after Eritrea became independent from Italy. It is estimated that as many as 100,000 Eritreans are of Italian descent.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243]

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Eritrea)

Eritrea is a multilingual country. The nation has no official language, as the Constitution establishes the "equality of all Eritrean languages".[^cite_note-auto3-1] Eritrea has nine national languages which are [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language), [Tigre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigre_language), [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_language), [Beja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_language), [Bilen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilen_language), [Kunama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunama_language), [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_language), and [Saho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saho_language). Tigrinya, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) serve as de facto working languages, with English used in university education and many technical fields. While [Italian](14708.md), the former colonial language, holds no government-recognised status in Eritrea, it is spoken by a few monolinguals and in commerce at times, and Asmara had the [Scuola Italiana di Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Italiano_Statale_Omnicomprensivo_di_Asmara), an Italian government-operated school that was shut down in 2020.[^cite_note-Ethner-244] Some native Eritreans also assimilated the language of the Italian Eritreans and spoke an Italian-Tigrinya [pidgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pidgin): [Eritrean Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrean).[^cite_note-245]

Most of the languages spoken in Eritrea belong to the [Ethiopian Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Semitic_languages) branch of the [Afroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages) family.[^cite_note-Minahan-246] Other Afroasiatic languages in the [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_languages) branch are also widely spoken in the country.[^cite_note-Minahan-246] The latter include [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_language), [Beja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_language), [Blin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blin_language), and [Saho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saho_language). In addition, [Nilo-Saharan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan_languages) ([Kunama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunama_language) and [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_language)) are [natively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_language) spoken by the Nilotic Kunama and Nara ethnic groups that live in the western and northwestern part of the country.[^cite_note-Minahan-246]

Smaller groups speak other Afroasiatic languages, such as the newly recognized [Dahlik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlik_language) and Arabic (the [Hejazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejazi_Arabic) and [Hadhrami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadhrami_Arabic) [dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Arabic) spoken by the Rashaida and Hadhrami, respectively).

The two main religions followed in Eritrea are [Christianity](5211.md) and [Islam](6037917.md). However, the number of adherents of each faith is subject to debate. According to the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), as of 2020, 62.9% of the population of Eritrea adhered to Christianity, 36.6% followed Islam, and 0.4% practiced [traditional African religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions). The remainder observed [Judaism](15624.md), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Buddhism](3267529.md), other faiths (<0.1% each), or were religiously unaffiliated (0.1%).[^cite_note-Pew-247] The U.S. Department of State estimated that as of 2019, 49% of the population of Eritrea adhered to Christianity, 49% followed Islam, and 2% observed other religions, including traditional faiths and animism.[^cite_note-webcitation.org-248] The World Religion Database reports that in 2020, 47% of the population were Christian and 51% were Muslim.[^cite_note-249] Christianity is the oldest world religion practiced in the country, and the first Christian monastery [Debre Sina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debre_Sina_\(monastery\)) was built during the fourth century. [^cite_note-250]

Since May 2002, the government of Eritrea has officially recognized the [Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Orthodox_Tewahedo_Church) ([Oriental Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox)), [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam), the [Eritrean Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Catholic_Church) (a [Metropolitanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_\(religious_jurisdiction\)) [sui juris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_iuris)), and the [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical) [Lutheran](23371382.md) church. All other faiths and denominations are required to undergo a registration process.[^cite_note-Religious_persecution_in_Eritrea-251] Among other things, the government registration system requires religious groups to submit personal information on their membership to be allowed to [worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worship).[^cite_note-Religious_persecution_in_Eritrea-251]

The Eritrean government is against what it deems as "reformed" or "radical" versions of its established religions. Therefore, alleged radical forms of Islam and Christianity, [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses), and numerous other [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) Evangelical denominations are not registered and cannot worship freely. Three named Jehovah's Witnesses are known to have been imprisoned since 1994 along with 51 others.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254] The government treats Jehovah's Witnesses especially harshly, denying them ration cards and work permits.[^cite_note-255] Jehovah's Witnesses were stripped of their citizenship and basic civil rights by presidential decree in October 1994.[^cite_note-256]

In its 2017 religious freedom report, the U.S. State Department named Eritrea a Country of Particular Concern (CPC).[^cite_note-257]

### Health

Main article: [Health in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Eritrea)

Eritrea has achieved significant improvements in health care and is one of the few countries to be on target to meet its [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goal) (MDG) for health, in particular child health.[^cite_note-ODI1-258] [Life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) at birth increased from 39.1 years in 1960 to 66.44 years in 2020;[^cite_note-auto2-259] maternal and [child mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_mortality) rates dropped dramatically and the health infrastructure expanded.[^cite_note-ODI1-258]

The [World Health Organisation](33583.md) (WHO) in 2008 found average life expectancy to be slightly less than 63 years, a number that has increased to 66.44 in 2020.[^cite_note-auto2-259] [Immunisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunization) and child nutrition have been tackled by working closely with schools in a multi-sectoral approach; the number of children vaccinated against measles almost doubled in seven years, from 40.7% to 78.5% and the prevalence of underweight children decreased by 12% from 1995 to 2002 (severe underweight prevalence by 28%).[^cite_note-ODI1-258] The National Malaria Protection Unit of the Ministry of Health registered reductions in malarial mortality by as much as 85% and in the number of cases by 92% between 1998 and 2006.[^cite_note-ODI1-258] The Eritrean government has banned [female genital mutilation](11408.md) (FGM), saying the practice was painful and put women at risk of life-threatening health problems.[^cite_note-260]

However, Eritrea still faces many challenges. Although the number of physicians increased from only 0.2 in 1993 to 0.5 in 2004 per 1000 people, this is still very low.[^cite_note-ODI1-258] [Malaria](20423.md) and [tuberculosis](30653.md) are common.[^cite_note-afro.who.int-261] HIV prevalence for ages 15 to 49 years exceeds 2%.[^cite_note-afro.who.int-261] The fertility rate is about 4.1 births per woman.[^cite_note-afro.who.int-261] Maternal mortality dropped by more than half from 1995 to 2002 but is still high.[^cite_note-ODI1-258] Similarly, the number of births attended by skilled health personnel doubled from 1995 to 2002 but still is only 28.3%.[^cite_note-ODI1-258] Severe [infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infection) is a major cause of death in newborns.[^cite_note-afro.who.int-261] Per-capita expenditure on health is low.[^cite_note-afro.who.int-261]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Eritrea)



The [Eritrea Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea_Institute_of_Technology)



Eritrean pupils in uniform

There are five levels of education in Eritrea: [pre-primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool_education), [primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_education), [middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school), [secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education), and [post-secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education). There are nearly 1,270,000 students in the primary, middle, and secondary levels of education.[^cite_note-uis.unesco.org-262] There are approximately 824 schools,[^cite_note-263] two universities, (the [University of Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Asmara) and the [Eritrea Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea_Institute_of_Technology)), and several smaller colleges and technical schools.

The Eritrea Institute of Technology (EIT) is a technological institute located near the town of Himbrti, [Mai Nefhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mai_Nefhi) outside [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara). The institute has three colleges: Science, Engineering and Technology, and Education. The institute began with approximately 5,500 students during the 2003–2004 [academic year](1778340.md). The EIT was opened after the University of Asmara was reorganized. According to the Ministry of Education, the institution was established, as one of many efforts to achieve equal distribution of higher learning in areas outside the capital city, Asmara. Accordingly, several similar colleges have also been established in other parts of the country. The Eritrea Institute of Technology is the main local institute of higher studies in science, engineering, and education. The University of Asmara is the oldest in the country and was opened in 1958.[^cite_note-264] It is not currently operating.

As of 2018, the overall adult literacy rate in Eritrea is 76.6% (84.4% for men and 68.9% for women). For youth 15–24, the overall literacy rate is 93.3% (93.8% for men and 92.7% for women).[^cite_note-265]

Education in Eritrea is officially compulsory for children aged 6 to 13 years.[^cite_note-uis.unesco.org-262] Statistics vary at the elementary level, suggesting that 70% to 90% of school-aged children attend primary school; approximately 61% attend secondary school. Student-teacher ratios are high: 45\:1 at the elementary level and 54\:1 at the secondary level. Class sizes average 63 and 97 students per classroom at the elementary and secondary school levels, respectively.

Barriers to education in Eritrea include traditional [taboos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taboos), school fees (for registration and materials), and the [opportunity costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_cost) of [low-income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-income) households.[^cite_note-266]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Eritrea)

Eritrean culture encompasses the collective cultural heritage of the various populations native to Eritrea and the rich culture inherited through its long history. Modern-day Eritrea is also defined by the struggle for independence.[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268] The nation has a rich oral and literary tradition, which ranges across all nine ethnic groups and includes poetry and proverbs, songs and chants, folk tales, histories, and legends. It also has a rich history in theatre and painting, often colourful and depicting a reflection of the Eritrean people's history.[^cite_note-269]

One of the most recognizable parts of Eritrean culture is the [coffee ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_ceremony_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea).[^cite_note-270] Coffee ([Ge'ez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ge'ez_alphabet) ቡን *būn*) is offered when visiting friends, during festivities, or as a staple of daily life. The coffee is served in three brews or rounds: the first is called *awel* in Tigrinya (meaning "first"), the second is called *kalaay* (meaning "second"), and the third round is called *bereka* (meaning "to be blessed").

Traditional Eritrean attire is highly varied among its ethnic groups. In larger cities, most people dress in Western casual dress such as jeans and shirts. In offices, both men and women often dress in suits. A common traditional clothing for [Christian](18337522.md) Tigrinya highlanders consists of bright white gowns (called *[zurias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuria)*) for women and a white shirt and pants for men. In Muslim communities in the Eritrean lowlands, women traditionally dress in brightly colored clothes. Besides convergent culinary tastes, Eritreans share an appreciation for similar music, jewelry, fragrances, and fabrics, as many other populations in the region.[^cite_note-Tekle197-271]

### World Heritage Site and Italian architecture

On 8 July 2017, the entire capital city of [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara) was listed as a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md), with the inscription taking place during the 41st World Heritage Committee Session.[^cite_note-272]



[Eritrean national museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Eritrea) in Asmara



[The Fiat Tagliero Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Tagliero_Building), Futurist-style service station designed by [Giuseppe Pettazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Pettazzi) during the Italian colonial era

The city has thousands of [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_deco_architecture), [futurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurist), [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist), and [rationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalism_\(architecture\)) buildings, constructed during the period of [Italian Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea).[^cite_note-auto1-273][^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278] Asmara, a small town in the nineteenth century, began a period of rapid growth in 1889.[^cite_note-279] The city also became a place "to experiment with radical new designs", mainly futuristic and Art Deco inspired.[^cite_note-280] Though city planners, architects, and engineers were largely European and members of the indigenous population were largely used as construction workers, Asmarinos still identify with their city's legacy.[^cite_note-281]

The city includes examples of most early twentieth-century architectural styles. Some buildings are [neo-Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Romanesque_architecture), such as the [Church of Our Lady of the Rosary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady_of_the_Rosary,_Asmara). Art Deco influences are found throughout the city. [Cubist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism) influences show on the Africa Pension Building and on a small collection of buildings. The [Fiat Tagliero Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Tagliero_Building) shows the peak of [Futurism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurism), just as it became fashionable in Italy. In recent times, some more functional buildings have been built in Asmara.

Many buildings such as opera houses, hotels, and cinemas were built during this period. Some notable buildings include the [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) [Cinema Impero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_Impero) (opened in 1937 and considered a leading example of Art Deco architecture[^cite_note-282]), Cubist Africa Pension, [eclectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism_in_architecture) Eritrean Orthodox [Enda Mariam Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enda_Mariam_Cathedral,_Asmara) and [Asmara Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara_Theatre), the [futurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurist_architecture) [Fiat Tagliero Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Tagliero_Building), the [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) [Asmara city hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor's_Palace_\(Asmara\)).

A statement from UNESCO read:

> It is an exceptional example of early modernist urbanism at the beginning of the 20th century and its application in an African context.

— [UNESCO](21786641.md)[^cite_note-auto1-273]

### Music

Main article: [Music of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Eritrea)



Eritrean artist [Helen Meles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Meles)

Eritrea's ethnic groups each have distinct styles of music and accompanying dances. The best-known Tigrinya traditional musical genre is the guaila. Traditional instruments of Eritrean folk music include the strung [krar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krar), [kebero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebero), [begena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begena), [masenqo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masenqo), and the wata. Tigrinya singer [Helen Meles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Meles) is noted for her powerful voice and wide singing range.[^cite_note-Blum-283] Other prominent local musicians include the Kunama singer [Dehab Faytinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehab_Faytinga), [Ruth Abraha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Abraha), [Bereket Mengisteab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bereket_Mengisteab), the late [Yemane Ghebremichael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemane_Ghebremichael) and the late [Abraham Afewerki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Afewerki).

Dancing plays an important role in Eritrean society. The nine ethnic groups have many exuberant dances.[^cite_note-traditionalmusic-284] Styles differ among the ethnic groups; for instance, the Bilen and Tigre styles involve shaking their shoulders, while standing and rotating in a circle towards the end of the dance. This differs from the Tigrinya style, which involves first dancing rotating anti-clockwise and later speeding up the pace of the dance and ceasing the circular rotation. The Kunama ethnic group has dances with ritual significance, including "tuka (rites of passage); indoda (prayers for rain); sangga-nena (peaceful mediation); and shatta (showcases of endurance and courage)". They are often fast-paced and are accompanied by drum beats.[^cite_note-traditionalmusic-284]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Eritrean cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_cuisine)



Eritrean [injera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injera) with various stews

A typical traditional Eritrean dish consists of [injera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injera) accompanied by a spicy stew, which frequently includes beef, chicken, lamb, or fish.[^cite_note-Kittler-285] Overall, Eritrean cuisine strongly resembles that of neighboring [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_cuisine),[^cite_note-Kittler-285][^cite_note-Tekle142-286] though Eritrean cooking tends to feature more [seafood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafood) than Ethiopian cuisine on account of their coastal location.[^cite_note-Kittler-285] Eritrean dishes are also frequently "lighter" in texture than Ethiopian meals, using less [seasoned butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niter_kibbeh) and spices and more [tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato), as in the *[tsebhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsebhi) dorho* delicacy.

Additionally, due to its colonial history, Eritrean cuisine features more [Italian](14532.md) influences than Ethiopian cooking, including more [pasta](23871.md) and use of [curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry) powders and [cumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumin). [Italian Eritrean cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrean_cuisine) originated in the colonial [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy), when a large number of Italians moved to Eritrea. They brought pasta to [Italian Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea), which is now one of the main foods eaten in [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara). Common dishes are "pasta al sugo e berbere" (pasta with tomato sauce and berbere spice), [lasagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasagna), and "cotoletta alla Milanese" (veal Milanese).[^cite_note-Mftdbeaeccdtmts-287]

In addition to coffee, local alcoholic beverages are enjoyed. These include *sowa*, a bitter drink made from fermented barley, and *mies*, a fermented honey wine.[^cite_note-Mettm-288]

### Sport

Main article: [Sport in Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Eritrea)

[Football](10568.md) and [cycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_of_Eritrea) are the most popular sports in Eritrea.[^cite_note-289][^cite_note-290]

Cycling has a long tradition in Eritrea and was first introduced during the colonial period.[^cite_note-291][^cite_note-292] The [Tour of Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_of_Eritrea), a multi-stage cycling event, was first held in 1946 and most recently held in 2017.



Eritrea's men's national cycling team, ranked 16th in the world as of February 2023,

[^cite_note-293]

[^cite_note-294]

&#x20;and first place in Africa

[^cite_note-295]

Eritrea's national cycling teams are ranked first on the African continent,[^cite_note-296] with the men's team ranked 16th globally as of February 2023.[^cite_note-297] The Eritrean national cycling team has won the African Continental cycling championship several years in a row. In 2013, the women's team won the gold medal in the [African Continental Cycling Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Continental_Cycling_Championships) for the first time, and again in 2015 and 2019. The men's team has won gold in the African Continental cycling championships eight times between 2010 and 2022.[^cite_note-298][^cite_note-299][^cite_note-300][^cite_note-301]

Eritrea has more than 500 elite cyclists of both genders.[^cite_note-302] More than 20 Eritrean riders have signed professional contracts to international cycling teams. In 2015, [Daniel Teklehaimanot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Teklehaimanot) and [Merhawi Kudus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merhawi_Kudus) became the first cyclists from Africa to compete in the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France).[^cite_note-303][^cite_note-Cnbcfertf-304] In 2022, [Biniam Girmay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biniam_Girmay) was the first African rider to win both [Gent-Wevelgem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gent%E2%80%93Wevelgem) and a stage in one of the [Grand Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Tour_\(cycling\)) during [Giro d'Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giro_d'Italia). Three-time [African female champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Continental_Cycling_Championships) [Mossana Debesay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosana_Debesay) became the first African female cyclist to compete in the Olympics, representing Eritrea in the [Tokyo 2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics)[^cite_note-305][^cite_note-306]

Eritrean athletes in other sports have also seen some success in the international arena. Eritrean athlete [Zersenay Tadese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zersenay_Tadese) formerly held the world record in the half marathon.[^cite_note-307] In 2015, marathon runner [Ghirmay Ghebreslassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghirmay_Ghebreslassie) became the first Eritrean to win gold at a [World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Championships_in_Athletics) for his country.[^cite_note-308] Eritrea made its Winter Olympic debut in [Pyeongchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyeongchang), [South Korea](27019.md), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Winter_Olympics). Eritrea's team was represented by their flagbearer, [Shannon-Ogbnai Abeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon-Ogbnai_Abeda), who competed in [alpine ski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_skier).[^cite_note-309]

The Eritrea [national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrea_national_football_team) has never qualified for either the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) or [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md), and has only participated in the qualification processes for these tournaments sporadically. After taking part in the [2008 Africa Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification), they declined to enter qualification for another Africa Cup of Nations until returning for the [2027](81670241.md) tournament.[^cite_note-310] Their withdrawal from international football followed multiple incidents wherein Eritrean players defected from Eritrea and sought asylum while playing away games in foreign countries.[^cite_note-311] Eritrea did not play a single international fixture between January 2020 and March 2026, which led to their removal from the [FIFA World Rankings](719095.md).[^cite_note-312]
