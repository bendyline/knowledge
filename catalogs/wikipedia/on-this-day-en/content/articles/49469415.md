**Richard Robson** [FAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Australian_Academy_of_Science) [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (born 4 June 1937) is an English and Australian chemist and Professor of Chemistry at the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne).[^cite_note-1] Robson specialises in [coordination polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_polymers), particularly [metal-organic frameworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal-organic_framework).[^cite_note-Hoskins-1989-2] He has been described as "a pioneer in crystal engineering involving [transition metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_metals)".[^cite_note-Wise1998-3][^cite_note-BattenNeville2009-4] In 2025, he was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](25416893.md) jointly with [Susumu Kitagawa](55513524.md) and [Omar M. Yaghi](17344326.md) for the development of metal-organic frameworks.[^cite_note-5]

## Early life and education

Robson was born in [Glusburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glusburn), [West Yorkshire](149139.md) (now [North Yorkshire](143759.md)), England, on 4 June 1937.[^cite_note-NobelPrize.Org-6][^cite_note-o192-7] He read chemistry at [Brasenose College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasenose_College,_Oxford),[^cite_note-8] earning a BA in 1959 and a DPhil in 1962.[^cite_note-RS-9][^cite_note-NobelPrize.Org-6] His doctoral research, supervised by John A. Barltrop at the [Dyson Perrins Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyson_Perrins_Laboratory), focused on the [photochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochemistry) of organic molecules.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-i311-11]

Robson conducted postdoctoral research at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) (1962–64) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) (1964–65) under [Henry Taube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Taube) before accepting a lectureship in chemistry at the University of Melbourne in 1966, where he remained for the rest of his career.[^cite_note-RS-9][^cite_note-12]

## Research

Richard Robson's groundbreaking research established foundational principles in the field of coordination polymers, particularly for infinite polymeric frameworks—later termed [metal–organic frameworks](9821563.md) (MOFs).[^cite_note-Hoskins-1989-2][^cite_note-13] His interest in the field was sparked in 1974 while constructing large wooden models of crystalline structures for first-year chemistry lectures.[^cite_note-14]

In the 1990s, Robson created a new class of coordination polymers that underpinned an entire modern field of chemistry.[^cite_note-Pursuit-15] His innovative approach used copper(I), which favours a tetrahedral geometry, in combination with a custom-designed tetranitrile organic linker.[^cite_note-Pursuit-15] This method produced crystalline scaffolds with a diamond-like structure but with significant, engineered void space within the framework.[^cite_note-Pursuit-15]

## Honors and awards



Robson with [Susumu Kitagawa](55513524.md) and [Omar Yaghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Yaghi)

Robson received the Burrows Award from the Inorganic Division of The [Royal Australian Chemical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Chemical_Institute) in 1998 and was elected a Fellow of the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science) in 2000.[^cite_note-k890-16] He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 2022.[^cite_note-17]

Robson shared the 2025 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his early contribution to the field of [metal–organic frameworks](9821563.md) (MOFs).

## Personal life

His daughter is former TV presenter [Naomi Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Robson).[^cite_note-18]
