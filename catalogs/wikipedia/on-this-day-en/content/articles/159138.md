The **Council on American–Islamic Relations** (**CAIR**) is an American [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) [civil rights](37071.md)[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] and [advocacy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy_group). It is headquartered on [Capitol Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Hill) in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), with regional offices nationwide. Through civil rights actions, media relations, civic engagement, and education, CAIR works to promote social, legal and political activism among [Muslims in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-4]

The [United Arab Emirates](69328.md),[^cite_note-5] [Texas](29810.md),[^cite_note-6] and [Florida](18933066.md)[^cite_note-7] have designated CAIR as a [terrorist organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organization).

## History

### Early years (1994–2001)

CAIR was founded in June 1994.[^cite_note-north_american_muslim_resource_guide-8] CAIR's first office was located in Washington, D.C., as is its present-day headquarters on [Capitol Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Hill). Its founding was partly in response to the film *[True Lies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Lies)*, which Arab and Muslim groups condemned for its stereotyping of Arab and Muslim villains.[^cite_note-jack_shaheen-9] The offices opened a month before the film's release. CAIR's first advocacy campaign was in response to an offensive greeting card that used the term "[Shi'ite](26961.md)" to refer to human excrement. CAIR led a national campaign and used activists to pressure the greeting card company, which eventually withdrew the card from the market.[^cite_note-north_american_muslim_resource_guide-8][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

In 1995, CAIR handled its first case of [hijab](68301.md) discrimination, in which a Muslim employee was denied the right to wear the hijab; this type of complaint became one of the most common received by CAIR's civil rights department.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

CAIR continued its advocacy work in the aftermath of the April 19, 1995 [Oklahoma City bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City_bombing) of the Murrah Federal Building. Following the attack, Muslim-Americans were subjected to an upsurge in harassment and discrimination, including a rise in hate crimes nationally;[^cite_note-15] 222 hate crimes against Muslims nationwide were reported in the days immediately following the bombing.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The bombing gave CAIR national stature for their efforts to educate the public about Islam and religious bias in America; their report was featured on the front page of *[The New York Times](30680.md)* on August 28, 1995, and was subsequently mentioned on *[ABC World News Tonight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_World_News_Tonight)*.[^cite_note-north_american_muslim_resource_guide-8]

In 1996, CAIR began "CAIR-NET", a read-only e-mail [listserv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listserv) aimed to help American Muslims identify and combat anti-Muslim prejudice in the U.S. and Canada. CAIR-NET contained descriptions of news, bias incidents or hate speech and hate crimes, often followed by information as to whom readers may contact to influence resolution of an issue.[^cite_note-18] CAIR also held its first [voter registration drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_registration_drive) in 1996; CAIR continues to encourage active political participation by American Muslims, for them to address political candidates and elected representatives with greater frequency.[^cite_note-19]

In 1996, CAIR published a report *The Usual Suspects* regarding its perception of anti-Muslim rhetoric in the media after the crash of [TWA Flight 800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWA_Flight_800). Their research showed 138 uses of the terms "Muslim" and "Arab" in the 48 hours after the crash in [Reuters](18998750.md), [UPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Press_International), and [AP](18935732.md) articles covering the incident. The official [NTSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTSB) report said that the crash was most likely caused by mechanical failure.[^cite_note-20]

In 1997, CAIR objected to the production of sneakers made by [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) with a design on the heel similar to the Arabic word for "Allah".[^cite_note-21] As part of an agreement reached between CAIR officials and Nike representatives, Nike apologized to the Muslim community, recalled the products carrying the design, launched an investigation as to how the logo came about, and built a number of children's playgrounds near some Islamic centers in America.[^cite_note-22]

In 1997, as [depictions of Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depictions_of_Muhammad) are seen as [blasphemous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_and_blasphemy) by some Muslims, CAIR wrote to United States Supreme Court Chief Justice [William Rehnquist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Rehnquist) requesting that the sculpted representation of [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) on the north frieze inside the Supreme Court building be removed or sanded down. The court rejected CAIR's request.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

### Post-9/11 (2001–2006)

In October 2001, CAIR opposed the [United States invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Afghanistan) after the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md).[^cite_note-25] By January 2002, four months after the attacks, the CAIR said that it had received 1,658 reports of discrimination, profiling, harassment, and physical assaults against persons appearing Arab or Muslim, a threefold increase over the prior year. The reports included beatings, death threats, abusive police practices, and employment and airline-related discrimination."[^cite_note-26]

A CAIR initiative funded in part by a \$500,000 donation from Saudi Prince [Al-Waleed bin Talal bin Abdul Aziz Al Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Waleed_bin_Talal) sent a set of 18 books and tapes to public libraries written by Muslim and non-Muslim authors on Islamic history and practices, as well as an English translation of the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran).[^cite_note-tro-27]

In 2005, CAIR coordinated the joint release of a [fatwa](11259.md) by 344 American Muslim organizations, mosques, and imams nationwide that stated: "Islam strictly condemns religious extremism and the use of violence against innocent lives. There is no justification in Islam for extremism or terrorism. Targeting civilians' life and property through [suicide bombings](18835454.md) or any other method of attack is *[haram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haraam)* or forbidden—and those who commit these barbaric acts are criminals, not [martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr)." The fatwa cited passages from the Quran and hadith that prohibit violence against innocent people and injustice, and was signed by the [Fiqh Council of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh_Council_of_North_America).[^cite_note-28]

Also in 2005, following the [Qur'an desecration controversy of 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an_desecration_controversy_of_2005) at the [Guantanamo Bay detention camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp), CAIR initiated an "Explore the Quran" campaign, aimed at providing free copies of the Quran to any person who requested it.[^cite_note-29]

In June 2006, CAIR announced a \$50 million project to create a better understanding of Islam and Muslims in the US. (\$10 million per year for five years), in a project to be spearheaded by [Paul Findley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Findley), a former US Congressman.[^cite_note-30]

Also in 2006, CAIR sent a group of representatives to Iraq to urge kidnappers to release American journalist [Jill Carroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Carroll).[^cite_note-31] Carroll was eventually released unharmed.[^cite_note-32]

In December 2006, California Senator [Barbara Boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Boxer) withdrew a "certificate of accomplishment" originally given to former CAIR official [Basim Elkarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basim_Elkarra) after Boxer's staff looked into CAIR, and she became concerned about some of CAIR's past statements and actions, and statements by some law enforcement officials that it provides aid to international terrorist groups.[^cite_note-tro-27][^cite_note-33]

### Holy Land Foundation case (2007)

In 2007, CAIR was named, along with 245 others, by U.S. Federal prosecutors in a list of [unindicted co-conspirators or joint venturers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unindicted_co-conspirators) in a [Hamas](13913.md) funding case involving the [Holy Land Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land_Foundation_for_Relief_and_Development),[^cite_note-34] which in 2009, caused the [FBI](11127.md) to cease working with CAIR outside of criminal investigations due to its designation.[^cite_note-foxnews.com-35] CAIR was never charged with any crime, and it complained that the designation had tarnished its reputation.[^cite_note-\:8-36]

Specifically, in May 2007, the U.S. filed an action against the Holy Land Foundation (the largest Muslim charity in the United States at the time[^cite_note-mistrial-37]) for providing funds to Hamas, and federal prosecutors filed pleadings. Along with 245 other organizations, the prosecutors publicly listed CAIR (and its chairman emeritus, [Omar Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Ahmad)),[^cite_note-NYSun-38] [Islamic Society of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Society_of_North_America) (largest Muslim umbrella organization in the United States), [Muslim American Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_American_Society) and [North American Islamic Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Islamic_Trust) as unindicted co-conspirators or joint venturers, a legal designation that prosecutors can employ for a variety of reasons including grants of [immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunity_from_prosecution), pragmatic considerations, and evidentiary concerns. While being listed as co-conspirator does not mean that CAIR has been charged with any crime, the organization was concerned that the label will forever taint it.[^cite_note-NationalLawyers-39]

On October 22, 2007, the Holy Land Foundation trial ended in a mistrial.[^cite_note-mistrial-37] All defendants were convicted upon retrial in 2008.[^cite_note-40]

In 2008, the FBI discontinued its long-standing relationship with CAIR. Officials said the decision followed the conviction of the HLF directors for funneling millions of dollars to [Hamas](13913.md), revelations that Nihal Awad had participated in planning meetings with HLF, and CAIR's failure to provide details of its ties to Hamas.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] During a 2008 retrial of the HLF case, FBI Special Agent Lara Burns labeled CAIR "a [front group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_group) for Hamas".[^cite_note-43] In January 2009, the FBI's DC office instructed all field offices to cut ties with CAIR, as the ban extended into the Obama administration.[^cite_note-44]

U.S. Congressmen [Sue Myrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sue_Myrick) (R-N.C.), [Trent Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Franks) (R-Ariz.), [John Shadegg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Shadegg) (R-Ariz.), and [Paul Broun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Broun) (R-Ga.) wrote [Attorney General](44002.md) [Eric Holder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Holder) on October 21, 2009, that they were concerned about CAIR's relationships with terrorist groups, and requesting that the [Department of Justice](52563.md) (DOJ) provide a summary of DOJ's evidence and findings that led DOJ to name CAIR an unindicted co-conspirator in the Holy Land Foundation terrorism trial.[^cite_note-thehill.com-45] The four Congressmen also wrote [House of Representatives Sergeant at Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_at_Arms_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives) [Wilson Livingood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_Livingood) a letter the same day asking that he work with members of the House [Judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_the_Judiciary), [Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Homeland_Security), and [Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Permanent_Select_Committee_on_Intelligence) Committees to determine if CAIR was successful in placing interns in the committees' offices, to review FBI and DOJ evidence regarding CAIR's Hamas ties, and to determine whether CAIR is a security threat. Congresswoman [Loretta Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Sanchez) (D-Calif.), "appalled", said "I urge the rest of my colleagues to join me in denouncing this [witch hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch_hunt)."[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] She was echoed by [Keith Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Ellison_\(politician\)) (D-Minn.), the first Muslim elected to the U.S. Congress, in a speech that included a statement by the House's Tri-Caucus.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] The four Republican Congressmen, joined by Senator [Tom Coburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Coburn) (R-Okla.) and Congressman [Patrick McHenry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_McHenry) (R-N.C.), then wrote [IRS Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRS_Commissioner) [Douglas H. Shulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_H._Shulman) on November 16, 2009, asking that CAIR be investigated for "excessive lobbying".[^cite_note-50] CAIR spokesman Ibrahim Hooper welcomed the scrutiny from Republican lawmakers, and said, "We've always stayed within our legal limits \[for lobbying]. If anything, we don't have enough staff to lobby as much as we legally can."[^cite_note-thehill.com-45]

In 2009, CAIR and two other organizations requested a federal judge to [expunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expungement) the designation of their organizations as unindicted co-conspirators.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-\:7-52] The judge ruled that the federal prosecutors should have filed the list of unindicted co-conspirators [under seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_seal), and the organizations' [due process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_process) rights under the [Fifth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) were violated by the public naming, but did not expunge the designation.[^cite_note-\:8-36][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-\:7-52]

### 2009 to present

CAIR condemned the [Fort Hood shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Fort_Hood_shooting) in 2009 and expressed prayers for the victims and condolences for their families.[^cite_note-MT_3-54]

CAIR pointed to an arrest of five men in Pakistan on December 10, 2009, as a "success story"[^cite_note-\:6-55] between Muslims and Muslim community organizations (like CAIR) and American law enforcement authorities. When the five men left [Washington](108956.md) for [Karachi](17123.md) on November 28, the families of the men discovered an extremist videotape.[^cite_note-\:6-55] Worried, they contacted CAIR, which set up a meeting with the FBI on December 1, and the families shared their sons' computers and electronic devices with FBI agents.[^cite_note-\:6-55] A U.S. law enforcement official described them as models of cooperation. CAIR hoped the event would ease "strained" relations of American Muslims with the FBI.[^cite_note-\:6-55]

In January 2012, CAIR's [Michigan](18859.md) chapter took a stance along with the [American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American-Arab_Anti-Discrimination_Committee) in defending four Muslim high school football players accused of attacking a quarterback during a game. The players were allegedly targeted for criminal prosecution over the attack because of their ethnic origin.[^cite_note-56] A judge later dropped the charges after deciding they had no merit.[^cite_note-57]

CAIR has opposed proposed United States legislation and executive orders which would have designated the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) as a foreign terrorist organization, saying that such a designation would "inevitably be used in a political campaign to attack those same groups and individuals, to marginalize the American Muslim community and to demonize Islam".[^cite_note-58]

In 2021 the director of the San Francisco branch of CAIR, Zahra Billoo, gave a speech in which she denounced a [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution) to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict and stated that "We need to pay attention" to the [ADL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League) and [Hillel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillel_International), "because just because they are your friends today, doesn't mean that they have your back when it comes to human rights." Later on in her speech, Billoo told the audience to "know your enemies".[^cite_note-\:2-59] Her remarks prompted [Jonathan Greenblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Greenblatt), CEO of the ADL, to accuse Billoo of promoting [white supremacist](33178.md) rhetoric. On December 11, CAIR supported Billoo's remarks.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-\:3-61]

In October 2025, CAIR-Ohio Director, Khaled Tuurani, participated in an online conference hosted by the Al-Zaytouna Center in Beirut which included [Majed al-Zeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majed_al-Zeer), a Hamas official who was designated by the US government in 2024 as a Specially Designated Terrorist for his role in Hamas fundraising efforts.[^cite_note-extremism.gwu.edu-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] The two reportedly appeared on separate panels. The title of the event was, "Palestinians Abroad and Regional and International Strategic Transformations in the Light of Al-Aqsa Flood."[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-extremism.gwu.edu-62]

In December 2025, president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) made anti-Somali comments about [Somalis](1571696.md) and Somali-Americans, calling them [garbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbage), and saying he wants all [Somalis](1571696.md) to be deported back to the Horn of Africa.[^cite_note-66] In the aftermath, some female Somali Americans or female Horner Americans in Minnesota have stated that they've been religiously harassed with comments about their [hijab](68301.md), and others have been sexually assaulted by members of the U.S. [ICE](216537.md) agency.[^cite_note-67] In response, an official press release by CAIR stated that it condemned Trump's comments about people of Somali descent, referring to the comments as "vile".[^cite_note-68] In late December 2025, [United States Director of National Intelligence](456082.md), [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md) referred to a conference that CAIR attended earlier in 2025 that she alleged "issued a call to action to use American legal and political systems to implement Sharia law".[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

Local CAIR chapters such as the Michigan chapter organized a "Remember Through Service" campaign which was a video and billboard media campaign which featured positive representations of Muslim-Americans including a Muslim first responder during the September 11 World Trade Center events.[^cite_note-71]

## Litigation

### Workplace discrimination

One of the largest categories of cases CAIR deals with is workplace discrimination.[^cite_note-72] CAIR has filed successful civil rights litigation on behalf of Muslim Americans who suffered employment discrimination due to their religion, including police officers[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] and hospital workers.[^cite_note-77] CAIR also filed an amicus brief[^cite_note-78] on behalf of the plaintiff to the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) for *[Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Abercrombie & Fitch Stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_Employment_Opportunity_Commission_v._Abercrombie_&_Fitch_Stores)*, in which the Court ruled 8–1 that refusing to hire a woman because she may wear her hijab at the workplace amounts to religious discrimination in hiring.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

### Local government

In 2012, after the City Council in [St. Anthony, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anthony,_Minnesota), voted 4–1 to reject a building plan for the Abu-Huraira Islamic Center,[^cite_note-82] CAIR began legal proceedings and urged the federal government to investigate the city for violating the [Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Land_Use_and_Institutionalized_Persons_Act).[^cite_note-83] In 2014, the city agreed to a settlement after a federal lawsuit was opened against them, allowing the Abu-Huraira Islamic Center to begin services.[^cite_note-84] CAIR also helped the American Islamic Center (AIC) file a complaint against the city of [Des Plaines, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Plaines,_Illinois), to the US Department of Justice after the city refused to allow the AIC to operate its place of worship. After a federal suit was filed, the city agreed to pay \$580,000 to the AIC in a settlement agreement.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

In 2012, CAIR successfully filed suit striking down [State Question 755](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Oklahoma_State_Question_755), a [ban on Sharia law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_on_Sharia_law) in [Oklahoma](22489.md) on grounds that it violated the [First Amendment](31653.md) right to [free exercise of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_religion_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

In 2017, CAIR secured an \$85,000 settlement for Kirsty Powell, whose hijab was forcibly removed by police while in custody.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

### Federal government

CAIR has been involved in legal action against the US Government on several occasions. In 2003, CAIR along with the American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee filed suit in *Muslim Community Association of Ann Arbor v. Ashcroft*, which challenged the constitutionality of the [USA PATRIOT Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_PATRIOT_Act).[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] The case forced Congress to make substantial changes to Section 215 of the act, which helped it avoid being in violation of the First Amendment and had the effect of resolving the lawsuit.[^cite_note-94] CAIR also filed amicus briefs against US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) over [Executive Order 13769](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_13769)[^cite_note-95] and [Executive Order 13780](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_13780),[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] which banned all travellers and temporary visa holders of 7 Muslim-majority countries, as well as all refugees, from entering the United States. CAIR began maintaining a group of immigration lawyers in Chicago O'Hare airport after Executive Order 13769 went into effect[^cite_note-98] and caused the immediate revocation of over 100,000 temporary visas.[^cite_note-99]

CAIR litigated on behalf of [Gulet Mohamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulet_Mohamed), a 19-year-old [Virginia](32432.md) teenager who was kidnapped and tortured in [Kuwait](7515890.md) after the FBI placed him on a [no-fly list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-fly_list).[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] CAIR argued successfully that the teen's placement on the US no-fly list was "patently unconstitutional" and that Mohamed had a constitutional right to come home.[^cite_note-102]

### *Muslim Mafia* lawsuit

The 2009 book *[Muslim Mafia: Inside the Secret Underworld That's Conspiring to Islamize America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Mafia:_Inside_the_Secret_Underworld_That's_Conspiring_to_Islamize_America)* by Paul David Gaubatz and [Paul Sperry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_E._Sperry) portrays CAIR "as a subversive organization allied with international terrorists".[^cite_note-co-103][^cite_note-mcclatchydc200911-104]

Consequently, CAIR brought a federal civil lawsuit in 2009 against Dave Gaubatz and his son for allegedly stealing documents, which were used in the making of Gaubatz's book.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] U.S. District Judge [Colleen Kollar-Kotelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleen_Kollar-Kotelly) concluded that the Gaubatzs "unlawfully obtained access to, and have already caused repeated [public disclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_disclosure_of_private_information#Public_disclosure) of, material containing CAIR's proprietary, confidential and [privileged information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privilege_\(evidence\))," which CAIR says included names, addresses, telephone numbers and e-mail addresses of CAIR employees and donors. As a result, the judge ordered Gaubatz to remove certain documents from his website. Judge Kollar-Kotelly also said that CAIR's employees have reported a dramatic increase in the number of threatening communications since the release of Gaubatz's book.[^cite_note-mcclatchydc200911-104]

## Operations

CAIR's literature describes the group as promoting understanding of Islam and protecting Muslim civil liberties. It has intervened on behalf of many American Muslims who claim discrimination, profiling, or harassment.[^cite_note-CAIR-25FACTS-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

## Concerns and incidents

### Anti-Zionism

Main article: [Anti-Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism)

Organizations like the [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League) (ADL) argue that CAIR leaders use antisemitic rhetoric, and frequently equivocate between [Zionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism), Israelis, and Jews. Zahra Billoo, Executive Director of CAIR's San Francisco Bay Area chapter, has referred to "Zionists" as the enemies of Muslims and advised the Muslim community to be wary of "Zionist synagogues." Similarly, CAIR's national executive director, Nihad Awad, has labeled "Zionist organizations" as enemies of the Muslim community, asserting that they form the backbone of the Islamophobia network in the United States. He has also claimed that Zionist groups have corrupted the U.S. government, and denied the legitimacy of Israel's existence.[^cite_note-112] He also stated that he had been happy that Hamas attacked Israel and claimed that Hamas had "broken the siege" against Israel.[^cite_note-\:9-113]

In September 2025, Senator [Tom Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cotton) petitioned [US Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Education) Secretary [Linda McMahon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_McMahon) to investigate CAIR's efforts to influence [K-12 schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%E2%80%9312) regarding anti-Israel, radical, and pro-terror ideology.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] House Education and Workforce Committee Chairman [Tim Walberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Walberg) subsequently expressed similar concerns about CAIR's educational partnerships with K-12 schools, calling them "deeply troubling given CAIR's ties to terrorism and antisemitism."[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

In October 2025, US Representative [Elise Stefanik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Stefanik) of New York and Senator [Tom Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cotton) of Arkansas asked the US Treasury Secretary [Scott Bessent](38532044.md) to investigate CAIR to determine if it has financial links to Hamas which would be a violation of US sanctions on Hamas.[^cite_note-\:9-113][^cite_note-118] Earlier Cotton, chairman of the [Senate Republican Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Republican_Conference), had urged the IRS to investigate CAIR's tax-exempt status.[^cite_note-119] CAIR responded in a letter that the request was "baseless".[^cite_note-120]

### Gender bias

A Florida CAIR chapter has been accused of ignoring misconduct involving its leaders.[^cite_note-121] [NPR](29697232.md) reported: "When concerned parties brought \[gender bias] allegations to senior CAIR officials in Washington, D.C., and Florida, former employees said, there was little, if any, follow-up action. They said leaders were aware of some of the allegations as early as 2016."[^cite_note-\:1-122] NPR "interviewed 18 former employees at the national office and several prominent chapters who said there was a general lack of accountability when it came to perceived gender bias, religious bias or mismanagement".[^cite_note-\:1-122]

### Labor organizing

NPR's investigation reported that CAIR "thwart\[ed] employees' efforts to unionize in the national office in 2016".[^cite_note-\:1-122] "Service Employees International Union Local 500 said in filings Wednesday that the Council on American-Islamic Relations was trying to bust its effort to organize the civil rights group's staff. CAIR responded with a statement Thursday calling the charge 'meritless'."[^cite_note-123]

### LGBT rights

In 2016, in the wake of the [Pulse nightclub shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_nightclub_shooting), CAIR representatives met with LGBT leaders to condemn the attack, gather in solidarity, and voice their support for LGBT rights.[^cite_note-124] CAIR Sacramento director Basim Elkarra also released a statement offering "support and allyship to the LGBTQ community, which has been a faithful ally against Islamophobia."[^cite_note-125] CAIR director Nihad Awad also voiced his support, saying that American "Muslims stand united" with the LGBT community.[^cite_note-126]

In Michigan, CAIR voiced concerns over an amendment to the [Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott-Larsen_Civil_Rights_Act), advocating that the amendment's LGBT rights provisions include faith protections.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

CAIR and several other Muslim organizations have opposed mandatory "sexually-themed lessons" in [Montgomery County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Maryland). These lessons include LGBT-inclusive content. CAIR issued a statement saying parents should be notified in advance so their children can opt-out of "sexually-themed content." CAIR released a statement saying that "Schools should respect their authority by restoring the option to opt their children out of reading sexually-themed content or participating in sexually-themed lessons and discussions, without any adverse consequences.*"*[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

## Designation as terrorist organization

### By the UAE

In November 2014, CAIR was designated a terrorist organization by the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md),[^cite_note-wam.ae-131] which claimed that the organization has ties to the Muslim Brotherhood.[^cite_note-Wapo20141107-132][^cite_note-GulfNews20141116-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

CAIR called the move "shocking and bizarre", and some international American terrorism analysts were also critical. *The Washington Post* wrote: "CAIR and the Muslim American Society are not alone in their shock. Diverse groups across Europe were also added to the list, leaving many observers perplexed at the scope and sheer scale of the list. Norway's foreign ministry publicly requested an explanation as to why one of the country's largest Islamic groups, the [Islamic Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabita_Mosque), was included, and the U.S. State Department said they would be seeking more information from the U.A.E."[^cite_note-Wapo20141107-132] In January 2015, CAIR said it would seek to appeal the designation in the UAE.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] CAIR has criticized UAE for targeting and detaining American Muslims, such as the civil rights attorney and human rights activist Asim Ghafoor, the former attorney of [Jamal Khashoggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Khashoggi)*,* the *Washington Post* journalist [assassinated by agents of the Saudi government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Jamal_Khashoggi) in the Saudi consulate in Istanbul.[^cite_note-\:5-139] Ghafoor helped establish the organization [Democracy for the Arab World Now (DAWN)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_for_the_Arab_World_Now), which has focused part of its work on human rights violations in the UAE. CAIR has claimed that, "The real reason the UAE targeted Ghafoor became even clearer when Emirati media outlets began publishing unhinged, Islamophobic hit-pieces slandering him as a 'terrorist' for his pro-democracy work with DAWN and other groups."[^cite_note-\:5-139] It also argued that, "the UAE's history targeting American Muslims" is part of a effort to spread authoritarianism in the Middle East.[^cite_note-\:5-139]

UAE Minister of State for Foreign Affairs [Anwar Gargash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Gargash) rejected criticism of the designation, saying that "The noise (by) some Western organizations over the UAE's terrorism list originates in groups that are linked to the Muslim Brotherhood and many of them work on incitement and creating an environment of extremism."[^cite_note-Reuters201411202-140]

### By US state governments

In 1994, during a talk surrounding the situation in the Palestinian occupied territories, CAIR's executive director [Nihad Awad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihad_Awad) stated that he supported the social programs Hamas provided to Palestinians over those supplied by the [Palestinian Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO). Awad later clarified that he did reject the more "radical" elements of the group, however in 2006, Awad disavowed Hamas entirely.[^cite_note-\:11-141] However several individual CAIR members have been indicted on terrorism-related charges.[^cite_note-\:12-142]

In 2004, the [Bush Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush) made the decision to not indict CAIR on criminal charges for its alleged support of Hamas. The decision to not indict CAIR came at the same time prosecutors were building a case against the [Holy Land Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land_Foundation_for_Relief_and_Development), an Islamic charity with connections to CAIR that was charged with funding Hamas in 2008.[^cite_note-143]

In 2007, an award was rescinded honoring [Basim Elkarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basim_Elkarra), the executive director of the Sacramento chapter of CAIR. [Barbara Boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Boxer), who at the time was the political official who initially granted Elkarra the certificate, stated that the reason for the revoked plaque had to do with CAIR's inability to label Hamas and Hezbollah as terrorist organizations.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-\:12-142] After the reward by Boxer's office was revoked the FBI launched a probe which investigated threats made on Elkarra's life in response to the incident.[^cite_note-\:12-142]

In 2008, during the trial of the United States versus the [Holy Land Foundation for Relief and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land_Foundation_for_Relief_and_Development), the FBI agent Lara Burns accused CAIR of being a front group for Hamas. CAIR was one of the 300 or so unindicted groups labeled as "co-conspirators" in the Holy Land case.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-\:13-146]

In 2017, Ohio Republican [Josh Mandel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Mandel) stated that "CAIR is [Hamas](13913.md)." A day later Mandel also accused CAIR of being connected to the Muslim Brotherhood.[^cite_note-\:11-141]

The [federal government of the United States](195149.md) has not listed CAIR as a terrorist organization.[^cite_note-\:0-147][^cite_note-148] However, some [state](18618239.md) governments have declared CAIR as such.

On November 18, 2025, [Texas](29810.md) Governor [Greg Abbott](1492735.md) officially declared the organization as a [foreign terrorist organization](421835.md). This includes also the Muslim Brotherhood, and stating that CAIR has direct ties to [Hamas](13913.md). In his proclamation, Abbott accused the Muslim Brotherhood and CAIR of supporting terrorism internationally and promoting the implementation of sharia law. He also referenced alleged links between CAIR, [Hamas](13913.md), and individuals previously convicted on terrorism-related charges. Following this act, the organization will not be able to acquire land in Texas.[^cite_note-\:10-149] On December 9, 2025, [Florida](18933066.md) Gov. [Ron DeSantis](36729527.md) designated the Council on American‑Islamic Relations (CAIR) as a "foreign terrorist organization" along with the Muslim Brotherhood.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152]

## Reception

### Praise

U.S. Senator [Barbara Boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Boxer)'s 2006 decision to withdraw a "certificate of accomplishment" originally given to former CAIR official Basim Elkarra on grounds of suspicions about the organization's background "provoked an outcry from organizations that vouch for the group's advocacy, including the ACLU and the California Council of Churches."[^cite_note-scr-153] [Maya Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Harris), executive director of the ACLU of Northern California, criticized Senator Boxer's decision and added that CAIR has "been a leading organization that has advocated for civil rights and civil liberties in the face of fear and intolerance, in the face of religious and [ethnic profiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_profiling)."[^cite_note-scr-153]

[Pulitzer Prize](24230.md)-winning journalist [Nicholas Kristof](435304.md) advocated for people to support and sign up as members of CAIR in response to the [2016 election of US President Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-154]

In 2016, the [University of Saint Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saint_Thomas_\(Minnesota\)) named the Minnesota branch of CAIR as the winner of its Winds of Change Award at its Forum on Workplace Inclusion.[^cite_note-155]

The [Seattle](11388236.md) chapter of the [League of Women Voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Women_Voters) awarded the Washington branch of CAIR one of its 2015 Champion of Voting and Civil Rights Awards, praising "their work encouraging voting and community involvement by members of the Muslim American community".[^cite_note-156]

### Criticism

In 2007, the [United States government](195149.md) named the [Council on American-Islamic Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_on_American-Islamic_Relations) (CAIR) as an unindicted co-conspirator in the [Holy Land Foundation terrorism financing case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land_Foundation_trial?action=edit\&redlink=1). Federal prosecutors alleged that CAIR was created by a Palestine Committee of the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md) and had ties to [Hamas](13913.md). The government also asserted that CAIR promoted [Wahhabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism) and that some of its officials had links to individuals later convicted of terrorism-related offenses.[^cite_note-\:13-146]

However, these allegations were part of the government’s pre-trial proffer and were never adjudicated against CAIR itself, because CAIR was not a defendant in the case. In 2010, after a lengthy trial, five former officials of the Holy Land Foundation were convicted, but CAIR was never charged with any crime.

In 2009, CAIR filed a motion to remove its name from the list of unindicted co-conspirators, arguing that the designation was harming its reputation and that the government lacked evidence to support the allegations. The U.S. government opposed the motion, maintaining its suspicions, but ultimately a federal judge ruled that while the government had presented sufficient factual basis to include CAIR at the time of the original filing, the court could not force the government to remove CAIR from the list because the list was an internal prosecutorial tool, not a public finding of guilt. The judge noted that CAIR had not been given an opportunity to contest the allegations in court, but also that the government had not proven its claims against CAIR in any adversarial proceeding. [^cite_note-157]

Notably, CAIR has publicly condemned many acts of terrorism, including the 2002 [Passover massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passover_massacre) (the bombing of the Park Hotel in [Netanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanya), [Israel](9282173.md))—an attack for which [Hamas](13913.md) later claimed responsibility.

In December 2023, the [Biden administration](65732174.md) cut off contact with CAIR after its executive director stated he was "happy to see" Palestinians break [Israel's siege on the Gaza Strip](19224310.md) on [October 7](74998317.md).[^cite_note-158] Awad also stated that Palestinians "have the right to self-defense" but that Israel "as an occupying power" does not.[^cite_note-NYT_Dec_8_2023-159]

In response to the criticism from the Biden administration, Awad released a statement and held a press conference claiming that his original remarks were taken out of context. He stated that, "Ukrainians, Palestinians and other occupied people have the right to defend themselves and escape occupation by just and legal means, but targeting civilians is never an acceptable means of doing so ...Targeting civilians is unacceptable, no matter whether they are Israeli or Palestinian or any other nationality."[^cite_note-\:4-160][^cite_note-161]

Hindutva groups and Zionists have accused it of pursuing an [Islamist](15012.md) agenda.[^cite_note-scr-153][^cite_note-auto-162] CAIR denies these allegations.[^cite_note-163] [Zuhdi Jasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuhdi_Jasser), a physician and Republican politician in Arizona, has criticized CAIR and argued that its agenda is focused on "victimization".[^cite_note-164] Prominent figure of the [Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist) movement and vocal critic of Islam,[^cite_note-165] [Sam Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Harris_\(author\)), criticized CAIR by saying the organization is "an Islamist public relations firm posing as a civil-rights lobby".[^cite_note-166]

Some Muslims have criticized CAIR for taking a [conservative religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement) approach on some issues. These critics claim that past statements by the organization, such as the claim that the headscarf is a religious requirement for Muslim women, often follow conservative Saudi religious doctrine and do not capture diverse religious perspectives.[^cite_note-scr-153] The claim that the headscarf is religious obligation upon Muslim women has been criticized by some Muslim scholars and academics, including [Khaled Abou El Fadl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Abou_El_Fadl).[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169]

## Funding

CAIR has an annual budget of around \$3 million (as of 2007).[^cite_note-scr-153] It states that while the majority of its funding comes from American Muslims, it accepts donations from individuals of any faith and also foreigners.[^cite_note-TopInternet-170] In the past CAIR has accepted donations from individuals and foundations close to Arab governments including major donations for special projects, like a five hundred thousand donation from [Prince Alwaleed bin Talal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Waleed_bin_Talal_Al_Saud) of Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-scr-153][^cite_note-\:13-146] Within CAIR there is debate regarding foreign funding, and several CAIR branches have criticized the national office for accepting foreign donations.[^cite_note-scr-153]

In April 2011, Rep. [Frank Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wolf_\(politician\)), R-Va. cited a 2009 letter sent from CAIR's executive director, Nihad Awad, to [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) asking Gaddafi for funding for a project called the Muslim Peace Foundation at a U.S. House of Representatives Appropriations sub-committee hearing.[^cite_note-171] [Steven Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Emerson) called the funding request "hypocritical",[^cite_note-Lawmaker_Fox-172] while CAIR spokesman, Ibrahim Hooper, said that the Muslim Peace Foundation was Awad's personal initiative "unrelated to CAIR", that CAIR didn't receive any money from the Libyan government, and also that CAIR was one of the first American organizations to call for a no-fly zone to protect Libyan citizens from Gaddafi during the [2011 Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2011\)).[^cite_note-Lawmaker_Fox-172]
