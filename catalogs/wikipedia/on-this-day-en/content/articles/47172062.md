**Natasha Cloud** (born February 22, 1992) is an American professional [basketball](3921.md) player for the [Chicago Sky](2717051.md) of the [Women's National Basketball Association](57721.md) (WNBA) and for the [Phantom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_BC) of [Unrivaled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrivaled_\(basketball\)).

## Early life

Cloud was born on February 22, 1992, in [Broomall, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broomall,_Pennsylvania), to Sharon Cloud. Sharon cheated on her husband, Emil, resulting in conception of Natasha.[^cite_note-1] Natash was raised by Emil instead of her biological father, who she did not know growing up.[^cite_note-2] She is one of five siblings. She is of mixed race heritage and was raised in a predominantly white community.[^cite_note-3]

During high school, Cloud led [Cardinal O'Hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_O'Hara_High_School_\(Springfield,_Pennsylvania\)) to the [Pennsylvania Interscholastic Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Interscholastic_Athletic_Association) Class AAAA state finals as a junior and to the second round as a senior. She earned First Team All-Delco honors in both her junior and senior year. As a junior, she additionally earned a Pennsylvania AAAA Third Team All-State selection.[^cite_note-SJU_bio-4]

Natasha Cloud gained recognition after being named AAAA First Team All-State as a senior. During the season, she averaged 12.3 points, 7.9 rebounds, 5.2 assists, and 4.0 steals per game.[^cite_note-SJU_bio-4] She earned the Michael Menichini Award in 2009.

Following her high school career, Cloud received a scholarship to play at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park) as a [Terrapin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_Terrapins_women's_basketball).

## College career

### University of Maryland

#### 2010–2011

Cloud played in 31 of 32 games of her freshman year and started six times.[^cite_note-UM_Terps_bio-5] During the season, she led the team in assists twice and in blocks three times while also being named a Scholar Athlete.[^cite_note-UM_Terps_bio-5]

Her coach, [Brenda Frese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Frese), stated that:

> Natasha is a very athletic and unselfish player who will do whatever her team needs to help us win. She's a strong defensive player, who can play either guard spot and is a terrific passer. Natasha has a ton of personality, is a good student and fits right in with our team.[^cite_note-UM_Terps_bio-5]

Following the 2010-2011 basketball season, Cloud transferred to [Saint Joseph's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph's_University).

### Saint Joseph's University

#### 2011–2012

Due to [NCAA](60706.md) transfer rules, Cloud sat out the 2011–2012 season. She was named a member of the [SJU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph's_University) Director's Honor Roll.[^cite_note-SJU_bio-4]

#### 2013–2014

&#x20;Cloud served as co-captain of her team. She earned [Atlantic 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_10_Conference) (A10) Defensive Player of the Year and was additionally named to the A10 All-Conference Second Team.[^cite_note-SJU_bio-4] She was named to the [Nancy Lieberman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Lieberman_Award) Watch List for the top point guard in the nation.&#x20;



Cloud (center) at [Saint Joseph's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph's_Hawks_women's_basketball)

During the season, Cloud averaged 11.5 points, 6.6 rebounds, and 7.6 assists per game. She ranked second nationally in assists per game, setting the SJU single-season record for assists (243) and leading the A10 in assists and assists to turnover ratio.[^cite_note-SJU_bio-4] During the [2014 NCAA tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NCAA_Division_I_women's_basketball_tournament), she scored 13 points, seven rebounds, four assists, two blocked shots, and two steals in a first round win over [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Bulldogs_women's_basketball). During the second round, she posted 10 points, six assists, one block, and one steal against [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UConn_Huskies_women's_basketball), the eventual champions.[^cite_note-SJU_bio-4]

#### 2014–2015

Cloud served as the co-captain of her team for the second straight year. She was named Atlantic 10 All-Conference First Team and was an All-Defensive Team selection. She was a finalist for the [Nancy Lieberman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Lieberman_Award) and [Naismith Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naismith_Trophy) Watch List nominee.&#x20;

During the season, Cloud averaged 12.9 points a game and led the Atlantic 10 in assists and averaged minutes.[^cite_note-SJU_bio-4] She scored a career-high 29 points, 5 rebounds, 3 assists, 2 steals and a blocked shot against [Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Lady_Flames_basketball).[^cite_note-SJU_bio-4]

## Professional career



Cloud with the [Washington Mystics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Mystics) in 2019

### WNBA

#### Washington Mystics

Cloud was selected in the second round and 15th overall by the [Washington Mystics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Mystics) in the 2015 [WNBA](57721.md) draft.[^cite_note-WNBA-6] During her rookie season, she averaged 3.6 points, 2.8 rebounds, and 3.4 assists per game over an average of 19.3 minutes.[^cite_note-WNBA-6]

In 2016, Cloud suffered a left hip injury during practice at the [Verizon Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_One_Arena).[^cite_note-7]

On October 10, 2019, Cloud won her first WNBA championship.[^cite_note-8]

In June 2020, Cloud announced that she would forgo the [2020 WNBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_WNBA_season) due to concerns regarding the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and a desire to focus on social justice advocacy.[^cite_note-9]

#### Phoenix Mercury

After playing for the Mystics for nine years, Cloud signed to the Phoenix Mercury in 2024.[^cite_note-10]

On February 2, 2025, Cloud was traded to the [Connecticut Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Sun). The deal was originally reported as Cloud, [Rebecca Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Allen_\(basketball\)), and the 12th pick in the [2025 WNBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_WNBA_draft) for [Alyssa Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alyssa_Thomas) and [Tyasha Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyasha_Harris);[^cite_note-11] however, it was officially part of a larger four-team trade.[^cite_note-12]

#### New York Liberty

On March 16, 2025, Cloud was traded once again, this time to the [New York Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Liberty) for the 7th pick in the 2025 draft, and a first round draft pick in 2026.[^cite_note-13] Cloud started 41 games for the New York Liberty in 2025, averaging 10.1 points, 5.1 assists, and 1.2 steals per game.[^cite_note-\:0-14]

#### Chicago Sky

On May 4, 2026, Cloud signed a one-year, \$550,000 contract, with the [Chicago Sky](2717051.md).[^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The general manager of the Chicago Sky, Jeff Pagliocca, said in a statement, "Natasha is one of the best passers and defenders in the league. She fits with the other proven winners on our roster."[^cite_note-\:0-14] During a game against the [Indiana Fever](240214.md) on August 23, Cloud was involved in a verbal altercation with former NBA player [Enes Kanter](22346872.md), who was sitting courtside and walked onto the court. Kanter had been involved in an anti-trans campaign surrounding the WNBA and was wearing a shirt with similar messaging. The incident resulted in Kanter being escorted from the arena.[^cite_note-17]

### International

During the 2015–2016 season, Cloud played in Istanbul, Turkey for [Beşiktaş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be%C5%9Fikta%C5%9F_JK_\(women's_basketball\)).[^cite_note-18]

### Athletes Unlimited Pro Basketball

From 2022 to 2024, Cloud played for [Athletes Unlimited Pro Basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletes_Unlimited_Pro_Basketball). She was a member of the Player Executive Committee from 2022 to 2023 and named to the league's 2023 All Defense Team.[^cite_note-19]

### Unrivaled

On August 27, 2024, it was announced that Cloud would appear and play in the inaugural season of [Unrivaled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrivaled_\(basketball_league\)), the women's 3-on-3 basketball league founded by [Napheesa Collier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napheesa_Collier) and [Breanna Stewart](32433243.md).[^cite_note-20] Cloud was initially drafted to [Laces BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laces_BC) before being traded to [Lunar Owls BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Owls_BC) and then finally to [Phantom BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_BC) in the franchise's first trade movement.[^cite_note-21] She played 18 games with Phantom BC, averaging 9.7 points, 4.9 rebounds, and 4.7 assists.[^cite_note-22]

## National team career

Cloud played for [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_women's_national_basketball_team) at the [2021 FIBA Women's Asia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIBA_Women's_Asia_Cup) in [Amman, Jordan](45007.md).[^cite_note-23] She helped her team to reach the promotion playoff final, before losing to [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_women's_national_basketball_team) and missing promotion to Division A.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Personal life



Cloud with the Washington Mystics in 2019

Natasha Cloud is a [lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian) who has been an outspoken activist for marginalized communities. She has denounced the WNBA ignoring LGBTQ players in the league's marketing and promotional drives.[^cite_note-27]

She married professional [softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball) player [Aleshia Ocasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleshia_Ocasio) in 2020.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] They later divorced.[^cite_note-30] As of 2025, Cloud was dating her then-teammate [Isabelle Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_Harrison) since 2021.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

In December 2020, Cloud was named to [Forbes 30 Under 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_30_Under_30) alongside fellow WNBA players [A'ja Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A'ja_Wilson) and [Chiney Ogwumike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiney_Ogwumike).[^cite_note-33]

In February 2024, Cloud joined the WNBA Changemakers Collective and their collaboration with VOICEINSPORT (VIS) as a mentor, "aimed at keeping girls in sport and developing diverse leaders on the court and beyond the game."[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]
