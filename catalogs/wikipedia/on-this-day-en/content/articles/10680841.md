# Organisations listed as terrorist organisations under Australia's Criminal Code

In [Australia](4689264.md), **listed terrorist organisations** are organisations that have been designated by the [Australian Government](1222540.md) as [terrorist organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organisation) under the country's [Criminal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law_of_Australia). A list of terrorist organisations was first created under the *Security Legislation Amendment (Terrorism) Act 2002*, as part of the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93United_States_relations)–led "[war on terror](13425800.md)". Most of the organisations on the list have never targeted Australia.

## History

Further information: [Terrorism in Australia](9971246.md)

The *Security Legislation Amendment (Terrorism) Act 2002* was passed by the [Australian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Parliament) and inserted a range of terrorist organisation offences into the *[Criminal Code Act 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law_of_Australia)* (Cth). For example, the Act made it an offence to provide support to a terrorist organisation.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-ANS1-2]

The [paramilitary](146730.md) wing of [Hamas](13913.md), the [Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izz_al-Din_al-Qassam_Brigades), was formerly listed as a terrorist organisation from 5 November 2003 until 2022.[^cite_note-Hamas-3] In July 2014, it was reported that the entirety of Hamas was being listed by the [Abbott government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbott_government);[^cite_note-4] however, it did not appear on the list, and Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigades was re-listed in 2015, 2018 and 2021.[^cite_note-5] Hamas was listed in March 2022, replacing Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigades.[^cite_note-Hamas-3][^cite_note-6]

Most of the listed organisations are [Islamic](566821.md). In 2021, the Australian Government listed for the first time two [far-right](5210960.md) [neo-Nazi](54361.md) organisations, [Sonnenkrieg Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonnenkrieg_Division) and [The Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Base_\(hate_group\)), followed by the [National Socialist Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist_Order) in 2022.[^cite_note-ANS2-7]

In addition to The Base, the entirety of [Hezbollah](13919.md) was added on 24 November 2021.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In 2025, the Australian Government announced that the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) would be listed after it was revealed [Iran was responsible for at least two antisemitic attacks in Australia](80881934.md).[^cite_note-10] On 27 November, the government listed the IRGC under a new category as a "[state sponsor of terrorism](317645.md)" with the *Criminal Code Amendment (State Sponsors of Terrorism) Act 2025*.[^cite_note-IRGC-11][^cite_note-12] Restrictions on state sponsors of terrorism and the processes for listing are similar to those of terrorist organisations.[^cite_note-IRGC-11][^cite_note-ANS3-13]

## Oversight and monitoring

The list of designated terror organisations is maintained by the [Attorney-General's Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General's_Department_\(Australia\)). For listing as a terrorist organisation, an organisation may be found to be such by a court as part of a prosecution for a terrorist offence, or the designation may be made by regulation upon the motion of the [Attorney-General of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_of_Australia) under Division 102 of the *Criminal Code Act 1995*.[^cite_note-ANS1-2] Listing, de-listing and re-listing follows a protocol that mainly involves the [Australian Security Intelligence Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Security_Intelligence_Organisation) and the Attorney-General's Department.[^cite_note-14] Such action in general is not made with reference to designations made or proposed by other countries or multinational organisations. Until 2023, listings would lapse after three years and an organisation would need to be re-listed. The *Counter-Terrorism Legislation Amendment (Prohibited Hate Symbols and Other Measures) Act 2023* changed this so that listings now continue indefinitely.[^cite_note-ANS2-7]

Under Division 103 of the *[Criminal Code Act 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Code_Act_1995)*, it is illegal to finance terrorism.[^cite_note-Criminal_Code_Act-15] The [Australian Transaction Reports and Analysis Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Transaction_Reports_and_Analysis_Centre) (AUSTRAC) monitors financial transactions involving listed terrorist organisations.

## Terrorism financing laws

Australian anti-terrorism financing laws include:

- *[Criminal Code Act 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law_of_Australia)* (Cth):

  [^cite_note-Criminal_Code_Act-15]

  - section 102.6 – getting funds to, from or for a terrorist organisation

  - section 102.7 – providing support to a terrorist organisation

  - section 103.1 – financing terrorism

  - section 103.2 – financing a terrorist, and

  - section 119.4(5) – giving or receiving goods and services to promote the commission of a foreign incursion offence.

- *[Charter of the United Nations](88805.md) Act 1945* (Cth):

  [^cite_note-Charter_of_the_United_Nations_Act-21]

  - section 20 – dealing with freezable assets, and

  - section 21 – giving an asset to a proscribed person or entity.

These offences sanction persons and entities under Australian and international law.[^cite_note-Charter_of_the_United_Nations_Act-21] The responsibility of prosecuting these offences in Australia rests with the [Australian Federal Police](341221.md), state police forces and the [Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_Public_Prosecutions_\(Australia\)).
