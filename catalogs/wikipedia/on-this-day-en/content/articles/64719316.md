**Yamandú Ramón Antonio Orsi Martínez**[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] (born 13 June 1967) is a Uruguayan politician and history teacher who has been the 43rd [president of Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Uruguay) since March 2025.[^cite_note-3] A member of the left-wing [Movement of Popular Participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Popular_Participation), the main faction of the [Broad Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Front_\(Uruguay\)) party, he served as intendant of the [Canelones Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canelones_Department) from 2015 to February 2020, and a second term from November 2020 to 2024.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

A graduate from the [Instituto de Profesores Artigas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_de_Profesores_Artigas) in 1991 as a history teacher, he worked in different secondary schools in the Canelones, [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Department), and [Maldonado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldonado_Department) departments.[^cite_note-8] He became politically active during his adolescence, being part of the [Vertiente Artiguista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertiente_Artiguista) until 1990, when he joined the [Movement of Popular Participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Popular_Participation) (MPP) that had been founded the previous year.

## Early life and education

Yamandú Ramón Antonio Orsi Martínez[^cite_note-9] was born in rural [Canelones Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canelones_Department) on 13 June 1967, to Pablo "Bebe" Orsi, a [rural worker](10721543.md), and Carmen "Beba" Martínez, a [seamstress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dressmaker). He has an older sister, María Orsi, Sra. del Luján.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Orsi is of paternal [Italian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Uruguayans), tracing his heritage to [Osiglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osiglia), a municipality situated in the province of [Savona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Savona), in the [Liguria](71864.md) region of Italy.[^cite_note-lr21.com.uy-12] Orsi claims that the first of his paternal ancestors [immigrated to Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Uruguay) at the beginning of the 19th century.[^cite_note-13] Following his presidential election victory, Paola Scarzella, the current mayor of Osiglia, congratulated Orsi and expressed her pride upon learning of his ancestral ties.[^cite_note-lr21.com.uy-12] Italian news outlets revealed that Orsi is the great-grandson of Giovanni Orsi, a native of Osiglia who immigrated to Uruguay.[^cite_note-14] On his maternal side, he is of [Spanish descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Uruguayans) whose ancestors were one of the first settlers in [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo) from the [Canary Islands](5717.md).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

Raised in a rural area between the towns of [Santa Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Rosa,_Uruguay) and [San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio,_Canelones), the family struggled financially during Orsi's adolescence years and, for a time, lived in a house without electricity.[^cite_note-17] At the age of five, he moved to the city of [Canelones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canelones,_Uruguay) due to his father's spine disease, which prevented him from working in the fields.[^cite_note-18] The family set up a grocery store there, where Orsi began his primary studies at Primary School No. 110 and his secondary studies at the Liceo [Tomás Berreta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1s_Berreta).[^cite_note-19]

Coming from a [Roman Catholic](606848.md) family, Orsi was an [altar boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar_boy) for the neighborhood chapel.[^cite_note-20] In his adolescence, he practiced [folk dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_dance), and at the age of fifteen, he won a contest to be part of a municipal cast, which he integrated until he was 26.[^cite_note-21] In turn, in his teens he became politically active, militating in the [Vertiente Artiguista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertiente_Artiguista) until 1990, when he joined the [Movement of Popular Participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Popular_Participation) (MPP). He began by participating in a collection of signatures for the [1989 amnesty referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Uruguayan_amnesty_referendum) on the [Law on the Expiration of the Punitive Claims of the State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_on_the_Expiration_of_the_Punitive_Claims_of_the_State).[^cite_note-22]

In 1986, he began a degree in [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations) at the [University of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Republic_\(Uruguay\)), however, he dropped out after a month. Subsequently, he enrolled at the [Instituto de Profesores Artigas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_de_Profesores_Artigas) in Montevideo to study for a teaching post in history in secondary education, graduating in 1991.[^cite_note-23]

## Political career

### Early political career



Orsi giving a speech at a ceremony commemorating the [Battle of Las Piedras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Las_Piedras_\(1811\)); 18 May 2023.

In 2004, while still working as a history teacher in [Maldonado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldonado_Department), he ran unsuccessfully for the [Chamber of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Representatives_of_Uruguay) as the fourth candidate on the electoral list of the [Movement of Popular Participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Popular_Participation) in the [Canelones Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canelones_Department).[^cite_note-24] In early 2005, he was announced as [Marcos Carámbula's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Car%C3%A1mbula's?action=edit\&redlink=1) first alternate in his candidacy for Intendant of Canelones. Following Carámbulas's victory in the [municipal elections of that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Uruguayan_municipal_elections), Orsi was appointed Secretary General of the Intendancy.[^cite_note-\:0-25] After the [2010 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Uruguayan_municipal_elections), he was confirmed in office after the re-election of the Carámbula administration.[^cite_note-26] Since July 2005, he has been a member of the national and departmental board in Canelones of the [Movement of Popular Participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Popular_Participation).[^cite_note-\:0-25]

### Intendant of Canelones (2015–2024)

In early March 2015, he resigned to run for Intendant. His candidacy was supported by various sectors of the [Broad Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Front_\(Uruguay\)), such as the MPP, the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Uruguay), the [Vertiente Artiguista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertiente_Artiguista), and Casa Grande.[^cite_note-27] In the [2015 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Uruguayan_municipal_elections), he was elected Intendant of the Canelones Department with 37% of the vote, being the candidate with the most votes from the party with the most votes, according to the *[Ley de Lemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ley_de_Lemas)* system.[^cite_note-28] He took office on 9 July 2015.[^cite_note-29] In October 2019, facing the second round of the [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Uruguayan_general_election), Orsi was appointed campaign manager for [Broad Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Front_\(Uruguay\)) nominee [Daniel Martínez Villamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Mart%C3%ADnez_\(politician\)).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] On 7 February 2020, he resigned from the position of Intendant of Canelones, being succeeded by Tabaré Costa.[^cite_note-32] However, he launched his campaign for re-election, and in the [municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Uruguayan_municipal_elections) of that year, he was re-elected in office.[^cite_note-33]

### 2024 presidential campaign

After the [Broad Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Front_\(Uruguay\))'s electoral defeat in the [2019 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Uruguayan_general_election), Orsi began to be seen as a contender for the [2024 presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Uruguayan_presidential_primaries).[^cite_note-34] On 19 March 2023, the [Movement of Popular Participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Popular_Participation) officially announced its support for his candidacy.[^cite_note-35] He also received the support of the [Vertiente Artiguista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertiente_Artiguista) and other leftist sectors of the party.[^cite_note-36] On 9 December 2023, during the Broad Front Congress, Orsi's candidacy was made official, as well as that of [Mario Bergara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Bergara_\(politician\)), [Carolina Cosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Cosse), and [Andrés Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Lima_\(politician\)).[^cite_note-37]

## Presidency (2025–present)

Main article: [Presidency of Yamandú Orsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Yamand%C3%BA_Orsi)



Orsi during his presidential inauguration at the [Legislative Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Palace_of_Uruguay); 1 March 2025.

Orsi was the Broad Front's candidate for president in the [2024 Uruguayan general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Uruguayan_general_election). He emerged with a plurality of 43.2–44% in the first round on 27 October and faced [Álvaro Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Delgado_\(politician\)) of the [Coalición Multicolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalici%C3%B3n_Multicolor) in the runoff on 24 November,[^cite_note-38] which Orsi won.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-toostable-40] After the projection of the results, he was congratulated by several heads of state across the world, including from [Gabriel Boric](47329497.md), [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md), and [Claudia Sheinbaum](57813311.md). Additionally, he was also congratulated by international politicians such as [Yolanda Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yolanda_D%C3%ADaz), [Cristina Fernández de Kirchner](1644780.md), and [Mauricio Macri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Macri). After the night of the presidential runoff, Orsi's first public activity as president-elect was visiting former president [José Mujica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mujica) and his wife, former vice president [Lucía Topolansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_Topolansky), at their private residence in the outskirts of [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo). The following day, outgoing president [Luis Lacalle Pou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Lacalle_Pou) chaired a meeting with the nation's [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Uruguay) to plan out the presidential transition for Orsi.[^cite_note-41]

On 23 September, during his campaign ahead of the 2024 general elections, Orsi announced his first cabinet member, economist [Gabriel Oddone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Oddone) would proceed as the [Ministry of Economy and Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_and_Finance_\(Uruguay\)) during his administration.[^cite_note-42] On 27 November, Orsi assigned [Alejandro Sánchez Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_S%C3%A1nchez_Pereira) to serve as the [Secretary of the Presidency.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Presidency_of_Uruguay)[^cite_note-43] In the following days, Orsi began appointing more representatives for his upcoming administration, including [Alfredo Fratti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Fratti), recommended by José Mujica himself, as [Minister of Livestock, Agriculture, and Fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Livestock,_Agriculture,_and_Fisheries), and [Cristina Lustemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Lustemberg) as [Minister of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Health_\(Uruguay\)), among others.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] By 16 December, Orsi officially announced all the members of his cabinet.[^cite_note-46]

Upon his election as president-elect, Orsi met with several international politicians in the preceding days to discuss relations between Uruguay and their nation, including meetings on 28 November, with the [Chinese Ambassador to Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_China_to_Uruguay), Huang Yazhong, who delivered him a letter from [General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) to congratulate his win in hopes of deepening ties with China,[^cite_note-47] and with [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md) in [Brasília](4752.md) to discuss infrastructure projects.[^cite_note-48]

On 6 December, the 2024 summit of [Mercosur](25869317.md) began in Montevideo, where Orsi participated as an invited guest of former President Lacalle Pou. At the summit, the final text of the [EU–Mercosur free trade agreement](61184322.md) was concluded after nearly 25 years of negotiations, during which, Orsi held bilateral meetings with regional leaders, including [Luis Arce](38820569.md), [Santiago Peña](58414156.md), and [Gustavo Petro](13391962.md).[^cite_note-49]

On 2 February 2025, Orsi announced that he would not be moving to the [official presidential residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residencia_de_Su%C3%A1rez) in Montevideo and would instead continue to live in his residence in Salinas.[^cite_note-50]

## Personal life

Orsi is married to [Laura Alonsopérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Alonsop%C3%A9rez?action=edit\&redlink=1), an actress and ballet dancer originally from [Maldonado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldonado_Department).[^cite_note-51] Together, the couple have two children, twins Lucía and Victorio, born in 2012.[^cite_note-52] Orsi resides with his family in the municipality of [Salinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinas,_Uruguay).[^cite_note-53] Despite his Catholic upbringing, Orsi now considers himself an [agnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnosticism).[^cite_note-\:1-54] Orsi is an avid [association football](10568.md) fan and a supporter of [Club Atlético Peñarol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1arol).[^cite_note-55]
