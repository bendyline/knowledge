**Iceberg lettuce**, also called **crisphead lettuce**, is a common [cultivar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultivar) of [lettuce](57079.md). Created in 1894 in the United States by [Burpee Seeds and Plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burpee_Seeds_and_Plants), it has since become a staple type of lettuce consumed globally.&#x20;

## Name

The iceberg lettuce received its name from the way it was packaged for transport – the heads were once packaged on [crushed ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crushed_ice).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChicago_Tribune2007-1] It is sometimes called crisphead lettuce.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETuriniCahnCantwellJackson20111-2]

## History

Iceberg lettuce was cultivated in 1894 by [Burpee Seeds and Plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burpee_Seeds_and_Plants),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChicago_Tribune2007-1] which introduced it into their seed catalog the same year and marketed it with the slogan, "there is no handsomer or more solid cabbage lettuce in cultivation".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMedleyEllis201640-3] In the [United States](3434750.md) in the early 20th-century, iceberg lettuce was grown in the summer at high altitudes in [Colorado](5399.md) and [Idaho](14607.md) and in spring in [Arizona](21883824.md) and the [Imperial Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Valley) of California.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMedleyEllis201640-3] It quickly became the most common variety of lettuce grown in the [Western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States), while the [Boston Head lettuce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Head_lettuce?action=edit\&redlink=1) remained the most popular in the [Eastern United States](509486.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMedleyEllis201640-3] In 1922, the Sawdey-Hunt Company became the biggest buyer of the lettuce. They sold heads to hotel chains around the United States, most notably to [Waldorf Astoria Hotels & Resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldorf_Astoria_Hotels_&_Resorts).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMedleyEllis201640-3] The cultivar is sought after for its mild flavor and crisp texture, and it is consumed internationally.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemuOanh20251-4]

## Production

Iceberg lettuce matures faster in cooler temperatures, reaching prime age in around 60 days.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemuOanh20252-5] It is highly sensitive to heat, and it is recommended to harvest during the coldest time of the day.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemuOanh20253-6] Overly mature specimens have very firm and hard heads while immature ones have loose heads and are more prone to damage.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemuOanh20252-5] It is highly [perishable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perishable_food), and a study in Sweden found it was the third-most lost produce in retail stores.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEErikssonStridHansson201214–20-7][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemuOanh20252-5] The shelf life is often shortened due to improper packaging.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemuOanh20252-5] They are best suited to plastic wrap packaging, which lowers the risk of damage and keeps moisture out.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemuOanh20259-8]

### United States

The [state of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_California) produces iceberg lettuce all year round with its peak season in May and June and a low season in December, January, and February. In these months, the majority of the nation's supply is provided by Arizona.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETuriniCahnCantwellJackson20116-9] In 2009, California had 114,000 acres (46,000 ha) of iceberg lettuce of which the average yield was 20.7 short tons per acre (46 t/ha). Within the state, cultivation is most common in the [Central Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Coast_\(California\)), [South Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_\(California\)), and [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Valley_\(California\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETuriniCahnCantwellJackson20111-2]

Planting the iceberg lettuce in the [midsummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsummer) period results in a harvest around 70 to 80 days later.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETuriniCahnCantwellJackson20111-2] Planting in the cooler periods of late [autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn) and [winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter) can result in a harvest over 130 days later.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETuriniCahnCantwellJackson20111-2] Depending on the location, iceberg lettuce has been grown in [silt loam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silt_loam) and clay soil, although the latter needs appropriate drainage.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETuriniCahnCantwellJackson20112-10] A majority of iceberg lettuce in California were planted with precision planters and pelleted seeds.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETuriniCahnCantwellJackson20112-10] Californian iceberg lettuce is typically shipped around the United States and Canada, though some smaller quantities are also shipped to Europe.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETuriniCahnCantwellJackson20116-9]

## Recall due to contamination

Due to high water content and raw consumption, iceberg lettuce is susceptible to [pathogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathogen) contamination during harvesting and processing, with large-scale farming increasing the reach of contamination events across international [supply chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain). A major example occurred in July 2026, when global salad supplier [Taylor Farms](36866102.md) had to recall iceberg lettuce linked to a *[Cyclospora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclospora_\(genus\))* parasite outbreak causing over 1,600 illnesses, highlighting vulnerabilities in centralized agricultural production.[^cite_note-11]
