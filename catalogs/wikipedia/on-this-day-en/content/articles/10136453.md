**Pekka Olavi Haavisto** (Finnish: [\[ˈpekːɑ ˈolɑʋi ˈhɑːʋisto\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Finnish); born 23 March 1958) is a Finnish politician of the [Green League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_League) who served as the [Minister for Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_\(Finland\)) from 2019 to 2023.

Haavisto returned to the [Finnish Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Finland) in the [Finnish parliamentary election of March 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Finnish_parliamentary_election) after an absence of 12 years and was re-elected in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Finnish_parliamentary_election), [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Finnish_parliamentary_election), and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Finnish_parliamentary_election). Between April 1995 and April 1999 he was the [Minister of Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Environment_\(Finland\)) in the [Lipponen I Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipponen_I_Cabinet). In October 2013 he was appointed as the Minister for International Development after [Heidi Hautala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidi_Hautala) resigned from the job. He has also been a member of the [Helsinki City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_City_Council).

Haavisto finished second in the Finnish presidential elections in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Finnish_presidential_election), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Finnish_presidential_election) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Finnish_presidential_election); twice to [Sauli Niinistö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauli_Niinist%C3%B6) and finally to [Alexander Stubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Stubb). Haavisto became the first openly [gay](5171128.md) candidate to run for the presidency of Finland.

## Political career



[Pertti Salolainen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertti_Salolainen) and Pekka Haavisto in 1994

Haavisto was a member of the Parliament of Finland from 1987 to 1995. He was the chairperson of the Green League from 1993 to 1995. He served as the [Minister of the Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Environment_\(Finland\)) in [Paavo Lipponen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paavo_Lipponen)'s first cabinet between 1995 and 1999. He was the first [European](9239.md) cabinet minister representing a [Green party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldwide_green_parties).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

From 1999 to 2005, Haavisto worked for the [United Nations](31769.md) in various tasks. He led the [United Nations Environment Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme) (UNEP) research groups in [Kosovo](17391.md), [Afghanistan](737.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Liberia](17791.md), [Palestine](241405.md) and [Sudan](27421.md). He also coordinated the UN investigation in the effects of [depleted uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depleted_uranium) in Kosovo, [Montenegro](20760.md), [Serbia](29265.md) and [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md).[^cite_note-4] Haavisto also represented the UNEP in the investigations in the [Baia Mare mining accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Baia_Mare_cyanide_spill) in [Romania](25445.md).[^cite_note-5] In 2005 he was appointed as the special representative of the [European Union](9317.md) in [Sudan](27421.md) where he participated in the [Darfur](605837.md) peace talks.[^cite_note-6]

In 2007 and 2011 Haavisto was re-elected to Parliament by the electoral district of [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_\(electoral_district\)).

### Presidential election 2012



Pekka Haavisto in 2011.

In 2011, Haavisto was nominated as the Green League candidate for the [Finnish presidential election of 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Finnish_presidential_election).[^cite_note-7] In the first round of the election on 22 January 2012, he finished second with 18.8 percent of the votes. In the run-off on 5 February, he garnered more than one million votes (37.4 percent), coming second to [National Coalition Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coalition_Party) candidate, former Finance Minister [Sauli Niinistö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauli_Niinist%C3%B6).[^cite_note-2012-first-round-8] Haavisto was the first openly gay candidate and the first male candidate to have served in [civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siviilipalvelus) instead of the [military service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_Finland) to make it to the second round of a presidential election in Finland.

### Presidential election 2018

In February 2017, Haavisto announced that he would reprise his candidacy in the 2018 [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Finnish_presidential_election).[^cite_note-9] The decision came after Haavisto had been approached multiple times by the Green League.[^cite_note-10] In the election, Haavisto placed second with 12.4% of the votes, while President Niinistö went on to secure his second term with a majority of votes.[^cite_note-11]

### Return as chairman

In October 2018, the chairman of the Green League [Touko Aalto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touko_Aalto) announced that he was resigning from his post, citing depression and fatigue.[^cite_note-12] The decision prompted an immediate leadership election, with the goal to elect a temporary chairman to lead the party into the [2019 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Finnish_parliamentary_election) and until the next party convention. As many members of the party called for more prominent and experienced politicians to take part in the election, Haavisto announced his candidacy.[^cite_note-13] On 4 November 2018, he defeated MP [Outi Alanko-Kahiluoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outi_Alanko-Kahiluoto) in the leadership election and became temporary chairman.[^cite_note-14]

In June 2019, Haavisto stepped down as the chairman of the party. He was succeeded by [Maria Ohisalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Ohisalo).[^cite_note-15]

### Minister of Foreign Affairs, 2019–2023



Haavisto signing Finland's application for [NATO membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland%E2%80%93NATO_relations) in 2022

Following their best ever result in the [2019 national elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Finnish_parliamentary_election) and after becoming part of the incoming governing coalition under the leadership of [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Finland) [Antti Rinne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antti_Rinne), the Green League named Haavisto as the next foreign minister.[^cite_note-16] In this capacity, he chaired the meetings of the [Foreign Affairs Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_Council) during Finland's rotating [presidency of the Council of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_the_Council_of_the_European_Union) in 2019.[^cite_note-auto-17]

In 2019, Haavisto called for the EU to intensify efforts on improving relations with Russia in the face of international conflict, transatlantic tensions and [Brexit](41688778.md).[^cite_note-auto-17]



Haavisto's press conference about the [situation in al-Hawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hawl_refugee_camp) in 2019.

In December 2020, the Parliament's Constitutional Law Committee found that Haavisto, who had pushed for Finnish children from the [al-Hawl refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hawl_refugee_camp) in northern [Syria](7515849.md) to be brought to Finland quickly in late 2019, had broken [Finnish law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_law) by trying to replace a [foreign ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_for_Foreign_Affairs_\(Finland\)) official who refused to act after a difference of opinion over the exact repatriation proceedings. The events were leaked to the press causing a political crisis.[^cite_note-18] Haavisto quickly reverted his decision to demote the official and offered him the same position as earlier. The Committee declared it had not found reason to bring [criminal charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_charge) against the foreign minister, due to the minister's higher than usual prosecution threshold required by the constitution,[^cite_note-yle.fi-19] but specified that Haavisto had operated in contravention to the Administration Law and the Foreign Relations Act. Two Green League MPs published a dissenting opinion and advocated for a softer verdict, causing controversy in the Committee.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In Parliament, Haavisto received the Parliament's support in a 101-68 [interpellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpellation_\(politics\)) vote. Haavisto's decision to return the refugees received the Parliament's support on a 110-79 interpellation vote.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

In February and March 2021, Haavisto was under criminal investigation for an alleged Highway Code violation in December 2020. The complaint originated from a video recorded by a reporter for the gossip magazine *[Seiska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiska)*, which included commentary that Haavisto had not stopped for two pedestrian crossings. The prosecutor who reviewed the video evidence decided not to press charges, citing construction work that had disabled the first crossing and created an unclear situation for the other.[^cite_note-25]



Haavisto meets with [IAEA](14984.md) Director General, [Rafael Mariano Grossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Mariano_Grossi) during his official visit at the Agency headquarters in Vienna, Austria. 1 November 2022.



Haavisto meets with [U.S. Secretary of State](32293.md) [Antony J. Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) in [Washington](108956.md) in May 2022.

Haavisto was appointed EU's special envoy to Ethiopia during the [Tigray War](65760352.md). In February 2021, Haavisto stated that violence and suffering in Ethiopia was "out of control" and could cause a large refugee crisis.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] In June 2021, he briefed the European Parliament on the conflict.[^cite_note-28] Haavisto warned of a possible [ethnic cleansing of Tigrayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crimes_in_the_Tigray_War#Ethnic_cleansing_of_Tigrayans_in_the_Western_Zone), stating that Ethiopia's leaders in closed-door talks said "they are going to wipe out the Tigrayans for 100 years." Ethiopia's foreign ministry dismissed Haavisto's comments as "ludicrous" and a "hallucination of sorts or a lapse in memory of some kind."[^cite_note-29] Haavisto stated that Ethiopian criticism will have no effect on his work as envoy and referred to an upcoming UN human rights report on the situation.[^cite_note-30]

On 17 May 2022, Haavisto signed Finland's [application to join NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland%E2%80%93NATO_relations).[^cite_note-31] Haavisto visited [Kyiv](585629.md) in November, stating that "Finland strongly condemns Russia's illegal [invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). We will continue our firm support to Ukraine as long as necessary."[^cite_note-32]

### 2024 presidential election

In June 2023, Haavisto announced that he would reprise his candidacy in the [2024 Finnish presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Finnish_presidential_election) as an independent candidate.[^cite_note-33] Haavisto finished second in the first round of voting held on 18 January 2024 and later lost to [Alexander Stubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Stubb) in the runoff on 11 February 2024. Stubb officially took office as the 13th President of Finland on 1 March 2024.

## Personal life

After completing the [matriculation examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation_examination) of the [upper secondary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_secondary_school),[^cite_note-eduskunta.fi-38] Haavisto began studying [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sciences) at the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki). He dropped the studies after a year and did not complete the degree.[^cite_note-39] As a young man he chose [civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siviilipalvelus) over military service in the [Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Defence_Forces).[^cite_note-40]

Haavisto is openly [gay](5171128.md). Although his homosexuality was an open secret during the 1990s, his sexual orientation wasn't public until 2002, when he entered in a [registered partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registered_partnership) with [Nexar Antonio Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nexar_Antonio_Flores),[^cite_note-41] an [Ecuadorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorians) man born in [Esmeraldas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esmeraldas,_Ecuador) in 1978. They have been together since 1997 when they met in a [Bogotá](211271.md) night club.[^cite_note-eduskunta.fi-38][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

According to the ranking of the Finnish *[Ulkopolitiikka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulkopolitiikka)* magazine in 2009, Haavisto was internationally the 5th most influential person in Finland.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]
