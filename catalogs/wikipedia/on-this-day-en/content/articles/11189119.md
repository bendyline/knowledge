The [Theban Necropolis](3300379.md) is located on the west bank of the [Nile](21244.md), opposite [Luxor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxor), in [Egypt](8087628.md). As well as the more famous royal tombs located in the [Valley of the Kings](3036884.md) and the [Valley of the Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_the_Queens), there are numerous other tombs, more commonly referred to as Tombs of the Nobles (Luxor), the burial places of some of the powerful courtiers and persons of the ancient city.

There are at least 415 cataloged tombs, designated **TT** for **T**heban **T**omb. There are other tombs whose position has been lost, or for some other reason do not conform to this classification. Theban tombs tended to have clay [funerary cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funerary_cone) placed over the entrance of the tomb chapels. During the [New Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom) they were inscribed with the title and name of the tomb owner, sometimes with short prayers. Of the 400 recorded sets of cones, only about 80 come from cataloged tombs.[^cite_note-1]

The numbering system was first published [Arthur Weigall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Weigall)'s 1908 *Report on the Tombs of Shêkh Abd’ el Gûrneh and el Assasîf* (up to TT 45–100)[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] and then more fully in [Alan Gardiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Gardiner) and [Arthur Weigall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Weigall)'s 1913 *A Topographical Catalogue of the Private Tombs of Thebes* (TT 1–252).[^cite_note-4] Gardiner and Weigall acknowledged that the numbers do not follow any topographical order, and are due the order in which the tombs were discovered.[^cite_note-5] Their publication was followed by [Reginald Engelbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Engelbach)'s *A Supplement to the Topographical Catalogue of the Private Tombs of Thebes* (TT 253 to 334), extended further in [Bernard Bruyère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Bruy%C3%A8re),[^cite_note-6] [N. de Garis Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._de_Garis_Davies),[^cite_note-7] [Ahmed Fakhry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Fakhry),[^cite_note-8] and later in [Bertha Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertha_Porter) & [Rosalind Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalind_Moss)'s *Topographical Bibliography of Ancient Egyptian Hieroglyphic Texts, Reliefs, and Paintings*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPorterMoss1960-9][^cite_note-Kaczanowicz_2020_pp._37–58-10]

In 1996, [Friederike Kampp-Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friederike_Seyfried?action=edit\&redlink=1) published *Die thebanische Nekropole zum Wandel des Grabgedankens von der XVIII. bis zur XX. Dynastie* (2 vols.),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKampp1996-11] documenting over 400 private tombs. She assigned numbers between dashes to previously unnumbered tombs – mainly undecorated and/or previously undocumented. These tombs are not included in the lists below.
