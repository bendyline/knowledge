**Min Aung Hlaing**[^cite_note-17] (born 3 July 1956) is a Burmese politician and retired military officer who has been the 11th [president of Myanmar](11195083.md) since April 2026, having ruled the country continuously since seizing power in February 2021 through a [military coup d'état](66567997.md) against the elected government. Prior to the start of his nominally civilian presidency, he was a military ruler under various titles.[^cite_note-18] He was appointed the [commander-in-chief of Defence Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief_of_Defence_Services)—the leader of the [Tatmadaw](20394.md)—in 2011 and remained in that post through the period of military rule, until stepping down in 2026 to become the president.

Born in [Minbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minbu), Min Aung Hlaing studied law at the [Rangoon Arts and Science University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangoon_Arts_and_Science_University) before joining the military. Rising through its ranks, he became Commander-in-Chief of Defence Services by 2011 and a [senior general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_general_\(Myanmar\)) by 2013. After the [2015 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Myanmar_general_election) saw the [National League for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_for_Democracy) (NLD) assume power, he worked to ensure the military's continued role in politics and forestalled the [peace process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_peace_process) with [ethnic armed organizations](48437766.md). A [United Nations](31769.md) fact-finding mission found he orchestrated the [Rohingya genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide), for which the International Criminal Court is [formally considering issuing an arrest warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Bangladesh/Myanmar) against him. He maintained an adversarial relationship with democratically elected State Counsellor [Aung San Suu Kyi](2847.md) of the NLD, though she defended him against genocide charges. Min Aung Hlaing baselessly claimed widespread voting irregularities and electoral fraud in the [2020 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Myanmar_general_election), in which the NLD won a landslide re-election. He then seized power from Aung San Suu Kyi in the 2021 coup d'état. With the outbreak of [mass protests against his rule](66612294.md), Min Aung Hlaing ordered a clampdown and suppression of demonstrations, sparking an [ongoing civil war](69807451.md).

Min Aung Hlaing's forces have employed [scorched earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorched_earth) tactics in the civil war, including airstrikes on civilians. He has ordered the execution of prominent pro-democracy activists, the first use of the death penalty in decades. In 2023, he passed an election law that heavily favored the military-backed USDP and used the law as a pretext to ban the NLD. In 2024, he activated [Myanmar's conscription law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Military_Service_Law) to draft 60,000 young people into the Tatmadaw. In foreign policy, he has resisted influence from the Association of Southeast Asian Nations ([ASEAN](106539.md)) and relied on greater cooperation with [Russia](20155347.md), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Myanmar_relations), and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93Myanmar_relations). In response to his [human rights](1460094.md) abuses and [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Myanmar), Min Aung Hlaing and his government have been subjected to an extensive series of international sanctions, returning Myanmar to its former status as a [pariah state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pariah_state). He oversaw the heavily controlled [2025–26 general election](69631013.md) that officially showed a landslide USDP victory. Afterwards, he stepped down as Commander-in-Chief of Defence Services and was [elected](82700906.md) by the [Pyidaungsu Hluttaw](29739324.md) as the president of Myanmar, leading the transition to a nominally civilian government.

## Early life and education

Min Aung Hlaing was born on 3 July 1956 in [Minbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minbu), [Magway Region](493360.md), [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-independence_Burma,_1948%E2%80%931962) (now [Myanmar](19457.md)), to Khin Hlaing and Hla Mu, as the fourth of five children.[^cite_note-19] His parents were teachers from [Dawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawei), in [Tanintharyi Region](15162240.md).[^cite_note-20] His family moved to [Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay) for work when he was 5 years old. His father, Khin Hlaing, was an [artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artist).[^cite_note-dsinfo-21]

Min Aung Hlaing passed his [matriculation exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Entrance_Examination) in 1972 at [Basic Education High School No. 1 Latha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Education_High_School_No._1_Latha) (BEHS 1 Latha) of Rangoon (now [Yangon](57404.md)).[^cite_note-Asean-22][^cite_note-23] He attended and studied [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law) at the [Rangoon Arts and Science University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Yangon) from 1973 to 1974. On his third attempt, he was admitted to the [Defence Services Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Services_Academy) in 1974 as part of the 19th Intake, and he graduated in 1977.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] According to classmates, Min Aung Hlaing was taciturn, and an unremarkable cadet.[^cite_note-\:4-26] He was reportedly shunned by classmates because of his reserved personality.[^cite_note-Asean-22]

## Military service

Following graduation from the [Defence Services Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Services_Academy) (DSA) in 1977 with the 19th intake, Min Aung Hlaing went on to serve in different command positions, rising slowly through the ranks.[^cite_note-\:4-26] He was assigned to the No. (313) [Light Infantry Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_infantry) ([Hmawbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmawbi_District)), then known as the No. (1) Shan Rifle Battalion, under the command of the No. (77) Light Infantry Division. Former [president of Myanmar](11195083.md) [Thein Sein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thein_Sein) was also assigned to this battalion, and former [Director of Defence Service Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Chief_of_Military_Security_Affairs) General [Khin Nyunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khin_Nyunt) served as a company commander in the same unit.[^cite_note-27]

Early in his career, military colleagues gave him a nickname referring to cat feces, "something deposited quietly but leaving a powerful stink."[^cite_note-\:5-28]

In 1979, during an offensive known as Operation Min Yan Aung (Victorious King) against the [Communist Party of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Burma) (CPB) in what is now [Matman Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matman_Township) in [Wa State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa_State), east of the [Thanlwin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanlwin_River), Min Aung Hlaing served as an [Intelligence Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_officer) (IO) ranked as a [Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant) (2 stars) at 23 years old.[^cite_note-\:12-29]

In 1989, as a [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain) (3 stars), Min Aung Hlaing took part in the Battle of Wan Kha Thit, better known as the [Battle of Kawmoora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kawmoora). This battle was a clash between army troops controlled by then [Commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) [Senior General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_general_\(Myanmar\)) [Than Shwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Than_Shwe) and the [Karen National Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_National_Union) (KNU). Due to the base's difficult position to attack, the Myanmar military repeatedly assaulted it throughout 1989 but failed to seize it, halting operations in 1990 after suffering hundreds of casualties.[^cite_note-\:12-29]

To ward off harm from enemy shells and bullets, Min Aung Hlaing carried a Buddha statue in his bag of topographic maps worn across his chest. Despite making speeches for officer cadets to be brave soldiers like him, he never led any missions against [KNU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_National_Union) troops.[^cite_note-\:12-29]

Min Aung Hlaing served as a Battalion Officer Commanding, ranked as a [Major](201920.md), at the No. (369) Light Infantry Battalion ([Homalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homalin)) under the Regional Operations Command ([Kalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalay)) of the Northwestern Regional Military Command. During his service, he imprisoned the pregnant wife of a sergeant who was deemed to have deserted the battalion. At that time, the General Officer Commanding of the Regional Operations Command (Kalay) was [Brigadier General](206220.md) Thura [Aung Ko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aung_Ko_\(politician\)), and the [Tactical Operation Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade#Myanmar) Officer Commanding was [Colonel](199023.md) Kyaw Thu. During an inspection, Colonel Kyaw Thu discovered the woman locked in the battalion prison and questioned [Major](36301471.md) Min Aung Hlaing why he did such a thing. Min Aung Hlaing explained that she was imprisoned because her husband had deserted. Colonel Kyaw Thu responded, "Do not do such a disgraceful thing. It's nonsense to arrest the wife just because her husband deserted. Release her now."[^cite_note-30] The woman was only released thanks to Colonel Kyaw Thu's intervention.[^cite_note-31]

Min Aung Hlaing served as the 19th rector of the [Defence Services Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Services_Academy) (DSA), having graduated from its 19th intake. During his tenure, he was involved in an incident with [Nay Shwe Thway Aung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nay_Shwe_Thway_Aung) (also known as Phoe La Pyae), the 8-year-old grandson of [Senior General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_general_\(Myanmar\)) [Than Shwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Than_Shwe). Nay Shwe Thway Aung visited the academy with a colonel acting as his personal bodyguard. At Nay Shwe Thway Aung's request, Min Aung Hlaing organized a football match, dismissing the Officer Cadets from their Physical Training (PT) session. Throughout the match, Min Aung Hlaing frequently inquired about Nay Shwe Thway Aung's well-being, contrasting with his usually strict demeanor towards the officers on the field.[^cite_note-rector19-32]

Min Aung Hlaing was also known for his strict enforcement of military regulations. He reported several coaches and cadets to the Military Appointment General (MAG) for minor infractions, such as not wearing helmets while riding motorcycles. These actions were perceived as efforts to secure his promotion to [Major General](36301471.md) and to obtain a position as a General Officer Commanding as a Regional Military Command. As a result of his reports, the officers and cadets faced significant career setbacks, with many unable to advance beyond the rank of [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain).[^cite_note-rector19-32]

As he rose through the ranks, Min Aung Hlaing earned a reputation as a hardliner.[^cite_note-Asean-22] His military work earned him the favour of Senior General [Than Shwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Than_Shwe).[^cite_note-\:10-33] Min Aung Hlaing is characterized as having a "big man" management style not conducive to collaboration or listening.[^cite_note-\:10-33]

In 2002, he was promoted to commander of the [Triangle Regional Military Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_Regional_Military_Command) in eastern [Shan State](493376.md) and was a central figure in negotiations with two rebel groups, the [United Wa State Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Wa_State_Army) (UWSA) and the [National Democratic Alliance Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance_Army) (NDAA).[^cite_note-Asean-22] Min Aung Hlaing was reportedly close with former Thai prime minister and a General [Prem Tinsulanonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prem_Tinsulanonda), considering Prem a father figure.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

Min Aung Hlaing supported the military crackdown of the [Saffron Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron_Revolution) in 2008.[^cite_note-Asean-22] He rose to prominence in 2009 after leading an offensive against the insurgent [Myanmar Nationalities Democratic Alliance Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Nationalities_Democratic_Alliance_Army) (MNDAA) in [Kokang](1635498.md).[^cite_note-David-36] In June 2010, Min Aung Hlaing replaced General [Shwe Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shwe_Mann) as Joint Chief of Staff of the [Army](20394.md), [Navy](9502915.md), and [Air Force](9484818.md).[^cite_note-Asean-22]

### Corruption

Min Aung Hlaing has been the subject of controversy for his family's extensive business assets and potential conflicts of interest.[^cite_note-37] He is a major shareholder in the army-owned [Myanma Economic Holdings Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanma_Economic_Holdings_Limited) (MEHL). During the 2010–11 fiscal year, he had owned 5,000 shares and received an annual dividend of \$250,000 (\~\$349,994 in 2024).[^cite_note-38] He sits on MEHL's Patron Group, which runs the conglomerate.[^cite_note-39]

Min Aung Hlaing's son, [Aung Pyae Sone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aung_Pyae_Sone), owns a number of companies, including Sky One Construction Company and Aung Myint Mo Min Insurance Company.[^cite_note-\:3-40] He also has a majority stake in [Mytel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mytel), a national telecoms carrier.[^cite_note-\:3-40] In 2013, his son Aung Pyae Sone won a no-bid government permit well below market rates, for a 30-year lease on land at the Yangon [People's Square and Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Square_and_Park) for a high-end restaurant and art gallery, following his father's promotion to [Commander-in-Chief](56636725.md).[^cite_note-41] Aung Pyae Sone also runs *A\&M Mahar*, which offers [Food and Drug Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Drug_Administration_\(Myanmar\)) (FDA) approvals and customs clearance services for drugs and medical devices.[^cite_note-\:1-42] Myanmar's customs department is led by Kyaw Htin, a former MEHL director.[^cite_note-\:1-42]

His daughter [Khin Thiri Thet Mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khin_Thiri_Thet_Mon) founded a major film studio, [7th Sense Creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Sense_Creation), in 2017.[^cite_note-\:2-43] That same year, his daughter-in-law, Myo Yadanar Htaik, founded another entertainment company, Stellar Seven Entertainment.[^cite_note-\:2-43] The [US Embassy in Yangon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Yangon) came under media scrutiny in December 2020, for collaborating with 7th Sense Creation, because Min Aung Hlaing is technically subject to [US economic sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_sanctions).[^cite_note-44]

## Commander-in-chief (2011–2026)

### 2011–2015: Union Solidarity and Development Party rule



Min Aung Hlaing meeting with Indian prime minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) in [New Delhi](51585.md) on 29 July 2015

In the lead-up to 2011, the military began embarking on a [series of political reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%932015_Myanmar_political_reforms) to transition Myanmar to a quasi-democracy. The ruling junta, the [State Peace and Development Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Peace_and_Development_Council) (SPDP), engineered its formal departure from power, after holding the [2010 Myanmar general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Myanmar_general_election), which was won by the [Union Solidarity and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Solidarity_and_Development_Party) (USDP), the military's proxy party. On 30 March 2011, outgoing head of state, Senior General [Than Shwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Than_Shwe), then the incumbent [Commander-in-Chief of Myanmar's Armed Forces](56636725.md), appointed Min Aung Hlaing as his successor, ahead of more senior officers.[^cite_note-\:6-45][^cite_note-46] Min Aung Hlaing's appointment coincided with the USDP's rise to power, during which he oversaw a series of military reforms, and supported efforts by the USDP-led government to strike [peace deals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_peace_process) with [ethnic armed organisations](48437766.md).[^cite_note-\:6-45]

Not long after becoming [Commander-in-chief](56636725.md), he removed the Adjutant General, [Lieutenant General](201916.md) Kyaw Phyo and General Officer Commanding of Central Regional Military Command [Major General](36301471.md) Tin Ngwe and built the atmosphere of fear among the top brass.[^cite_note-rector19-32]

In November 2011, according to *[The Irrawaddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Irrawaddy)*, it was "widely believed" that following Min Aung Hlaing's meetings with [Chinese military](66890.md) officials that month and his leadership in creating a bilateral agreement on defense cooperation with the Chinese, he had also held talks with Chinese vice-president [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) regarding cooperation from [China](5405.md) with respect to the [Kachin Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_conflict).[^cite_note-David-36]

On 27 March 2012, during a speech in [Naypyidaw](2270051.md), Min Aung Hlaing defended the military's continued role in [national politics](20390.md).[^cite_note-David-36][^cite_note-47] On 3 April 2012, the [Government of Myanmar](20390.md) announced that Min Aung Hlaing had been promoted to [vice-senior general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-senior_general) (four-star general), the [second highest rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_ranks_of_Myanmar) in the [Myanmar Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Armed_Forces).[^cite_note-David-36] He was promoted to the rank of [senior general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_general_\(Myanmar\)) (five-star general), the highest rank in the Myanmar's Armed Forces in March 2013.[^cite_note-ranksawarded-48]

In 2014, as Min Aung Hlaing approached the age of 60, which is the mandatory age of retirement for military officers, the Armed Forces' Department of Defence Council issued a directive, enabling Min Aung Hlaing to extend his [mandatory retirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_retirement) age to 65, in 2021.[^cite_note-\:7-49]

In August 2015, the USDP fractured, and President [Thein Sein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thein_Sein) purged the faction led by [Shwe Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shwe_Mann), a former general and Speaker of the [Pyithu Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyithu_Hluttaw).[^cite_note-\:6-45] Min Aung Hlaing oversaw a direct military intervention to oust Shwe Mann from power, indicating the military's desire to continue furthering its agenda through USDP.[^cite_note-\:8-50] Shwe Mann had advocated for legislation and constitutional amendments that would have decreased the military's influence, against the interests of the military and USDP.[^cite_note-\:8-50]

### 2016–2020: Transition to National League for Democracy rule



Min Aung Hlaing meeting with Philippine president [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) in [Nay Pyi Taw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nay_Pyi_Taw), [Myanmar](19457.md) on 20 March 2017

The [2015 Myanmar general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Myanmar_general_election) saw the [National League for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_for_Democracy) (NLD), led by [Aung San Suu Kyi](2847.md), win in a landslide. With the transition from an USDP to NLD-led government, Min Aung Hlaing shifted his priorities to recovering state power for the military establishment.[^cite_note-\:6-45] His intransigence and refusal to cooperate with the civilian-led government undermined progress toward [Myanmar's peace process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_peace_process).[^cite_note-\:6-45] As the NLD assumed power, Min Aung Hlaing began intensifying an ongoing [military crackdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide) on the [Rohingya](1918632.md), beginning in October 2016.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] He maintained an adversarial relationship with democratically elected [State Counsellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Counsellor_of_Myanmar) Aung San Suu Kyi, though she defended him against genocide charges.[^cite_note-53] At the [Union Peace Conference - 21st Century Panglong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Peace_Conference_-_21st_Century_Panglong) in August 2020, he warned the NLD against [scapegoating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scapegoating) the military for its role in the [ongoing ethnic conflicts](13299430.md).[^cite_note-\:9-54]



Min Aung Hlaing meeting with Japanese prime minister [Shinzō Abe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinz%C5%8D_Abe) in Tokyo, 4 August 2017

Min Aung Hlaing also began to signal his interest in civilian politics.[^cite_note-\:6-45] He began assuming a more statesman-like persona, and became increasingly assertive about the military's role.[^cite_note-\:9-54][^cite_note-\:5-28] In the lead-up to the [2020 Myanmar general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Myanmar_general_election), he worked with the [USDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDP_\(Burma\)) to position himself as the next [President](11195083.md).[^cite_note-\:9-54] Throughout 2019, Min Aung Hlaing made several public appearances dubbed a "charm offensive," at several religious sites and charity functions,[^cite_note-55] raising speculation about his political ambitions.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-\:7-49] To cultivate his public persona, he began two [Facebook](7529378.md) pages that commanded a combined following of 4.1 million followers.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] In January 2020, Min Aung Hlaing met with Chinese leader [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) in [Nay Pyi Taw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nay_Pyi_Taw). Xi promoted the practical cooperation under the framework of the [One Belt One Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative) to achieve results at an early date and benefit [Myanmar's people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_people).[^cite_note-59] In May 2020, Min Aung Hlaing reshuffled senior military ranks, promoting a new generation of officers loyal to him, including [Kyaw Swar Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaw_Swar_Lin), who became the military's youngest [lieutenant general](201916.md).[^cite_note-60]



Min Aung Hlaing meeting with Russian defense minister [Sergei Shoigu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Shoigu), 17 August 2019

War with the [Arakan Army](38106209.md) intensified during this period, and the military was accused of targeting [Arakanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) civilians and their properties. On 17 March 2019, [Kyaw Zaw Oo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaw_Zaw_Oo), an Arakanese MP, published a bilingual open letter to Min Aung Hlaing about the many human rights violations of the [Tatmadaw](20394.md) in [Rakhine State](493373.md) that harmed the lives and property of civilians and damaged buildings of cultural heritage.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

In February 2020, Min Aung Hlaing, his wife [Kyu Kyu Hla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyu_Kyu_Hla) and with his close astrologer [Vasipake Sayadaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasipake_Sayadaw) placed the "[Hti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hti)" umbrella atop [Bagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagan)'s most powerful ancient [Htilominlo Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Htilominlo_Temple). The meaning of the temple name is "need the royal umbrella, need the King". He was following in the footsteps of some of Myanmar's most powerful political figures including his predecessor, Senior General [Than Shwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Than_Shwe). Many people believed that the ceremony was a [yadaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadaya) and seeking divine blessings for his glory.[^cite_note-63]

In November 2020, Min Aung Hlaing made a series of public comments questioning the legitimacy of the upcoming [2020 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Myanmar_general_election), in potential violation of the Civil Services Personnel Law.[^cite_note-64] On 5 November, the Tatmadaw declared that Min Aung Hlaing's rank is equivalent to [Vice President of Myanmar](30762522.md).[^cite_note-65] After casting his ballot in the 2020 election, Min Aung Hlaing vowed to accept the election results.[^cite_note-66] The 2020 election saw NLD win in a larger landslide than in 2015, forestalling Min Aung Hlaing's political ambitions. Min would have been required to retire as Commander-in-Chief of Defence Services due to a statutory age limit.[^cite_note-67] In response, the military began intensifying allegations of [electoral fraud](382165.md) and irregularities, submitting formal complaints to the [Union Election Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Election_Commission) (UEC).[^cite_note-68]

### Retirement

Min Aung Hlaing retired on 30 March 2026, after [Union Solidarity and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Solidarity_and_Development_Party) MP [Kyaw Kyaw Htay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaw_Kyaw_Htay?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Pyithu Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyithu_Hluttaw) committee put his name forward for the committee's nomination for the [2026 Myanmar presidential election](82700906.md). He was required to retire from the military and thus step down as commander-in-chief to run for president under the constitution. The NDSC appointed [Ye Win Oo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye_Win_Oo), a Min Aung Hlaing loyalist, to succeed him.[^cite_note-\:13-69][^cite_note-\:14-70]

Min Aung Hlaing during the [Tatmadaw](20394.md)'s 76th anniversary day on 27 March 2021

On 27 January 2021, Min Aung Hlaing publicly remarked that he would not rule out a [coup d'état](25914154.md) and abolition of the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Constitution_of_Myanmar), if allegations of [voter fraud](382165.md) during last year's election were not adequately addressed. These comments sparked concern about another potential coup.[^cite_note-71] The following day, the UEC issued a statement rejecting claims of electoral fraud, citing the lack of evidence submitted to substantiate these claims.[^cite_note-72] On 29 January, the military issued clarifying statements pledging to protect and abide by the constitution and applicable laws.[^cite_note-73]

On 1 February 2021, Min Aung Hlaing [detained](148681.md) elected leaders including President [Win Myint](37228839.md), State Counsellor [Aung San Suu Kyi](2847.md) and declared himself as [Commander-in-chief of Myanmar](56636725.md), one day before democratically elected members of parliament were scheduled to be sworn in as members of the [Pyidaungsu Hluttaw](29739324.md) (Assembly of the Union).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] The following day, he established the [State Administration Council](66589972.md) (SAC) as the country's interim ruling body.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

On 22 May 2021, Min Aung Hlaing gave his first interview since the coup to Hong Kong-based Chinese language [Phoenix Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Television). During the interview, he referred to deposed leader Aung San Suu Kyi and he said that she "is in good health. She is at her home and healthy. She is going to face trial at the court in a few days."[^cite_note-79] On the same day, *[Myanmar Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Now)* reported that shortly after the coup, Min Aung Hlaing appointed himself indefinitely as the commander-in-chief and therefore the *[de facto](43594.md)* leader of [Myanmar](19457.md).[^cite_note-80]

Six months after the coup, on 1 August 2021, Min Aung Hlaing formed a [caretaker government](240683.md) and established himself as the country's [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Myanmar).[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] He also remained as the chairman of the SAC until its dissolution.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

### Myanmar civil war

See also: [Myanmar civil war (2021–present)](69807451.md)

With the outbreak of [mass protests against his rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Myanmar_protests), Min Aung Hlaing ordered a clampdown and suppression of demonstrations,[^cite_note-87] sparking an [ongoing civil war](69807451.md).[^cite_note-88]

Min Aung Hlaing's forces have employed [scorched earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorched_earth) tactics in the [civil war](69807451.md), including [airstrikes](323167.md) on civilians.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] He has ordered the execution of prominent pro-democracy activists, the first use of the death penalty in decades.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] After four [pro-democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-democracy) [activists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activism) were executed on 24 July 2022, the chairman of the [ASEAN](106539.md), [Hun Sen](393779.md), UN representatives, and [Western](21208200.md) leaders condemned the executions.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

&#x20;In foreign policy, he has resisted influence from [Association of Southeast Asian Nations](106539.md) (ASEAN) and relied on greater cooperation with [Russia](25391.md), [China](5405.md), and [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] On 7 September 2022, Min Aung Hlaing met with [Russian president](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) on the sidelines of the [Eastern Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Economic_Forum) (EEF), in [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok), Russia, the first time that the pair have met since the [2021 coup](66567997.md).[^cite_note-97] In response to his human rights abuses and corruption, Min Aung Hlaing and his government have been subjected to an extensive series of international sanctions, returning Myanmar to its former status as a [pariah state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pariah_state). The Economist Intelligence Unit's 2022 *[Democracy Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index)* rated Myanmar under Min Aung Hlaing as the second-most authoritarian regime in the world, with only [Afghanistan](737.md) rated less democratic.[^cite_note-98]

### Acting Presidency (2023–2026)

In January 2023, Min Aung Hlaing enacted a new [electoral law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_law) aimed at rigging the [next general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Myanmar_general_election) in favor of the [USDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Solidarity_and_Development_Party).[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] He is himself considered a likely USDP nominee for president in the subsequent presidential election.[^cite_note-101]

Min Aung Hlaing refused to give up his [emergency powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_powers) when they were constitutionally set to expire on 1 February 2023, further delaying new elections.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

In March 2023, Min Aung Hlaing made a rare public appearance at the [Armed Forces Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Day_\(Myanmar\)) parade stating that his government would continue to fight back against [resistance groups](69807451.md) in the country and their "acts of terror". Hlaing called his critics supporters of [terrorism](30636.md).[^cite_note-104]



Min Aung Hlaing with Russian State Duma Chairman [Vyacheslav Volodin](32671558.md), 4 March 2025

Starting in January 2024, multiple pro-military figures condemned Min Aung Hlaing for incompetence and excessive self-interest after the [Tatmadaw](20394.md) suffered an unprecedented string of defeats during [Operation 1027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_1027).[^cite_note-105] In February 2024, to address the Tatmadaw's personnel issues, Min Aung Hlaing activated Myanmar's 1959 [People's Military Service Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Military_Service_Law) for the first time, with plans to draft 60,000 young men and women.[^cite_note-conscription-106][^cite_note-107] Men aged 18–35 and women aged 18–27 will be required to serve up to five years under the [state of emergency](298608.md), or face five years imprisonment.[^cite_note-108]

In March 2024, Min Aung Hlaing claimed at the Armed Forces Day parade young people are being tricked into supporting the resistance against the military, and accused "some powerful nations" of trying to interfere with Myanmar's internal affairs.[^cite_note-109]

On 13 April 2023, Min Aung Hlaing was featured on *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazine's list of the "100 Most Influential People of 2023".[^cite_note-110]

While attending the [Defence Services Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Services_Academy) during [Thingyan](4087333.md), the traditional New Year Water Festival on the evening of 14 April 2024, Min Aung Hlaing escaped a rocket attack by the [Mandalay People's Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDY-PDF) (MDY-PDF).[^cite_note-111]

According to SAC media, resistance groups in Yangon attempted to assassinate Min Aung Hlaing with explosives and firearms in June 2024.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

On 22 July 2024 Min Aung Hlaing became acting president after [Myint Swe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myint_Swe) took medical leave.[^cite_note-114]

Min Aung Hlaing ordered the arrest of [Nay Soe Maung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nay_Soe_Maung), the son-in-law of Senior General [Than Shwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Than_Shwe), on 23 October 2024 in Pyigyitagun Township, [Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay).[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] This arrest occurred during the ongoing challenges to his rule following the coup.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] The groundwork for such actions against opposition figures was laid by Than Shwe, whose enduring influence over the military had previously established its stronghold in Myanmar's political landscape.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]



Min Aung Hlaing with Indian prime minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md), 3 April 2025

In January 2025, [ASEAN](106539.md) upheld its decision to ban Min Aung Hlaing from attending its summits and limit Myanmar's participation to a non-political level.[^cite_note-122]

On 31 January 2025, Myanmar's ruling military extended the state of emergency for another six months, with the decision approved unanimously by the [National Defense and Security Council](47091432.md) (NDSC) under Section 425 of the 2008 Constitution. Min Aung Hlaing, as chairman of the council, stated that maintaining stability remained necessary before elections could be held.[^cite_note-The_Irrawaddy-123][^cite_note-FOX2548_&_WIPROUD-124]

The state of emergency, first declared following the February 2021 military takeover, has been extended multiple times. Under Myanmar's constitutional framework, elections cannot be conducted while a state of emergency is in place. The military has previously announced plans to hold elections in 2025, though the latest extension means they cannot take place until at least the second half of the year.[^cite_note-The_Irrawaddy-123][^cite_note-FOX2548_&_WIPROUD-124]

Min Aung Hlaing made an official visit to [Moscow](19004.md) on 3 March 2025 at [Putin](32817.md)'s invitation, holding talks at the [Grand Kremlin Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Kremlin_Palace) the following day.[^cite_note-125] During this visit, they oversaw the signing of ten memorandums of understanding covering sectors such as nuclear energy, space exploration, trade, education, and investment.[^cite_note-126] An agreement was also reached to build a small-scale nuclear power plant in Myanmar, starting with a capacity of 110 megawatts and potential expansion. Additionally, a memorandum on space exploration and satellite technology led to reports of a satellite imagery analysis center being established in Myanmar with Russian support.[^cite_note-127] Min Aung Hlaing expressed gratitude for Russia's support in international forums and reiterated Myanmar's backing for [Russia's actions in Ukraine](70149799.md). Putin also invited him to attend the [Victory Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Day_\(Russia\)) celebrations in Moscow on 9 May 2025.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

During a meeting with Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) at the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin) on March 4, 2025, Min Aung Hlaing claimed that [the Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buddha) had prophesied Putin's rise to power. According to Min Aung Hlaing, the prophecy foretold that a figure, referred to as the "rat king," would emerge as the emperor of the Russian people after the year 2000 of the [Buddhist calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_calendar) and become a key ally to the kings of [Burma](19457.md). He recounted the story behind the prophecy:[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

> "During the time of our Lord Buddha, when a rat king named Thawma dug up tubers and offered them to the Buddha, the Buddha smiled. When asked why he smiled, the Lord Buddha replied that the rat king Thawma would be reborn as the emperor of the Russian people, one of the 12 Western peoples, during the reign of King Pāpa after the year 2000 of the Buddhist era. He would be unrivaled in the art of weaponry, a unique weapons master emperor, and would become a close and friendly ally of the Burmese kings."

— Min Aung Hlaing

Min Aung Hlaing then connected the prophecy to contemporary geopolitics, stating that the current strategic alliance between [Russia](25391.md) and [Myanmar](19457.md), and that Russia has achieved a world-leading position in weapons and technology. Therefore, the Buddha's prophecy had come true "in a remarkably coincidental and wonderful way". He also presented President Putin with a copy of the book *U Aung Zeya's Biography*, written in 1838 by the Pali scholar U Saw, which contains references to the Rat King.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]



Min Aung Hlaing at the [BIMSTEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIMSTEC) summit in Bangkok, Thailand, 4 April 2025

On 27 March 2025, during the 80th anniversary of [Myanmar Armed Forces Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Day_\(Myanmar\)), Min Aung Hlaing said that a general election would be held by the end of the year, pledging that the military would hand over power to the winning party in a structured and lawful manner.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

Following the [2025 Myanmar earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Myanmar_earthquake) on 28 March, Min Aung Hlaing made a rare invitation for other countries and international organizations to provide relief to Myanmar.[^cite_note-137] On 3 April, he visited Thailand to attend the [BIMSTEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIMSTEC) summit in [Bangkok](56615.md).[^cite_note-138]

On 20 March 2026, Min Aung Hlaing underwent surgery at the No. 2 Military Hospital in Naypyidaw due to lumbar spondylosis with spinal stenosis. He publicly appeared during a 24 March meeting where he urged [Thingyan](4087333.md) festivals to continue despite fuel shortages.[^cite_note-139]

## Presidency (2026–present)

Main article: [Second Min Aung Hlaing cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Min_Aung_Hlaing_cabinet)

On 30 March 2026, he was nominated for the vice presidency by the Electoral College of the [Pyithu Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyithu_Hluttaw) by the [Union Solidarity and Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Solidarity_and_Development_Party) MP [Kyaw Kyaw Htay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaw_Kyaw_Htay?action=edit\&redlink=1) for the committee's nomination for the [2026 Myanmar presidential election](82700906.md). As he was required to retire from the military, he stepped down as commander-in-chief on the same day to run for president under the constitution. The NDSC appointed [Ye Win Oo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye_Win_Oo), a Min Aung Hlaing loyalist, to succeed him.[^cite_note-\:13-69][^cite_note-\:14-70] On 3 April, Min Aung Hlaing was elected the country's 11th president with 429 of 584 votes, while [Nyo Saw](72867108.md) of the Tatmadaw and [Nan Ni Ni Aye](58423397.md) of the [Amyotha Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amyotha_Hluttaw) were elected vice presidents.[^cite_note-140] On April 10, 2026, Min Aung Hlaing took the official oath of office at the Pyidaungsu Hluttaw.[^cite_note-141] That same day, he established a new [Union Government of Myanmar](32796180.md), appointing many individuals who had served in his previous ministries.[^cite_note-142]

From 30 May to 3 June, Min embarked on a visit to India, his first trip overseas after being inaugurated as president. During the visit, he met with Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md), President [Droupadi Murmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droupadi_Murmu) and other top officials.[^cite_note-143] From 15 to 19 June, he visited China, his second trip overseas. During his visit, he met with Chinese leader [Xi Jinping](2017814.md), Premier [Li Qiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Qiang) and National People's Congress Standing Committee Chairman [Zhao Leji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Leji). He also visited [Shanghai](27643.md), where he attended the Myanmar‑China Investment and Trade Networking Summit.[^cite_note-144]

## War crimes

Further information: [War crimes in the Myanmar civil war (2021–present)](75969577.md) and [Rohingya genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide)



Destroyed village in [Rakhine State](493373.md), September 2017

The [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md) (UNHRC) reported that Min Aung Hlaing's soldiers have been deliberately targeting civilians in northern states of Myanmar and have been committing systemic discrimination and human rights violations against [minority communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_Myanmar) in [Rakhine State](493373.md).[^cite_note-145] In particular, he has been accused of [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) against the [Rohingya people](1918632.md).[^cite_note-146] These human rights violations could amount to [genocide](12441.md), [crimes against humanity](61099.md), and [war crimes](48626.md).[^cite_note-147]

In 2018, the United Nations [Independent International Fact-Finding Mission](15911381.md) on Myanmar, led by [Marzuki Darusman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marzuki_Darusman), determined that Min Aung Hlaing and other [Myanmar's military generals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Burmese_generals) oversaw atrocities against the [Rohingya](1918632.md) in [Rakhine](493373.md), [Kachin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_state) and [Shan states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_state), and did so with [genocidal intent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocidal_intent).[^cite_note-UNReport-148] The UN investigative panel said that Min Aung Hlaing, along with four other commanders ([Soe Win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soe_Win_\(general\)), Aung Kyaw Zaw, [Maung Maung Soe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maung_Maung_Soe_\(general\)), and Than Oo) should be tried for war crimes and crimes against humanity (including genocide) in the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) (ICC) or an [ad hoc international tribunal](433437.md).[^cite_note-UNReport-148]

[Facebook](7529378.md) banned Min Aung Hlaing from its platform along with 19 other top Burmese officials and organisations to prevent further heated ethnic and religious tensions in Myanmar. This action followed a UN investigation's report that certain military leaders in Myanmar should be investigated and prosecuted for genocide against [Rohingya Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_Muslims).[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] [Twitter](9988187.md) later banned him on 16 May 2019.[^cite_note-151]

The United States has [imposed sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_sanctions) against Min Aung Hlaing. In July 2019, the [U.S. government](195149.md) banned him from travel to the US.[^cite_note-\:0-152] In December 2020, it froze Min Aung Hlaing's American-based assets and criminalized financial transactions between him and anyone in the US.[^cite_note-\:0-152][^cite_note-153]

### Arrest warrant request

Main article: [International Criminal Court investigation in Bangladesh/Myanmar § Cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Bangladesh/Myanmar#Cases)

In November 2024, as part of the [International Criminal Court investigation in Bangladesh/Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Bangladesh/Myanmar), the [prosecutor of the ICC](33903993.md), [Karim Ahmad Khan](11606957.md), requested an arrest warrant for Min Aung Hlaing, stating that there were reasonable grounds to believe that Hlaing was responsible for crimes against humanity for his role in the genocide.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-BBC_ICC_arrest_warrant_request_MM-155][^cite_note-156]

On 14 February 2025, a court in Argentina, acting on a petition from the Burmese Rohingya Organisation UK and citing the principle of [universal jurisdiction](48640.md), issued arrest warrants against Min Aung Hlaing and other senior Myanmar officials on charges of "[genocide and crimes against humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide)" against the [Rohingyas](1918632.md).[^cite_note-157] In response, Myanmar military spokesperson Major General [Zaw Min Tun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaw_Min_Tun_\(general\)) rejected the ruling, calling it "baseless" and accusing Argentina's judiciary of political interference. He defended Min Aung Hlaing, asserting that the accusations were politically motivated and lacked legitimacy.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]

## Sanctions

The [U.S. Department of the Treasury](53667.md) has imposed [sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions) on Min Aung Hlaing since 10 December 2019, pursuant to Executive Order 13818, which builds upon and implements the [Global Magnitsky Human Rights Accountability Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_Act) and targets perpetrators of serious human rights abuse and corruption. He has committed serious human rights abuses against members of [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_Myanmar) across Myanmar. The [US sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_sanctions) include a [freezing of assets](5847461.md) under the US and a ban on transactions with any [US person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_person).[^cite_note-160] In 2022, he was also placed on the sanctions list of the [OFAC](231045.md) pursuant to Executive Order 14014, in response to the 2–21 coup.[^cite_note-161] OFAC also sanctioned some of the companies that Hlaing and/or his associates owned or controlled, including the [MEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanma_Economic_Holdings_Limited) conglomerate headed by Hlaing.[^cite_note-treasury_sanctions_2021_03_25_treasury_gov-162]

The [Government of Canada](616255.md) has imposed sanctions on him since 18 February 2021, pursuant to Special Economic Measures Act and Special Economic Measures (Burma) Regulations, in response to the gravity of the human rights and humanitarian situation in Myanmar. Canadian sanctions include a freezing of assets and a ban on transactions with any Canadian person.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164]

[HM Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Treasury) and the [Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office](59653.md) of the [United Kingdom](31717.md) have imposed sanctions on him since 25 February 2021, for his responsibility for serious human rights violations in Myanmar. The [UK sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_sanctions) include a freezing of assets under the UK and travel ban to the UK.[^cite_note-165]

The [Council of the European Union](5866.md) has also imposed [sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_Burma) on him since 22 March 2021, pursuant to Council Regulation (EU) 2021/479 and Council Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/480 which amended Council Regulation (EU) No 401/2013, for his responsibility for the 2021 coup and the subsequent military and police repression against [peaceful demonstrators](66612294.md). The [EU sanctions](74746546.md) include a freezing of assets and a ban on entry or transit to the bloc.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167]

## Awards and decorations

### Domestic honors

On 17 April 2022, Min Aung Hlaing gave himself Myanmar's two highest titles; the *[Thadoe Thiri Thudhamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Union_of_Burma)* ([The Most Glorious Order of Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiri_Thudhamma_Thingaha)) and the *Thadoe Maha Thray Sithu* ([the Order of the Union of Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Tharay_Sithu)).[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174]

On 7 October 2019, the [Young Men's Buddhist Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Men's_Buddhist_Association_\(Burma\)) (YMBA) awarded him the title of *Mingaladhamma Zawtika Dhaza* and the permanent patron of the YMBA.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176] On 9 December 2020, YMBA awarded him the title of *Thado Thiri Agga Maha Mingalar Zawtika*.[^cite_note-177]



[Thai delegation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Thailand) with members of the [State Peace and Development Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Peace_and_Development_Council) (SPDC), including Min Aung Hlaing (third from the right), [Senior General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_general_\(Myanmar\)) [Than Shwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Than_Shwe) (first from the right), [Vice-Senior General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_senior_general) [Maung Aye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maung_Aye) (second from the left), [Thura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_names) [Shwe Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shwe_Mann) (to the left of the middle), and [Thein Sein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thein_Sein) (first from the left), at the Zeyathiri Beikman, [Naypyidaw](2270051.md) on 11 October 2010.

### Academic titles

On 23 June 2020, the Federal Service for Military-Technical Cooperation (FSMTC) awarded an honorary medal to Min Aung Hlaing.[^cite_note-rus-178]

On 20 August 2020, the [Military University of the Ministry of Defense of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_University_of_the_Ministry_of_Defense_of_the_Russian_Federation) honoured Min Aung Hlaing the title of Honorary Professor.[^cite_note-rus-178]

On September 4, 2020, the [Institute of Oriental Studies of the Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Oriental_Studies_of_the_Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) conferred Min Aung Hlaing an honorary doctorate for his performance in strengthening the Russia–Myanmar strategic partnership.[^cite_note-rus-178][^cite_note-179]

On February 5, 2026, the [University of Yangon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Yangon) awarded Min Aung Hlaing an Honorary Doctor of Public Administration degree for his efforts in maintaining stability, economic recovery, and education reform.[^cite_note-180]
