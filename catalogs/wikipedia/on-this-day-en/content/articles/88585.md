**Fujian**[^cite_note-8] is a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) in [southeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_China). Fujian is bordered by [Zhejiang](96602.md) to the north, [Jiangxi](173817.md) to the west, [Guangdong](65109.md) to the south, and the [Taiwan Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Strait) to the east. Its capital is [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou) and its largest prefecture city by population is [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou), with other notable cities including the port city of [Xiamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen) and [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou). Fujian is located on the west coast of the Taiwan Strait as the closest province geographically and culturally to [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan). Additionally, a small portion of historical Fujian is administered by the [government of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_China) ([Taiwan](25734.md)), romanized as [Fuchien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuchien_\(Republic_of_China_province\)), a result of the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War).

While the population predominantly identifies as [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), it is one of China's most culturally and linguistically diverse provinces. The dialects of the language group [Min Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Chinese) are most commonly spoken within the province, including the [Fuzhou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_dialect) and other [Eastern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Min) languages of Northeastern Fujian and [Hokkien dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien_dialect) and other [Southern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Min) languages of southeastern Fujian. The capital city of [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou) and Fu'an of [Ningde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningde) prefecture along with [Cangnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cangnan_County) county-level city of [Wenzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzhou) prefecture in [Zhejiang](96602.md) province make up the Min Dong linguistic and cultural region of Northeastern Fujian. [Hakka Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese) is also spoken in Fujian, by the [Hakka people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_people). Min dialects, Hakka, and [Standard Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese) are mutually unintelligible. Due to emigration, much of the ethnic Chinese populations of Taiwan, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Philippines speak Southern Min (or Hokkien).

With a population of 41.5 million, Fujian ranks [15th in population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_population) among Chinese provinces. In 2022, its GDP reached CN¥5.31 trillion (US\$790 billion by nominal GDP), ranking 4th in [East China](683693.md) region and [8th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) nationwide in GDP.[^cite_note-data2022-9] Fujian's GDP per capita is above the national average, at CN¥126,829 (US\$18,856 in nominal), the second highest [GDP per capita of all Chinese provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita) after [Jiangsu](166412.md).[^cite_note-data2022-9]

Fujian is considered one of China's leading provinces in education and research. As of 2023, two major cities in the province ranked in the top 45 cities in the world (Xiamen 38th and Fuzhou 45th) by scientific research output, as tracked by the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index).[^cite_note-\:0-10]

## Name

The name *Fujian* (福建) originated from the combination of the city names of [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou) (福州) and nearby [Jianzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jianzhou_\(Fujian\)) (建州, or present-day [Nanping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanping) (南平)). The province's name of *Fujian* means "happy establishment".[^cite_note-11]

One of the [Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters) in the province [*_fu_*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_\(character\)) ([福](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%A6%8F#Chinese); *fú* [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/12/Zh-f%C3%BA.ogg/Zh-f%C3%BA.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/12/Zh-f%C3%BA.ogg/Zh-f%C3%BA.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), meaning 'fortune' or 'good luck', is represented both as a Chinese [ideograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideograph) and, at times, [pictorially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictorial), in one of its [homophonous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophone) forms. It is often found on a figurine of the male god of the same name, one of the [trio of "star gods"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanxing_\(deities\)) *Fú*, *Lù*, and *Shòu*. The second character, *_jian_* ([建](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%BB%BA#Chinese); *jiàn*) is translated as 'establish' and 'to establish'.

Fujian is historically rendered in [postal romanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_postal_romanization) and [Wade-Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade-Giles) as **Fukien** or **Fuchien** for much of the 20th century.[^cite_note-12] The name of this province adopted its current [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin) name of "Fujian" in 1958 by the PRC authorities; after the United States government normalized relations with the PRC in 1979, the [International Organization for Standardization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Organization_for_Standardization) adopted pinyin in 1982, followed by the [United Nations](31769.md) in 1986 with several Western media outlets (such as the [Associated Press](18935732.md)) adopted pinyin as the years went by. The [Government of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_China) on [Taiwan](25734.md) (who adopted pinyin in 2009) continues to use the older transliterations of Fujian for its [own province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuchien_Province,_Republic_of_China).

### Prehistoric Fujian

Recent archaeological discoveries in 2011 demonstrate that Fujian had entered the [Neolithic Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Age) by the middle of the 6th millennium BC.[^cite_note-13] From the Keqiutou site (7450–5590 BP), an early Neolithic site in [Pingtan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingtan_Island) located about 70 kilometres (43 mi) southeast of Fuzhou, numerous tools made of stones, [shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoskeleton), [bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone), [jades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade), and [ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic) (including wheel-made ceramics) have been unearthed, together with [spinning wheels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_wheel), which is definitive evidence of [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving).

The Tanshishan (曇石山) site (5500–4000 BP) in suburban Fuzhou spans the Neolithic and [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) Age where semi-underground circular buildings were found in the lower level. The Huangtulun (黃土崙) site (c. 1325 BC), also in suburban Fuzhou, was of the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) in character.

Tianlong Jiao (2013)[^cite_note-Jiao2013-14] notes that the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) appeared on the coast of Fujian around 6,000 B.P. During the Neolithic, the coast of Fujian had a low population density, with the population depending on mostly on fishing and hunting, along with limited agriculture.

There were four major Neolithic cultures in coastal Fujian, with the earliest Neolithic cultures originating from the north in coastal [Zhejiang](96602.md).[^cite_note-Jiao2013-14]

- Keqiutou culture (壳丘头文化; c. 6000 – c. 5500 BP, or c. 4050 – c. 3550 BC)

- Tanshishan culture (昙石山文化; c. 5000 – c. 4300 BP, or c. 3050 – c. 2350 BC)

- Damaoshan culture (大帽山文化; c. 5000 – c. 4300 BP)

- Huangguashan culture (黄瓜山文化; c. 4300 – c. 3500 BP, or c. 2350 – c. 1550 BC)

There were two major Neolithic cultures in inland Fujian, which were highly distinct from the coastal Fujian Neolithic cultures.[^cite_note-Jiao2013-14] These are the Niubishan culture (牛鼻山文化) from 5000 to 4000 years ago, and the Hulushan culture (葫芦山文化) from 2050 to 1550 BC.

### Minyue kingdom

Main article: [Minyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minyue)



Map of [Minyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minyue)

Fujian was also where the kingdom of [Minyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minyue) was located. The word "Mǐnyuè" was derived by combining "Mǐn" ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 闽; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 閩; [Pe̍h-ōe-jī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%CC%8Dh-%C5%8De-j%C4%AB): *bân*), which is perhaps an ethnic name (simplified Chinese: 蛮; traditional Chinese: 蠻; pinyin: *mán*; Pe̍h-ōe-jī: *bân*), and "[Yuè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiyue)", after the [State of Yue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Yue), a [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period) kingdom in [Zhejiang](96602.md) to the north. This is because the royal family of Yuè fled to Fujian after its kingdom was annexed by the [State of Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Chu) in 306 BC. Mǐn is also the name of the [main river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_River_\(Fujian\)) in this area, but the ethnonym is probably older.

### Qin dynasty

The Qin deposed the King of Minyue, establishing instead a paramilitary province there called Minzhong Commandery. Minyue was a *de facto* kingdom until one of the emperors of the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty), the first unified imperial Chinese state, abolished its status.[^cite_note-Britannica-15]

### Han dynasty

See also: [Han campaigns against Minyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_campaigns_against_Minyue)

In the aftermath of the Qin dynasty's fall, [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu%E2%80%93Han_Contention) broke out between two warlords, [Xiang Yu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_Yu) and [Liu Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Gaozu_of_Han). The Minyue king Wuzhu sent his troops to fight with Liu and his gamble paid off. Liu was victorious and founded the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty). In 202 BC, he restored Minyue's status as a [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary_state) independent kingdom. Thus Wuzhu was allowed to construct his fortified city in Fuzhou as well as a few locations in the [Wuyi Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyi_Mountains), which have been excavated in recent years. His kingdom extended beyond the borders of contemporary Fujian into eastern Guangdong, eastern [Jiangxi](173817.md), and southern [Zhejiang](96602.md).[^cite_note-16]

After Wuzhu's death, Minyue maintained its militant tradition and launched several expeditions against its neighboring kingdoms in [Guangdong](65109.md), [Jiangxi](173817.md), and [Zhejiang](96602.md), primarily in the 2nd century BC. This was stopped by the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) as it [expanded southward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southward_expansion_of_the_Han_dynasty). The Han emperor eventually decided to get rid of the potential threat by launching a [military campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_campaigns_against_Minyue) against Minyue. Large forces approached Minyue simultaneously from four directions via land and sea in 111 BC. The rulers in Fuzhou surrendered to avoid a futile fight and destruction and the first kingdom in Fujian history came to an abrupt end.

Fujian was part of the much larger [Yang Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Province) (Yangzhou), whose provincial capital was designated in Liyang (歷陽; present-day [He County, Anhui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_County,_Anhui?action=edit\&redlink=1)).

The [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) collapsed at the end of the 2nd century AD, paving the way for the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) era. [Sun Quan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Quan), the founder of the [Kingdom of Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Wu), spent nearly 20 years subduing the Shan Yue people, the branch of the Yue living in mountains.

### Jin era

The first wave of [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_of_the_eight_clans) of the noble class arrived in the province in the early 4th century when the [Western Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Jin_dynasty) collapsed and the north was torn apart by [civil wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Eight_Princes) and [rebellions by tribal peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upheaval_of_the_Five_Barbarians) from the north and west. These immigrants were primarily from eight families in [central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China): [Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_\(surname\)) ([陈](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%99%88#Simplified_Chinese)), [Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_\(surname\)) ([林](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%9E%97#Chinese)), [Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huang_\(surname\)) ([黄](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%BB%84#Simplified_Chinese)), [Zheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_\(surname\)) ([郑](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%83%91#Simplified_Chinese)), [Zhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhan_\(surname\)) ([詹](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%A9%B9#Chinese)), [Qiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi%C5%AB_\(surname\)) ([邱](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%82%B1#Chinese)), [He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_\(surname\)) ([何](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E4%BD%95#Chinese)), and [Hu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_\(surname\)) ([胡](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%83%A1#Chinese)). To this day, the first four remain the most popular surnames in Fujian.

Nevertheless, isolation from nearby areas owing to rugged terrain contributed to Fujian's relatively undeveloped economy and level of development, despite major population boosts from northern China during the "barbarian" rebellions. The population density in Fujian remained low compared to the rest of China. Only two [commanderies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandery_\(China\)) and sixteen counties were established by the Western Jin dynasty. Like other southern provinces such as [Guangdong](65109.md), [Guangxi](47836950.md), [Guizhou](173815.md), and [Yunnan](166410.md), Fujian often served as a destination for exiled prisoners and dissidents at that time.

During the [Southern and Northern Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_and_Northern_Dynasties) era, the [Southern Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Dynasties) ([Liu Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Song_dynasty), [Southern Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Qi), [Liang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liang_dynasty) ([Western Liang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Liang_\(555%E2%80%93587\))), and [Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_dynasty)) reigned south of the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River), including Fujian.

### Sui and Tang dynasties

See also: [Early western influence in Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_western_influence_in_Fujian)

During the [Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty) and [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) eras a large influx of migrants settled in Fujian.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Britannica-15]

During the Sui dynasty, Fujian was again part of [Yang Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Province).

During the Tang, Fujian was part of the larger [Jiangnan East Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangnan_East_Circuit), whose capital was at [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou,_Jiangsu). Modern-day Fujian was composed of around 5 prefectures and 25 counties.

The Tang dynasty (618–907) oversaw the next golden age of China, which contributed to a boom in Fujian's culture and economy. Fuzhou's economic and cultural institutions grew and developed. The later years of the Tang dynasty saw several political upheavals in the Chinese heartland, prompting even larger waves of northerners to immigrate to the northern part of Fujian.

### Five Dynasties Ten Kingdoms



Situation of Fujian in 957

As the Tang dynasty ended, China was torn apart in the period of the [Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms). During this time, a second major wave of immigration arrived in the safe haven of Fujian, led by Wang Brothers ([Wang Chao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Chao_\(Tang_dynasty\)), [Wang Shengui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Shengui?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Wang Shenzhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Shenzhi)), who set up an independent [Kingdom of Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_\(Ten_Kingdoms\)) with its capital in Fuzhou. After the death of the founding king, however, the kingdom suffered from internal strife, and was soon absorbed by [Southern Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Tang), another southern kingdom.[^cite_note-18]

Parts of northern Fujian were conquered by the [Wuyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyue) Kingdom to the north as well, including the Min capital Fuzhou.

[Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou) city was blooming into a seaport under the reign of the [Min Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_\(Ten_Kingdoms\)).[^cite_note-《伊本・白图泰游记》-19][^cite_note-20]

*[Qingyuan Jiedushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingyuan_Jiedushi)* was a military/governance office created in 949 by [Southern Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Tang)'s second emperor [Li Jing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Jing_\(Southern_Tang\)) for the warlord [Liu Congxiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Congxiao), who nominally submitted to him but controlled Quan (泉州, in modern [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou), Fujian) and Zhang (漳州, in modern [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou), Fujian) Prefectures in *de facto* independence from the Southern Tang state.[^cite_note-ZZTJ288-21] (Zhang Prefecture was, at times during the circuit's existence, also known as Nan Prefecture (南州).)[^cite_note-HS483-22] Starting in 960, in addition to being nominally submissive to Southern Tang, Qingyuan Circuit was also nominally submissive to [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty), which had itself become Southern Tang's nominal overlord.[^cite_note-XZZTJ1-23]



Map showing the location of Qingyuan Circuit

After Liu's death, the circuit was briefly ruled by his biological nephew/adoptive son [Liu Shaozi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Shaozi), who was then overthrown by the officers [Zhang Hansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Hansi) and [Chen Hongjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Hongjin). Zhang then ruled the circuit briefly, before Chen deposed him and took over.[^cite_note-HS483-22] In 978, with Song's determination to unify Chinese lands in full order, Chen decided that he could not stay *de facto* independent, and offered the control of the circuit to Song's [Emperor Taizong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizong_of_Song), ending Qingyuan Circuit as a *de facto* independent entity.[^cite_note-XZZTJ9-24]

### Song dynasty

The area was reorganized into the [Fujian Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_Circuit) in 985, which was the first time the name "Fujian" was used for an administrative region.

### Vietnam

Many Chinese migrated from Fujian's major ports to Vietnam's [Red River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_River_Delta). The settlers then created Trần port and [Vân Đồn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Don).[^cite_note-WernerWhitmore2012-25] Fujian and Guangdong Chinese moved to the Vân Đồn coastal port to engage in commerce.[^cite_note-Truong2007-26]

During the [Lý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BD_dynasty) and [Trần dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%E1%BA%A7n_dynasty), many Chinese ethnic groups with the surname Trần (陳) migrated to Vietnam from what is now Fujian or Guangxi. They settled along the coast of Vietnam and the capital's southeastern area.[^cite_note-27] The Vietnamese Trần clan traces their ancestry to Trần Tự Minh (227 BC). He was a Qin General during the Warring state period who belonged to the indigenous Mân, a Baiyue ethnic group of Southern China and Northern Vietnam. Tự Minh also served under King [An Dương Vương](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_D%C6%B0%C6%A1ng_V%C6%B0%C6%A1ng) of [Âu Lạc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%82u_L%E1%BA%A1c) kingdom in resisting [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_\(state\))'s conquest of Âu Lạc. Their genealogy also included Trần Tự Viễn (582 – 637) of Giao Châu and Trần Tự An (1010 – 1077) of Đại Việt. Near the end of the 11th century the descendants of a fisherman named Trần Kinh, whose hometown was in Tức Mạc village in Đại Việt (Modern day Vietnam), would marry the royal Lý clan, which was then founded the Vietnam Tran dynasty in 1225.[^cite_note-EmbreeLewis1988-28]

In Vietnam, the Trần served as officials. The surnames are found in the Trần and Lý dynasty Imperial exam records.[^cite_note-Woodside1971-29] Chinese ethnic groups are recorded in Trần and Lý dynasty records of officials.[^cite_note-Gunn2011-30] Clothing, food, and languages were fused with the local Vietnamese in [Vân Đồn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A2n_%C4%90%E1%BB%93n_District) district where the Chinese ethnic groups had moved after leaving their home province of what is now Fujian, Guangxi, and Guangdong.

In 1172, Fujian was attacked by [Pi-she-ye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi-she-ye) pirates from [Taiwan](25734.md) or the [Visayas](72418.md), Philippines.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]<sup>: 165–166 </sup>

### Yuan dynasty

After the establishment of the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), Fujian became part of [Jiangzhe province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangzhe_province), whose capital was at [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou). From 1357 to 1366 Muslims in [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou) participated in the [Ispah Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ispah_Rebellion), advancing northward and even capturing Putian and Fuzhou before the rebellion was crushed by the Yuan. Afterward, Quanzhou city lost foreign interest in trading and its formerly welcoming international image as the foreigners were all massacred or deported.

Yuan dynasty General [Chen Youding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Youding), who had put down the Ispah Rebellion, continued to rule over the Fujian area even after the outbreak of the [Red Turban Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Turban_Rebellion). Forces loyal to the eventual Ming dynasty founder [Zhu Yuanzhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Yuanzhang) (Hongwu Emperor) defeated Chen in 1367.[^cite_note-34]

### Ming dynasty

After the establishment of the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), Fujian became a province, with its capital at Fuzhou. In the early Ming era, Fuzhou [Changle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changle,_Fuzhou) was the staging area and supply depot of [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He)'s [naval expeditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_voyages). Further development was severely hampered by the [sea trade ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hai_jin), and the area was superseded by nearby ports of [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou), [Ningbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningbo) and [Shanghai](27643.md) despite the lifting of the ban in 1550. Large-scale piracy by [Wokou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wokou) was eventually wiped out by the Chinese military.

An account of the Ming dynasty Fujian was written by No In (Lu Ren 鲁认).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

The [Pisheya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iloilo#History) appear in Quanzhou Ming era records.[^cite_note-37]

### Qing dynasty

The late Ming and early [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) symbolized an era of a large influx of refugees and another 20 years of sea trade ban under the [Kangxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor), a measure intended to counter the [refuge Ming government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Tungning) of [Koxinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koxinga) in the [island of Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_of_Taiwan).

The sea ban implemented by the Qing forced many people to evacuate the coast to deprive Koxinga's Ming loyalists of resources. This has led to the myth that it was because Manchus were "afraid of water".

Incoming refugees did not translate into a major labor force, owing to their re-migration into prosperous regions of [Guangdong](65109.md). In 1683, the Qing dynasty conquered Taiwan in the [Battle of Penghu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Penghu) and annexed it into Fujian, as the [Taiwan Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Prefecture). Many more [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) then settled in Taiwan. Today, most Taiwanese are descendants of [Hokkien people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien_people) from Southern Fujian. Fujian and Taiwan were originally treated as one province ([Fujian-Taiwan-Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian-Taiwan-Province)), but starting in 1885, they split into two separate provinces.[^cite_note-38]

In the 1890s, the Qing ceded Taiwan to Japan via the [Treaty of Shimonoseki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Shimonoseki) after the [First Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sino-Japanese_War). In 1905–1907 Japan made overtures to enlarge its sphere of influence to include Fujian. Japan was trying to obtain French loans and also avoid the [Open Door Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Door_Policy). Paris provided loans on condition that Japan respected the Open Door principles and didn't violate China's territorial integrity.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### Republic of China



Map of the [Constitution Protection Region of Southern Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Protection_Region_of_Southern_Fujian)

See also: [Constitution Protection Region of Southern Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Protection_Region_of_Southern_Fujian); [Fujian People's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_People's_Government); and [Fuchien Province, Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuchien_Province,_Republic_of_China)

The [Xinhai revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_revolution) overthrew the Qing dynasty and brought the province into the rule of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)).

The [anarchist](12.md) [Constitution Protection Region of Southern Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Protection_Region_of_Southern_Fujian) was established by [Chen Jiongming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Jiongming) from 1918 to 1920.

Fujian briefly established the independent [Fujian People's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_People's_Government) in 1933. It was re-controlled by the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) in 1934.

Fujian came under a Japanese sea blockade during [World War II](32927.md).

## People's Republic of China



Historical location of Fujian in the [Republican era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\))

After the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) unified the country and took over most of Fujian, excluding the [Quemoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinmen) and [Matsu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsu_Island).

In its early days, Fujian's development was relatively slow in comparison to other coastal provinces due to potential conflicts with Kuomintang-controlled Taiwan. Today, the province has the highest forest coverage rate while enjoying a high growth rate in the economy. The GDP per capita in Fujian is ranked 4-6th place among provinces of China in recent years.

Development has been accompanied by a large influx of population from the overpopulated areas to Fujian's north and west, and much of the farmland and forest, as well as cultural heritage sites such as the temples of king Wuzhu, have given way to ubiquitous high-rise buildings. Fujian faces challenges to sustain development while at the same time preserving Fujian's natural and cultural heritage.

In 2023, the [Central Committee of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) and the [State Council of China](44498.md) jointly proposed making Fujian a [demonstration zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_Demonstration_Zone) in [cross-strait](2596399.md) integration between Taiwan and mainland China. Under the plan, the Chinese government would boost economic and transportation cooperation with Taiwan and make it easier for Taiwanese people to live, buy property, access social services and study in Fujian.[^cite_note-41]

## Geography



[Wuyi Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyi_Mountains)



[Min River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_River_\(Fujian\)) in [Nanping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanping)

The province is mostly mountainous and is traditionally said to be "eight parts mountain, one part water, and one part farmland" (八山一水一分田). The northwest is higher in altitude, with the [Wuyi Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyi_Mountains) forming the border between Fujian and [Jiangxi](173817.md). It is the most forested provincial-level administrative region in China, with a 62.96% forest coverage rate in 2009.[^cite_note-42] Fujian's highest point is [Mount Huanggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Huanggang) in the Wuyi Mountains, with an altitude of 2,157 metres (1.340 mi).

Fujian faces [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea) to the east, [South China Sea](74209.md) to the south, and the [Taiwan Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Strait) to the southeast. The coastline is rugged and has many bays and islands. Major islands include [Quemoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quemoy) (also known as Kinmen, controlled by the Republic of China), [Haitan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitan_Island), and [Nanri Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanri_Island). [Meizhou Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meizhou_Island) occupies a central place in the cult of the goddess [Matsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazu_\(goddess\)), the patron deity of Chinese sailors.

The [Min River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_River_\(Fujian\)) and its tributaries cut through much of northern and central Fujian. Other rivers include the [Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_River_\(Fujian\)) and the [Jiulong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiulong_River). Due to its uneven topography, Fujian has many cliffs and rapids.

Fujian is separated from Taiwan by the 180 kilometres (110 mi)-wide Taiwan Strait. Some of the small islands in the Taiwan Strait are also part of the province. The islands of [Kinmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinmen) and [Matsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsu_Islands) are under the administration of the Republic of China.

Fujian contains several [faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)), the result of a collision between the [Asiatic Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate) and the [Philippine Sea Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea_Plate). The Changle-Naoao and Longan-Jinjiang fault zones in this area have annual displacement rates of 3–5 mm. They could cause major earthquakes in the future.[^cite_note-43]



Xiamen in Southeastern Fujian

Fujian has a [subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate), with mild winters. In January, the coastal regions average around 7–10 °C (45–50 °F) while the hills average 6–8 °C (43–46 °F). In the summer, temperatures are high, and the province is threatened by [typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon) coming in from the [Pacific](23070.md). Average annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) is 1,400–2,000 millimetres (55–79 in).

## Transportation

### Roads



Kuiqi interchange in Fuzhou

As of 2012, there are 54,876 kilometres (34,098 miles) of highways in Fujian, including 3,500 kilometres (2,200 miles) of expressways. The top infrastructure projects in recent years have been the Zhangzhou-Zhaoan Expressway (US\$624 million) and the Sanmingshi-Fuzhou expressway (US\$1.40 billion). The [12th Five-Year Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-year_plans_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), covering the period from 2011 to 2015, aims to double the length of the province's expressways to 5,500 kilometres (3,400 mi).[^cite_note-China_Briefing_Business_Guide:_North_East_China-44]

### Railways



Fuzhou train station

Due to Fujian's mountainous terrain and traditional reliance on maritime transportation, railways came to the province comparatively late. The first rail links to neighboring [Jiangxi](173817.md), [Guangdong](65109.md), and [Zhejiang](96602.md) Province, opened respectively, in 1959, 2000, and 2009. As of October 2013, Fujian has four rail links with Jiangxi to the northwest: the [Yingtan–Xiamen Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yingtan%E2%80%93Xiamen_Railway) (opened 1957), the [Hengfeng–Nanping Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengfeng%E2%80%93Nanping_Railway) (1998), [Ganzhou–Longyan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzhou%E2%80%93Longyan_Railway) (2005) and the high-speed [Xiangtang–Putian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangtang%E2%80%93Putian_Railway) (2013). Fujian's rail link to Guangdong to the west, the [Zhangping–Longchuan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangping%E2%80%93Longchuan_Railway) (2000), was joined with the high-speed [Xiamen–Shenzhen Railway (Xiashen Line)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen%E2%80%93Shenzhen_Railway) in late 2013. The Xiashen Line forms the southernmost section of China's [Southeast Coast High-Speed Rail Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Coast_High-Speed_Rail_Corridor). The [Wenzhou–Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzhou%E2%80%93Fuzhou_Railway) and [Fuzhou–Xiamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou%E2%80%93Xiamen_Railway) sections of this corridor entered operation in 2009 and link Fujian with Zhejiang with trains running at speeds of up to 250 km/h (155 mph).

Within Fujian, coastal and interior cities are linked by the [Nanping–Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanping%E2%80%93Fuzhou_Railway) (1959), [Zhangping–Quanzhou–Xiaocuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangping%E2%80%93Quanzhou%E2%80%93Xiaocuo_Railway) (2007) and [Longyan–Xiamen Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longyan%E2%80%93Xiamen_Railway), (2012). To attract Taiwanese investment, the province intends to increase its rail length by 50 percent to 2,500 km (1,553 mi).[^cite_note-China_Expat_City_Guide:_Dandong-45]

### Air

The major airports are [Fuzhou Changle International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_Changle_International_Airport), [Xiamen Gaoqi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen_Gaoqi_International_Airport), [Quanzhou Jinjiang International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou_Jinjiang_International_Airport), [Nanping Wuyishan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanping_Wuyishan_Airport), [Longyan Guanzhishan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longyan_Guanzhishan_Airport) and [Sanming Shaxian Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanming_Shaxian_Airport). Xiamen is capable of handling 15.75 million passengers as of 2011. Fuzhou is capable of handling 6.5 million passengers annually with a cargo capacity of more than 200,000 tons. The airport offers direct links to 45 destinations including international routes to Japan, Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore, and Hong Kong.[^cite_note-China_Expat_City_Guide:_Dandong-45]

All of the prefecture-level cities except Nanping, Sanming, and Longyan are found along the coast.

These nine prefecture-level cities are subdivided into 84 [county-level divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County-level_division) (31 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_China), 11 [county-level cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County-level_cities), and 42 [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(People's_Republic_of_China\))). Those are in turn divided into 1,102 [township-level divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China#Township_level) (653 [towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_\(China\)), 233 [townships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townships_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), 19 [ethnic townships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_township), and 195 [subdistricts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdistricts_of_China)).

The [People's Republic of China](5405.md) claims five of the six townships of [Kinmen County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinmen), Republic of China (Taiwan) as a county of the prefecture-level city of [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou).[^cite_note-statqz-50][^cite_note-govqz-51][^cite_note-xzqhqz-52]

The PRC claims [Wuqiu Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuqiu,_Kinmen), Kinmen County, Republic of China (Taiwan) as part of [Xiuyu District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiuyu_District) of the prefecture-level city of [Putian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putian).

Finally, the PRC claims [Lienchiang County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lienchiang_County) (Matsu Islands), Republic of China (Taiwan) as a township of its [Lianjiang County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianjiang_County), which is part of the prefecture-level city of [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou).

Together, these three groups of islands make up the Republic of China's province of [Fuchien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuchien_Province,_Republic_of_China).

## Economy



[Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou), the capital and largest city in Fujian

Fujian is one of the more affluent provinces in China, with many industries spanning tea production, clothing, and sports manufacturers such as [Anta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anta_Sports), [361 Degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/361_Degrees), [Xtep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xtep), [Peak Sport Products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_Sport_Products) and [Septwolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septwolves_\(clothing\)). Fujian was one of the first provinces in China authorized by the central government to receive foreign investments.[^cite_note-\:26-61]<sup>: 148 </sup> Many foreign firms have operations in Fujian. They include Boeing, Dell, GE, Kodak, Nokia, Siemens, Swire, TDK, and Panasonic.[^cite_note-62] Within Fujian, the city of Xiamen was one of China's first [special economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zones_of_China) ("SEZs").[^cite_note-\:26-61]<sup>: 158 </sup>

In 2022, Fujian's GDP was CN¥5.31 trillion (US\$790 billion in nominal), [ranking 8th in GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) nationwide and appearing in the world's top 20 [largest sub-national economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country_subdivisions_by_GDP_over_200_billion_US_dollars).[^cite_note-data2022-9] Along with its coastal neighbours [Zhejiang](96602.md) and Guangdong, Fujian's GDP per capita is above the national average, at CN¥126,829 (US\$18,856 in nominal), the second highest [GDP per capita of all Chinese provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita) after [Jiangsu](166412.md).[^cite_note-data2022-9] The primary, secondary and tertiary economy respectively contributed to ¥307 billion (\$45.7 billion), ¥2.51 trillion (\$372.8 billion), and ¥2.50 trillion (\$371 billion) to Fujian's economy.[^cite_note-data2022-9]

In terms of agricultural land, Fujian is hilly and farmland is sparse. [Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) is the main crop, supplemented by [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato) and [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) and [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley).[^cite_note-65] Cash crops include [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane) and [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed). Fujian leads the provinces of China in [longan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longan) production, and is also a major producer of [lychees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lychee) and [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea). Seafood is another important product, with [shellfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellfish) production especially prominent.

Because of its geographic location with Taiwan, Fujian has been considered the battlefield frontline in a potential war between mainland China and Taiwan. Hence, it received much less investment from the Chinese central government and developed much slower than the rest of China before 1978. Since 1978, when China opened to the world, Fujian has received significant investment from overseas Fujianese around the world, Taiwanese and foreign investment.

[Minnan Golden Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnan_Golden_Triangle), which includes [Xiamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen), [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou), and [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou), accounts for 40 percent of the GDP of Fujian.

Fujian will be the major economic beneficiary of the opening up of [direct transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Links) with Taiwan, which commenced on December 15, 2008. This includes direct flights from Taiwan to major Fujian cities such as Xiamen and Fuzhou. In addition, ports in Xiamen, Quanzhou, and Fuzhou will upgrade their port infrastructure for increased economic trade with Taiwan.[^cite_note-economist.com-66][^cite_note-67]

Fujian is the host of [China International Fair for Investment and Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_International_Fair_for_Investment_and_Trade) annually. It is held in Xiamen to promote foreign investment for all of China.

## Demographics



The ethnic townships in Fujian

As of 1832, the province was described as having an estimated "population of fourteen millions."[^cite_note-Roberts1-69] In 2021, Fujian's population was estimated to be 41.87 million, with an urbanization rate of 69.7%.[^cite_note-data2022-9]

Fujianese who are legally classified as Han Chinese make up 98% of the population. Various [Min Chinese speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Chinese_speakers) make up the largest subgroups classified as Han Chinese in Fujian, such as [Fuzhounese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_people), [Putian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putian_people), [Hoklo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoklo_people) and [Fuzhou Tanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_Tanka).

The [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_people), a Chinese [sub-branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese_subgroups) with their own distinct identity, mostly live in the southwestern parts of Fujian. The [She](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_\(ethnic_group\)), an ethnic group scattered over mountainous regions in the north, is the largest [minority ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_ethnic_groups) of the province.[^cite_note-70]

Many ethnic Chinese around the world (especially in [Southeast Asia](28741.md)) trace their ancestries to the Fujianese branches of the [Hoklo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoklo_people) and [Teochew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_people) peoples. Descendants of [Southern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Min)-speaking emigrants make up the majorities of ethnic-Chinese populations in [Taiwan](25734.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Singapore](27318.md), [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei), [Thailand](30128.md), [Indonesia](14579.md), and [Philippines](23440.md). [Eastern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Min)-speaking people (especially [Fuzhounese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_people)) are one of the major sources of Chinese immigrants to the United States since the 1990s.[^cite_note-Sound-71]

Ancient temple in Fujian



[Kompyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kompyang) (房村光餅) sold on the streets of Fujian cities

Because of its mountainous nature and waves of migration from central China and assimilation of numerous foreign ethnic groups such as maritime traders in the course of history, Fujian is one of the most culturally and linguistically diverse provinces in China. Local dialects can become unintelligible within 10 kilometres (6.2 mi), and the regional cultures and ethnic composition can be completely different from each other as well. This is reflected in the expression that "if you drive five miles in Fujian the culture changes, and if you drive ten miles, the language does".[^cite_note-NYTDialects-77] Most varieties spoken in Fujian are assigned to a broad [Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Chinese) category. Recent classifications subdivide Min into[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

- [Eastern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Min) (the former Northern group), including the [Fuzhou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_dialect)

- [Northern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Min), spoken in inland northern areas

- [Pu-Xian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu-Xian_Min), spoken in central coastal areas

- [Central Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Min), spoken in the west of the province

- [Shao-Jiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shao-Jiang_Min), spoken in the northwest

- [Southern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Min), including the [Amoy dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoy_dialect) and [Taiwanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_Hokkien)

The seventh subdivision of Min, [Qiong Wen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiong_Wen), is not spoken in Fujian. [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese), another subdivision of spoken Chinese, is spoken around [Longyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longyan) by the [Hakka people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_people) who live there.

As is true of other provinces, the official language in Fujian is [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese), which is used for communication between people of different localities,[^cite_note-NYTDialects-77] although native Fujian peoples still converse in their native languages and dialects respectively.

Several regions of Fujian have their own form of [Chinese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_opera). [Min opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_opera) is popular around [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou); [Gaojiaxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaojiaxi?action=edit\&redlink=1) around [Jinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinjiang_City) and [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou); [Xiangju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangju_\(Fujian\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) around [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou); [Fujian Nanqu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_Nanqu?action=edit\&redlink=1) throughout the south, and [Puxianxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puxianxi?action=edit\&redlink=1) around [Putian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putian) and [Xianyou County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianyou_County).

[Fujian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_cuisine), with an emphasis on [seafood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafood), is one of the eight great traditions of [Chinese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine). It is composed of traditions from various regions, including [Fuzhou cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_cuisine), [Taiwanese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_cuisine), [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnan_region) cuisine and [Teochew cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_cuisine). The most prestigious dish is Fotiaoqiang (literally "[Buddha jumps over the wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha_jumps_over_the_wall)"), a complex dish making use of many ingredients, including [shark](43617.md) [fin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fin), [sea cucumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumber_\(food\)), [abalone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abalone) and [Shaoxing wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaoxing_wine) (a type of [Chinese alcoholic beverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_alcoholic_beverage)).

Many well-known [teas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea) originate from Fujian, including [oolong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oolong), [Wuyi Yancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyi_Yancha?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Lapsang souchong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapsang_souchong) and [Fuzhou jasmine tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_jasmine_tea?action=edit\&redlink=1). Indeed, the [tea processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_processing) techniques for three major classes of tea, namely, oolong, [white tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_tea), and [black tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_tea) were all developed in the province. [Fujian tea ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_tea_ceremony) is an elaborate way of preparing and serving tea. The English word "tea" is borrowed from Hokkien. [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese) and [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Cantonese) pronounce the word *chá*.

[Nanyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanguan_music) is a popular form of [music of Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Fujian).

[Fuzhou bodiless lacquer ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou_bodiless_lacquer_ware?action=edit\&redlink=1), a noted type of [lacquer ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacquer_ware), is noted for using a body of [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) and/or [plaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaster) to form its shape; the body later removed. [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou) is also known for [Shoushan stone carvings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoushan_stone_carvings).

## Tourism



Stone Statue of Laozi.



Hekeng village, in [Shuyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuyang,_Fujian) Town, is one of the many tulou villages of Fujian's [Nanjing County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_County).

Fujian is home to several tourist attractions, including four UNESCO World Heritage Sites, one of the highest in China.

### Cultural features

The [Fujian Tulou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_Tulou) are Chinese rural dwellings unique to the Hakka in southwest Fujian. These 46 buildings[^cite_note-80] were listed by the [UNESCO](21786641.md) as one of the [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) in 2008.

[Gulangyu Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulangyu_Island), Xiamen, is notable for its beaches, winding lanes, and rich architecture. The island is on China's list of National Scenic Spots and is classified as a [5A tourist attraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_Attraction_Rating_Categories_of_China) by the [China National Tourism Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Tourism_Administration) (CNTA). It was listed by the [UNESCO](21786641.md) as one of the [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 2017. Also in Xiamen is the [South Putuo Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Putuo_Temple).

The [Guanghua Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanghua_Temple_\(Putian\)) is a Buddhist temple in [Putian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putian). It was built in the penultimate year of the [Southern Chen dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_dynasty). Located in the northern half of the mouth of Meizhou Bay, it is about 1.8 nautical miles from the mainland and faces the Strait of Taiwan to the southeast. Covering an area of six square miles, the island is swathed in luxuriant green foliage. The coastline is indented with over 12 miles of the beach area. Another Buddhist temple, [Nanshan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshan_Temple_\(Zhangzhou\)) is located in [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou).

The [Kaiyuan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiyuan_Temple_\(Quanzhou\)) is a [Buddhist temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temple) in West Street, [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou), the largest in Fujian, with an area of 78,000 square metres (840,000 square feet).[^cite_note-81] Although it is known as both a Hindu and Buddhist temple, on account of added Tamil-Hindu influences, the main statue in the most important hall is that of Vairocana Buddha, the main Buddha according to [Huayan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huayan_Buddhism).

In the capital of Fuzhou is the [Yongquan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongquan_Temple_\(Fuzhou\)), a [Buddhist temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temple) built during the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty).

The [Chongwu Army Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongwu_Army_Temple) honors twenty-seven fallen soldiers of the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) who died during an attack by Nationalist forces in 1949, including five who died shielding a teenage girl during the attack.[^cite_note-82] The site is frequented by locals and tourists.[^cite_note-83]

Around Meizhou Islands is the [Matsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsu_\(goddess\)) [pilgrimage](23763.md).

### Natural features

[Mount Taimu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Taimu) is a mountain and a scenic resort in [Fuding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuding). It offers a grand view of mountains and sea and is famous for its natural scenery including granite caves, odd-shaped stones, cliffs, clear streams, cascading waterfalls, and cultural attractions such as ancient temples and cliff Inscriptions.

The [Danxia landform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danxia_landform) in [Taining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taining_County) was listed by the [UNESCO](21786641.md) as one of the [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) in 2010. It is a unique type of [petrographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrography) [geomorphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphology) found in [China](5405.md). Danxia [landform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landform) is formed from red-coloured [sandstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) and [conglomerates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\(geology\)) of largely [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) age. The landforms look very much like [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) topography that forms in areas underlain by [limestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), but since the rocks that form danxia are sandstones and conglomerates, they have been called "pseudo-karst" landforms. They were formed by endogenous forces (including [uplift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_uplift)) and exogenous forces (including [weathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weathering) and [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion)).

The [Wuyi Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyi_Mountains) was the first location in Fujian to be listed by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as one of the [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) in 1999. They are a mountain range in the prefecture of [Nanping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanping) and contain the highest peak in Fujian, [Mount Huanggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Huanggang). It is famous as a natural landscape garden and a summer resort in China.[^cite_note-84]

## Sports

Fujian includes professional sports teams in both the [Chinese Basketball Association](939720.md) and the [Chinese League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_League_One).

The representative of the province in the [Chinese Basketball Association](939720.md) is the [Fujian Sturgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_Sturgeons), who are based in [Jinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinjiang,_Fujian), Quanzhou. The Fujian Sturgeons made their debut in the 2004–2005 season, and finished in seventh and last place in the South Division, out of the playoffs. In the 2005–2006 season, they tied for fifth, just one win away from making the [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playoffs).

The [Xiamen Blue Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen_Blue_Lions) formerly represented Fujian in the [Chinese Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Super_League), before the team's closure in 2007. Today the province is represented by [Fujian Tianxin F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_Tianxin_F.C.), who play in the [China League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_League_Two), and the [Fujian Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_Broncos_F.C.).

## Education and research

Fujian is considered one of China's leading provinces in education and research. As of 2023, two major cities in the province ranked in the top 45 cities in the world (Xiamen 38th and Fuzhou 45th) by scientific research output, as tracked by the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index).[^cite_note-\:0-10]
