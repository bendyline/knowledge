The **2025–26 European windstorm season** was the eleventh year of the continent's coordinated severe weather naming programme. While the official list of names was unveiled on 1 September 2025, the season’s duration typically spans from the start of September 2025 through to 31 August 2026, with the exception of the Eastern Mediterranean group, which the season begins on 1 October.&#x20;

This year represented the seventh season of collaboration within the Western Group, where the Netherlands [Koninklijk Nederlands Meteorologisch Instituut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninklijk_Nederlands_Meteorologisch_Instituut) (KNMI) works alongside the UK’s [Met Office](319671.md) and Ireland’s [Met Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met_%C3%89ireann). To the south-west, the national meteorological agencies of [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPMA), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEMET), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteo_France), and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Meteorological_Institute) have united for their ninth year of partnership, joined for the first time by [Luxembourg](17515.md).&#x20;

The Mediterranean regions also entered their fifth year of formal naming. This included the Eastern Mediterranean group—comprising [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_National_Meteorological_Service), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Meteorological_Service), and [Cyprus](5593.md)—and the Central Mediterranean group, which includes [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servizio_Meteorologico), [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenian_Environment_Agency), [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Meteorological_and_Hydrological_Service), [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrometeorological_Institute_of_Montenegro), [North Macedonia](23564616.md), and [Malta](19137.md). Furthermore, a significant change occurred on 1 January 2026, when the Northern Group, [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Meteorological_Institute), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Meteorological_and_Hydrological_Institute) and [Denmark](4219738.md), adopted a predetermined list of names for the first time.&#x20;

## Background and naming

### Definitions and naming conventions

Main article: [Weather system naming in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_system_naming_in_Europe)

Further information: [List of historical European windstorm names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_historical_European_windstorm_names)

There is no universal definition of what constitutes a windstorm in Europe, nor is there a universal system of naming storms.&#x20;

In the western group, consisting of the UK, Ireland, and the Netherlands, a storm is named if one of those meteorological agencies issues an orange warning (known as amber in the UK per its [National Severe Weather Warning Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Severe_Weather_Warning_Service) system), which generally requires a likelihood of widespread sustained wind speeds greater than 65 km/h (18 m/s; 35 kn; 40 mph), or widespread wind gust speeds over 110 km/h (31 m/s; 59 kn; 68 mph). Required speeds vary slightly by agency and by season. Likelihood of on-land human impacts and inherent severity of the system are further factors in whether to use the next pre-listed name.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-UK_Storms_-_Met_Office-2][^cite_note-3]&#x20;

The south-western group conducts its own naming (Spain, Portugal, and France). They typically name a storm if it prompts the issuance of an orange warning, usually for wind, rain or in some cases snow.[^cite_note-4]&#x20;

For Greece to call upon the next name of its group, the criteria are when forecast winds are above 50 km/h (14 m/s; 27 kn; 31 mph) over land plus expected to impact infrastructure significantly.[^cite_note-5] For Denmark to do so, a windstorm must have an hourly average wind speed of at least 90 km/h (25 m/s; 49 kn; 56 mph).[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

The Meteorology Department of the [Free University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin) (FUB) names all high and low-pressure systems that affect Europe, though they do not assign names to any actual storms.[^cite_note-7] A windstorm that is associated with one of these pressure systems is sometimes recognized by the name given to the associated pressure system by the FUB. Named windstorms that have been recognized by a European meteorological agency are described in this article.&#x20;

Invoking the next listed name in Europe is commonly by a storm's forecast conditions in the next day or so – as public awareness and preparedness are often cited as the main purpose.[^cite_note-UK_Storms_-_Met_Office-2] A nascent storm very occasionally does not become or remain a storm when reaching the forecasting authority.&#x20;

Main article: [Hurricane Gabrielle (2025)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Gabrielle_\(2025\))

#### Date named and background

Hurricane Gabrielle, named by the [National Hurricane Center](255317.md), formed in the [main development region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_development_region) of the Atlantic Ocean on 17 September. It peaked as a powerful Category 4 hurricane east of Bermuda. The system then began to accelerate as it approached the [Azores](3226.md) archipelago. Gabrielle gradually weakened, and became [extratropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_cyclone) a few hundred nautical miles west-southwest of the Azores on 25 September. The storm traversed the Azores then slowed, and moved over the Western [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) on 28 September where it opened up into a trough late that day.[^cite_note-19]&#x20;

#### Weather warnings issued

A [hurricane watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_watch) was issued for the Azores on 22 September. The watch was upgraded to a warning on 24 September.[^cite_note-20] The [IPMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Portugu%C3%AAs_do_Mar_e_da_Atmosfera) issued orange and yellow alerts for all of the Azores. [Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores_Island_\(Azores\)) and [Corvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvo_Island) were placed under a red alert for precipitation. [Terceira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terceira_Island), [Faial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faial_Island), [Pico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_Island), [São Jorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Jorge_Island), and [Graciosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graciosa) were placed under a red alert for wind and rough seas.[^cite_note-21] Orange alerts were eventually issued for the eastern islands.[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

As ex-Hurricane Gabrielle impacted the Iberian Peninsula, red alerts for rain were issued for eastern parts of Spain, and 200 mm (7.9 in) of precipitation was expected to fall within 24 hours.[^cite_note-23]&#x20;

#### Storm impacts

Schools and government buildings in the central and western Azores were closed for Gabrielle.[^cite_note-24] Ports in [Madalena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madalena,_Azores), [Lajes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajes_\(Praia_da_Vit%C3%B3ria\)), and [Horta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horta,_Azores) were closed.[^cite_note-25] Flight plans were interrupted from 25 to 27 September in the Azores.[^cite_note-26] This unusual event prompted officials to urge residents and visitors to take all necessary precautions as the storm approached the islands.[^cite_note-27]&#x20;

#### Date named and background

Amy, known as *Detlef* in Germany, was the first storm named by the western group (Met Office) on 1 October. A powerful "weather bomb", it reached a peak intensity of 942 hPa (27.8 inHg) and produced a 139 mph gust at Folgefonna, Norway. The storm caused widespread damage and four deaths across France, Ireland, and Poland. Over 832,000 homes lost power across Northern Europe, with Sweden reporting its worst outages since 2019.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]&#x20;

#### Weather warnings issued

Chief Meteorologist Tom Crabtree says, the system would bring gale-force winds across northern and island areas of Scotland, with gusts widely reaching 50 to 60 miles per hour (22 to 27 m/s; 43 to 52 kn; 80 to 97 km/h) inland in the Caithness, Sutherland, and Hebrides areas, and potentially reaching 70 to 80 miles per hour (31 to 36 m/s; 61 to 70 kn; 110 to 130 km/h) in places. A yellow rain warning was in force for western and central parts of [Scotland](26994.md) as 20–30 mm (0.79–1.18 in) of rain was expected widely in a 6–9 hour period with 40–50 mm (1.6–2.0 in) over some hilly areas in the Southwest and the Southern Highlands. Accompanying this was a large yellow wind warning for Scotland, which was upgraded to an Amber warning in northwestern parts of Scotland as well as western parts of Northern Ireland. A yellow wind warning from northern and western [England](9316.md), stretching down to north [Wales](69894.md), was in force with another wind warning in force for [Northern Ireland](21265.md), an area where 60–70 miles per hour (27–31 m/s; 52–61 kn; 97–113 km/h) winds where expected widely with 80 miles per hour (36 m/s; 70 kn; 130 km/h) and above expected in exposed areas. A yellow wind warning was later issued, covering the rest of south-west, central, southern, south-eastern and eastern England, as well as southern Wales, with gusts of 45–55 mph (20–25 m/s; 39–48 kn; 72–89 km/h) possible in places, and perhaps 60–65 mph (27–29 m/s; 52–56 kn; 97–105 km/h) along some exposed coasts.[^cite_note-Met_Office-32]&#x20;

For [Ireland](147575.md) a yellow wind warning was in place for the whole country, with the main cause for concern for [Met Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met_%C3%89ireann) being difficult travelling conditions, debris and loose objects displaced, and some fallen trees possible. A man in his forties died in the town of [Letterkenny](620333.md) in [County Donegal](45576.md) around 16\:15 on 3 October.[^cite_note-33]&#x20;

For parts of southern and western [Norway](21241.md), red-severity warnings for extremely strong wind gusts along the [Skagerrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skagerrak) coast and extremely heavy rain in parts of [Telemark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemark) and [Vestfold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestfold). Widespread orange-severity warnings were also in effect for very strong winds, very heavy rain, flooding, and the potential for debris avalanches and debris flows across various parts of the region. These will result in an expected high risk of cancelled flights, ferries and other transport disruptions.[^cite_note-34]&#x20;

[Danish Meteorological Institute](4219738.md) issued warnings for high winds, heavy rain, and elevated water levels this weekend due to Storm Amy. Gale-force to stormy-gale winds, with hurricane-force gusts possible, were expected sweep the region, mainly on Jutland's coasts. Simultaneously, widespread heavy rain is expected Saturday, potentially dropping 40–60 mm (1.6–2.4 in) in some spots. High water levels up to 3.0 m (9.8 ft) were anticipated in the [Wadden Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadden_Sea) and along the west coast of [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland).[^cite_note-35]&#x20;

#### Storm impacts

In [France](5843419.md) two people have died due to the storm, the first one drowned and the second got killed in his car with a collapsed tree and second person was injured. [^cite_note-36]&#x20;

In [Scotland](26994.md), a gust of 96 mph (43 m/s; 83 kn; 154 km/h) was recorded on [Tiree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiree) whilst power cuts were reported in the Highlands. In [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow), a derelict building collapsed onto a car amid high winds on [Broomielaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broomielaw), forcing the road to close. A train travelling between [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen) and [Inverurie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverurie) hit a fallen tree, leaving around a dozen passengers stranded on the train. The [Forth Road Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forth_Road_Bridge) was closed, whilst high-sided vehicles were expected to be prevented from crossing the [Clackmannanshire Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clackmannanshire_Bridge), [Kincardine Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kincardine_Bridge), [Tyne Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyne_Bridge) and possibly the [Queensferry Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensferry_Crossing). The [Highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Main_Line), [Kyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Line), [West Highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Highland_Line) and [Wick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_North_Line) railway lines were closed and speed restrictions were placed on all Scottish routes, whilst many ferries from [Cairnryan Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairnryan_Harbour) to Northern Ireland were cancelled.[^cite_note-37]&#x20;

In [Wales](69894.md), around 600 properties lost power in [Carmarthenshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmarthenshire) and the [Severn Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severn_Bridge) was closed in both directions. A fallen tree near [Gobowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobowen) forced rail services to be suspended between [Shrewsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury) and [Wrexham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrexham).[^cite_note-38]&#x20;

#### Date named and background

Named by Météo-France on 22 October, Benjamin, known as *Joshua* by the FUB was a significant system that affected a broad area from France and Spain to the UK and Norway. It attained a minimum pressure of 969 hPa (28.6 inHg) and produced wind gusts up to 100 mph. The storm caused notable travel disruptions and structural damage across Western Europe.[^cite_note-aemet-9][^cite_note-51]&#x20;

#### Weather warnings issued

Met Office issued yellow warnings for rain and wind across many parts of England and South Wales.[^cite_note-52] Météo-France placed orange warnings for wind, wave and rain-flood.[^cite_note-53] Belgium and the Netherlands also issued orange wind warnings for coastal areas.[^cite_note-54] AEMET issued a red alert for stormy seas for sections of the northern Spanish coast.[^cite_note-55]&#x20;

#### Storm impacts

The storm resulted in one fatality and hundreds of millions of euros in insured losses. In France, wind gusts peaking at 104 mph (168 km/h) caused over 140,000 households to lose power, while a 45-year-old tourist was swept away by a river in Corsica. Additionally, 16 people were injured across the [Charente-Maritime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charente-Maritime) and [Gironde](64654.md) departments. Travel across the continent was crippled, with Amsterdam [Schiphol Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schiphol_Airport) cancelling more than 200 flights and London [Heathrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow) reporting dozens of cancellations. The aviation disruption affected over 32,000 passengers at Schiphol alone, as safety protocols restricted operations to a single runway. In the Netherlands and the UK, fallen trees and downed power lines halted train services between major cities like Amsterdam, Rotterdam, and London, while ferry crossings in the [English Channel](9230.md) were suspended due to treacherous maritime conditions. Infrastructure damage was extensive, with local flooding submerged roadways and coastal erosion damaging tourist sites like the Dune du Pilat. In the UK, more than 2,000 homes lost power, and heavy rainfall led to over a dozen rivers overflowing their banks, causing localised flooding in eastern and southern counties. As the storm system moved toward the North Sea, it pulled in a mass of Arctic air that resulted in a sharp temperature crash and the season's first significant snowfall in the Scottish Highlands.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-MF_Bilan-Oct-61]&#x20;

A tornado north of Paris in [Val-d’Oise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-d%E2%80%99Oise) on 20 October, days before Benjamin, is not related to the storm, according to [Météo-France](4732627.md)[^cite_note-MF_Bilan-Oct-61] and [Keraunos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keraunos).[^cite_note-62]&#x20;

#### Weather warnings issued

[Spain](26667.md) and [Portugal](23033.md) experienced the most intense effects and subsequent warnings from Storm Claudia, which centered over the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula) and the [Canary Islands](5717.md). Portugal's [IPMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPMA) had issued widespread Yellow Warnings for Rain, Strong Winds (up to 80–90 km/h, 22–25 m/s, 43–49 kn, 50–56 mph gusts), and Rough Seas across the mainland, particularly affecting coastal and northern districts. Widespread Yellow Alerts were in place across the archipelago for heavy rain and gusty winds, and Yellow Alerts were also issued for heavy rainfall and winds up to 80 km/h (22 m/s; 43 kn; 50 mph) in the mainland's northwest, particularly in Galicia.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]&#x20;

The [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [Ireland](147575.md) felt the influence of an associated frontal system trailing Claudia. The [Met Office](319671.md) issued a joint Yellow Rain and Wind Warning for South West England (including Cornwall, Devon, and Somerset), where heavy rain was expected to accumulate significantly (up to 60–80 mm (2.4–3.1 in) over exposed high ground), accompanied by strong southerly winds and coastal gales, leading to difficult driving conditions and localised flooding. Furthermore, [Met Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met_%C3%89ireann) had issued Status Yellow Rain Warnings for various counties in the south and east of Ireland, and the UK Met Office maintained Yellow Rain Warnings for Northern Ireland due to persistent rain and stronger winds along the Irish Sea coastlines.[^cite_note-71]&#x20;

On 13 November, the Met Office issued a severe Amber Rain Warning for 14 November, covering large areas of Wales, the Midlands, western East Anglia, and South West England due to Storm Claudia. The key impacts of this high-level alert include a risk of danger to life from fast-flowing floodwater, homes and businesses being flooded, widespread travel cancellations and delays (especially on road and rail), and the potential for some communities to be cut off by floodwater. Rainfall was expected to be exceptional, with totals of 50–150 mm (2.0–5.9 in) possible across the affected areas.[^cite_note-Met_Office-32] Also, Met Éireann issued an Orange Rain Warning for three eastern counties: [Dublin](8504.md), [Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wexford), and [Wicklow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicklow). This high-level warning signifies a significant risk of severe and dangerous flooding due to exceptionally high rainfall totals falling on already saturated ground, leading to extremely hazardous driving conditions and major travel disruption across these areas.[^cite_note-72]&#x20;

A large crowd forms outside [Swindon railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swindon_railway_station) as trains are cancelled, delayed or simply out of the question because of flooding on the tracks

As of 22\:15 on 14 November, Environment Agency has issued more than 150 flood alerts and 39 flood warnings.[^cite_note-73] Two men have been taken to hospital after a head-on crash in a Black Country residential street.[^cite_note-74] Rail services across central and southern areas, including the lines between London Paddington and Bristol/South Wales, and routes in the Midlands (such as around Birmingham New Street), experienced major disruption and cancellations due to flooded tracks, with some passengers advised not to travel. Gusty winds, up to 70 mph (31 m/s; 61 kn; 110 km/h) in parts of North West England and north-west Wales, added to the hazardous conditions. There were reports of power cuts and road closures due to flooding and fallen trees, with conditions expected to remain difficult until the rain eases on Saturday morning.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]&#x20;

Storm Claudia caused significant weather impacts across Ireland, with the main concern being widespread flooding expected to persist through the weekend. The Status Orange Rain Warning for Dublin, Wexford, and Wicklow led to hazardous travelling conditions, numerous road closures due to debris and flooding (including in Dublin), and the cancellation of several flights at Dublin Airport. Areas under the broader Yellow warning also saw significant local flooding, particularly in the Laois/Offaly region.[^cite_note-78] A major incident was declared in South Wales due to "severe and widespread" flooding in [Monmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monmouth).[^cite_note-79]&#x20;

#### Weather warnings issued

The Portuguese weather agency (IPMA)[^cite_note-148] and the Spanish agency (AEMET)[^cite_note-149] issued a wide range of warnings for Storm Francis, which is expected to bring a significant shift from dry, polar air to intense subtropical instability. In [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira), the most severe conditions were anticipated, with Orange warnings rising to Red issued for the highlands and southern slopes due to persistent, heavy rainfall, hail, and thunderstorms, with wind gusts reaching up to 115 km/h. Across the [Canary Islands](5717.md), the regional government has declared alerts for rain and wind, while AEMET has specifically activated Yellow warnings for heavy rainfall and thunderstorms, particularly in [Tenerife](269223.md), [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria), and [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma), where gusts of 80–90 km/h and rough seas with waves up to 4 meters are expected. On the Portuguese mainland, all districts are under Yellow warnings for New Year's Day as the storm's frontal systems bring heavy rain and maritime turbulence, while in Spain, widespread showers and high-altitude snow are forecast for the [Andalucía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaluc%C3%ADa) region through the first weekend of the year.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]&#x20;

The Moroccan General Directorate of Meteorology (DGM) issued Red Alerts—the highest possible warning level—for the provinces of [Agadir Ida-Outanane](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agadir_Ida-Outanane\&action=edit\&redlink=1), [Taroudant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taroudant), and [Essaouira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essaouira). These regions braced for extreme rainfall totals of up to 120 mm (4,700 thou) in 24 hours, raising the immediate risk of flash floods in low-lying valleys and "[wadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadis)" (dry riverbeds). In [Algeria](358.md), coastal provinces were under orange alerts for both torrential rain and gale-force winds. The storm drove high waves against the northern shoreline, leading to the suspension of several maritime transport routes. Deep in the interior, the Atlas Mountains saw significant snow accumulation above 1,200 meters. These conditions made many mountain passes impassable, isolating several rural communities.[^cite_note-152] The [Italian Civil Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Italian_Civil_Protection\&action=edit\&redlink=1) has issued Orange and Yellow alerts across several regions. In the north, particularly around Trieste and the Adriatic coast, Orange Alerts are active for Wind and Snow, with "Bora" gusts reaching up to 150 km/h causing severe blizzard conditions. Central and [Southern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy) are under Yellow Alerts for Rain and Thunderstorms. These warnings highlight the risk of localized flash flooding and landslides as the storm's center moves across the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) toward the coast.[^cite_note-153]&#x20;

The National Institute of Meteorology has issued Yellow Alerts for Rain and Wind. These warnings indicate that the weather is "potentially dangerous," with rainfall totals expected to reach 40 mm in the northwest. There is also a specialized Cold Wave Warning for the high-altitude regions of Jendouba and Siliana. This covers the risk of Snow and Ice, which is rare for the region and expected to disrupt local mountain transit.[^cite_note-154]&#x20;

In the [Balkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan) region, warning levels have reached the maximum severity. [Croatia](5573.md) and [Montenegro](20760.md) have issued Red Alerts (the highest level) for Hurricane-force Winds, specifically the storm, which is gusting over 160 km/h. [Slovenia](27338.md) and Bosnia are under Orange Alerts for Snow and Blizzards, with fresh accumulations exceeding 40–60 cm expected to cause a total standstill in transportation. Serbia remains under Yellow and Orange Alerts for Extreme Cold, as temperatures are recorded at 15 °C below the seasonal average.[^cite_note-meteo.hr-82] The HMIK issued flood warnings, for the west of Kosovo,[^cite_note-155] and later the warnings were extended to most of Kosovo.&#x20;

#### Storm impacts

Storm Francis hit the [Canary Islands](5717.md) and [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira) first, with wind gusts peaking at 120 km/h (75 mph) in Tenerife. In the highlands of Madeira, the Portuguese weather agency (IPMA) upgraded warnings to Red as the storm delivered 115 km/h gusts and 4-meter swells, while La Palma recorded torrential rainfall exceeding 76 liters per square meter in just a few hours. As the storm moved from the [Gulf of Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_C%C3%A1diz) toward the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) on 4 January, the snow level in [Catalonia](6822.md) and the [Ebro Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebro_Valley) was expected to start dropping toward 200 metres, with a risk of snow reaching sea level by Monday morning.[^cite_note-156]&#x20;

Storm Francis caused significant devastation across [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe), leading to at least eight confirmed deaths and widespread destruction. In [Spain](26667.md), three people were killed in Andalusia—including a man whose van was swept away in [Málaga](184763.md) and a young person on a motorbike in Granada—while the region faced record-breaking rainfall of up to 290 mm, collapsing bridges and forcing the evacuation of nearly 500 homes. In Portugal, the storm (identified as part of the same system) resulted in two deaths in the [Setúbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set%C3%BAbal) district due to house flooding, alongside nearly 1,000 emergency incidents and massive power outages. Additionally, three fatalities occurred in Southwestern France as the storm's moisture turned into "black ice" upon meeting a polar air mass, causing lethal road accidents.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158]&#x20;

This "danger to life" alert was triggered when river levels were forecast to surpass their highest ever recorded levels at the Fenny Bridges gauge. Emergency services focused on evacuations at the Finnimore Industrial Estate and Thorne Farm Way, where deep, fast-flowing water inundated properties from the early hours of Tuesday morning.[^cite_note-Paz-212] Another severe flood warning was issued from the Environment Agency for Upper Frome in Dorchester.[^cite_note-Hatton-211]&#x20;

In [Northern Ireland](21265.md), the scale of disruption was immense, with the number of confirmed school closures rising to over 300 institutions. Major academic bodies, including [Queen’s University Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen%E2%80%99s_University_Belfast) and multiple [Ulster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_University) campuses, completely suspended physical operations. Beyond education, the storm caused significant power outages, with [Northern Ireland Electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Electricity) (NIE) reporting that 10,000 properties lost power, while an additional 20,000 homes and businesses faced blackouts across the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md).[^cite_note-Paz-212]&#x20;

The storm effectively halted travel across the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea) and Scottish coastlines. [Caledonian MacBrayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonian_MacBrayne) (CalMac) was forced to cancel almost all its ferry sailings, including those to [Mull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Mull), [Iona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iona), and [Barra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra), while [Stena Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stena_Line) suspended all morning crossings between [Belfast](5046.md) and [Liverpool](18081.md). Aviation also took a hit, with Belfast City Airport cancelling at least 30 flights and regional carrier [Loganair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loganair) grounding 20 services across its network due to the extreme wind gusts.[^cite_note-Paz-212]&#x20;

There was one fatality as a result of the storm after a lorry crashed into a river in the [New Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Forest), killing the driver.[^cite_note-213]&#x20;

#### Date named and background

Storm Kristin was named by the IPMA on 27 January 2026, as a significant impact was forecasted.[^cite_note-aemet-9]&#x20;

Kristin developed from a baroclinic disturbance generated by Storm Joseph, which subsequently developed into a compact but extremely powerful storm. Under influence of a trough in the [jet stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_stream), Kristin underwent a process of explosive cyclogenesis. In the hours before landfall, Kristin developed a sting jet, as Kristin was a warm seclusion cyclone. Instead of weakening, like numerical weather predictions models suggested beforehand, Kristin made landfall in the Leiria district at peak intensity.&#x20;

Red warnings for wind were issued for northern parts of Portugal with 140 km/h winds forecasted.[^cite_note-217] [AEMET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEMET), The State Meteorological Agency for [Spain](26667.md) has issued a red alert for the interior of Pontevedra due to forecasts of torrential rain exceeding 120mm. Strong winds are a major concern across the country with hurricane-force gusts expected to impact the far southeast and various interior regions. Much of the Spanish interior remains under orange or yellow alerts for gusts that could reach 100 km/h and cause structural damage. Significant snowfall is predicted for the northern plateau at altitudes as low as 600 meters which may disrupt major motorways such as the A-1 and A-6. These regions face high-speed winds and significant hydrogeological risks, while a Yellow Alert covered much of the rest of the peninsula for rain, wind, and thunderstorms. In the north, Red Alerts for Avalanches (Level 4/5) were in effect for the [Western Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Alps), while the south remained in a [State of Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Emergency)[^cite_note-219] following last week's Storm Harry, which triggered the massive 4 km-long landslide currently threatening the town of [Niscemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niscemi), [Sicily](27619.md).[^cite_note-220]&#x20;

A wind gust of 108 km/h was reported in Cáceres during the event. Road transport was also disrupted across the region. In the province of Cáceres, seven roads and three rural tracks were reported with circulation problems (snow, river overflows, or waterlogged carriageways), and two trucks overturned on the A-66 due to strong winds. In [Bulgaria](3415.md), The National Institute of Meteorology and Hydrology (NIMH) has issued a Code Orange warning for 24 out of 28 districts, forecasting significant snowfall of up to 20 cm and severe blizzards with visibility-reducing snow drifts. In [Romania](25445.md), The National Meteorological Administration (ANM) has issued a severe weather advisory for a sharp cold snap, with temperatures expected to plunge to -15 °C in mountain depressions and widespread frost across the country.&#x20;

National meteorological services issued severe alerts for the arrival of the storm on 1 February 2026. The Croatian Meteorological and Hydrological Service (DHMZ) issued red wind warnings for the Velebit Channel and the Kvarner region, where hurricane-force Bora winds were forecast to exceed 150 km/h.[^cite_note-225] In Italy, the Protezione Civile issued orange and red warnings for torrential rain and flash flooding across Sicily and Calabria. Additionally, yellow marine warnings were maintained for the Ionian Sea as the system moved toward the Greek FIR.[^cite_note-226]&#x20;

#### Storm impacts

The storm was responsible for significant structural damage and travel chaos across the region. In Croatia, extreme Bora winds forced the closure of the A1 motorway and the [Krk Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krk_Bridge), while maritime transport was suspended due to gusts of 155 km/h. In Greece, torrential rain led to flooding in [Agiokampos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agiokampos) and the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese), where roads were submerged by rising waters. In southern Italy, the system exacerbated existing damage from earlier cyclones, contributing to land subsidence and evacuations in Niscemi, while all ferry connections to the Aeolian Islands were suspended due to sea conditions.[^cite_note-227]&#x20;

In Portugal, a 70-year-old man lost his life in the southern [Alentejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alentejo) region after his vehicle was swept off a flooded road near a dam on Wednesday night. Meanwhile, in Spain, emergency services are desperately searching for a girl (some reports specify a young woman) who was dragged away by the [Turvilla River](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Turvilla_River\&action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Sayalonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayalonga), [Málaga](184763.md). In [Morocco](19291.md), the Ministry of the Interior confirmed that over 108,000 people have been evacuated, primarily in the Larache province, due to the surging [Loukkos River](22848386.md). In Andalusia, Spain, approximately 4,000 people were forced to leave their homes as the mountain village of [Grazalema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazalema) recorded a staggering 400mm (16 inches) of rain in a single day—roughly what Madrid receives in an entire year. &#x20;According to MeteoAlarm, the weather warnings across Central and Eastern Europe and the Baltic region primarily comprised orange (severe) and yellow (moderate) alerts for severe thunderstorms, heavy rainfall, and strong wind gusts. Orange weather warnings were widely issued across [Poland](22936.md), [Slovakia](26830.md), the [Czech Republic](5321.md), and parts of the [Balkans](4829.md) for intense thunderstorm activity accompanied by torrential downpours exceeding 35–50 mm, hail, and damaging wind gusts reaching 90 to 110 km/h. These were flanked by extensive yellow warnings for wind and rain across neighboring regions, including [Hungary](13275.md), [Austria](26964606.md), [Germany](11867.md), and into the Baltics—specifically [Lithuania](17675.md), [Estonia](28222445.md), and [Latvia](17514.md)—where moderate thunderstorm activity, localised flash flooding, and strong coastal wind gusts were forecast.

#### Storm impacts

The passage of the storm's convective squall line produced damaging straight-line wind gusts, frequent cloud-to-ground lightning, severe hail swaths, and short-duration torrential downpours across multiple nations. Early severe convection initiated near the Alpine region, developing mountain-triggered supercells that produced strong wind squalls, heavy hail, and localised intense rain across northern [Switzerland](26748.md) and southern [Germany](11867.md).[^cite_note-ESTOFEX-337] As the system expanded northward and eastward across German states, severe weather warnings were activated for damaging convective wind gusts and localized flash flooding.[^cite_note-Warnings-338] Tracking eastward, the squall lines generated intense hail corridors and severe gale-force wind gusts across central and northern [Polish voivodeships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_voivodeships). The storm caused widespread agricultural damage, fallen timber, localised municipal power disruptions, and structural damage to local utility networks.[^cite_note-ESTOFEX-337] Upon entering Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania, the storm's trailing cold front produced marine squalls over the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) basin, urban flash flooding in coastal municipalities, and localised transport network delays before the system moved away.[^cite_note-Warnings-338]&#x20;

Severe damages has been reported in Latvia, with the [Latvian Environment, Geology and Meteorology Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_Environment,_Geology_and_Meteorology_Centre) describing it as the strongest summer storm in the region since 1966, where comprehensive observation of wind gust in Latvia began.[^cite_note-339] A gust of 33 m/s (74 mph) has been recorded at [Ventė Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vent%C4%97_Cape).[^cite_note-Priska_Lithuania-340] The storm left at least 277,000 households in Latvia without power and another 219,000 in Lithuania [^cite_note-Priska_Lithuania-340][^cite_note-Priska_Latvia-341]. 1 people has been reported dead in Lithuania due to a fallen tree, and another person was killed by collapsed buildings in Latvia.[^cite_note-342] [^cite_note-Priska_Latvia-341]&#x20;

### Wildlife impacts



Dead [Atlantic puffin *(Fratercula arctica)*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fratercula_arctica) following mass beaching in Southern France due to sustained winter storms.

In February 2026, nearly 25,000 seabirds, primarily Atlantic Puffins (*[Fratercula arctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fratercula_arctica)*), were found stranded along the British, French (20,000), Spanish (4,400) and Portuguese (1,200) Atlantic coasts due to severe weather conditions.[^cite_note-2026_LPO-343] Successive winter storms prevent sea birds from feeding, leading to mass starvation and exhaustion, a known phenomenon with marginal overall effect on the affected species if occasional.[^cite_note-344]&#x20;

Wildlife centers in regions like Brittany and Nouvelle-Aquitaine are struggling to treat hundreds of weakened survivors.[^cite_note-345] Local associations have deployed emergency volunteers to patrol beaches in order to collect and rescue injured birds.[^cite_note-346] The public is called to not directly touch the animals to prevent additional stress, use gloves to avoid contaminations and to report sightings to bird monitorings groups such as [eBird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBird) and [Ligue pour la protection des oiseaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_pour_la_protection_des_oiseaux) (LPO).[^cite_note-2026_LPO-343]&#x20;
