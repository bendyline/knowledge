**Iraqis** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): العراقيون *al-ʿIrāqiyyūn*) are the [citizens and nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_nationality_law) of the [Republic of Iraq](7515928.md). The majority of Iraqis are [Arabs](2185.md),[^cite_note-21] with [Kurds](35423251.md) accounting for the largest ethnic minority, followed by [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Turkmen).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] Other ethnic groups from the country include [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people), [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans), and other Mesopotamian Minorities. Approximately 95% of Iraqis adhere to [Islam](6037917.md), with nearly 64% of this figure consisting of [Shia Muslims](22350732.md) and the remainder consisting of [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam_in_Iraq). The largest minority religion is [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Iraq) at 1%, while other religions collectively represent as much as 4% of the Iraqi populace.

The territory of modern-day Iraq largely overlaps with what was historically known as [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), which was [home to many noteworthy civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization#Mesopotamia), such as [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer), [Akkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire), [Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria), and [Babylonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia). The fall of these native Mesopotamian civilizations, particularly [Babylon in the 6th century BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Babylon), marked the beginning of centuries-long foreign conquests and rule.[^cite_note-\:3-24] Recent studies showed that two individuals from the same group (Iraqi Arab or Iraqi Kurd) could differ more genetically from each other than the average genetic difference between Iraqi Arabs and Iraqi Kurds as populations.[^cite_note-25]

[Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language) are Iraq's two official languages; [Mesopotamian Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_Arabic) is the Iraqi [Arabic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Arabic), having emerged in the aftermath of the [Arab conquest of Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_conquest_of_Mesopotamia) in the 7th century. The process of [Arabization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabization) and [Islamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization) that began during the medieval era resulted in the decline of various [Eastern Aramaic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Aramaic_languages) and local religions, most notably during the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate), when the city of [Baghdad](4492.md) became the capital of the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world) and the centre of the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age). Mesopotamian Arabic is considered to be the most Aramaic-influenced dialect of Arabic, as Aramaic originated in Mesopotamia and spread throughout the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent) during the [Neo-Assyrian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire), eventually becoming the *lingua franca* of the entire region prior to the [early Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests).[^cite_note-CMK-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Other languages spoken within the Iraqi community include [Turkmen Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Turkmen_language), [Neo-Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Aramaic_languages), and [Mandaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaic_language).[^cite_note-30]

Main articles: [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), [History of Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Mesopotamia), and [History of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Iraq)

In ancient and medieval times [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) was the political and cultural centre of many great empires and civilizations, such as the [Akkadian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire), [Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria), [Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire) and [Babylonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire).[^cite_note-JM-31][^cite_note-WS-32] The ancient Mesopotamian civilization of [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer) is the oldest known [civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization) in the world,[^cite_note-BMC-33] and thus Iraq is widely known as the [Cradle Of Civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization).[^cite_note-JM-31] Iraq remained an important centre of civilization for millennia, up until the Muslim conquest of Mesopotamia and subsequently [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) (of which [Baghdad](4492.md) was the [capital](181337.md)), which was the most advanced empire of the [medieval world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) (see [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age)). Hence, [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) has witnessed many periods of emigration and immigration in the past.

## Genetics

Further information: [Genetic history of the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_the_Middle_East), [Genetic history of the Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_the_Arab_world), and [Assyrian people § Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people#Genetics)

One study found that [Haplogroup J-M172](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J-M172) originated in northern Iraq.[^cite_note-Zahery-34] In spite of the importance of this region, genetic studies on the Iraqi people are limited and generally restricted to analysis of classical markers due to Iraq's modern political instability,[^cite_note-Zahery-34] although there have been several published studies displaying a genealogical connection between all Iraqi peoples and the neighboring countries, across religious, ethnic and linguistic barriers. Studies indicate that the different ethno-religious groups of Iraq ([Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia)) share significant similarities in genetics and that Mesopotamian Arabs, who make up the majority of Iraqis, Genetic analysis indicates that Iraqi Arabs have the smallest genetic distance to Iraqi Kurds, and show similarly close affinities to Yemenis and Kuwaitis.[^cite_note-Hayder_Lazim,_Eida_Khalaf_Almohammed,_Sibte_Hadi_&_Judith_Smith_-_Scientific_Reports_volume_10,_Article_number:_15289_(2020)-35]

No significant differences in Y-DNA variation were observed among Iraqi Mesopotamian Arabs, Assyrians, or Kurds.[^cite_note-Zahery-34] Modern genetic studies indicate that Iraqi Arabs and Iraqi Kurds are distantly related, though Iraqi Mesopotamian Arabs are more related to [Iraqi-Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi-Assyrians) than they are to [Iraqi Kurds](35423251.md).[^cite_note-CS-36][^cite_note-Genetic-37]

For both mtDNA and Y-DNA variation, the large majority of the [haplogroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup) observed in the Iraqi population ([H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_H_\(mtDNA\)), [J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J_\(mtDNA\)), [T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_T_\(mtDNA\)), and [U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_U_\(mtDNA\)) for the mtDNA, [J-M172](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J-M172) and [J-M267](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J-M267) for the Y-DNA) are those considered to have originated in [Western Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Asia) and to have later spread mainly in West Asia.[^cite_note-Zahery-34] The Eurasian haplogroups [R1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1b) and [R1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1a) represent the second most frequent component of the Iraqi Y-chromosome gene pool, the latter suggests that the population movements from [Central Asia](6742.md) into modern Iran also influenced Iraq.[^cite_note-Zahery-34]

Many historians and anthropologists provide strong circumstantial evidence to posit that Iraq's [Marsh Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh_Arabs) share very strong links to the ancient Sumerians[^cite_note-BMC-33][^cite_note-38]—the oldest human civilization in the world and most ancient inhabitants of central-southern Iraq.

The Iraqi-Assyrian population was found to be significantly related to other Iraqis, especially Mesopotamian Arabs,[^cite_note-Genetic-37][^cite_note-BMC-33] likely due to the assimilation of indigenous Assyrians with other people groups who occupied and settled Mesopotamia after the fall of the [Neo-Babylonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire).[^cite_note-39]

According to Dogan et al. (2017), the most prevalent lineages among north Iraqis are J1 (17.98%), R1b (12.81%), R1a (12.40%) and J2a1b (12.19%) but distributions vary according to ethnicity. 14 different haplogroups were observed in Iraqi Arabs, with the three most common being J1 (38.61%), R1a (12.87%) and T (8.91%). The high prevalence of J1 is indicative of the indigeneity of Iraqi Arabs, which is similarly observed in Marsh Arabs. Prevalence of R and J macrohaplogroups is also attributed to pre-[Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum) events in the Near East. Meanwhile, 15 different haplogroups were observed in Kurds, with the three most common being J2a1b (20.20%), J1 / R1a (17.17%) and E1b1b (13.13%). 10 different haplogroups were observed in Syriacs, with the three most common being R1b (30.23%), T (17.44%) and J2a1b (15.12%). 16 different haplogroups were observed in Turkmens, with the three most common being E1b1b (17.53%), J1 / J2a1b / R1a (12.37%) and G2a (10.31%). 11 different haplogroups were observed in Yazidis, with the three most common being R1b (20.79%), L (11.88%) and G2a / J2a1x J2a1b/h (10.89%).[^cite_note-\:3-24]

## Languages

Iraq's national languages are [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_languages). The two main regional dialects of Arabic spoken by the Iraqi people are [Mesopotamian Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_Arabic) (spoken in the Babylonian alluvial plain and Middle Euphrates valley) and [South Mesopotamian Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Mesopotamian_Arabic) and [North Mesopotamian Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Mesopotamian_Arabic) (spoken in the Assyrian highlands).[^cite_note-40] The two main dialects of Kurdish spoken by [Kurdish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) are [Central Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Kurdish) (spoken in the [Erbil](463860.md) and [Sulaymaniyah Governorates](752619.md))[^cite_note-KRG-41] and [Northern Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Kurdish) (spoken in [Dohuk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dohuk_Governorate)).[^cite_note-KRG-41] In addition to Arabic, most Assyrians and [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans) speak [Neo-Aramaic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Aramaic_languages). Mesopotamian Arabic has an [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic_language) [substratum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratum_\(linguistics\)#Substratum).[^cite_note-CMK-26]

### Ethnicities

Iraq's population was estimated to be 39,650,145 in 2021 (residing in Iraq).[^cite_note-42] Arabs are the majority ethnic group in Iraq, at around 80%.[^cite_note-43] The [Kurds](35423251.md) are the largest ethnic minority. [Turkmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Turkmens) are the third largest ethnic group in the country. This is followed by [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) and [Armenians](387816.md) (500,000), [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis) (500,000), [Marsh Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh_Arabs) (20,000), [Shabaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabak_people) (250,000), and [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajam_of_Iraq) (300,000-500,000). Other minorities include [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans) (6,000), [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_people) (50,000) and [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians_in_Iraq) (2,000). The most spoken language is [Mesopotamian Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_Arabic), followed by [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_languages), [Iraqi Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Turkmen#Language) dialects and [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Aramaic_languages). The percentages of different ethno-religious groups residing in Iraq vary from source to source due to the last Iraqi census having taken place over 30 years ago. A new census of Iraq was planned to take place in 2020.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-\:0-45][^cite_note-\:1-46][^cite_note-\:2-47][^cite_note-48]

### Religions

See also: [Irreligion in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_Iraq)

Iraqis are diverse in their faiths. Over 95% of Iraqis are Muslim, divided between 55% Shias and 40% Sunnis.[^cite_note-49] In 1968 the Iraqi constitution established [Islam](6037917.md) as the official religion of the state.

Religion in Iraq (2019)

[^cite_note-50]

1. [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Iraq) (95.0%)

2. [Yazidism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidism) (1.25%)

3. [Christianity](5211.md) (1.25%)

4. others (incl.[Yarsanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarsanism), [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism), [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith), [Mandaeism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeism), [Irreligion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_Iraq)) (2.00%)

In addition, [Christianity in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Iraq) consists of various denominations. The majority of Iraqi Christians are [Chaldean Catholic Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi-Assyrians), whilst non-Syriac Christians are mostly Iraqi Arabs and Armenians. [Iraqi-Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi-Assyrians) largely belong to the [Syriac Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Church), the [Assyrian Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East), [Chaldean Catholic Church](698821.md), [Ancient Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Church_of_the_East), and the [Syriac Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Catholic_Church). Iraqi Arab Christians belong to the [Greek Orthodox Church of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church_of_Antioch) and the [Melkite Greek Catholic Church of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church), and [Iraqi-Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Iraq) belong to the [Armenian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) and [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church). Their numbers inside Iraq have dwindled to around 500,000+ since 2003.[^cite_note-51]

Other religious groups include [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans), [Shabaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabak_people), [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis) and followers of other [minority religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_religion). Furthermore, [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Iraq) had also been present in Iraq in significant numbers historically, and [Iraq had the largest Jewish population in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Iraq), but their population dwindled, after virtually all of them migrated to [Israel](9282173.md) between 1949 and 1952. From 1949 to 1951, 104,000 Jews were evacuated from Iraq in Operations Ezra and Nechemia (named after the Jewish leaders who took their people back to Jerusalem from exile in Babylonia beginning in 597 B.C.E.); another 20,000 were smuggled out through Iran.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

## Diaspora

Main articles: [Iraqi diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_diaspora) and [Refugees of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_Iraq)

The [Iraqi diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_diaspora) is not a sudden exodus but one that has grown rapidly through the 20th century as each generation faced some form of radical transition or political conflict. From 1950 to 1952, Iraq saw a great exodus of roughly 120,000–130,000 of its Jewish population under the Israel-led "[Operation Ezra and Nehemiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ezra_and_Nehemiah)". There were at least two large waves of expatriation of both Christians and Muslims alike. A great number of Iraqis left the country during the regime of [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) and large numbers have left during the [Iraq war](5043324.md) and its aftermath.
