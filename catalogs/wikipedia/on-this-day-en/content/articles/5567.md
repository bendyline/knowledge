&#x20;The **politics of [Ivory Coast](57744.md)** takes place in a framework of a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic), whereby the [President of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ivory_Coast) is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md), and of a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the President and the Government. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in both the government and parliament.

The capital since 1983 is [Yamoussoukro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamoussoukro); however, [Abidjan](21371767.md) remains the commercial center. Most countries maintain their embassies in Abidjan. A civil war was fought in Ivory Coast between 2002–2004 and a second civil war broke out in March 2011 following contested elections that saw president-elect Alassane Ouattara come into power in 2011 and reelected in 2015.

## Civil war

Further information: [First Ivorian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Ivorian_Civil_War)

Troops, mostly hailing from the north of the country, mutinied in the early hours of 19 September 2002. They soon after launched attacks in many cities, including Abidjan. By lunchtime, they had control of the north of the country. Their principal claim relates to the definition of who is a citizen of Ivory Coast (and so who can stand for election as president), voting rights and their representation in government in [Abidjan](21371767.md). The events in Abidjan shows that it is not a tribal issue, but a crisis of transition from a dictatorship to a democracy, with the clashes inherent in the definition of citizenship. Forces involved in the conflict include:

- Government forces, the National Army (FANCI), also called *loyalists*, formed and equipped essentially since 2003

- The *[Young Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congr%C3%A8s_Panafricain_des_Jeunes_et_des_Patriotes)*: nationalist groups aligned with President [Laurent Gbagbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Gbagbo)

- [Mercenaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercenaries) recruited by President Gbagbo:&#x20;

  - allegedly, [Belarusians](3457.md)

  - some former combatants of [Liberia](17791.md), including under-17 youths, forming the so-called "Lima militia" <https://web.archive.org/web/20050714110654/http://hrw.org/english/docs/2005/03/30/cotedi10404.htm>

- *[New Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forces_Nouvelles_de_C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire)* (Forces Nouvelles, FN), ex-northern rebels, who hold 60% of the country; their political expression is the *Mouvement patriotique de Côte d'Ivoire*, or MPCI

- French forces: troops sent within the framework of [Opération Licorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Licorne) and under [UN mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Mandate) ([United Nations Operation in Côte d'Ivoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire)), 3000 men in February 2003 and 4600 in November 2004;

- Soldiers of the [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States) (ECOWAS), *White helmets*, also under the UN.

[Ivory Coast](57744.md)'s 1959 constitution provides for strong presidency within the framework of a [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers). The executive is personified in the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ivory_Coast), elected for a five-year term. The president is commander in chief of the armed forces, may negotiate and ratify certain [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty), and may submit a bill to a national [referendum](51582.md) or to the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Ivory_Coast). According to the constitution, the President of the National Assembly assumes the presidency in the event of a vacancy, and he completes the remainder of the deceased president's term. The cabinet is selected by and is responsible to the president. Changes are being proposed to some of these provisions, to extend term of office to 7 years, establish a senate, and make president of the [senate](44417.md) interim successor to the president.

Laurent Gbagbo took power following a popular overthrow of the interim leader Gen. [Robert Guéï](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gu%C3%A9%C3%AF) who had claimed a dubious victory in presidential elections; Gen. Guéï himself had assumed power on 25 December 1999, following a military coup against the government of former President [Henri Konan Bédié](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Konan_B%C3%A9di%C3%A9). Gbagbo was elected president in 2000 in an election boycotted by many oppositional forces. The president is elected by popular vote for a five-year term. The prime minister is appointed by the president. [Alassane Ouattara](1173624.md) is currently the president of Ivory Coast. He was reelected in the [2015 Ivorian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivorian_presidential_election,_2015).

After a new [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ivory_Coast) was approved by [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivorian_constitutional_referendum,_2016), it is expected President [Alassane Ouattara](1173624.md) would appoint a [Vice-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-President_of_Ivory_Coast) before [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivorian_presidential_election,_2020). The [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ivory_Coast) and [Vice-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-President_of_Ivory_Coast) will run on a joint ticket from [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivorian_presidential_election,_2020). They will be both elected for a five-year term, with only one possible reelection. The [Vice-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-President_of_Ivory_Coast) will replace the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ivory_Coast) in case of death, resignation and any other vacancy.

In November 2020, Alassane Ouattara won third term in office in [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Ivorian_presidential_election) boycotted by the opposition. His opponents argued it was illegal for president Ouattara to run for a third term.[^cite_note-1]

## Legislative branch



[National Assembly of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Ivory_Coast).

[Parliament of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Ivory_Coast) is a bicameral body composed by the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Ivory_Coast) and the [Senate of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_\(Ivory_Coast\)). Prior to November 2016 and the future creation of the Senate, the [Parliament of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Ivory_Coast) was only composed of the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Ivory_Coast). The [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Ivory_Coast) (*Assemblée Nationale*) has 255 members, elected for a five-year term in single-seat [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency). It passes on legislation typically introduced by the president although it also can introduce legislation. The [Senate of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_\(Ivory_Coast\)) (*Sénat*) will have two-thirds of the senators indirectly elected and one-third appointed by the president-elect, elected for a five-year term in single-seat [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency). Ivory Coast is a [one party dominant state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_party_dominant_state) with the [Rally of the Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_of_the_Republicans) in power.

## Judicial branch

Main article: [Judicial system of the Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_system_of_the_Ivory_Coast)

The judicial system culminates in the [Supreme Court of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Ivory_Coast?action=edit\&redlink=1). The High Court of Justice is competent to try government officials for major offenses. The Supreme Court or Court Supreme consists of four chambers: Judicial Chamber for criminal cases, Audit Chamber for financial cases, Constitutional Chamber for judicial review cases, and Administrative Chamber for civil cases; there is no legal limit to the number of members.

## Administrative divisions

Main article: [Departments of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Ivory_Coast)

For administrative purposes, Ivory Coast is divided into 58 [departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_\(subnational_entity\)), each headed by a [prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect) appointed by the central government. There are 196 [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_\(subnational_entity\)), each headed by an elected mayor, plus the city of [Abidjan](21371767.md) with ten mayors.

The 58 departments (*départements*, singular - *département*) are listed in the article [Departments of Ivory Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Ivory_Coast).
