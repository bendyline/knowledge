**Beit Jinn** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بيت جن), also known as **Bayt Jin**, **Beit Jann** or **Beyt Jene**, is a village in southern [Syria](7515849.md), administratively part of the [Rif Dimashq Governorate](4521946.md), located southwest of [Damascus](8914.md) on the foothills of [Mount Hermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hermon). According to the [Syria Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Statistics_\(Syria\)), Beit Jinn had a population of 2,846 at the 2004 census.[^cite_note-CBS-1] Its inhabitants are predominantly [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam).[^cite_note-\:0-2]

The village is the administrative center of the Beit Jinn subdistrict, which consists of nine villages, with a combined population of 15,668.[^cite_note-CBS-1] The subdistrict has Sunni population and contains a Druze religious shrine.[^cite_note-\:0-2] Nearby localities include [Arnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnah) to the north, [Darbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darbal) to the northeast, [Mazraat Beit Jinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazraat_Beit_Jinn) to the east, [Harfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harfa) to the southeast, and [Hader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hader,_Quneitra_Governorate) to the southwest. The [Nahr al-Awaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awaj) river (generally identified with the biblical [Pharpar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharpar)) passes near the town.

## History

Beit Jinn was visited by [Andalusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) geographer [Ibn Jubayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Jubayr) in the late 12th century, during [Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) rule. He noted that it was "a village between [Darayyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darayya) and [Baniyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banias) lying among the hills."[^cite_note-3]

In 1838, during [Ottoman](22278.md) rule, [Eli Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Smith) noted Beit Jinn's population as being predominantly [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim).[^cite_note-4]

### 2017 Beit Jinn offensive

In December 2017, according to the [Syrian Observatory for Human Rights](34029669.md), control of the village was a source of fighting between the [Syrian Arab Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Arab_Armed_Forces) and the [al-Qaeda](1921.md)-affiliated Levant Liberation Committee.[^cite_note-5] Following the [Beit Jinn offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Jinn_offensive) of late 2017, the Syrian government took control of the area. The local rebels surrendered and were allowed to leave.[^cite_note-6]
