# Sanriku



The Sanriku region of Japan

**Sanriku** (三陸), sometimes known as **Rikushū** (陸州), lies on the northeastern side of the island of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu), corresponding to today's [Aomori](61732.md), [Iwate](183477.md) and parts of [Miyagi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyagi_Prefecture) and has a long history.

The 36 bays of this irregular coastline tend to amplify the destructiveness of tsunami waves which reach the shores of Sanriku.[^cite_note-1]

## History

On January 19, 1869, in the aftermath of the [Boshin War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boshin_War), the provinces of [Mutsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutsu_Province) and [Dewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewa_Province) were divided. Mutsu was split into new five provinces: [Rikuō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutsu_Province_\(1868\)) (also read *Mutsu*), [Rikuchū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikuch%C5%AB_Province), [Rikuzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikuzen_Province), [Iwashiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwashiro_Province) and [Iwaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwaki_Province_\(1868\)). The first three of these collectively known as the "Three Riku", or *Sanriku*, with san (三) meaning "three."[^cite_note-2]

The new provinces became quickly obsolete in July 1871 when the [abolition of the han system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_han_system) divided Japan into its present [prefectures](74517.md) that became the sole divisions used by the government. However, the label lives on in common usages such as the [Sanriku Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanriku_Coast), which extends along Japan's Pacific coastline from Aomori in the north down to the [Oshika Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshika_Peninsula) in Miyagi.[^cite_note-3]
