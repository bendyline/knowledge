The **regions of Greece** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): περιφέρειες, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek): *periféreies*) are the country's thirteen second-level [administrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Greece) entities, counting [decentralized administrations of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_administrations_of_Greece) as first-level. Regions are divided into [regional units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_units_of_Greece), known as [prefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_of_Greece) until 2011.

## History

The current regions were established in July 1986 (the presidential decree officially establishing them was signed in 1987), by decision of the interior minister, [Menios Koutsogiorgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menios_Koutsogiorgas), as second-level administrative entities, complementing the [prefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_of_Greece) (Law 1622/1986).[^cite_note-1] Before 1986, there was a traditional division into broad [historical–geographical regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_regions_of_Greece) (γεωγραφικά διαμερίσματα), which, however, was often arbitrary; not all of the pre-1986 traditional historical-geographic regions had official administrative bodies. Although the post-1986 regions were mostly based on the earlier divisions, they are usually smaller and, in a few cases, do not overlap with the traditional definitions: for instance, the region of [Western Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Greece), which had no previous analogue, comprises territory belonging to the [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese) peninsula and the traditional region of [Central Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Greece_\(geographic_region\)).

As part of a [decentralization](49139.md) process inspired by Interior Minister [Alekos Papadopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alekos_Papadopoulos), they were accorded more powers in the 1997 [Kapodistrias reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapodistrias_reform) of local and regional government. They were transformed into fully separate entities by the 2010 [Kallikratis Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kallikratis_Plan) (Law 3852/2010), which entered into effect on 1 January 2011. In the 2011 changes, the government-appointed general secretary (γενικός γραμματέας) was replaced with a popularly elected regional governor (περιφερειάρχης) and a regional council (περιφερειακό συμβούλιο) with five-year terms. Many powers of the prefectures, which were also abolished or reformed into [regional units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_units_of_Greece), were transferred to the region level. The regional organs of the central government were in turn replaced by seven [decentralized administrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_administrations_of_Greece), which group from one to three regions under a government-appointed general secretary.

Bordering the region of [Central Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Macedonia) there is one autonomous region, [Mount Athos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Athos) (*Agion Oros*, or "Holy Mountain"), an [autonomous monastic community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastic_community_of_Mount_Athos) under Greek sovereignty. It is located on the easternmost of the three large peninsulas protruding into the [Aegean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea) from the [Chalkidiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalkidiki) peninsula.
