# Ambassadors of the United States



President [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) with a group of ambassadors in March 1961

**Ambassadors of the United States** are persons nominated by the [president](24113.md) to serve as the United States' [diplomatic representatives](82433.md) to foreign nations, international organizations, and as [ambassadors-at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador-at-large). Under [Article II, Section 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appointments_Clause) of the [U.S. Constitution](31644.md), their appointment must be [confirmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent#United_States) by the [United States Senate](24909346.md);[^cite_note-1] while an ambassador may be [appointed during a recess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recess_appointment), they can serve only until the end of the next session of Congress, unless subsequently confirmed.[^cite_note-recess1-2]

Ambassadors are the highest-ranking diplomats of the U.S. and are usually based at the [embassy](8970.md) in the host country. They are under the jurisdiction of the [Department of State](31975.md) and answer directly to the [secretary of state](32293.md); however, ambassadors serve "[at the pleasure of the President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At_the_pleasure_of_the_President)", meaning they can be dismissed at any time. Appointments change regularly for various reasons, such as reassignment or retirement.

An ambassador may be a career [Foreign Service officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Service_officer) (career diplomat – CD) or a [political appointee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_appointments_in_the_United_States) (PA). In most cases, career foreign service officers serve a tour of approximately three years per ambassadorship, whereas political appointees customarily tender their resignations upon the inauguration of a new president. From 1974 to 2025, between 57% and 74% of ambassadors were career diplomats.[^cite_note-3]

The [State Department](31975.md) publishes a monthly list of ambassadors.[^cite_note-StateDept1-4] A listing by country of past [chiefs of mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_mission) is maintained by the [Office of the Historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Historian) of the U.S. Department of State,[^cite_note-StateDept3-5] along with the names and appointment dates of past and present [ambassadors-at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador-at-large)[^cite_note-StateDept4-6] and mission to [international organizations](18201224.md).[^cite_note-StateDept5-7]

## Special envoys, representatives, coordinators, and other officeholders

Main article: [United States special envoy](48752185.md)

These diplomatic officials report directly to the [Secretary of State](32293.md). Many oversee a portfolio not restricted to one nation, often an overall goal, and are not usually subject to [Senate](24909346.md) confirmation.[^cite_note-AFSA_Special_Envoys-38][^cite_note-List_of_Bureaus-34][^cite_note-39] Unlike the State Department offices and diplomats listed in other sections of this article, the offices and special envoys, representatives or coordinators listed in this section are created and staffed by direction of top federal executive administrators – primarily U.S. Presidents and Secretaries of State – whose political or organizational management philosophies may not be shared by their successors.[^cite_note-WashDiplo1-40][^cite_note-CNNaug2017-41][^cite_note-WashDiplo2-42] As such, many of these positions may go unfilled upon assumption of office by successor presidential administrations, with their offices sometimes merged with or subsumed into other offices, or abolished altogether.

- [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan): According to the U.S. State Department, "The United States and the Kingdom of Bhutan have not established formal diplomatic relations; however, the two governments have informal and cordial relations."

  [^cite_note-bhutan-43]

  &#x20;Informal contact with the nation of Bhutan is maintained through the U.S. Embassy in New Delhi.

  [^cite_note-bhutan-43]

- [Iran](14653.md): On April 7, 1980, the United States broke diplomatic relations with Iran after the 1979 [Iranian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_revolution).

  [^cite_note-iran-44]

  &#x20;On April 24, 1981, the Swiss government assumed representation of U.S. interests in [Tehran](57654.md), and Algeria assumed representation of Iranian interests in the United States.

  [^cite_note-45]

  &#x20;Currently, Iranian interests in the United States are represented by the government of Pakistan. The [U.S. Department of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_State) named Iran a "[State Sponsor of Terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Sponsors_of_Terrorism_\(U.S._list\))" on January 19, 1984.

  [^cite_note-state2010-46]

- [North Korea](21255.md): The Democratic People's Republic of Korea is not on friendly terms with the United States, and while talks between the two countries are ongoing, there is no exchange of ambassadors. [Sweden](5058739.md) functions as Protective Power for the United States in [Pyongyang](47948.md) and performs limited consular responsibilities for U.S. citizens in North Korea.

  [^cite_note-47]

- [Syria](7515849.md): On February 6, 2012, the United States suspended operations at its embassy in [Damascus](8914.md).

  [^cite_note-48]

  &#x20;On May 5, 2014, the United States recognized the [National Coalition of Syrian Revolutionary and Opposition Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coalition_of_Syrian_Revolutionary_and_Opposition_Forces) as the [foreign mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_mission) of Syria.

  [^cite_note-49]

- [Taiwan](25734.md): With the [normalization](277880.md#Normalization) of relations with the People's Republic of China in 1979, the United States has not maintained official diplomatic relations with Taiwan. Relations between Taiwan and the United States are maintained through an unofficial instrumentality, the [American Institute in Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_in_Taiwan), functions as a *de facto* embassy, performing most consular functions and staffed by [Foreign Service officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Service_officer).

  [^cite_note-50]
