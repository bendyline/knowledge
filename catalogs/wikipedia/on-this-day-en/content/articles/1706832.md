**His Majesty's Naval Base Clyde** (**HMNB Clyde**) is the [Royal Navy](26061.md)'s main [shore establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shore_establishment) in [Scotland](26994.md). It is home to the [United Kingdom](31717.md)'s fleet of [nuclear submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_submarine), including [attack submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_submarine) and [ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarine). The main part of the base, officially named **HMS *Neptune*** and commonly known as **Faslane**, lies on [Faslane Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faslane_\(bay\)) in [Gare Loch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_Loch), an inlet of the [Firth of Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firth_of_Clyde). The base also includes the [Royal Naval Armaments Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Armaments_Depot_Coulport) at Coulport on [Loch Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loch_Long), which stores, maintains and issues [Trident missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trident_missile).

## History

Faslane Port was first constructed in the [Second World War](32927.md). During the 1960s, the [British Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Government) began negotiating the [Polaris Sales Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polaris_Sales_Agreement) with the United States regarding the purchase of a [Polaris missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polaris_missile) system to fire British-built nuclear weapons from five specially constructed submarines. In the end, only four were constructed: [HMS *Resolution*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Resolution_\(S22\)), [HMS *Repulse*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Repulse_\(S23\)), [HMS *Renown*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Renown_\(S26\)) and [HMS *Revenge*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Revenge_\(S27\)). These four submarines were permanently based at Faslane.[^cite_note-2]

Faslane itself was chosen to host these vessels at the height of the [Cold War](325329.md) because of its geographic position, which forms a [bastion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastion_\(naval\)) on the relatively secluded but deep and easily navigable [Gare Loch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_Loch) and [Firth of Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firth_of_Clyde) on the west coast of Scotland. This position provides for rapid and stealthy access through the [North Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Channel_\(British_Isles\)) to the submarine patrolling areas in the [North Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic), through the [GIUK gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIUK_gap) to the [Norwegian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Sea). At the time it was chosen, the location was also close to the American SSBN base at [Holy Loch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Loch), which operated between 1961 and 1992. One boat was always on patrol at any given time.[^cite_note-3]

In 1971, the base was home to the 3rd Submarine Squadron of Nuclear Fleet and Diesel Patrol Submarines, "the fighters", and the [10th Submarine Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Submarine_Squadron_\(United_Kingdom\)), consisting of the four Polaris submarines, "the bombers".[^cite_note-4]

On 19 March 2026, a 34-year-old Iranian man and a 31-year-old Romanian woman were arrested at the base after being refused permission to enter. Their intentions were unclear.[^cite_note-5]

## Role and operations



HMS *Vigilant* alongside at Faslane



HMS *Astute* arriving at Faslane for the first time

HMNB Clyde lies on the eastern shore of [Gare Loch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_Loch) in [Argyll and Bute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argyll_and_Bute), to the north of the [Firth of Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firth_of_Clyde) and 25 mi (40 km) west of [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow). The [submarine base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_base) encompasses a number of separate sites, the primary two being:

- Faslane, 25 miles from Glasgow;

- [RNAD Coulport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNAD_Coulport), beside [Loch Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loch_Long), 2 mi (3.2 km) west of Faslane by air, approximately 8 mi (13 km) by road or 19 mi (31 km) by sea.

Faslane is also a [Defence Equipment and Support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Equipment_and_Support) site, operated in dual site organisation with Great Harbour, [Greenock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenock), by [Babcock Marine and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babcock_International_Group),[^cite_note-23] and managed by [Serco Denholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serco_Denholm).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]



[USS *Arleigh Burke*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Arleigh_Burke) departing HMNB Clyde

The naval shore establishment at Faslane is HMS *Neptune*. Naval personnel appointed to the base who do not belong to a seagoing vessel make up ship's company. Both Gare Loch and Loch Long are [sea lochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_loch) extending northwards from the Firth of Clyde. The base serves as home base to Britain's fleet of [*Vanguard*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguard-class_submarine) nuclear-powered and nuclear-armed submarines, as well as conventionally armed [nuclear-powered submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSN_\(hull_classification_symbol\)), supported by the [43 Commando Fleet Protection Group Royal Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43_Commando_Fleet_Protection_Group_Royal_Marines).[^cite_note-27]

In command of HMNB Clyde is the Naval Base Commander (Clyde), [Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(Royal_Navy\)) Sharon Malkin. The base is home to a number of lodger units, including [Flag Officer Scotland and Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_Officer_Scotland_and_Northern_Ireland) (who is also [Rear Admiral Submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_Admiral_Submarines)), the Northern Diving Group and the Scottish headquarters of the [Ministry of Defence Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_Police). It is base to 3,000 service personnel, 800 of their families and 4,000 civilian workers, largely from [Babcock Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babcock_Marine), forming a major part of the economy of Argyll and Bute and [West Dunbartonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Dunbartonshire).[^cite_note-28]

In 2018, the Secretary of State for Scotland at the time, [David Mundell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Mundell), said: "The UK's entire submarine fleet will be based at Faslane by 2020. This will reinforce Scotland's vital role in protecting our country, and guarantee skilled, secure jobs on the Clyde for years to come."[^cite_note-29]

## Safety and accidents at Faslane

Exercise Evening Star is the annual test of the emergency response routines to a nuclear weapon accident at Faslane. It is conducted by the [Office for Nuclear Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_for_Nuclear_Regulation). In 2011 the test failed as "a number of command and control aspects of the exercise were not considered to have been adequately demonstrated".[^cite_note-ONR-30]

In 2013–14 there were 99 radiation accidents concerning nuclear reactors, and 6 with nuclear weapons. These are the highest numbers for at least six years. The [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence) (MoD) maintains that there was no risk to the public as most of them were minor accidents. The SNP defence spokesman, [Angus Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angus_Robertson), called the figures "totally shocking".[^cite_note-31]

In 2015 the MoD argued that it was "entirely misleading" to focus only on the number of incidents, because they include "very minor issues such as the failure to fill out the correct form before painting works began." Indeed, the MoD stated that this "rigorous system shows how seriously MoD takes all aspects of nuclear safety, ensuring lessons are learned, and we can be clear that none of the events in the reports posed any risk to the health of our personnel, or to any members of the public." Indeed, one of the recorded events was the incorrect labeling of an empty pallet. Minor events were reported and investigated so that performance could be continuously improved. "This comprehensive, independent recording process allows Clyde to maintain a robust reporting culture, undertake learning from experience and to take early corrective action," the Defence Minister, Philip Dunne, told MPs.[^cite_note-heraldscotland-20150301-32]

In August 2025, Defence Procurement Minister [Maria Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Eagle) announced that a Category A event had occurred at the base between January and April 2025, defined as an incident with "actual or high potential for radioactive release to the environment". She would not provide details for national security reasons. She also stated two Category B events ("contained radiation exposure") and 100 Category C and D failures had occurred between 22 April 2024 and 22 April 2025. All the events were at [International Nuclear and Radiological Event Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Nuclear_and_Radiological_Event_Scale) level one and no staff or public were harmed.[^cite_note-sky-20250814-33][^cite_note-nei-20250819-34]

## Anti-nuclear demonstrations

Wikinews logo

Wikinews has related news:&#x20;

- [ Nuclear weapons protest at Faslane leads to 47 arrests](https://en.wikinews.org/wiki/Nuclear%20weapons%20protest%20at%20Faslane%20leads%20to%2047%20arrests)

Given the presence of these nuclear capable missiles, Faslane has attracted demonstrations by [Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Nuclear_Disarmament) and other Scottish [pressure groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy_group), including [Trident Ploughshares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trident_Ploughshares). Since 1982, a permanent [peace camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faslane_Peace_Camp) is outside the base gates, where there are frequent demonstrations and regular Wednesday protests. The presence of Faslane is also an issue in [Scottish politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Scotland).[^cite_note-35]

The [Scottish National Party](28887.md) (SNP), the [Scottish Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Socialist_Party), and the [Scottish Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Greens) all oppose the deployment of nuclear weapons although the SNP have made assurances that they would retain the base for the servicing of conventionally armed and conventionally powered naval units. Members of those parties and indeed some from the [Labour Party](19279158.md) are often present at rallies. Also, some former independents, such as [George Galloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Galloway) attend rallies outside Faslane.[^cite_note-36]



Police dismantling a [blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faslane_365) of protesters from York at the south gate of the Faslane base

### Faslane 365

The Faslane 365 campaign was a one-year protest at the base. It was a civil resistance initiative to apply critical public pressure for the disarmament of Britain's nuclear weapons.[^cite_note-Faslane365-37]

The campaign was launched in September 2006, with the first protest action commencing on 1 October 2006 carried out by a campaigning group of women associated with protests at [Greenham Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenham_Common). It formally ended with a "Big Blockade" on 1 October 2007.[^cite_note-38]

131 blockading groups took part in Faslane 365 and 1150 arrests were made.[^cite_note-Faslane365-37]
