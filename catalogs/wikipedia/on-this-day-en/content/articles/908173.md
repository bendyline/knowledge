The **Antonov An-26** ([NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name): **Curl**, nicknamed **Nastia** in the USSR) is a twin-engined [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) [regional airliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_airliner) and [military transport aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transport_aircraft), designed and produced in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) from 1969 to 1986.[^cite_note-Gordon_An-24-3] It is the third member of the Antonov An-24 family, coming after the [An-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-24) and [An-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-30), while preceding the [An-32](908204.md) and cancelled [An-132](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov/Taqnia_An-132). The An-26 is still license-produced in [China](5405.md) as the modern version of the [Xi'an Y-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an_Y-7). Early versions of the Y-7 utilized the An-24 design.

## Development

While the [An-24T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-24) tactical transport had proved successful in supporting Soviet troops in austere locations, its ventral loading hatch restricted the handling of cargo, and in particular vehicles, and made it less effective than hoped in parachuting men and supplies.[^cite_note-Gordon_An-24p2741-4] As a result, interest in a version with a retractable cargo ramp increased, and the [Antonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov) design bureau decided in 1966 to begin development on the new An-26 derivative, in advance of an official order. The cargo ramp was based on that design and allowed the cargo deck to be sealed and pressurised in flight. When loading cargo, it could either be lowered to allow vehicles to be driven in, or slid beneath the aircraft's fuselage, so that cargo could be loaded straight in off a truck bed. In March 1968, the OKB received official permission to begin development.[^cite_note-Gordon_An-24p412-5] Particular attention was given to the military mission, and the majority of early An-26 production was delivered to the [VTA (voyenno-transportnaya aviatsiya)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Transport_Aviation).[^cite_note-Gordon_An-24-3]

Using the majority of the An-24 airframe, it has high-set cantilevered wings, wing-mounted twin [Ivchenko AI-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivchenko_AI-24) turboprops with a turbojet engine in the starboard nacelle for use as an [auxiliary power unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_power_unit) and also for extra take-off thrust, plus long main undercarriage legs. The An-26 includes military equipment, such as [tip-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tip-up_seat) paratroop [canvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canvas) seats, an overhead traveling hoist, bulged observation windows and parachute static line attachment cables. It can be configured in 20–30 minutes from the troop transport or freight mission to the [medical evacuation](146346.md) role with up to 24 stretchers fitted.[^cite_note-Taylor1974-6]

The An-26 made its public debut at the 27th [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show) at [Le Bourget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Bourget) where the second prototype, CCCP-26184 (c/n00202), was shown in the static aircraft park.

The An-26 is also manufactured without a license agreement[^cite_note-Gordon_An-24p58-7] in China by the Xian Aircraft factory as the **Y-14**, later changed to be included in the [Xi'an Y-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an_Y-7) series.[^cite_note-Gordon_An-24p58-7]

## Operational history

The An-26 has a secondary bomber role with underwing bomb racks. The racks are attached to the fuselage in front of and behind the rear landing gear. In the bombing role it was extensively used by the [Vietnam People's Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_People's_Air_Force) during the [Cambodian–Vietnamese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War) (by modifying the cargo bay with a bomb release system, allowing captured South Vietnamese [Mark 82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_82_bomb) bombs to roll out of the cargo bay) and [Sudanese Air Force](550818.md) during the [Second Sudanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sudanese_Civil_War) and the [War in Darfur](5043637.md).[^cite_note-8] Russian Forces have also trained with the An-26 as a bomber.[^cite_note-9] In 1977, the Afghan Air Force received the An-26 aircraft and in 1986,[^cite_note-\:1-10] they had 36 of them which were used for [airborne assaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_assault) conducted by the [Afghan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_Afghanistan)'s commando and parachute battalions[^cite_note-11] and two military transport squadrons.[^cite_note-12]

One An-26 was involved in the [Purulia Incident in 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purulia_arms_drop_case) in which arms were dropped in the Purulia district of [West Bengal](34040.md), [India](14533.md). The reason behind the drop is not disclosed to the public due to national security.[^cite_note-Gordon_An-24p2741-4]

## Accidents and incidents



[Sudan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_Air_Force) Antonov An-26-100 crash-landed in 1997 at the airstrip of [Gogrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gogrial). The plane was hit by [SPLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_People's_Liberation_Army)-fire and had to make an emergency landing.

### 1970s

- 23 May 1976: An Aeroflot An-26 (CCCP-26567) crashed short of the runway near [Teply Klyuch Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teply_Klyuch_Airport), [Russia](25391.md).

  [^cite_note-88]

- 14 July 1977: A [National Air Force of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Air_Force_of_Angola) An-26 was shot down by [UNITA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNITA) rebels near [Cuangar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuangar); 30 people on board died.

  [^cite_note-89]

- 18 August 1977: An Aeroflot An-26 (CCCP-26536) landed hard at [Ust-Kuyga Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ust-Kuyga_Airport) due to pilot error; no casualties.

  [^cite_note-90]

- 9 December 1978: An Aeroflot An-26 (CCCP-26547) lost control and crashed shortly after takeoff from Cherskiy Airport due to a shifted load; all seven on board died. The cargo had not been secured properly.

  [^cite_note-91]

- 26 March 1979: Aeroflot Flight 37293, an An-26 (CCCP-26569), struck a wooded hillside near [Baykit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baykit), Russia, four of 12 on board died.

  [^cite_note-92]

  [^cite_note-93]

### 1980s

- 12 December 1980: A [Soviet Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces) An-26 was shot down by guerrilla forces in [Angola](701.md) near the border with [Namibia](21292.md); five people on board died.

  [^cite_note-94]

- 23 December 1981: Aeroflot Flight 22237, an An-26 (CCCP-26505), crashed while on approach to Severo-Yeniseisk Airport in poor weather during an attempted go-around after descending too soon; two of seven on board died. The flight mechanic and navigator were drunk.

  [^cite_note-95]

- 14 January 1982: An [Ethiopian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Air_Force) An-26 crashed near [Addis Ababa](52593.md); 73 Ethiopian, Libyan and Cuban troops died. This accident remains the deadliest involving the An-26.

  [^cite_note-96]

- 11 February 1982: Vietnam People's Air Force An-26 *26264* was shot down by two Royal Thai Air Force Northrop F-5Es and crashed in a rice field near Prachinburi, Thailand, during an intelligence-gathering mission from Phnom Penh; reportedly, one of 13 on board died.

  [^cite_note-97]

- 15 March 1982: A [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy) An-26 (MSN 6805) crashed shortly after a night-time take off from [Anapa Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anapa_Airport) when the flaps were retracted prematurely; all nine people on board died.

  [^cite_note-98]

- 29 November 1982: a [TAAG Angola Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAAG_Angola_Airlines) An-26 (D2-TAB) flew into a mountain in the [Bibala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibala) region; all 15 people on board died.

  [^cite_note-99]

- 23 December 1982: An Aeroflot An-26 (CCCP-26627) crashed on takeoff from Rostov Airport; all 16 on board died. The aircraft was overloaded.

  [^cite_note-100]

- 6 May 1983: a [Soviet Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces) An-26 hit trees on a night-time approach in heavy snow as it was trying to land at [Klyuchi, Klyuchevsky District, Altai Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klyuchi,_Klyuchevsky_District,_Altai_Krai); 33 of the 37 people on board died.

  [^cite_note-101]

- 3 July 1984: A [Peruvian Air Force](1040948.md) An-26 (FAP-377) crashed into mountains northeast of [Lima](85423.md); all five people on board died.

  [^cite_note-102]

- 22 January 1985: a [Soviet Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces) An-26 operating in [Afghanistan](737.md) exceeded the maximum allowable speed and broke apart; all eight people on board died.

  [^cite_note-103]

- 3 May 1985: Soviet Air Force An-26 *101 red* (callsign "CCCP-26492") collided in mid-air with [Aeroflot Flight 8381](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot_Flight_8381), a Tupolev Tu-134, due to ATC errors; all 94 on board both aircraft died.

- 4 September 1985: A [Bakhtar Afghan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhtar_Afghan_Airlines) An-26 (YA-BAM) [was shot down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Bakhtar_Afghan_Airlines_Antonov_An-26_shootdown) by a [SAM](182664.md) near [Kandahar](17260.md); all 52 people on board died.

- 30 March 1986: A [Mozambique Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Mozambique) An-26 crashed while trying to land at [Pemba Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pemba_Airport_\(Mozambique\)). All three crew and 41 of the 46 passengers died.

- 6 December 1986: a [Hungarian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Air_Force) An-26 (MSN 2210) crashed after take-off from [Budapest-Ferihegy Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest-Ferihegy_Airport), most likely because of icing. Four of the five people on board died.

  [^cite_note-104]

- 9 February 1987: an [Afghan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force) An-26 was shot down by [Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) guerrillas shortly after takeoff from [Kabul International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_International_Airport); all 36 people on board died.

  [^cite_note-105]

- 19 February 1987: a [Soviet Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Forces) An-26 crashed in fog near [Stadnitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadnitsa) while attempting to land at [Vinnytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia); all nine people on board died.

  [^cite_note-106]

- 6 March 1987: an [Aeroflot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroflot) An-26 (CCCP-26007) struck a mountain near [Almaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almaty) after failing to change heading; all nine people on board died.

## Specifications

*Data from* Jane's All The World's Aircraft 1988–89[^cite_note-Janes_88_p222-3-197]

**General characteristics**

- **Crew:** five (two pilots, one radio operator, one flight engineer, one navigator)

- **Capacity:** 40 passengers / 5,500 kg (12,100 lb)

- **Length:** 23.8 m (78 ft 1 in)

- **Wingspan:** 29.3 m (96 ft 2 in)

- **Height:** 8.58 m (28 ft 2 in)

- **Wing area:** 74.98 m

  2

  &#x20;(807.1 sq ft)

- **[Aspect ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio_\(aeronautics\)):** 11.7\:1

- **Empty weight:** 15,020 kg (33,113 lb)

- **Max takeoff weight:** 24,000 kg (52,911 lb)

- **Powerplant:** 2 × [Progress AI-24VT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_AI-24VT) Turboprop engines, 2,103 kW (2,820 hp) each

- **Powerplant:** 1 × [Tumansky Ru19A-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumansky_Ru19A-300?action=edit\&redlink=1) Turbojet booster / APU, 7.85 kN (1,760 lbf) thrust

- **Propellers:** 4-bladed Constant speed metal bladed propellers, 3.90 m (12 ft 10 in) diameter

**Performance**

- **Cruise speed:** 440 km/h (270 mph, 240 kn)

- **Range:** 2,500 km (1,600 mi, 1,300 nmi) with maximum fuel

- **Range with max payload:** 1,100 km (680 mi)

- **Service ceiling:** 7,500 m (24,600 ft)

- **Rate of climb:** 8 m/s (1,600 ft/min)
