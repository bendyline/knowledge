The ***Trump*-class battleship** is a proposed [nuclear-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion#Military_nuclear_ships) [guided-missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided-missile) [warship](263960.md) for the [United States Navy](20518076.md), announced by U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) in December 2025.[^cite_note-CNN-2025-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-USNI-2025-6] The class is also known as **BBG(X)**[^cite_note-fn1-10] or **BBGN** in Navy documents,[^cite_note-BBG(X)-1][^cite_note-media.defense.gov-11] and is intended to initially consist of the [lead ship](855210.md) **USS *Defiant* (BBG-1)** and an as-yet unnamed other vessel. The class is envisioned as adding a [nuclear-capable cruise missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear-capable_cruise_missile) option to the U.S. Navy surface fleet, along with the non-nuclear [Conventional Prompt Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_Prompt_Strike) hypersonic attack missile, as offensive weapons.[^cite_note-12]

The [Trump administration](78298108.md) has stated its intention to revitalize [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding) in the [United States](3434750.md) alongside the construction of the *Trump* class. Analysts have expressed skepticism about the *Trump*-class, citing its lack of funding, unprecedented design, and high development costs. Its classification as a [battleship](4054.md) is debated, as it lacks the heavy armor and large-caliber [naval guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_gun) typical of historical battleships. The naming of the class after an incumbent president has also broken traditional conventions.

## History

### Background

A ship with 9 large guns in three triple turrets bearing hull number 63.

[USS *Missouri*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Missouri_\(BB-63\)) in 1991 after returning from the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War). The [*Iowa*-class battleships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa-class_battleship) were constructed during [World War II](32927.md) and were the last ships operated by the U.S. Navy to be classified as battleships.

A ship with two turrets where their gun barrels are concealed completely. The ship's sides are sloping inwards towards the top.

[USS *Zumwalt*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Zumwalt) in 2016. The [*Zumwalt*-class destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer) are the largest [surface combatants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_combatant) currently operated by the U.S. Navy and were designed in part to replace [gunfire support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunfire_support) and other capabilities of the *Iowa* class.

The U.S. Navy has not had a [battleship](4054.md) in commission since the retirement of the last [*Iowa*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa-class_battleship) battleship [USS *Missouri*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Missouri_\(BB-63\)) in 1992.[^cite_note-13] There have been no plans for new ones since the cancellation of the [*Montana* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana-class_battleship) in 1943.[^cite_note-14]

The retirement of the *Iowa*-class led to a [battleship retirement debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battleship_retirement_debate) on how the Navy should replace their capabilities. The [*Zumwalt*-class destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zumwalt-class_destroyer) was developed to replace their [naval gunfire support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_gunfire_support) function, but the class was canceled after only three ships were constructed.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The Navy also launched studies into a Future Surface Combatant (FSC) to replace the [*Ticonderoga*-class cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticonderoga-class_cruiser)—which will reach the end of their service lives in the 2020s—as well as older flights of the [*Arleigh Burke*-class destroyer](197341.md).[^cite_note-17] The FSC evolved into the Large Surface Combatant (LSC) program, which became the [DDG(X)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDG\(X\)). The DDG(X) program office was established in June 2021.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

Rear Admiral [Derek Trinque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Trinque?action=edit\&redlink=1) revealed in January 2026 that the DDG(X) hull was too small for the desired number of VLS for [Conventional Prompt Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_Prompt_Strike) (CPS) missiles plus a gun mount. The U.S. Navy considered building two DDG(X) variants. However, it pivoted to a larger vessel capable of accommodating both after the Trump administration showed interest in a modern battleship, which became the *Trump* class.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Vice Admiral [Brendan McLane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_McLane), commander of Naval Surface Force, said in February 2026 that the Flight III *Arleigh Burke* class could no longer accommodate new systems.[^cite_note-22]

### Development

Donald Trump announces plans for a "Golden Fleet" in December 2025

Donald Trump announces plans for a "Golden Fleet" in December 2025

On 22 December 2025, U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) announced the *Trump* class, with two ships to be initially constructed of 10 planned, and eventual plans for "between 20 and 25" as part of a "Golden Fleet".[^cite_note-CNN-2025-7][^cite_note-BBC-2025-23] The first ship is planned to be named USS *Defiant* (BBG-1).[^cite_note-24] [John C. Phelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Phelan), then-[U.S. Secretary of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Secretary_of_the_Navy), said the ships would carry conventional guns and [nuclear-armed cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear-armed_cruise_missile).[^cite_note-25] Trump said the ships would be built at the [Hanwha Philly Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanwha_Philly_Shipyard) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Pennsylvania](23332.md), which is owned by [South Korean](27019.md) [conglomerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\(company\)) [Hanwha Group](32047031.md).[^cite_note-cnbc251223-26]

The announcement of the class came amid warnings by U.S. officials that [Chinese shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding#Worldwide_shipbuilding_industry) has surpassed the United States in capacity and output, and is part of the [second Trump administration](78298108.md)'s goal to enlarge the U.S. Navy and revitalize the U.S. shipbuilding industry.[^cite_note-BBC-2025-23][^cite_note-27]

[Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense) officials said the BBG(X) program will incorporate technology from the DDG(X) program and replace it.[^cite_note-28]

Phelan helped conceive the class to gain favor with President Trump but was ousted in April 2026. The administration did not state a reason for Phelan's removal. *[Politico](9172097.md)* and *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that anonymous DoD officials said that [Secretary of Defense](43998.md) [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md) and [Deputy Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Defense) [Steve Feinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Feinberg) had grown frustrated with his advocacy of the expensive battleships, which they felt was impeding construction of the smaller and cheaper warships they prioritized, such as [uncrewed surface vessels](7808557.md). His ouster was thought to represent a setback for the class.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-NYT_2026-04-23-30] The *Times* reported that Phelan "struggled to come up with a plan to deliver the \[battleships] on the nearly impossible timeline that Mr. Trump had demanded", and told Trump that to attain the 2028 delivery schedule, the ships might need to be built in European shipyards, which Trump would not accept.[^cite_note-NYT_2026-04-23-30]

The ships will be nuclear-powered.[^cite_note-bbgn-31] On 11 May 2026, Acting Secretary of the Navy [Hung Cao](76401211.md) said three of the battleships, designated BBGNs to reflect their nuclear power, would be built in fiscal 2027 to 2031.[^cite_note-media.defense.gov-11][^cite_note-fn2-32]

In May 2026, construction of the first *Trump*-class battleship was expected to begin in 2028 for delivery in 2036.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

On 4 June 2026, the [House Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Armed_Services) voted to require the Secretary of the Navy to submit a report by March 2027 detailing acquisition plans for the *Trump* class, and for mitigation of impacts on [*Gerald R. Ford*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier) [aircraft carrier](2219.md) construction, as part of their FY2027 defense budget bill. The minority members on the Committee unsuccessfully tried to insert language cutting advance procurement funding for the battleships by \$1 billion.[^cite_note-Shelbourne-36]

On 5 August 2026, the [Congressional Budget Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Budget_Office) (CBO) estimated that the construction of 15 *Trump*-class battleships would cost \$275 billion, with the lead ship USS *Defiant* costing \$23.4 billion and the following ships averaging \$18 billion.[^cite_note-CBO-AUG26-2][^cite_note-37]

### Planned armament

Navy officials have said ships of the class are to include a main battery of a [Surface Launch Cruise Missile-Nuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_Launch_Cruise_Missile-Nuclear) (SLCM-N) system, a 12-cell [Conventional Prompt Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_Prompt_Strike) (CPS) [hypersonic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_missile) system, and a 128-cell [Mark 41 vertical launching system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_41_vertical_launching_system) (VLS).[^cite_note-38] A [secondary battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_armament) is to consist of a 32-megajoule [railgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railgun), two [5-inch/62-caliber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-inch/62_caliber_Mark_45_gun) (127 mm) guns, and a pair of 300 or 600-kilowatt [laser weapons](4714516.md). A [defensive battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-defense) is planned with two [RAM launchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116), four [Mark 38 30 mm machine gun systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_38_30_mm_machine_gun_system), four ODIN lasers, and two [anti-drone](38212231.md) systems. The ship is to have an enclosed hangar for [VTOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTOL) aircraft such as [helicopters](8286923.md), the [V-22 Osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-22_Osprey), and future vertical-lift manned and [unmanned aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_aircraft).[^cite_note-Primary-3] The lasers, nuclear cruise missiles, and the railgun could be added to the base design.[^cite_note-USNI-2025-6]

[General Atomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Atomics) said in January 2026 that it was talking with the Navy about the "role of railguns".[^cite_note-39]

Directed-energy weapons such as megawatt-class lasers could be installed as defensive weapons; some current U.S. warships have a 60-kilowatt [High Energy Laser with Integrated Optical-dazzler and Surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Energy_Laser_with_Integrated_Optical-dazzler_and_Surveillance) (HELIOS) or less-powerful ODIN.[^cite_note-40]

On 14 May 2026, Acting Navy Secretary [Hung Cao](76401211.md) said during a [House Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Armed_Services) hearing that the ship is to have 200 VLS cells.[^cite_note-41]

## Reactions



Graphic representation of the weaponry that a *Trump*-class ship would carry

### Design

Analysts have expressed doubts that the ships will enter service because they have not been funded and their unique and unprecedented design will make development costly and slow.[^cite_note-suciu-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-Lamothe20251223-45]

The classification of the *Trump*-class ship as a battleship has been questioned. Mark Cancian with the [Center for Strategic and International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Strategic_and_International_Studies) (CSIS) wrote that the term has historically been used for warships with large guns, such as 16-inch guns, and [heavy armor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_armour), which the *Trump* class would lack, and that the ship's profile is more typical of a guided-missile "[battlecruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlecruiser)" like Russia's [*Kirov* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirov-class_battlecruiser).[^cite_note-CSIS-46]

According to the [U.S. Naval Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Naval_Institute), the definition of the term "battleship" has evolved over time, from wooden ships with many guns ([ship of the line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_the_line) \[of battle]), through to the 20th-century usage of high-caliber gunned ships with heavy armor. The ship is meant to survive in a fleet battle, and thus armor is not definitional to the core of the battleship. The importance of the large gun debate depends upon whether the gun is the most important component of the ship's firepower.[^cite_note-47] There was no effective change to ideas about the battleship after the Second World War until the advent of the guided missile.

[CNN](62028.md) analyst Stephen Collinson has stated that the procurement of the *Trump* class would likely revive the [battleship retirement debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battleship_retirement_debate).[^cite_note-48] Cancian has written that the ship "will never sail" as its high cost will prompt a "future administration \[to] cancel the program before the first ship hits the water".[^cite_note-CSIS-46]

### U.S. labor force

Some have questioned whether the United States has an adequate labor force to build the ships in American shipyards.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] This argument notes that during World War II, tens of thousands of men and women worked in shifts around the clock at each of the U.S. naval yards that produced the *Iowa*-class. More than 71,000 people were employed at the [Brooklyn Navy Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Navy_Yard) alone.[^cite_note-suciu-42] Cancian notes that U.S. shipbuilders were so [short of skilled labor](2364729.md#Labour_shortage) in 2025 that they had been increasing wages to attract workers from competing yards.[^cite_note-CSIS-46]

In January 2026, the [Congressional Research Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Research_Service) stated that the U.S. Navy intends to award contracts to multiple shipbuilders for the construction of a *Trump*-class battleship. The report identified three capable shipyards: [Bath Iron Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath_Iron_Works) and [Ingalls Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingalls_Shipbuilding), which have built every U.S. Navy cruiser and destroyer since 1985; and [Newport News Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News_Shipbuilding), which has specialized in the construction and overhauling of [nuclear-powered aircraft carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear-powered_aircraft_carrier).[^cite_note-crsIF13142-5]

Trump had previously indicated that the ships would be built at [Hanwha Philly Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanwha_Philly_Shipyard).[^cite_note-cnbc251223-26] As of January 2026, the yard has only ever built commercial vessels and lacks any experience with defense-related contracts.[^cite_note-51] After Hanwha acquired the financially struggling shipyard in 2024, in August 2025 it pledged an investment toward a \$5 billion infrastructure plan, with a goal of eventually building warships.[^cite_note-52] However, in May 2026, top Navy officials announced that they would most likely build the battleships at the Newport News shipyard, due to the battleships' nuclear propulsion; the [House Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Armed_Services) wrote legislation that suggested Dry Dock 12.[^cite_note-Shelbourne-36]

### Name

The *Trump*-class name would not follow [United States ship naming conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_ship_naming_conventions). American battleships have been named after U.S. states, with the exception of [USS *Kearsarge*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Kearsarge_\(BB-5\)), a [pre-dreadnought battleship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-dreadnought_battleship) ordered in 1895;[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] while names of presidents have been used for aircraft carriers before,[^cite_note-Lamothe20251223-45][^cite_note-Suciu-55] [naming a warship after a living person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._military_vessels_named_after_living_Americans), once rare, is no longer unusual in the United States.[^cite_note-Suciu-55] However, it is atypical for presidents to name things after themselves. The naming came in the [context of the recent addition of Trump's name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branding_of_United_States_government_programs_and_facilities_after_Donald_Trump) to the [Kennedy Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center) and the [U.S. Institute of Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Institute_of_Peace), as well as the new [Trump account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_account) and [Trump Gold Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_Gold_Card).[^cite_note-56]
