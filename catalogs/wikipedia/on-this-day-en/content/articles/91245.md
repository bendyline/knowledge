**Stafford County** is a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(United_States\)) located in the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_\(U.S._state\)) of [Virginia](32432.md). It is approximately 40 miles (64 km) south of [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) It is part of the [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia) region, and the D.C area. It is one of the fastest-growing and highest-income counties in America. As of the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population sits at 156,927.[^cite_note-USCensusEst2019-1] Its [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) is [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford,_Virginia).[^cite_note-GR6-2]

Located across the Rappahannock River from the City of [Fredericksburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredericksburg,_Virginia) and [Spotsylvania County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotsylvania_County,_Virginia), Stafford County is part of the [Washington-Arlington-Alexandria, DC-VA-MD-WV Metropolitan Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_metropolitan_area). In 2006, and again in 2009, Stafford was ranked by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine as the 11th [highest-income county in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest-income_counties_in_the_United_States). According to a Census Bureau report released in 2019, Stafford County is currently the sixth highest-income county in North America.

## History

For thousands of years, various native cultures succeeded each other in their territories along the [Potomac River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_River) and its [tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributaries). By the time of English colonization, there were 32 [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages)-speaking [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) tribes in the present-day coastal [Tidewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidewater_\(geographic_term\)) Virginia area, including those of the [Patawomeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patawomeck) and numerous tribes that were part of the [Powhatan Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powhatan_Confederacy). The former small tribe, still centered in Stafford County, was recognized by the state of Virginia in 2010.

The Indigenous' first recorded encounter with Europeans in this area was in 1608, with [John Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Smith_\(explorer\)) of the [Jamestown Settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown_Settlement). During a time of recurring tension between the early English colonists and the local natives, the colonists led by [Samuel Argall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Argall) captured [Chief Powhatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Powhatan)'s daughter, [Pocahontas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocahontas), while she was living with her husband, Kocoum, at the [Patawomeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patawomeck) town of [Passapatanzy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passapatanzy). The colonists took her from Passapatanzy, located in the eastern part of what is now Stafford County, to a secondary English settlement, known as [Henricus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henricus) (or Henrico Town). During her captivity there, [Alexander Whitaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Whitaker) converted Pocahontas to Christianity. She took the name "Rebecca" at her baptism. Rebecca/Pocahontas married English colonist [John Rolfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rolfe) on April 5, 1614, in [Jamestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown,_Virginia).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Their [mixed-race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-race) descendants were among the [First Families of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Families_of_Virginia).

The English colonial government of Virginia imposed its own order on the land and peoples. In 1664 it established Stafford County from territory previously part of [Westmoreland County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmoreland_County,_Virginia)[^cite_note-autogenerated1-5] (which had been created from [Northumberland County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland_County,_Virginia) in 1653). It was named after [Staffordshire](58733.md), England. As originally delineated, Stafford County included a much larger area than its current borders. As population grew, the following counties and jurisdictions were created: [Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia), [Fairfax](91327.md), and [Prince William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William_County,_Virginia) counties, and the [City of Alexandria](18940583.md).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-5] It is part of the area now considered [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia).

[George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) spent much of his childhood in the lower part of the county at his family's home [Ferry Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferry_Farm), which at the time was part of King George County and was along the [Rappahannock River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappahannock_River) across from [Fredericksburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredericksburg,_Virginia). [Colonial Forge High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Forge_High_School) was built on a tract of land owned in colonial times by his father [Augustine Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_Washington).[^cite_note-6] [George Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason), another [Founding Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Fathers_of_the_United_States), also lived in Stafford during his formative years.[^cite_note-7]

[Aquia Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquia_Church), built in 1757, is unusual among local structures for having been designed on the plan of a [Greek cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cross) rather than the more standard Roman Cross design. In addition, Aquia Church has a rare three-tiered pulpit; it has been designated as a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark). The [Episcopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_\(United_States\)) church continues to be active today.[^cite_note-8]

Stafford County industry and resources were important to the colony and early nation. During the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), the Stafford [ironworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironworks) furnished arms for the colonial rebel soldiers.[^cite_note-9] [Aquia Creek sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquia_Creek_sandstone), quarried from [Government Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Quarry_at_Government_Island), was used to build the [White House](33057.md) and the [U.S. Capitol](31979.md).[^cite_note-10]

During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), the county was part of the battlegrounds, occupied repeatedly by more than 100,000 troops for several years. In 1862, before and after the [Battle of Fredericksburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fredericksburg), some 10,000 slaves left area plantations and city households to cross the [Rappahannock River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappahannock_River), reaching the Union lines and gaining freedom. This exodus and Trail of Freedom is commemorated by historical markers on both sides of the river, in Fredericksburg and in Stafford County.[^cite_note-trail-11] The [Battle of Aquia Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aquia_Creek) took place in the [Aquia Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquia_Harbour,_Virginia) area.[^cite_note-12] Both the [Union Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army) and the [Confederate Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_Army) struggled to control the strategic [Potomac Creek Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_Creek_Bridge) at various times during the war.

[Falmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falmouth,_Virginia), a town bordering Fredericksburg, was the home of late-19th century American Impressionist artist [Gari Melchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gari_Melchers). His house, [Belmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gari_Melchers_Home), still stands and is listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places).

### 20th century to present



Stafford County Courthouse in 1929

Stafford County has developed to be one of the core counties of the [Washington metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_metropolitan_area) and Northern Virginia, the seat of government and numerous major defense installations. [Marine Corps Base Quantico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Base_Quantico) occupies northern areas of the county. The FBI Academy, FBI National Laboratory and NCIS HQ are located on the Stafford side of MCBQ. Many residents commute north to work there and in other defense and federal facilities, as well as private companies, in Washington and its environs on Interstate Highway 95, U.S. Route 1, and by [Virginia Railway Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Railway_Express). Stafford County is suburban and home to many Washington, D.C., commuters and workers who work for the federal government. It is also home to many military families because of its proximity to MCBQ. Theoe facts contribute to the high-income of families in Stafford County and the high number of home-ownership and college graduates. Stafford County is also one of the few counties in America where black households make more than white households. In recent years Stafford County has experienced major suburbanization, and growth as more and more workers move to the suburbs for their families. Thus, many developers are currently active in Stafford County in housing and retail projects.

In the early morning hours of May 9, 2008, a [tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-May_2008_tornado_outbreak_sequence) touched down in the southern part of the county, severely damaging about 140 suburban homes.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The county was severely affected by "[Snowmageddon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmageddon)," the massive blizzards of December 2009 and February 2010, and received some of the heaviest snow in the D.C. metropolitan area, with about 25 inches of snow in December, and 19 inches in February.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the county has a total area of 280 square miles (730 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 269 square miles (700 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 11 square miles (28 km<sup>2</sup>) (3.9%) is water.[^cite_note-GR1-17] The independent city of Fredericksburg developed at the fall line of the Rappahannock, supporting mills run by water power. To the northwest of there is the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_region_of_Virginia) area. [Aquia Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquia_Creek) empties into the tidal segment of the Potomac River at Brent Point. Its location and proximity to Washington, D.C., has caused many [federal government](195149.md) workers and commuters to live in the county. Stafford County's location, close to Washington, D.C., and [MCB Quanico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Base_Quantico), are one of the major reasons for the county's [suburbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbanization) and growth.

The county is divided into seven magisterial districts: George Washington, Hartwood, Falmouth, Griffis-Widewater, Aquia, Garrisonville, and Rockhill. The magisterial districts, roughly equal in population, each elect one supervisor to the Board of Supervisors which governs Stafford County. The county operates under the county form of the County Executive system of government, with an elected Board of Supervisors. The Board hires a professional, nonpartisan County Administrator to manage government agencies. The current County Administrator is Thomas C. Foley. Stafford County is currently a battleground county politically which leans Republican. Due to its rapid suburbanization and growth of families, and Federal Government employees from D.C. and being a part of Northern Virginia it has trended Democratic; however, it has stayed Republican at the state and local level. The Board of Supervisors currently has a Republican majority, that is also true of the Stafford School Board (although they are elected on a bipartisan basis). In [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election_in_Virginia), [Joe Biden](145422.md) became the first Democratic candidate to carry Stafford County since [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election_in_Virginia).[^cite_note-18]

Stafford County is represented by Congressman [Eugene Vindman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Vindman) of [Virginia's 7th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia's_7th_congressional_district) in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md). On the state level, it is represented by Republican [Tara Durant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_Durant) and Democrat [Jeremy McPike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_McPike) in the [Virginia State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Senate). Democrats [Candi King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candi_King), [Joshua G. Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_G._Cole) and Republican [Paul Milde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Milde_\(politician\)) represent the county in the [Virginia House of Delegates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_House_of_Delegates).

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the county had a population of 156,927. The median age was 36.0 years. 26.7% of residents were under the age of 18 and 11.0% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 98.6 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 96.1 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-36][^cite_note-Census2020PL-37]

The racial makeup of the county was 57.5% White, 19.4% [Black or African American](2154.md), 0.7% [American Indian and Alaska Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 4.0% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), 0.2% [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) and [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander), 6.0% from some other race, and 12.2% from [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans). [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) residents of any race comprised 15.1% of the population.[^cite_note-Census2020PL-37]

81.4% of residents lived in urban areas, while 18.6% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-38]

There were 50,869 households in the county, of which 42.0% had children under the age of 18 living with them and 20.5% had a female householder with no spouse or partner present. About 16.5% of all households were made up of individuals and 5.9% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-36]

There were 52,793 housing units, of which 3.6% were vacant. Among occupied housing units, 77.2% were owner-occupied and 22.8% were renter-occupied. The homeowner vacancy rate was 1.2% and the rental vacancy rate was 5.2%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-36]

### 2010 Census

As of the census[^cite_note-GR8-39] of 2010, there were 128,961 people, 38,237 households, and 24,481 families residing in the county. The population density was 342 inhabitants per square mile (132/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 31,405 housing units at an average density of 116 units per square mile (45 units/km<sup>2</sup>). Due to the rapid suburbanization of the county the demographics have increasingly changed in the past 20 years and will continue to change in the near future as Stafford County continues to grow.

By 2005, Stafford County's population was 72.8% non-Hispanic whites. black Americans were 17.0% of the total population. Native Americans were 0.4% of the county total, Asians 2.3%, and Native Hawai'ians and other Pacific islanders 0.2%. Latinos were 6.4% of the population. This was higher than the total for all of Virginia.

As of 2000 there were 38,187 households, out of which 46.90% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 68.00% were married couples living together, 9.30% had a female householder with no husband present, and 18.90% were non-families. 13.80% of all households were made up of individuals, and 3.40% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 3.01 and the average family size was 3.32.

In the county, the age distribution of the population shows 31.60% under the age of 18, 7.80% from 18 to 24, 33.70% from 25 to 44, 21.10% from 45 to 64, and 5.90% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 33 years. For every 100 females, there were 101.10 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 99.50 males.

The median income for a household in the county was \$75,546, and the median income for a family was \$78,575 (these figures had risen to \$85,793 and \$95,433 respectively as of a 2007 estimate[^cite_note-40]). Males had a median income of \$47,080 versus \$31,469 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the county was \$24,762. About 2.40% of families and 3.50% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 3.30% of those under age 18 and 5.30% of those age 65 or over. Stafford County is also one of the seven counties in America where black households make more than white households. Between 2010 and 2014 the typical black household made \$105,628 in the county. The highest out of all the seven counties, while white households made an average \$99,533.

## Transportation

Stafford County is approximately 40 miles south of Washington D.C. Stafford County is the southern terminus of the I-95 Express lanes, which start in the southern portion of the county north of Exit 133, and go to Washington D.C. The Express lanes originally terminated in the north-central portion of the county; the extension to its current length was fully completed in December 2023.[^cite_note-43]

&#x20;A 2017 study done by INRIX Roadway Analytics awarded Southbound I-95 from D.C. to the southern tip of Stafford County the worst single traffic hotspot in the nation. A stretch of Northbound I-95 from Northern Spotsylvania County to the Northern tip of Stafford County was ranked the seventh worst traffic hotspot in the nation.[^cite_note-44] Due to booming growth in the county, and Northern Virginia, Stafford County experiences regular gridlock and has one of the worst traffic situations in Northern Virginia, Virginia, and the nation. Thus, the Commonwealth of Virginia has poured big amounts of money in road infrastructure in Stafford County. Major U.S. Routes also pass through, such as U.S. 1, and U.S. 17.

## Notable people

- [Palmer Hayden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmer_Hayden), American painter who depicted black American life, landscapes, seascapes, and African influences during the Harlem Renaissance.

- [Erin Cahill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Cahill), actress

- [Peter Vivian Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Vivian_Daniel), associate justice of the Supreme Court of the United States 1842–1860

- [James Garrard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Garrard), Governor of Kentucky 1796–1804

- [Mark Lenzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Lenzi), Olympic medalist in diving, 25th (Barcelona, 1992) and 26th (Atlanta, 1996) Olympic Games

- [Arlene Limas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlene_Limas), Olympic medalist in taekwondo, 24th Olympic Games (Seoul, 1988)

- [John Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maine), former pitcher for the New York Mets, born in Fredericksburg, graduated from North Stafford High School

- [Jessica Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Player), child actress, graduated from Stafford High School

- [Pocahontas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocahontas), Powhatan princess who resided here with her husband, Kocoum at the time of her abduction

- [Gregg Ritchie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Ritchie), former Major League Baseball player and coach, and head coach of the George Washington University baseball team

- [Jeff Rouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Rouse_\(swimmer\)), Olympic medalist in swimming, 25th (Barcelona, 1992) and 26th (Atlanta, 1996) Olympic games

- [Torrey Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrey_Smith), National Football League wide receiver

- [George Mason III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_III), Father of [George Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason) and member of [Virginia House of Burgesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_House_of_Burgesses).

- [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington), boyhood home Ferry Farm is located in Stafford County
