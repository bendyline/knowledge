An **intelligence agency** is a [government agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_agency) responsible for the collection, analysis, and exploitation of information in support of [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement), [national security](240468.md), [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military), [public safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_safety), and [foreign policy](233404.md) objectives.[^cite_note-BI-1]&#x20;

Means of information gathering are both overt and covert and may include [espionage](10501.md), [communication interception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence), [cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis), cooperation with other institutions, and evaluation of public sources. The assembly and propagation of this information is known as [intelligence analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_analysis) or [intelligence assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_assessment).&#x20;

## Objectives

Intelligence agencies can provide the following services for their national governments.&#x20;

- Give early warning of impending crisis;

- Serve national and international [crisis management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_management) by helping to discern the intentions of current or potential opponents;

- Inform national defense planning and [military operations](666301.md), known as [military intelligence](146678.md);

- Protect sensitive information secrets, both of their own sources and activities, and those of other state agencies;

- Covertly influence the outcome of events in favor of [national interests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_interest), or influence [international security](1946540.md); and

- Defense against the efforts of other national intelligence agencies, known as [counterintelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterintelligence).

There is a distinction between "security intelligence" and "foreign intelligence". Security intelligence pertains to [domestic security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_security) and related threats, including [terrorism](30636.md) and espionage. Foreign intelligence involves information collection relating to the political, or economic activities of foreign states.&#x20;

Some agencies have been involved in [assassination](2963.md), [arms trafficking](42210.md), [coups d'état](25914154.md), and the placement of misinformation [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda) and other [covert](613453.md) and [clandestine operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clandestine_operations) to support their own or their governments' interests.&#x20;
