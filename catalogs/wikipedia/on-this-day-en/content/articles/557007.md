**Nicolas Paul Stéphane Sarközy de Nagy-Bocsa** ([/sɑːrˈkoʊzi/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*sar-KOH-zee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); French: [\[nikɔla pɔl stefan saʁkɔzi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ec/Sarkozy.ogg/Sarkozy.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ec/Sarkozy.ogg/Sarkozy.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; born 28 January 1955) is a French former politician who served as the [president of the French Republic](24899.md) from 2007 to 2012.

Sarkozy was born in [Paris](22989.md). He was the mayor of [Neuilly-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly-sur-Seine) from 1983 to 2002, he was [Minister of the Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Economy_and_Finance_\(France\)) under Prime Minister [Édouard Balladur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Balladur) (1993–1995) during [François Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand)'s second term. During [Jacques Chirac's second presidential term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac's_second_presidential_term), he served as [Minister of the Interior](871703.md) and as [Minister of Finances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finances_\(France\)). He was the leader of the [Union for a Popular Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement) (UMP) party from 2004 to 2007.

He won the [2007 French presidential election](11105698.md) by a 53.1% to 46.9% margin against [Ségolène Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9gol%C3%A8ne_Royal), the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)) (PS) candidate. During his term, he faced the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), the [late-2000s recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late-2000s_recession), and the [European sovereign debt crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_sovereign_debt_crisis), the [Russo-Georgian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Georgian_War) (for which he negotiated a ceasefire), and the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) (especially in [Tunisia](30188.md), [Libya](17633.md), and [Syria](7515849.md)). He initiated the [reform of French universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberties_and_Responsibilities_of_Universities_\(France\)) (2007) and the [pension reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensions_in_France) (2010). He married Italian-French singer-songwriter [Carla Bruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Bruni) in 2008 at the [Élysée Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lys%C3%A9e_Palace) in Paris. In the [2012 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_French_presidential_election), Sarkozy was defeated by the PS candidate [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Hollande) by a 3.2% margin. After leaving office, Sarkozy pledged to retire but returned in 2014 as UMP leader (renamed [The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)) in 2015). After defeat in the [Republican presidential primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_The_Republicans_\(France\)_presidential_primary) in 2016, he retired from public life.

He was [charged with corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Sarkozy_corruption_trial) by French prosecutors in two cases, notably concerning the [alleged Libyan interference in the 2007 French elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alleged_Libyan_financing_in_the_2007_French_presidential_election). In 2021, Sarkozy [was convicted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Sarkozy_corruption_trial) of corruption in two separate trials, receiving a three-year sentence (two suspended, one in prison, under appeal) and a one-year sentence served under home confinement. He lost an appeal in May 2023.[^cite_note-1] In February 2024, his campaign finance sentence was revised to six months in prison and six months suspended.[^cite_note-sentencerevised-2] In September 2025, he was convicted of criminal conspiracy over his "corruption pact" with [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) and sentenced to five years in prison, plus a €100,000 fine. On 21 October 2025, Sarkozy commenced his sentence at [La Santé Prison](627392.md), in segregation, while lawyers appeal.[^cite_note-\:1-3] He was released three weeks into his five year sentence on 10 November.[^cite_note-4] One of the conditions of Sarkozy's release is that he must not make contact with any employees of the justice ministry.

## Personal life

### Family background

Main article: [Family history of Nicolas Sarkozy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_history_of_Nicolas_Sarkozy)

Nicolas Paul Stéphane Sarközy de Nagy-Bocsa was born on 28 January 1955 in Paris. He is the son of [Pál István Ernő Sárközy de Nagy-Bócsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_history_of_Nicolas_Sarkozy)[^cite_note-5] ([Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language): *nagybócsai Sárközy Pál*; [\[ˈnɒɟboːt͡ʃɒi ˈʃaːrkøzi ˈpaːl\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hungarian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/87/Pal_sarkozy.ogg/Pal_sarkozy.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/87/Pal_sarkozy.ogg/Pal_sarkozy.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>—in Hungarian *Nagy-Bócsay Sárközy Pál István Ernő*;[^cite_note-nytimes.com-6] 5 May 1928 – 4 March 2023), a [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people) [aristocrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy), and [Andrée Jeanne "Dadu" Mallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_history_of_Nicolas_Sarkozy) (12 October 1925 – 12 December 2017),[^cite_note-7] whose [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Greeks) [Greek Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Jew) father (Sarkozy's grandfather) converted to [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) to marry Sarkozy's French Catholic maternal grandmother.[^cite_note-BBCProf-8][^cite_note-9] They were married in the Saint-François-de-Sales church, [17th arrondissement of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_arrondissement_of_Paris), on 8 February 1950, and divorced in 1959.[^cite_note-10]

### Early life

During Sarkozy's childhood, his father founded his own advertising agency and became wealthy. The family lived in a mansion owned by Sarkozy's maternal grandfather, Benedict Mallah, in the [17th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_arrondissement) of Paris. The family later moved to [Neuilly-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly-sur-Seine), one of the wealthiest [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_in_France) of the [Île-de-France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele-de-France_\(region\)) *[région](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9gions_in_France)* immediately west of Paris. According to Sarkozy, his staunchly [Gaullist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaullist) grandfather was more of an influence on him than his father, whom he rarely saw. Sarkozy was raised Catholic.[^cite_note-BBCProf-8]

Sarkozy said that being kept at a distance by his father shaped much of who he is today. He also has said that, in his early years, he felt inferior to his wealthier and taller classmates.[^cite_note-cath_nay-11] He has spoken about the difficulties he faced as a child of divorced parents at a time when divorce was uncommon.[^cite_note-12] "What made me who I am now is the sum of all the humiliations suffered during childhood", he said later.[^cite_note-cath_nay-11]

### Education

Sarkozy was enrolled in the *[Lycée Chaptal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Chaptal)*, a well-regarded public middle and high school in Paris' [8th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_arrondissement_of_Paris), where he failed his *[sixième](379033.md#Secondary_education)*. His family then sent him to the *Cours Saint-Louis de Monceau*, a private Catholic school in the 17th arrondissement, where he was reportedly a mediocre student,[^cite_note-cath_nay-11] but where he nonetheless obtained his *[baccalauréat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baccalaur%C3%A9at)* in 1973.

Sarkozy enrolled at the *[Université Paris X Nanterre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_X:_Nanterre)*, where he graduated with an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in [private law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_law) and, later, with a [D.E.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEA_\(former_French_degree\)) degree in business law. Paris X Nanterre had been the starting place for the [May '68](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_1968_events_in_France) student movement and was still a stronghold of [leftist](18499.md) students. Described as a quiet student, Sarkozy soon joined the right-wing student organisation, in which he was very active. He completed his [military service](32488770.md) as a part-time [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Air_Force) cleaner.[^cite_note-13]

After graduating from university, Sarkozy entered [Sciences Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences_Po), where he studied between 1979 and 1981, but failed to graduate[^cite_note-14] due to an insufficient command of the English language.[^cite_note-cath_nay-11]

After passing the [bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(law\)), Sarkozy became a lawyer specialising in [business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_law) and [family law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_law)[^cite_note-cath_nay-11] and was one of [Silvio Berlusconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Berlusconi)'s French lawyers.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Marriages

#### Marie-Dominique Culioli

Sarkozy married his first wife, Marie-Dominique Culioli, on 23 September 1982, with prominent right-wing politician [Charles Pasqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Pasqua) serving as best man. (Pasqua later became a political opponent.)[^cite_note-18] Culioli's father was a [pharmacist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacist) from [Vico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vico,_Corse-du-Sud) (a village north of [Ajaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajaccio), Corsica); her uncle was [Achille Peretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achille_Peretti_\(politician\)), the mayor of [Neuilly-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly-sur-Seine) from 1947 to 1983 and Sarkozy's political mentor. They had two sons, Pierre (born in 1985), now a hip-hop producer,[^cite_note-19] and [Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Sarkozy) (born in 1986), now a local politician in the city of Neuilly-sur-Seine, where Sarkozy started his own political career. Sarkozy divorced Culioli in 1996, after they had been separated for several years.

#### Cécilia Ciganer-Albéniz

As mayor of [Neuilly-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly-sur-Seine), Sarkozy met former fashion model and public relations executive [Cécilia Ciganer-Albéniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9cilia_Attias) (great-granddaughter of composer [Isaac Albéniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Alb%C3%A9niz) and daughter of a Moldovan father), when he officiated at her wedding[^cite_note-20] to television host [Jacques Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Martin_\(TV_host\)). In 1988, she left her husband for Sarkozy, and divorced one year later. She and Sarkozy married in October 1996, with witnesses [Martin Bouygues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Bouygues) and [Bernard Arnault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Arnault).[^cite_note-21] They have one son, [Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Sarkozy), born 28 April 1997.

Between 2002 and 2005, the couple often appeared together on public occasions, with Cécilia Sarkozy acting as the chief aide for her husband.[^cite_note-22] On 25 May 2005, however, the Swiss newspaper *[Le Matin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Matin_\(Switzerland\))* revealed that she had left Sarkozy for Moroccan national [Richard Attias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Attias), head of [Publicis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicis) in New York.[^cite_note-23] There were other accusations of a private nature in *Le Matin*, which led to Sarkozy [suing](113258.md) the paper.[^cite_note-24] In the meantime, he was said to have had an affair with a journalist of *[Le Figaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Figaro)*, [Anne Fulda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Fulda).[^cite_note-telegraph3-25]

Sarkozy and Cécilia ultimately divorced on 15 October 2007, soon after his election as president.[^cite_note-26]

#### Carla Bruni



Sarkozy and his wife Carla Bruni greet President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) at the G8 Summit dinner in [Deauville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deauville), France, 26 May 2011.

Less than a month after separating from Cécilia, Sarkozy met Italian-born singer, songwriter and former fashion model [Carla Bruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Bruni) at a dinner party, and soon entered into a relationship with her.[^cite_note-27] They married on 2 February 2008 at the [Élysée Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lys%C3%A9e_Palace) in Paris.[^cite_note-28]

The couple have a daughter, Giulia, born on 19 October 2011.[^cite_note-29] It was the first time a French president has publicly had a child while in office.[^cite_note-30]

### Personal wealth

Sarkozy declared to the [Constitutional Council](1141259.md) a [net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_worth) of €2 million, most of the assets being in the form of [life insurance](383367.md) policies.[^cite_note-31] As the French President, one of his first actions was to give himself a pay raise: his yearly salary went from €101,000 to €240,000, matching other European officeholders.[^cite_note-32] He is also entitled to a mayoral, parliamentarian and presidential pension as a former Mayor of Neuilly-sur-Seine, member of the National Assembly and President of France.

## Early political career

Sarkozy is recognised by French parties on both the Right and Left as a skilled politician and striking [orator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orator).[^cite_note-33] His supporters within France emphasize his [charisma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charisma), political innovation and willingness to "make a dramatic break" amid mounting disaffection against "politics as usual". Overall, he is considered more pro-American and pro-Israeli than most French politicians.

From 2004 to 2007, Sarkozy was president of the [Union pour un Mouvement Populaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement) (UMP), France's major right-wing political party, and he was [Minister of the Interior](871703.md) in the government of Prime Minister [Dominique de Villepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_de_Villepin), with the honorific title of [Minister of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State), making him effectively the number three official in the French State after President [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac) and Villepin. His ministerial responsibilities included law enforcement and working to co-ordinate relationships between the national and local governments, as well as [Minister of Worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Worship_\(France\)): in this role he created the [French Council of the Muslim Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Council_of_the_Muslim_Faith) (CFCM). Previously, he was a député in the [French National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Assembly). He was forced to resign this position in order to accept his ministerial appointment. He previously also held several ministerial posts, including [Finance Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Minister).

### In Government: 1993–1995

Sarkozy's political career began when he was 23, when he became a city councillor in [Neuilly-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly-sur-Seine). A member of the [Neo-Gaullist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Gaullist) party RPR, he went on to be elected [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_\(France\)) of that town, after the death of the incumbent mayor [Achille Peretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achille_Peretti_\(politician\)). Sarkozy had been close to Peretti, as his mother was Peretti's secretary. A more senior RPR councillor, [Charles Pasqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Pasqua), wanted to become mayor, and asked Sarkozy to organize his campaign. Instead, Sarkozy took that opportunity to propel himself into the office of mayor.[^cite_note-34] He was the youngest mayor of any town in France with a population of over 50,000. He served from 1983 to 2002. In 1988, he became a deputy in the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Assembly).

In 1993, Nicolas Sarkozy was in the national news for personally negotiating with the "Human Bomb", a man who had [taken small children hostages in a kindergarten in Neuilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly_kindergarten_hostage_crisis).[^cite_note-NYTimesWin-35] The "Human Bomb" was killed after two days of talks by [policemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Police) of the [RAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recherche_Assistance_Intervention_Dissuasion), who entered the school stealthily while the attacker was resting.

At the same time, from 1993 to 1995, he was Minister for the Budget and spokesman for the executive in the cabinet of Prime Minister [Édouard Balladur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Balladur). Throughout most of his early career, Sarkozy had been seen as a protégé of [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac). During his tenure, he increased France's public debt more than any other French Budget Minister, by the equivalent of €200 billion (US\$260 billion) (FY 1994–1996). The first two budgets he submitted to the parliament (budgets for FY1994 and FY1995) assumed a yearly budget deficit equivalent to six percent of GDP.[^cite_note-36] According to the [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty), the French yearly budget deficit may not exceed three percent of France's GDP.

In 1995, he spurned Chirac and backed Édouard Balladur for [President of France](24899.md). After Chirac won the election, Sarkozy lost his position as Minister for the Budget, and found himself outside the circles of power.

However, he returned after the right-wing defeat at the [1997 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_French_legislative_election), as the number two candidate of the RPR. When the party leader [Philippe Séguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_S%C3%A9guin) resigned, in 1999, he took the leadership of the Neo-Gaullist party. But it obtained its worst result at the [1999 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_European_Parliament_election_in_France), winning 12.7% of the votes, less than the dissident [Rally for France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_for_France) of Charles Pasqua. Sarkozy lost the RPR leadership.



Sarkozy speaking at the congress of his party, 28 November 2004

In 2002, however, after his re-election as President of the French Republic (see [2002 French presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_French_presidential_election)), Chirac appointed Sarkozy as minister of the interior in the cabinet of Prime Minister [Jean-Pierre Raffarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Raffarin), despite Sarkozy's support of Edouard Balladur for president in 1995.[^cite_note-37] Following Chirac's 14 July keynote speech on road safety, Sarkozy as Minister of the Interior pushed through new legislation leading to the mass purchase of speed cameras and a campaign to increase the awareness of dangers on the roads.

In the cabinet reshuffle of 30 April 2004, Sarkozy became finance minister. Tensions continued to build between Sarkozy and Chirac and within the UMP party, as Sarkozy's intentions of becoming head of the party after the resignation of [Alain Juppé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Jupp%C3%A9) became clear.

In party elections of 10 November 2004, Sarkozy became leader of the UMP with 85% of the vote. In accordance with an agreement with Chirac, he resigned as finance minister. Sarkozy's ascent was marked by the division of UMP between *sarkozystes*, such as Sarkozy's "first lieutenant", [Brice Hortefeux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brice_Hortefeux), and Chirac loyalists, such as [Jean-Louis Debré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Louis_Debr%C3%A9).

Sarkozy was made Chevalier de la Légion d'honneur (Knight of the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour)) by President Chirac in February 2005. He was re-elected on 13 March 2005 to the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_France). (As required by the constitution,[^cite_note-38] he had to resign as a deputy when he became minister in 2002.)

On 31 May 2005, the main French news radio station *[France Info](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_Info)* reported a rumour that Sarkozy was to be reappointed minister of the interior in the government of [Dominique de Villepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_de_Villepin) without resigning from the UMP leadership. This was confirmed on 2 June 2005, when the members of the government were officially announced.

### First term as minister of the interior: 2002–2004

Towards the end of his first term as minister of the interior, in 2004, Sarkozy was the most divisive conservative politician in France, according to polls conducted at the beginning of 2004.

Sarkozy has sought to ease the sometimes tense relationships between the general French population and the [Muslim community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_France). Unlike the Catholic Church in France with its official leaders or Protestants with their umbrella organisations, the French Muslim community had a lack of structure with no group that could legitimately deal with the French government on their behalf. Sarkozy supported the foundation in May 2003 of the private non-profit [Conseil français du culte musulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Council_of_the_Muslim_Faith) ("French Council of the Muslim Faith"), an organisation meant to be representative of French Muslims.[^cite_note-39] In addition, Sarkozy has suggested amending the [1905 law on the separation of Church and State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_French_law_on_the_separation_of_Church_and_State), mostly in order to be able to finance mosques and other Muslim institutions with public funds[^cite_note-40] so that they are less reliant on money from outside France. It was not followed by any concrete measure.

### Minister of finance: 2004

During his short appointment as [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_\(France\)), Sarkozy was responsible for introducing a number of policies. The degree to which this reflected *libéralisme* (a hands-off approach to running the economy) or more traditional French state *dirigisme* (intervention) is controversial. He resigned the day following his election as president of the [UMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement).

- In September 2004, Sarkozy oversaw the reduction of the government ownership stake in [France Télécom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_T%C3%A9l%C3%A9com) from 50.4 per cent to 41 per cent.

  [^cite_note-41]

- Sarkozy backed a partial [nationalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalisation) of the large engineering company [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom) decided by his predecessor when the company was exposed to bankruptcy in 2003.

  [^cite_note-42]

- In June 2004, Sarkozy reached an agreement with the major retail chains in France to concertedly lower prices on household goods by an average of two percent; the success of this measure is disputed, with studies suggesting that the decrease was close to one percent in September.

  [^cite_note-43]

- Taxes: Sarkozy avoided taking a position on the ISF ([solidarity tax on wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_tax_on_wealth)). This is considered an ideological symbol by many on the [left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_left) and [right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_right). Some in the business world and on the liberal right, such as [Alain Madelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Madelin), wanted it abolished. For Sarkozy, that would have risked being categorised by the left as a gift to the richest classes of society at a time of economic difficulties.

  [^cite_note-44]

### Second term as minister of the interior: 2005–2007



Sarkozy as Minister of the Interior with U.S. Secretary of State [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice), after their bilateral meeting in Washington, D.C., 12 September 2006

During his second term at the Ministry of the Interior, Sarkozy was initially more discreet about his ministerial activities: instead of focusing on his own topic of law and order, many of his declarations addressed wider issues, since he was expressing his opinions as head of the UMP party.

Main article: [Response to the 2005 civil unrest in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Response_to_the_2005_civil_unrest_in_France)

However, the [civil unrest in autumn 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_civil_unrest_in_France) put law enforcement in the spotlight again. Sarkozy was accused of having provoked the unrest by calling young delinquents from [housing projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_project) a "rabble" *("[racaille](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/racaille)")* in [Argenteuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argenteuil) near Paris, and controversially suggested cleansing the minority suburbs with a [Kärcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A4rcher). After the accidental death of two youths, which sparked the riots, Sarkozy first blamed it on "hoodlums" and gangsters. These remarks were sharply criticised by many on the left wing and by a member of his own government, Delegate Minister for Equal Opportunities [Azouz Begag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azouz_Begag).[^cite_note-45]

After the rioting, he made a number of announcements on future policy: selection of immigrants,  greater tracking of immigrants, and a reform of the 1945 ordinance government justice measures for young delinquents.

### UMP leader: 2004–2007



Nicolas Sarkozy in 2006 with Cypriot opposition leader [Nicos Anastasiades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicos_Anastasiades)

Before he was elected President of France, Sarkozy was president of [UMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement), the French conservative party, elected with 85 per cent of the vote. During his presidency, the number of members increased significantly. In 2005, he supported a "yes" vote in the [French referendum on the European Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_referendum_on_the_European_Constitution), but the "No" vote won.

Throughout 2005, Sarkozy called for radical changes in France's economic and social policies. These calls culminated in an interview with *[Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde)* on 8 September 2005, during which he claimed that the French had been misled for 30 years by false promises.[^cite_note-46] Among other issues:

- he called for a simplified and "fairer" [taxation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_code), with fewer loopholes and a maximum taxation rate (all *direct* taxes combined) at 50 per cent of revenue;

- he approved measures reducing or denying social support to [unemployed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment) workers who refuse work offered to them;

- he pressed for a reduction in the [budget deficit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_deficit), claiming that the French state had been living off [credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_\(finance\)) for some time.

Such policies are what are called in France *[libéral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_and_radicalism_in_France)* (that is, in favour of [laissez-faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire) economic policies) or, with a pejorative undertone, *[ultra-libéral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraliberalism#Market_liberalism)*. Sarkozy rejects this label of *libéral* and prefers to call himself a pragmatist.

Sarkozy opened another avenue of controversy by declaring that he wanted a reform of the immigration system, with [quotas](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/quota) designed to admit the skilled workers needed by the French economy. He also wanted to reform the current French system for foreign students, saying that it enabled foreign students to take open-ended curricula in order to obtain residency in France; instead, he wanted to select the best students for the best curricula in France.

In early 2006, the [French parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_parliament) adopted a controversial bill known as [DADVSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DADVSI), which reforms [French copyright law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_copyright_law). Since his party was divided on the issue, Sarkozy stepped in and organised meetings between various parties involved. Later, groups such as the Odebi League and EUCD.info alleged that Sarkozy personally and unofficially supported certain amendments to the law, which enacted strong penalties against designers of [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer) systems.

### Presidential election: 2007



[Ségolène Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9gol%C3%A8ne_Royal) was Sarkozy's final opponent during the 2007 campaign.

Main article: [French presidential election, 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_presidential_election,_2007)

Sarkozy was a likely candidate for the presidency in 2007; in an oft-repeated comment made on television channel [France 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_2), when asked by a journalist whether he thought about the presidential election when he shaved in the morning, Sarkozy commented, "Not just when I shave".[^cite_note-47]

On 14 January 2007, Sarkozy was chosen by the [UMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement) to be its candidate in the 2007 presidential election. Sarkozy, who was running unopposed,[^cite_note-48] won 98 percent of the votes. Of the 327,000 UMP members who could vote, 69 per cent participated in the online ballot.[^cite_note-49]

In February 2007, Sarkozy appeared on a televised debate on [TF1](571365.md) where he expressed his support for [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action) and the freedom to work overtime. Despite his opposition to [same-sex marriage](92656.md), he advocated [civil unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_unions) and the possibility for same-sex partners to inherit under the same regime as married couples. The law was voted in July 2007.[^cite_note-50]

On 7 February, Sarkozy decided in favour of a projected second, non-[nuclear](22153.md), [aircraft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_French_aircraft_carrier) for the national Navy (adding to the nuclear *[Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_\(R_91\))*), during an official visit in [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon) with Defence Minister [Michèle Alliot-Marie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mich%C3%A8le_Alliot-Marie). "This would allow permanently having an operational ship, taking into account the constraints of maintenance", he explained.[^cite_note-51]

On 21 March, President Jacques Chirac announced his support for Sarkozy. Chirac pointed out that Sarkozy had been chosen as presidential candidate for the ruling UMP party, and said: "So it is totally natural that I give him my vote and my support."[^cite_note-52] To focus on his campaign, Sarkozy stepped down as minister of the interior on 26 March.[^cite_note-53]

During the campaign, rival candidates had accused Sarkozy of being a "candidate for brutality" and of presenting hard-line views about France's future.[^cite_note-54] Opponents also accused him of courting conservative voters in policy-making in a bid to capitalise on right-wing sentiments among some communities. However, his popularity was sufficient to see him polling as the frontrunner throughout the later campaign period, consistently ahead of rival Socialist candidate, [Ségolène Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9gol%C3%A8ne_Royal).



Demonstrations in Paris, 6 May 2007, following the election of Nicolas Sarkozy

The first round of the presidential election was held on 22 April 2007. Sarkozy came in first with 31.18 per cent of the votes, ahead of Ségolène Royal of the Socialists with 25.87 percent. In the second round, Sarkozy came out on top to win the election with 53.06 per cent of the votes ahead of Ségolène Royal with 46.94 per cent.[^cite_note-55] In his speech immediately following the announcement of the election results, Sarkozy stressed the need for France's modernisation, but also called for national unity, mentioning that Royal was in his thoughts. In that speech, he claimed "The French have chosen to break with the ideas, habits and behaviour of the past. I will restore the value of work, authority, merit and respect for the nation."

## Presidency of France (2007–2012)

Main article: [Presidency of Nicolas Sarkozy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Nicolas_Sarkozy)

### Inauguration



Sarkozy greets U.S. First Lady [Laura Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Bush) in Germany, June 2007.

On 6 May 2007, Nicolas Sarkozy became the sixth person to be elected President of the Fifth Republic (which was established in 1958), and the 23rd President in French history.

The official transfer of power from Chirac to Sarkozy took place on 16 May at 11\:00 am (9\:00 UTC) at the [Élysée Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lys%C3%A9e_Palace), where he was given the authorization codes of the [French nuclear arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_de_Frappe).[^cite_note-56] In the afternoon, the new president flew to Berlin to meet with German Chancellor [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel).

Under Sarkozy's government, [François Fillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Fillon) replaced [Dominique de Villepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_de_Villepin) as prime minister.[^cite_note-57] Sarkozy appointed [Bernard Kouchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Kouchner), the left-wing founder of [Médecins Sans Frontières](20498.md), as his foreign minister, leading to Kouchner's expulsion from the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)). In addition to Kouchner, three more Sarkozy ministers are from the left, including [Éric Besson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_Besson), who served as [Ségolène Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9gol%C3%A8ne_Royal)'s economic adviser at the beginning of her campaign. Sarkozy also appointed seven women to form a total cabinet of 15; one, Justice Minister [Rachida Dati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachida_Dati), is the first woman of Northern African origin to serve in a French cabinet. Of the 15, two attended the elite [École nationale d'administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_nationale_d'administration) (ENA).[^cite_note-58] The ministers were reorganised, with the controversial creation of a 'Ministry of Immigration, Integration, National Identity and Co-Development'—given to his right-hand man [Brice Hortefeux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brice_Hortefeux)—and of a 'Ministry of Budget, Public Accounts and Civil Administration'—handed out to [Éric Wœrth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_W%C5%93rth), supposed to prepare the replacement of only a third of all civil servants who retire. However, after the 17 June parliamentary elections, the Cabinet was adjusted to 15 ministers and 16 deputy ministers, totalling 31 officials.

Sarkozy broke with the custom of [amnestying](18940624.md) traffic tickets and of releasing thousands of prisoners from overcrowded jails on [Bastille Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastille_Day), a tradition that [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) had started in 1802 to commemorate the [storming of the Bastille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storming_of_the_Bastille) during the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution).[^cite_note-WP_Moore-59]



Nicolas Sarkozy and General Jean-Louis Georgelin, Chief of the Defence Staff, reviewing troops during the Bastille Day 2008 military parade on the Champs-Élysées, Paris

In 2007 and 2008, French President Nicolas Sarkozy,[^cite_note-canada.com-60] Canadian Prime Minister [Stephen Harper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Harper), and Quebec Premier [Jean Charest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Charest)[^cite_note-61] all spoke in favour of a Canada – EU free trade agreement. In October 2008, Sarkozy became the first French President to address the [National Assembly of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Quebec). In his speech, he spoke out against Quebec separatism, but recognised Quebec as a nation within Canada. He said that, to France, Canada was a friend, and Quebec was family.[^cite_note-canada.com-60]

### Release of hostages

Shortly after taking office, Sarkozy began negotiations with Colombian president [Álvaro Uribe](243680.md) and the left-wing [guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla) [FARC](26100.md), regarding the release of hostages held by the rebel group, especially Franco-Colombian politician [Ingrid Betancourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrid_Betancourt). According to some sources, Sarkozy himself asked for Uribe to release FARC's "chancellor" [Rodrigo Granda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Granda).[^cite_note-62]

Furthermore, he announced on 24 July 2007, that French and European representatives had obtained the extradition of the [Bulgarian nurses detained in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV_trial_in_Libya) to their country. In exchange, he signed with [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) security, health care and immigration pacts—and a \$230 million (168 million euros) [MILAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MILAN) antitank missile sale.[^cite_note-WP_Moore-59] The contract was the first made by Libya since 2004, and was negotiated with [MBDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBDA), a subsidiary of [EADS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS). Another 128 million euro contract would have been signed, according to Tripoli, with EADS for a [TETRA radio system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_Trunked_Radio). The [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)) (PS) and the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_\(France\)) (PCF) criticised a "state affair" and a "barter" with a "[Rogue state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_state)".[^cite_note-63] The leader of the PS, [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Hollande), requested the opening of a parliamentary investigation.[^cite_note-WP_Moore-64]

### Green policy

On 8 June 2007, during the [33rd G8 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_G8_summit) in [Heiligendamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiligendamm), Sarkozy set a goal of reducing French [CO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) emissions by 50 percent by 2050 in order to prevent [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming). He then pushed forward Socialist [Dominique Strauss-Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Strauss-Kahn) as European nominee to the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF).[^cite_note-65] Critics alleged that Sarkozy proposed to nominate Strauss-Kahn as managing director of the IMF to deprive the Socialist Party of one of its more popular figures.[^cite_note-66]

In 2010, a study of [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale) and [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) universities ranked France the most respectful country of the [G20](2206608.md) concerning the environment.[^cite_note-67]

### Economic policy

The [Union for a Popular Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement) (UMP), Sarkozy's party, won a majority at the [June 2007 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_French_legislative_election), although by less than expected. In July, the UMP majority, seconded by the *[Nouveau Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouveau_Centre)*, ratified one of Sarkozy's electoral promises, which was to partially revoke the [inheritance tax](5824627.md).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] The inheritance tax formerly provided €8 billion in revenue.[^cite_note-70]



Sarkozy (at left) attending the [G-8 Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35th_G8_summit) in 2009

Sarkozy's UMP majority prepared a budget that reduced taxes, in particular for [upper middle-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_middle-class) people, supposedly in an effort to [boost GDP growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply-side_economics), but did not reduce state expenditures. He was criticised by the European Commission for doing so.

On 23 July 2008, parliament voted the "loi de modernisation de l'économie" (Modernization of the Economy Law), which loosened restrictions on retail prices and reduced limitations on the creation of businesses. The Government also made changes to long-standing French work-hour regulations, allowing employers to negotiate overtime with employees and making all hours worked past the traditional French 35-hour week tax-free.[^cite_note-71]



Nicolas Sarkozy addresses the E-G8 Forum in Paris in 2011.

However, as a result of the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), Sarkozy returned to the [state interventionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_interventionism) of his predecessors, declaring that "laissez-faire capitalism is over" and denouncing the "dictatorship of the market". Confronted with the suggestion that he had become a socialist, he responded: "Have I become socialist? Perhaps." He also pledged to create 100,000 state-subsidised jobs.[^cite_note-72]

### Security policy

Sarkozy's government issued a [decree](322949.md) on 7 August 2007 to generalise a voluntary [biometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric) profiling program of travellers in airports. The program, called 'Parafes', was to use fingerprints. The [new database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governmental_databases) would be interconnected with the [Schengen Information System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_Information_System) (SIS) as well as with a national database of wanted persons ([FPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fichier_des_Personnes_Recherch%C3%A9es)). The [Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_nationale_de_l'informatique_et_des_libert%C3%A9s) (CNIL) protested against this new decree, opposing itself to the recording of fingerprints and to the interconnection between the SIS and the FPR.[^cite_note-Parafes-73]

### Constitutional reform

On 21 July 2008, the French parliament passed constitutional reforms, which Sarkozy had made one of the key pledges of his presidential campaign. The vote was 539 to 357, one vote over the three-fifths majority required; the changes are not yet finalised. They would introduce a two-term limit for the presidency, and end the president's right of collective pardon. They would allow the president to address parliament in session, and parliament to set its own agenda. They would give parliament a veto over some presidential appointments, while ending government control over parliament's committee system. He claimed that these reforms strengthen parliament, while some opposition socialist lawmakers described it as a "consolidation of a monocracy".[^cite_note-74]

### International affairs



President Nicolas Sarkozy with [President of Brazil](273638.md) [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff)

During his 2007 presidential campaign, Sarkozy promised a strengthening of the [entente cordiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entente_cordiale) with the United Kingdom[^cite_note-75] and closer [cooperation with the United States](686250.md).[^cite_note-76] Despite this, Sarkozy opposed George W. Bush in granting [membership in NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO) for [Ukraine](31750.md) and [Georgia](48768.md) during the organization's [Bucharest summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Bucharest_summit) in April 2008, forming a common front with Germany's chancellor [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel) and foreign minister [Frank-Walter Steinmeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank-Walter_Steinmeier).[^cite_note-77]

Sarkozy wielded special international power when France held the rotating EU Council Presidency from July through December 2008. Sarkozy publicly stated his intention to attain EU approval of a [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) energy package before the end of his EU Presidency. This energy package would clearly define climate change objectives for the EU and hold members to specific reductions in emissions. In further support of his collaborative outlook on climate change, Sarkozy led the EU into a partnership with China.[^cite_note-78] On 6 December 2008, Nicolas Sarkozy, as part of France's then presidency of the Council of the EU, met the [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenzin_Gyatso) in Poland and outraged China, which announced that it would postpone the China-EU summit indefinitely.[^cite_note-79]



Sarkozy with British Prime Minister [David Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Cameron), November 2010

On 3 April 2009, at the [NATO](21133.md) Summit in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), Sarkozy announced that France would offer [asylum](1621432.md) to a former [Guantanamo captive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_captive).[^cite_note-HoustonChronicle2009-04-02-80][^cite_note-AssociatedPress2009-04-03-81] Sarkozy warned that "We are on the path to failure if we continue to act as we have" at the U.N. Climate Summit on 22 September 2009.[^cite_note-82]

On 5 January 2009, Sarkozy called for a ceasefire plan for the [Gaza Strip Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932009_Israel%E2%80%93Gaza_conflict).[^cite_note-83] The plan, which was jointly proposed by Sarkozy and Egyptian ex-President [Hosni Mubarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosni_Mubarak), envisioned the continuation of the delivery of aid to [Gaza](12047.md) and talks with Israel on border security, a key issue for Israel as it says [Hamas](13913.md) smuggles its rockets into Gaza through the Egyptian border. Welcoming the proposal, US Secretary of State [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice) called for a "ceasefire that can endure and that can bring real security".[^cite_note-84]

### Military intervention in Libya



Sarkozy at the Paris Summit of 19 March 2011, which marked the start of a military intervention in Libya

[Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md)'s official visit to Nicolas Sarkozy in December 2007 triggered a strong wave of protests against the President in France.[^cite_note-85]

In March 2011, after having been criticized for his unwillingness to support the Egyptian and Tunisian revolutions, and persuaded by the philosopher [Bernard-Henri Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard-Henri_Levy) to have France actively engage against the forces of the Libyan leader, [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md), Sarkozy was amongst the first Heads of State to demand the resignation of Gaddafi and his government, which was then fighting a [civil war in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_Civil_War). On 10 March 2011, Sarkozy welcomed to the Elysée Palace three emissaries from the [Libyan National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) (NTC), brought to him by Bernard-Henri Levy, who mediated at the meeting. Sarkozy promised them that a no-fly zone would be imposed on Gaddafi's aeroplanes. He also promised them French military assistance. On 17 March 2011, at the behest of France, [resolution 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1973) was adopted by the Security Council of the United Nations, permitting the creation of a "no fly" zone over Libya, and for the undertaking of "necessary measures" for the protection of the country's civilian population.

On 19 March 2011, Sarkozy officially announced the beginning of a [military intervention in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_military_intervention_in_Libya), with [France's participation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op%C3%A9ration_Harmattan). These actions of Sarkozy were favorably received by the majority of the French political class and public opinion.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]



Popularity polls during his presidency

In 2016, the Foreign Affairs Committee of the [British Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Parliament) published a report stating that the military intervention "was based on erroneous assumptions" that the threat of a massacre of civilian populations has been "overvalued" and that the coalition "Has not verified the real threat to civilians"; He also believes that the true motivations of Nicolas Sarkozy were to serve French interests and to "improve his political situation in France".[^cite_note-89]

### 2012 presidential campaign

Main article: [French presidential election, 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_presidential_election,_2012)

Sarkozy was one of ten candidates who qualified for the first round of voting.[^cite_note-conseil_constitutionnel-90] [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Hollande), the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)) candidate, received the most votes in the first round held on 22 April election, with Sarkozy coming second, meaning that both progressed to the second round of voting on 5–6 May 2012.[^cite_note-91] Sarkozy lost in the runoff and conceded to Hollande. He received an estimated 48.38% compared to Hollande's 51.62%.[^cite_note-92]

## Post-presidency (2012–present)

### Temporary retirement: 2012–2014

After his defeat at the 2012 election, Sarkozy asked his supporters to respect Hollande's victory. He invited his successor to attend his last 8 May [Victory in Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day) commemoration in office. His last day as President of the French Republic was 15 May.

Shortly after, Sarkozy briefly considered a career in private equity and secured a €250 million commitment from the [Qatar Investment Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Investment_Authority) to back his planned buyout firm. He abandoned his private equity plans when he decided to make a political comeback in 2014.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-ft.com-94]

### Return to politics: 2014–2016

Further information: [The Republicans (France) presidential primary, 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)_presidential_primary,_2016)



Nicolas Sarkozy's rally, Belfort, 12 March 2015

On 19 September 2014, Sarkozy announced that he was returning to politics and would run for chairman of the UMP party,[^cite_note-return-95] and was elected to the post on 29 November 2014.[^cite_note-96] Led by Sarkozy, UMP won over two-thirds of the 102 local *départements* in the nationwide elections on 29 March 2015.[^cite_note-97] On 13 December, the Republicans won the majority of regional office races, another set of national elections.[^cite_note-98] (On 30 May, the UMP's name was changed to the Republicans.)

In January 2016, Sarkozy published the book *[La France pour la vie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_France_pour_la_vie)*. Though he insisted it was not a campaign book, it was widely perceived as one, since he had previously published a book ahead of his campaign in 2007.[^cite_note-legifsarkozy-99] On August 22, he announced his candidacy for [2016 Republican presidential primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)_presidential_primary,_2016).[^cite_note-100] After placing third behind [François Fillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Fillon) and [Alain Juppé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Jupp%C3%A9), he decided to endorse Fillon and signaled his retirement from politics.[^cite_note-101]

### Retirement from politics: 2017–present



Sarkozy with [Mohammad bin Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_bin_Salman), [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md) and [Juan Carlos Varela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Varela) at the [FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in [Russia](25391.md), 14 June 2018

In 2020, Sarkozy was [charged with corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Sarkozy_corruption_trial) by French prosecutors in two cases, notably concerning the [alleged Libyan interference in the 2007 French elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alleged_Libyan_financing_in_the_2007_French_presidential_election). For his second conviction in September 2021, he received a one-year sentence, which he is allowed to serve under home confinement; his lawyer said he would appeal this decision.[^cite_note-France242021-102]

Despite his retirement, Sarkozy remained active and influential in conservative circles. On 18 December 2024, Sarkozy was definitively sentenced to three years in prison, including one to be served under electronic monitoring, for corruption and influence peddling. He has appealed the decision to the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

On 25 September 2025, Sarkozy was sentenced to five years in prison after being found guilty of [criminal conspiracy](3406102.md) in connection with alleged Libyan financing of his 2007 presidential campaign. He was acquitted of [passive corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_corruption), illegal campaign financing, and concealing the embezzlement of public funds.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

On 21 October 2025, Sarkozy was jailed in Paris to start a five-year sentence due to his connection with alleged Libyan financing of a presidential campaign, becoming the first French postwar leader to be incarcerated.[^cite_note-110]

## Controversies

During his presidency, Sarkozy was generally disliked by the left and criticised by some on the right, most vocally by moderate Gaullist supporters of [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac) and [Dominique de Villepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_de_Villepin).[^cite_note-135] The [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party)-leaning magazine *[L'Humanité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Humanit%C3%A9)* has accused Sarkozy of [populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism).[^cite_note-136]

### Views on religions



Many on the left distrust Sarkozy; specific "anti-Sarko" movements have been started.

In 2004 Sarkozy co-authored a book, *La République, les religions, l'espérance* (*The Republic, Religions, and Hope*),[^cite_note-137] in which he argued that the young should not be brought up solely on secular or [republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_France) values. He advocated reducing the [separation of church and state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state), arguing for the government [subsidies](146719.md) for mosques to encourage Islamic integration into French society.[^cite_note-138] He has opposed financing of religious institutions with funds from outside France. After meeting with [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise), Sarkozy was criticized by some for meeting with a member of the [Church of Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scientology), which has been seen by some as a cult.[^cite_note-139] Sarkozy was criticized by some after he claimed "the roots of France are essentially Christian" at a December 2007 speech in Rome. Similarly, he drew criticism after he called Islam "one of the greatest and most beautiful civilizations the world has known" at a speech in [Riyadh](51554.md) in January 2008.[^cite_note-140]

In the midst of a tense period and following the death of an 11-year-old boy, caught in the crossfire of a gang brawl in the Paris suburb of [La Courneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Courneuve) in June 2005, Sarkozy went to the scene and said: "*on va nettoyer au [Kärcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A4rcher) la cité*" ("we will clean the area with a pressure washer"). ### Ties to France's business elite

Several media outlets and commentators have documented the perception that Nicolas Sarkozy’s presidency was marked by unusually close ties to France's business elite,[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178] leading to accusation of ‘cronyism’.[^cite_note-179]

Academic research has examined how Nicolas Sarkozy’s 2007 election influenced corporate valuations and executive behavior in firms linked to him. In a 2014 study published in the *Journal of Public Economics*, by Renaud Coulomb and Marc Sangnier[^cite_note-180] analyzed 23 firms in the SBF 120 whose owners or executives were identified as close to Sarkozy. Comparing firms stock returns to broader market movements in the months before the election, they found that these firms experienced an average extra stock market increase of 3% due to his election, higher than the 2% observed for firms expected to benefit from his policy program, implying that personal ties to the president had a measurable economic effect.

A subsequent study, published in 2021 in the *Journal of the European Economic Association* by Thomas Bourveau, Renaud Coulomb, and Marc Sangnier,[^cite_note-181] examined insider trading behaviors of executives connected to Sarkozy. Using over 10,900 transactions by 1,827 executives between 2006 and 2008, the authors found that executives linked to Sarkozy were more likely to trade closer to company earnings announcements and to violate reporting deadlines after the election (compared to non-connected executives). Furthermore, market participants reacted positively to share purchases by executives connected to Sarkozy following his election (compared to non-connected executives), suggesting that investors perceived these trades as containing accrued valuable private information. The uncovered empirical evidence is coherent with an increase in illegal insider trading (the fact for an insider to trade their firm stocks using private information) for connected executives after his election. While these patterns do not establish *proven* illegal insider trading, they suggest that political proximity may have altered financial behavior, potentially reflecting a perception of reduced regulatory risk for some politically connected executives.

## Legal affairs

### Political and financial scandals and criminal convictions

On 5 July 2010, following its investigations on the [Bettencourt affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettencourt_affair), online newspaper [Mediapart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediapart) ran an article in which Claire Thibout, a former accountant of billionairess [Liliane Bettencourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liliane_Bettencourt), accused Sarkozy and [Eric Woerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Woerth) of receiving illegal campaign donations in 2007, in cash.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183]

On 1 July 2014, Sarkozy was detained for questioning by police over claims he had promised a prestigious role in Monaco to a high-ranking judge, Gilbert Azibert, in exchange for information about the investigation into alleged illegal campaign funding. Mr Azibert, one of the most senior judges at the [Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeal_\(France\)), was called in for questioning on 30 June 2014.[^cite_note-184] It is believed to be the first time a former French president has been held in police custody, although his predecessor, Jacques Chirac, was found guilty of embezzlement and breach of trust while he was mayor of Paris and given a suspended prison sentence in 2011.[^cite_note-185] After 15 hours in police custody, Sarkozy was put under official investigation for "active corruption", "misuse of influence" and "obtained through a breach of professional secrecy" on 2 July 2014.[^cite_note-186] Mr Azibert and Sarkozy's lawyer, Thierry Herzog, are also now under official investigation. The two accusations carry sentences of up to 10 years in prison.[^cite_note-187] The developments were seen as a blow to Sarkozy's attempts to challenge for the presidency in 2017.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189] Nevertheless, he later stood as a candidate for the Republican party nomination,[^cite_note-BBC_22_August_2016-190] but was eliminated from the contest in November 2016.[^cite_note-BBC_21_November_2016-191] A trial on this case, Sarkozy's first, started on 23 November 2020.[^cite_note-abc1-192]

On 16 February 2016, Sarkozy was indicted on "illegal financing of political campaign" charges related to overspending in his 2012 presidential campaign and retained as witness in connection with the [Bygmalion scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement#Bygmalion_Scandal).[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196]

In April 2016, Arnaud Claude, former law partner of Sarkozy, was named in the [Panama Papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Papers).[^cite_note-197]

On 23 November 2020, the [trial of Nicolas Sarkozy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Sarkozy_corruption_trial) started. He is accused of corruption and influence peddling, for an attempted [bribery](260979.md) of a judge. The trial was postponed until 26 November, following a request from one of his co-defendants for health reasons.[^cite_note-198]



Sarkozy in 2022

On 1 March 2021, a court in [Paris](22989.md) found former French President Nicolas Sarkozy guilty of corruption, trading in influence in a wiretapping and illegal data exchange case involving a number of individuals like magistrate [Gilbert Azibert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Azibert) and Sarkozy's former lawyer [Thierry Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Herzog). Both of these men were tried with him and all three were convicted. Sarkozy and his two co-defendants were sentenced to three years (two years of the sentence suspended) with twelve months in prison.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-verdictandsentence-200] Sarkozy appealed the ruling, which postponed any sentence imposement.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202] The appeal process in 2024, reduced his eventual prison incarceration by half, to that of six-months.[^cite_note-\:2-203]

On 20 May 2021, a second criminal trial, this time pertaining to the Bygmalion Scandal related to illegal campaign funding, began for Sarkozy, as well as 13 other defendants who were said to have been involved in the Bygmalion scandal.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-trialnext-205] Sarkozy's second corruption trial involved allegations of diverting tens of millions of euros which was intended to be spent on his failed 2012 re-election campaign and then hiring a PR firm to cover it up.[^cite_note-trialnext-205][^cite_note-sarkozysecond-206] The illicit campaign finance money was instead used to overspend on lavish campaign rallies and events.[^cite_note-sarkozysecond-206][^cite_note-trialnext-205]

On 30 September 2021, Sarkozy was convicted along with his co-defendants.[^cite_note-sarkozyagainconviction-207][^cite_note-sarkozysecond-206] For this conviction, Sarkozy was given a one-year prison sentence, though he was also given the option to serve this sentence at home with an electronic bracelet.[^cite_note-sarkozysecond-206][^cite_note-sarkozyagainconviction-207] On 18 December 2024, the Court of Cassation rejected Nicolas Sarkozy's appeal in cassation and the co-defendants, thus making Nicolas Sarkozy's conviction final; he immediately announced that they would refer the matter to the European Court of Human Rights.[^cite_note-208]

In 2023, Sarkozy's attempt to appeal the decision was denied, and he has been banned from holding public office for three years and will still have the option of serving his sentence from home with an electronic bracelet.[^cite_note-209]

In February 2024, an appeals court in Paris upheld an earlier 2021, lower court decision requiring Sarkozy to serve his sentence for the campaign overspending conviction.[^cite_note-210] However, the one-year sentence was revised so that he would instead serve six months of it in prison and six months of it suspended.[^cite_note-\:2-203]

### Libyan campaign financing case

Main article: [Libyan financing in the 2007 French presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_financing_in_the_2007_French_presidential_election)



Former Libyan leader Muammar Gaddafi allegedly paid €50 million to Sarkozy in exchange for access.

Shortly after his inauguration as President of France in 2007, Nicolas Sarkozy invited Libyan leader [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) to France over the objections of both the political opposition and members of his own government.[^cite_note-bbc2007-211] The visit marked the first time Gaddafi had been to France in more than 35 years and, during it, France agreed to sell Libya 21 [Airbus](26220236.md) aircraft and signed a nuclear cooperation agreement.[^cite_note-bbc2007-211] Negotiations for the purchase of more than a dozen [Dassault Rafale](33731380.md) fighter jets, plus military helicopters, were also initiated during the trip.[^cite_note-212]

During the 2011 [Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War) – a conflict in which France intervened – [Saif-al-Islam Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saif-al-Islam_Gaddafi) said in an interview with [euronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews) that the Libyan state had donated €50 million to Sarkozy's 2007 presidential campaign in exchange for access and favors by Sarkozy.[^cite_note-ind-213][^cite_note-eun12-214]

Investigative website [Mediapart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediapart) subsequently published several documents appearing to prove a payment of €50 million, and also published a claim by [Ziad Takieddine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziad_Takieddine) (disclaimer by the same person in another video) that he had personally handed three briefcases stuffed with cash to Sarkozy.[^cite_note-ind-213][^cite_note-bbc897-215] French magistrates later acquired diaries of former Libyan oil minister [Shukri Ghanem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shukri_Ghanem) in which payments to Sarkozy were mentioned.[^cite_note-f3242-216] Shortly thereafter, however, Ghanem was found dead, floating in the [Danube](21209639.md) in [Austria](26964606.md) and thereby preventing his corroboration of the diaries.[^cite_note-eun12-214][^cite_note-f3242-216]

In January 2018, British police arrested Alexandre Djouhri on a [European Arrest Warrant](2347206.md).[^cite_note-france3533-217] Djouhri was an associate of Sarkozy and had refused to respond to a French judicial summons for questioning over allegations he had helped launder Libyan funds on behalf of Sarkozy.[^cite_note-france3533-217]

On 25 September 2025, Sarkozy was convicted of criminal association in the Libyan financing case and sentenced to five years in prison and a €100,000 fine.[^cite_note-218] He was acquitted of other charges and announced he would appeal, while the court ruled the sentence enforceable pending appeal.[^cite_note-219] The verdict came two days after the death of key accuser [Ziad Takieddine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziad_Takieddine).[^cite_note-220]

On 21 October 2025, Sarkozy entered [La Santé Prison](627392.md) in [Montparnasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montparnasse) to commence his five-year term in segregation. Lawyers have applied for his release.[^cite_note-\:1-3] On 10 November 2025, a court ordered his release under judicial supervision pending an appeal trial scheduled in March 2026.[^cite_note-221] On 26 November, the [Court of Cassation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Cassation_\(France\)) upheld Sarkozy's conviction.[^cite_note-222]

Sarkozy spent roughly 20 days in prison. Sarkozy has stated he is innocent and that he plans to prove it in his upcoming 2026 appeal trial.[^cite_note-223]

In December 2025, Sarkozy published a memoir of his spell in prison, entitled *A Prisoner's Diary*.[^cite_note-224]
