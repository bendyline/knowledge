The **Federal Assembly**,[^cite_note-1] also known as the **Swiss Parliament**, is the federal [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) parliament[^cite_note-2] of [Switzerland](26748.md). It comprises the 200-seat [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)) and the 46-seat [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_States_\(Switzerland\)). It meets in [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern) in the [Federal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Palace_of_Switzerland).

The [houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_chamber) have identical powers. Members of both houses represent the [cantons](180752.md), but, whereas seats in the National Council are distributed in proportion to population, each canton has two seats in the Council of States, except the six '[half-cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-canton)', which have one seat each. Both are elected in full once every four years, with the [last election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Swiss_federal_election) being held in 2023.

The Federal Assembly possesses the federal government's [legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), along with the separate constitutional right of [citizen's initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative). For a law to pass, it must be passed by both houses. The two houses may come together as a [United Federal Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Federal_Assembly) in certain circumstances, such as to elect the [Federal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Council) (the [head of government](37570.md) and [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state)), the [Federal Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Chancellor_of_Switzerland), the [federal judges](19359207.md) or (only in times of great national danger) a [general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_\(Switzerland\)).

## History

Prior to the establishment of the [federal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_as_a_federal_state) in 1848, the only central organ of [Switzerland](26748.md) was the Federal Diet (*[Tagsatzung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagsatzung)*). Following the [Sonderbund War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonderbund_War) in 1847, the *Tagsatzung* became responsible for drawing up the [Swiss Federal Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Constitution).[^cite_note-DHS-3]

The process of formulating [legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) resulted in clashing opinions, in particular in relation to the representation of the various cantons: the [radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland), in the majority in the largest [cantons](180752.md), pushed for a system where representation was purely proportional to the population of each township; the small cantons, for their part, feared being marginalized. After long debates, a compromise was found by adopting the American model of [bicameralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism); the parliament will be composed of two chambers with equal power, and the agreement of both will be required to take a decision. The [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)), which represents the people, will comprise representatives from each canton with their distribution being proportional to the population of the cantons, while the [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_States_\(Switzerland\)), which represents the cantons, will be composed of the same number of representatives from each canton. According to the [Constitution of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_as_a_federal_state#1848_Constitution), the Federal Assembly is "the supreme authority of the Confederation".[^cite_note-DHS-3]

The *Tagsatzung* accepted the draft constitution in June 1848. On September 12, following the vote of the various cantons, it noted that the Constitution had been approved and dissolved itself on September 22, as required by the transitional provisions of the approved text. During the month of October 1848, elections were organized in the cantons in order to elect the deputies. After a few skirmishes, particularly in the canton of Fribourg, the results were announced which confirmed the victory of the radicals, who won more than three-quarters of the seats in the National Council and 30 of the 44 seats in the Council of States. On, November 16, 1848, Parliament elected the first Federal Council.[^cite_note-DHS-3] In 1874, following the revision of the Constitution and the introduction of extended popular rights, the Federal Assembly became "the supreme authority of the Confederation subject to the rights of the people and the cantons".[^cite_note-DHS-3]

The organization of the two councils has changed little over time. When the National Council was created, the total number of seats was 111.[^cite_note-DHS-3] This number was not fixed and evolved in proportion to the growth of the Swiss population until 1962 when the definitive number of seats was established at 200; the term of office, meanwhile, was increased from the original three years to four years in 1931. The mode of election, originally according to the majority system, transitioned to [proportional representation](23298.md) in 1918.[^cite_note-4] The Council of States, meanwhile, was not modified until 1979, by adding two new seats for the [Canton of Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Jura) which had just been created.[^cite_note-DHS-3]

## Composition

The Federal Assembly is made up of two chambers:

- the [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)), with 200 seats; and

- the [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_States_\(Switzerland\)), with 46 councillors.

Seats in the [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)) are allocated to the [cantons](180752.md) proportionally, based on population. In the [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_States_\(Switzerland\)), every [canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_\(country_subdivision\)) has two seats (except for the former "half-cantons", which have one seat each).

### United Federal Assembly

On occasions the two houses sit jointly as the "**United Federal Assembly**" ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Vereinigte Bundesversammlung*; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Assemblée fédérale, Chambres réunies*; [Italian](14708.md): *Assemblea federale plenaria*; [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language): *Assamblea federala plenara*). This is done to:

- elect members of the [Federal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Council), the [Federal Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Chancellor_of_Switzerland), the [federal judges](19359207.md) or (only in times of great national danger) a [general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_\(Switzerland\))

- arbitrate in the event of conflicts between [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government) authorities;

- issue [pardons](288593.md); or

- listen to special announcements

The United Federal Assembly is presided by the [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\))'s presidency.

The Federal Assembly also confirms the appointment of the [Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Data_Protection_and_Information_Commissioner) (appointed by the [Federal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Council)).[^cite_note-5]

Parties can cooperate in *parliamentary groups*, also called [political groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_group), allowing smaller parties access to rights as part of a [caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucus). At least five members from the same Council are needed to form a group. Only informal groups exist in the [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_States_\(Switzerland\)). Members of the [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)) are required to be in a formal group in order to be able to sit on a committee.[^cite_note-6]

Since March 2009, there have been six groups in the Federal Assembly. The latest group to form was the [Conservative Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland) which split off the Swiss People's Party in 2008. The [Christian Democrats/EPP/glp Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democrats/EPP/glp_Group) (CEg) was formed after the 2007 elections, out of the former Christian Democratic (C) and EPP (E) groups. The current FTP/Liberal group (RL) was formed in 2003 out of the former FDP (R) and Liberal (L) groups; since the 2009 fusion of the [Free Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland) and [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Switzerland) Parties, RL is once again a single-party group. In 2011, the CEg was disbanded, the Green Liberals formed their own parliamentary group (GL) and the three Christian parties formed the Christian-Evangelical Group (CE).
