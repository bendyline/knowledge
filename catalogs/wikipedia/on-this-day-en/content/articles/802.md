**Ankara**[^cite_note-14] is the [capital city](181337.md) of [Turkey](11125639.md). Located in the [central part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Anatolia_Region) of [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia), the city has a population of over 5.3 million residents in its urban center,[^cite_note-15] out of 6 million residents in [Ankara Province](370068.md).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-Population_of_Turkey-6][^cite_note-citypopulation.de-4] Ankara is Turkey's [second-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Turkey) by population after [Istanbul](3391396.md).

Ankara was historically known as Ancyra[^cite_note-17] and Angora.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Serving as the capital of the ancient [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) state of [Galatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatia) (280–64 BC), and later of the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) province with the [same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatia_\(Roman_province\)) (25 BC–7th century), Ankara has various [Hattian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattians), [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites), [Lydian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia), [Phrygian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygia), [Galatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatians_\(people\)), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_civilization), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), and [Ottoman](22278.md) [archeological sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archeological_site). The Ottomans made the city the capital of the [Anatolia Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia_Eyalet) (1393 – late 15th century), the [Angora Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Eyalet) (1827–1864), and the [Angora Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_Vilayet) (1867–1922). On 23 April 1920, the [Grand National Assembly of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey) was established in Ankara, which became the headquarters of the [Turkish National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement) during the [Turkish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_War_of_Independence). Ankara became the new Turkish capital upon the establishment of the Republic on 29 October 1923, succeeding the former Ottoman capital Istanbul following the [fall of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defeat_and_dissolution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire).

The historical center of Ankara is a rocky hill rising 150 m (500 ft) over the left bank of the [Ankara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_River), a tributary of the [Sakarya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakarya_River). The hill remains crowned by the ruins of [Ankara Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Castle). Few of its outworks have survived, but there are well-preserved examples of [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_architecture) and [Ottoman architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_architecture) throughout the city.

The [government](3680197.md) is a prominent employer, but Ankara is also an important commercial and industrial city located at the center of Turkey's road and railway networks. The city gave its name to the [Angora wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_wool) shorn from [Angora rabbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_rabbit), the long-haired [Angora goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_goat) (the source of [mohair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohair)), and the [Angora cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Angora). The area is also known for its pears, honey and [Muscat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat_\(grape\)) grapes. Although situated in one of the driest regions of Turkey and surrounded mostly by [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) vegetation (except for the forested areas on the southern periphery), Ankara can be considered a [green city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_city) in terms of green areas per inhabitant, at 72 square meters (775 square feet) per head.[^cite_note-21] Home to [numerous universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Ankara),[^cite_note-22] Ankara is among the top 100 science and technology clusters in the world.[^cite_note-23]

## Etymology

The orthography of the name *Ankara*[^cite_note-24] has varied over the ages. It has been identified with the [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites) cult center *Ankuwaš*,[^cite_note-qgmpff-25][^cite_note-xtxpyw-26] although this remains a matter of debate.[^cite_note-Gorny-27] In classical antiquity and during the medieval period, the city was known as *Ánkyra* (Ἄγκυρα, lit. '[anchor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchor)') in [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) and *Ancyra* in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin); the [Galatian Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatian_language) name was probably a similar variant. Following its annexation by the [Seljuk Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Turks) in 1073, the city became known in many European languages as *Angora*; it was also known in [Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language) as *Engürü* (انگورو).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaynes187845-28][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm191140–41-29] The form "Angora" is preserved in the names of breeds of many different kinds of animals, and in the names of several locations in the US (see [Angora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_\(disambiguation\))).

The region's history can be traced back to the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) [Hattic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattians) [civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattic_language), which was succeeded in the 2nd millennium BC by the [Hittites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites), in the 10th century BC by the [Phrygians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygia), and later by the [Lydians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia), [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), [Greeks](42056.md), [Galatians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatia), [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), and [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) (the [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Seljuq_Empire) [Sultanate of Rûm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_R%C3%BBm), the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and finally republican [Türkiye](11125639.md)).

### Ancient history

The oldest settlements in and around the city center of Ankara belonged to the [Hattic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattians) [civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattic_language) which existed during the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) and was gradually absorbed c. 2000 – 1700 BC by the [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_language) [Hittites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites). The city grew significantly in size and importance under the [Phrygians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygia) starting around 1000 BC, and experienced a large expansion following the mass migration from [Gordion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordium), (the capital of [Phrygia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygia)), after an earthquake which severely damaged that city around that time. In Phrygian tradition, King [Midas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midas) was venerated as the founder of Ancyra, but [Pausanias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausanias_\(geographer\)) mentions that the city was actually far older, which accords with present archeological knowledge.[^cite_note-30]

Phrygian rule was succeeded first by [Lydian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia) and later by [Persian](14653.md) rule, though the strongly Phrygian character of the peasantry remained, as evidenced by the gravestones of the much later Roman period. Persian sovereignty lasted until the Persians' defeat at the hands of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) who conquered the city in 333 BC. Alexander came from [Gordion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordium) to Ankara and stayed in the city for a short period. After his death at [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon) in 323 BC and the subsequent division of his empire among his generals, Ankara, and its environs fell into the share of [Antigonus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigonus_I_Monophthalmus).

Another important expansion took place under the [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic_Greeks) of [Pontos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontus_\(region\)) who came there around 300 BC and developed the city as a trading center for the commerce of goods between the [Black Sea](3386.md) ports and Crimea to the north; Assyria, Cyprus, and Lebanon to the south; and Georgia, Armenia and Persia to the east. By that time, the city also took its name Ἄγκυρα (*Ánkyra*, meaning *[anchor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchor)* in [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language)) which, in slightly modified form, provides the modern name of *Ankara*.

### Celtic history



The *[Dying Galatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dying_Galatian)* was a famous statue commissioned some time between 230 and 220 BC by King [Attalos I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attalus_I) of [Pergamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon) to honor his victory over the [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) [Galatians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatia) in [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia). Roman marble copy of a [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_civilization) work of the late 3rd century BC, at the [Capitoline Museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitoline_Museums), Rome.

In 278 BC, the city, along with the rest of central Anatolia, was occupied by a [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) group, the [Galatians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatia) (Gauls), who were the first to make Ankara one of their main tribal centers, the headquarters of the [Tectosages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectosages) tribe.[^cite_note-31] Other centers were [Pessinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pessinus), today's Ballıhisar, for the [Trocmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trocmi) tribe, and [Tavium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavium), to the east of Ankara, for the [Tolistobogii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolistobogii) tribe. The city was then known as *Ancyra*. The Celtic element was probably relatively small in numbers; a warrior aristocracy which ruled over [Phrygian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygian_language)-speaking peasants. However, the [Celtic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages) continued to be spoken in Galatia for many centuries. At the end of the 4th century, [St. Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome), a native of Dalmatia, observed that the language spoken around Ankara was very similar to that being spoken in the northwest of the Roman world near [Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trier).

### Roman history

The city was subsequently passed under the control of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). In 25 BC, Emperor [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) raised it to the status of a *[polis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polis)* and made it the capital city of the [Roman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Galatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatia_\(Roman_province\)).[^cite_note-TIB-32] Ankara is famous for the *[Monumentum Ancyranum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumentum_Ancyranum)* (*Temple of Augustus and Rome*) which contains the official record of the *Acts of Augustus*, known as the *[Res Gestae Divi Augusti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Res_Gestae_Divi_Augusti)*, an inscription cut in marble on the walls of this temple. The ruins of Ancyra still furnish today valuable [bas-reliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief), inscriptions and other architectural fragments. Two other Galatian tribal centers, [Tavium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavium) near [Yozgat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yozgat), and [Pessinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pessinus) (Balhisar) to the west, near Sivrihisar, continued to be reasonably important settlements in the Roman period, but it was Ancyra that grew into a grand metropolis.

An estimated 200,000 people lived in Ancyra in good times during the Roman Empire, a far greater number than was to be the case from after the fall of the Roman Empire until the early 20th century. The small [Ankara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_River) ran through the center of the Roman town. It has now been covered and diverted, but it formed the northern boundary of the old town during the Roman, Byzantine and Ottoman periods. Çankaya, the rim of the majestic hill to the south of the present city center, stood well outside the Roman city, but may have been a summer resort. In the 19th century, the remains of at least one [Roman villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_villa) or large house were still standing not far from where the Çankaya Presidential Residence stands today. To the west, the Roman city extended until the area of the Gençlik Park and Railway Station, while on the southern side of the hill, it may have extended downward as far as the site presently occupied by [Hacettepe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacettepe_University). It was thus a sizeable city by any standards and much larger than the Roman towns of [Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaul) or [Britannia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannia).

Ancyra's importance rested on the fact that it was the junction point where the roads in northern Anatolia running north–south and east–west intersected, giving it major strategic importance for Rome's eastern frontier.[^cite_note-TIB-32] The great imperial road running east passed through Ankara and a succession of emperors and their armies came this way. They were not the only ones to use the Roman highway network, which was equally convenient for invaders. In the second half of the 3rd century, Ancyra was invaded in rapid succession by the [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths) coming from the west (who rode far into the heart of [Cappadocia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappadocia), taking slaves and pillaging) and later by the [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_people). For about a decade, the town was one of the western outposts of one of Palmyrean empress [Zenobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenobia) in the [Syrian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Desert), who took advantage of a period of weakness and disorder in the Roman Empire to set up a short-lived state of her own.

The town was reincorporated into the Roman Empire under Emperor [Aurelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelian) in 272. The [tetrarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrarchy), a system of multiple (up to four) emperors introduced by [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian) (284–305), seems to have engaged in a substantial program of rebuilding and of road construction from Ancyra westwards to Germe and [Dorylaeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorylaeum) (now [Eskişehir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eski%C5%9Fehir)).

In its heyday, Roman Ancyra was a large market and trading center but it also functioned as a major administrative capital, where a high official ruled from the city's Praetorium, a large administrative palace or office. During the 3rd century, life in Ancyra, as in other Anatolian towns, seems to have become somewhat militarized in response to the invasions and instability of the town.

### Byzantine history



The *[Column of Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_of_Julianus)* (362) was erected in honor of the Roman emperor [Julian the Apostate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate)'s visit to Ancyra.

The city is well known during the 4th century as a center of Christian activity (see also [below](#Ecclesiastical_history)), due to frequent imperial visits, and through the letters of the pagan scholar [Libanius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libanius).[^cite_note-TIB-32] Bishop [Marcellus of Ancyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcellus_of_Ancyra) and [Basil of Ancyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_of_Ancyra) were active in the theological controversies of their day, and the city was the site of no fewer than three church synods in [314](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synod_of_Ancyra), 358 and 375, the latter two in favor of [Arianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianism).[^cite_note-TIB-32]

The city was visited by Emperor [Constans I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constans_I) (r. 337–350) in 347 and 350, [Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_\(emperor\)) (r. 361–363) during his Persian campaign in 362, and Julian's successor [Jovian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jovian_\(emperor\)) (r. 363–364) in winter 363/364 (he entered his [consulship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_consul) while in the city). After Jovian's death soon after, [Valentinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentinian_I) (r. 364–375) was acclaimed emperor at Ancyra, and in the next year his brother [Valens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valens) (r. 364–378) used Ancyra as his base against the usurper [Procopius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procopius_\(usurper\)).[^cite_note-TIB-32] When the province of Galatia was divided sometime in 396/99, Ancyra remained the civil capital of Galatia I, as well as its ecclesiastical center ([metropolitan see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_see)).[^cite_note-TIB-32] Emperor [Arcadius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadius) (r. 383–408) frequently used the city as his summer residence, and some information about the ecclesiastical affairs of the city during the early 5th century is found in the works of [Palladius of Galatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladius_of_Galatia) and Nilus of Ancyra.[^cite_note-TIB-32]

In 479, the rebel [Marcian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcian_\(usurper\)) attacked the city, without being able to capture it.[^cite_note-TIB-32] In 610/11, [Comentiolus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comentiolus_\(brother_of_Phocas\)), brother of Emperor [Phocas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phocas) (r. 602–610), launched his own unsuccessful rebellion in the city against [Heraclius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraclius) (r. 610–641).[^cite_note-TIB-32] Ten years later, in 620 or more likely 622, it was captured by the [Sassanid Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Persia) during the [Byzantine–Sassanid War of 602–628](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Sassanid_War_of_602%E2%80%93628). Although the city returned to Byzantine hands after the end of the war, the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) presence left traces in the city's archeology, and likely began the process of its transformation from a [late antique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antique) city to a medieval fortified settlement.[^cite_note-TIB-32]

In 654, the city, also known in Arabic sources as *Qalat as-Salasil* ("fortress of the chains"),[^cite_note-33] was captured for the first time by the [Arabs](2185.md) of the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate), under [Muawiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu'awiya_I), the future founder of the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate).[^cite_note-TIB-32] At about the same time, the [themes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_\(Byzantine_district\)) were established in Anatolia, and Ancyra became capital of the [Opsician Theme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opsician_Theme), which was the largest and most important theme until it was split up under Emperor [Constantine V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_V) (r. 741–775); Ancyra then became the capital of the new [Bucellarian Theme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucellarian_Theme).[^cite_note-TIB-32] The city was captured at least temporarily by the Umayyad prince [Maslama ibn Hisham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslama_ibn_Hisham) in 739/40, the last of the Umayyads' territorial gains from the Byzantine Empire.[^cite_note-34] Ancyra was attacked without success by [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid) forces in 776 and in 798/99. In 805, Emperor [Nikephoros I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikephoros_I) (r. 802–811) strengthened its fortifications, a fact which probably saved it from sack during the [large-scale invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_invasion_of_Asia_Minor_\(806\)) of Anatolia by Caliph [Harun al-Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harun_al-Rashid) in the next year.[^cite_note-TIB-32] Arab sources report that Harun and his successor [al-Ma'mun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ma'mun) (r. 813–833) took the city, but this information is later invention. In 838, however, during the [Amorium campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Amorium), the armies of Caliph [al-Mu'tasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mu'tasim) (r. 833–842) converged and met at the city; abandoned by its inhabitants, Ancara was razed to the ground, before the Arab armies went on to besiege and destroy [Amorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amorium) reaching as far as [Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna).[^cite_note-TIB-32] In 859, Emperor [Michael III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_III) (r. 842–867) came to the city during a campaign against the Arabs, and ordered its fortifications restored.[^cite_note-TIB-32] In 872, the city was menaced, but not taken, by the [Paulicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulicians) under [Chrysocheir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysocheir).[^cite_note-TIB-32] The last Arab raid to reach the city was undertaken in 931, by the Abbasid governor of [Tarsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarsus_\(city\)), [Thamal al-Dulafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thamal_al-Dulafi), but the city again was not captured.[^cite_note-TIB-32]

### Ecclesiastical history



[St. Theodotus of Ancyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodotus_of_Ancyra_\(martyr\))

Early Christian martyrs of Ancyra, about whom little is known, included Proklos and Hilarios who were natives of the otherwise unknown nearby village of Kallippi, and suffered repression under the emperor [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) (98–117). In the 280s we hear of Philumenos, a Christian corn merchant from southern Anatolia, being captured and martyred in Ankara, and Eustathius.

As in other Roman towns, the reign of [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian) marked the culmination of the persecution of the Christians. In 303, Ancyra was one of the towns where the co-emperors Diocletian and his deputy [Galerius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galerius) launched their anti-Christian persecution. In Ancyra, their first target was the 38-year-old Bishop of the town, whose name was Clement. Clement's life describes how he was taken to Rome, then sent back, and forced to undergo many interrogations and hardship before he, and his brother, and various companions were put to death. The remains of the church of [St. Clement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_of_Ancyra) can be found today in a building just off Işıklar Caddesi in the Ulus district. Quite possibly this marks the site where Clement was originally buried. Four years later, a doctor of the town named Plato and his brother Antiochus also became celebrated martyrs under Galerius. [Theodotus of Ancyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodotus_of_Ancyra_\(martyr\)) is also venerated as a saint.

However, the persecution proved unsuccessful and in 314 Ancyra was the center of [an important council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synod_of_Ancyra) of the [early church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERockwell1911-35] its 25 disciplinary canons constitute one of the most important documents in the early history of the administration of the [Sacrament of Penance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confession_\(religion\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERockwell1911-35] The synod also considered ecclesiastical policy for the reconstruction of the [Christian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church) after the persecutions, and in particular the treatment of *[lapsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapsi_\(Christianity\))*—[Christians](18337522.md) who had given in to forced [paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism) (sacrifices) to avoid [martyrdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrdom) during these persecutions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERockwell1911-35]

Though paganism was probably tottering in Ancyra in Clement's day, it may still have been the majority religion. Twenty years later, Christianity and [monotheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotheism) had taken its place. Ancyra quickly turned into a Christian city, with a life dominated by monks and priests and theological disputes. The town council or senate gave way to the bishop as the main local figurehead. During the middle of the 4th century, Ancyra was involved in the complex theological disputes over the nature of Christ, and a form of [Arianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianism) seems to have originated there.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParvis2006325–345-36]

In 362–363, Emperor Julian passed through Ancyra on his way to an ill-fated campaign against the Persians, and according to Christian sources, engaged in a persecution of various holy men.[^cite_note-gibbon-37] The stone base for a statue, with an inscription describing Julian as "Lord of the whole world from the British Ocean to the barbarian nations", can still be seen, built into the eastern side of the inner circuit of the walls of Ankara Castle. The Column of Julian which was erected in honor of the emperor's visit to the city in 362 still stands today. In 375, Arian bishops met at Ancyra and deposed several bishops, among them [St. Gregory of Nyssa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Nyssa).

In the late 4th century, Ancyra became something of an imperial [holiday resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort). After [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) became the [East Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) capital, emperors in the 4th and 5th centuries would retire from the humid summer weather on the [Bosporus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosphorus) to the drier mountain atmosphere of Ancyra. [Theodosius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_II) (408–450) kept his court in Ancyra in the summers. Laws issued in Ancyra testify to the time they spent there.

The [Metropolis of Ancyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_of_Ancyra) continued to be a residential [see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_see) of the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md) until the 20th century, with about 40,000 faithful, mostly Turkish-speaking, but that situation ended as a result of the 1923 [Convention Concerning the Exchange of Greek and Turkish Populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_Concerning_the_Exchange_of_Greek_and_Turkish_Populations). The earlier [Armenian genocide](140376.md) put an end to the residential eparchy of Ancyra of the [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church), which had been established in 1850.[^cite_note-Bull_Universi_Dominici_gregis-38][^cite_note-gallica.bnf.fr-39] It is also a titular metropolis of the [Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople).

Both the Ancient Byzantine Metropolitan archbishopric and the 'modern' Armenian eparchy are now listed by the [Catholic Church](606848.md) as [titular sees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see),[^cite_note-40] with separate [apostolic successions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_succession).

### Seljuk and Ottoman history



Ottoman houses in Hamamönü district

After the [Battle of Manzikert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manzikert) in 1071, the [Seljuk Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Turks) overran much of Anatolia. By 1073, the Turkish settlers had reached the vicinity of Ancyra, and the city was captured shortly after, at the latest by the time of the rebellion of [Nikephoros Melissenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikephoros_Melissenos) in 1081.[^cite_note-TIB-32] In 1101, when the [Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusade_of_1101) under [Raymond IV of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_IV_of_Toulouse) arrived, the city had been under [Danishmend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danishmend) control for some time. The Crusaders captured the city, and handed it over to the Byzantine emperor [Alexios I Komnenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexios_I_Komnenos) (r. 1081–1118).[^cite_note-TIB-32] Byzantine rule did not last long, and the city was captured by the Seljuk [Sultanate of Rum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Rum) at some unknown point; in 1127, it returned to Danishmend control until 1143, when the Seljuks of Rum retook it.[^cite_note-TIB-32]

After the [Battle of Köse Dağ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_K%C3%B6se_Da%C4%9F) in 1243, in which the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) defeated the Seljuks, most of Anatolia became part of the dominion of the Mongols. Taking advantage of Seljuk decline, a semi-religious cast of craftsmen and trade people named *[Ahiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahis)* chose Angora as their independent city-state in 1290. [Orhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orhan), the second [Bey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bey) of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), captured the city in 1356. [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) defeated [Bayezid I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayezid_I) at the [Battle of Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ankara) in 1402 and took the city, but in 1403 Angora was again under Ottoman control.

The [Levant Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant_Company) maintained a factory in the town from 1639 to 1768.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm191140–41-29] In the 19th century, its population was estimated at 20,000 to 60,000.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaynes187845-28] It was sacked by [Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khedivate_of_Egypt) under [Ibrahim Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Pasha_of_Egypt) in 1832.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm191140–41-29]

From 1867 to 1922, the city served as the capital of the [Angora Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_Vilayet), which included most of ancient Galatia.

Prior to [World War I](4764461.md), the town had a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) [consulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consul_\(representative\)) and a population of around 28,000, roughly 1⁄3 of whom were Christian.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm191140–41-29]

### Turkish republican capital



President [Mustafa Kemal Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) (center) and Prime Minister [İsmet İnönü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0smet_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC) (left) leaving the [Grand National Assembly of Türkiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey) during the 7th anniversary celebrations of the Turkish Republic in 1930

Following the [Ottoman](22278.md) defeat in [World War I](4764461.md), the Ottoman capital [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Constantinople) (modern Istanbul) and much of [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) was occupied by the Allies, who planned to share these lands between [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece_\(Gl%C3%BCcksburg\)), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) and the United Kingdom, leaving for the Turks the core piece of land in central Anatolia. In response, the leader of the Turkish nationalist movement, [Mustafa Kemal Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk), established the headquarters of his [resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement) in Angora in 1920. After the [Turkish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_War_of_Independence) was won and the [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) was superseded by the [Treaty of Lausanne (1923)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne_\(1923\)), the Turkish nationalists replaced the Ottoman Empire with the [Republic of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Turkey) on 29 October 1923. A few days earlier, Angora had officially replaced Constantinople as the new Turkish capital city, on 13 October 1923,[^cite_note-britannica-41] and Republican officials declared that the city's name is Ankara.[^cite_note-42]



A view of the old general directorate building of [Ziraat Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziraat_Bankas%C4%B1). It was designed by Istanbul-born [Italian Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantines_\(Latin_Christians\)) architect Giulio Mongeri and built between 1926 and 1929.

After Ankara became the capital of the newly founded Republic of Turkey, new development divided the city into an old section, called *Ulus*, and a new section, called *Yenişehir*. Ancient buildings reflecting Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman history and narrow winding streets mark the old section. The new section, now centered on [Kızılay Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%B1z%C4%B1lay_Meydan%C4%B1), has the trappings of a more modern city: wide streets, hotels, theaters, shopping malls, and high-rises.

Government offices and foreign embassies are also located in the new section. Ankara has experienced a phenomenal growth since it was made Turkey's capital in 1923, when it was "a small town of no importance".[^cite_note-43] In 1924, the year after the government had moved there, Ankara had about 35,000 residents. By 1927 there were 44,553 residents and by 1950 the population had grown to 286,781. After 1930, the city officially became known in Western languages as Ankara. By the late 1930s, the English name "Angora" was no longer in popular use.[^cite_note-44]



The [Presidential Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Library_\(Turkey\)) in Ankara is the largest library in Turkey, with a collection of over 4 million printed books and over 120 million electronic editions published in 134 languages.

[^cite_note-Presidential_Library-45]

Ankara continued to grow rapidly during the latter half of the 20th century and eventually outranked [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir) as Turkey's second-largest city, after [Istanbul](3391396.md). Ankara's urban population reached 4,587,558 in 2014, while the population of [Ankara Province](370068.md) reached 5,150,072 in 2015.[^cite_note-citypopulation1-46]

The [Presidential Palace of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Complex_\(Turkey\)) is situated in Ankara. This building serves as the main residence of the president.

Geographically, Ankara is located in between the [Kızılırmak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%B1z%C4%B1l%C4%B1rmak_River) and [Sakarya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakarya_River) rivers, and the Sakarya River forms its border with [Eskişehir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eski%C5%9Fehir) in the west. Ankara shares its borders with [Bolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolu) and [Çankırı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87ank%C4%B1r%C4%B1) in the north; [Konya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konya) in the south and [Kırıkkale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%B1r%C4%B1kkale) in the east.[^cite_note-49]

Ankara and [its province](370068.md) are located in the [Central Anatolia Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Anatolia_Region) of Turkey. The Çubuk Brook flows through the city center of Ankara. It is connected in the western suburbs of the city to the [Ankara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_River), which is a [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the [Sakarya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakarya_River).

### Climate

Ankara has a [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) (*BSk*), while under the [Trewartha climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification), the city is classified as [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dc*). Due to its elevation and inland location, Ankara has cold and snowy winters, and hot and dry summers. Rainfall occurs mostly during the spring and autumn. The city lies in [USDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDA) [Hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone) 7b, and its annual average precipitation is fairly low at 414 millimeters (16 in), nevertheless precipitation can be observed throughout the year. Monthly mean temperatures range from 0.9 °C (33.6 °F) in January to 24.3 °C (75.7 °F) in July, with an annual mean of 12.6 °C (54.7 °F).[^cite_note-eather1-50] Ankara's overall temperature regime is very similar to [New York City](645042.md).

Ankara [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area)

Ankara had a population of 75,000 in 1927. There were 74,632 male residents and 48,882 female residents in Ankara according to the 1935 census.[^cite_note-54] As of 2022, the population of the Ankara Province was 5,782,285.[^cite_note-tuik-53] When Ankara became the capital of the Republic of Turkey in 1923, it was designated as a planned city for 500,000 future inhabitants. During the 1920s, 1930s and 1940s, the city grew in a planned and orderly pace. However, from the 1950s onward, the city grew much faster than envisioned, because unemployment and poverty forced people to migrate from the countryside into the city to seek a better standard of living. As a result, many illegal houses called [gecekondu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gecekondu) were built around the city, causing the unplanned and uncontrolled urban landscape of Ankara, as not enough planned housing could be built fast enough. Although precariously built, the vast majority of them have electricity, running water and modern household amenities.

Nevertheless, many of these gecekondus have been replaced by huge public housing projects in the form of [tower blocks](118767.md) such as Elvankent, [Eryaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eryaman,_Etimesgut) and [Güzelkent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCzelkent,_Etimesgut); and also as mass housing compounds for military and civil service accommodation. Although many gecekondus still remain, they too are gradually being replaced by mass housing compounds, as empty land plots in the city of Ankara for new construction projects are becoming impossible to find.

[Çorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87orum) and [Yozgat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yozgat), which are located in [Central Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Anatolia_region) and whose population is decreasing, are the provinces with the highest net migration to Ankara.[^cite_note-55] About one third of the Central Anatolia population of 15,608,868 people resides in Ankara.

The [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_rate) in the whole province for people who are 15 years old or older is 98.18% according to 2020 TÜİK data. Ankara Province also has the highest percentage of tertiary education graduates in Turkey with 29.08% of the population having either an undergraduate, master's or doctor's degree.[^cite_note-56]

## Economy and infrastructure



Başkent Millet Park in Ankara

Ankara has long been a productive agricultural region in Anatolia. In the Ottoman period, Ankara was well known for producing grain, cotton, and fruits.[^cite_note-57]

The city has exported [mohair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohair) (from the [Angora goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_goat)) and [Angora wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_wool) (from the [Angora rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_rabbit)) internationally for centuries. In the 19th century, the city also exported substantial amounts of [goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goatskin_\(material\)) and cat skins, [gum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gum_arabic), [wax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beeswax), [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey), berries, and [madder root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madder_root).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaynes187845-28] It was connected to [Istanbul](3391396.md) by railway before the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), continuing to export mohair, wool, berries, and grain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm191140–41-29]

The [Central Anatolia Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Anatolia_Region) is one of the primary locations of grape and [wine production in Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_wine), and Ankara is particularly famous for its [Kalecik Karası](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalecik_Karas%C4%B1) and [Muscat grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat_\(grape_and_wine\)); and its [Kavaklıdere wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavakl%C4%B1dere_\(winery\)), which is produced in the [Kavaklıdere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavakl%C4%B1dere,_Ankara) neighborhood within the [Çankaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87ankaya,_Ankara) district of the city. Ankara is also famous for its pears. Another natural product of Ankara is its indigenous type of [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey) (*Ankara Balı*) which is known for its light color and is mostly produced by the [Atatürk Forest Farm and Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk_Forest_Farm_and_Zoo) in the Gazi district, and by other facilities in the Elmadağ, Çubuk and Beypazarı districts. [Çubuk-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87ubuk-1_Dam) and [Çubuk-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87ubuk-2_Dam) dams on the Çubuk Brook in Ankara were among the first dams constructed in the Turkish Republic.



[Kızılay Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%B1z%C4%B1lay_Square) and [Emek Business Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahramanlar_Business_Center) (1959–1965), the first [International Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Style_\(architecture\)) office tower and shopping center in Turkey.

[^cite_note-59]

Ankara is the center of the state-owned and private Turkish [defence](608296.md) and [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_manufacturer) companies, where the industrial plants and headquarters of the [Turkish Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Aerospace_Industries), [MKE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_and_Chemical_Industry_Corporation_\(Turkey\)), [ASELSAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASELSAN), [HAVELSAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAVELSAN), [ROKETSAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROKETSAN), [FNSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FNSS_Defence_Systems),[^cite_note-60] [Nurol Makina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurol_Makina),[^cite_note-61] and numerous other firms are located. Exports to foreign countries from these defense and aerospace firms have steadily increased in the past decades. The [IDEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Defence_Industry_Fair) in Ankara is one of the largest international expositions of the global arms industry. A number of the global [automotive companies](163778.md) also have production facilities in Ankara, such as the German bus and truck manufacturer [MAN SE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAN_SE).[^cite_note-62] Ankara hosts the [OSTIM Industrial Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSTIM_Industrial_Zone), Turkey's largest [industrial park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_park).

A large percentage of the complicated employment in Ankara is provided by the state institutions; such as the [ministries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\)), subministries, and other administrative bodies of the Turkish government. There are also many foreign citizens working as diplomats or clerks in the [embassies](8970.md) of their respective countries.

### Ankara public transportation statistics

The average amount of time people spend commuting on public transit in Ankara on a weekday is 71 minutes. 17% of public transit passengers, ride for more than two hours every day. The average amount of time people wait at a stop or station for public transit is sixteen minutes, while 28% of users wait for over twenty minutes on average every day. The average distance people usually ride in a single trip with public transit is 9.9 km (6.2 mi), while 27% travel for over 12 km (7.5 mi) in a single direction.[^cite_note-66]

## Politics

See also: [List of mayors of Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Ankara)





[Mansur Yavaş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansur_Yava%C5%9F) of the [CHP](404748.md) is the Mayor of Ankara.

Since 8 April 2019, the mayor of Ankara is [Mansur Yavaş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansur_Yava%C5%9F) from the [Republican People's Party](404748.md) (CHP), who won the [mayoral election in 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Ankara_mayoral_election) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Ankara_mayoral_election).

&#x20;Ankara is politically a triple battleground between the ruling conservative [AK Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK_Party), the opposition [Kemalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemalism) center-left [Republican People's Party](404748.md) (CHP) and the nationalist far-right [MHP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Movement_Party). The [province of Ankara](370068.md) is divided into 25 districts. Historically, the CHP's key and almost only political stronghold in Ankara lay within the central area of [Çankaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87ankaya,_Ankara_\(district\)), which is the city's most populous district. While the CHP has always gained between 60 and 70% of the vote in Çankaya since 2002, political support elsewhere throughout Ankara was minimal. The high population within Çankaya, as well as [Yenimahalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenimahalle) to an extent, has allowed the CHP to take overall second place behind the AK Party in both local and general elections, with the MHP a close third, despite the fact that the MHP was politically stronger than the CHP in almost every other district. Overall, the AK Party enjoyed the most support throughout the city. The electorate of Ankara thus tended to vote in favor of the political right, far more so than the other main cities of [Istanbul](3391396.md) and [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir). In retrospect, the [2013–14 protests against the AK Party government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_protests_in_Turkey) were particularly strong in Ankara, proving to be fatal on multiple occasions.[^cite_note-67]

## Main sights

### Ancient/archeological sites



[Ankara castle and citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Castle)



At the [Monumentum Ancyranum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumentum_Ancyranum) (*Temple of Augustus and Rome*) in Ulus, the primary intact copy of [Res Gestae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Res_Gestae_Divi_Augusti) written by the first Roman emperor [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) survives.

The foundations of the [Ankara castle and citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Castle) were laid by the Galatians on a prominent [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) outcrop ([39°56′28″N 32°51′50″E  /  39.941°N 32.864°E  / 39.941; 32.864](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Ankara\&params=39.941_N_32.864_E_type\:landmark)), and the rest was completed by the Romans. The Byzantines and Seljuks further made restorations and additions. The area around and inside the citadel, being the oldest part of Ankara, contains many fine examples of traditional architecture. There are also recreational areas to relax. Many restored traditional Turkish houses inside the citadel area have found new life as restaurants, serving local cuisine. The citadel was depicted in various Turkish banknotes during 1927–1952 and 1983–1989.[^cite_note-74]

The remains, the stage, and the backstage of the [Antique Rome theatre of Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antique_Rome_theatre_of_Ankara) can be seen outside [the castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Castle). Roman statues that were found here are exhibited in the [Museum of Anatolian Civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Anatolian_Civilizations). The seating area is still under excavation.

The Augusteum,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911b953-75] now known as the [Temple of Augustus and Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Augustus_and_Rome), was built 25 x 20 BC following the conquest of Central Anatolia by the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). Ancyra then formed the capital of the new [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Galatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatia). After the death of [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) in AD 14, a copy of the text of the *[Res Gestae Divi Augusti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Res_Gestae_Divi_Augusti)* (the *[Monumentum Ancyranum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumentum_Ancyranum)*) was inscribed on the interior of the temple's *_pronaos_* in Latin and a Greek translation on an exterior wall of the *_cella_*. The temple on the ancient acropolis of Ancyra was enlarged in the 2nd century and converted into a church in the 5th century. It is located in the Ulus quarter of the city. It was subsequently publicized by the [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) ambassador [Ogier Ghiselin de Busbecq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogier_Ghiselin_de_Busbecq) in the 16th century.

The [Roman Baths of Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Baths_of_Ankara) have all the typical features of a classical [Roman bath complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermae): a *[frigidarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigidarium)* (cold room), a *[tepidarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepidarium)* (warm room) and a *[caldarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldarium)* (hot room). The baths were built during the reign of the Roman emperor [Caracalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracalla) in the early 3rd century to honor [Asclepios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asclepius), the God of Medicine. Today, only the basement and first floors remain. It is situated in the Ulus quarter.

The [Roman Road of Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Road_of_Ankara) or *Cardo Maximus* was found in 1995 by Turkish archeologist Cevdet Bayburtluoğlu. It is 216 meters (709 feet) long and 6.7 meters (22.0 feet) wide. Many ancient artifacts were discovered during the excavations along the road and most of them are displayed at the [Museum of Anatolian Civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Anatolian_Civilizations).[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-Antik_Ankara-77]

The Column of Julian or Julianus, now in the Ulus district, was erected in honor of the Roman emperor [Julian the Apostate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate)'s visit to Ancyra in 362.

### Mosques





**Top**: Kocatepe Mosque (1987)\
**Bottom**: Ahmet Hamdi Akseki Mosque (2013)

[Kocatepe Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kocatepe_Mosque) is the largest mosque in the city. Located in the Kocatepe quarter, it was constructed between 1967 and 1987 in classical [Ottoman style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_architecture) with four minarets. Its size and prominent location have made it a landmark for the city.

[Ahmet Hamdi Akseki Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmet_Hamdi_Akseki_Mosque) is located near the Presidency of Religious Affairs on the [Eskişehir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eski%C5%9Fehir) Road. Built in the Turkish neoclassical style, it is one of the largest new mosques in the city, completed and opened in 2013. It can accommodate 6 thousand people during general prayers, and up to 30 thousand people during funeral prayers. The mosque was decorated with [Anatolian Seljuk style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_Seljuk_architecture) patterns.[^cite_note-78]

Yeni (Cenab Ahmet) Mosque is the largest Ottoman mosque in Ankara and was built by the famous architect Sinan in the 16th century. The mimber (pulpit) and mihrap (prayer niche) are of white marble, and the mosque itself is of Ankara stone, an example of very fine workmanship.



[Hacı Bayram Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hac%C4%B1_Bayram_Mosque) (1428)

[Hacı Bayram Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hac%C4%B1_Bayram_Mosque), in the Ulus quarter next to the [Temple of Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumentum_Ancyranum), was built in the early 15th century in Seljuk style by an unknown architect. It was subsequently restored by architect [Mimar Sinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimar_Sinan) in the 16th century, with [Kütahya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BCtahya) tiles being added in the 18th century. The mosque was built in honor of [Hacı Bayram-ı Veli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hac%C4%B1_Bayram-%C4%B1_Veli), whose tomb is next to the mosque, two years before his death (1427–28).[^cite_note-79] The usable space inside this mosque is 437 m<sup>2</sup> (4,704 sq ft) on the first floor and 263 m<sup>2</sup> (2,831 sq ft) on the second floor.

Ahi Elvan Mosque was founded in the Ulus quarter near the Ankara Citadel and was constructed by the [Ahi fraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahis) during the late 14th and early 15th centuries. The finely carved walnut mimber (pulpit) is of particular interest.[^cite_note-80]

The [Alâeddin Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al%C3%A2eddin_Mosque) is the oldest mosque in Ankara. It has a [carved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_carving) [walnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juglans) [mimber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minbar), the inscription on which records that the mosque was completed in early [AH 574](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar) (which corresponds to the summer of 1178 AD) and was built by the [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_dynasty) prince Muhiddin Mesud Şah (died 1204), the [Bey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bey) of Ankara, who was the son of the [Anatolian Seljuk sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_Seljuk_Sultanate) [Kılıç Arslan II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilij_Arslan_II) (reigned 1156–1192.)

### Modern monuments

There are at least 50 monuments and reliefs throughout the city. Some notables are:

The [Security Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Monument), located in [Güvenpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCvenpark) near Kızılay Square, was erected in 1935 and bears Atatürk's advice to his people: "Turk! Be proud, work hard, and believe in yourself." (There is debate on whether or not Atatürk actually said "Use your mind"(Turkish: *öğün*) instead of "Be proud"(Turkish: *övün*))[^cite_note-82]

The monument was depicted on the [reverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obverse_and_reverse) of the Turkish 5 [lira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_lira) banknote of 1937–1952 and of the 1000 lira banknotes of 1939–1946.[^cite_note-\:0-83]

Erected in 1978 at [Sıhhiye Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C4%B1hhiye_Square), the Hatti Monument impressive monument symbolizes the [Hatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattians) Sun Disc (which was later adopted by the [Hittites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites)) and commemorates Anatolia's earliest known civilization. The Hatti Sun Disc has been used in the previous logo of Ankara Metropolitan Municipality. It was also used in the previous logo of the Ministry of Culture & Tourism.

The [Monument to Turkish Soldiers Fighting in Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_Turkish_Soldiers_Fighting_in_Korea) opened in 1973 to commemorate the veterans and martyrs of the [Turkish Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Brigade).[^cite_note-84]

### Inns



[Çengelhan Rahmi M. Koç Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87engelhan_Rahmi_M._Ko%C3%A7_Museum)'s courtyard has been covered with a glass roof.

[Suluhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suluhan) is a historical Inn in Ankara. It is also called the *Hasanpaşa Han*. It is about 400 meters (1,300 ft) southeast of [Ulus Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulus_Square) and situated in the Hacıdoğan neighborhood. According to the *vakfiye* (inscription) of the building, the Ottoman era *han* was commissioned by Hasan Pasha, a regional [beylerbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beylerbey), and was constructed between 1508 and 1511, during the final years of the reign of Sultan [Bayezid II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayezid_II).[^cite_note-Ank-85] There are 102 rooms (now shops) which face the two yards.[^cite_note-86] In each room there is a window, a niche and a chimney.[^cite_note-Ank-85]

[Çengelhan Rahmi M. Koç Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87engelhan_Rahmi_M._Ko%C3%A7_Museum) is a museum of industrial technology situated in [Çengel Han](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87engel%20Han), an Ottoman era Inn which was completed in 1523, during the early years of the reign of Sultan [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent). The exhibits include industrial/technological artifacts from the 1850s onwards. There are also sections about Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, the founder of modern Turkey; Vehbi Koç, Rahmi Koç's father and one of the first industrialists of Turkey, and Ankara city.

## Shopping



Armada Shopping Mall

Foreign visitors to Ankara usually like to visit the old shops in *Çıkrıkçılar Yokuşu* (Weavers' Road) near Ulus, where myriad things ranging from traditional fabrics, hand-woven carpets and leather products can be found at bargain prices. *Bakırcılar Çarşısı* (Bazaar of Coppersmiths) is particularly popular, and many interesting items, not just of copper, can be found here like jewelry, carpets, costumes, antiques and embroidery. Up the hill to the castle gate, there are many shops selling a huge and fresh collection of spices, [dried fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dried_fruit), nuts, and other produce.



[Atakule Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atakule) and Atrium Shopping Mall

Modern shopping areas are mostly found in [Kızılay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%B1z%C4%B1lay,_Ankara), or on Tunalı Hilmi Avenue, including the modern mall of [Karum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_shopping_malls_in_Turkey#Ankara) (named after the ancient [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria) merchant colonies called [*Kârum*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BCltepe#K%C3%A2rum_Kane%C5%A1) that were established in [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Anatolia_Region) [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) at the beginning of the [2nd millennium BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_millennium_BC)) which is located towards the end of the Avenue; and in [Çankaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87ankaya,_Ankara), the quarter with the highest elevation in the city. [Atakule Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atakule) next to [Atrium Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atakule#Atrium_shopping_mall) in Çankaya has views over Ankara and also has a [revolving restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolving_restaurant) at the top. The symbol of the Armada Shopping Mall is an [anchor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchor), and there is a large anchor monument at its entrance, as a reference to the ancient Greek name of the city, Ἄγκυρα (Ánkyra), which means anchor. Likewise, the anchor monument is also related with the Spanish name of the mall, Armada, which means [naval fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_fleet).



[Sheraton Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheraton_Ankara) and Karum Shopping Mall

As Ankara started expanding westward in the 1970s, several modern, suburbia-style developments, mini-cities and business districts such as [Söğütözü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B6%C4%9F%C3%BCt%C3%B6z%C3%BC) began to rise along the western highway, also known as the [Eskişehir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eski%C5%9Fehir) Road. The *Armada*, *CEPA* and *Kentpark* malls on the highway, the *Galleria*, *Arcadium* and *Gordion* in Ümitköy, and a huge mall, *Real* in Bilkent Center, offer North American and European style shopping opportunities (these places can be reached through the Eskişehir Highway.) There is also the newly expanded *[ANKAmall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANKAmall)* at the outskirts, on the Istanbul Highway, which houses most of the well-known international brands. This mall is the largest throughout the Ankara region. In 2014, a few more shopping malls were open in Ankara. They are *Next Level* and *Taurus* on the Boulevard of [Mevlana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mevlana) (also known as [Konya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konya) Road).

### Museums

There are about 50 museums in the city. Some notables include;

#### Museum of Anatolian Civilizations

The [Museum of Anatolian Civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Anatolian_Civilizations) (*Anadolu Medeniyetleri Müzesi*) is situated at the entrance of the [Ankara Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Castle). It is an old 15th century [bedesten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedesten) (covered bazaar)[^cite_note-88] that has been restored and now houses a collection of [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic), [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic), [Hatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_empire), [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites), [Phrygian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygia), [Urartian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) works as well as a major section dedicated to [Lydian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia) treasures.

#### Anıtkabir



[Atatürk's Mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C4%B1tkabir) is the mausoleum of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, founder of the Republic of Turkey.

[Anıtkabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C4%B1tkabir) is located on an imposing hill, which forms the *Anıttepe* quarter of the city, where the mausoleum of [Mustafa Kemal Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk), founder of the Republic of Turkey, stands. Completed in 1953, it is a fusion of ancient and modern architectural styles. An adjacent museum houses a wax statue of Atatürk, his writings, letters and personal items, as well as an exhibition of photographs recording important moments in his life and during the establishment of the Republic. Anıtkabir is open every day, while the adjacent museum is open every day except Mondays.

#### Ankara Aviation Museum

[Ankara Aviation Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Aviation_Museum) (*Hava Kuvvetleri Müzesi Komutanlığı*) is located near the Istanbul Road in Etimesgut. The museum opened to the public in September 1998.[^cite_note-89] It is home to various missiles, avionics, aviation materials and aircraft that have served in the [Turkish Air Force](1799517.md) (e.g. combat aircraft such as the [F-86 Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-86_Sabre), [F-100 Super Sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-100_Super_Sabre), [F-102 Delta Dagger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-102_Delta_Dagger), [F-104 Starfighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-104_Starfighter), [F-5 Freedom Fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-5), [F-4 Phantom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-4_Phantom_II); and cargo planes such as the [Transall C-160](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transall_C-160).) Also a Hungarian [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-21), a Pakistani [MiG-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-19), and a Bulgarian [MiG-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-17) are on display at the museum.

#### Cermodern

Cermodern is the modern-arts museum of Ankara, inaugurated on 1 April 2010. It is situated in the renovated building of the historic [TCDD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_State_Railways) Cer Atölyeleri, formerly a workshop of the Turkish State Railways. The museum incorporates the largest exhibition hall in Turkey. The museum holds periodic exhibitions of modern and contemporary art as well as hosting other contemporary arts events.

#### Ankara Ethnography Museum



[Ethnography Museum of Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography_Museum_of_Ankara)

[Ankara Ethnography Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography_Museum_of_Ankara) (*Etnografya Müzesi*) is located opposite to the [Ankara Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Opera_House) on Talat Paşa Boulevard, in the Ulus district. There is a fine collection of folkloric items, as well as artifacts from the Seljuk and Ottoman periods. In front of the museum building, there is a [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble) and [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze) [equestrian statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrian_statue) of [Mustafa Kemal Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) (who wears a Republic era modern military uniform, with the rank [Field Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Marshal)) which was crafted in 1927[^cite_note-90] by the Italian sculptor [Pietro Canonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Canonica).

#### Mehmet Akif Literature Museum Library

The [Mehmet Akif Literature Museum Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmet_Akif_Literature_Museum_Library) is an important literary museum and archive opened in 2011 and dedicated to [Mehmet Akif Ersoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmet_Akif_Ersoy) (1873–1936), the poet of the [Turkish National Anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Anthem).

#### METU Science and Technology Museum / Archaeology Museum

Both the [METU Science and Technology Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METU_Science_and_Technology_Museum) (*ODTÜ Bilim ve Teknoloji Müzesi*) and the Archaeology Museum (*ODTÜ Arkeoloji Müzesi*) are located inside the [Middle East Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METU) campus.

#### Republic & War of Independence Museum



The [War of Independence Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Independence_Museum), used as the first Turkish Grand National Assembly building

Both the [Republic Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Museum) and [War of Independence Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Independence_Museum) are located on [Ulus Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulus_Square). They were originally the first and second Parliament building (TBMM) of the Republic of Turkey, respectively. The War of Independence was planned and directed here as recorded in various photographs and items presently on exhibition. In another display, [wax figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wax_sculpture) of former presidents of the Republic of Turkey are on exhibit.

#### State Art and Sculpture Museum



[State Art and Sculpture Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Art_and_Sculpture_Museum)

The [State Art and Sculpture Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Art_and_Sculpture_Museum) (*Resim-Heykel Müzesi*) which opened to the public in 1980[^cite_note-91] is close to the Ethnography Museum and houses a rich collection of Turkish art from the late 19th century to the present day. There are also galleries which host guest exhibitions.

#### TCDD Open Air Steam Locomotive Museum

The [TCDD Open Air Steam Locomotive Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCDD_Open_Air_Steam_Locomotive_Museum) is an [open-air museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-air_museum) which traces the history of [steam locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_locomotive).

### Art Galleries

There are two well-established art galleries in Ankara: Galeri Nev and Siyah Beyaz.

#### Galeri Nev

[Galeri Nev](https://galerinev.art/en) is a long-established art gallery based in Ankara, Turkey. Founded in 1984, it has hosted numerous exhibitions and contributed to the development of contemporary art in Turkey.[^cite_note-92]

#### Galeri Siyah Beyaz

[Galeri Siyah Beyaz](https://galerisiyahbeyaz.com/) is a well-known art gallery founded in 1984 in Ankara, Turkey. The gallery showcases modern and contemporary artworks and hosts both solo and group exhibitions.[^cite_note-93]

### Sports



[Ankara Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Arena)

As with all other cities of Turkey, football is the most popular sport in Ankara. The city currently has one [football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_team) competing in the top flight of Turkish football, [Gençlerbirliği](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen%C3%A7lerbirli%C4%9Fi_S.K.). [Ankara Keçiörengücü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Ke%C3%A7i%C3%B6reng%C3%BCc%C3%BC) currently compete in the second tier of Turkish football, the [TFF First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TFF_First_League). [Ankaragücü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKE_Ankarag%C3%BCc%C3%BC), founded in 1910, currently compete in the third tier, the [TFF Second League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TFF_Second_League). Ankaragücü are the oldest club in Ankara and are associated with Ankara's military arsenal manufacturing company [MKE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_and_Chemical_Industry_Corporation). They were the [Turkish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Cup) winners in 1972 and 1981. Gençlerbirliği, founded in 1923, are known as the *Ankara Gale* or the *Poppies* because of their colors: red and black. They were the Turkish Cup winners in 1987 and 2001. Ankara Keçiörengücü also compete in this tier. They were founded in 1987, and bear purple and white colours on their home kit. [Büyükşehir Belediye Ankaraspor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCk%C5%9Fehir_Belediye_Ankaraspor) also played in the Süper Lig until 2010, when they were expelled. The club was reconstituted in 2014 as [Osmanlıspor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmanl%C4%B1spor) but have since returned to their old identity as [Ankaraspor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankaraspor). Ankaraspor currently play in the TFF Second League at the Etimesgut Belediyesi Atatürk Stadium. Gençlerbirliği's B team, [Hacettepe S.K.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacettepe_S.K.) (formerly known as Gençlerbirliği OFTAŞ) played in the Süper Lig but folded in 2023. [Ankara Demirspor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Demirspor) and [Etimesgut Belediyespor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etimesgut_Belediyespor) also play in the TFF Second League. Ankara has a large number of minor teams, playing at regional levels, including [Çankaya FK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87ankaya_FK), Altındağspor,[^cite_note-tff-94] [Mamak FK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamak_FK), Çubukspor, and Bağlumspor.

In the [Turkish Basketball Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketbol_S%C3%BCper_Ligi), Ankara is represented by [Türk Telekom B.K.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrk_Telekom_B.K.), who play at the [Ankara Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Arena). [TED Ankara Kolejliler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TED_Ankara_Kolejliler), MKE Ankaragücü, and [OGM Ormanspor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OGM_Ormanspor) play in the second-tier [Turkish First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrkiye_Basketbol_Ligi). Türk Telekom became the fourth team in the country's history to participate in [EuroCup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroCup_Basketball) finals ([22-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_EuroCup_Basketball)).

[Halkbank Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halkbank_Ankara) is the leading domestic powerhouse in men's volleyball, having won many championships and cups in the [Turkish Men's Volleyball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Men's_Volleyball_League) and even the [CEV Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEV_Cup) in 2013.

[Ankara Buz Pateni Sarayı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_Ice_Palace) is where the [ice skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_skating) and [ice hockey](14790.md) competitions take place in the city.

There are many popular spots for [skateboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboarding) which is active in the city since the 1980s. Skaters in Ankara usually meet in the park near the [Grand National Assembly of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey).

## Parks



Botanik Park



[Göksu Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6ksu_Park)

Ankara has many parks and open spaces mainly established in the early years of the Republic and well maintained and expanded thereafter. The most important of these parks are: [Gençlik Parkı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen%C3%A7lik_Park%C4%B1) (houses an [amusement park](137327.md) with a large pond for rowing), Botanik Park, Seğmenler Park, Anayasa Park, [Kuğulu Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku%C4%9Fulu_Park) (famous for the swans received as a gift from the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRC) government), [Abdi İpekçi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdi_%C4%B0pek%C3%A7i) Park, [Esertepe Parkı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esertepe_Park%C4%B1), Güven Park (see above for the monument), Kurtuluş Park (has an ice-skating rink), [Altınpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt%C4%B1npark) (also a prominent exposition/fair area), Harikalar Diyarı (claimed to be Biggest Park of Europe inside city borders) and Göksu Park. [Dikmen Vadisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikmen_Vadisi) (Dikmen Valley) is a 70 hectares (170 acres) park and recreation area situated in Çankaya district.

Gençlik Park was depicted on the [reverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obverse_and_reverse) of the Turkish 100 [lira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_lira) banknotes of 1952–1976.[^cite_note-\:0-83]

[Atatürk Forest Farm and Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk_Forest_Farm_and_Zoo) (*Atatürk Orman Çiftliği*) is an expansive recreational farming area which houses a [zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo), several small agricultural farms, [greenhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse), restaurants, a [dairy farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_farming) and a brewery. It is a pleasant place to spend a day with family, be it for having picnics, hiking, biking or simply enjoying good food and nature. There is also an exact replica of the house where Atatürk was born in 1881, in [Thessaloniki](40471.md), Greece. Visitors to the "Çiftlik" (farm) as it is affectionately called by Ankarans, can sample such famous products of the farm such as old-fashioned beer and [ice cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cream), fresh [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product) and meat rolls/kebabs made on charcoal, at a traditional restaurant (*Merkez Lokantası*, Central Restaurant), cafés and other establishments scattered around the farm.

## Fauna

### Angora cat

Main article: [Turkish Angora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Angora)



[Angora cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Angora) with odd eyes (heterochromia), which is common among the Angoras

Ankara is home to a world-famous domestic [cat breed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_breed) – the [Turkish Angora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Angora), called *Ankara kedisi* (Ankara cat) in Turkish. Turkish Angoras are one of the ancient, naturally occurring cat breeds, having originated in Ankara and its surrounding region in central Anatolia.

They mostly have a white, silky, medium to long length coat, no undercoat and a fine bone structure. There seems to be a connection between the Angora Cats and [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_\(cat\)), and the Turkish Angora is also a distant cousin of the [Turkish Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Van). Although they are known for their shimmery white coat, there are more than twenty varieties including black, blue and reddish fur. They come in [tabby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabby_cat) and tabby-white, along with smoke varieties, and are in every color other than pointed, lavender, and cinnamon (all of which would indicate breeding to an outcross.)

Eyes may be blue, green, or amber, or even one blue and one amber or green. The [W gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_coat_genetics#White_spotting_and_epistatic_white) which is responsible for the white coat and blue eye is closely related to the hearing ability, and the presence of a blue eye can indicate that the cat is deaf to the side the blue eye is located. However, a great many blue and [odd-eyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd-eyed_cat) white cats have normal hearing, and even deaf cats lead a very normal life if kept indoors.

Ears are pointed and large, eyes are almond shaped and the head is massive with a two plane profile. Another characteristic is the tail, which is often kept parallel to the back.

### Angora goat

Main article: [Angora goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_goat)



Angora goat

The [Angora goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_goat) ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Ankara keçisi*) is a breed of [domestic goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat) that originated in Ankara and its surrounding region in central Anatolia.[^cite_note-Ekarius-97]

This breed was first mentioned in the time of [Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses), roughly in 1500 BC.[^cite_note-98] The first Angora goats were brought to Europe by [Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), about 1554, but, like later imports, were not very successful. Angora goats were first introduced in the United States in 1849 by James P. Davis. Seven adult goats were a gift from Sultan [Abdülmecid I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd%C3%BClmecid_I) in appreciation for his services and advice on the raising of cotton.

The [fleece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pile_\(textile\)) taken from an Angora goat is called [mohair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohair). A single goat produces between five and eight kilograms (11 and 18 [pounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_\(mass\))) of hair per year. Angoras are shorn twice a year, unlike [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_sheep), which are shorn only once. Angoras have high nutritional requirements due to their rapid hair growth. A poor quality diet will curtail mohair development. The United States, [Turkey](11125639.md), and South Africa are the top producers of mohair.

For a long period of time, Angora goats were bred for their white coat. In 1998, the Colored Angora Goat Breeders Association was set up to promote breeding of colored Angoras. Today, Angora goats produce white, black (deep black to greys and silver), red (the color fades significantly as the goat gets older), and brownish fiber.

Angora goats were depicted on the [reverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obverse_and_reverse) of the Turkish 50 [lira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_lira) banknotes of 1938–1952.[^cite_note-\:0-83]

### Angora rabbit

Main article: [Angora rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_rabbit)



Angora rabbit

The [Angora rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_rabbit) ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Ankara tavşanı*) is a variety of domestic rabbit bred for its long, soft hair. The Angora is one of the oldest types of domestic rabbit, originating in Ankara and its surrounding region in central Anatolia, along with the [Angora cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Angora) and [Angora goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_goat). The rabbits were popular pets with French royalty in the mid-18th century, and spread to other parts of Europe by the end of the century. They first appeared in the United States in the early 20th century. They are bred largely for their long [Angora wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_wool), which may be removed by [shearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep_shearing), combing, or [plucking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plucking_\(hair_removal\)) (gently pulling loose wool).

Angoras are bred mainly for their wool because it is silky and soft. They have a humorous appearance, as they resemble a fur ball. Most are calm and docile but should be handled carefully. Grooming is necessary to prevent the fiber from matting and felting on the rabbit. A condition called "wool block" is common in Angora rabbits and should be treated quickly.[^cite_note-99] Sometimes they are shorn in the summer as the long fur can cause the rabbits to overheat.
