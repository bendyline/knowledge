**Mallorca**,[^cite_note-2] also spelled **Majorca** in English,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] is the largest of [Spain](26667.md)'s [Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_Islands), and the [seventh largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_in_the_Mediterranean#By_area) in the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md).

Its capital, [Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca), is also the capital of the [autonomous community](55002.md) of the Balearic Islands. The Balearic Islands have been an autonomous region of Spain since 1983.[^cite_note-MALRCA3-6] Two smaller islands lie just off the coast of Mallorca: [Cabrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabrera,_Balearic_Islands) (southeast of Palma) and [Dragonera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonera) (west of Palma). The island's [anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthem) is "[La Balanguera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Balanguera)".

Along with other Balearic Islands, [Menorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menorca), [Ibiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza), and [Formentera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formentera), Mallorca is a highly popular holiday destination, particularly for tourists from the [Netherlands](21148.md), [Ireland](14560.md), [Germany](11867.md), and the [United Kingdom](31717.md). The international airport, [Palma de Mallorca Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca_Airport), is one of the busiest in Spain; it was used by 28 million passengers in 2017, with use increasing every year between 2012 and 2017.[^cite_note-7]

## Etymology

The name originates from Classical Latin *insula maior*, meaning "larger island". In Medieval Latin, this evolved into *Maiorca*, referring to "the larger one" in contrast with *Menorca*, "the smaller one". The term *Maiorca* was later modified by central Catalan scribes through a process of [hypercorrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercorrection), resulting in the form *Mallorca*, which eventually became the standard spelling.[^cite_note-8]

## History

### Prehistoric settlements



Example of prehistoric talaiot in Mallorca



Archeological evidence indicates the presence of the *porc negre* (black pig) in pre-Roman settlements.

[^cite_note-9]

The Balearic Islands were first colonised by humans during the [3rd millennium BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_millennium_BC), around 2500–2300 BC from the Iberian Peninsula or southern France, by people associated with the [Bell Beaker culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Beaker_culture).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The arrival of humans resulted in the rapid extinction of the three species of terrestrial mammals native to Mallorca, the dwarf goat-antelope [*Myotragus balearicus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myotragus), the giant dormouse *[Hypnomys morpheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnomys_morpheus),* and the shrew *[Nesiotites hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesiotites_hidalgo)*, all three of which had been continuously present on Mallorca for over 5 million years.[^cite_note-12] The island's prehistoric settlements are called *talaiots* or *talayots*. The people of the islands raised Bronze Age megaliths as part of their [Talaiotic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talaiotic_culture).[^cite_note-MALRCA1-13] A non-exhaustive list of settlements is the following:

- [Capocorb Vell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capocorb_Vell) ([Llucmajor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llucmajor) municipality)

- Necròpoli de Son Real (east of Can Picafort, [Santa Margalida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Margalida) municipality)

- Novetiforme Alemany ([Magaluffa](4603840.md), [Calvià, Miconio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvi%C3%A0))

- Poblat Talaiòtic de S'Illot ([S'Illot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S'illot_\(Mallorca\)), [Sant Llorenç des Cardassar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Lloren%C3%A7_des_Cardassar) municipality)

- Poblat Talaiòtic de Son Fornés ([Montuïri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montu%C3%AFri) municipality)

- Sa Canova de Morell (road to [Colònia de Sant Pere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col%C3%B2nia_de_Sant_Pere), [Artà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art%C3%A0) municipality)

- [Ses Païsses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ses_Pa%C3%AFsses) ([Artà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art%C3%A0) municipality)

### Phoenicians, Romans, and Late Antiquity



Ruins of the Roman city of [Pollentia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alc%C3%BAdia)

The [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians), a seafaring people from the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant), arrived around the eighth century BC and established numerous colonies.[^cite_note-14] The island eventually came under the control of [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) in North Africa, which had become the principal Phoenician city. After the [Second Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Punic_War), Carthage lost all of its overseas possessions and the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) took over.[^cite_note-15]

The island was occupied by the Romans in 123 BC under [Quintus Caecilius Metellus Balearicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintus_Caecilius_Metellus_Balearicus). It flourished under Roman rule, during which time the towns of Pollentia ([Alcúdia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alc%C3%BAdia)), and Palmaria ([Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca)) were founded. In addition, the northern town of [Bocchoris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocchoris_\(City\)), dating back to pre-Roman times, was a federated city to [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome).[^cite_note-16] The local economy was largely driven by [olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) cultivation, [viticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viticulture), and [salt mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_mining). Mallorcan soldiers were valued within the Roman legions for their skill with the [sling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sling_\(weapon\)) ([Balearic slingers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_slingers)).[^cite_note-17]

In 427, [Gunderic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunderic) and the [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals) captured the island. [Geiseric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geiseric), son of Godigisel, governed Mallorca and used it as his base to loot and plunder settlements around the Mediterranean[^cite_note-The_Dark_Ages_in_Mallorca-18] until Roman rule was restored in 465.

### Middle Ages

#### Late Antiquity and Early Middle Ages

In 534, Mallorca was recaptured from the [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals) by the [Eastern Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), led by [Apollinarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollinarius_\(governor\)). Under Roman rule, [Christianity](5211.md) thrived and numerous churches were built.

From 707, the island was increasingly attacked by [Muslim](6037917.md) raiders from North Africa. Recurrent invasions led the islanders to ask [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) for help.[^cite_note-The_Dark_Ages_in_Mallorca-18]

#### Islamic Mallorca



Arab Baths in [Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca)

In 902, Issam al-Khawlani<sup>([es](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issam%20al-Khawlan%C3%AD))([ca](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issam%20al-Khawlan%C3%AD))</sup> ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عصام الخولاني) [conquered the Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Majorca), and they became part of the [Emirate of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba). The town of Palma was reshaped and expanded, and became known as Medina Mayurqa. Later on, with the [Caliphate of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba) at its height, the [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) improved agriculture with [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) and developed local industries.

The caliphate was dismembered in 1015. Mallorca came under rule by the [Taifa of Dénia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_D%C3%A9nia), and from 1087 to 1114, was an [independent Taifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Mallorca). During that period, the island was visited by [Ibn Hazm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Hazm). However, [an expedition of Pisans and Catalans in 1114–15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1113%E2%80%931115_Balearic_Islands_expedition), led by [Ramon Berenguer III, Count of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Berenguer_III,_Count_of_Barcelona), overran the island, laying siege to Palma for eight months. After the city fell, the invaders retreated due to problems in their own lands. They were replaced by the [Almoravides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravides) from North Africa, who ruled until 1176. The Almoravides were replaced by the [Almohad dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_dynasty) until 1229. [Abu Yahya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Yahya_Muhammad_ibn_Ali_ibn_Abi_Imran_al-Tinmalali) was the last Moorish leader of Mallorca.[^cite_note-19]

#### Medieval Mallorca

Main article: [Conquest of Majorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Majorca)

In the ensuing confusion and unrest, King [James I of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon), also known as James the Conqueror, launched an [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Majorca) which landed at [Santa Ponça](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ponsa), Mallorca, on 8–9 September 1229 with Catalan forces consisting of 15,000 men and 1,500 horses. His forces entered the city of Medina Mayurqa on 31 December 1229. In 1230, he annexed the island to his [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) under the name *[Regnum Maioricae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Majorca)*.

### Modern era



A 1683 map of Mallorca, by [Vicente Mut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Mut_Armengol)

From 1479, the Crown of Aragon was in [dynastic union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynastic_union) with that of [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile). The [Barbary corsairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_corsairs) of North Africa often attacked the Balearic Islands, and in response, the people built coastal [watchtowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchtower) and fortified churches. In 1570, King [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) and his advisors were considering complete evacuation of the Balearic islands.[^cite_note-20]

In the early 18th century, the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession) resulted in the replacement of that dynastic union with a unified Spanish monarchy under the rule of the new [Bourbon Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_Dynasty). The last episode of the War of Spanish Succession was the conquest of the island of Mallorca. It took place on 2 July 1715 when the island capitulated to the arrival of a Bourbon fleet. In 1716, the [Nueva Planta decrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Planta_decrees) made Mallorca part of the [Spanish province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Spain) of Baleares, roughly the same to present-day [Illes Balears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illes_Balears) province and autonomous community.

### 20th century and today

A [Nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_under_Franco) stronghold at the start of the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), Mallorca was subjected to an [amphibious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) landing, on 16 August 1936, aimed at driving the Nationalists from Mallorca and reclaiming the island for the [Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic). Although the Republicans heavily outnumbered their opponents and managed to push 12 km (7.5 mi) inland, superior Nationalist air power, provided mainly by [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy#Fascist_regime_\(1922%E2%80%931943\)) as part of the [Italian occupation of Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_occupation_of_Mallorca), forced the Republicans to retreat and to leave the island completely by 12 September. Those events became known as the [Battle of Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mallorca).[^cite_note-21]

Since the 1950s, the advent of mass [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) has transformed the island into a destination for foreign visitors and attracted many service workers from mainland Spain. The boom in tourism caused Palma to grow significantly.

In the 21st century, urban redevelopment, under the so‑called *Pla Mirall* (English "Mirror Plan"), attracted groups of [immigrant workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant_worker) from outside the [European Union](9317.md), especially from Africa and South America.[^cite_note-22]

#### Archaeology

In September 2019, A 3,200-year-old well-preserved [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) [sword](27863.md) was discovered by archaeologists under the leadership of Jaume Deya and Pablo Galera on the Mallorca Island in the [Puigpunyent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puigpunyent) from the stone megaliths site called [Talaiot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talaiot).[^cite_note-23] Specialists assumed that the weapon was made when the [Talaiotic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talaiotic_culture) was in critical decline. The sword will be on display at the nearby Mallorca Museum.[^cite_note-24]

### Palma

Main article: [Palma de Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca)

The capital of Mallorca, Palma, was founded as a Roman camp called Palmaria upon the remains of a [Talaiotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talaiot) settlement. The turbulent history of the city had it subject to several Vandal sackings during the [fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire). It was later reconquered by the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), established by the Moors (who called it Medina Mayurqa), and finally occupied by [James I of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon). In 1983, Palma became the capital of the [autonomous region](55002.md) of the [Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_Islands). Palma has a famous tourist attraction, the cathedral, Catedral-Basílica de Santa María de Mallorca, standing in the heart of the City looking out over the sea.[^cite_note-25]

## Climate

Mallorca has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): [*Csa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate)), with mild and relatively wet winters and hot, bright, dry summers. Precipitation in the [Serra de Tramuntana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Tramuntana) is markedly higher. Summers are hot in the plains, and winters are mild, getting colder and wetter in the Tramuntana range, where brief episodes of snow during the winter are not unusual, especially in the [Puig Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puig_Major). The two wettest months in Mallorca are October and November. Storms and heavy rain are not uncommon during the autumn.[^cite_note-26]

## Geography



Satellite image

### Geology

Mallorca and the other Balearic Islands are geologically an extension of the fold mountains of the [Betic Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betic_Cordillera) of [Andalusia](2736.md). They consist primarily of sediments deposited in the [Tethys Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tethys_Sea) during the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) era. These marine deposits have given rise to calcareous rocks which are often [fossiliferous](10958.md). The folding of the Betic Cordillera and Mallorcan ranges resulted from [subduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction) of the [African Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Plate) beneath the [Eurasian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate) with eventual collision.[^cite_note-33] Tectonic movements led to different elevation and lowering zones in the late [Tertiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_\(period\)), which is why the connection to the mainland has been severed at the current sea level.

The limestones, which predominate throughout Mallorca, are readily water-soluble, and have given rise to extensive areas of [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst). In addition to limestone, [dolomitic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomite_\(rock\)) are mainly present in the mountainous regions of Mallorca; the Serra de Tramuntana and the Serres de Llevant. The Serres de Llevant also contain [marl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marl), the more rapid erosion of which has resulted in the lower elevations of the island's southeastern mountains. Marl is limestone with a high proportion of [clay minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_minerals). The eroded material was washed into the sea or deposited in the interior of the island of the Pla de Mallorca, bright marls in the north-east of the island and ferrous clays in the middle of Mallorca, which gives the soil its characteristic reddish colour.[^cite_note-34]

### Mountains of Mallorca

Mallorca features a landscape characterised by a series of mountain ranges. The highest peak, [Puig Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puig_Major), stands at approximately 1,445 meters (4,741 feet) above sea level.[^cite_note-35] Other notable peaks include [Puig de Massanella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puig_de_Massanella), [Puig Tomir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puig_Tomir), [Puig de l'Ofre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puig_de_l'Ofre?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Puig des Teix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puig_des_Teix?action=edit\&redlink=1), all exceeding 1,000 meters (3,280 feet) in elevation.[^cite_note-36] These mountains are part of the [Serra de Tramuntana range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Tramuntana) with numerous peaks over 1,000 meters, offering opportunities for hiking and exploration with views of the [Mediterranean](19006.md). While not towering in comparison to some mountain ranges globally, the Mallorcan mountains provide visitors with diverse outdoor experiences and panoramic views of the island's rugged terrain and coastline.

### Regions



Topography

Mallorca is the largest island of Spain by area and second most populated (after [Tenerife](269223.md) in the [Canary Islands](5717.md)).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Mallorca has two mountainous regions, the [Serra de Tramuntana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Tramuntana) and Serres de Llevant. Both are about 70 km (43 mi) in length and occupy the northwestern and eastern parts of the island respectively.

The highest peak in Mallorca is [Puig Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puig_Major), at 1,445 m (4,741 ft), in the Serra de Tramuntana.[^cite_note-MALRCA-39] As this is a military zone, the neighbouring peak at [Puig de Massanella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puig_de_Massanella) is the highest accessible peak at 1,364 m (4,475 ft). The northeast coast comprises two bays: the Badia de Pollença and the larger Badia d'Alcúdia.

The northern coast is rugged and has many cliffs. The central zone, extending from Palma, is a generally flat, fertile plain known as *Es Pla*. The island has a variety of [caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave) both above and below the sea – two of the caves, the [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level) [Coves dels Hams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coves_dels_Hams) and the [Coves del Drach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coves_del_Drach), also contain underground lakes and are open to tours. Both are located near the eastern coastal town of [Porto Cristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Cristo). Small uninhabited islands lie off the southern and western coasts; the [Cabrera Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabrera_Archipelago) is administratively grouped with Mallorca (in the municipality of Palma), while [Dragonara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonara) is administratively included in the municipality of Andratx. Other notable areas include the [Alfabia Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfabia_Mountains), [Es Cornadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Els_Cornadors) and [Cap de Formentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_de_Formentor). The [Cap de Formentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_de_Formentor) is one of the places where the tourists can enjoy the pleasure of its beach which is golden and very thin.[^cite_note-40]

### World Heritage Site

The Cultural Landscape of the [Serra de Tramuntana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Tramuntana) was registered as a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 2011.[^cite_note-41]

## Population

Mallorca is the most populous island in the Balearic Islands and the second most populous island in Spain, after [Tenerife](269223.md),[^cite_note-popdataboe-42] in the [Canary Islands](5717.md), being also the fourth most populous island in the Mediterranean after [Sicily](27619.md), [Sardinia](29376.md) and [Cyprus](5593.md).[^cite_note-43] It had a Census population of 920,605 inhabitants at the start of 2021,[^cite_note-Estadística,_Madrid_2021-44] and an official estimate of 940,332 at the start of 2023.[^cite_note-Estadística,_Madrid_2023-1]

## Economy



The beaches in the southeast of Mallorca are popular tourist attractions.



The main base of the economy of Mallorca is tourism. [Escorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escorca) ([Serra de Tramuntana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_de_Tramuntana)).

Since the 1950s, Mallorca has become a major tourist destination, and the tourism business has become the main source of revenue for the island.[^cite_note-45]

The island's popularity as a tourist destination has steadily grown since the 1950s, with many artists and academics choosing to visit and live on the island. The number of visitors to Mallorca continued to increase with holiday makers in the 1970s approaching 3 million a year. In 2010 over 6 million visitors came to Mallorca. In 2013, Mallorca was visited by nearly 9.5 million tourists, and the [Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_Islands) as a whole reached 13 million tourists.[^cite_note-46] In 2017, ten million tourists visited the island.[^cite_note-47] The rapid growth of the tourism industry has led to some locals protesting the effects of mass tourism on the island.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

Mallorca has been jokingly referred to as the 17th Federal State of Germany, due to the high number of German tourists,[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] although people from the island reject this label and deem it "an insult".[^cite_note-53]

Mallorca has recently also become a business hub economy of its own, due to the high number of foreign enterprises having either relocated to the island, or expanded their presence there. A significant number of expats settling in Mallorca has also contributed to the island's business economy.&#x20;

Attempts to [build illegally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_building) caused a scandal in 2006 in Port [Andratx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andratx) that the newspaper *[El País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pa%C3%ADs)* named "caso Andratx".[^cite_note-elpais06-54] A main reason for illegal building permits, corruption and [black market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market) construction is that communities have few ways to finance themselves other than through permits.[^cite_note-wdr-55] The former mayor was incarcerated in 2009 after being prosecuted for taking bribes to permit illegal house building.[^cite_note-elpais09-56][^cite_note-57]

## Politics and government



Emblem of the Mallorca Insular Council

### Regional government

See also: [Government of Marga Prohens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Marga_Prohens)

The Balearic Islands, of which Mallorca forms part, are one of the [autonomous communities of Spain](55002.md). [As a whole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_Islands), they are currently governed by the [People's Party of the Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_of_the_Balearic_Islands) (PP), with [Marga Prohens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marga_Prohens) as their President.[^cite_note-59]

### Insular government

The specific government institution for the island is the [Insular Council of Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_Council_of_Mallorca?action=edit\&redlink=1) commonly known as *Council of Mallorca*, created in 1978.[^cite_note-60]

It is responsible for culture, roads, railways (see [Serveis Ferroviaris de Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serveis_Ferroviaris_de_Mallorca)) and municipal administration. As of September 2023, [Llorenç Galmés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloren%C3%A7_Galm%C3%A9s?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([PP](404727.md)) serves as president of the Insular Council.[^cite_note-61]

#### Results of the elections to the Council of Mallorca

Elections are held every four years concurrently with [local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Spain#Local_elections). From 1983 to 2007, councilors were indirectly elected from the results of the election to [Parliament of the Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_Balearic_Islands) for the constituency of [Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallorca_\(Parliament_of_the_Balearic_Islands_constituency\)). Since 2007, however, separate direct elections are held to elect the Council.

## Culture

### Archduke Ludwig Salvator of Austria



A sculpture of Ludwig Salvator in Mallorca

[Archduke Ludwig Salvator of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduke_Ludwig_Salvator_of_Austria) ([Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language): *Arxiduc Lluís Salvador*) was a pioneer of tourism in the Balearic Islands. He first arrived on the island in 1867, travelling under his title "Count of Neuendorf". He later settled in Mallorca, buying up wild areas of land in order to preserve and enjoy them. Nowadays, a number of hiking routes are named after him.[^cite_note-63]

Ludwig Salvator loved the island of Mallorca. He became fluent in Catalan, carried out research into the island's flora and fauna, history, and culture to produce his main work, *Die Balearen*, a comprehensive collection of books about the Balearic Islands, consisting of 7 volumes. It took him 22 years to complete.[^cite_note-64]

Nowadays, several streets or buildings on the island are named after him (i.e., *Arxiduc Lluís Salvador*).

### Chopin in Mallorca



Chopin's piano in [Valldemossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valldemossa), Mallorca

The Polish composer and pianist [Frédéric Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Chopin), together with French writer Amantine Lucile Aurore Dupin (pseudonym: [George Sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Sand)), resided in [Valldemossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valldemossa) in the winter of 1838–39. Apparently, Chopin's health had already deteriorated and his doctor recommended that he go to the Balearic Islands to recuperate, where he still spent a rather miserable winter.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

Nonetheless, his time in Mallorca was a productive period for Chopin. He managed to finish the [Preludes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preludes_\(Chopin\)), Op. 28, that he started writing in 1835. He was also able to undertake work on his [Ballade No. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballade_No._2_\(Chopin\)), Op. 38; two Polonaises, Op. 40; and the [Scherzo No. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scherzo_No._3_\(Chopin\)), Op. 39.[^cite_note-67]

### Literature

French writer Amantine Lucile Aurore Dupin (pseudonym: [George Sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Sand)), at that time in a relationship with [Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopin), described her stay in Mallorca in *[A Winter in Majorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Winter_in_Majorca)*, published in 1855. Other famous writers used Mallorca as the setting for their works. While on the island, the Nicaraguan poet [Rubén Darío](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Dar%C3%ADo) started writing the novel *El oro de Mallorca*, and wrote several poems, such as *La isla de oro*.[^cite_note-cervantes-68]

The poet [Miquel Costa i Llobera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miquel_Costa_i_Llobera) wrote in 1875 his famous ode, [the Pine of Formentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pine_of_Formentor), as well as other poems concerning old Mallorcan traditions and fantasies. Many of the works of [Baltasar Porcel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltasar_Porcel) take place in Mallorca.



&#x20;House of poet [Miquel Costa i Llobera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miquel_Costa_i_Llobera)

[Agatha Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agatha_Christie) visited the island in the early 20th century and stayed in Palma and Port de Pollença.[^cite_note-69] She would later write the book *[Problem at Pollensa Bay and Other Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_at_Pollensa_Bay_and_Other_Stories)*, a collection of short stories, of which the first one takes place in [Port de Pollença](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_de_Pollen%C3%A7a), starring [Parker Pyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Pyne).

[Jorge Luis Borges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Luis_Borges) visited Mallorca twice, accompanied by his family.[^cite_note-70] He published his poems *La estrella* (1920) and *Catedral* (1921) in the regional magazine *Baleares*.[^cite_note-71] The latter poem shows his admiration for the monumental [Cathedral of Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_Cathedral).[^cite_note-72]

[Nobel Prize](21201.md) winner [Camilo José Cela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilo_Jos%C3%A9_Cela) came to Mallorca in 1954, visiting [Pollença](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollen%C3%A7a), and then moving to [Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca), where he settled permanently.[^cite_note-73] In 1956, Cela founded the magazine *Papeles de Son Armadans*.[^cite_note-74] He is also credited as founder of [Alfaguara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfaguara).



[Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Graves)' grave

The English writer and poet [Robert Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Graves) moved to Mallorca with his family in 1946. The house is now a museum. He died in 1985 and was buried in the small churchyard on a hill at [Deià](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dei%C3%A0).[^cite_note-75]

[Ira Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_Levin) set part of his dystopian novel *[This Perfect Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Perfect_Day)* in Mallorca, making the island a centre of resistance in a world otherwise dominated by a computer.

### Music and dance

The [Ball dels Cossiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_dels_Cossiers) is the island's traditional dance. It is believed to have been imported from Catalonia in the 13th or 14th century, after the [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) conquest of the island under King [James I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon).[^cite_note-76] In the dance, three pairs of dancers, who are typically male, defend a "Lady," who is played by a man or a woman, from a [demon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demon) or [devil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil). Another Mallorcan dance is [Correfoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correfoc), an elaborate festival of dance and pyrotechnics that is also of Catalan origin. The island's folk music strongly resembles that of [Catalonia](6822.md), and is centered around traditional instruments like the [xeremies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xeremies?action=edit\&redlink=1) (bagpipe) and [guitarra de canya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitarra_de_canya) (a reed or bone [xylophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylophone)-like instrument suspended from the neck).[^cite_note-MyGuide-77] While folk music is still played and enjoyed by many on the island, a number of other musical traditions have become popular in Mallorca in the 21st century, including [electronic dance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music), classical music, and [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz), all of which have annual festivals on the island.[^cite_note-78]

### Art

[Joan Miró](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mir%C3%B3), a Spanish painter, sculptor, and ceramicist, had close ties to the island throughout his life. He married Pilar Juncosa in Palma in 1929 and settled permanently in Mallorca in 1954.[^cite_note-79] The [Fundació Pilar i Joan Miró in Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundaci%C3%B3_Pilar_i_Joan_Mir%C3%B3_in_Mallorca) has a collection of his works. [Es Baluard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Es_Baluard) in Palma is a museum of modern and contemporary art which exhibits the work of Balearic artists and artists related to the Balearic Islands.

### Film

The [Evolution Mallorca International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_Mallorca_International_Film_Festival) is the fastest growing Mediterranean film festival and has taken place annually every November since 2011, attracting filmmakers, producers, and directors globally. It is hosted at the Teatro Principal in Palma de Mallorca.[^cite_note-80]

### Mallorcan cartographic school

Main article: [Majorcan cartographic school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorcan_cartographic_school)



Map of Mallorca and Menorca by the [Ottoman](22278.md) admiral [Piri Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piri_Reis)

Mallorca has a long history of seafaring. The [Majorcan cartographic school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorcan_cartographic_school) or the "[Catalan](6822.md) school" refers to a collection of [cartographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartographer), [cosmographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmographer), and [navigational instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigational_instrument) makers who flourished in Mallorca and partly in mainland [Catalonia](6822.md) in the 13th, 14th, and 15th centuries. Mallorcan cosmographers and cartographers developed breakthroughs in cartographic techniques, namely the "normal [portolan chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portolan_chart)", which was fine-tuned for navigational use and the plotting by compass of navigational routes, prerequisites for the discovery of the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World).

### Cuisine



[Ensaïmades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensa%C3%AFmada), a type of Mallorcan pastry

In 2005, there were over 2,400 restaurants on the island of Mallorca according to the Mallorcan Tourist Board, ranging from small bars to full restaurants. Olives and almonds are typical of the Mallorcan diet. Among the foods that are typical from Mallorca are *[sobrassada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobrassada)*, *arròs brut* (saffron rice cooked with chicken, pork and vegetables), and the sweet pastry *[ensaïmada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensa%C3%AFmada)*. Also Pa amb oli is a popular dish.[^cite_note-81] [Majorcan soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorcan_soup) a common rustic food which is often closer in texture to a casserole or very thick stew,[^cite_note-\:3-82][^cite_note-\:0-83] and is typically is eaten with a fork rather than a spoon.[^cite_note-84]

[Herbs de Majorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbs_de_Majorca) is a herbal liqueur.

## Language

The two official languages of Mallorca are [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language) and [Spanish](26825.md),[^cite_note-web.parlamentib.es-85] a dialect of the former being the [indigenous language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_language) of Mallorca.[^cite_note-86] The local [dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect) of Catalan spoken in the island is *[Mallorquí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_dialect)*, with slightly different variants in most villages. Education is bilingual in Catalan and Spanish, with some teaching of English.[^cite_note-87]

In 2012, the then-governing [People's Party](404727.md) announced its intention to end preferential treatment for Catalan in the island's schools to bring parity to the two languages of the island. It was said that this could lead Mallorcan Catalan to become extinct in the fairly near future, as it was being used in a situation of [diglossia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diglossia) in favour of the Spanish language.[^cite_note-88] However, following a [May 2015 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_parliamentary_election,_2015) that swept a pro-Catalan party into power, this policy was dropped.[^cite_note-89]

## Notable Mallorcans



*Ars magna*, by Ramon Llull

Some of the earliest famous Mallorcans lived on the island before its reconquest from the Moors. Famous Mallorcans include:

- [Ramon Llull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Llull), a medieval friar, writer and philosopher, who wrote the first major work of [Catalan Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_Literature);

- [Al-Humaydī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Azdi_al-Humaydi), Moorish historian, born on the island in 1029.

- [Abraham Cresques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Cresques), a 14th-century Jewish [cartographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography) of the [Majorcan cartographic school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorcan_cartographic_school) from Palma, believed to be the author of the [Catalan Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_Atlas);

- [Catalina Tomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalina_Tomas), 16th-century [canoness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoness) and [mystic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism), one of the [patron saints](68055.md) of the island

- [Junípero Serra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun%C3%ADpero_Serra), the [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) friar who founded the mission chain in [Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California) in 1769.

- [Miquel Costa i Llobera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miquel_Costa_i_Llobera), a famous Mallorcan poet, who wrote [The Pine of Formentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pine_of_Formentor).

- [Joaquín Jovellar y Soler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaqu%C3%ADn_Jovellar_y_Soler), 19th century military commander.

- [Antonio Maura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Maura), two-time Spanish Prime Minister during the reign of King [Alfonso XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_XIII_of_Spain).

- [Robert Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Graves), English writer and poet who lived for many years in Mallorca, buried in a small churchyard on a hill at [Deià](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dei%C3%A0)

- [Joan Daurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Daurer), painter active between 1358-1374.
