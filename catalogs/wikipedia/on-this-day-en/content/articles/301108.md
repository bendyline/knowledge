The **Pan-American Highway** is a vast network of roads that stretches about 30,000 kilometers (19,000 mi) from [Prudhoe Bay, Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudhoe_Bay,_Alaska), United States, in the northernmost part of [North America](21139.md), to [Ushuaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushuaia), Argentina, at the southern tip of [South America](26769.md). The highway connects 14 countries: [Canada](5042916.md), the [United States](3434750.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), [Guatemala](17238567.md), [El Salvador](9356.md), [Honduras](13394.md), [Nicaragua](21362.md), [Costa Rica](5551.md), [Panama](22997.md), [Colombia](5222.md), [Ecuador](9334.md), [Peru](170691.md), [Chile](5489.md), and [Argentina](18951905.md).

No road in the U.S. (except Alaska) or Canada is officially designated as part of the Pan-American Highway, which officially begins at the Mexico–United States border in [Nuevo Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevo_Laredo) and runs south.

The Pan-American Highway is interrupted at the [Darién Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Gap), a roadless, dense rainforest area between Panama and Colombia. No road traverses the Gap, and no car ferries have operated in the area for decades; drivers often opt to send their car by cargo ship from one country to the other. This means North and South America are separated by road.

## Concept of the highway

The highway was built in stages. The first, not long after one could drive across the United States on a paved road, was the highway from [Laredo, Texas](136773.md), to Mexico City. The second stage was the Inter-American Highway to [Panama City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_City); previously there were no roads, and little commerce between most Central American countries. There was no road between Costa Rica and Panama until, concerned about access to the [Panama Canal](24849.md) in a war situation, the [U.S. Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Corps_of_Engineers) began a highway in 1941.[^cite_note-1]

The third stage, which has not been completed and may never be, continues onward to the southern tip of South America at [Tierra del Fuego National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego_National_Park), near [Ushuaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushuaia), Argentina. Both Panama and Colombia, as well as environmentalists, are opposed to building a highway through the [Darién Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Gap) that separates the two continents.

A Cuban proposal that was not carried out was to include a "circuito del Caribe" (Caribbean circuit). This would have expanded the highway to [Puerto Juárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ju%C3%A1rez), Mexico ([Cancún](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canc%C3%BAn)), and from there by ferry to [Pinar del Río, Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinar_del_R%C3%ADo,_Cuba), from there by road to Havana, and by ferry again to [Key West, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_West,_Florida), and the [Overseas Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Highway).[^cite_note-2] The deterioration of relations between Cuba and the U.S. after the [Cuban Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Revolution) of 1959 ended talk of this project.

## Development and construction



1946 map of the Pan-American Highway (outside the United States)

The concept of an overland route from one tip of the Americas to the other was originally proposed as a railroad. In 1884 the U.S. Congress passed a law with a plan to build an inter-American rail system.[^cite_note-Hot-3] This was discussed at the [First Pan-American Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_International_Conference_of_American_States) in 1889; however, construction never started. It was abandoned in concept after the independence of Panama in 1903, when work on the canal began.[^cite_note-Hot-3]

The concept of building a highway, rather than a railroad, emerged at the Fifth [International Conference of American States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-American_Conference) in 1923, after the automobile and other vehicles had begun to replace railroads for both passenger and goods transportation. The first conference regarding the construction of the highway opened in Buenos Aires on October 3, 1925.[^cite_note-4]

Finally, on July 29, 1937, in the latter years of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, Peru, Canada, and the United States signed the *Convention on the Pan-American Highway*, whereby they agreed to achieve speedy construction, by all adequate means.[^cite_note-5] Thirteen years later, in 1950, Mexico became the first Latin American country to complete its portion of the highway.[^cite_note-Silverstein-6]

No single route in the United States (except in Alaska) has been designated, much less marked, as the U.S. portion of the Pan-American Highway. However, [I-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_25) is labeled as the Pan-American freeway in states such as New Mexico and Colorado. According to the federal Department of Transportation, the [Interstate Highway System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System) is the United States' section of the highway. In Canada the highway is not marked. Much of the highway in Latin America is marked as *Vía Panam* or *Vía Panamericana*.

## Northern section

### Alaska

The [Alaska Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Highway) through Alaska, [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon) and [British Columbia](3392.md) is commonly considered a *[de facto](43594.md)* northerly extension of the Pan-American Highway, which continues further north with the [Dalton Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_Highway) in Alaska. With this route, the Pan-American Highway begins in [Prudhoe Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudhoe_Bay,_Alaska), near [Deadhorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadhorse,_Alaska). Traveling south, the route follows the length of the [Dalton Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_Highway) (Alaska Route 11) changing to [Alaska Route 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Route_2), the Alaskan portion of the Alaska Highway, near [Fairbanks, Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairbanks,_Alaska). From Fairbanks, the route follows Alaska Route 2 southeast to the [Canada–United States border](630260.md) southeast of [Northway, Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northway,_Alaska), and adjacent to the [Tetlin National Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetlin_National_Wildlife_Refuge).

### Canada

In Canada, no particular road has been officially designated as the Pan-American Highway. The [National Highway System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_System_\(Canada\)), which includes but is not limited to the [Trans-Canada Highway](70775.md), is the country's only official inter-provincial highway system. However, several Canadian highways are a natural extension of several key American highways that reach the Canada–US border. [British Columbia Highway 97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Highway_97) and Highway 2 to Alberta both pick up where the southern end of the Alaska highway leaves off. Highway 97 becomes [U.S. Route 97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_97) at the Canada–US border. [British Columbia Highway 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Highway_99) provides an alternate route from Highway 97 just north of [Cache Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cache_Creek,_British_Columbia); it runs through [Whistler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistler,_British_Columbia) and [Vancouver](32706.md) before ending at the Canada–US border at the north end of Interstate 5 in [Washington state](13015878.md), the beginning of the official Pan-American route south of British Columbia. Meanwhile, [Alberta Highway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Highway_2) runs south and east to [Alberta Highway 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Highway_3) leading into [Lethbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethbridge), then south on [Alberta Highway 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Highway_4) to the Canada–US border, where it becomes [Interstate 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_15) in [Montana](19978.md). This is the first official stretch of the Pan-American Highway south of the Alberta route, both of which are also part of the [CANAMEX Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CANAMEX_Corridor).

#### Yukon

Crossing the border into Canada, Alaska Highway 2 turns into Yukon Highway 1. The first significant settlement along the way is [Beaver Creek, Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_Creek,_Yukon). At [Haines Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haines_Junction), where it meets [Yukon Highway 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haines_Highway), Yukon Highway 1 turns east toward [Whitehorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehorse,_Yukon), the capital of the [Yukon Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon).[^cite_note-8]

Through most of Whitehorse, [Yukon Highway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klondike_Highway) and Yukon Highway 1 share an alignment. Yukon Highway 1 cuts southeast toward [Marsh Lake, Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh_Lake,_Yukon) while Yukon Highway 2 cuts south to [Skagway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skagway,_Alaska), Alaska. Eventually, Yukon Highway 1 intersects with [Yukon Highway 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagish_Road) and [Yukon Highway 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlin_Road) at [Jake's Corner, Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake's_Corner,_Yukon); the Pan-American Highway continues on Yukon 1 east-northeast from this junction.

At [Johnson's Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson's_Crossing,_Yukon), Yukon Highway 1 meets [Yukon Highway 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canol_Road) and travels southeast through [Teslin, Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teslin,_Yukon). The Pan-American Highway continues on Yukon 1 as it crosses over into [British Columbia](3392.md) (B. C.). After several miles, the Highway reenters the Yukon (once again as Highway 1) and continues southeast of [Watson Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_Lake,_Yukon) until it, once again, enters British Columbia as B.C. Highway 97.

#### British Columbia

After travelling about 8 km (5 mi) past the British Columbia–Yukon border, the Pan-American Highway reaches the first settlement in British Columbia at [Lower Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Post,_British_Columbia). After travelling about 32 km (20 mi) east, the highway *once again* re-enters the Yukon for roughly 8 mi (13 km). The Highway then re-enters British Columbia (as BC 97) for the final time. The Pan-American Highway continues south to southeast through a long uninhabited stretch until it passes through the villages of [Fireside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fireside,_British_Columbia) and [Coal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_River,_British_Columbia), then runs east parallel to the [Liard River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liard_River).[^cite_note-9]

The Pan-American Highway continues on B.C. Highway 97 as it passes through [Toad River Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toad_River,_British_Columbia), and then Summit Lake, which is nested between Stone Mountain and Mount Saint George. Further down the road, B.C. Highway 97 intersects with B.C. Highway 77; the Pan-American Highway continues along B.C. 97 east to [Fort Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Nelson,_British_Columbia).

From Fort Nelson, the Highway travels south for about 290 km (180 mi) until it reaches [Fort St. John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_St._John,_British_Columbia). It continues on B.C. Highway 97 southeast for another 60 km (37 mi) to reach the end of the Alaska Highway at [Dawson Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson_Creek).

#### Alberta

After B.C Highway 97, the unofficial route becomes [Alberta Highway 43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Highway_43). In approximately 7.2 km (4.5 mi), Highway 43 enters into the first settlement, Demmitt. For about 75 km (47 mi), Highway 43 goes into [Grande Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Prairie). At [Clairmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clairmont,_Alberta), Highway 43, turns to [Alberta Highway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Highway_2), Highway 43 goes left. Highway 43 goes for 455 km (283 mi) before reaching [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton). The unofficial route turns two ways, one way goes to [Lloydminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloydminster), [Minneapolis](6097240.md), and [Dallas](53838.md) and merges with the second way. The second way goes to [Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary) and the US border.

### Contiguous United States



Interstate 35 in the U.S. state of Iowa. I-35 is a de facto branch of the Pan-American Highway.



Interstate 25 in the U.S. state of New Mexico. I-25 is part of the other de facto branch, and is signed "Pan-American Freeway" in Albuquerque.

In 1966, the U.S. [Federal Highway Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Highway_Administration) designated the entire Interstate Highway System as part of the Pan-American Highway System,[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] but this has not been expressed in any of the official interstate signage. Of the many freeways that make up this comprehensive system, several are notable because of their mainly north–south orientation and their links to the main Mexican route and its spurs, as well as to key routes in Canada that link to the Alaska Highway.

These include the following:

- [Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5) runs north from the [Mexican border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border) in [San Diego](28504.md), California, to [Blaine, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaine,_Washington), south of [Vancouver, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver,_British_Columbia), then links indirectly with [British Columbia Highway 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Highway_99) north of the Canada–US border. A technically direct link between the same interstate and the [U.S. Route 97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_97) system can be found near [Weed, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weed,_California). US Route 97 runs northeast then north through Oregon and Washington from this junction, and becomes BC Highway 97 at the border with Canada.

- [Interstate 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_15) links San Diego with [Alberta Highway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Highway_2) that eventually crosses into British Columbia and ends at the southern terminus of the Alaska Highway. [Interstate 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_8) provides an east–west link from San Diego to [Interstate 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_10) near [Phoenix, Arizona](49121.md). The latter continues to Tucson and links with [Interstate 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_19), which becomes a spur of the Pan-American highway through Mexico at the [Nogales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogales,_Sonora) border crossing.

- [Interstate 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_25) runs north from [Interstate 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_10) at [Las Cruces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Cruces,_New_Mexico), [New Mexico](21649.md), to [Interstate 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90) in [Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_Wyoming), [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming). This route has no direct extension into Canada but links indirectly to Interstate 15. [Interstate 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_25) in [Albuquerque, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albuquerque,_New_Mexico), was named the [Pan-American Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-American_Freeway),

### Related North American highways

Several North American routes have names that make no direct reference to the Pan-American Highway, in part because some sections follow highways that are not up to full freeway standard.

- The [CANAMEX Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CANAMEX_Corridor) is designated from Mexico City to the western United States from Arizona to Montana,

  [^cite_note-14]

  &#x20;and continues north into western Canada. Although lacking any official status for the Pan American Highway in Canada, this is the only official North American highway that runs through Canada, the U.S., and Mexico to link the Alaska Highway with the Pan-American Highway at Mexico City. Unlike corresponding Pan American routes in the American southwest, the Canamex Highway bypasses San Diego by using several non-interstate highways to provide a shortcut from I-15 at [Las Vegas, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas,_Nevada) to I-10 at [Phoenix, Arizona](49121.md) for traffic accessing I-15 from the Nogales border crossing.

- The [CanAm Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CanAm_Highway) follows Interstate 25 from El Paso to [U.S. Route 85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_85) north of [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver), [Colorado](5399.md), then continues into the Canadian province of [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan), following parts of provincial highways 35, 39, 6, 3, and 2 in succession before terminating at [La Ronge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ronge). This route was first proposed during the 1920s, but was never properly promoted or developed. A section of the CanAm in southern Saskatchewan has deteriorated to the point where it is no longer a paved highway.

  [^cite_note-SP-15]

- The [NAFTA Superhighway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAFTA_Superhighway) tag has been unofficially used in connection with [Interstate 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_35) from Laredo, Texas to the Canadian border; there it downgrades to a non-freeway route ending at [Thunder Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_Bay), Ontario. A spur follows [Interstate 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_29) to the border, where it also downgrades to an arterial highway that extends to [Winnipeg](100730.md), Manitoba. The NAFTA highway sometimes unofficially includes [Interstate 69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_69), which is mostly complete from western Kentucky to the Canada–US border at [Port Huron, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Huron,_Michigan). In Canada, [Ontario Highway 402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Highway_402) and other freeways in the [Quebec City–Windsor Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City%E2%80%93Windsor_Corridor) can be considered a northeastward extension of this version of the NAFTA superhighway. To the southwest, from western Kentucky to the Mexican border, there is no single superhighway yet completed. Pending completion of I-69, the main highway links to Mexico follow parts of US routes 45 and 51 from Kentucky to western Tennessee, I-155 into Missouri, parts of Interstates 55 and 40 from Missouri to Arkansas, and [I-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_30) to the Texas stretch of I-35 that continues to the Mexican border at [Laredo, Texas](136773.md). The section of I-69 to be completed south of Kentucky is expected eventually to continue southwestward to the Texas Gulf Coast. It will have a spur linking to the original Pan-American route through Mexico to Laredo, and additional branches extending to the Mexican spurs that cross the border at [Pharr, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharr,_Texas), and [Brownsville, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsville,_Texas).

### Mexico



1933 map of the Inter-American Highway portion of the Pan-American Highway.

The official route of the Pan-American Highway through Mexico (where it is known as the [Inter-American Highway](4211634.md)) starts at [Nuevo Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevo_Laredo), Tamaulipas (opposite [Laredo, Texas](136773.md)), and goes south to Mexico City along [Mexican Federal Highway 85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_85).[^cite_note-16]

An alternative route begins at the border crossing between [San Diego, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego,_California) and [Tijuana, Baja California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana,_Baja_California). [Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5) in the United States connects to Mexican Federal Highway 1 at the busiest international border crossing in the world. The Pan-American Highway continues south to Mexico City along two separate routes: historic [Mexican Federal Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_1) and toll [Mexican Federal Highway 1D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_1D) via Baja California Peninsula or [Mexican Federal Highway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_2) via the mainland.

The Pan-American Highway (as Mexico Highway 85D) enters Mexico City,[^cite_note-17] but downtown Mexico City can be bypassed using Mexico Highway 136 (a divided limited-access route) and Mexico Highway 115, which reconnects to Mexico Highway 95D south of the [Mexican Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_District).

Later branches were built to the border as follows:

- [Nogales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogales,_Arizona) spur – [Mexican Federal Highway 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_15) from Mexico City

- [El Paso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Texas) spur – [Mexican Federal Highway 45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_45) from Highway 85 north of Mexico City to [Ciudad Juárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Ju%C3%A1rez), [Chihuahua](23962301.md)

- [Eagle Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Pass,_Texas) spur – unknown, possibly [Mexican Federal Highway 57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_57) from Mexico City to [Piedras Negras, Coahuila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedras_Negras,_Coahuila)

- [Pharr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharr,_Texas) spur – [Mexican Federal Highway 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_40) from [Monterrey](40455.md) to [Reynosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reynosa), Tamaulipas

- [Brownsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsville,_Texas) spur – [Mexican Federal Highway 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_101) from [Ciudad Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Victoria) to [Matamoros, Tamaulipas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matamoros,_Tamaulipas)

From Mexico City to the border with [Guatemala](17238567.md), the highway follows [Mexican Federal Highway 190](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_190).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] In the inaugural [Carrera Panamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrera_Panamericana) road race, organized by the Mexican government, the terminus of this southern route was said to be at [Ciudad Cuauhtémoc, Chiapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Cuauht%C3%A9moc,_Chiapas), at the Guatemalan border.[^cite_note-21]

Specifically, as the Pan-American Highway continues south of Mexico City, it runs through the city of [Cuernavaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuernavaca) about 48 km (30 mi) south of the Mexican capital. Here, the Pan-American Highway heads east along [Federal Highway 190](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_190) through the state of [Puebla](413436.md); for about 32 km (20 mi), it is a limited-access divided highway. The route then reverts to an undivided highway and enters the state of [Oaxaca](156896.md). From [Huajuapan de León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huajuapan_de_Le%C3%B3n) to the Oaxaca state capital of [Oaxaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaxaca,_Oaxaca) is about 140 km (87 mi).

From the city of Oaxaca, the Highway continues southeast as Mexico Highway 190 for about 230 km (140 mi) to the village of [Juchitán de Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juchit%C3%A1n_de_Zaragoza). The Pan-American Highway is now in southern Mexico, which is a combination of small mountains, hills, and jungles. It is another 90 km (56 mi) to the border with the state of [Chiapas](6787.md) where the Highway crosses the [Continental Divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Divide). From the Oaxaca–Chiapas state border, it is 160 km (99 mi) to the Chiapas state capital of [Tuxtla Gutiérrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuxtla_Guti%C3%A9rrez). The Highway then crosses the [Mexico–Guatemala border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala%E2%80%93Mexico_border) at [Ciudad Cuauhtémoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Cuauht%C3%A9moc,_Chiapas).

### Central America



Pan-American Highway in [Chimaltenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimaltenango) (Guatemala), 2015.

The Pan-American (or Inter-American) highway passes through the Central American countries with the highway designation of CA-1 (Central American Highway 1). [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize) was supposedly included in the route at one time, after it switched to driving on the right. Before independence, as British Honduras, it was the only Central American country to drive on the left side of the road.[^cite_note-22]

#### Guatemala

Upon crossing into Guatemala, Mexico Highway 190 becomes Central America Highway 1 and continues for about 80 km (50 mi) from the border village of [La Mesilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Mesilla) to the city of [Huehuetenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huehuetenango) near the [Maya](18449273.md) ruins of [Zaculeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaculeu). The Pan-American Highway crosses the Continental Divide again and into the [Sierra de los Cuchumatanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_los_Cuchumatanes) mountains.

From Huehuetenango to [Chimaltenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimaltenango) is roughly 160 km (99 mi) with Mayan ruins at [Iximché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iximch%C3%A9), just north of [Tecpán Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecp%C3%A1n_Guatemala). From Chimaltenango, it is about 35 to 40 km (22 to 25 mi) to [Guatemala City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_City), as a divided highway.

From Guatemala City to [Cuilapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuilapa) it is only partially a divided highway for 50 km (31 mi) and another 55 km (34 mi) to [Jutiapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutiapa). The highway continues as CA Highway 1 and approaches the [border with El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala%E2%80%93El_Salvador_border). It is 50 km (31 mi) to the border crossing at San Cristobal Frontera.

In Guatemala, the Pan-American highway passes through 10 [departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Guatemala), including [The Department of Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_Department), where it passes through [Guatemala City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_City).

#### El Salvador



Carretera Panamericana westbound at [Nejapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nejapa). Note "CA-1 A", signage for alternative CA-1, north bypass of San Salvador.

[El Salvador](9356.md) is the smallest country (by area) along the route of the Pan-American Highway. After crossing into El Salvador at [Candelaria de la Frontera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candelaria_de_la_Frontera), the Inter-American Highway continues toward [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_El_Salvador) as Central America Highway 1. From the border crossing to Santa Ana is about 13 km (8.1 mi).

From Santa Ana, this is a divided highway all along its 45 km (28 mi) to [San Salvador, El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvador,_El_Salvador)'s capital and largest city. At [Nueva San Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_San_Salvador), the highway passes near the Volcano de San Salvador.

From San Salvador to [Cojutepeque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cojutepeque) is about 15 km (9.3 mi); following the highway southeast to [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel,_El_Salvador) is about 65 km (40 mi); the divided highway ends at San Vicente. From San Miguel to the [El Salvador–Honduras border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador%E2%80%93Honduras_border) is about 30 km (19 mi).

In El Salvador, the highway also passes through the cities of [Santa Tecla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Tecla,_El_Salvador), [Antiguo Cuscatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiguo_Cuscatl%C3%A1n), and [San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Mart%C3%ADn,_San_Salvador).

#### Honduras

The highway crosses the border into Honduras at El Amatillo near [Nacaome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacaome) (40 km (25 mi) from border). Just past Nacaome is a highway traveling north to [Tegucigalpa](25994100.md), the capital of Honduras. Traveling south, it is 40 km (25 mi) to [Choluteca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choluteca,_Choluteca), the fourth-largest city in Honduras. From Choluteca to the border crossing, just past [San Marcos de Colón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marcos_de_Col%C3%B3n), is about 68 km (42 mi). The Pan-American Highway's total distance in Honduras is about 148 km (92 mi).

#### Nicaragua

From Honduras, it passes into Nicaragua at [El Espino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Espino,_Nicaragua?action=edit\&redlink=1), passing through the Nicaraguan cities of [Somoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somoto), [Estelí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estel%C3%AD), Sebaco, [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua), [Jinotepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinotepe), and [Rivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivas,_Nicaragua) before entering Costa Rica at Peñas Blancas.

From the crossing at the [Honduras–Nicaragua border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras%E2%80%93Nicaragua_border), the highway continues as Central America Highway 1 to the town of [Ocotal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocotal), about 42 km (26 mi). From Ocotal to [Estelí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estel%C3%AD) is about 58 km (36 mi), and on to the village of [Sébaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9baco) is about 46 km (29 mi). At this point, the Inter-American Highway turns from southeast to south towards [Ciudad Darío](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Dar%C3%ADo), which is 15 km (9.3 mi) from Sébaco. From Ciudad Dario to the village of [San Benito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Benito,_Nicaragua?action=edit\&redlink=1) is 55 km (34 mi).

From San Benito, it is about 38 km (24 mi) to Nicaragua's capital and largest city, [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua), on the shores of [Lake Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Managua). From Managua south to the town of [Jinotepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinotepe,_Carazo) is about 50 km (31 mi), and Jinotepe to the town of [Rivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivas,_Nicaragua) is about 70 km (43 mi). Around this area the Highway is in view of [Lake Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nicaragua), which is the largest lake in Central America. From Rivas to the [Nicaragua–Costa Rica border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua%E2%80%93Costa_Rica_border) is about 35 km (22 mi).

#### Costa Rica

In Costa Rica, the Pan-American Highway is known as *Carretera Interamericana* (Inter-American Highway) and is composed of two segments [Carretera Interamericana Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_1_\(Costa_Rica\)) (Route 1) and [Carretera Interamericana Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_2_\(Costa_Rica\)) (Route 2).

It passes through [Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia,_Costa_Rica), [San José](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jos%C3%A9,_Costa_Rica), [Cartago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartago,_Costa_Rica), [Pérez Zeledón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9rez_Zeled%C3%B3n_\(canton\)), Palmares, Neily, before crossing into Panama at Paso Canoas.

The highest point in the entire Pan-American Highway occurs at the [Cerro de la Muerte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_de_la_Muerte) (Death Hill) in the Carretera Interamericana Sur segment, at 3,335 m (10,942 ft).

An alternative route used by cross country buses and freight transportation that avoids crossing through the [Greater Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Metropolitan_Area_\(Costa_Rica\)) and Cerro de la Muerte, is by taking [Route 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_23_\(Costa_Rica\)) in [Puntarenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puntarenas_\(canton\)) canton from Route 1, then [Route 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_27_\(Costa_Rica\)) and [Route 34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_34_\(Costa_Rica\)), and taking Route 2 in [Osa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osa_\(canton\)) canton.

After entering Costa Rica, the Highway separates two national parks, the [Santa Rosa National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Rosa_National_Park) to the west and [Guanacaste National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanacaste_National_Park_\(Costa_Rica\)) to the east. From the Nicaragua–Costa Rica border to the town of [Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia,_Costa_Rica) is about 76.8 km (47.7 mi). In the region of Costa Rica, the Pan-American Highway runs just west of the [Cordillera de Guanacaste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanacaste_Cordillera) (Guanacaste Mountains), which includes the active volcanoes of [Rincón de la Vieja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinc%C3%B3n_de_la_Vieja_Volcano) and [Miravalles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miravalles_Volcano). While travelling through Costa Rica, north of San Jose, the highway route is known as Costa Rica Highway 1 instead of CA Highway 1. From San Jose south to Panama, the highway route is known as Costa Rica Highway 2.[^cite_note-23]

Liberia to the town of [Barranca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranca,_Costa_Rica?action=edit\&redlink=1) is 120 km (75 mi) from Barranca; the [Cordillera de Tilarán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_de_Tilar%C3%A1n) (Tilarán Mountains) can be seen from the Inter-American Highway. The Tilarán range includes [Arenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arenal_Volcano), one of the world's most active volcanoes. From Barranca, the highway heads east across the mountains and the Continental Divide once again. From Barranca, it is roughly 77 km (48 mi) to the town of [Alajuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alajuela).

After Alajuela the [Cordillera Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central,_Costa_Rica) (Central Mountains) come into view from the Inter-American Highway. The Central Mountains include four large volcanoes--[Poás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po%C3%A1s_Volcano), [Barva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barva_Volcano), [Irazú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraz%C3%BA_Volcano) and [Turrialba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turrialba_Volcano). From Alajuela to San José is about fifteen km (9.3 mi).

[San José](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jos%C3%A9,_Costa_Rica) is the capital and largest city in Costa Rica. Leaving San José, the Inter-American Highway winds its way roughly southeast. From San José to [San Isidro de El General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro_de_El_General) is about 136 km (85 mi).

From San Isidro, the [Cordillera de Talamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_de_Talamanca) (Talamanca Mountains) rise up from the rain forest canopy. The Talamanca range, which is non-volcanic, includes [Cerro Chirripó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Chirrip%C3%B3), Costa Rica's highest mountain peak at 3,820 m (12,530 ft). From San Isidro to [Palmar Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmar_Sur) is roughly 122 km (80 mi), and Palmar Sur to the [Costa Rica–Panama border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica%E2%80%93Panama_border) is 91.9 km (57.1 mi).

#### Panama



Pan-American Highway at [David, Chiriquí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David,_Chiriqu%C3%AD)

From the Costa Rica–Panama border to La Concepción, the highway length is approximately 20 km (10 mi), from La Concepción to [David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David,_Chiriqu%C3%AD) the distance is 25 km (16 mi). The highway enters Panama traveling generally from west to east.

David, the capital of the [Chiriqui Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiriqu%C3%AD_Province), is located about 8 km (5 mi) north of the town of [Pedregal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedregal,_Panam%C3%A1_District) and the [Gulf of Chiriquí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Chiriqu%C3%AD). From David, the highway travels east about 90 km (56 mi) to [Tolé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tol%C3%A9). From Tolé to the town of [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago,_Panama) the distance is approximately 80 km (50 mi). About halfway to Santiago, the Pan-American Highway crosses over the [San Pablo river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pablo_River_\(Panama\)).

From Santiago to [Aguadulce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguadulce,_Panama), the Pan American Highway travels 61 km (38 mi), where it reenters the tropical lowlands. From Aguadulce to [Penonomé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penonom%C3%A9,_Cocl%C3%A9), it stretches about 48 km (30 mi). This section of highway crosses the [Santa María river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_River_\(Panama\)).

From Penonomé, the highway travels southeast, then northeast, then roughly north in a loop as it avoids crossing into Panama's Central Mountains. From Penonomé to [La Chorerra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Chorrera,_Panama) the distance is 100 km (62 mi), while from La Chorerra, the distance is 25 km (16 mi) to [Balboa](529028.md) just west of [Panama City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_City).

Panama City is the capital and largest city in Panama. Before entering the city, the Pan-American Highway crosses over the Panama Canal on the [Centennial Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial_Bridge,_Panama), From Panama City, the Highway turns northeast. From Panama City to [Chepo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chepo,_Panam%C3%A1_Province), the highest is roughly 55 km (34 mi), from Chepo to [Cañita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C3%B1ita,_Panama) is another 25 km (16 mi).

At the village of Cañita is the old terminus of the northern route of the Pan-American Highway. The highway continues another 201 km (125 mi) past Cañita to the village of [Yaviza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaviza), a village near the junction of the [Tuira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuira_River) and [Chucunaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chucunaque_River) rivers. It is here that the northern section of the Pan-American Highway ends. Southeast of here is the virtually impenetrable [Darién Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Gap), a 100 km (62 mi) stretch of rugged, mountainous jungle terrain. The highway is being extended 6 km to [Pinogana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinogana), which will include a bridge over the Chucunaque River at Yaviza.[^cite_note-24]

United States government funding was particularly significant to complete the high-level [Bridge of the Americas](1996352.md) over the Panama Canal, during the years when the canal was administered by the United States.

## Darién Gap

Main article: [Darién Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Gap)



Map of the Darién Gap and the break in the Pan-American Highway between Yaviza, Panama and Turbo, Colombia

The Pan-American Highway is interrupted between Panama and Colombia by a 106-kilometre (66 mi) stretch of marshland known as the Darién Gap. The highway terminates at [Turbo, Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo,_Colombia), and [Yaviza, Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaviza,_Panama). Because of swamps, marshes, and rivers, construction would be very expensive. Although technically possible, crossing on foot is both extremely difficult and dangerous.

Efforts have been made for decades to eliminate the gap in the Pan-American Highway, but have been controversial. Planning began in 1971 with the help of United States funding,[^cite_note-25] but this was halted in 1974 after concerns raised by environmentalists. Another effort to build the road began in 1992, but by 1994 a United Nations agency reported that the road, and the subsequent development, would cause extensive environmental damage. The [Embera-Wounaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embera-Wounaan) and [Guna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guna_people) have also expressed concern that the road could bring about the potential erosion of their cultures.

First Mexican by-foot crossers take a rest by the "Lost Corvair" abandoned in 1961 by a failed caravan from Chicago.

The first Mexican by-foot crossers take a rest by the "Lost Corvair", abandoned in 1961 by a caravan from Chicago.

The Darién Gap has challenged adventurers for many years. A 1961 expedition with three [Chevrolet Corvair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Corvair) rear-engine cars and two support trucks completed the trip south from Chicago through to the Colombian border.[^cite_note-26] A 1971–72 British expedition from Alaska to Argentina attempted to transit the Gap with two standard-production [Range Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_Rover_Classic), supported by a team of [Land Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Rover_series). They barely succeeded in thrashing a passage through the extreme terrain.[^cite_note-27] In 1979, a team led by Mark Smith drove standard production [CJ7-model Jeeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeep_CJ) from South to North, traversing the Gap with difficulty.[^cite_note-28] In 1984, Loren and Patty Upton made the first "all land" vehicle crossing of the Gap using a 1966 Jeep CJ-5 named *Sand Ship Discovery*. It took months of slogging, winching, chopping, and digging their way through the inhospitable jungles of the Darién Gap.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

One proposed option to bridge the gap is a short ferry link from Colombia to a new ferry port in Panama,[^cite_note-31] with an extension of the existing Panama highway that would complete the highway without violating these environmental concerns. However, past attempts to operate such a service have ended in failure.

## Southern section



A **Vía PanAm** shield sign is sometimes found on routes in South American countries (such as Chile) associated with the Pan-American Highway.



Sculpture of a native man standing at the entrance of [Fusagasugá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusagasug%C3%A1), Colombia, over the Highway 40.

### Colombia and Venezuela

The southern part of the highway begins in [Turbo, Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo,_Colombia), from where it follows Colombia Highway 62 to [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn). On [Google Maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Maps), the beginning of the highway is at a marker at the end of a road built out to a place along the proposed extended highway in the flatlands 11 miles from the [Atrato River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrato_River) and 20 miles from the main highway called "El Cuarenta" or "Lomas Aisladas" ("Isolated hills"), which has a marker "Inicio del tramo sur de la carretera Panamericana" ("Start of the southern section of the Pan-American Highway"). At Medellín, Colombia Highway 56 leads to Bogotá, but Colombia Highway 25 turns south for a more direct route. Colombia Highway 40 is routed southwest from Bogotá to join Highway 25 at [Zarzal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarzal). Highway 25 continues all the way to the border with Ecuador.[^cite_note-32]

Travelers along the [Inter-American Highway](4211634.md) portion of the Pan-American Highway in Panama can take a ferry from [Panama City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_City) to the port of [Buenaventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenaventura,_Colombia), which is 115 km northwest of [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali). Cali represents a major junction between Buenaventura and two northern spurs of the Pan-American Highway that connect from northern Colombia and Venezuela.

The main route of the Pan-American Highway in Colombia (starting from the northeast) begins just east of [Cúcuta](623811.md), the capital city of the department of [Norte de Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_de_Santander). The highway follows [Colombia Route 55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_Route_55?action=edit\&redlink=1) for 63 km from Cúcuta to Pamplona, where it shifts to [Colombia Route 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_Route_66?action=edit\&redlink=1) for 45 km to reach the border with the department of [Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_Department).

From the department border, Route 66 continues southwest for 50 km toward [Bucaramanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucaramanga), state capital of [Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_Department) located on a plateau in the [Cordillera Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Oriental,_Colombia). From Bucaramanga, the Pan-American Highway switches from Route 66 to [Colombia Route 45A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_Route_45A?action=edit\&redlink=1), which it follows south by southwest to the town of Barbosa. This 203-km stretch on Route 45A is a toll road. Approximately 26 km of this stretch of highway enters the department of [Boyacá](185665.md) and reenters Santander between Vado Real and Guepsa. From Barbosa, the Pan-American Highways switches from Route 45A to [Colombia Route 62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_Route_62?action=edit\&redlink=1) and immediately reenters Boyacá towards Tunja, the state capital of Boyacá.

A 53-km stretch of highway connects Barbosa with [Tunja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunja), an important agriculture and mining center in the region. The Pan-American Highway switches routes again in Tunja, returning to [Colombia Route 55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_Route_55?action=edit\&redlink=1) on its way to [Cundinamarca](189405.md) and the national capital, Bogotá. The stretch of highway from Tunja to the departmental border with Cundinamarca is 54 km and is a toll road. From the Cundinamarca departmental line, the highway continues another 26 km without tolls before becoming a toll road again. From that point, the highway reaches Bogotá in 52 km.

In Bogotá, the highway crosses from the north to the southwest portion of the city, switching from Route 55 to [Colombia Route 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_Route_40?action=edit\&redlink=1). Continuing as a toll road from Bogotá, it travels for 128 km through [Fusagasugá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusagasug%C3%A1) to the departmental border with [Tolima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolima_Department).

From the Tolima departmental border, the highway continues as a toll road for another 16 km to [El Espinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Espinal_\(Tolima\)). It travels west and after another 37 km, reaches the city of [Ibagué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibagu%C3%A9). From Ibagué to the [Quindío](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quind%C3%ADo_Department) departmental border near La Linea is about 77 km.

Upon crossing into Quindío, [Colombia Route 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_Route_40?action=edit\&redlink=1) continues westward for another four km before reaching [Calarcá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calarc%C3%A1), where Route 40 splits into two spurs. One enters [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia,_Colombia). The spurs rejoin about 18 km southwest of Calarcá at the town of Club Campestre. From Club Campestre, it covers another 16 km until reaching the [Valle del Cauca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_del_Cauca) departmental border.

From the border, the Pan-American Highway travels 26 km to the town of La Paila, where it crosses [Colombia Route 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_Route_25?action=edit\&redlink=1). At this junction, the two routes merge and become a toll road for 61 km from that point to Buga. At Buga, Route 40 splits west toward the city of Buenaventura and the Pacific Ocean; the Pan-American Highway continues south on Route 25 for another 42 km until arriving near Palmira. From Palmira, the highway continues southwest for 23 km, where it reaches the large city of [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali). From Cali to the [Cauca](549662.md) departmental border is 19 km.

The highway continues south along Route 25 throughout its length in the department of Cauca. After 50 km, the highway becomes a toll road at Santander de Quilichao until reaching Santander de Quilichao and [Popayán](657155.md) after 74 km. It continues generally southwest from Popayán; reaching Mojarras after 135 km. At Mojarras, Route 25 splits into two spurs; the western spur is preferred for traveling south toward the [Nariño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nari%C3%B1o) departmental border. From Mojarras to the departmental border near Remolino is 36 km.

From the Nariño departmental border, the highway continues south as Route 25. From the border to the city of [Pasto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasto_\(Colombia\)) is 84 km. From Pasto to the national border between Colombia and Ecuador near Ipiales is 82 km. The Pan-American crosses the border at the [Rumichaca Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumichaca_Bridge).

#### Simón Bolívar Highway

Another route, known as the Simón Bolívar Highway, runs from Bogotá (Colombia) to La Guaira (Venezuela). It begins by using Colombia Highways 55 & 66 all the way to the border with Venezuela. From there it uses Venezuela Highway 1 to Caracas and Venezuela Highway 9 to its end at La Guaira.

The road ends at [Venezuela Highway 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_Highway_9?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Güiria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCiria,_Venezuela?action=edit\&redlink=1), a small town in the state of [Sucre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucre_\(state\)) just west of [Trinidad](3565457.md) along the Gulf of Paria coastline. From Güiria, the highway winds west 76 km (47 mi) towards the town of [Yaguaraparo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaguaraparo,_Venezuela?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Once the highway reaches Yaguaraparo, on the southern portion of the [Paria Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paria_Peninsula) along the [Gulf of Paria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Paria), Highway 9 continues west for approximately 83 km (52 mi) to the towns of [Casanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casanay,_Venezuela) and [Pantoño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panto%C3%B1o,_Venezuela?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Upon reaching Casanay, the highway crosses [Venezuela Highway 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_Highway_10?action=edit\&redlink=1), a major north–south highway. From Casanay and neighboring Pantoño, it continues west, paralleling the [Gulf of Cariaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cariaco_Basin). The highway crosses Secondary Highway 2 at [Villa Frontado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Frontado,_Venezuela?action=edit\&redlink=1), which travels south into the state of [Monagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monagas). The distance from Casanay to [Cumaná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuman%C3%A1,_Venezuela) is about 90 km (56 mi). From Cumaná, the road travels southwest approximately 65 km to the border with the state of [Anzoátegui](474415.md).

After crossing into Anzoátegui, it almost immediately enters the city of [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona,_Venezuela). Travelling from Barcelona and nearby [Puerto La Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_La_Cruz), the highway continues westward. For about 47 km, it becomes a limited-access [expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited-access_road), returning to a two-lane highway at [Puerto Píritu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_P%C3%ADritu,_Venezuela). The highway travels another 62 km reaches the border with the state of [Miranda](556284.md) at the town of [Boca de Uchire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_de_Uchire?action=edit\&redlink=1). This portion includes a short run through the *[llanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanos)*, or Venezuelan savannas.

About 34 km west of Boca de Uchire, the highway starts climbing up the [Cordillera Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Colombia\)), in the [Andes Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes_Mountains). Highway 9 begins here to move further away from the Caribbean Sea coastline. From Boca de Uchire to El Guapo, the distance is 65 km; from El Guapo to [Caucagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucagua,_Venezuela?action=edit\&redlink=1) adds 59 km.

At Caucagua, the Pan-American Highway crosses [Venezuela Highway 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_Highway_12?action=edit\&redlink=1). Highway 9 continues through the Cordillera Central. After about 21 km, the highway becomes a limited-access expressway for 32 km west towards the Caracas metropolitan area and the [Venezuelan Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_Federal_District).

[Maracay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracay) is the capital city of Aragua state in central Venezuela. It was officially established on March 5, 1701, by [Bishop Diego de Baños y Sotomayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_Diego_de_Ba%C3%B1os_y_Sotomayor?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the valleys of Tocopio and Tapatapa (what is known today as the central valley of Aragua) in northern Venezuela. From Maracay, the highway extends about 44 km to Valencia, passing San Joaquin and near Cuacara en route to the city.

[Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia,_Carabobo) is the capital city of Carabobo state. In Valencia, the highway shifts northwest and passes through mountains that are part of the [Sistema Coriano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistema_Coriano). The distance between Valencia and the small town of [El Palito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Palito,_Carabobo?action=edit\&redlink=1) on the Caribbean Sea is approximately 40 km. At El Palito, the road joins with [Venezuela Highway 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_Highway_3?action=edit\&redlink=1) for 10 km, then splits off at Morón. The distance from Morón to the state border with [Yaracuy state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaracuy_State) and the village of Guaremal is about 20 km.

From Guaremal to [San Felipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Felipe,_Venezuela), the major city in Yaracuy, is about 26 km. The city itself is located on a route adjacent to Highway 1. The road runs 73 km from San Felipe to the state border with [Lara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lara_State) (located just past the town of Cambural). The distance from the border to [Barquisimeto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquisimeto) is about 16 km.

From Barquisimeto, Highway 1 continues roughly west, then southwest (at around Agua Salada) for 147 km to the state line with Trujillo, near El Empedrado.

Once in the state of [Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_State) (near the town of Parajá), the Pan-American Highway continues in a southwest direction; it does not travel through the state capital city of [Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo,_Trujillo) but connects to Trujillo by way of state highways 3 and 1. The highway's length in this state is about 111 km.

The highway enters [Mérida state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida_State) near Arapuey. As in Trujillo, the highway does not travel through the major population centers, of which the largest is [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_M%C3%A9rida). Highway 1 connects to Mérida via an 88-km stretch of Mérida state highway 4. The highway covers 104 km in Mérida.

Upon entering the state of [Táchira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1chira_State), the high extends about 58 km from the border to the junction with [Venezuela Highway 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_Highway_6?action=edit\&redlink=1). From the junction to the city of [San Cristóbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal,_T%C3%A1chira) the distance is 44 km, although there is a separate expressway that parallels the Pan-American Highway along this stretch. From San Cristóbal to the Venezuela-Colombia border, near San Antonio de Táchira, the distance is 32 km.

### Peru, Ecuador, and Chile



Monument on the Equator on the highway near [Cayambe, Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayambe,_Ecuador).

[Ecuador Highway 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuador_Highway_35) runs the whole length of the country. [Ecuador Highway 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuador_Highway_35), the "Troncal de la Sierra" (Highland's Road), is commonly known to Ecuadorians as "La Panamericana" and forms Ecuador's contribution to the project. It connects cities and towns from the Sierra region, from Tulcán at the north (border with Colombia), passing through Quito, the country's capital, to the southern border with Peru. Part of this highway is a toll-road administered by Panavial, a private concessionary. The road condition is quite good, but it mostly goes through mountains and it has some bad trails around the province of Cañar (center of the country), making it a fairly dangerous road to drive on.

The Ecuadorian portion begins at the Colombian border in [Carchi province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carchi_Province) and almost immediately enters the city of [Tulcán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulc%C3%A1n), the capital of Carchi province. From Tulcán, the road continues south for 125 km, reaching [Ibarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibarra,_Ecuador). From Ibarra, the highway continues south for 115 km until reaching Quito. From Quito, the highway continues south for 89 km next reaching [Latacunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latacunga).

From Latacunga, the Pan-American Highway continues south for 47 km to [Ambato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambato_\(canton_seat\)). From Ambato, the road continues south for 52 km to [Riobamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riobamba). [Azogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azogues) is the capital of [Cañar Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C3%B1ar_Province). From Azogues the road becomes six lanes wide (three north, three south) built in 1995, until it reaches [Cuenca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuenca,_Ecuador).

From north of the city the Pan-American Highway takes the name of Avenida Circunvalación Sur (Avenue South Ring) until exiting to the south, where it shrinks to two lanes (one north, one south) enlarged in 2009 until reaching [Loja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loja,_Ecuador).

[Peru Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_Highway_1) carries the Pan-American Highway all the way through Peru to the border with Chile. The northern terminus of the highway is located in [Aguas Verdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguas_Verdes) ([Tumbes Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbes_Region)) at the border with Ecuador. Starting in this point, the highway is known as *Carretera Panamericana Norte* ("North Pan-American Highway") until it reaches a point located in central Lima, the country's capital.

From this point south the highway is called *Carretera Panamericana Sur* ("South Pan-American Highway"), until it reaches the southern border, located in the Santa Rosa Border Post (36 km south of [Tacna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacna), the highway's closest major city), in the [Tacna Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacna_Region) at the border with Chile.

From Lima to [Cañete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vicente_de_Ca%C3%B1ete) 148 km.

In Chile, the highway follows [Chile Route 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_Route_5) south to ([Llaillay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llaillay)), a point north of Santiago, where the highway splits into two parts, one of which goes through Chilean territory to [Puerto Montt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Montt), where it splits again, to [Quellón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quell%C3%B3n) on [Chiloé Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilo%C3%A9_Island), and to its continuation as the *[Carretera Austral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carretera_Austral)*. The other part goes east along [Chile Route 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_Route_60), which goes through the Andes to the Christ the Redeemer Tunnel, inside the [Los Libertadores Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paso_Internacional_Los_Libertadores). The Chilean-Argentinian border is located in the middle of the tunnel.[^cite_note-33]

### Bolivia

Bolivia’s access to the highway is primarily through [Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_1_\(Bolivia\)) and [Route 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_4_\(Bolivia\)). Bolivia is not on the main road of the highway. The Bolivian branch of the road passes through cities such as [El Alto](3475756.md), [Tarija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarija), [Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potos%C3%AD), [La Paz](42821.md), and [Oruro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oruro).

In Argentina, the [Argentina National Route 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_7_\(Argentina\)) starts in the Christ the Redeemer tunnel, and continues to Buenos Aires, the end of the main highway.[^cite_note-34] The highway network also continues south of Buenos Aires along [Argentina National Route 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_3_\(Argentina\)) towards the city of [Ushuaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushuaia) in [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego). Plans have been discussed for including the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies) in the Pan American Highway system. According to these, a system of ferries would be established to connect terminal points of the highway. Travelers would then be able to ferry from Key West to Havana, drive to the eastern tip of Cuba, ferry to Haiti, drive through Haiti and the Dominican Republic, and ferry again to Puerto Rico. Included in this system would also be a ferry from the western tip of Cuba to the Yucatán Peninsula. Mexico has already surveyed a route which will run across the Yucatán, Campeche, and Chiapas to San Cristobal de Las Casas, on the Pan American Highway. ("The Pan American Highway System" by Travel Division Pan American Union, Washington D.C. October 1947)

## Art and culture

Travel writer [Tim Cahill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Cahill_\(writer\)) wrote a book, *Road Fever*, about his record-setting 24-day drive from [Ushuaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushuaia) in the Argentine province of [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego,_Antarctica,_and_South_Atlantic_Islands_Province) to [Prudhoe Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudhoe_Bay) in the U.S. state of Alaska with professional long-distance driver Garry Sowerby, much of their route following the Pan-American Highway.[^cite_note-35]

In the British motoring show *[Top Gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gear_\(2002_TV_series\))*, the presenters drove on a section of the road in their off-road vehicles in the Bolivian Special.

## Sports

In 2003, [Kevin Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Sanders_\(motorcyclist\)), a [long-distance rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_riding), broke the [Guinness World Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) for the fastest traversal of the highway by motorcycle in 34 days.[^cite_note-Independent2005-36]

In 2018, British cyclist Dean Stott, who had planned on riding the length of the Americas in 110 days to set a new Guinness World Record, completed the 14,000-mile (22,500 km) journey in just under 100 days, riding south-to-north, breaking the record, set by Mexico's Carlos Santamaría Covarrubias in 2015, by 17 days.[^cite_note-37] Stott was inspired to push the timetable after learning that he and his wife had been invited to the [wedding of Prince Harry and Meghan Markle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Prince_Harry_and_Meghan_Markle), and would have missed the event had he stuck to his original schedule.[^cite_note-38] Stott's record lasted just a couple of months, as Austrian endurance cyclist Michael Strasser, riding north-to-south, broke the record with a time of 84 days, 11 hours and 50 minutes (July 23 – October 16, 2018).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

In 2024, American endurance cyclist Bond Almand IV broke Strasser's record, riding north-to-south in 75 days, 17 hours, and 55 minutes (August 31 – November 15, 2024).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

Between 23 August and 19 December 2025, [Canadian](19851291.md) Ashleigh Myles, of [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia), was the first woman to bike the entire Pan-American highway. It took her 118 days.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]
