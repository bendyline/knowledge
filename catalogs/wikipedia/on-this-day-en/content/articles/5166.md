**Copenhagen**[^cite_note-8] ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *København* [\[kʰøpm̩ˈhɑwˀn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Danish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/eb/Da-K%C3%B8benhavn.ogg/Da-K%C3%B8benhavn.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/eb/Da-K%C3%B8benhavn.ogg/Da-K%C3%B8benhavn.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is the capital and most populous city of [Denmark](76972.md), with an [urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) population of 1.4 million.[^cite_note-Danmarks_Statistik-9][^cite_note-ReferenceA-10] The city is situated mainly on the island of [Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealand) (*Sjælland*), with a smaller part on the island of [Amager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager). Copenhagen is separated from [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6), [Sweden](5058739.md), by the [Øresund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund) strait. The [Øresund Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund_Bridge) connects the two cities by rail and road.

Settled as a [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) fishing village in the 10th century in the vicinity of what is now [Gammel Strand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gammel_Strand), Copenhagen became the capital of Denmark in the early 15th century. During the 16th century, the city served as the *[de facto](43594.md)* capital of the [Kalmar Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmar_Union) and the seat of the Union's [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy), which governed most of the modern-day [Nordic region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_region) as part of a Danish [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation) with [Sweden](5058739.md) and [Norway](21241.md). The city flourished as the cultural and economic centre of [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) during the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance). By the 17th century, it had become a regional centre of power, serving as the heart of the Danish government and [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Denmark). During the 18th century, Copenhagen suffered a [devastating plague outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War_plague_outbreak) and urban [conflagrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflagration). Major redevelopment included the construction of the prestigious district of [Frederiksstaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksstaden) and the establishment of cultural institutions such as the [Royal Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Theatre) and the [Royal Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Fine_Arts). The city became the centre of the [Danish slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_slave_trade) during this period. In 1807, the city was [bombarded by a British fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Copenhagen_\(1807\)) during the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), before the [Danish Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Golden_Age) brought a [Neoclassical look](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) to Copenhagen's architecture. After [World War II](32927.md), the [Finger Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_Plan) fostered the development of housing and businesses along the five urban railway routes emanating from the city centre.

Since the turn of the 21st century, Copenhagen has seen strong urban and cultural development, facilitated by investment in its institutions and infrastructure. The city is the [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Denmark), [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Denmark), and [governmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Denmark) centre of [Denmark](76972.md); it is one of the major financial centres of [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe) with the [Copenhagen Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Stock_Exchange). Copenhagen's economy has developed rapidly in the [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector), especially through initiatives in [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology), [pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceuticals), and [clean technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_technology). Since the completion of the [Øresund Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund_Bridge), Copenhagen has increasingly integrated with the Swedish province of [Scania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scania) and its largest city, [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6), forming the [Øresund Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund_Region). With several bridges connecting the various districts, the cityscape is characterised by parks, promenades, and waterfronts. Copenhagen's landmarks, such as [Tivoli Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivoli_Gardens), *[The Little Mermaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Mermaid_\(statue\))* statue, the [Amalienborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalienborg) and [Christiansborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg) palaces, [Rosenborg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenborg_Castle), [Frederik's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik's_Church), [Børsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B8rsen), and many museums, restaurants, and nightclubs are significant tourist attractions.

Copenhagen is home to the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen), the [Technical University of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Denmark), [Copenhagen Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Business_School), and the [IT University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IT_University_of_Copenhagen). The University of Copenhagen, founded in 1479, is the oldest university in Denmark. Copenhagen is home to the football clubs [F.C. Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.C._Copenhagen) and [Brøndby IF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8ndby_IF). The annual [Copenhagen Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Marathon) was established in 1980. Copenhagen is one of the most bicycle-friendly cities in the world. [Movia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movia_\(transit_agency\)) is a public mass transit company serving all of eastern Denmark except [Bornholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornholm). The [Copenhagen Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Metro), launched in 2002, serves central Copenhagen. Additionally, the [Copenhagen S-train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_S-train), the [Lokaltog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokaltog) ([private railway](https://da.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatbane)), and the [Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Line_\(Denmark\)) network serve and connect central Copenhagen to outlying boroughs. Serving roughly 2.7 million passengers a month, [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport), [Kastrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastrup), is the busiest airport in the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries).

## Etymology

Copenhagen's name (*København* in Danish), reflects its origin as a harbour and a place of commerce. The original designation in [Old Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse), from which Danish descends, was **_Kaupmannahǫfn_** [\[ˈkɔupˌmɑnːɑˌhɔvn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)[^cite_note-11] meaning 'merchants'[^cite_note-12] [harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor)': the direct corresponding English [cognates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognate) of this name would be "[chapmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman_\(occupation\))'s [haven](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/haven)"[^cite_note-13], the city's English toponym however was acquired through [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German) *Kopenhagen*. Copenhagen's [Swedish](26689.md) name is *Köpenhamn*, a direct translation of the mutually intelligible Danish name.

By the time [Old Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Danish) was spoken, the capital was called **_Køpmannæhafn_**, with the current name deriving from centuries of subsequent regular [sound change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_change) by [monophthongization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monophthongization) of /au/ to /o/ as seen in [Asbjörn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asbj%C3%B8rn)-[Osborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osborn_\(surname\)), Austrie etc., and this change in [pronunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pronunciation) happened some time around 1400.

The city's Latin name *Hafnia*, from the final *[havn](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/havn#Danish)* or "haven", is the namesake of the element [hafnium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafnium).[^cite_note-Authier2013-14]

### Early history

Substantial discoveries of [flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint) tools in the area provide evidence of human settlements dating to the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age).[^cite_note-15] Many historians believe the town dates to the late [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age), and was possibly founded by [Sweyn I Forkbeard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweyn_I_Forkbeard).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECunningham201335-16]

Multiple finds indicate that Copenhagen originated was a city by the 11th century. The first habitations were probably centred on [Gammel Strand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gammel_Strand) (literally 'old shore') in the 11th century or even earlier.[^cite_note-17] The natural harbour and good [herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herring) stocks seem to have attracted seasonal fishermen and merchants from the 11th century and more permanently in the 13th century.[^cite_note-khdsd-18] Recent [archaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological) finds during work on the city's rail system revealed the remains of a large merchant mansion from c. 1020 near [Kongens Nytorv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongens_Nytorv). The remains of a church with graves from the 11th century were unearthed near where [Strøget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Str%C3%B8get) meets [Rådhuspladsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A5dhuspladsen). Excavations in [Pilestræde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilestr%C3%A6de) uncovered a well from the late 12th century.&#x20;

The earliest written mention of the town was in the 12th century when [Saxo Grammaticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxo_Grammaticus) in [Gesta Danorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesta_Danorum) referred to it as *Portus Mercatorum*, meaning "Merchants' Harbour" or, in the [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language) of the time, *Købmannahavn*.[^cite_note-metro1415-19] Traditionally, Copenhagen's founding has been dated to [Bishop Absalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_Absalon)'s construction of a modest fortress on the little island of [Slotsholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slotsholmen) in 1167 where [Christiansborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg_Palace) stands today.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavies1944365-20] This was in response to attacks by [Wendish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wends) pirates who plagued the coastline during the 12th century.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarding200938-21] Defensive ramparts and moats were completed, and by 1177 St. Clemens Church had been built. Attacks by the Wends continued, and after the original fortress was eventually destroyed by the marauders, islanders replaced it with [Copenhagen Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Castle).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChristopher200678-22]

### Middle Ages

In 1186, a letter from [Pope Urban III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_III) states that King [Valdemar I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdemar_I) gave the castle of *Hafn* (Copenhagen) and its surrounding lands, including the town of Hafn, to [Absalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absalon), Bishop of Roskilde (1158–1191) and Archbishop of Lund (1177–1201). Upon Absalon's death, the property was to come into the ownership of the [Bishopric of Roskilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Roskilde_\(Roman-Catholic\)).[^cite_note-khdsd-18] Around 1200, the [Church of Our Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady_\(Copenhagen\)) was constructed on higher ground to the northeast of the town, which began to develop around it.[^cite_note-khdsd-18]

As the town became more prominent, it was repeatedly attacked by the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League). In 1368, it was successfully invaded during the [Second Danish-Hanseatic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Danish-Hanseatic_War). As the fishing industry thrived in Copenhagen, particularly in the [herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herring) trade, the city began expanding to the north of Slotsholmen.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarding200938-21] In 1254, it received a city charter under Bishop [Jakob Erlandsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Erlandsen)[^cite_note-Copenhagen1898-23] who garnered support from the local fishing merchants against the king by granting them special privileges.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBooth20039-24] In the mid 1330s, the first land assessment of the city was published.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBooth20039-24]

With the establishment of the [Kalmar Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmar_Union) (1397–1523) between [Denmark](76972.md), [Norway](21241.md) and [Sweden](5058739.md), by about 1416 Copenhagen had emerged as the capital of Denmark when [Eric of Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_of_Pomerania) moved his seat to Copenhagen Castle.[^cite_note-Ingebritsen2006-25][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChristopher200678-22] The [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) was inaugurated on 1 June 1479 by King [Christian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_I), following approval from Pope [Sixtus IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixtus_IV).[^cite_note-histuni-26] This makes it the oldest university in Denmark and [one of the oldest in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_medieval_universities). Originally controlled by the [Catholic Church](606848.md), the university's role in society was forced to change during the [Reformation in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation_in_Denmark) in the late 1530s.[^cite_note-histuni-26]

### 16th and 17th centuries



The [Danish War Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_War_Museum), the former arsenal



Børsen, the former stock exchange (completed in 1640)

Before the Reformation of 1536, the city was faithful to [Christian II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_II), who was Catholic. It was successfully besieged in 1523 by the forces of [Frederik I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik_I_of_Denmark), who supported [Lutheranism](23371382.md). Later, towers were built along the city wall. The city supported Christian II's alliance with [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6) and [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck) but a siege from July 1535 to July 1536 forced it to capitulate to [Christian III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_III). During the second half of the century, the city prospered from increased trade across the [Baltic](3335.md) supported by Dutch shipping. [Christoffer Valkendorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoffer_Valkendorff), a high-ranking statesman, defended the city's interests and contributed to its development.[^cite_note-khdsd-18] The Netherlands had also become primarily Protestant, as were northern German states.

During the reign of [Christian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV) between 1588 and 1648, Copenhagen experienced dramatic growth. On his initiative, in the early 17th century, two important buildings were completed on [Slotsholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slotsholmen): the [Tøjhus Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B8jhus_Museum) and [Børsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B8rsen), the stock exchange. To foster international trade, the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_East_India_Company) was founded in 1616. East of the city, the king developed the district of [Christianshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianshavn) with canals and ramparts inspired by Dutch planning. Intended as a fortified trading centre, it ultimately became part of Copenhagen.[^cite_note-kher-27] Christian IV sponsored many other ambitious building projects including [Rosenborg Slot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenborg_Slot) and the [Rundetårn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rundet%C3%A5rn).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarding200938-21] In 1658–1659, the city withstood a siege by the [Swedes](5058739.md) under [Charles X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_X_Gustav_of_Sweden) and [a major assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_on_Copenhagen_\(1659\)).[^cite_note-kher-27]

By 1661, Copenhagen had asserted its position as capital of Denmark and Norway. All major institutions were located there, as was the fleet and most of the army. Its defences were enhanced with the completion of the [Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastellet,_Copenhagen) in 1664 and the extension of [Christianshavns Vold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianshavns_Vold) with its bastions in 1692, leading to the creation of a new base for the fleet at [Nyholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmen,_Copenhagen).[^cite_note-kher-27][^cite_note-28]

### 18th century



Frederik VIII's Palace, also known as Brockdorff Palace in [Frederiksstaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksstaden), part of the [Amalienborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalienborg_Palace)

Copenhagen lost some 22,000 of its population of 65,000 to the [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_plague_during_the_Great_Northern_War) in 1711.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWoodward199810-29] The city was also struck by two major fires that destroyed much of its infrastructure.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChristopher200678-22] The [Copenhagen Fire of 1728](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Fire_of_1728) was the largest in the history of Copenhagen. It began on the evening of 20 October, and burned until the morning of 23 October, destroying 28% of the city as a whole, 47% of the medieval part, and leaving about 20% of the population homeless. Along with the [1795 fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_fire_of_1795), it is the main reason why few traces of the old town can be found in the modern city.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Much rebuilding followed. In 1733, work began on the royal residence of [Christiansborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg_Palace_\(1st\)), completed in 1745. In 1749, development of the prestigious district of [Frederiksstaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksstaden) was started. Designed by [Nicolai Eigtved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolai_Eigtved) in the [Rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo) style, its centre contained the mansions which now form [Amalienborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalienborg) Palace.[^cite_note-32] Major extensions to the naval base of [Holmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmen_Naval_Base) were undertaken, while the city's cultural importance was enhanced with the [Royal Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Theatre) and the [Royal Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Fine_Arts).[^cite_note-Københavns_historie_1728-1914-33]

The [Danish slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_slave_trade) began in the 17th century and rapidly expanded in the 18th century. Between 1660 and 1806, Danish merchants, many of them based in Copenhagen, transported some 120,000 [enslaved Africans](49005.md) to the [Danish West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_West_Indies). These merchants were mostly affiliated with the slave-trading [Danish West India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_West_India_Company) and [Danish Asiatic Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Asiatic_Company), both of which were headquartered in Copenhagen. Many buildings in Copenhagen, such as the [Moltke Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moltke_Mansion), [Yellow Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Palace,_Copenhagen) and the [Vestindisk Pakhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestindisk_Pakhus) were funded with profits from the slave trade.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] In the second half of the 18th century, Copenhagen benefited from Denmark's neutrality in the wars between Europe's main powers, allowing it to play an important role in trade between the states around the Baltic Sea. After the fire of 1794 destroyed Christiansborg and a 1795 fire seriously damaged the city, work began on the classical Copenhagen landmark of [Højbro Plads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B8jbro_Plads) while [Nytorv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nytorv) and [Gammel Torv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gammel_Torv) were converged.[^cite_note-Københavns_historie_1728-1914-33]

### 19th century

To prevent Denmark from allying with [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_First_Republic), the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) sent Admiral [Sir Hyde Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Parker_\(admiral\)) to attack the [Royal Dano-Norwegian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dano-Norwegian_Navy). On 2 April 1801, Parker's fleet encountered the Dano-Norwegian navy anchored near Copenhagen. Vice-Admiral [Horatio Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horatio_Nelson) led the main attack.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson2005247-36] The Dano-Norwegian fleet put up heavy resistance, and the battle is often considered Nelson's hardest-fought, surpassing even the battle at [Trafalgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trafalgar).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPocock1994229-37] It was during this battle that Lord Nelson was said to have "put the telescope to the blind eye" in order not to see Admiral Parker's signal to [cease fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cease_fire). The British ultimately won the battle, sinking or capturing most of the Dano-Norwegian fleet, which led Denmark to agree not to ally with France.[^cite_note-38]



Gottlieb Bindesbøll's [Thorvaldsen Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorvaldsen_Museum)



Danish soldiers returning to Copenhagen in 1849, after the [First Schleswig War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Schleswig_War) – painting by [Otto Bache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Bache) (1894)

In 1807, due to continued fears that Denmark would ally with France, Britain sent another fleet, led Admiral [James Gambier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gambier), to Copenhagen to seize or destroy the Dano-Norwegian navy. The British published a demand to the Dano-Norwegian fleet to surrender. The Danish responded with "what amounted to a declaration of war".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmith1998204-39] Then Gambier's fleet bombarded Copenhagen from 2 to 5 September. The bombardment, which saw the deployment of [Congreve rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congreve_rocket), killed 195 civilians and wounded 768, along with burning approximately 1,000 structures, including the [Church of Our Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady,_Copenhagen). The British artillery had a [longer-range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_of_a_projectile) than Copenhagen's [old defence-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Copenhagen) range. A British landing force of 30,000 men entered and occupied Copenhagen. The British suffered almost 200 casualties, while the Danish suffered 3,000. Virtually the whole Dano-Norwegian fleet surrendered and the British either burnt them or brought them back to Britain. Denmark declared war on Britain, leading to the [Gunboat War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboat_War), which lasted until the 1814 [Treaty of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kiel).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHinde1973168-42]



[Slotsholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slotsholmen) canal, as seen from the Børsen building (c. 1900). In the background from left to right: [Church of the Holy Ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Ghost,_Copenhagen), [Trinitatis Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinitatis_Complex), [St. Nicholas Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Nicholas'_Church,_Copenhagen), and [Holmen Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmen_Church).

Despite the turmoil the Napoleonic Wars brought to the city, Copenhagen soon experienced a period of intense cultural creativity known as the [Danish Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Golden_Age). Painting prospered under [C.W. Eckersberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.W._Eckersberg) and his students while [C.F. Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Hansen) and [Gottlieb Bindesbøll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottlieb_Bindesb%C3%B8ll) brought a [Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) look to the city's architecture.[^cite_note-43] In the early 1850s, the ramparts were opened to build housing around [The Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lakes,_Copenhagen) ([Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Søerne*) west of the old defences. The ramparts were out of date as a defence system, and had limited the city's area, causing poor [sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation). By the 1880s, the districts of [Nørrebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rrebro) and [Vesterbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbro,_Copenhagen) developed to accommodate those who came from the provinces to participate in the city's industrialization. From 1886, the west rampart ([Vestvolden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestvolden)) was flattened, allowing major extensions to the harbour leading to the establishment of the [Freeport of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeport_of_Copenhagen) 1892–94.[^cite_note-44] Electricity came in 1892 with electric trams in 1897. The housing outside the old ramparts saw a huge population growth, from 120,000 in 1840 to 400,000 by 1901.[^cite_note-Københavns_historie_1728-1914-33]

### 20th century



Central Copenhagen in 1939

By the beginning of the 20th century, Copenhagen had become a thriving industrial and administrative city. With its new [city hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_City_Hall) and [railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Central_Station), its centre was drawn towards the west.[^cite_note-Københavns_historie_1728-1914-33] New housing developments grew up in [Brønshøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8nsh%C3%B8j) and [Valby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valby) while [Frederiksberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg) became an enclave within the city of Copenhagen.[^cite_note-45] The northern part of [Amager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager) and Valby were also incorporated into the City of Copenhagen in 1901–02.[^cite_note-46]

As a result of Denmark's neutrality in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), Copenhagen prospered from trade with both Britain and Germany while the city's defences were kept fully manned by some 40,000 soldiers for the duration of the war.[^cite_note-47]

In the 1920s there were serious shortages of goods and housing. Plans were drawn up to demolish the old part of [Christianshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianshavn) and to get rid of the worst of the city's slum areas.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchaldemose2005161-48] However, it was not until the 1930s that substantial housing developments ensued,[^cite_note-Københavns_historie_siden_1914-49] with the demolition of one side of Christianhavn's [Torvegade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torvegade) to build five large blocks of flats.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchaldemose2005161-48]

#### World War II

See also: [Denmark in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_in_World_War_II) and [Danish resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_resistance_movement)



The RAF's bombing of the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) headquarters in March 1945 was coordinated with the Danish resistance movement.



People celebrating the [liberation of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Denmark) at Strøget in Copenhagen, 5 May 1945. Germany surrendered three days later.

[In Denmark during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_in_World_War_II), Copenhagen was [occupied by German troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Denmark) along with the rest of the country from 9 April 1940 until 4 May 1945. German leader [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) hoped that Denmark would be "a model [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate)"[^cite_note-50] and initially the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) authorities sought an agreement with the Danish government. The [1943 Danish parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1943_Danish_Folketing_election) was also allowed to take place, with only the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Denmark) excluded. But in August 1943, after the government's collaboration with the occupation forces collapsed, several ships were sunk in Copenhagen Harbor by the [Royal Danish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Navy) to prevent their use by the Germans. Around that time the Nazis [started to arrest Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_policy_of_Nazi_Germany), although [most managed to escape to Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_of_the_Danish_Jews).[^cite_note-occupation-51]

In 1945 [Ole Lippman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Lippman), leader of the Danish section of the [Special Operations Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Operations_Executive), invited the British [Royal Air Force](25679.md) to assist their operations by attacking Nazi headquarters in Copenhagen. Accordingly, air vice-marshal [Sir Basil Embry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_Embry) drew up plans for a spectacular precision attack on the [Sicherheitsdienst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicherheitsdienst) and [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) building, the former offices of the [Shell Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_Oil_Company). Political prisoners were kept in the attic to prevent an air raid, so the RAF had to bomb the lower levels of the building.[^cite_note-milhist.dk-52]

The attack, known as "[Operation Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Carthage)", came on 22 March 1945, in three small waves. In the first wave, all six planes (carrying one bomb each) hit their target, but one of the aircraft crashed near Frederiksberg Girls School. Because of this crash, four of the planes in the two following waves assumed the school was the military target and aimed their bombs at the school, leading to the death of 123 civilians (of which 87 were schoolchildren).[^cite_note-milhist.dk-52] However, 18 of the 26 political prisoners in the Shell Building managed to escape while the Gestapo archives were completely destroyed.[^cite_note-milhist.dk-52]

On 8 May 1945 Copenhagen was officially liberated by British troops commanded by [Field Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Marshal) [Bernard Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Montgomery) who supervised the surrender of 30,000 Germans situated around the capital.[^cite_note-53]

#### Post-war decades

Soon after the end of the war, an innovative urban development project known as the [Finger Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_Plan) was introduced in 1947, encouraging the creation of new housing and businesses interspersed with large green areas along five "fingers" stretching out from the city centre along the [S-train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-train) routes.[^cite_note-OECD2010-54][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECervero1998132-55] With the expansion of the welfare state and women entering the work force, schools, nurseries, sports facilities and hospitals were established across the city. As a result of student unrest in the late 1960s, the former Bådsmandsstræde Barracks in [Christianshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianshavn) was occupied, leading to the establishment of [Freetown Christiania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freetown_Christiania) in September 1971.[^cite_note-kkkh-56]

Motor traffic in the city grew significantly and in 1972 the trams were replaced by buses. From the 1960s, on the initiative of the young architect [Jan Gehl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Gehl), pedestrian streets and cycle tracks were created in the city centre.[^cite_note-57] Activity in the port of Copenhagen declined with the closure of the Holmen Naval Base. [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport) underwent considerable expansion, becoming a hub for the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries). In the 1990s, large-scale housing developments were realised in the harbour area and in the west of [Amager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager).[^cite_note-Københavns_historie_siden_1914-49] The national library's [Black Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Diamond_\(library\)) building on the waterfront was completed in 1999.[^cite_note-58]

### 21st century



Copenhagen Opera House

Since summer 2000, Copenhagen and the Swedish city of [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6) have been connected by rail and road by the [Øresund Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund_Bridge). As a result, Copenhagen has become the centre of a larger metropolitan area spanning both nations. The bridge has brought about considerable changes in public [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_system) and has led to the extensive redevelopment of [Amager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager).[^cite_note-kkkh-56] The city's service and trade sectors developed and a number of banking and financial institutions were established. Educational institutions also gained importance, especially the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) with its 35,000 students.[^cite_note-59]

An important development for the city was the opening of the [Copenhagen Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Metro) railway system in 2002. This was extended until 2007, transporting some 54 million passengers by 2011.[^cite_note-60] The [Copenhagen Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Opera_House), a gift to the city from the shipping magnate [Mærsk Mc-Kinney Møller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A6rsk_Mc-Kinney_M%C3%B8ller) on behalf of the A.P. Møller foundation, was completed in 2004.[^cite_note-61]



The Harbour Bath at [Islands Brygge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_Brygge)&#x20;

In December 2009 Copenhagen hosted the worldwide climate meeting [COP15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COP15).[^cite_note-62] In 2019 Copenhagen was crowned the *best swimming city* by [CNN](62028.md) and Copenhagen harbour now has 13 official swimming zones and three [harbour baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Harbour_Baths). Illegal bathers are fined by the police.[^cite_note-63]

On 3 July 2022, three people were killed in [a shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Copenhagen_mall_shooting) at [Field's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field's) mall in Copenhagen. Police chief inspector Søren Thomassen announced the arrest of a 22-year-old man and said that the police cannot rule out an act of terrorism.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

## Geography



Satellite image of Copenhagen



The red line shows the approximate extent of the urban area of Copenhagen.



Copenhagen metropolitan area

Copenhagen is part of the [Øresund Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund_Region), which consists of [Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealand), [Lolland-Falster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolland-Falster) and [Bornholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornholm) in Denmark and [Scania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scania) in Sweden.[^cite_note-66] It is located on the eastern shore of the island of Zealand, partly on the island of [Amager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager) and on a number of natural and artificial islets between the two. Copenhagen faces the [Øresund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund) to the east, the strait of water that separates Denmark from Sweden, and which connects the [North Sea](21179.md) with the [Baltic Sea](3335.md). The [Swedish](5058739.md) city of [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6) and the town of [Landskrona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landskrona) lie on the Swedish side of the sound directly across from Copenhagen.[^cite_note-67] By road, Copenhagen is 42 kilometres (26 mi) northwest of [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6), Sweden, 85 kilometres (53 mi) northeast of [Næstved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A6stved), 164 kilometres (102 mi) northeast of [Odense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odense), 295 kilometres (183 mi) east of [Esbjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esbjerg) and 188 kilometres (117 mi) southeast of [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus) by sea and road via [Sjællands Odde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sj%C3%A6llands_Odde).[^cite_note-68]

The city centre lies in the area originally defined by the old [ramparts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampart_\(fortification\)), which are still referred to as the [Fortification Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortification_Ring,_Copenhagen) (*Fæstningsringen*) and kept as a partial green band around it.[^cite_note-69] Then come the late-19th- and early-20th-century residential neighbourhoods of [Østerbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98sterbro), [Nørrebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rrebro), [Vesterbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbro,_Copenhagen) and [Amagerbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagerbro). The outlying areas of [Kongens Enghave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongens_Enghave), [Valby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valby), [Vigerslev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigerslev), [Vanløse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanl%C3%B8se), [Brønshøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8nsh%C3%B8j), [Utterslev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utterslev) and [Sundby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundby,_Copenhagen) followed from 1920 to 1960. They consist mainly of residential housing and apartments often enhanced with parks and greenery.[^cite_note-kgdsd-70]

### Topography

The central area of the city consists of relatively low-lying flat ground formed by [moraines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraine) from the last ice age while the hilly areas to the north and west frequently rise to 50 m (160 ft) above sea level. The slopes of [Valby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valby) and [Brønshøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8nsh%C3%B8j) reach heights of over 30 m (98 ft), divided by valleys running from the northeast to the southwest. Close to the centre are the [Copenhagen lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lakes,_Copenhagen) of Sortedams Sø, Peblinge Sø and Sankt Jørgens Sø.[^cite_note-kgdsd-70]

Copenhagen rests on a [subsoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsoil) of flint-layered limestone deposited in the [Danian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danian) period some 60 to 66 million years ago. Some [greensand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensand) from the [Selandian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selandian) is also present. There are a few faults in the area, the most important of which is the Carlsberg fault which runs northwest to southeast through the centre of the city.[^cite_note-71] During the last ice age, glaciers eroded the surface leaving a layer of moraines up to 15 m (49 ft) thick.[^cite_note-72]

[Geologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologically), Copenhagen lies in the northern part of Denmark where the land is rising because of [post-glacial rebound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-glacial_rebound).

### Beaches



Amager Strandpark



Kalvebod Bølge – public beach within the city

[Amager Strandpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager_Strandpark), which opened in 2005, is a 2 km (1 mi) long [artificial island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_island), with a total of 4.6 km (2.9 mi) of beaches. It is located just 15 minutes by bicycle or a few minutes by metro from the city centre.[^cite_note-73] In [Klampenborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klampenborg), about 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) from downtown Copenhagen, is [Bellevue Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue_Beach). It is 700 metres (2,300 ft) long and has both lifeguards and freshwater showers on the beach.[^cite_note-74]

The beaches are supplemented by a system of [Harbour Baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Harbour_Baths) along the Copenhagen waterfront. The first and most popular of these is located at [Islands Brygge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_Brygge), literally meaning Iceland's Quay, and has won international acclaim for its design.[^cite_note-75]

## Climate



[Frederiksberg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg_Palace) in winter

Copenhagen features a [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperateness) [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dobk*).[^cite_note-76] The city is located in a climate zone characterized by the influence of the warm Gulf Stream. This means that Copenhagen is approximately 5 °C warmer than the city's latitude would otherwise dictate. Its weather is subject to [low-pressure systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_pressure_area) from the [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic) which result in unstable conditions with alternating periods of rain and sun throughout the year. Apart from slightly higher rainfall from July to September, precipitation is moderate. While snowfall occurs mainly from late December to early March, there can also be rain, with average temperatures above the freezing point.[^cite_note-77]

July is the sunniest month of the year with an average of over seven hours of sunshine a day. July is the warmest month with an average daytime high of 21 °C. By contrast, the average hours of sunshine are less than two per day in November and only one and a half per day from December to February. In the spring, it gets warmer again with four to six hours of sunshine per day from March to May. February is the driest month of the year.[^cite_note-78] Exceptional weather conditions can bring as much as 50 cm of snow to Copenhagen in a 24-hour period during the winter months[^cite_note-79] while summer temperatures have been known to rise to heights of 33 °C (91 °F).[^cite_note-80]

Because of Copenhagen's northern latitude, the number of daylight hours varies considerably between summer and winter. On the summer solstice, the sun rises at 04\:26 and sets at 21\:58, providing 17 hours 32 minutes of daylight. On the winter solstice, it rises at 08\:37 and sets at 15\:39 with 7 hours and 1 minute of daylight. There is therefore a difference of 10 hours and 31 minutes in the length of days and nights between the summer and winter solstices.[^cite_note-81]

## Administration



Copenhagen City Hall (right) on City Hall Square in the city centre

According to [Statistics Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Denmark), the [urban area of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area_of_Copenhagen) (*Hovedstadsområdet*) consists of the [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Denmark) of [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Municipality), [Frederiksberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg_Municipality), [Albertslund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertslund_Municipality), [Brøndby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8ndby_Municipality), [Gentofte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentofte_Municipality), [Gladsaxe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladsaxe_Municipality), [Glostrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glostrup_Municipality), [Herlev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herlev_Municipality), [Hvidovre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hvidovre_Municipality), [Lyngby-Taarbæk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyngby-Taarb%C3%A6k_Municipality), [Rødovre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B8dovre_Municipality), [Tårnby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A5rnby_Municipality) and [Vallensbæk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallensb%C3%A6k_Municipality) as well as parts of [Ballerup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballerup_Municipality), [Rudersdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudersdal_Municipality) and [Furesø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fures%C3%B8_Municipality) municipalities, along with the cities of [Ishøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ish%C3%B8j) and [Greve Strand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greve_Strand).[^cite_note-pop1-4][^cite_note-urban-90] They are located in the [Capital Region](2521189.md) (*Region Hovedstaden*). Municipalities are responsible for a wide variety of [public services](1291782.md), which include land-use planning, environmental planning, public housing, management and maintenance of local roads, and social security. Municipal administration is also conducted by a [mayor](3319811.md), a [council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_City_Council), and an executive.[^cite_note-91]

Copenhagen Municipality is by far the largest municipality, with the historic city at its core. The seat of Copenhagen's municipal council is the [Copenhagen City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_City_Hall) (*_Rådhus_*), which is situated on [City Hall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Hall_Square,_Copenhagen). The second largest municipality is Frederiksberg, an enclave within Copenhagen Municipality.

Copenhagen Municipality is divided into [ten districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Copenhagen) (*bydele*):[^cite_note-Københavns_bydele-92] [Indre By](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indre_By), [Østerbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98sterbro), [Nørrebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rrebro), [Vesterbro/Kongens Enghave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbro/Kongens_Enghave), [Valby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valby), [Vanløse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanl%C3%B8se), [Brønshøj-Husum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8nsh%C3%B8j-Husum), [Bispebjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bispebjerg), [Amager Øst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager_%C3%98st), and [Amager Vest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager_Vest). Neighbourhoods of Copenhagen include [Slotsholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slotsholmen), [Frederiksstaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksstaden), [Islands Brygge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_Brygge), [Holmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmen,_Copenhagen), [Christiania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freetown_Christiania), [Carlsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_\(district\)), [Sluseholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sluseholmen), [Sydhavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongens_Enghave), [Amagerbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagerbro), [Ørestad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98restad), [Nordhavnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordhavnen), [Bellahøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellah%C3%B8j), [Brønshøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8nsh%C3%B8j), [Ryparken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryparken), and [Vigerslev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigerslev).

### Law and order

Most of Denmark's top legal courts and institutions are based in Copenhagen. A modern-style court of justice, *[Hof- og Stadsretten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hof-_og_Stadsretten)*, was introduced in Denmark, specifically for Copenhagen, by [Johann Friedrich Struensee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Friedrich_Struensee) in 1771.[^cite_note-93] Now known as the [City Court of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Court_House) (*Københavns Byret*), it is the largest of the 24 city courts in Denmark with jurisdiction over the municipalities of Copenhagen, [Dragør](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag%C3%B8r) and [Tårnby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A5rnby). With its 42 judges, it has a Probate Division, an Enforcement Division and a Registration and Notorial Acts Division while bankruptcy is handled by the [Maritime and Commercial Court of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_and_Commercial_Court_of_Copenhagen).[^cite_note-94] Established in 1862, the Maritime and Commercial Court (*Sø- og Handelsretten*) also hears commercial cases including those relating to trade marks, marketing practices and competition for the whole of Denmark.[^cite_note-95] Denmark's [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Denmark) (*Højesteret*), located in [Christiansborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg_Palace) on Prins Jørgens Gård in the centre of Copenhagen, is the country's final court of appeal. Handling civil and criminal cases from the subordinate courts, it has two chambers which each hear all types of cases.[^cite_note-96]

The [Danish National Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_National_Police) and Copenhagen Police headquarters is situated in the Neoclassical-inspired [Politigården](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politig%C3%A5rden) building built in 1918–1924 under architects [Hack Kampmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hack_Kampmann) and [Holger Alfred Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holger_Jacobsen). The building also contains administration, management, emergency department and radio service offices.[^cite_note-97]

The [Copenhagen Fire Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Fire_Department) forms the largest municipal fire brigade in Denmark with some 500 fire and ambulance personnel, 150 administration and service workers, and 35 workers in prevention.[^cite_note-98] The brigade began as the Copenhagen Royal Fire Brigade on 9 July 1687 under King Christian V. After the passing of the Copenhagen Fire Act on 18 May 1868, on 1 August 1870 the Copenhagen Fire Brigade became a municipal institution in its own right.[^cite_note-99] The fire department has its headquarters in the [Copenhagen Central Fire Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Central_Fire_Station) which was designed by [Ludvig Fenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludvig_Fenger) in the [Historicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historicism_\(art\)) style and inaugurated in 1892.[^cite_note-100]

### Environmental planning

Main article: [Energy in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Denmark)



The [European Environment Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Environment_Agency) on [Kongens Nytorv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongens_Nytorv)

Copenhagen is recognised as one of the most environmentally friendly cities in the world.[^cite_note-101] As a result of its commitment to high environmental standards, Copenhagen has been praised for its [green economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_economy), ranked as the top green city for the second time in the 2014 *Global Green Economy Index (GGEI)*.[^cite_note-ggei-102][^cite_note-103] In 2001 a large offshore [wind farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_farm) was built just off the coast of Copenhagen at [Middelgrunden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middelgrunden_wind_farm). It produces about 4% of the city's energy.[^cite_note-104] Years of substantial investment in [sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment) have improved [water quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_quality) in the harbour to an extent that the [Inner Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Harbour,_Copenhagen) can be used for swimming with facilities at a number of locations.[^cite_note-105]



Middelgrunden offshore wind farm

Copenhagen aims to be [carbon-neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon-neutral) by 2025. Commercial and residential buildings are to reduce electricity consumption by 20 per cent and 10 per cent respectively, and total heat consumption is to fall by 20 per cent by 2025. Renewable energy features such as solar panels are becoming increasingly common in the newest buildings in Copenhagen. [District heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_heating) will be carbon-neutral by 2025, by waste [incineration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incineration) and biomass. New buildings must now be constructed according to Low Energy Class ratings and in 2020 near net-zero energy buildings. By 2025, 75% of trips should be made on foot, by bike, or by using public transit. The city plans that 20–30% of cars will run on electricity or [biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel) by 2025. The investment is estimated at \$472 million public funds and \$4.78 billion private funds.[^cite_note-106]

The city's urban planning authorities continue to take full account of these priorities. Special attention is given both to climate issues and efforts to ensure maximum application of [low-energy standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-energy_house). Priorities include [sustainable drainage systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_drainage_system),[^cite_note-107] [recycling rainwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_conservation), [green roofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_roof) and efficient [waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management) solutions. In city planning, streets and squares are to be designed to encourage cycling and walking rather than driving.[^cite_note-108]

### Religion

See also: [List of churches in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_churches_in_Copenhagen) and [Religion in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Denmark)



The [Church of Our Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady_\(Copenhagen\)), situated on [Frue Plads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frue_Plads)

A majority (56.9%) of those living in Copenhagen are members of the Lutheran [Church of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Denmark) which is 0.6% lower than one year earlier according to 2019 figures.[^cite_note-116] The National Cathedral, the [Church of Our Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady_\(Copenhagen\)), is one of the dozens of churches in Copenhagen. There are also several other Christian communities in the city, of which the largest is [Roman Catholic](606848.md).[^cite_note-117]

Foreign migration to Copenhagen, rising over the last three decades, has contributed to increasing religious diversity; the [Grand Mosque of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mosque_of_Copenhagen), the first in Denmark, opened in 2014.[^cite_note-118] [Islam](6037917.md) is the second largest religion in Copenhagen, accounting for approximately 10% of the population.[^cite_note-Nydell-119][^cite_note-Farmer_2010-120][^cite_note-Micklethwait-121] While there are no official statistics, a significant portion of the estimated 175,000–200,000 Muslims in the country live in the Copenhagen urban area, with the highest concentration in [Nørrebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rrebro) and the Vestegnen.[^cite_note-osf-122] There are also some 7,000 Jews in Denmark, most of them in the Copenhagen area where there are several synagogues.[^cite_note-123] It has a membership of 1,800 members.[^cite_note-NAME-124] There is a long history of Jews in the city, and the first synagogue in Copenhagen was built in 1684.[^cite_note-bh.org.il-125] Today, the history of the Jews of Denmark can be explored at the [Danish Jewish Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Jewish_Museum) in Copenhagen.

### Quality of living

For a number of years, Copenhagen has ranked high in international surveys for its [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life). Its stable economy together with its education services and level of social safety make it attractive for locals and visitors alike. Although it is one of the world's most expensive cities, it is also one of the most liveable with its public transport, facilities for cyclists and its environmental policies.[^cite_note-126] In elevating Copenhagen to "most liveable city" in 2013, *[Monocle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocle_\(media_company\))* pointed to its open spaces, increasing activity on the streets, city planning in favour of cyclists and pedestrians, and features to encourage inhabitants to enjoy city life with an emphasis on community, culture and cuisine.[^cite_note-127] The city was voted 2024 second most liveable city by [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md).[^cite_note-128] Other sources have ranked Copenhagen high for its business environment, accessibility, restaurants and environmental planning.[^cite_note-129] However, Copenhagen ranks only 39th for student friendliness in 2012. Despite a top score for quality of living, its scores were low for employer activity and affordability.[^cite_note-130]

## Economy

Copenhagen is the major economic and [financial centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_centre) of Denmark. The city's economy is based largely on services and commerce. Statistics for 2010 show that the vast majority of the 350,000 workers in Copenhagen are employed in the [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector), especially transport and communications, trade, and finance, while less than 10,000 work in the manufacturing industries. The [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector) workforce is around 110,000, including education and healthcare.[^cite_note-131] From 2006 to 2011, the economy grew by 2.5% in Copenhagen, while it fell by some 4% in the rest of Denmark.[^cite_note-132] In 2017, the wider [Capital Region of Denmark](2521189.md) had a gross domestic product (GDP) of €120 billion, and the 15th largest [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) of regions in the European Union.[^cite_note-133] As of Copenhagen Green Economy Leader Report made by [London School of Economics and Political Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics_and_Political_Science) – Copenhagen is widely recognised as a leader in the global green economy. The Copenhagen region accounts for almost 40% of Denmark's output and has enjoyed long-term stable growth. At a national level, Danish GDP per capita is ranked among the top 10 countries in the world. At the same time, the city's growth has been delivered while improving environmental performance and transitioning to a low-carbon economy.



[The Crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crystal,_Copenhagen), headquarters of Nykredit bank

Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alm._Brand), [Danske Bank](738035.md), [Nykredit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nykredit) and [Nordea Bank Danmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordea_Bank_Danmark). The [Copenhagen Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Stock_Exchange) (CSE) was founded in 1620 and is now owned by [Nasdaq, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasdaq,_Inc.) Copenhagen is also home to a number of international companies including [A.P. Møller-Mærsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.P._M%C3%B8ller-M%C3%A6rsk), [Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novo_\(company\)), [Carlsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Group) and [Novozymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novozymes).[^cite_note-134] City authorities have encouraged the development of [business clusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cluster) in several innovative sectors, which include information technology, [biotechnology](4502.md), [pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceuticals), [clean technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_technology) and [smart city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_city) solutions.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]



Former Scandinavian headquarters for the [Swiss](26748.md) pharmaceutical company [Ferring Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferring_Pharmaceuticals) which has now relocated to the neighbouring Tårnby municipality.

[Life science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology) is a key sector with extensive [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) activities. [Medicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicon_Valley) is a leading bi-national life sciences cluster in Europe, spanning the Øresund Region. Copenhagen is rich in companies and institutions with a focus on [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) within the field of biotechnology,[^cite_note-137] and the Medicon Valley initiative aims to strengthen this position and to promote cooperation between companies and academia. Many major Danish companies like Novo and [Lundbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lundbeck), both of which are among the [50 largest pharmaceutical and biotech companies in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_pharmaceutical_companies), are located in this business cluster.[^cite_note-usatoday-138]

Shipping is another important sector with Maersk, the world's largest [shipping company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ship_companies), having their world headquarters in Copenhagen. The city has an industrial harbour, [Copenhagen Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Port). Following decades of stagnation, it has experienced a resurgence since 1990 following a merger with [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6) harbour. Both ports are operated by [Copenhagen Malmö Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Malm%C3%B6_Port) (CMP). The central location in the Øresund Region allows the ports to act as a hub for freight that is transported onward to the [Baltic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_countries). CMP annually receives about 8,000 ships and handled some 148,000 [TEU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-foot_equivalent_unit) in 2012.[^cite_note-139]

Copenhagen has some of the highest gross wages in the world.[^cite_note-140] High taxes mean that wages are reduced after mandatory deduction. A *beneficial researcher scheme* with low taxation of foreign specialists has made Denmark an attractive location for [foreign labour](4599312.md). It is, however, also among the most expensive cities in Europe.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

Denmark's [Flexicurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexicurity) model features some of the most flexible hiring and firing legislation in Europe, providing attractive conditions for [foreign investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) and international companies looking to locate in Copenhagen.[^cite_note-143] In [Dansk Industri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dansk_Industri)'s 2013 survey of employment factors in the ninety-six municipalities of Denmark, Copenhagen came in first place for educational qualifications and for the development of private companies in recent years, but fell to 86th place in local companies' assessment of the employment climate. The survey revealed considerable dissatisfaction in the level of dialogue companies enjoyed with the municipal authorities.[^cite_note-144]

### Tourism

See also: [Tourism in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Denmark)

Tourism is a major contributor to Copenhagen's economy, attracting visitors due to the city's harbour, cultural attractions and award-winning restaurants. Since 2009, Copenhagen has been one of the fastest growing metropolitan destinations in Europe.[^cite_note-tourismstats-145] Hotel capacity in the city is growing significantly. From 2009 to 2013, it experienced a 42% growth in international bed nights (total number of nights spent by tourists), tallying a rise of nearly 70% for Chinese visitors.[^cite_note-tourismstats-145] The total number of bed nights in the Capital Region surpassed 9 million in 2013, while international bed nights reached 5 million.[^cite_note-tourismstats-145]

In 2010, it is estimated that [city break](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/city%20break) tourism contributed to DKK 2 billion in turnover. However, 2010 was an exceptional year for city break tourism and turnover increased with 29% in that one year.[^cite_note-146] 680,000 cruise passengers visited the port in 2015.[^cite_note-147] In 2019 Copenhagen was ranked first among Lonely Planet's top ten cities to visit.[^cite_note-148] In October 2021, Copenhagen was shortlisted for the [European Commission](9974.md)'s 2022 European Capital of Smart Tourism award along with [Bordeaux](4097.md), [Dublin](8504.md), [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), [Ljubljana](42374.md), [Palma de Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca) and [Valencia](63861.md).[^cite_note-149]

## Cityscape



The city skyline features many towers and spires.

The city's appearance today is shaped by the key role it has played as a regional centre for centuries. Copenhagen has a multitude of districts, each with its distinctive character and representing its own period. Other distinctive features of Copenhagen include the abundance of water, its many parks, and the [bicycle paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_path) that line most streets.[^cite_note-150]

### Architecture

See also: [Architecture in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_in_Copenhagen), [Listed buildings in Copenhagen Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_buildings_in_Copenhagen_Municipality), [List of buildings in and around Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_buildings_in_and_around_Copenhagen), and [List of tallest buildings in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Copenhagen)



[Nyhavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyhavn) is a 17th-century waterfront lined by brightly coloured townhouses.



The central square, [Amagertorv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagertorv), dates back to the Middle Ages.



[Amager Bakke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager_Bakke), a power plant and recreational facility was named the *World Building of the Year 2021*.

The oldest section of Copenhagen's [inner city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_city) is often referred to as *Middelalderbyen* (the [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) city).[^cite_note-151] However, the city's most distinctive district is [Frederiksstaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksstaden), developed during the reign of [Frederick V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_V_of_Denmark). It has the [Amalienborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalienborg) Palace at its centre and is dominated by the dome of [Frederik's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik's_Church) (or the Marble Church) and several elegant 18th-century [Rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo) mansions.[^cite_note-152] The inner city includes [Slotsholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slotsholmen), a little island on which [Christiansborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg_Palace) stands and [Christianshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianshavn) with its canals.[^cite_note-153] [Børsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B8rsen) on Slotsholmen and [Frederiksborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksborg_Palace) in Hillerød are prominent examples of the [Dutch Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Renaissance) style in Copenhagen. Around the historical city centre lies a band of congenial residential boroughs ([Vesterbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbro,_Copenhagen), [Inner Nørrebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rrebro), [Inner Østerbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indre_%C3%98sterbro)) dating mainly from late 19th century. They were built outside the old ramparts when the city was finally allowed to expand beyond its fortifications.[^cite_note-154]

Sometimes referred to as "the City of Spires", Copenhagen is known for its generally horizontal skyline, broken mainly by the spires and towers of its churches and castles. Most characteristic of all is the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) spire of the [Church of Our Saviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Saviour,_Copenhagen) with its narrowing external spiral stairway that visitors can climb to the top.[^cite_note-155] Other important spires are those of [Christiansborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg_Palace), the [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_City_Hall) and the former Church of St. Nikolaj that now houses a [modern art](163543.md) venue. Not quite so high are the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) spires of [Rosenborg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenborg_Castle) and the "dragon spire" of [Christian IV's former stock exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B8rsen), so named because it resembles the intertwined tails of four dragons.[^cite_note-156]

Copenhagen is recognised globally for its [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning).[^cite_note-157] Mixed land use in the city centre enables high footfall in public spaces. Such is the result of thorough replanning in the second half of the 20th century. This approach has been associated with the allocation of public space to pedestrians, cyclists and public transport alongside motor vehicles and with efforts to limit the spatial fragmentation associated with car-oriented development. The emphasis on accessible public spaces, proximity and neighbourhood life has also been discussed in relation to slower forms of urban living and opportunities for social interaction.[^cite_note-158]

Recent years have seen a boom in modern architecture in Copenhagen[^cite_note-baumeister1-159] both for [Danish architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_architecture) and for works by international architects. For a few hundred years, virtually no foreign architects had worked in Copenhagen, but since the turn of the millennium the city and its immediate surroundings have seen buildings and projects designed by top international architects. British design magazine *[Monocle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocle_\(2007_magazine\))* named Copenhagen the *World's best design city 2008*.[^cite_note-160]

Copenhagen's urban development in the first half of the 20th century was heavily influenced by industrialisation. After World War II, Copenhagen Municipality adopted [Fordism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordism) and repurposed its medieval centre to facilitate private automobile infrastructure in response to innovations in transport, trade and communication.[^cite_note-\:0-161] Copenhagen's spatial planning in this time frame was characterised by the separation of land uses: an approach which requires residents to travel by car to access facilities of different uses.[^cite_note-162]

The boom in urban development and [modern architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture) has brought some changes to the city's skyline. A political majority has decided to keep the historical centre free of high-rise buildings, but several areas will see or have already seen massive urban development. [Ørestad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98restad) now has seen most of the recent development. Located near [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport), it currently boasts one of the largest malls in Scandinavia and a variety of office and residential buildings as well as the [IT University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IT_University_of_Copenhagen) and a high school.[^cite_note-163]

### Parks, gardens and zoo

Main article: [Parks and open spaces in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_open_spaces_in_Copenhagen)



Rosenborg Castle and park in central Copenhagen

Copenhagen is a green city with many parks, both large and small. [King's Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenborg_Castle_Gardens) (*_Kongens Have_*), the garden of [Rosenborg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenborg_Castle), is the oldest and most frequented of them all.[^cite_note-www1-164] It was [Christian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV) who first developed its landscaping in 1606. Every year it sees more than 2.5 million visitors[^cite_note-165] and in the summer months it is packed with sunbathers, picnickers and ballplayers. It serves as a [sculpture garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture_garden) with both a permanent display and temporary exhibits during the summer months.[^cite_note-www1-164] Also located in the city centre are the [Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen_Botanical_Garden) noted for their large complex of 19th-century greenhouses donated by [Carlsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Group) founder [J. C. Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._C._Jacobsen).[^cite_note-166] [Fælledparken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A6lledparken) at 58 ha (140 acres) is the largest park in Copenhagen.[^cite_note-167]

It is popular for sports fixtures and hosts several annual events including a free opera concert at the opening of the opera season, other open-air concerts, [carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival) and Labour Day celebrations, and the [Copenhagen Historic Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Historic_Grand_Prix), a race for [antique cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antique_car). A historical [green space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt) in the northeastern part of the city is [Kastellet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastellet,_Copenhagen), a well-preserved Renaissance [citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel) that now serves mainly as a park.[^cite_note-168] Another popular park is the [Frederiksberg Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg_Gardens), a 32-hectare [romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism) [landscape park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_garden). It houses a colony of tame [grey herons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_heron) and other [waterfowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfowl).[^cite_note-169] The park offers views of the elephant house of the adjacent [Copenhagen Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Zoo), designed by world-famous British architect [Norman Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Foster).[^cite_note-170] [Langelinie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langelinie), a park and promenade along the inner [Øresund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund) coast, is home to one of Copenhagen's most-visited tourist attractions, the [Little Mermaid statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Mermaid_statue).[^cite_note-171]

In Copenhagen, many [cemeteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cemeteries) double as parks, though only for the more quiet activities such as sunbathing, reading and meditation. [Assistens Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistens_Cemetery_\(Copenhagen\)), the burial place of [Hans Christian Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen), is an important green space for the district of [Inner Nørrebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rrebro) and a Copenhagen institution. The lesser known [Vestre Kirkegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestre_Cemetery) is the largest cemetery in Denmark (54 ha (130 acres)) and offers a maze of dense groves, open lawns, winding paths, hedges, overgrown tombs, monuments, tree-lined avenues, lakes and other [garden features](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_feature).[^cite_note-172]

It is official municipal policy in Copenhagen that by 2015 all citizens must be able to reach a park or beach on foot in less than 15 minutes.[^cite_note-173] In line with this policy, several new parks, including the innovative [Superkilen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superkilen) in the Nørrebro district, have been completed or are under development in areas lacking green spaces.[^cite_note-174]

### Landmarks by district

#### Indre By

The historic centre of the city, [Indre By](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indre_By) or the Inner City, features many of Copenhagen's most popular monuments and attractions. The area known as [Frederiksstaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksstaden), developed by [Frederik V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik_V_of_Denmark) in the second half of the 18th century in the [Rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo) style, has the four mansions of [Amalienborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalienborg), the royal residence, and the wide-domed [Marble Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik's_Church) at its centre.[^cite_note-175] Directly across the water from Amalienborg, the 21st-century [Copenhagen Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Opera_House) stands on the island of [Holmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmen,_Copenhagen).[^cite_note-176] To the south of Frederiksstaden, the [Nyhavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyhavn) canal is lined with colourful houses from the 17th and 18th centuries, many now with lively restaurants and bars.[^cite_note-177] The canal runs from the harbour front to the spacious square of [Kongens Nytorv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongens_Nytorv) which was laid out by [Christian V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_V) in 1670. Important buildings include [Charlottenborg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottenborg_Palace), famous for its art exhibitions, the [Thott Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_France,_Copenhagen) (now the French embassy), the [Royal Danish Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Theatre) and the [Hotel D'Angleterre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_D'Angleterre), dated to 1755.[^cite_note-178] Other landmarks in Indre By include the parliament building of [Christiansborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christiansborg), the [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_City_Hall) and [Rundetårn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rundet%C3%A5rn), originally an observatory. There are also several museums in the area including [Thorvaldsen Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorvaldsen_Museum) dedicated to the 18th-century sculptor [Bertel Thorvaldsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertel_Thorvaldsen).[^cite_note-179] Closed to traffic since 1964, [Strøget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Str%C3%B8get), one of the world's oldest and longest pedestrian streets, runs the 3.2 km (2.0 mi) from Rådhuspladsen to Kongens Nytorv. With its speciality shops, cafés, restaurants, and [buskers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buskers), it is always full of life and includes the old squares of [Gammel Torv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gammel_Torv) and [Amagertorv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagertorv), each with a fountain.[^cite_note-180] [Rosenborg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenborg_Castle) on [Øster Voldgade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98ster_Voldgade) was built by Christian IV in 1606 as a summer residence in the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) style. It houses the Danish crown jewels and [crown regalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Crown_Regalia), the [coronation throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throne_Chair_of_Denmark) and tapestries illustrating Christian V's victories in the [Scanian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scanian_War).[^cite_note-181]

#### Christianshavn



Christianshavn Canal

[Christianshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianshavn) lies to the southeast of [Indre By](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indre_By) on the other side of the harbour. The area was developed by [Christian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV) in the early 17th century. Impressed by the city of [Amsterdam](844.md), he employed Dutch architects to create canals within its [ramparts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianshavns_Vold) which are still well preserved today.[^cite_note-kher-27] The canals themselves, branching off the central [Christianshavn Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianshavn_Canal) and lined with house boats and pleasure craft are one of the area's attractions.[^cite_note-182] Another interesting feature is [Freetown Christiania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freetown_Christiania), a fairly large area which was initially occupied by squatters during student unrest in 1971. Today it still maintains a measure of autonomy. The inhabitants openly sell drugs on "Pusher Street" as well as their arts and crafts. Other buildings of interest in Christianshavn include the [Church of Our Saviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Saviour,_Copenhagen) with its spiralling steeple and the magnificent Rococo [Christian's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian's_Church,_Copenhagen). Once a warehouse, the [North Atlantic House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_House) now displays culture from Iceland and Greenland and houses the [Noma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noma_\(restaurant\)) restaurant, known for its Nordic cuisine.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

#### Vesterbro



Halmtorvet in Vesterbro

[Vesterbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbro,_Copenhagen), to the southwest of Indre By, begins with the [Tivoli Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivoli_Gardens), the city's top tourist attraction with its fairground atmosphere, its [Pantomime Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantomime_Theatre), its [Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivolis_Koncertsal) and its many rides and restaurants.[^cite_note-185] The [Carlsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_\(district\)) neighbourhood has some interesting vestiges of the old brewery of the same name including the [Elephant Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_Gate_and_Tower,_Carlsberg) and the [Ny Carlsberg Brewhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ny_Carlsberg_Brewhouse).[^cite_note-186] The [Tycho Brahe Planetarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe_Planetarium) is located on the edge of [Skt. Jørgens Sø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lakes,_Copenhagen), one of the Copenhagen lakes.[^cite_note-187] [Halmtorvet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmtorvet), the old hay market behind the [Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Central_Station), is an increasingly popular area with its cafés and restaurants. The former cattle market Øksnehallen has been converted into a modern exhibition centre for art and photography.[^cite_note-188] [Radisson Blu Royal Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radisson_Blu_Royal_Hotel), built by Danish architect and designer [Arne Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arne_Jacobsen) for the airline [Scandinavian Airlines System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Airlines_System) (SAS) between 1956 and 1960 was once the tallest hotel in Denmark with a height of 69.60 m (228.3 ft) and the city's only skyscraper until 1969.[^cite_note-189] Completed in 1908, [Det Ny Teater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Det_Ny_Teater) (the New Theatre) located in a passage between [Vesterbrogade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbrogade) and [Gammel Kongevej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gammel_Kongevej) has become a popular venue for musicals since its reopening in 1994, attracting the largest audiences in the country.[^cite_note-190]

#### Nørrebro



[Dronning Louises Bro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dronning_Louises_Bro) leading into [Nørrebrogade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rrebrogade)

[Nørrebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rrebro) to the northwest of the city centre has recently developed from a working-class district into a colourful cosmopolitan area with antique shops, non-Danish food stores and restaurants. Much of the activity is centred on [Sankt Hans Torv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankt_Hans_Torv)[^cite_note-191] and around [Rantzausgade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rantzausgade). Copenhagen's historic cemetery, [Assistens Kirkegård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistens_Cemetery_\(Copenhagen\)) halfway up Nørrebrogade, is the resting place of many famous figures including [Søren Kierkegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8ren_Kierkegaard), [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr), and [Hans Christian Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen) but is also used by locals as a park and recreation area.[^cite_note-192]

#### Østerbro



The Gefion Fountain

Just north of the city centre, [Østerbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98sterbro) is an upper middle-class district with a number of fine mansions, some now serving as embassies.[^cite_note-193] The district stretches from Nørrebro to the waterfront where *[The Little Mermaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Mermaid_\(statue\))* statue can be seen from the promenade known as [Langelinie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langelinie). Inspired by [Hans Christian Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen)'s fairy tale, it was created by [Edvard Eriksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Eriksen) and unveiled in 1913.[^cite_note-194] Not far from the Little Mermaid, the old [Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastellet,_Copenhagen) (*Kastellet*) can be seen. Built by [Christian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV), it is one of northern Europe's best preserved fortifications. There is also a windmill in the area.[^cite_note-195] The large [Gefion Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gefion_Fountain) (*Gefionspringvandet*) designed by [Anders Bundgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Bundgaard) and completed in 1908 stands close to the southeast corner of Kastellet. Its figures illustrate a Nordic legend.[^cite_note-196]

#### Frederiksberg



Frederiksberg Palace

[Frederiksberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg), a separate municipality within the [urban area of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area_of_Copenhagen), lies to the west of Nørrebro and Indre By and north of Vesterbro. Its landmarks include [Copenhagen Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Zoo) founded in 1869 with over 250 species from all over the world and [Frederiksberg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg_Palace) built as a summer residence by [Frederick IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_IV_of_Denmark) who was inspired by Italian architecture. Now a military academy, it overlooks the extensive landscaped [Frederiksberg Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg_Gardens) with its [follies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follies), waterfalls, lakes and decorative buildings.[^cite_note-197] The wide tree-lined avenue of [Frederiksberg Allé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg_All%C3%A9) connecting [Vesterbrogade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbrogade) with the Frederiksberg Gardens has long been associated with theatres and entertainment. While a number of the earlier theatres are now closed, the [Betty Nansen Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Nansen_Teatret) and Aveny-T are still active.[^cite_note-198]

#### Amagerbro

Amagerbro (also known as Sønderbro) is the district located immediately south-east of [Christianshavn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianshavn) at northernmost [Amager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager). The old city moats and their surrounding parks constitute a clear border between these districts. The main street is [Amagerbrogade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagerbrogade) which after the harbour bridge [Langebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langebro), is an extension of [H. C. Andersens Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._C._Andersens_Boulevard) and has a number of various stores and shops as well as restaurants and pubs.[^cite_note-199] Amagerbro was built up during the two first decades of the twentieth century and is the city's southernmost block built area with typically 4–7 floors. Further south follows the Sundbyøster and Sundbyvester districts.[^cite_note-200]

#### Other districts

Not far from [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport) on the [Kastrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastrup) coast, [The Blue Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aquarium_Denmark) completed in March 2013 now houses the national aquarium. With its 53 aquariums, it is the largest facility of its kind in Scandinavia.[^cite_note-201] [Grundtvig's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grundtvig's_Church), located in the northern suburb of [Bispebjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bispebjerg), was designed by [P.V. Jensen Klint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.V._Jensen_Klint) and completed in 1940. A rare example of [Expressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressionist_architecture) church architecture, its striking [west façade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westwork) is reminiscent of a church organ.[^cite_note-202]

## Culture



*[The Little Mermaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Mermaid_\(statue\))* statue, an icon of the city and a popular tourist attraction

Apart from being the national capital, Copenhagen also serves as the cultural hub of Denmark and one of the major hubs in wider Scandinavia. Since the late 1990s, it has undergone a transformation from a modest Scandinavian capital into a metropolitan city of international appeal, in the same league as cities such as [Barcelona](4443.md) and [Amsterdam](844.md).[^cite_note-203] This is a result of huge investments in infrastructure and culture as well as the work of successful new Danish architects, designers and chefs.[^cite_note-baumeister1-159][^cite_note-iht2007-204] [Copenhagen Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Fashion_Week) takes place every year in February and August.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206]

### Museums

See also: [List of museums in and around Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_and_around_Copenhagen)

Copenhagen has a wide array of museums of international standing. The [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Denmark), *Nationalmuseet*, is Denmark's largest museum of [archaeology](18951655.md) and [cultural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_history), comprising the histories of Danish and foreign cultures alike.[^cite_note-207] Denmark's [National Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statens_Museum_for_Kunst) (*Statens Museum for Kunst*) is the national art museum with collections dating from the 12th century to the present. In addition to Danish painters, artists represented in the collections include [Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubens), [Rembrandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rembrandt), [Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picasso), [Braque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braque), [Léger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernand_L%C3%A9ger), [Matisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matisse), [Emil Nolde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Nolde), [Olafur Eliasson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olafur_Eliasson), [Elmgreen & Dragset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmgreen_&_Dragset), [Superflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superflex), and [Jens Haaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Haaning).[^cite_note-208]



Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek art museum

Another important Copenhagen art museum is the [Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ny_Carlsberg_Glyptotek) founded by second generation [Carlsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Group) philanthropist [Carl Jacobsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Jacobsen) and built around his personal collections. Its main focus is classical [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) sculptures and antiquities and a collection of [Rodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodin) sculptures, the largest outside France. Besides its sculpture collections, the museum also holds a comprehensive collection of paintings of [Impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionist) and [Post-Impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Impressionist) painters such as [Monet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monet), [Renoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renoir), [Cézanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9zanne), [van Gogh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Gogh) and [Toulouse-Lautrec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse-Lautrec) as well as works by the Danish [Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age) painters.[^cite_note-209]

[Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Museum_of_Modern_Art) is a [Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Modern_Art) situated on the coast just north of Copenhagen. It is located in the middle of a sculpture garden on a cliff overlooking [Øresund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund). Its collection of over 3,000 items includes works by [Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picasso), [Giacometti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacometti) and [Dubuffet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubuffet).[^cite_note-210] The [Danish Design Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Design_Museum) is housed in the 18th-century former [Frederiks Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiks_Hospital) and displays [Danish design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_design) as well as international design and crafts.[^cite_note-211]

Other museums include: the [Thorvaldsens Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorvaldsens_Museum), dedicated to the [oeuvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_of_art) of romantic Danish sculptor [Bertel Thorvaldsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertel_Thorvaldsen) who lived and worked in Rome;[^cite_note-212] the [Cisternerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisternerne) museum, an exhibition space for contemporary art, located in former [cisterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern) that come complete with [stalactites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalactite) formed by the changing water levels;[^cite_note-213] and the [Ordrupgaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordrupgaard) Museum, located just north of Copenhagen, which features 19th-century French and Danish art and is noted for its works by [Paul Gauguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Gauguin).[^cite_note-214]

### Entertainment and performing arts



The Royal Danish Theatre main building

The [Royal Danish Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Theatre) stages [opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera) and drama productions, and is also home to the [Royal Danish Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Ballet). Founded in 1748 along with the theatre, it is one of the oldest ballet troupes in Europe, and is noted for its [Bournonville style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournonville_School) of ballet.[^cite_note-215] The [Royal Danish Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Playhouse) is a new building, completed in 2008, that is part of the Royal Danish Theatre.[^cite_note-n990-216]



The [Royal Danish Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Playhouse) (left) and Opera House (background, right)

The new [Copenhagen Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Concert_Hall) opened in January 2009. Designed by [Jean Nouvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Nouvel), it has four halls with the main [auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditorium) seating 1,800 people. It serves as the home of the [Danish National Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_National_Symphony_Orchestra) and, along with the [Walt Disney Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Concert_Hall) in [Los Angeles](18110.md), is one of the most expensive concert halls ever built.[^cite_note-217] Another important venue for classical music is the [Tivoli Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivoli_Concert_Hall) located in the [Tivoli Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivoli_Gardens).[^cite_note-218]

The [Danish National School of Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_National_School_of_Performing_Arts) (DASPA) has a campus in Copenhagen. Before being amalgamated with five other schools in 2015, it was known as the Statens Scenekunstskole, or National Performing Arts School.[^cite_note-219]

Designed by [Henning Larsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henning_Larsen), the [Copenhagen Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Opera_House) (*Operaen*) opened in 2005. It is among the most modern [opera houses](155040.md) in the world.[^cite_note-220]

Copenhagen has a significant [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) scene that has existed for many years. It developed when a number of American [jazz musicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_musician) such as [Ben Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Webster), [Thad Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thad_Jones), [Richard Boone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_B._Boone), Ernie Wilkins, [Kenny Drew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Drew), [Ed Thigpen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Thigpen), [Bob Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Rockwell), [Dexter Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dexter_Gordon), and others such as [rock guitarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_guitarist) [Link Wray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray) came to live in Copenhagen during the 1960s. Every year in early July, Copenhagen's streets, squares, parks as well as cafés and concert halls fill up with big and small jazz concerts during the [Copenhagen Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Jazz_Festival). One of Europe's top jazz festivals, the annual event features around 900 concerts at 100 venues with over 200,000 guests from Denmark and around the world.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEO'Brien200520-221]

The largest venue for [popular music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music) in Copenhagen is [Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega,_Copenhagen) in the [Vesterbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbro,_Copenhagen) district. It was chosen as "best concert venue in Europe" by international music magazine *Live*. The venue has three concert halls: the great hall, Store Vega, accommodates audiences of 1,550, the middle hall, Lille Vega, has space for 500 and Ideal Bar Live has a capacity of 250.[^cite_note-vega-222] Every September since 2006, the Festival of Endless Gratitude (FOEG) has taken place in Copenhagen. This festival focuses on [indie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_art) [counterculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterculture), [experimental pop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_rock) and [left field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_field) music combined with visual arts exhibitions.[^cite_note-223]

Free performances take place along [Strøget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Str%C3%B8get), particularly between [Nytorv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nytorv) and [Højbro Plads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B8jbro_Plads), where musicians, [magicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_\(illusion\)), [jugglers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugglers) and other [street performers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_performers) can be found in the late afternoon and evening.[^cite_note-224]

### Literature



Copenhagen's main public library

Most of Denmarks's major publishing houses are based in Copenhagen. These include the book publishers [Gyldendal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyldendal) and [Akademisk Forlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akademisk_Forlag) and newspaper publishers [Berlingske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlingske) and [Politiken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JP/Politikens_Hus) (the latter also publishing books).[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226] Many of the most important contributors to Danish literature such as Hans Christian Andersen (1805–1875) with his fairy tales, the philosopher Søren Kierkegaard (1813–1855) and playwright [Ludvig Holberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludvig_Holberg) (1684–1754) spent much of their lives in Copenhagen. Novels set in Copenhagen include *[Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_\(Thorup_novel\))* (1973) by [Kirsten Thorup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Thorup), *[The Copenhagen Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Copenhagen_Connection)* (1982) by [Barbara Mertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Mertz), *[Number the Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_the_Stars)* (1989) by [Lois Lowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lois_Lowry), *[Miss Smilla's Feeling for Snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Smilla's_Feeling_for_Snow)* (1992) and *[Borderliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borderliners)* (1993) by [Peter Høeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_H%C3%B8eg), *[Music and Silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_and_Silence)* (1999) by [Rose Tremain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Tremain), *[The Danish Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Danish_Girl)* (2000) by [David Ebershoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ebershoff), and *[Sharpe's Prey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpe's_Prey)* (2001) by [Bernard Cornwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Cornwell). [Michael Frayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Frayn)'s 1998 play *[Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_\(play\))* about the meeting between the [physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr) and [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg) in 1941 is also set in the city. [Tove Ditlevsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tove_Ditlevsen) wrote a series of memoirs, called *[The Copenhagen Trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Copenhagen_Trilogy)*. On 15–18 August 1973, an oral literature conference took place in Copenhagen as part of the 9th International Congress of Anthropological and Ethnological Sciences.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJasonSegal19777-227]

The [Royal Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Library,_Denmark), belonging to the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen), is the largest library in the Nordic countries with an almost complete collection of all printed Danish books since 1482. Founded in 1648, the Royal Library is located at four sites in the city, the main one being on the Slotsholmen waterfront.[^cite_note-228] Copenhagen's public library network has over 20 outlets, the largest being the Central Library (*Københavns Hovedbibliotek*) on [Krystalgade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krystalgade) in the inner city.[^cite_note-229]

### Art



Interior of the National Gallery (*Statens Museum for Kunst*), combining new and old architecture

Copenhagen has a wide selection of art museums and galleries displaying both historic works and more modern contributions. They include [Statens Museum for Kunst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statens_Museum_for_Kunst), i.e. the Danish national art gallery, in the [Østre Anlæg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98stre_Anl%C3%A6g) park, and the adjacent [Hirschsprung Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirschsprung_Collection) specialising in the 19th and early 20th century. [Kunsthal Charlottenborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsthal_Charlottenborg) in the city centre exhibits national and international contemporary art. [Den Frie Udstilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_Frie_Udstilling) near the [Østerport Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98sterport_Station) exhibits paintings created and selected by contemporary artists themselves rather than by the official authorities. The [Arken Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arken_Museum_of_Modern_Art) is located in southwestern [Ishøj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ish%C3%B8j).[^cite_note-230] Among artists who have painted scenes of Copenhagen are [Martinus Rørbye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinus_R%C3%B8rbye) (1803–1848),[^cite_note-231] [Christen Købke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christen_K%C3%B8bke) (1810–1848)[^cite_note-232] and the prolific [Paul Gustav Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Gustav_Fischer) (1860–1934).[^cite_note-233]

A number of notable sculptures can be seen in the city. In addition to *The Little Mermaid* on the waterfront, there are two historic equestrian statues in the city centre: [Jacques Saly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Saly)'s *[Frederik V on Horseback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik_V_on_Horseback)* (1771) in Amalienborg Square[^cite_note-234] and the statue of [Christian V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_V) on Kongens Nytorv created by Abraham-César Lamoureux in 1688 who was inspired by the statue of [Louis XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIII) in Paris.[^cite_note-Nørregård-Nielsen2009-235] [Rosenborg Castle Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_art_in_Rosenborg_Castle_Gardens) contains several sculptures and monuments including [August Saabye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Saabye)'s Hans Christian Andersen, [Aksel Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksel_Hansen)'s Echo, and [Vilhelm Bissen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilhelm_Bissen)'s [Dowager Queen Caroline Amalie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Amalie_of_Augustenburg).[^cite_note-236]

Copenhagen is believed to have invented the [photomarathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photomarathon) photography competition, which has been held in the City each year since 1989.[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238]

### Cuisine

For a broader look at this topic, see [Danish cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_cuisine).



Noma (previous location shown) is an example of Copenhagen's renowned experimental restaurants, and has gained three [Michelin stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_Guide).

As of 2014, Copenhagen has 15 [Michelin-starred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_Guide) restaurants, the most of any Scandinavian city.[^cite_note-239] The city is increasingly recognized internationally as a gourmet destination.[^cite_note-240] These include Den Røde Cottage, Formel B Restaurant, Grønbech & Churchill, Søllerød Kro, Kadeau, Kiin Kiin (Denmark's first Michelin-starred Asian gourmet restaurant), the French restaurant Kong Hans Kælder, Relæ, Restaurant AOC with two Stars, and [Noma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noma_\(restaurant\)) (short for [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *nordisk* *mad*, English: Nordic food) as well as [Geranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geranium_\(restaurant\)) with three. Noma was ranked as the [Best Restaurant in the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restaurant_\(magazine\)_Top_50) by *[Restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restaurant_\(magazine\))* in 2010, 2011, 2012, and again in 2014,[^cite_note-241] sparking interest in the [New Nordic Cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Nordic_Cuisine).[^cite_note-242]

Apart from the selection of upmarket restaurants, Copenhagen offers a great variety of Danish, ethnic and experimental restaurants. It is possible to find modest eateries serving [open sandwiches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_sandwich), known as [smørrebrød](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sm%C3%B8rrebr%C3%B8d) – a traditional, Danish lunch dish; however, most restaurants serve international dishes.[^cite_note-243] [Danish pastry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_pastry) can be sampled from any of numerous bakeries found in all parts of the city. The Copenhagen Bakers' Association (Danish: *Københavns Bagerlaug*) dates back to the 1290s and Denmark's oldest confectioner's shop still operating, *Conditori La Glace*, was founded in 1870 in Skoubogade by Nicolaus Henningsen, a trained master baker from [Flensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flensburg).[^cite_note-244]

Copenhagen has long been associated with beer. [Carlsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Group) beer has been brewed at the brewery's premises on the border between the [Vesterbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesterbro,_Copenhagen) and [Valby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valby) districts since 1847 and has long been almost synonymous with [Danish beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_beer) production. However, recent years have seen an explosive growth in the number of [microbreweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbrewery) so that Denmark today has more than 100 breweries, many of which are located in Copenhagen. Some like Nørrebro Bryghus also act as [brewpubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewpubs) where it is also possible to eat on the premises.[^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246]

### Nightlife and festivals



Copenhagen [Pride Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_Parade), 2008

Copenhagen has one of the highest number of restaurants and bars per capita in the world.[^cite_note-247] The nightclubs and bars stay open until 5 or 6 in the morning, some even longer. Denmark has a very liberal alcohol culture and a strong tradition for beer breweries, although binge drinking is frowned upon and the Danish Police take [driving under the influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_under_the_influence) very seriously.[^cite_note-248] Inner city areas such as [Istedgade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istedgade) and [Enghave Plads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enghave_Plads) in Vesterbro, [Sankt Hans Torv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankt_Hans_Torv) in Nørrebro and certain places in Frederiksberg are especially noted for their [nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife). Notable nightclubs include Bakken Kbh, ARCH (previously ZEN), Jolene, The Jane, Chateau Motel, KB3, At Dolores (previously Sunday Club), Rust, Vega Nightclub and Culture Box.[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250]

Copenhagen has several recurring community festivals, mainly in the summer. [Copenhagen Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Carnival) has taken place every year since 1982 during the [Whitsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitsun) Holiday in [Fælledparken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A6lledparken) and around the city with the participation of 120 bands, 2,000 dancers and 100,000 spectators.[^cite_note-Timeline-251] Since 2010, the old B\&W Shipyard at [Refshaleøen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refshale%C3%B8en) in the harbour has been the location for [Copenhell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhell), a [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) rock music festival. [Copenhagen Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Pride) is a [LGBT pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_pride) festival taking place every year in August. The Pride has a series of different activities all over Copenhagen, but it is at the [City Hall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Hall_Square,_Copenhagen) that most of the celebration takes place. During the Pride the square is renamed Pride Square.[^cite_note-252] [Copenhagen Distortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Distortion) has emerged to be one of the biggest street festivals in Europe with 100,000 people joining to parties in the beginning of June every year.[^cite_note-253]

### Amusement parks



The [Pantomime Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantomime_Theatre), opened in 1874, is the oldest building in the Tivoli Gardens.

Copenhagen has the oldest and third-oldest [amusement parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amusement_parks) in the world.[^cite_note-www3-254][^cite_note-255]

[Dyrehavsbakken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyrehavsbakken), a fair-ground and pleasure-park established in 1583, is located in [Klampenborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klampenborg) just north of Copenhagen in a forested area known as [Dyrehaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyrehaven). Created as an amusement park complete with rides, games and restaurants by [Christian IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_IV), it is the oldest surviving amusement park in the world.[^cite_note-www3-254] [Pierrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierrot#Denmark) (Danish: *Pjerrot*), a nitwit dressed in white with a scarlet grin wearing a boat-like hat while entertaining children, remains one of the park's key attractions. In Danish, Dyrehavsbakken is often abbreviated as *Bakken*. There is no entrance fee to pay and [Klampenborg Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klampenborg_Station) on the [C-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_\(S-train\)), is situated nearby.[^cite_note-256]

The [Tivoli Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivoli_Gardens) is an amusement park and pleasure garden located in central Copenhagen between the [City Hall Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_City_Hall_Square,_Copenhagen) and the [Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Central_Station). It opened in 1843, making it the third-oldest amusement park in the world, the second being [Wurstelprater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurstelprater) in Vienna. Among its rides are the oldest still operating rollercoaster *Rutschebanen* from 1915 and the oldest [ferris wheel](175541.md) still in use, opened in 1943.[^cite_note-257] Tivoli Gardens also serves as a venue for various performing arts and as an active part of the cultural scene in Copenhagen.[^cite_note-258]

## Education



The main building of the University of Copenhagen

Copenhagen has over 94,000 students enrolled in its largest universities and institutions: [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) (38,867 students),[^cite_note-259] [Copenhagen Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Business_School) (20,000 students),[^cite_note-260] [Metropolitan University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_University_College) and [University College Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Capital) (10,000 students each),[^cite_note-261] [Technical University of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Denmark) (7,000 students),[^cite_note-262] KEA (c. 4,500 students),[^cite_note-263] [IT University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IT_University_of_Copenhagen) (2,000 students) and the [Copenhagen campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg_University_%E2%80%93_Copenhagen) of [Aalborg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalborg_University) (2,300 students).[^cite_note-264]

The University of Copenhagen is Denmark's oldest university founded in 1479. It attracts some 1,500 international and exchange students every year. The [Academic Ranking of World Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities) placed it 30th in the world in 2016.[^cite_note-265]

The Technical University of Denmark is located in [Lyngby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyngby) in the northern outskirts of Copenhagen. In 2013, it was ranked as one of the leading technical universities in Northern Europe.[^cite_note-266] The IT University is Denmark's youngest university, a mono-faculty institution focusing on technical, societal and business aspects of information technology.[^cite_note-267]

The [Danish Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Academy_of_Fine_Arts) has provided education in the arts for more than 250 years. It includes the historic School of Visual Arts, and has in later years come to include a [School of Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstakademiets_Arkitektskole), a [School of Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danmarks_Designskole) and a School of Conservation.[^cite_note-268] [Copenhagen Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Business_School) (CBS) is an [EQUIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EQUIS)-accredited business school located in [Frederiksberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg_Municipality).[^cite_note-269] There are also branches of both [University College Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Capital) and [Metropolitan University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_University_College) inside and outside Copenhagen.[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271]

## Sport

The city has a variety of sporting teams. The major [football](10568.md) teams are the historically successful [FC København](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_K%C3%B8benhavn)[^cite_note-272] and [Brøndby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8ndby_IF). FC København plays at [Parken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parken) in Østerbro. Formed in 1992, it is a merger of two older Copenhagen clubs, [B 1903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B_1903) (from the inner suburb Gentofte) and [KB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kj%C3%B8benhavns_Boldklub) (from Frederiksberg).[^cite_note-273] Brøndby plays at [Brøndby Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8ndby_Stadion) in the inner suburb of [Brøndbyvester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8ndbyvester). [BK Frem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BK_Frem) is based in the southern part of Copenhagen (Sydhavnen, Valby). Other teams of more significant stature are [FC Nordsjælland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Nordsj%C3%A6lland) (from suburban [Farum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farum)), [Fremad Amager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremad_Amager), [B93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boldklubben_1893), [AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akademisk_Boldklub), [Lyngby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyngby_Boldklub) and [Hvidovre IF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hvidovre_IF).[^cite_note-nysport-274]



Copenhagen Marathon, 2008

Copenhagen has several [handball teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_handball)—a sport which is particularly popular in Denmark. Clubs playing in the "highest" leagues include Ajax, Ydun, and HIK ([Hellerup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellerup)).[^cite_note-nysport-274] The [København Håndbold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B8benhavn_H%C3%A5ndbold) women's club has recently been established.[^cite_note-275] Copenhagen also has [ice hockey](14790.md) teams, of which three play in the top league, [Rødovre Mighty Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B8dovre_Mighty_Bulls), [Herlev Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herlev_Eagles) and [Hvidovre Ligahockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hvidovre_Ligahockey) all inner suburban clubs. [Copenhagen Ice Skating Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kj%C3%B8benhavns_Sk%C3%B8jtel%C3%B8berforening) founded in 1869 is the oldest ice hockey team in Denmark but is no longer in the top league.[^cite_note-276]

[Rugby union](25405.md) is also played in the Danish capital with teams such as [CSR-Nanok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSR-Nanok), Copenhagen Business School Sport Rugby, [Frederiksberg RK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksberg_RK), [Exiles RUFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exiles_RUFC) and [Rugbyklubben Speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RK_Speed). [Rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) is now played in Copenhagen, with the national team playing out of [Gentofte Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentofte_Stadion). The [Danish Australian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Australian_Football_League), based in Copenhagen is the largest [Australian rules football](2403.md) competition outside of the [English-speaking world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-speaking_world).[^cite_note-nysport-274][^cite_note-277]

[Copenhagen Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Marathon), Copenhagen's annual [marathon](49132.md) event, was established in 1980.[^cite_note-278] [Round Christiansborg Open Water Swim Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slotsholmen#Round_Christiansborg_Open_Water_Swim) is a 2-kilometre (1.2-mile) [open water swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_water_swimming) competition taking place each year in late August.[^cite_note-279] This amateur event is combined with a 10-kilometre (6-mile) Danish championship.[^cite_note-280] In 2009 the event included a 10-kilometre (6-mile) FINA World Cup competition in the morning. Copenhagen hosted the [2011 UCI Road World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_UCI_Road_World_Championships) in September 2011, taking advantage of its bicycle-friendly infrastructure. It was the first time that Denmark had hosted the event since 1956, when it was also held in Copenhagen.[^cite_note-281]

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Copenhagen)



Aerial view of Copenhagen seen from an airplane departing from Copenhagen Airport

### Airport

The greater Copenhagen area has a very well established transportation infrastructure making it a hub in Northern Europe. [Copenhagen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Airport), opened in 1925, is Scandinavia's largest airport, located in [Kastrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastrup) on the island of Amager. It is connected to the city centre by metro and main line railway services.[^cite_note-282] October 2013 was a record month with 2.2 million passengers, and November 2013 figures reveal that the number of passengers is increasing by some 3% annually, about 50% more than the European average.[^cite_note-283]

### Road, rail and sea

Copenhagen has an extensive road network including motorways connecting the city to other parts of Denmark and to Sweden over the [Øresund Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98resund_Bridge).[^cite_note-nt-284] The car is still the most popular form of transport within the city itself, representing two-thirds of all distances travelled. This can however lead to serious congestion in rush hour traffic.[^cite_note-285] The Øresund train links Copenhagen with Malmö 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.&#x20;

Copenhagen is also served by a daily ferry connection to [Oslo](22309.md) in Norway.[^cite_note-ferry-286]

In 2012, Copenhagens Malmö Port handled 372 cruise ships and 840,000 passengers [^cite_note-ferry-286] across its three cruise terminals Langelinie, Nordre Toldbod and the largest Ocean Quay (Oceankaj), it takes about 5 minutes by public transport to get to the city centre [^cite_note-287][^cite_note-288][^cite_note-289] the terminals also feature large power connections that allow suitably equipped ships to plugin and turn off all their engines.[^cite_note-290]

The [Copenhagen S-Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_S-Train), [Copenhagen Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Metro) and the regional train networks are used by about half of the city's passengers, the remainder using bus services. [Nørreport Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rreport_Station) near the city centre serves passengers travelling by main-line rail, [S-train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-train), regional train, metro and bus. Some 750,000 passengers make use of public transport facilities every day.[^cite_note-nt-284] [Copenhagen Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Central_Station) is the hub of the [DSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSB_\(railway_company\)) railway network serving Denmark and international destinations.[^cite_note-291]

The [Copenhagen Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Metro) expanded radically with the opening of the [City Circle Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M3_\(Copenhagen_Metro\)) (M3) on 29 September 2019.[^cite_note-292] The new line connects all inner boroughs of the city by metro, including the [Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Central_Station), and opens up 17 new stations[^cite_note-293] for Copenhageners. On 28 March 2020, the 2.2 km (1.4 mi) Nordhavn extension of the Harbour Line ([M4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_\(Copenhagen_Metro\))) opened.[^cite_note-294] Running from [Copenhagen Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Central_Station), the new extension is a branch line of [M3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M3_\(Copenhagen_Metro\)) Cityring to Østerport.[^cite_note-295] The new metro lines are part of the city's strategy to transform mobility towards sustainable modes of transport such as public transport and cycling as opposed to automobility.[^cite_note-296]

Copenhagen is cited by urban planners for its exemplary integration of public transport and urban development. In implementing its [Finger Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_Plan), Copenhagen is considered the world's first example of a [transit metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_metropolis),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECervero1998132-55] and areas around S-Train stations like [Ballerup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballerup) and [Brøndby Strand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B8ndby_Strand) are among the earliest examples of [transit-oriented development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit-oriented_development).[^cite_note-297]

### Cycling



The extensive use of bicycles in Copenhagen illustrated here at the [Nørreport railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B8rreport_railway_station)

Main article: [Cycling in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Copenhagen)

See also: [Cycling advocacy § Copenhagenization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_advocacy#Copenhagenization)

Copenhagen has been rated as one of the most [bicycle-friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle-friendly) cities in the world since 2015, with bicycles outnumbering its inhabitants.[^cite_note-298][^cite_note-299][^cite_note-Copenhagen's_piles_of_bicycles-300] In 2012 some 36% of all working or studying city-dwellers cycled to work, school, or university. With 1.27 million km (790,000 mi) covered every working day by [Copenhagen's cyclists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Copenhagen) (including both residents and commuters), and 75% of Copenhageners cycling throughout the year.[^cite_note-301] The city's [bicycle paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_infrastructure) are extensive and well used, boasting 400 kilometres (250 miles) of cycle lanes not shared with cars or pedestrians, and sometimes equipped with their own signal systems – giving the cyclists a lead of a couple of seconds to accelerate.[^cite_note-Copenhagen's_piles_of_bicycles-300][^cite_note-302]

## Healthcare

See also: [Healthcare in Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Denmark)



Rigshospitalet is one of the largest hospitals in Denmark.

Promoting health is an important issue for Copenhagen's municipal authorities. Central to its sustainability mission is its "Long Live Copenhagen" (*Længe Leve København*) scheme in which it has the goal of increasing the life expectancy of citizens, improving quality of life through better standards of health, and encouraging more productive lives and equal opportunities.[^cite_note-kkhealth-303] The city has targets to encourage people to exercise regularly and to reduce the number of people who smoke and consume alcohol.[^cite_note-kkhealth-303]

[Copenhagen University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_University_Hospital) forms a conglomerate of several hospitals in [Region Hovedstaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_Hovedstaden) and [Region Sjælland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_Sj%C3%A6lland), together with the faculty of [health sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_sciences) at the University of Copenhagen; [Rigshospitalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigshospitalet) and [Bispebjerg Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bispebjerg_Hospital) in Copenhagen belong to this group of university hospitals.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKjaerKrogsgaardMagnussonLars_Engebretsen200811-304] Rigshospitalet began operating in March 1757 as Frederiks Hospital,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchaldemose2005107-305] and became state-owned in 1903. With 1,120 beds, Rigshospitalet has responsibility for 65,000 inpatients and approximately 420,000 outpatients annually. It seeks to be the number one specialist hospital in the country, with an extensive team of researchers into cancer treatment, surgery and radiotherapy.[^cite_note-306] In addition to its 8,000 personnel, the hospital has training and hosting functions. It benefits from the presence of in-service students of medicine and other healthcare sciences, as well as scientists working under a variety of research grants. The hospital became internationally famous as the location of [Lars von Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_von_Trier)'s television horror mini-series *[The Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riget)*. Bispebjerg Hospital was built in 1913, and serves about 400,000 people in the Greater Copenhagen area, with some 3,000 employees.[^cite_note-307] Other large hospitals in the city include [Amager Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amager_Hospital) (1997),[^cite_note-308] [Herlev Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herlev_Hospital) (1976),[^cite_note-309] [Hvidovre Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hvidovre_Hospital) (1970),[^cite_note-310] and [Gentofte Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentofte_Hospital) (1927).[^cite_note-311]

## Media



The Aller Media conglomerate building in Havneholm

Many Danish media corporations are located in Copenhagen. [DR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DR_\(broadcaster\)), the major Danish [public service](178808.md) broadcasting corporation consolidated its activities in a new headquarters, [DR Byen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DR_Byen), in 2006 and 2007. Similarly [TV2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_2_\(Denmark\)), which is based in [Odense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odense), has concentrated its Copenhagen activities in a modern media house in [Teglholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teglholmen).[^cite_note-312] The two national daily newspapers *[Politiken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politiken)* and *[Berlingske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlingske)* and the two [tabloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabloid_\(newspaper_format\)) *[Ekstra Bladet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekstra_Bladet)* and *[BT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.T._\(tabloid\))* are based in Copenhagen.[^cite_note-313] *[Kristeligt Dagblad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristeligt_Dagblad)* is based in Copenhagen and is published six days a week.[^cite_note-314] Other important media corporations include [Aller Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aller_Media) which is the largest publisher of weekly and monthly magazines in [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia),[^cite_note-315] the [Egmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egmont_Publishing) [media group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_group)[^cite_note-316] and [Gyldendal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyldendal), the largest Danish publisher of books.[^cite_note-317]

Copenhagen has a large film and television industry. [Nordisk Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordisk_Film), established in Valby, Copenhagen in 1906 is the oldest continuously operating film production company in the world.[^cite_note-Timeline-251] In 1992 it merged with the Egmont media group and currently runs the 17-screen [Palads Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palads_Cinema) in Copenhagen. [Filmbyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmbyen) (movie city), located in a former [military camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_camp) in the suburb of [Hvidovre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hvidovre), houses several movie companies and [studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_studio). [Zentropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zentropa) is a film company, co-owned by Danish [director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_director) [Lars von Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_von_Trier). He is behind several international movie productions as well and founded the [Dogme Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogme_95).[^cite_note-318] [CPH\:PIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPH\:PIX) is Copenhagen's international feature film festival, established in 2009 as a fusion of the 20-year-old [NatFilm Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NatFilm_Festival) and the four-year-old CIFF. The CPH\:PIX festival takes place in mid-April. [CPH\:DOX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPH\:DOX) is Copenhagen's international documentary film festival, every year in November. In addition to a documentary film programme of over 100 films, CPH\:DOX includes a wide [event programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Event_program) with dozens of events, concerts, exhibitions and parties all over town.[^cite_note-cphpix1-319]
