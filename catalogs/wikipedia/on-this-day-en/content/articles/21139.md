**North America** is a [continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent)[^cite_note-8] in the [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere) and [Western](73162.md) hemispheres.[^cite_note-9] It is bordered to the north by the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean), to the east by the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), to the southeast by [South America](26769.md) and the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md), and to the south and west by the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md).

North America covers an area of around 24,709,000 square kilometers (9,540,000 square miles), representing approximately 16.5% of [Earth](9228.md)'s land area and 4.8% of its total surface area. It is the third-largest continent by size after [Asia](689.md) and [Africa](5334607.md), and the [fourth-largest continent by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_continents_and_continental_subregions_by_population) after Asia, Africa, and [Europe](9239.md). As of 2021, North America's population is estimated at over 592 million people in [23 independent states and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_and_dependent_territories_in_North_America), or about 7.5% of the world's population.

The continent includes the subregions of [Northern America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_America) (comprising [Canada](5042916.md), [Greenland](12118.md), and the [continental United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_United_States) as well as the insular territories of [Bermuda](3460.md) and [Saint Pierre and Miquelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pierre_and_Miquelon)) and [Middle America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_America_\(Americas\)) (comprising [Mexico](3966054.md), [Central America](6121.md) and the [Caribbean](18956035.md)). In [human geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas_\(terminology\)#Human_geography), the terms "North America" and "North American" often refer to just Northern America.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-MoC-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

It is unknown exactly how and when the [first human populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peopling_of_the_Americas) reached North America. People are known to have lived in the [Americas](29833.md) at least 20,000 years ago,[^cite_note-15] but various evidence points to possibly earlier dates.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The [Paleo-Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians) period in North America followed the Last Glacial Period, and lasted until about 10,000 years ago when the [Archaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_\(North_America\)) began. The [classic stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_stage) followed the Archaic period, and lasted from approximately the 6th to 13th centuries. [Norse exploration and colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_settlement_of_North_America) began in the late 10th century; the Norse built [several settlements in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_settlements_in_Greenland) and [at least one settlement in Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Anse_aux_Meadows).

Beginning in 1492, the [voyages of Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages_of_Christopher_Columbus) led to [a transatlantic exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_exchange), including [migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_migration) of [European settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) during the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery) and the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period). Present-day cultural and ethnic patterns reflect interactions between early European colonists, [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), [enslaved Africans](49005.md), later immigrants from Europe and Asia, and descendants of these respective groups. European colonization has led to most North Americans speaking [European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_languages) (such as [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [Spanish](26825.md), and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language)) and the cultures of the region commonly reflect [Western traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture). However, there remain significant indigenous populations that continue to adhere to their respective pre-colonial cultural and linguistic traditions.

## Name

Further information: [Naming of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_of_the_Americas), [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), [Turtle Island](14640744.md), and [Vinland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinland)



A 1621 map of North America



A 1908 map of North America, published in *The Harmsworth atlas and Gazetter*

The [Americas](29833.md) were named after the [Italian](385155.md) explorer [Amerigo Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci) by [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people) cartographers [Martin Waldseemüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Waldseem%C3%BCller) and [Matthias Ringmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Ringmann).[^cite_note-18] Vespucci explored [South America](26769.md) between 1497 and 1502, and was the first European to suggest that the Americas represented a landmass then unknown to the Europeans. In 1507, Waldseemüller published a world map, and placed the word "America" on the continent of present-day South America.[^cite_note-19] The continent north of present-day Mexico was then referred to as Parias.[^cite_note-20] On a 1553 world map published by [Petrus Apianus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrus_Apianus),[^cite_note-21] North America was called "Baccalearum", meaning "realm of the Cod fish", in reference to the abundance of [cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cod) on the [East Coast](89126.md).[^cite_note-22]

Waldseemüller used the [Latinized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinisation_of_names) version of Vespucci's name, Americus Vespucius, in its feminine form of "America", following the examples of "Europa", "Asia", and "Africa". Americus originated from [Medieval Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Latin) *Emericus* (see [Saint Emeric of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Emeric_of_Hungary)), coming from the Old High German name [Emmerich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmerich_\(name\)). Map makers later extended the name America to North America.

In 1538, [Gerardus Mercator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardus_Mercator) used the term America on his world map of the entire [Western Hemisphere](73162.md).[^cite_note-Cohen-23] On [his subsequent 1569 map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercator_1569_world_map), Mercator called North America "America or New India" (*America sive India Nova*).[^cite_note-24]

The [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) called its territories in North and South America "Las Indias", and the name given to the state body that oversaw the region was called the [Council of the Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Indies).

## Definition



A 2005 [NASA](18426568.md) satellite image of North America

The [United Nations](31769.md) and its [statistics division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Statistics_Division) recognize North America as comprising three subregions: [Northern America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_America), [Central America](6121.md) (which [includes Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme#Central_America)), and the [Caribbean](18956035.md).[^cite_note-25] In the limited context of regional trade agreements such as [NAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAFTA), the term is used to reference three countries: [Canada](5042916.md), Mexico, and the [United States](3434750.md).

According to the [six-continent model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent#Number), which is prevalent in [Latin America](18524.md) and across the [Romance-speaking world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages) as well as in [Greece](12108.md), "North America" designates a subregion comprising Canada, Mexico, [Saint Pierre and Miquelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pierre_and_Miquelon), and the United States; and often [Bermuda](3460.md), [Clipperton Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clipperton_Island), and [Greenland](12118.md).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

North America has historically been known by other names, including Spanish North America, [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France), [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America) and *América [Septentrional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septentrional)*, the [first official name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solemn_Act_of_the_Declaration_of_Independence_of_Northern_America) given to Mexico.[^cite_note-AGN-31]

### Regions

Further information: [List of regions of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regions_of_Canada) and [List of regions of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regions_of_the_United_States)

North America includes several regions and subregions, each of which have their own respective cultural, economic, and geographic regions. Economic regions include several regions formalized in 20th- and 21st-century trade agreements, including [NAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Free_Trade_Agreement) between [Canada](5042916.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), and the [United States](3434750.md), and [CAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic%E2%80%93Central_America_Free_Trade_Agreement) between [Central America](6121.md), the [Dominican Republic](8060.md), and the United States.

North America is divided linguistically and culturally into two primary regions, [Anglo-America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-America) and [Latin America](18524.md). Anglo-America includes most of North America, [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize), and [Caribbean](18956035.md) islands with [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)-speaking populations. There are also regions, including [Louisiana](18130.md) and [Quebec](7954867.md), with large [Francophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophone) populations; in Quebec and [Saint Pierre and Miquelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Pierre_and_Miquelon), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) is the official language.[^cite_note-language-32].

The southern portion of North America includes Central America and non-English-speaking Caribbean nations.[^cite_note-BritannicaCA-33][^cite_note-34] The north of the continent maintains recognized regions as well. In contrast to the common definition of North America, which encompasses the whole North American continent, the term "North America" is sometimes used more narrowly to refer only to four nations, Canada, Greenland, Mexico, and the U.S.[^cite_note-CIAGreenland-35][^cite_note-CountryReports-36][^cite_note-eNotes-37][^cite_note-Trilateral-38][^cite_note-39] The U.S. Census Bureau includes Saint Pierre and Miquelon, but excludes [Mexico](3966054.md) from its definition.[^cite_note-40]

The term Northern America refers to the northernmost countries and territories of North America: the U.S., [Bermuda](3460.md), Canada, Greenland, and St. Pierre and Miquelon.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Although the term does not refer to a unified region,[^cite_note-43] [Middle America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_America_\(Americas\)) includes Mexico, Central America, and the Caribbean.[^cite_note-44]

North America's largest countries by land area are Canada and the U.S., both of which have well-defined and recognized subregions. In Canada, these include (from east to west) [Atlantic Canada](71095.md), [Central Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Canada), the [Canadian Prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Prairies), the [British Columbia Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Coast), [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada), and [Northern Canada](79987.md). In the U.S., they include [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England), the [Mid-Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_\(United_States\)), and the [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_states), [East North Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_North_Central_states), [West North Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_North_Central_states), [East South Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_South_Central_states), [West South Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_South_Central_states), [Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_states), and [Pacific states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States). The [Great Lakes region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_region) and the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) include areas in both the U.S. and Canada.

### Pre-Columbian era

The [indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) have many [creation myths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_myth), some of which assert that they have been present on the land since its creation,[^cite_note-Curtin2014-45] but there is no evidence that humans evolved there.[^cite_note-Krensky-46] The specifics of the initial [settlement of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_of_the_Americas) by ancient Asians are subject to ongoing research and discussion.[^cite_note-White2006-47] The traditional theory has been that hunters entered the [Bering Land Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beringia) between eastern [Siberia](29639.md) and present-day [Alaska](624.md) from 27,000 to 14,000 years ago.[^cite_note-HavilandPrins2013-48][^cite_note-Sonneborn-2007-49][^cite_note-51] A growing viewpoint is that the first American inhabitants sailed from Beringia some 13,000 years ago,[^cite_note-52] with widespread habitation of the Americas during the end of the [Last Glacial Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period), in what is known as the [Late Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Glacial_Maximum#North_America), around 12,500 years ago.[^cite_note-Pauketat2012-53] The oldest [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) in North America date from 15,000 to 10,000 years before present.[^cite_note-54] [Genetic research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) and [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology) indicate additional waves of migration from Asia via the [Bering Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Strait) during the Early-Middle [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene).[^cite_note-SkoglundMallick2015-55][^cite_note-BellwoodNess2014-56][^cite_note-57]

Prior to the arrival of European explorers and colonists in North America, the [natives of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians) were divided into many different polities, ranging from small [bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_society) of a few families to large empires. They lived in several [culture areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), which roughly correspond to [geographic and biological zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_North_America) that defined the representative cultures and lifestyles of the indigenous people who lived there, including the [bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_Bison) [hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer) of the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_Indian) and the farmers of [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica). Native groups also are classified by their [language families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_languages_of_the_Americas), which included [Athabaskan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athabaskan_languages) and [Uto-Aztecan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uto-Aztecan_languages) languages. Indigenous peoples with similar languages did not always share the same [material culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_culture), however, and were not necessarily always allies. Anthropologists speculate that the [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit) of the high [Arctic](36971.md) arrived in North America much later than other native groups, evidenced by the disappearance of [Dorset culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset_culture) artifacts from the [archaeological record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_record) and their replacement by the [Thule people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_people).

During the thousands of years of native habitation on the continent, cultures changed and shifted. One of the oldest yet discovered is the [Clovis culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_culture) (c. 9550–9050 BCE) in modern [New Mexico](21649.md).[^cite_note-bradshaw-58] Following the early [Archaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_\(North_America\)), major regional developments included the [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture) (spanning the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) valley with [platform-mound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_mound) urban centers) and the [Ancestral Puebloans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_Puebloans) of the [Four Corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Corners) region. The more southern cultural groups of North America were responsible for the [domestication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestication) of many common [crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crops) now used around the world, such as tomatoes, [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(plant\)), and [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize). As a result of the development of agriculture in the south, many other cultural advances were made there. The [Mayans](18449273.md) developed a [writing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_script), built [huge pyramids and temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_pyramids), had a [complex calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_calendar), and developed the concept of zero around 400 CE.[^cite_note-59]

The first recorded European references to North America are in [Norse sagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinland_sagas) where it is referred to as [Vinland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinland).[^cite_note-60] The earliest verifiable instance of [pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_trans-oceanic_contact) by any European culture with the North America mainland has been dated to around 1000 CE.[^cite_note-61] [The site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Anse_aux_Meadows#Norse_site_\(c._1000\)), situated at the northernmost extent of the island named [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland), has provided unmistakable evidence of [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen) settlement.[^cite_note-62] Norse explorer [Leif Erikson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leif_Erikson) (c. 970–1020 CE) is thought to have visited the area.[^cite_note-64] Erikson was the first European to make landfall on the continent (excluding Greenland).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

The Mayan culture was still present in [southern Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucatan_Peninsula) and [Guatemala](17238567.md) when the Spanish [conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) arrived, but political dominance in the area had shifted to the [Aztec Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_Empire), whose capital city [Tenochtitlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenochtitlan) was located further north in the [Valley of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Mexico). The Aztecs were conquered in 1521 by [Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s).[^cite_note-Grunberg-67]

### Post-contact, 1492–1910

Main article: [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas)

Further information: [Timeline of the European colonization of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_European_colonization_of_North_America), [British America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America), [French America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_America), [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), and [Russian colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_colonization_of_the_Americas)



World map of German [cartographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography) [Martin Waldseemüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Waldseem%C3%BCller) (Germany, 1507), which first used the name America



A 1702 map of North America showing forts, towns, and (in solid colors) areas occupied by [European colonial settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas)

During the so-called [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery), Europeans explored overseas and staked claims to various parts of North America, much of which was already settled by indigenous peoples. Upon Europeans' arrival in the "[New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World)", indigenous peoples had a variety of reactions, including curiosity, trading, cooperation, resignation, and resistance. The [indigenous population declined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) substantially following European arrival, primarily due to the introduction of Eurasian diseases, such as [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox), to which the indigenous peoples lacked immunity, and because of [violent conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indian_Wars) with Europeans.[^cite_note-68] Indigenous culture changed significantly and their affiliation with political and cultural groups also changed. Several linguistic groups [died out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_death), and others changed quite quickly.

On North America's southeastern coast, Spanish explorer [Juan Ponce de León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Ponce_de_Le%C3%B3n), who had accompanied Columbus's second voyage, visited and named in 1513 *[La Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Florida)*.[^cite_note-69] As the colonial period unfolded, [Spain](26667.md), England, and France appropriated and claimed extensive territories on North America's eastern and southern coastlines. Spain established permanent settlements on the Caribbean islands of [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola) and [Cuba](5042481.md) in the 1490s, building cities, putting the resident indigenous populations to work, raising crops for Spanish settlers and panning gold to enrich the Spaniards. Much of the indigenous population died due to disease and overwork, spurring the Spaniards on to claim new lands and peoples. An expedition under the command of Spanish settler, [Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s), sailed westward in 1519 to what turned out to be the mainland in Mexico. With local indigenous allies, the Spanish [conquered the Aztec empire in central Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_the_Aztec_empire) in 1521. Spain then established permanent cities in [Mexico](3966054.md), Central America, and Spanish South America in the sixteenth century. Once Spaniards conquered the high civilization of the Aztecs and Incas, the Caribbean was a backwater of the Spanish empire.

Other European powers began to intrude on areas claimed by Spain, including the Caribbean islands. France took the western half of [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola) and developed [Saint-Domingue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Domingue) as a cane sugar producing colony worked by black slave labor. Britain took [Barbados](3455.md) and [Jamaica](15660.md), and the Dutch and Danes took islands previously claimed by Spain. Britain did not begin settling on the North American mainland until a hundred years after the first Spanish settlements, since it sought first to control nearby [Ireland](147575.md).

### English settlements

Main article: [British America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_America)

The first permanent English settlement was in [Jamestown, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown,_Virginia) in 1607, followed by additional colonial establishments on the [east coast](89126.md) from present-day [Georgia](48830.md) in the south to [Massachusetts](1645518.md) in the north, forming the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) of British America. The English did not establish settlements north or east of the [St. Lawrence Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_River_Valley) in present-day Canada until after the conclusion of the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War). Britain's early settlements in present-day Canada included [St. John's, Newfoundland](70581.md) in 1630 and [Halifax, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia) in 1749. The first permanent French settlement was in [Quebec City, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City,_Quebec), established in 1608.

### Seven Years' War

Main article: [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War)

With the British victory in the Seven Years' War, France in 1763 ceded to Britain its claims of North American territories east of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River). [Spain](26667.md), in turn, gained rights to the territories west of Mississippi, which then served as a border between Spain and Britain's territorial claims. French colonists settled [Illinois Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Country) after several generations of experience on North America, migrating over the Mississippi River to regions where Spain was not present and where they were able to leverage their earlier Louisiana French settlements around the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md). These early French settlers partnered with midwest indigenous tribes, and their mixed ancestry descendants later followed a westward expansion all the way to the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) on the present-day [U.S. West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._West_Coast).

### American Revolution

Main articles: [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) and [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War)

In 1776, after various attempts to reconcile differences with the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain), the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) in British America sent delegates to the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), who unanimously adopted the [Declaration of Independence](31874.md) on 4 July 1776, written primarily by [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson), a member of the [Committee of Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Five) charged by the Second Continental Congress with authoring it. In the Declaration, the thirteen colonies declared their independence from the British monarchy, then governed by King [George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III), and detailed the factors that contributed to their decision. With the signing and issuance of the Declaration of Independence, the thirteen colonies formalized and escalated the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), which had begun the year before at the [Battles of Lexington and Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Lexington_and_Concord) on 19 April 1775. Gathered in Philadelphia following the war's outbreak, delegates from the thirteen colonies established the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) from various [patriot militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_\(American_Revolution\)) then engaged in resisting the British, and appointed [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) as the Continental Army's military commander.

As the American Revolutionary War progressed, France and Spain, both then enemies of Britain, began to ultimately see the promise of a potential American victory in the war and began supporting Washington and the American Revolutionary cause. The [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_American_Revolutionary_War), in turn, was supported by [Hessian military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessian_\(soldier\)) units from present-day [Germany](11867.md).

In 1783, after an eight-year attempt to defeat the American rebellion, King George III acknowledged Britain's defeat in the war, leading to the signing of the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)) on 3 September 1783, which solidified the sovereign establishment of the United States.

### Westward expansion

Main article: [Westward Expansion Trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westward_Expansion_Trails)

Further information: [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition), [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase), and [United States Exploring Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Exploring_Expedition)



European colonization of North America and the [Territorial evolution of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_the_United_States) by [Gustav Droysen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Gustav_Ferdinand_Droysen?action=edit\&redlink=1)

By the late 18th century, Russia was established on the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) northern coastline, where it was engaged in [maritime fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_fur_trade) and was supported by various indigenous settlements in the region. As a result, the Spanish were showing more interest in controlling the trade on the Pacific coast and mapped most of its coastline. The first Spanish settlements were attempted in [Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California) during that period. Numerous overland explorations associated with [voyageurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyageurs), [fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade), and U.S. led expeditions, including the [Lewis and Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition), [Frémont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Fr%C3%A9mont) and [Wilkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Exploring_Expedition) expeditions, reached the Pacific.

In 1803, during the [presidency of Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Thomas_Jefferson), the third [U.S. president](24113.md), [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte) sold France's remaining North American territorial claims, which included regions west of the Mississippi River, to the U.S., in the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase). Spain and the U.S. settled their western boundary dispute in 1819 in the [Adams–Onís Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams%E2%80%93On%C3%ADs_Treaty). Mexico fought a lengthy war for independence from Spain, winning it for Mexico (which included Central America at the time) in 1821. The U.S. sought further westward expansion and fought the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War), gaining a vast territory that first Spain and then Mexico claimed but which they did not effectively control. Much of the area was in fact dominated by indigenous peoples, which did not recognize the claims of Spain, France, or the U.S. Russia sold its North American claims, which included the present-day U.S. state of [Alaska](624.md), to the U.S. in 1867.

### Canada and Panama Canal

Main articles: [Canada](5042916.md) and [Panama Canal](24849.md)

In 1867, colonial settlers north of the United States, unified as the dominion of Canada. The U.S. sought to dig a canal across the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama) in present-day [Panama](22997.md) in Central America, then a part of present-day [Colombia](5222.md). The U.S. aided Panamanians in a war that resulted in its separation from Colombia. The U.S. subsequently carved out the [Panama Canal Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Canal_Zone), and claimed sovereignty over it. After decades of work, the Panama Canal was completed, which connected the [Atlantic](698.md) and [Pacific](23070.md) oceans in 1913 and greatly facilitated global shipping navigation.

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_North_America)

North America occupies the northern portion of the landmass generally referred to as the New World, the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md), the Americas, or simply America, which, in many countries, is considered a single continent[^cite_note-IOC-70][^cite_note-Oceano-71][^cite_note-cincocontinentes-72] with North America a [subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent#Subcontinents).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-britannica-northamerica-74][^cite_note-75] North America is the third-largest continent by area after [Asia](689.md) and [Africa](5334607.md).[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

North America's only land connection to [South America](26769.md) is in present-day [Panama](22997.md) at the [Darien Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darien_Gap) on the [Colombia–Panama border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia%E2%80%93Panama_border), placing almost all of Panama within North America.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-North_America_Atlas-80] Alternatively, some geologists [physiographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiographic_region) locate its southern limit at the [Isthmus of Tehuantepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Tehuantepec), Mexico, with Central America extending southeastward to South America from this point.[^cite_note-81] The Caribbean islands, or West Indies, are considered part of North America.[^cite_note-britannica-northamerica-74] The continental coastline is long and irregular. The [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md) is the largest body of water indenting the continent, followed by [Hudson Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Bay). Others include the [Gulf of Saint Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Saint_Lawrence) and the [Gulf of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_California).

Before the Central American isthmus formed, the region had been underwater. The islands of the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies) delineate a submerged former [land bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_bridge), which had connected North and South America via what are now [Florida](18933066.md) and [Venezuela](32374.md).

There are several islands off the continent's coasts; principally, the Arctic Archipelago, the [Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamas), [Turks and Caicos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_and_Caicos), the [Greater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Antilles) and [Lesser Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles), the [Aleutian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands) (some of which are in the [Eastern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Hemisphere) proper), the [Alexander Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Archipelago), the many thousand islands of the British Columbia Coast, and Newfoundland. [Greenland](12118.md), a self-governing Danish island, and the [world's largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area), is on the same [tectonic plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_plate) (the [North American Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Plate)) and is part of North America geographically. In a geologic sense, Bermuda is not part of the Americas, but an oceanic island that was formed on the fissure of the [Mid-Atlantic Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_Ridge) over 100 [million years ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Million_years_ago) (mya). The nearest landmass to it is [Cape Hatteras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Hatteras), [North Carolina](21650.md). However, Bermuda is often thought of as part of North America, especially given its historical, political and cultural ties to [Virginia](32432.md) and other parts of the continent.

The vast majority of North America is on the North American Plate. Parts of western Mexico, including Baja California, and of [California](5407.md), including the cities of [San Diego](28504.md), [Los Angeles](18110.md), and [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz,_California), lie on the eastern edge of the [Pacific Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Plate), with the two plates meeting along the [San Andreas Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Andreas_Fault). The southernmost portion of the continent and much of the West Indies lie on the [Caribbean Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Plate), whereas the [Juan de Fuca Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Fuca_Plate) and [Cocos Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_Plate) border the North American Plate on its western frontier.

The continent can be divided into four great regions (each of which contains many subregions): the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) stretching from the Gulf of Mexico to the [Canadian Arctic](79987.md); the geologically young, mountainous west, including the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains), the [Great Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basin), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_California), and [Alaska](624.md); the raised but relatively flat plateau of the Canadian Shield in the northeast; and the varied eastern region, which includes the Appalachian Mountains, the coastal plain along the Atlantic seaboard, and the Florida peninsula. Mexico, with its long [plateaus](538005.md) and [cordilleras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_cordillera), falls largely in the western region, although the eastern coastal plain does extend south along the Gulf.

The western mountains are split in the middle into the main range of the Rockies and the [coast ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Coast_Ranges) in [California](5407.md), [Oregon](26811621.md), [Washington](13015878.md), and [British Columbia](3392.md), with the Great Basin—a lower area containing smaller ranges and low-lying deserts—in between. The highest peak is [Denali](207247.md) (also called Mount McKinley) in Alaska.

The [U.S. Geographical Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Geographical_Survey) (USGS) states that the geographic center of North America is "6 miles \[10 km] west of [Balta, Pierce County, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balta,_North_Dakota)" at about [48°10′N 100°10′W  /  48.167°N 100.167°W  / 48.167; -100.167](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=North_America\&params=48_10_N_100_10_W_), about 24 kilometers (15 mi) from [Rugby, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby,_North_Dakota). The USGS further states that "No marked or monumented point has been established by any government agency as the geographic center of either the 50 states, the conterminous United States, or the North American continent."[^cite_note-82] Nonetheless, there is a 4.6-meter (15 ft) field stone [obelisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obelisk) in Rugby claiming to mark the center. The North American [continental pole of inaccessibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_pole_of_inaccessibility) is located 1,650 km (1,030 mi) from the nearest coastline, between [Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen,_South_Dakota) and [Kyle, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle,_South_Dakota) at [43°22′N 101°58′W  /  43.36°N 101.97°W  / 43.36; -101.97  (Pole of Inaccessibility North America)](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=North_America\&params=43.36_N_101.97_W_\&title=Pole+of+Inaccessibility+North+America).[^cite_note-PIA-83]

### Climate



A Köppen climate classification map of North America

North America is a very large continent that extends from north of the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle) to south of the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer). Greenland, along with the [Canadian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Shield), is [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) with average temperatures ranging from 10 to 20 °C (50 to 68 °F), but central Greenland is composed of a very large ice sheet. This tundra radiates throughout Canada, but its border ends near the Rocky Mountains (but still contains Alaska) and at the end of the Canadian Shield, near the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes). Climate west of the [Cascade Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range) is described as being temperate weather with average precipitation 20 inches (510 millimeters).[^cite_note-88] Climate in coastal California is described to be [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate), with average temperatures in cities like [San Francisco](49728.md) ranging from 57 to 70 °F (14 to 21 °C) over the course of the year.[^cite_note-89]

Stretching from the [East Coast](89126.md) to eastern [North Dakota](21651.md), and stretching down to [Kansas](16716.md), is the [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) featuring intense seasons, with a large amount of annual precipitation, with places like [New York City](645042.md) averaging 50 in (1,300 mm).[^cite_note-90] Starting at the southern border of the humid continental climate and stretching to the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md) (while encompassing the eastern half of Texas) is the [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate). This area has the wettest cities in the [contiguous U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contiguous_U.S.), with annual precipitation reaching 67 in (1,700 mm) in [Mobile, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile,_Alabama).[^cite_note-91] Stretching from the borders of the humid continental and subtropical climates, and going west to the [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada), south to the southern tip of [Durango](222234.md), north to the border with tundra climate, the [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate)/[desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) climates are the driest in the U.S.[^cite_note-92] Highland climates cut from north to south of the continent, where [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics) or [temperate climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate) occur just below the tropics, as in central [Mexico](3966054.md) and [Guatemala](17238567.md). [Tropical climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate) appear in the [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island) regions and in the subcontinent's bottleneck, found in countries and states bathed by the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) or to the south of the Gulf of Mexico and the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md).[^cite_note-93] Precipitation patterns vary across the region, and as such [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate), [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate), and [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) types can be found, with rains and high temperatures throughout the year.

### Geology

Main article: [Geology of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_North_America)

#### Geologic history

[Laurentia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurentia) is an ancient [craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craton) which forms the geologic core of North America; it formed between 1.5 and 1.0 billion years ago during the [Proterozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proterozoic) eon.[^cite_note-94] The [Canadian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Shield) is the largest exposure of this craton. From the Late [Paleozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoic) to Early [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) eras, North America was joined with the other modern-day continents as part of the supercontinent [Pangaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangaea), with [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) to its east. One of the results of the formation of Pangaea was the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains), which [formed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taconic_orogeny) some 480 mya, making it among the oldest mountain ranges in the world. When Pangaea began to rift around 200 mya, North America became part of [Laurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurasia), before it separated from Eurasia as its own continent during the mid-[Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) period.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-95] The Rockies and other western mountain ranges began forming around this time from a period of mountain building called the [Laramide orogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laramide_orogeny), between 80 and 55 mya. The formation of the [Isthmus of Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Panama) that connected the continent to South America arguably occurred approximately 12 to 15 mya,[^cite_note-Origins-96] and the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) (as well as many other northern freshwater lakes and rivers) were carved by receding [glaciers](12463.md) about 10,000 years ago.

North America is the source of much of what humanity knows about [geologic time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologic_time) periods.[^cite_note-dinopedia-american-97] The geographic area that would later become the United States has been the source of more varieties of [dinosaurs](8311.md) than any other modern country.[^cite_note-dinopedia-american-97] According to [paleontologist](23084.md) Peter Dodson, this is primarily due to stratigraphy, climate and geography, human resources, and history.[^cite_note-dinopedia-american-97] Much of the Mesozoic Era is represented by exposed outcrops in the many arid regions of the continent.[^cite_note-dinopedia-american-97] The most significant [Late Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Jurassic) dinosaur-bearing fossil deposit in North America is the [Morrison Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrison_Formation) of the western U.S.[^cite_note-jurassicdistribution-98]

#### Canada

Main article: [Geology of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Canada)



A geologic map of North America published by the [U.S. Geographical Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Geographical_Survey)

[Canada](5042916.md) is geologically one of the oldest regions in the world, with more than half of the region consisting of [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) rocks that have been above sea level since the beginning of the [Palaeozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeozoic) era.[^cite_note-Marianopolis-86] Canada's mineral resources are diverse and extensive.[^cite_note-Marianopolis-86] Across the [Canadian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Shield) and in the north there are large iron, nickel, [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), copper, gold, lead, [molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum), and [uranium](31743.md) reserves. Large diamond concentrations have been recently developed in the Arctic,[^cite_note-99] making Canada one of the world's largest producers. Throughout the Shield, there are many mining towns extracting these minerals. The largest, and best known, is [Sudbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Sudbury), Ontario. Sudbury is an exception to the normal process of forming minerals in the Shield since there is significant evidence that the [Sudbury Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudbury_Basin) is an ancient [meteorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorite) [impact crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_crater). The nearby, but less-known [Temagami Magnetic Anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temagami_Magnetic_Anomaly) has striking similarities to the Sudbury Basin. Its magnetic anomalies are very similar to the Sudbury Basin, and so it could be a second metal-rich impact crater.[^cite_note-GH-100] The Shield is also covered by vast boreal forests that support an important logging industry.

#### United States

Main article: [Geology of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_the_United_States)



A 2003 image of North America's [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock) and terrain



A 2015 map of North America's [cratons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craton) and basement rocks

The United States can be divided into twelve main geological provinces:[^cite_note-101]

1. Pacific

2. [Columbia Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Plateau)

3. [Basin and Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basin_and_Range_Province)

4. [Colorado Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Plateau)

5. [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_the_Rocky_Mountains)

6. [Laurentian Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurentian_Upland)

7. [Interior Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_Plains)

8. [Interior Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Interior_Highlands)

9. [Appalachian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_the_Appalachians)

10. [Atlantic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Plain)

11. [Alaskan](624.md)

12. [Hawaiian](13270.md)

Each province has its own geologic history and unique features. The geology of [Alaska](624.md) is typical of that of the cordillera, while the major islands of [Hawaii](13270.md) consist of [Neogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neogene) [volcanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano) erupted over a [hot spot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotspot_\(geology\)).

#### Central America

Main article: [Geology of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Central_America)



[Central America](6121.md) rests on the [Caribbean Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Plate).

[Central America](6121.md) is geologically active with [volcanic eruptions](8921015.md) and earthquakes occurring from time to time. In 1976 Guatemala was hit by [a major earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Guatemala_earthquake), killing 23,000 people; Managua, the capital of Nicaragua, was devastated by earthquakes in 1931 and 1972, the last one killing about 5,000 people; three earthquakes devastated El Salvador, one in 1986 and two in 2001; one [earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Costa_Rica_earthquake) devastated northern and central Costa Rica in 2009, killing at least 34 people; in [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Honduras_earthquake) a powerful earthquake killed seven people in 2009.

[Volcanic eruptions](8921015.md) are common in the region. In 1968, the [Arenal Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arenal_Volcano), in Costa Rica, erupted and killed 87 people. Fertile soils from weathered volcanic lavas have made it possible to sustain dense populations in agriculturally productive highland areas.

Central America has many [mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range); the longest are the [Sierra Madre de Chiapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_de_Chiapas), the [Cordillera Isabelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Isabelia), and the [Cordillera de Talamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_de_Talamanca). Between the mountain ranges lie fertile valleys that are suitable for the people; in fact, most of the population of Honduras, Costa Rica, and Guatemala live in valleys. Valleys are also suitable for the production of coffee, beans, and other crops.

North America's GDP per capita was evaluated in October 2016 by the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF) to be \$41,830, making it the richest continent in the world,[^cite_note-IMFcapitaGDP-118] followed by [Oceania](22621.md).[^cite_note-cia-us-119]

Canada, Mexico, and the U.S. have significant and multifaceted economic systems. The U.S. has the largest economy in the world.[^cite_note-cia-us-119] In 2016, the U.S. had an estimated per capita gross domestic product (PPP) of \$57,466 according to the [World Bank](36752.md), and is the most technologically developed economy of the three.[^cite_note-120] The U.S.'s services sector comprises 77% of the country's GDP (estimated in 2010), industry comprises 22% and agriculture comprises 1.2%.[^cite_note-cia-us-119] The U.S. economy is also the fastest-growing economy in North America and the Americas as a whole,[^cite_note-IMFcapitaGDP-118][^cite_note-121] with the highest GDP per capita in the Americas as well.[^cite_note-IMFcapitaGDP-118]

Canada shows significant growth in the sectors of services, mining and manufacturing.[^cite_note-cia-ca-122] Canada's per capita GDP (PPP) was estimated at \$44,656 and it had the 11th-largest GDP (nominal) in 2014.[^cite_note-cia-ca-122] Canada's services sector comprises 78% of the country's GDP (estimated in 2010), industry comprises 20% and agriculture comprises 2%.[^cite_note-cia-ca-122] Mexico has a per capita GDP (PPP) of \$16,111 and as of 2014 is the 15th-largest GDP (nominal) in the world.[^cite_note-imf-mx-123] Being a [newly industrialized country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialized_country),[^cite_note-AIA-124] Mexico maintains both modern and outdated industrial and agricultural facilities and operations.[^cite_note-cia-mex-125] Its main sources of income are oil, industrial exports, manufactured goods, electronics, heavy industry, automobiles, construction, food, banking and financial services.[^cite_note-126]

The North American economy is well defined and structured in three main economic areas.[^cite_note-UANL-127] These areas are those under the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA), the [Caribbean Community and Common Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Community_and_Common_Market) (CARICOM), and the [Central American Common Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Common_Market) (CACM).[^cite_note-UANL-127] Of these trade blocs, the U.S. takes part in two. In addition to the larger trade blocs there is the [Canada-Costa Rica Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada-Costa_Rica_Free_Trade_Agreement) among numerous other [free-trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-trade_area) relations, often between the larger, more developed countries and Central American and Caribbean countries.



[Mexican President](24356889.md) [Enrique Peña Nieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Pe%C3%B1a_Nieto), [U.S. President](24113.md) [Donald Trump](4848272.md), and [Canadian Prime Minister](24135.md) [Justin Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Trudeau) sign the [U.S.–Mexico–Canada Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Mexico%E2%80%93Canada_Agreement) during the [2018 G20 Buenos Aires summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_G20_Buenos_Aires_summit)

NAFTA formed one of the four largest trade blocs in the world.[^cite_note-128] Its implementation in 1994 was designed for economic homogenization with hopes of eliminating barriers of trade and foreign investment between Canada, the U.S. and Mexico.[^cite_note-129] While Canada and the U.S. already conducted the largest bilateral trade relationship—and to present day still do—in the world and [Canada–U.S. trade relations](17206485.md) already allowed trade without national taxes and tariffs,[^cite_note-130] NAFTA allowed Mexico to experience a similar [duty-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duty-free) trade. The [free-trade agreement](2533562.md) allowed for the elimination of tariffs that had previously been in place on U.S.–Mexico trade. Trade volume has steadily increased annually and in 2010, surface trade between the three NAFTA nations reached an all-time historical increase of 24.3% or US\$791 billion.[^cite_note-131] The NAFTA trade bloc GDP (PPP) is the world's largest with US\$17.617 trillion.[^cite_note-132] This is in part attributed to the fact that the economy of the U.S. is the world's largest national economy; the country had a nominal GDP of approximately \$14.7 trillion in 2010.[^cite_note-bea2011-133] The countries of NAFTA are also some of each other's largest trade partners. The U.S. is the largest trade partner of Canada and Mexico,[^cite_note-134] while Canada and Mexico are each other's third-largest trade partners.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] In 2018, the NAFTA was replaced by the [U.S.–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Mexico%E2%80%93Canada_Agreement).

The Caribbean trade bloc (CARICOM) came into agreement in 1973 when it was signed by 15 Caribbean nations. As of 2000, CARICOM trade volume was US\$96 billion. CARICOM also allowed for the creation of a [common passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM_passport) for associated nations. In the past decade the trade bloc focused largely on free-trade agreements and under the CARICOM Office of Trade Negotiations free-trade agreements have been signed into effect.

Integration of Central American economies occurred under the signing of the Central American Common Market agreement in 1961; this was the first attempt to engage the nations of this area into stronger financial cooperation. The 2006 implementation of the Central American Free Trade Agreement (CAFTA) left the future of the CACM unclear.[^cite_note-137] The Central American Free Trade Agreement was signed by five Central American countries, the Dominican Republic, and the U.S. The focal point of CAFTA is to create a free trade area similar to that of NAFTA. In addition to the U.S., Canada also has relations in Central American trade blocs.

These nations also take part in inter-continental trade blocs. Mexico takes a part in the [G3 Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G3_Free_Trade_Agreement) with Colombia and Venezuela and has a trade agreement with the EU. The U.S. has proposed and maintained trade agreements under the [Transatlantic Free Trade Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_Free_Trade_Area) between itself and the [European Union](9317.md); the [U.S.–Middle East Free Trade Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Middle_East_Free_Trade_Area) between numerous Middle Eastern nations and itself; and the [Trans-Pacific Strategic Economic Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Pacific_Strategic_Economic_Partnership) between Southeast Asian nations, Australia, and New Zealand.

### Transport

Main article: [Transportation in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_North_America)



A 2006 map of the North American [Class I railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_I_railroad) network

The [Pan-American Highway](301108.md) route in the Americas is the portion of a network of roads nearly 48,000 km (30,000 mi) in length which travels through the mainland nations. No definitive length of the Pan-American Highway exists because the U.S. and Canadian governments have never officially defined any specific routes as being part of the Pan-American Highway, and Mexico officially has many branches connecting to the U.S. border. However, the total length of the portion from Mexico to the northern extremity of the highway is roughly 26,000 km (16,000 mi).

The first transcontinental railroad in the U.S. was built in the 1860s, linking the railroad network of the eastern U.S. with California on the Pacific coast. Finished on 10 May 1869 at the famous [golden spike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_spike) event at [Promontory Summit, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promontory_Summit,_Utah), it created a nationwide mechanized transportation network that revolutionized the population and economy of the [American western frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_frontier), catalyzing the transition from the [wagon trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon_train) of previous decades to a modern transportation system.[^cite_note-WDL-138] Although an accomplishment, it achieved the status of first transcontinental railroad by connecting myriad eastern U.S. railroads to the Pacific and was not the largest single railroad system in the world. The Canadian [Grand Trunk Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Railway) had, by 1867, already accumulated more than 2,055 km (1,277 mi) of track by connecting Ontario with the Canadian Atlantic provinces west as far as [Port Huron, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Huron,_Michigan), through [Sarnia, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnia,_Ontario).

Non-native nations' control and claims over North America, c. 1750 to 2008

Canada and the United States are the wealthiest and most [developed nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) on the continent followed by Mexico, a [newly industrialized country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialized_countries).[^cite_note-AIA-124] The countries of Central America and the Caribbean are at various levels of economic and human development. For example, small Caribbean island-nations, such as Barbados, Trinidad and Tobago, and Antigua and Barbuda, have a higher [GDP (PPP) per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)_per_capita) than Mexico due to their smaller populations. Panama and Costa Rica have a significantly higher [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_Human_Development_Index) and GDP than the rest of the Central American nations.[^cite_note-UNDP-139] Additionally, despite Greenland's vast resources in oil and minerals, much of them remain untapped, and the island is economically dependent on fishing, tourism, and subsidies from Denmark. Nevertheless, the island is highly developed.[^cite_note-140]

Demographically, North America is ethnically diverse. Its three largest groups are [Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people), [Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo), and [Blacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people).[^cite_note-Composed-141] There is a significant minority of Indigenous Americans and [Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people) among other less numerous groups.[^cite_note-Composed-141]

### Languages



[Chinatown, Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatowns_in_Queens) in [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens), [New York City](645042.md) has become the present-day global epicenter receiving [Chinese immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_emigration) as well as the international control center directing such migration, as numerous languages have become entrenched into North American society.

Main article: [Languages of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_North_America)

The dominant languages in North America are English, Spanish, and French. Danish is prevalent in Greenland alongside [Greenlandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_language), and Dutch is spoken side by side local languages in the [Dutch Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles). The term Anglo-America is used to refer to the [anglophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) countries of the Americas: namely Canada (where English and French are co-official) and the U.S., but also sometimes Belize and parts of the tropics, especially the [Commonwealth Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Caribbean). Latin America refers to the other areas of the Americas (generally south of the U.S.) where the [Romance languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_language), derived from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language), of Spanish and [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), (but French-speaking countries are not usually included) predominate: the other republics of Central America (but not always Belize), part of the Caribbean (not the Dutch-, English-, or French-speaking areas), Mexico, and most of South America (except [Guyana](17238691.md), [Suriname](26828.md), [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) \[France], and the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md) \[UK]).

The U.S. has an [ethnically diverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnically_diverse) population, and 37 ancestry groups have more than one million members.[^cite_note-An2000-144] The French language has historically played a significant role in North America and now retains a distinctive presence in some regions. Canada is officially bilingual. French is the official language of the province of [Quebec](7954867.md), where 95% of the people speak it as either their first or second language, and it is co-official with English in the province of [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick). Other French-speaking locales include the province of [Ontario](22218.md) (the official language is English, but there are an estimated 600,000 Franco-Ontarians), the province of [Manitoba](18926.md) (co-official as *[de jure](43594.md)* with English), the [French West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Indies) and [Saint-Pierre et Miquelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Pierre_et_Miquelon), as well as the U.S. state of [Louisiana](18130.md), where French is also an official language. Haiti is included with this group based on historical association but Haitians speak both [Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Creole_language) and French. Similarly, French and French Antillean Creole is spoken in Saint Lucia and the [Commonwealth of Dominica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Dominica) alongside English.

### Religions

Main article: [Religion in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_North_America)

See also: [Religions of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religions_of_the_world)



The percentage of people who identify with a religion in North America, according to 2010–2012 data

[Christianity](5211.md) is the largest religion in the United States, Canada, and Mexico. According to a 2012 [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) survey, 77% of the population considered themselves [Christians](18337522.md).[^cite_note-149] Christianity also is the predominant religion [in the 23 dependent territories in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_and_dependent_territories_in_North_America).[^cite_note-150] The U.S. has the [largest Christian population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_by_country) in the world, with nearly 247 million Christians (70%), although other countries have higher percentages of Christians among their populations.[^cite_note-151] Mexico has the world's second-largest number of Catholics, surpassed only by [Brazil](3383.md).[^cite_note-152]

According to the same study, the [religiously unaffiliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) (including [agnostics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnostic) and [atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist)) make up about 17% of the population of Canada and the U.S.[^cite_note-Religiously_Unaffiliated-153] Those with no religious affiliation make up about 24% of Canada's total population.[^cite_note-religion2011-154]

Canada, the U.S., and Mexico host communities of [Jews](25955086.md) (6 million or about 1.8%),[^cite_note-155] [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists) (3.8 million or 1.1%)[^cite_note-156] and [Muslims](19541.md) (3.4 million or 1.0%).[^cite_note-157] The largest number of Jews can be found in the U.S. (5.4 million),[^cite_note-158] Canada (375,000)[^cite_note-159] and Mexico (67,476).[^cite_note-INEGI-160] The U.S. hosts the largest Muslim population in North America with 2.7 million or 0.9%,[^cite_note-pew2015-161][^cite_note-Islam_in_the_United_States-162] while Canada hosts about one million Muslims or 3.2% of the population.[^cite_note-163] In Mexico there were 3,700 Muslims in 2010.[^cite_note-2010-census-164] In 2012, *[U-T San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-T_San_Diego)* estimated U.S. practitioners of Buddhism at 1.2 million people, of whom 40% are living in [Southern California](62520.md).[^cite_note-165]

The predominant religion in Mexico and Central America is Christianity (96%).[^cite_note-Christianity_in_its_Global_Context-166] Beginning with the Spanish colonization of Mexico in the 16th century, [Roman Catholicism](606848.md) was the only religion permitted by Spanish crown and Catholic church. A vast campaign of religious conversion, the so-called "spiritual conquest", was launched to bring the indigenous peoples into the Christian fold. The [Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Inquisition) was established to assure orthodox belief and practice. The Catholic Church remained an important institution, so that even after political independence, Roman Catholicism remained the dominant religion. Since the 1960s, there has been an increase in other Christian groups, particularly [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), as well as other religious organizations, and individuals identifying themselves as having no religion. Christianity is also the predominant religion in the Caribbean (85%).[^cite_note-Christianity_in_its_Global_Context-166] Other religious groups in the region are [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Islam](6037917.md), [Rastafari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastafari) (in Jamaica), and [Afro-American religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-American_religions) such as [Santería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santer%C3%ADa) and [Vodou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Vodou).

North America is the [fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_continents_by_population) most populous continent after Asia, Africa, and Europe.[^cite_note-167] Its most populous country is the U.S. with 342.9 million persons. The second-largest country is Mexico with a population of 126 million.[^cite_note-168] Canada is the third-most-populous country with 41.5 million.[^cite_note-169] The majority of Caribbean island-nations have national populations under a million, though Cuba, Dominican Republic, Haiti, Puerto Rico (a territory of the U.S.), Jamaica, and Trinidad and Tobago each have populations higher than a million.[^cite_note-cubastat-171][^cite_note-prez-172][^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] Greenland has a small population of 55,984 for its massive size (2.166 million km<sup>2</sup> or 836,300 mi<sup>2</sup>), and therefore, it has the world's lowest population density at 0.026 pop./km<sup>2</sup> (0.067 pop./mi<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-176]

While the U.S., Canada, and Mexico maintain the largest populations, large city populations are not restricted to those nations. There are also large cities in the [Caribbean](18956035.md). The largest cities in North America, by far, are Mexico City and New York City. These cities are the only cities on the continent to exceed eight million, and two of three in the Americas. Next in size are Los Angeles, [Toronto](64646.md),[^cite_note-177] Chicago, Havana, Santo Domingo, and [Montreal](7954681.md). Cities in the [Sun Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Belt) regions of the U.S., such as those in Southern California and [Houston](13774.md), [Phoenix](49121.md), Miami, [Atlanta](3138.md), and [Las Vegas](47737.md), are experiencing rapid growth. These causes included warm temperatures, retirement of [Baby Boomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_Boomers), large industry, and the influx of immigrants. Cities near the U.S. border, particularly in Mexico, are also experiencing large amounts of growth. Most notable is [Tijuana](101739.md), a city bordering San Diego that receives immigrants from all over Latin America and parts of Europe and Asia. Yet as cities grow in these warmer regions of North America, they are increasingly forced to deal with the major issue of [water shortages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_shortages).[^cite_note-178]

Eight of the top ten [metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) are [located in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) These metropolitan areas all have a population of above 5.5 million and include the [New York City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_metropolitan_area), [Los Angeles metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_metropolitan_area), [Chicago metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_metropolitan_area), and the [Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas%E2%80%93Fort_Worth_metroplex).[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-179] While the majority of the largest metropolitan areas are within the U.S., Mexico is host to the largest metropolitan area by population in North America: [Greater Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Mexico_City).[^cite_note-180] Canada also breaks into the top ten largest metropolitan areas with the [Toronto metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_metropolitan_area) having six million people.[^cite_note-statcan2006-181] The proximity of cities to each other on the [Canada–United States border](630260.md) and the [Mexico–U.S. border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93U.S._border) has led to the rise of international metropolitan areas. These [urban agglomerations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomerations) are observed at their largest and most productive in [Detroit–Windsor](7598140.md) and [San Diego–Tijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego%E2%80%93Tijuana) and experience large commercial, economic, and cultural activity. The metropolitan areas are responsible for millions of dollars of trade dependent on international freight. In Detroit-Windsor the Border Transportation Partnership study in 2004 concluded [US\$](18717338.md)13 billion was dependent on the Detroit–Windsor international border crossing while in San Diego–Tijuana freight at the [Otay Mesa Port of Entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otay_Mesa_Port_of_Entry) was valued at US\$20 billion.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-Planning_SD-TJ-183]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_North_America)



[Baseball](3850.md) is known as the national pastime of the United States, and is also played in Canada and many Latin American countries.



[The Nine Nations of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nine_Nations_of_North_America)

The cultures of North America are diverse. The United States and English Canada have many cultural similarities, while [French Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canada) has a distinct culture from [Anglophone Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone_Canada) that is protected by law. Since the United States was formed from portions previously part of the Spanish Empire and then independent Mexico, and since there has been considerable and continuing immigration of Spanish speakers from south of the U.S.–Mexico border, the [Southwest of the U.S.](179571.md) exhibits significant Hispanic cultural elements and traditions and considerable bilingualism. Mexico and Central America are part of [Latin America](18524.md) and are culturally distinct from anglophone and francophone North America. However, they share with the United States the establishment of post-independence governments that are federated representative republics with written constitutions dating from their founding as nations. Canada is a federated parliamentary democracy under a constitutional monarchy.

Canada's [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada) dates to 1867, with confederation, in the British North America Act, but not until 1982 did Canada have the power to amend its own constitution. Canada's Francophone heritage has been enshrined in law since the British parliament passed the [Quebec Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Act) of 1774. In contrast to largely Protestant Anglo settlers in North America, French-speaking Canadians were Catholic. The Quebec Act guaranteed the freedom of Catholic Francophone Canadians to practice their religion, restored the right of the Catholic Church to impose [tithes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tithes) for its support, and established French [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) in most circumstances.

The distinctiveness of French language and culture has been codified in Canadian law, so that both English and French are designated official languages. The U.S. has no official language, but its national language is English.

The Canadian government took action to protect [Canadian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_culture) by limiting non-[Canadian content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_content) in broadcasting, creating the [Canadian Radio and Telecommunications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Radio_and_Telecommunications_Commission) to monitor Canadian content. In Quebec, the provincial government established the [Quebec Office of the French Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Office_of_the_French_Language), often called the "language police" by Anglophones, which mandates the use of French terminology and signage in French.[^cite_note-184] Since 1968 the unicameral legislature has been called the [Quebec National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_National_Assembly). [Saint-Jean-Baptiste Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Jean-Baptiste_Day), 24 June, is the national holiday of Quebec and is celebrated by francophone Canadians throughout Canada. In Quebec, the [school system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Quebec) was divided into Catholic and Protestant, so-called confessional schools. Anglophone education in Quebec has been increasingly undermined.[^cite_note-185]

[Latino culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_culture) is strong in the [southwestern United States](179571.md), as well as in the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area) and [Florida](18933066.md), which draw Latin Americans from many countries in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md). [Northern Mexico](48157875.md), particularly in the cities of [Monterrey](40455.md), Tijuana, [Ciudad Juárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Ju%C3%A1rez), and [Mexicali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicali), is strongly influenced by the [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_States) and [way of life of the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_way) Monterrey, a modern city with a significant industrial group, has been regarded as the most [Americanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americanized) city in Mexico.[^cite_note-MonterreyFall-186]

The [Anglophone Caribbean states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone_Caribbean) have witnessed and participated in the decline of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) and its influence on the region, as well as the increase in the economic influence of [Northern America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_America) over the Anglophone Caribbean. This is partly due to the relatively small populations of the English-speaking Caribbean countries, and also because many of them now have more people [living abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate) than those remaining at home.[^cite_note-187]

[Greenland](12118.md) has experienced many immigration waves from [Northern Canada](79987.md), e.g. by the [Thule people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_people). Therefore, Greenland shares some cultural ties with the [indigenous peoples of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Canada). Greenland is also considered [Nordic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries) and has strong Danish ties due to [centuries of colonization by Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_colonization_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-188]

### Sport

Main article: [Sport in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_North_America)

The United States and Canada have major sports teams that compete against each other, including baseball, basketball, hockey, and soccer/football. Canada, Mexico, and the United States [jointly hosted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America_2026_FIFA_World_Cup_bid) the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).

The Native American game of [lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse_in_Canada) is considered a national sport in Canada. [Curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling) is an important winter sport in Canada, and the Winter Olympics includes it in the roster. The English sport of [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_Canada) is popular in parts of anglophone Canada and very popular in parts of the former British empire, but in Canada is considered a minor sport. Boxing is also a major sport in some countries, such as Mexico, Panama and Puerto Rico, and it is considered one of the main individual sports in the United States. Canada has a separate [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) from the U.S. teams.
