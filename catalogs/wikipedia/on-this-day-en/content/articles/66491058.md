**ShinyHunters** is a [black-hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-hat) criminal [hacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker) and [extortion](162449.md) group that has been active since 2019[^cite_note-Newman-2][^cite_note-Cimpanu-3] and is said to have been involved in a significant number of [data breaches](13307577.md).[^cite_note-Newman-2]

ShinyHunters has utilized [cloud](19541494.md) misconfigurations, [OAuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OAuth) token theft via integration companies, supply chain attacks, [zero-day exploits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-day_vulnerability),[^cite_note-4] [voice phishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_phishing) and other forms of advanced [social engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_engineering_\(security\)) to gain access to the systems of its targets. After gaining access to a target, the group has been known to [exfiltrate data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_exfiltration) and demand ransom payments. If targets do not pay the ransom, the stolen information is often leaked or sold on the [dark web](40712897.md).[^cite_note-Newman-2][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-Wired2026-1] ShinyHunters is believed to be affiliated with [The Com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Com), a large international network of [cybercriminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybercriminals).[^cite_note-Wired2026-1]

## Identity and membership

The name of the group is believed to be derived from [Shiny Pokémon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiny_Pok%C3%A9mon), an aspect of the [*Pokémon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_\(video_game_series\)) video game franchise whereby Pokémon have a rare chance of being encountered in an alternate, "shiny" color scheme; actively trying to collect such Pokémon through in-game strategies are often referred to as "shiny hunting".[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

There has been at least one instance of conflict over the legitimacy of the ShinyHunters name. The April 2026 breach of [Vercel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vercel) was carried out by an entity that claimed to be ShinyHunters, but the leadership associated with the real ShinyHunters denied that ShinyHunters was involved.[^cite_note-8]

ShinyHunters' membership is believed to partially overlap with other groups linked to international cybercrime network The Com, including [Scattered Spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattered_Spider) and [Lapsus\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapsus\$).[^cite_note-9] Cybercrime group [Scattered Lapsus\$ Hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattered_Lapsus\$_Hunters) claims to include members of all three groups.[^cite_note-\:0-10] One of three administrators of the SLSH Telegram channel, operating under the alias "Rey," was publicly identified in November 2025 as a 15-year-old from Jordan.[^cite_note-\:0-10] [Google](1092923.md)-owned cybersecurity firm [Mandiant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandiant) described ShinyHunters as "multiple threat clusters" operating under a single brand in January 2026,[^cite_note-\:1-11] and a February 2026 analysis by [Kim Zetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Zetter) describes ShinyHunters and its peer groups as "\[l]oose-knit cells" of The Com.[^cite_note-12]

## Legal actions

In May 2022, Sébastien Raoult, a French programmer suspected of belonging to the group, was arrested in Morocco and extradited to the United States. He faced 20 to 116 years in prison.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In January 2024, Raoult was sentenced to three years in prison and ordered to return \$5 million.[^cite_note-Jones-15] Twelve months of the sentence are for conspiracy to commit wire fraud and the remainder for aggravated [identity theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_theft).[^cite_note-Jones-15] He will face 36 months of supervised release afterwards.[^cite_note-Jones-15] Raoult had worked for the group for more than two years according to the [U.S. Attorney's Office for the Western District of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney_for_the_Western_District_of_Washington), but was not a major player within the group.[^cite_note-Jones-15]

In May-June 2025, U.S. prosecutors in the [District of Massachusetts](915466.md) charged Matthew D. Lane, a 19-year-old Massachusetts student, with hacking and extorting an education-technology provider widely reported to be [PowerSchool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PowerSchool). Prosecutors said that Lane used stolen contractor credentials to access the company's network in 2024, exfiltrate data on tens of millions of students and teachers, and demand a \$2.85 million [bitcoin](28249265.md) ransom. Lane agreed to plead guilty on May 20, 2025, and entered a guilty plea on June 6, 2025.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] In November 13, 2025, he was sentenced to four years in prison, three years of supervised release, a fine of \$25,000, restitution of \$14,075,540.58 and forfeiture.[^cite_note-18]

On June 25, 2025, French authorities announced that four members of the ShinyHunters cybercriminal group were arrested in multiple French regions for cybercrime activities. The coordinated global law enforcement effort targeted the "ShinyHunters", "Hollow", "Noct", and "Depressed" aliases.[^cite_note-19] However, the leadership remained active throughout and after the arrests.

  [^cite_note-Newman-2]

  [^cite_note-20]

- [Microsoft](19001.md): In May 2020, ShinyHunters also claimed to have stolen over 500 GB of Microsoft source code from the company's private [GitHub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitHub) account. Some cybersecurity experts doubted the claims until analyzing the code; upon analysis, ShinyHunters' claims were no longer in question. Microsoft told *Wired* in a statement that they are aware of the breach. Microsoft later secured its GitHub account, which was confirmed by ShinyHunters as they reported being unable to access any repositories.

## Snowflake data hacks

Main article: [Snowflake data breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake_data_breach)

In 2024, ShinyHunters claimed to have [hacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake_data_breach) [Snowflake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake_Inc.)-related customers including [Ticketmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticketmaster), [Santander Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_Bank), [Neiman Marcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neiman_Marcus), and many others.[^cite_note-hackers-detail-how-43] The group was also responsible for publishing data stolen from [Twilio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilio) and [Truist Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truist_Bank).[^cite_note-107]

In 2026, ShinyHunters executed another widespread data theft of [Snowflake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake_Inc.)-related customers through the third-party integrator Anodot.[^cite_note-108] Snowflake, Inc. confirmed the incident and is actively notifying potentially impacted customers. Subsequently, ShinyHunters is extorting "more than a dozen" companies that were affected in return to not publish the data. Google's threat intelligence group [Mandiant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandiant) confirmed that they are tracking the case.

## Salesforce data hacks

### Round 1

On June 4, 2025, ShinyHunters was tied to a widespread data-theft campaign targeting [Salesforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salesforce) cloud customers, which Google's Threat Intelligence team tracked as UNC6040.[^cite_note-cloud.google.com-55] ShinyHunters, working in conjunction with [Scattered Spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattered_Spider)[^cite_note-109] (now believed to be the same group) and [Lapsus\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapsus\$) (also now believed to be the same group or a part of), impersonated IT support staff and used [voice phishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_phishing) calls to trick employees into installing a malicious version of Salesforce's Data Loader tool, allowing them to access and extract sensitive customer data by abusing OAuth to bypass traditional authentication methods.[^cite_note-110] Following the successful intrusions, Google's Threat Intelligence team notes the victims of these intrusions receive an extortion or ransom email from ShinyHunters, which is also tracked as UNC6240.[^cite_note-cloud.google.com-55]

This sophisticated social engineering approach led to confirmed data breaches at major companies including [Google](1092923.md), [Cisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco), [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas), [Qantas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qantas), [Allianz Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz_Life), [Farmers Insurance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmers_Insurance_Group), [Workday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workday,_Inc.), [Pandora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandora_\(jewelry\)), [Chanel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanel), [TransUnion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransUnion), [LVMH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LVMH) subsidiaries, including but not limited to [Dior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dior), [Louis Vuitton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Vuitton), and [Tiffany & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_&_Co.)[^cite_note-Abrams-54]

### Round 2

Shortly after, on August 28, 2025, another campaign tracked by Google Threat Intelligence (formerly Mandiant) as UNC6395 used OAuth/refresh tokens stolen from Salesloft's Drift integration to access numerous Salesforce customer orgs between August 8–18, 2025, systematically exporting CRM data and hunting for credentials (e.g., AWS access keys, passwords, Snowflake tokens).[^cite_note-111] Google told reporters it was aware of over 700 potentially impacted organizations. Public disclosures tied to this campaign include [Cloudflare](32760230.md), [Workiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workiva), [Zscaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zscaler), [Tenable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenable,_Inc.), [CyberArk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CyberArk), [Elastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticsearch), [BeyondTrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeyondTrust), [Proofpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proofpoint), JFrog, [Rubrik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubrik), [Cato Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Networks), and [Palo Alto Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto_Networks), each confirming unauthorized access to data in their Salesforce environments following the Salesloft/Drift compromise.[^cite_note-112] ShinyHunters claimed responsibility to the press.[^cite_note-1.5B-Salesforce-records-113]

On September 17, 2025, *[BleepingComputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeping_Computer)* was able to confirm ShinyHunters was behind the UNC6395 campaign, the biggest SaaS compromise in history.[^cite_note-1.5B-Salesforce-records-113] ShinyHunters told *BleepingComputer* that the threat actors used the TruffleHog security tool to scan the source code for secrets, which resulted in the finding of OAuth tokens for the [Salesloft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salesloft) Drift and the Drift Email platforms. Using these stolen Drift OAuth tokens, the threat actors stole approximately 1.5 billion data records for 760 companies from the "Account", "Contact", "Case", "Opportunity", and "User" Salesforce object tables.

### Round 3

Three months later, on November 20, 2025, another campaign tracked by Google Threat Intelligence Group as UNC6395-adjacent actors known as ShinyHunters used OAuth/refresh tokens stolen from Gainsight Salesforce integration to access numerous customer instances.[^cite_note-techcrunch.com-114]

Salesforce publicly reported detecting unusual activity related to applications published by Gainsight that were connected to its platform, leading Salesforce to revoke OAuth access and refresh tokens and temporarily remove related applications from its AppExchange while the investigation was ongoing.[^cite_note-115]

The security incident bore extreme similarity to an earlier ShinyHunters-linked Salesloft Drift breach in August 2025, in which attackers stole OAuth tokens from the Salesloft integration known as Drift, enabling unauthorized access and data theft exfiltration of 760 customer Salesforce instances.[^cite_note-1.5B-Salesforce-records-113]

According to external reporting and industry analysis in a statement, Austin Larsen, the principal threat analyst of Google Threat Intelligence Group (formerly Mandiant), said that the company "is aware of more than 200 potentially affected Salesforce instances." Which lined up with what ShinyHunters told several media outlets specifically 285 Salesforce instances.[^cite_note-116] The hacking group claimed responsibility for hacks affecting [Atlassian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlassian), [Docusign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docusign), [F5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F5,_Inc.), [GitLab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitLab_Inc.), [Linkedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn), [Malwarebytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwarebytes), [SonicWall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SonicWall), [Thomson Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_Reuters), [Verizon](18619278.md), and more.[^cite_note-techcrunch.com-114]

### Round 4

On March 7, 2026, Salesforce released another security advisory linking a "known threat group" to exploiting misconfigurations in their Salesforce Experience Cloud software[^cite_note-117] also known as Salesforce Aura using a modified version of Google-owned [AuraInspector](https://github.com/google/aura-inspector) to scan for misconfigurations along with a custom developed tool by the "known threat group" to exploit and extract data from Experience Cloud misconfigurations.[^cite_note-118]

Two days later, on March 9, 2026, ShinyHunters claimed responsibility for these data theft hacks on their data leak site warning affected victims to pay up or their data would be leaked.[^cite_note-Toulas-119] One IOC listed in the exploitation shared a similar User-Agent seen in ShinyHunters' Snowflake data theft attacks "RapeFlake" while in this case it was "RapeForce".[^cite_note-Toulas-119]

The hacking group claimed to have breached about 400 companies affecting [Snowflake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake_Inc.), [Okta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okta,_Inc.), [Lastpass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LastPass), [Salesforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salesforce) itself, [Sony](26989.md), [AMD](2400.md), and "a lot more".[^cite_note-120]

Around the same time, the ShinyHunters also [hacked the antivirus company Aura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aura_data_breach) via social engineering.[^cite_note-121]

## Mixpanel data hacks

In November 2025, ShinyHunters was linked to a third-party analytics breach at [Mixpanel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixpanel). It affected multiple high-profile companies including Pornhub and OpenAI.[^cite_note-Cybernews-122]

Threat actors exploited a [smishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smishing)-based compromise of Mixpanel systems, resulting in the export of analytics-related datasets belonging to several customers. ShinyHunters subsequently leveraged this access to extort organizations, claiming to possess analytics records tied to platforms such as Pornhub's Premium service and, indirectly, data associated with OpenAI's API user interactions.[^cite_note-BC_Pornhub-71][^cite_note-123]

Both OpenAI and Pornhub confirmed that this breach was not a result of their own systems compromised but rather the third-party analytics breach at Mixpanel. Since then OpenAI does not use the analytics provider anymore.[^cite_note-Cybernews-122]

## Okta/SSO data hacks

In January 2026, ShinyHunters was linked by multiple media and threat-intelligence firms to a series of social-engineering campaigns targeted enterprise single sign-on (SSO) environments, including [Okta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okta,_Inc.). The attacks relied on voice-phishing ("vishing") and credential-harvesting infrastructure rather than exploitation of vulnerabilities in Okta's software, according to Okta and multiple security researchers.

According to a report by *[BleepingComputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeping_Computer)*, the ShinyHunters group claimed responsibility for a wave of voice-phishing ("vishing") campaigns that tricked employees into divulging their SSO credentials and multi-factor authentication codes. These credentials were subsequently used to access enterprise SSO dashboards and harvest data from connected software-as-a-service (SaaS) platforms for extortion purposes.[^cite_note-124]

Okta itself publicly warned of active attacks in which threat actors used custom phishing kits and voice-based social engineering to steal SSO credentials, including Okta logins, and abuse those credentials to access cloud applications and exfiltrate data. Okta noted that these attacks did not exploit inherent vulnerabilities in its products, but instead leveraged sophisticated phishing techniques against individual users.[^cite_note-125]

Threat-intelligence analysis published by Google Cloud's Threat Intelligence Group (formerly Mandiant) described how activity consistent with prior ShinyHunters-branded operations involved targeted voice-phishing and credential harvesting sites aimed at capturing SSO logins and MFA tokens. Once obtained, attackers could use the compromised SSO access to move laterally into applications such as Salesforce, Microsoft 365, and other enterprise services and then exfiltrate sensitive data. The analysis noted that some of the campaigns overlapped with ShinyHunters-branded activity tracked under multiple threat clusters.[^cite_note-\:1-11][^cite_note-126]

This ongoing data theft campaign, as described by Charles Carmakal, CTO of Mandiant at Google Cloud, employs a sophisticated social engineering approach that has led to data breaches at major companies including, but not limited to, [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), [University of Nevada, Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nevada,_Las_Vegas), [Grubhub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grubhub), [Crunchbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crunchbase), [Betterment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betterment_\(company\)), [Panera Bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panera_Bread), [Match Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_Group), [Tinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinder_\(app\)), [Hinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinge_\(app\)), [OkCupid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OkCupid), [Bumble Inc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumble_Inc.), [Odido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odido), and [Wynn Resorts](1642677.md).

According to a report by Silent Push, previously founded by [FireEye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FireEye), former owners of Mandiant, the ShinyHunters group and their broader collective "[Scattered LAPSUS\$ Hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattered_Lapsus\$_Hunters)" ("SLH" / "SLSH") are actively targeting over 100 high-profile organizations in this campaign.[^cite_note-127]

## Oracle PeopleSoft data hacks

In June 2026, ShinyHunters was linked to a widespread data theft and extortion campaign targeting [Oracle PeopleSoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeopleSoft) servers, which claims to have stolen data from over 100+ organizations across 300+ instances as first reported by [BleepingComputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeping_Computer).[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

ShinyHunters confirmed to BleepingComputer that they are using a "gadget chain" of old and [zero-day vulnerabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-day_vulnerability) to exploit Oracle PeopleSoft servers.

University of Nottingham is believed to be one of the first confirmed victims of this campaign.[^cite_note-BBC-101][^cite_note-Hartley-102]

[Mandiant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandiant) and Google Threat Intelligence Group published a report on June 11, 2026, which corroborated ShinyHunters' claims to have compromised more than 100 organizations. Google said it spotted malicious activity consistent with the exploitation of CVE-2026-35273[^cite_note-130] between May 27 and June 9, and it subsequently notified more than 100 organizations who were potentially compromised.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]
