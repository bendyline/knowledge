**Frederick Jay Ramsdell**[^cite_note-thesis-1] (born December 4, 1960)[^cite_note-NobelPrize.org-2] is an American [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist) and Nobel Prize laureate. As of 2025, he is an advisor at Sonoma Biotherapeutics, a biotechnology company co-founded by him.[^cite_note-NobelPrize.org-2][^cite_note-3]&#x20;

In 2025, he was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](52502.md) with [Mary E. Brunkow](81273907.md) and [Shimon Sakaguchi](48010182.md) for their work in [peripheral tolerance](13780711.md).[^cite_note-Nobel-4]&#x20;

## Early life and education

Ramsdell was born on December 4, 1960, in [Elmhurst, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmhurst,_Illinois).[^cite_note-NobelPrize.org-2]&#x20;

After graduating from [Homestead High School (California)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_High_School_\(California\)) in 1979,[^cite_note-latc-5] Ramsdell initially attended [Foothill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothill_College) because he could not afford a four-year UC education, before transferring to [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego).[^cite_note-6] He received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree with a major in biochemistry and cell biology from UCSD in 1983.[^cite_note-ucsdtoday-7] In the same year, he enrolled at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), as a doctoral student, and studied microbiology and immunology under the mentorship of Sidney Golub, receiving his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1987.[^cite_note-uclanewsroom-8]&#x20;

## Career

Ramsdell qualified for [food stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplemental_Nutrition_Assistance_Program) as a graduate student when he began his fellowship at the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health) for postgraduate training after earning his PhD.[^cite_note-latc-5] After that, he joined the biopharmaceutical company Immunex, where his research focus was [T cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T_cell) activation and tolerance, and gene discovery.[^cite_note-uclanewsroom-8][^cite_note-cbschicago-9] In 1994, he joined the [Bothell, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bothell,_Washington)-based biotechnology company Darwin Molecular (along with [Mary E. Brunkow](81273907.md)), where he established an immunology program. Darwin Molecular was acquired by [Chiroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiroscience) in 1996. Three years later, Chiroscience merged with [Celltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celltech) (briefly under the name Celltech Chiroscience). In 2004, Ramsdell and Brunkow left the company.[^cite_note-uclanewsroom-8][^cite_note-geekwire-10]&#x20;

He joined [ZymoGenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZymoGenetics) in 2004 where he led research teams focusing on novel proteins with potential regulatory activity in lymphoid cells.[^cite_note-picibio-11] In 2008, he started working at [Novo Nordisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novo_Nordisk) where he helped establish the firm's Inflammation Research Center in [Seattle](11388236.md) and became leader of the immunobiology group.[^cite_note-picibio-11][^cite_note-cbschicago-9]&#x20;

Later, he served as vice president at [aTyr Pharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATyr_Pharma) in [San Diego](28504.md),[^cite_note-picibio-11] before joining the [Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Institute_for_Cancer_Immunotherapy) in [San Francisco](49728.md), where he was chief scientific officer.[^cite_note-uclanewsroom-8]&#x20;

In 2019, he co-founded the San Francisco-based Sonoma Biotherapeutics with [Jeffrey Bluestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Bluestone), Qizhi Tang and [Alexander Rudensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Rudensky),[^cite_note-natureeisenstein-12] where he held the role of chief scientific officer.[^cite_note-13] As of 2025, he is the chair of the company's scientific advisory board.[^cite_note-sonomanobel-14]&#x20;

In the 1990s, while working at Celltech in Bothell, Washington, Ramsdell and Brunkow studied a strain of mice, called *scurfy*, characterized by serious [autoimmune disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmune_disease), setting out to identify the [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation) responsible for the phenotype. After establishing a candidate region on the [X chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_chromosome) containing approximately 20 potential genes, they identified an [insertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insertion_\(genetics\)) of two [base pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_pair) in a previously unknown gene, which they named *Foxp3*. In 2001, in collaboration with [Hans D. Ochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_D._Ochs), Robert Wildin, and their teams, Ramsdell and Brunkow demonstrated that mutations in the human *[FOXP3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FOXP3)* gene are found in [IPEX syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPEX_syndrome), a rare autoimmune disease.[^cite_note-naturenobel-15][^cite_note-16]&#x20;

## Honors and awards

In 2017, Ramsdell received, jointly with [Shimon Sakaguchi](48010182.md) and [Alexander Rudensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Rudensky), the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) for research in [polyarthritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyarthritis). They were cited for "their discoveries relating to [regulatory T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_T_cell), which counteract harmful immune reactions in arthritis and other autoimmune diseases".[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]&#x20;

Ramsdell was jointly awarded, with Sakaguchi and [Mary E. Brunkow](81273907.md), the 2025 [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](52502.md).[^cite_note-Nobel-4] On the day of the announcement, he was unable to be contacted to receive his prize, as he was hiking [off the grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-the-grid) in [Idaho](14607.md).[^cite_note-19] Ramsdell later told the [BBC](19344654.md) that his first response when he did learn the news from his wife was "I did not!", to which she replied that she had 200 text messages suggesting otherwise.[^cite_note-20] When in Sweden to receive the award, Ramsdell donated the hiking boots he was wearing when he found out he had won the Prize to the [Nobel Prize Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_Museum).[^cite_note-21]&#x20;
