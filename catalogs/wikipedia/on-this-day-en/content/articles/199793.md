**Baja California**,[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] officially the **Free and Sovereign State of Baja California**,[^cite_note-15] is a state in [Mexico](3966054.md). It is the northwesternmost of the [32 federal entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_divisions_of_Mexico) of Mexico. Before becoming a state in 1952, the area was known as the North Territory of Baja California (*Territorio Norte de Baja California*). It has an area of 70,113 square kilometers (27,071 sq mi) (3.57% of the land mass of Mexico) and comprises the northern half of the [Baja California peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California_peninsula), north of the [28th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28th_parallel_north), plus oceanic [Guadalupe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalupe_Island). The mainland portion of the state is bordered on the west by the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), on the east by [Sonora](83750.md) and the northern [Gulf of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_California), on the south by [Baja California Sur](199801.md), and by the north is the [United States](3434750.md) with the [US States](18618239.md) of [California](5407.md) to the north and [Arizona](21883824.md) slightly to the east.&#x20;

The state has an estimated population of 3,769,020 as of 2020,[^cite_note-collapse-Indicadores-5] significantly higher than the sparsely populated Baja California Sur to the south, and similar to [San Diego County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County,_California), to its north. Over 75% of the population lives in [Mexicali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicali) (the state's capital city), [Ensenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensenada,_Baja_California), or [Tijuana](101739.md) (the state's largest city). Other important cities include [San Felipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Felipe,_Baja_California), [Rosarito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosarito), and [Tecate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecate).

Baja California is the 12th-largest state by area in Mexico. Its geography ranges from beaches to forests and deserts. The backbone of the state is the Sierra de Baja California, where [Picacho del Diablo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picacho_del_Diablo), the highest point of the peninsula, is located. This mountain range effectively divides the weather patterns in the state. In the northwest, the weather is semi-dry and [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_\(climate\)). In the narrow center, the weather changes to be more humid due to altitude. It is in this area where a few valleys can be found, such as the [Valle de Guadalupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_de_Guadalupe), the major wine-producing area in Mexico. To the east of the mountain range, the [Sonoran Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoran_Desert) dominates the landscape. In the south, the weather becomes drier and gives way to the [Vizcaíno Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizca%C3%ADno_Desert). The state is also home to numerous islands off both of its shores. Baja California is also home to [Guadalupe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalupe_Island), the westernmost point of Mexico, as well as its northernmost point. The [Coronado Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronado_Islands), [Todos Santos islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Todos_Santos), and [Cedros Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedros_Island) are also on the Pacific shore. On the Gulf of California, the largest island is [Angel de la Guarda Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_de_la_Guarda_Island), separated from the peninsula by the deep and narrow [Canal de Ballenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_de_Ballenas).

## History

### Prehistory and Spanish colonial era



The [word "California"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_California) and its namesake ruler, [Queen Calafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calafia), originate in the 1510 epic *[Las Sergas de Esplandián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Sergas_de_Esplandi%C3%A1n)*, written by [Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garci_Rodr%C3%ADguez_de_Montalvo).

The first people came to the peninsula at least 11,000 years ago. At that time, two main native groups are thought to have been present on the peninsula – the [Cochimí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochim%C3%AD) in the south, and several groups belonging to the [Yuman language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuman_language_family) in the north, including the [Kiliwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiliwa_people), [Paipai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paipai_people), [Kumeyaay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumeyaay), [Cocopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocopa), and [Quechan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechan). These peoples were diverse in their adaptations to the region. The Cochimí of the peninsula's Central Desert were generalized hunter-gatherers who moved frequently; however, the Cochimí on Cedros Island off the west coast developed a strong maritime economy. The Kiliwa, Paipai, and Kumeyaay in the better-watered northwest were also hunter-gatherers, but that region supported denser populations and a more sedentary lifestyle. The Cocopa and Quechan of northeastern Baja California practiced agriculture in the floodplain of the lower [Colorado River](180968.md).

Another group of people was the Guachimis, who came from the north and created much of the [UNESCO World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_World_Heritage)-recognized [Sierra de Guadalupe cave paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_Guadalupe_cave_paintings). Not much is known about them except that they lived in the area between 100 BC and 1300 AD.[^cite_note-16]

Europeans reached the present state of Baja California in 1539, when [Francisco de Ulloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Ulloa) [reconnoitered](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/reconnoitered) its east coast on the Gulf of California and explored the peninsula's west coast at least as far north as Cedros Island. [Hernando de Alarcón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernando_de_Alarc%C3%B3n) returned to the east coast and ascended the lower Colorado River in 1540, and [Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Cabrillo) (or João Rodrigues Cabrilho (in Portuguese)) completed the reconnaissance of the west coast in 1542. [Sebastián Vizcaíno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Vizca%C3%ADno) again surveyed the west coast in 1602, but outside visitors during the following century were few.

The [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) founded a permanent mission colony on the peninsula at [Loreto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loreto,_Baja_California_Sur) in 1697. During the following decades, they gradually extended their sway throughout the present state of Baja California Sur. In 1751–1753, the Croatian Jesuit mission-explorer [Ferdinand Konščak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Kon%C5%A1%C4%8Dak) made overland explorations northward into the state of Baja California. Jesuit missions were subsequently established among the Cochimí at [Santa Gertrudis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misi%C3%B3n_Santa_Gertrudis) (1752), [San Borja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misi%C3%B3n_San_Francisco_Borja) (1762), and [Santa María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misi%C3%B3n_Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_de_los_%C3%81ngeles) (1767).

After the [expulsion of the Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Jesuits) in 1768, the short-lived [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) administration (1768–1773) resulted in one new mission at [San Fernando Velicatá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misi%C3%B3n_San_Fernando_Rey_de_Espa%C3%B1a_de_Velicat%C3%A1). More importantly, the 1769 expedition to settle Alta California under [Gaspar de Portolà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_de_Portol%C3%A0) and [Junípero Serra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun%C3%ADpero_Serra) resulted in the first overland exploration of the northwestern portion of the state.[^cite_note-17]

The [Dominicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order) took over management of the Baja California missions from the Franciscans in 1773. They established a chain of new missions among the northern Cochimí and western Yumans, first on the coast and subsequently inland, extending from [El Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misi%C3%B3n_Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_del_Sant%C3%ADsimo_Rosario_de_Vi%C3%B1acado) (1774) to [Descanso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misi%C3%B3n_El_Descanso) (1817), just south of Tijuana below the [Palóu Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pal%C3%B3u_Line).

In 1804, the Spanish crown divided California into [Alta ('Upper')](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California) and [Baja ('Lower') California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California_Peninsula) at the line separating the [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) missions in the north from the [Dominican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order) missions in the south. The colonial governors were José Joaquín de Arillaga (1804–1805), [Felipe de Goicoechea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_de_Goicoechea) (1806–1814), and [José Darío Argüello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Dar%C3%ADo_Arg%C3%BCello) (1814 – April 11, 1822).

### Post-independence, 1821–present

#### Early republic

[Mexican liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_in_Mexico) were concerned that the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) retained too much power in the post-independence period and sought to undermine it by mandating the [secularization of missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_secularization_act_of_1833) in 1833. In the aftermath of the [Mexican American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_American_War) (1846–1848) and the [Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guadalupe_Hidalgo), the United States gained sovereignty over territory previously held first by [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain) and then Mexico, most of which was sparsely settled. Alta California was incorporated into the U.S., and during the [California Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush), quickly gained enough population to be admitted to the union as a state. Baja California gains control of where is now the cities of [Tijuana](101739.md), [Mexicali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicali), and [Tecate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecate) from Alta California after the treaty and remained under Mexican control. In 1853, soldier of fortune ([mercenary](73374.md)) [William Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Walker_\(filibuster\)) captured [La Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz,_Baja_California_Sur), declaring himself president of the [Republic of Baja California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Baja_California). The Mexican government forced his retreat after several months.

#### Era of Porfirio Díaz

When liberal army general [Porfirio Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porfirio_D%C3%ADaz) came to power in 1876, he embarked on a major program to develop and modernize Mexico.

- 1884: Luis Huller and [George H. Sisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._Sisson) obtain a concession covering much of the present state in return for promises to develop the area.

- 1911: Mexicali and Tijuana are captured by the [Mexican Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Liberal_Party) (*Partido Liberal Mexicano*, PLM), but soon surrender to Federal forces.

Baja California would acquire a reputation as a stronghold for PAN into the 2010s.

## Geography



[Sierra de San Pedro Mártir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_San_Pedro_M%C3%A1rtir), with [Picacho del Diablo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picacho_del_Diablo) in the center

Baja California encompasses a territory within [the Californias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Californias) region of North America, which exhibits diverse geography for a relatively small area. The [Peninsular Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Ranges) of the California cordillera run down the geographic center of the state. The most notable ranges of these mountains are the [Sierra de Juárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_Ju%C3%A1rez) and the [Sierra de San Pedro Mártir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_San_Pedro_M%C3%A1rtir). These ranges are the location of forests reminiscent of [Southern California](62520.md)'s [San Gabriel Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_Mountains). Picacho del Diablo is the highest peak on the peninsula. Valleys between the mountain ranges are located within a climate zone that is suitable for agriculture. Such valleys include the Valle de Guadalupe and the Valle de Ojos Negros, areas that produce citrus fruits and grapes. The mineral-rich mountain range extends southwards to the Gulf of California, where the western slope becomes wider, forming the Llanos del Berrendo on the border with Baja California Sur. The mountain ranges located in the center and southern part of the state include the [Sierra de La Asamblea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_La_Asamblea), Sierra de Calamajué, Sierra de San Luis and the [Sierra de San Borja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_San_Borja).



*[Leptosyne maritima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coreopsis_maritima)* growing on the [Ensenada Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensenada_Municipality) coast, typical of the [California coastal sage and chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_coastal_sage_and_chaparral) ecoregion

Temperate winds from the Pacific Ocean and the cold [California Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Current) make the climate along the northwestern coast pleasant year-round.[^cite_note-21] As a result of the state's location on the California Current, rains from the north barely reach the peninsula, thus leaving southern areas drier. South of the [El Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Rosario,_Baja_California) River, the state changes from a Mediterranean landscape to a desert one. This desert exhibits diverse [succulent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succulent) species that flourish in part due to the [coastal fog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_fog).

To the east, the Sonoran Desert enters the state from both California and Sonora. Some of the highest temperatures in Mexico are recorded in or nearby the Mexicali Valley.[^cite_note-22] However, with irrigation from the Colorado River, this area has become a true [agricultural center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicali#The_building_of_an_agricultural_empire). The [Cerro Prieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Prieto) geothermal province is near Mexicali as well (this area is geologically part of a large [pull apart basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pull_apart_basin)); it produces about 80% of the electricity consumed in the state and enough additional power to export to California. [Laguna Salada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Salada_\(Baja_California\)), a saline lake below sea level lying between the rugged Sierra de Juárez and the Sierra de los Cucapah, is also in the vicinity of Mexicali. The state government has recently been considering plans to revive Laguna Salada.[^cite_note-23] The highest mountain in the Sierra de los Cucapah is Cerro del Centinela or [Mount Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Signal). The Cucapah are the primary indigenous people from the mountains north to [Yuma, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuma,_Arizona).



[Isla Partida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Partida), part of the [San Lorenzo Marine Archipelago National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Lorenzo_Marine_Archipelago_National_Park)

There are numerous islands on the Pacific shore. Guadalupe Island is located in the extreme west of the state's boundaries and is the site of large colonies of [sea lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_lion). Cedros Island exists in the southwest of the state's maritime region. The Todos Santos islands and Coronado Islands are located off the coasts of Ensenada and Tijuana, respectively. All of the islands in the Gulf of California on the Baja California side belong to the municipality of Mexicali.

Baja California obtains much of its water from the Colorado River. Historically, the river drained into the [Colorado River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_Delta) and then flowed into the Gulf of California, but due to large demands for water in the [American Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Southwest), less water now reaches the Gulf. The [Tijuana metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_metropolitan_area) also relies on the [Tijuana River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_River) as a source of water. Much of rural Baja California depends predominantly on wells, a few dams and even oases. Tijuana also purchases water from San Diego County's Otay Water District. Potable water is the largest natural resource issue of the state.

### Climate



Vineyard in the [Valle de Guadalupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_de_Guadalupe)

Baja California's climate varies from [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) to [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid_climate). The Mediterranean climate is found in the northwestern corner of the state, where the summers are dry and mild and the winters cool and rainy. This climate is observed in areas from Tijuana to [San Quintín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Quint%C3%ADn,_Baja_California) and nearby interior valleys. The cold oceanic California Current often creates a low-level marine fog near the coast. The fog occurs along any part of the Pacific coast of the state.



Snowfall at [Constitution 1857 National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_1857_National_Park)

The change of altitude towards the Sierra de Baja California creates an [alpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate) in this region. Summers are cool, while winters can be cold with below freezing temperatures at night. It is common to see snow in the Sierra de Juárez, Sierra de San Pedro Mártir and in the valleys in between the two ranges from December to April. Due to [orographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orographic) effects, precipitation is much higher in the mountains of northern Baja California than on the western coastal plain or eastern desert plain. Pine, cedar and fir forests are found in the mountains.

The mountains produce a [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) to the east of their peaks, creating an extremely arid environment. The Sonoran Desert region of Baja California experiences hot summers and nearly frostless mild winters. The Mexicali Valley (which is below sea level) experiences the highest temperatures in Mexico, frequently surpassing 47 °C (116.6 °F) in mid-summer, and exceeding 50 °C (122 °F) on some occasions.

Further south along the Pacific coast, the Mediterranean climate transitions into a desert climate, but it is milder and not as hot as along the gulf coast. Transition climates, from Mediterranean to desert, can be found from San Quintín to [El Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Rosario,_Baja_California). Further inland and along the Gulf of California, the vegetation is scarce and temperatures are very high during the summer months. The islands in the Gulf of California also have a desert climate. Some oases can be found in the desert where few towns are located – for instance, [Catavina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catavina), San Borja and Santa Gertrudis.[^cite_note-Martínez-Ballesté-24]

### Flora and fauna

Common trees at the higher elevations are the [Jeffrey pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_pine), [sugar pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_pine) and [pinon pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinon_pine).[^cite_note-25] [Understory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Understory) species include [manzanita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manzanita). There is a variety of reptiles, including the [western fence lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_fence_lizard), which is at the southern extent of its range.[^cite_note-26] The name of the fish genus *[Bajacalifornia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajacalifornia)* is derived from the Baja California peninsula.[^cite_note-27]

In the main wildlife refuges on the peninsula of Baja California, [Constitution 1857 National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_1857_National_Park) and [Sierra de San Pedro Mártir National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_de_San_Pedro_M%C3%A1rtir_National_Park), several coniferous species can be found. The most abundant are Jeffrey pine, *[Pinus ponderosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_ponderosa)*, *[Pinus cembroides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_cembroides)*, *[Pinus quadrifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_quadrifolia)*, *[Pinus monophylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_monophylla)*, *[Juniperus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniperus)*, [*Arctostaphylos pringlei* subsp. *drupacea*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctostaphylos_pringlei), *[Artemisia ludoviciana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemisia_ludoviciana)* and *[Adenostoma sparsifolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenostoma_sparsifolium)*. Baja California shares many plant species with the [Laguna Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Mountains) and [San Jacinto Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jacinto_Mountains) in southwest California. The lower elevations of the Sierra de Juárez are characterized by [chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaparral) and [desert shrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_shrub). [Guadalupe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalupe_Island) and its surrounding waters, 250 kilometres (160 miles) off the Pacific coast, has been designated the [Guadalupe Island Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalupe_Island_Biosphere_Reserve) to preserve endangered marine and terrestrial species of animals and plants.

The fauna in the parks include a large number of mammals, primarily [mule deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mule_deer), [bighorn sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bighorn_sheep), [cougars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar), [bobcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobcat), [ringtail cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringtail_cat), [coyotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coyote), [rabbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit), [squirrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel) and more than 30 species of [bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat). The park is also home to many avian species like [bald eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_eagle), [golden eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_eagle), [falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon), [woodpeckers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodpecker), [black vultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_vulture), [crows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crow), and several species of [Sittidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sittidae) and [duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck).

### 2010 earthquakes

Main article: [2010 Baja California earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Baja_California_earthquake)

At 3\:40\:41 pm PDT on Easter Sunday, 4 April 2010, a 7.2 M<sub>w</sub> (on the [moment magnitude scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale)) magnitude northwest-trending [strike-slip earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike-slip_earthquake) hit the [Mexicali Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicali_Valley), with its [epicenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicenter) 26 km (16 mi) southwest of the city of [Guadalupe Victoria, Baja California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalupe_Victoria,_Baja_California).[^cite_note-28] The main shock was felt as far as the [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_metropolitan_area), [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_metro), [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_metropolitan_area) and [Tucson metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson_metropolitan_area), and in [Yuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuma,_Arizona). At least a half-dozen [aftershocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftershock) with magnitudes between 5.0 and 5.4 were reported, including a 5.1-magnitude shaker at 4\:14 am that was centered near [El Centro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Centro,_California).[^cite_note-29] As of 6\:31 am PDT on 5 April 2010, two people were confirmed dead.[^cite_note-30]

## Politics

### State elections



[Marina del Pilar Ávila Olmeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_del_Pilar_%C3%81vila_Olmeda), the [governor of Baja California](3303570.md) since 2021

In Baja California, state elections are held every two years (every three years prior to 2019) for the positions of [state governor](3303570.md), 25 state deputies of the [Congress of Baja California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Baja_California), and 5 municipal mayors. Of the 25 state deputies, 17 are elected by relative majority in each of the electoral districts, while another 8 are elected by proportional representation. The 17 deputies elected by relative majority may be re-elected for up to four consecutive terms, while the other 8 deputies can only serve one term.

During the 2019 Baja California state election, [Jaime Bonilla Valdez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Bonilla_Valdez) of the [Morena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morena_\(political_party\))-led [Juntos Hacemos Historia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juntos_Hacemos_Historia) coalition won by a margin of 27.58% to become the governor of Baja California. In addition, 21 out of 25 state deputy positions and all 5 municipal mayoral positions were won by candidates aligned with the Juntos Hacemos Historia coalition.

During the 2021 Baja California state election, [Marina del Pilar Ávila Olmeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_del_Pilar_%C3%81vila_Olmeda) of the Morena-led [Juntos Hacemos Historia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juntos_Hacemos_Historia) coalition won by a margin of 17.49% to become the first female governor of Baja California. Of the state's 25 local deputies, 13 were won by Morena-aligned candidates, followed in a distant second place by candidates of the [Solidarity Encounter Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_Encounter_Party) (PES), [National Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Action_Party_\(Mexico\)) (PAN), and [Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Party_\(Mexico\)) (PT) with 3 seats each. All 5 municipal mayoral positions were again won by Morena-aligned candidates.

In the [2024 Baja California state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Baja_California_state_election), Morena gained an extra seat in the State Congress.

*Los Pioneros* monument in [Mexicali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicali), dedicated to the pioneers who settled the region

Although the state is predominantly European in ancestry, it has historically seen a [sizable East and Southeast Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Mexicans) immigrant population. Mexicali has a large Chinese community, as well as many [Filipinos](2298105.md) who arrived to the state during the eras of Spanish Philippines and American rule (1898–1946) in much of the 19th and 20th centuries. Tijuana and Ensenada were major ports of entry for East Asians entering the U.S. ever since the first [Asian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_American) were present in California.

According to the 2020 Census, 1.71% of Baja California's population identified as Black, [Afro-Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Mexican), or of African descent.[^cite_note-35]

Since 1960, large numbers of migrants from southern Mexican states have arrived to work in agriculture (especially the Mexicali Valley and nearby [Imperial Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Valley), California, U.S.) and manufacturing. The cities of Ensenada, Tijuana, and Mexicali grew as a result of migrants, primarily those who sought U.S. citizenship. Those temporary residents awaiting their entry into the United States are called *flotillas*, which is derived from the Spanish word *flota*, meaning 'fleet'.

There is also a sizable immigrant community from Central and South America, and from the United States and Canada. An estimated 200,000+ American [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriates) live in the state, especially in [coastal resort](293990.md) towns such as Ensenada, known for affordable homes purchased by retirees who continue to hold U.S. citizenship. San Felipe, Rosarito and Tijuana also have a large American population (second largest in Mexico after Mexico City), particularly for their cheaper housing and proximity to San Diego.

Some 60,000 Oaxacans live in Baja California. Some 40% of them lack proper birth certificates.[^cite_note-36]

According to a [Consejo Nacional de Ciencia y Tecnología](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consejo_Nacional_de_Ciencia_y_Tecnolog%C3%ADa_\(Mexico\)) (Conacyt) investigator, a little under a million people were classified as "poor" in the state, up from 2008 when there were roughly 810,000. Exactly who these people are, whether locals, interstate or international migrants, was not explained.[^cite_note-37]

## Education

Further information: [Category\:Universities and colleges in Baja California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Universities_and_colleges_in_Baja_California)

Baja California offers one of the best educational programs in the country, with high rankings in schooling and achievement.

The state government provides education and qualification courses to increase the workforce standards, such as school–enterprise linkage programs which help the development of a labor force according to the needs of the industry.

91.60% of the population from six to fourteen years of age attend elementary school. 61.95% of the population over fifteen years of age attends or has already graduated from high school. Public school is available in all levels from kindergarten to university.

The state has 32 universities offering 103 professional degrees. These universities have 19 research and development centers for basic and applied investigation in advanced projects related to biotechnology, physics, oceanography, computer science, digital [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power) technology, astronomy, aerospace, electrical engineering and clean energy, among others. At this educational level, supply is steadily growing. Baja California has developed a need to be self-sufficient in matters of technological and scientific innovation and to be less dependent on foreign countries. Current businesses demand new production processes as well as technology for the incubation of companies. The number of graduate degrees offered, including PhD programs, is 121. The state has 53 graduate schools.[^cite_note-GOBC-38]

## Economy

Located in [Rosarito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosarito), Baja Film Studios has become one of the premier production facilities with [horizon tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_tank).

[^cite_note-39]

As of 2005, Baja California's economy represents 3.3% of Mexico's [gross domestic product](12594.md), or US\$21.996 billion.[^cite_note-ICM-40] Baja California's economy has a strong focus on tariff-free export oriented manufacturing ([maquiladora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquiladora)). As of 2005, 284,255 people are employed in the manufacturing sector.[^cite_note-ICM-40] There are more than 900 companies operating under the federal [Prosec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosec_Mexico) program in Baja California.

### Employment

In 2021, Baja California generated 57,550 new jobs, about 15.2 jobs per 1,000 inhabitants, making it the 5th highest in the country and the second highest of any border state behind [Nuevo León](222247.md) (86,364 new jobs). The majority of these new jobs were generated in and around the cities of [Tijuana](101739.md), [Mexicali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicali), [Ensenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensenada,_Baja_California), [Playas de Rosarito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosarito,_Baja_California), and [Tecate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecate,_Baja_California). Industries that experienced the highest degree of growth in 2021 include [transformative industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disruptive_innovation), [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Mexico) and [communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Mexico), [commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce), and [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction#Industry_scale_and_characteristics).[^cite_note-EP2022-41]

As of November 2021, Baja California has the highest [employment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_rate) of any state in [northern Mexico](48157875.md), with a rate of 96.7%.[^cite_note-EP2022-41]

### Economic investment

As of September 2021, Baja California receives the third highest amount of [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) of any state in Mexico, constituting about 7.7% of the national total and behind only [Nuevo León](222247.md) (7.7%) and [Mexico City](18987.md) (16.5%). About 81.4% of Baja California's foreign domestic investment comes from the [United States](3434750.md), of which 50.3% comes from the construction of [natural gas](22131.md) pipelines and 8.2% comes car and truck manufacturing.[^cite_note-EP2022-41]

### Real estate

The Foreign Investment Law of 1973[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] allows foreigners to purchase land within the borders and coasts of Mexico by way of a trust handled through a Mexican bank (Fideicomiso). This trust assures to the buyer all the rights and privileges of ownership, and it can be sold, inherited, leased, or transferred at any time. Since 1994, the Foreign Investment Law stipulates that the Fideicomiso must be to a 50-year term, with the option to petition for a 50-year renewal at any time.[^cite_note-44]

Any Mexican citizen buying a bank trust property has the option to either remain within the trust or opt out of it and request the title in *escritura*.

Mexico's early history involved foreign invasions and the loss of vast amounts of land; in fear of history being repeated, the Mexican constitution established the concept of the "Restricted Zone".[^cite_note-45] In 1973, in order to bring in more foreign tourist investment, the Bank Trust of Fideicomiso was created, thus allowing non-Mexicans to own land without any constitutional amendment necessary.[^cite_note-46] Since the law went into effect, it has undergone many modifications in order to make purchasing land in Mexico a safer investment.

## Transport

### Highways

- [Mexican Federal Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_1) or the Transpeninsular Highway, completed 1973, south from Tijuana 713 km (436 miles) via Ensenada and El Rosario to Baja California Sur at Guerrero Negro.

- [Mexican Federal Highway 1D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_1D) toll road from Ensenada 98 km to US I-5 at Tijuana.

- [Mexican Federal Highway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_2) from Tijuana 214 km east via Tecate, La Rumorosa and Mexicali to Sonora at San Luis Rio Colorado.

- [Mexican Federal Highway 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_3) south from the US border at Tecate 112 km (70 miles) to Highway 1 at El Sauzal Rodriguez, just north of Ensenada (bypassing Tijuana), with a second segment from Highway 1 in Ensenada 196 km (122 miles) south-east via Real del Castillo to Highway 5 near the east coast in El Chinero, 55 km (34 miles) north of San Felipe (where all vehicles are subject to a military inspection).

- [Mexican Federal Highway 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_5) south from Mexicali 401 km (249 miles) to Highway 1 in San Felipe on the east coast, where a state highway continues to Puertecitos.

- [Mexican Federal Highway 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Federal_Highway_12) east from Highway 1 68 km to Bahía de los Ángeles on the east coast.

### Railroads

- [Ferromex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromex) in Mexicali connects with [Union Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad)'s former [Southern Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Pacific_Railroad) line at Calexio and runs east to Sonora at Riíto, with another line connecting with Yuma, Arizona across the Colorado River at Algodones.

- [The Baja California Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California_Railroad) operates the 71 km former Ferrocarril Tecate Tijuana line from the US [San Diego and Imperial Valley Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_and_Imperial_Valley_Railroad) in San Ysidro east via Tecate.

### Airports

The state is served by these international airports:

- [Tijuana International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_International_Airport)

- [Mexicali International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicali_International_Airport)

However, [San Diego International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_International_Airport) is also used by air travellers from the state. It is located 34 kilometres (21 mi) north west of Tijuana city centre. The Tijuana airport can be accessible by [Cross Border Xpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Border_Xpress).

### Waterways

Deep water ports at [Ensenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Ensenada) (a major naval base) and [Isla de Cedros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_de_Cedros).
