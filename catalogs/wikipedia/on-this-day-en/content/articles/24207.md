**Portsmouth** ([/ˈpɔːrtsməθ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/27/En-uk-Portsmouth.ogg/En-uk-Portsmouth.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/27/En-uk-Portsmouth.ogg/En-uk-Portsmouth.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*PORTS-məth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) and [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority) in [Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire), England. Most of Portsmouth is located on [Portsea Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsea_Island) off the south coast of England in the [Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solent), making Portsmouth the only city in England not located primarily on the [mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain). The city is located 22 miles (35 km) south-east of [Southampton](7920751.md), 50 miles (80 km) west of [Brighton and Hove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_and_Hove), and 74 miles (119 km) south-west of [London](17867.md). With a population last recorded at 208,100, it is the most densely populated city in the [United Kingdom](31717.md).[^cite_note-auto-9] Portsmouth forms part of the [South Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Hampshire) urban area with [Gosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosport), [Fareham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Fareham), [Havant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Havant), [Eastleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Eastleigh) and Southampton.

Portsmouth's history can be traced to [Roman times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) and it has contained a significant [Royal Navy dockyard and base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNB_Portsmouth) for centuries. Portsmouth was founded c. 1180 by Anglo-Norman merchant [Jean de Gisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_Gisors) in the south-west area of Portsea Island, a location now known as [Old Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Portsmouth).[^cite_note-portsmouth-guide_co_uk-10] Around this time, de Gisors ordered the construction of a chapel dedicated to [St Thomas Becket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Becket).[^cite_note-portsmouth-guide_co_uk-10] This became a parish church by the 14th century. Portsmouth was established as a town with a royal charter on 2 May 1194.[^cite_note-history3-11][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuail199414–18-12] The city is home to the first drydock ever built. It was constructed on the orders of [Henry VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VII_of_England) in 1496.[^cite_note-13]

Portsmouth has the world's oldest [dry dock](213739.md) currently in use, *"The Great Stone Dock"*; originally built in 1698, rebuilt in 1769 and presently known as "No.5 Dock".[^cite_note-14] The world's first mass [production line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_line) was established at the naval base's [Block Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Block_Mills) which produced pulley blocks for the [Royal Navy](26061.md). By the early-19th century, Portsmouth was the most heavily [fortified](204118.md) city in the world, and was considered "the world's greatest naval port" at the height of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) throughout *[Pax Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Britannica)*. By 1859, a ring of defensive land and sea forts, known as the [Palmerston Forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerston_Forts), had been [built around Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palmerston_Forts_at_Portsmouth) in anticipation of an invasion from continental Europe.

Portsmouth achieved [city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) on 21 April 1926.[^cite_note-15] During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) the city was bombed extensively in the [Portsmouth Blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Blitz), which resulted in the deaths of 930 people, and it was a pivotal embarkation point for the [D-Day landings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Day_landings). In 1982 a British task force departed from Portsmouth to serve in the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War). [HMY *Britannia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMY_Britannia) was formerly based in Portsmouth and oversaw the [transfer of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_sovereignty_over_Hong_Kong) in 1997, after which *Britannia* was retired from royal service, decommissioned and relocated to [Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith) as a museum ship.

[HMNB Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNB_Portsmouth) is an operational Royal Navy base and is home to two thirds of the UK's surface fleet. The base has long had the nickname Pompey, which it shares with the wider city of Portsmouth and [Portsmouth Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Football_Club). The naval base also contains the [National Museum of the Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_Royal_Navy) and [Portsmouth Historic Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Historic_Dockyard), which has a collection of historic warships, including the *Mary Rose*, [Lord Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horatio_Nelson,_1st_Viscount_Nelson)'s flagship, [HMS *Victory*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Victory) (the world's oldest naval ship still in commission), and [HMS *Warrior*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Warrior_\(1860\)), the Royal Navy's first [ironclad warship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad_warship).

The former [HMS *Vernon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Vernon_\(shore_establishment\)) shore establishment has been redeveloped into a large retail [outlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlet_store) destination known as [Gunwharf Quays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunwharf_Quays) which opened in 2001.[^cite_note-16] Portsmouth is among the few British cities with two cathedrals: the [Anglican Cathedral of St Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Cathedral) and the [Roman Catholic](606848.md) [Cathedral of St John the Evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St_John_the_Evangelist,_Portsmouth). The waterfront and [Portsmouth Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Harbour) are dominated by the [Spinnaker Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinnaker_Tower), one of the [United Kingdom's tallest structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_structures_in_the_United_Kingdom) at 560 feet (170 m).

[Southsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea) is Portsmouth's [seaside resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_coastal_resort), which was named after Southsea Castle. Southsea has two piers; [Clarence Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Pier) amusement park and [South Parade Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Parade_Pier). The world's only regular hovercraft service operates from [Southsea Hoverport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea_Hoverport) to [Ryde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryde) on the [Isle of Wight](15102.md). [Southsea Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea_Common) is a large open-air public recreation space which serves as a venue for a wide variety of annual events.

The city has several mainline railway stations that connect to [London Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Victoria_railway_station) and [London Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Waterloo_railway_station) amongst other lines in southern England. [Portsmouth International Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_International_Port) is a commercial cruise ship and ferry port for international destinations. The port is the second busiest in the United Kingdom after [Dover](6142372.md), handling around three million passengers a year. The city formerly had its own airport, [Portsmouth Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Airport_\(Hampshire\)), until its closure in 1973. The [University of Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Portsmouth) enrolled 23,000 students.

Portsmouth is the birthplace of notable people such as author [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens), engineer [Isambard Kingdom Brunel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isambard_Kingdom_Brunel), former Prime Minister [James Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Callaghan), actor [Peter Sellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sellers) and author-journalist [Christopher Hitchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Hitchens).

## History

Main article: [History of Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Portsmouth)

### Early history

The [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) built [Portus Adurni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portus_Adurni) (now called [Portchester Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portchester_Castle)), a [fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra), at nearby [Portchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portchester) in the late 3rd century.[^cite_note-17] The city's [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) (Anglo-Saxon) name, "Portesmuða", is derived from *port* (a haven) and *muða* (the mouth of a large river or estuary).[^cite_note-18] In the *[Anglo-Saxon Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle)*, a warrior named Port and his two sons killed a noble Briton in Portsmouth in 501.[^cite_note-19] [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill), in *[A History of the English-Speaking Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_History_of_the_English-Speaking_Peoples)*, wrote that Port was a pirate who founded Portsmouth in 501.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChurchill201541-20][^cite_note-21]

England's southern coast was vulnerable to [Danish Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danes_\(Germanic_tribe\)) invasions during the eighth and ninth centuries, and was conquered by Danish pirates in 787.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201526-22] In 838, during the reign of [Æthelwulf, King of Wessex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%86thelwulf,_King_of_Wessex), a Danish fleet landed between Portsmouth and Southampton and plundered the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201527-23] Æthelwulf sent Wulfherd and the governor of [Dorsetshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset) to confront the Danes at Portsmouth, where most of their ships were docked. Although the Danes were driven off, Wulfherd was killed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201527-23] The Danes returned in 1001 and pillaged Portsmouth and the surrounding area, threatening the English with extinction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201529-24][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201530-25] They were massacred by the English survivors the following year; rebuilding began, although the town experienced further attacks until [1066](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stamford_Bridge).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201531-26]

### Norman to Tudor

A front facing view of Portsmouth's Round Tower, which once guarded the entrance to Portsmouth Harbour. The Round Tower itself is made of stone and has a large circular base.

The [Round Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Tower_\(Portsmouth\)) was built in 1418 to defend the entrance to [Portsmouth Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Harbour).

Although Portsmouth was not mentioned in the 1086 [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book), *Bocheland* ([Buckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckland,_Portsmouth)), *Copenore* ([Copnor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copnor)), and *Frodentone* ([Fratton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fratton)) were.[^cite_note-history3-11] According to some sources, it was founded in 1180 by the Anglo-Norman merchant [Jean de Gisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_Gisors).[^cite_note-history2-27]

King [Henry II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_II_of_England) died in 1189; his son, [Richard I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_I_of_England) (who had spent most of his life in France), arrived in Portsmouth en route to his coronation in London.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201532-28] When Richard returned from captivity in [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Austria) in May 1194, he summoned an army and a fleet of 100 ships to the port.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201533-29] Richard gave Portsmouth market-town status with a royal charter on 2 May, authorising an annual fifteen-day free-market fair, weekly markets and a local court to deal with minor matters, and exempted its inhabitants from an £18 annual tax.[^cite_note-history3-11][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuail199414–18-12] The 1194 royal charter's 800th anniversary was celebrated in 1994 with ceremonies at the city museum.[^cite_note-30]

[King John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John,_King_of_England) reaffirmed Richard I's rights and privileges, and established a permanent naval base. The first docks were begun by [William of Wrotham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_of_Wrotham) in 1212,[^cite_note-history3-11][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201533-29] and John summoned his earls, barons, and military advisers to plan an invasion of [Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Normandy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201534-31] In 1229, declaring war against France, [Henry III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III_of_England) assembled a force described by historian Lake Allen as "one of the finest armies that had ever been raised in England".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201536-32] The invasion stalled, and returned from France in October 1231.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201537-33] Henry III summoned troops to invade [Guienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guienne) in 1242, and [Edward I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I) sent supplies for his army in France in 1295.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201537,_39-34] Commercial interests had grown by the following century, and its exports included wool, corn, grain, and livestock.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201543-35]

[Edward II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_II) ordered all ports on the south coast to assemble their largest vessels at Portsmouth to carry soldiers and horses to the [Duchy of Aquitaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Aquitaine) in 1324 to strengthen defences.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201544-36] A French fleet commanded by [David II of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_II_of_Scotland) attacked in the [English Channel](9230.md), ransacked the [Isle of Wight](15102.md) and threatened the town. [Edward III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III) instructed all maritime towns to build vessels and raise troops to rendezvous at Portsmouth.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201544-36] Two years later, a French fleet led by [Nicholas Béhuchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_B%C3%A9huchet) [raided Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raid_on_Portsmouth_\(1338\)) and destroyed most of the town; only the stone-built church and hospital survived.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESumption1990395,_396-37][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeward1988-38] After the raid, Edward III exempted the town from national taxes to aid its reconstruction.[^cite_note-porthis-39] In 1377, shortly after Edward died, the French landed in Portsmouth. Although the town was plundered and burnt, its inhabitants drove the French off to raid towns in the [West Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Country).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201548-40]

A black and white map of Portsmouth dated around 1540

Portsmouth c. 1540

[Henry V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_V_of_England) gathered his forces in Portsmouth for an invasion of France in 1415, it was while staying at Portchester Castle that the Southampton plot was uncovered. This campaign would culminate with victory at the battle of Agincourt.[^cite_note-41] He also built Portsmouth's first permanent [fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Portsmouth). In 1416, a number of French ships blockaded the town (which housed ships which were set to invade Normandy); Henry gathered a fleet at Southampton, and invaded the Norman coast in August that year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201549-42] Recognising the town's growing importance, he ordered a wooden [Round Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Tower_\(Portsmouth\)) to be built at the mouth of the harbour; it was completed in 1426.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201327-43] [Henry VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VII_of_England) rebuilt the fortifications with stone, assisted Robert Brygandine and Sir [Reginald Bray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Bray) in the construction of the world's first [dry dock](213739.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201333-44] and raised the [Square Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_Tower) in 1494.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201327-43] He made Portsmouth a Royal Dockyard, England's only dockyard considered "national".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201553-45] Although [King Alfred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_the_Great) may have used Portsmouth to build ships as early as the ninth century, the first warship recorded as constructed in the town was the [*Sweepstake*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweepstake_\(warship\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (built in 1497).[^cite_note-46]

[Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII) built [Southsea Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea_Castle), financed by the [Dissolution of the Monasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries), in 1539 in anticipation of a French invasion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen2015143-47][^cite_note-48] He also invested heavily in the town's dockyard, expanding it to 8 acres (3.2 ha).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201323-49] Around this time, a Tudor [defensive boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_\(navigational_barrier\)) stretched from the Round Tower to Fort Blockhouse in Gosport to protect Portsmouth Harbour.[^cite_note-boom-50]

From Southsea Castle, Henry witnessed his flagship *Mary Rose* sink in action against the French fleet in the 1545 [Battle of the Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Solent) with the loss of about 500 lives.[^cite_note-51] Some historians believe that the *Mary Rose* turned too quickly and submerged her open gun ports; according to others, it sank due to poor design.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201337-52] Portsmouth's fortifications were improved by successive monarchs. The town experienced an outbreak of [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_\(disease\)) in 1563, which killed about 300 of its 2,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-history2-27]

### Stuart to Georgian



A street view of [Old Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Portsmouth) — old buildings and cobbled streets.

In 1623, [Charles I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England) (then Prince of Wales) returned to Portsmouth from France and Spain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201554-53] His unpopular military adviser, [George Villiers, 1st Duke of Buckingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Villiers,_1st_Duke_of_Buckingham), was stabbed to death in an [Old Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Portsmouth) pub by war veteran [John Felton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Felton_\(assassin\)) five years later.[^cite_note-history3-11][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201554,_55-54] Felton never attempted to escape, and was caught walking the streets when soldiers confronted him; he said, "I know that he is dead, for I had the force of forty men when I struck the blow".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201556-55] Felton was hanged, and his body chained to a [gibbet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbet) on Southsea Common as a warning to others.[^cite_note-history2-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201556-55] The murder took place in the Greyhound public house on High Street, which is now Buckingham House and has a commemorative plaque.[^cite_note-56]

Most residents (including the mayor) supported the [parliamentarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundhead) during the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War), although military governor [Colonel Goring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Goring,_Lord_Goring) supported the [royalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalier).[^cite_note-history2-27] The town, a base of the parliamentarian navy, was blockaded from the sea. Parliamentarian troops were sent to [besiege it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Portsmouth), and the guns of Southsea Castle were fired at the town's royalist garrison. Parliamentarians in [Gosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosport) joined the assault, damaging [St Thomas's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Cathedral).[^cite_note-history2-27][^cite_note-siege-57] On 5 September 1642, the remaining royalists in the garrison at the Square Tower were forced to surrender after Goring threatened to blow it up; he and his garrison were allowed safe passage out of the city.[^cite_note-siege-57][^cite_note-siege1-58]

After the end of the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War), Portsmouth was among the first towns to declare [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) king and began to prosper.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201557-59] The first ship built in over 100 years, [*Portsmouth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_ship_Portsmouth_\(1650\)), was launched in 1650; twelve ships were built between 1650 and 1660. Under the [Commonwealth of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_England), [Robert Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Blake_\(admiral\)) used the harbour as his base during the [First Anglo-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Dutch_War) in 1652 and the [Anglo-Spanish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Spanish_War_\(1654%E2%80%931660\)). He died within sight of the town, returning from [Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1diz) in 1657.[^cite_note-siege1-58] After [the Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Restoration), Charles II married [Catherine of Braganza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Braganza) at the [Royal Garrison Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domus_Dei) on 14 May 1662.[^cite_note-garr-60][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201357-61] Catherine was reputed to have introduced the cultural practice of [tea drinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_culture#United_Kingdom) to England at this event.[^cite_note-62]

During the late 17th century, Portsmouth continued to grow; a new wharf was constructed in 1663 for military use, and a [mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mast_\(sailing\)) pond was dug in 1665. In 1684, a list of ships docked in Portsmouth was evidence of its increasing national importance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201558-63] Between 1667 and 1685, the town's fortifications were rebuilt; new walls were constructed with [bastions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastion) and two moats were dug, making Portsmouth one of the world's most heavily fortified places.[^cite_note-history2-27]

In 1759, General [James Wolfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wolfe) sailed to capture [Quebec](7954867.md); the expedition, although successful, cost him his life. His body was brought back to Portsmouth in November, and received high naval and military honours.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen201565-64] Two years later, on 30 May 1775, Captain [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook) arrived on [HMS *Endeavour*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Endeavour) after circumnavigating the globe.[^cite_note-history3-11][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECollingridge2003311-65] The 11-ship [First Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Fleet) left on 13 May 1787 to establish the first European colony in [Australia](4689264.md), the beginning of prisoner transportation;[^cite_note-Gutenberg_FF-66][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrost2012165-67] Captain [William Bligh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bligh) of [HMS *Bounty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Bounty) also sailed from the harbour that year.[^cite_note-history3-11][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013223-68] After the 28 April 1789 [mutiny on the *Bounty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutiny_on_the_Bounty), [HMS *Pandora*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Pandora_\(1779\)) was dispatched from Portsmouth to bring the mutineers back for trial. The [court-martial](58625.md) opened on 12 September 1792 aboard [HMS *Duke*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Duke_\(1777\)) in Portsmouth Harbour; of the ten remaining men, three were sentenced to death.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013223,_224-69][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHough1972276-70] In 1789, a chapel was erected in Prince George's Street and was dedicated to [St John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Apostle) by the Bishop of Winchester. Around this time, a [bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(law\)) was passed in the House of Commons on the creation of a canal to link Portsmouth to Chichester; however, the project was abandoned.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen2015130-71]

The city's nickname, Pompey, is thought to have derived from the log entry of [Portsmouth Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Point) (contracted "Po'm.P." – *Po'*&#x72;ts*m*outh *P.*&#x6F;int) as ships entered the harbour; navigational charts use the contraction.[^cite_note-72] According to one historian, the name may have been brought back from a group of Portsmouth-based sailors who visited [Pompey's Pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompey's_Pillar_\(column\)) in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), Egypt, around 1781.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201398-73] Another theory is that it is named after the harbour's guardship, [*Pompee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Pompee), a 74-gun French [ship of the line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_the_line) captured in 1793.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBreverton2010282-74]

Portsmouth's coat of arms is attested in the early 19th century as "azure a crescent or, surmounted by an estoile of eight points of the last."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBerryGlover1828-75] Its design is apparently based on 18th-century mayoral seals.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEast1891656-76] A connection of the coat of arms with the Great Seal of Richard I (which had a separate star and crescent) dates to the 20th century.[^cite_note-77]

### Industrial Revolution to Edwardian

A picture of the iron-clad HMS Warrior docked in Portsmouth's historic harbour. The ship has since been restored to its original Victorian condition.

[HMS *Warrior*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Warrior_\(1860\)) (launched in 1860) has been restored to its original Victorian condition.



View of Portsmouth harbour about 1860

[Marc Isambard Brunel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Isambard_Brunel) established the world's first mass-production line at [Portsmouth Block Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Block_Mills), making [pulley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulley) [blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_\(sailing\)) for [rigging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigging) on the navy's ships.[^cite_note-royal-78] The first machines were installed in January 1803, and the final set (for large blocks) in March 1805. In 1808, the mills produced 130,000 blocks.[^cite_note-block-79] By the turn of the 19th century, Portsmouth was the largest industrial site in the world; it had a workforce of 8,000, and an annual budget of £570,000.[^cite_note-80]

On 14 September 1805, [Vice-Admiral of the White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Admiral_of_the_White) [Horatio Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horatio_Nelson) left Portsmouth to command the fleet which won the [Battle of Trafalgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trafalgar) on 21 October.[^cite_note-history3-11] The [Royal Navy](26061.md)'s reliance on Portsmouth led to its becoming the most fortified city in the world.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPevsner1967422-81] The Navy's [West Africa Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa_Squadron), tasked with halting the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md), began operating out of Portsmouth in 1808.[^cite_note-BBC_slave_trade-82] A network of forts, known as the [Palmerston Forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerston_Forts), was [built around the town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palmerston_Forts_at_Portsmouth) as part of a programme led by Prime Minister [Lord Palmerston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Palmerston) to defend British military bases from an inland attack following an Anglo-French war scare in 1859. The forts were nicknamed "Palmerston's Follies" because their armaments were pointed inland and not out to sea.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201379-83]

In April 1811, the [Portsea Island Waterworks Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsea_Island_Waterworks_Company) constructed the first piped-water supply[^cite_note-water-84] to upper- and middle-class houses.[^cite_note-history2-27] It supplied water to about 4,500 of Portsmouth's 14,000 houses, generating an income of £5,000 a year.[^cite_note-water-84] HMS *Victory*'s active career ended in 1812, when she was moored in Portsmouth Harbour and used as a [depot ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depot_ship). The town of Gosport contributed £75 a year to the ship's maintenance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201339-85] In 1818, [John Pounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pounds) began teaching working-class children in the country's first [ragged school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragged_school).[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201366,_67-87] The Portsea Improvement Commissioners installed gas street lighting throughout Portsmouth in 1820,[^cite_note-history3-11] followed by Old Portsmouth three years later.[^cite_note-history2-27]

During the 19th century, Portsmouth expanded across Portsea Island. [Buckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckland,_Portsmouth) was merged into the town by the 1860s, and Fratton and [Stamshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamshaw) were incorporated by the next decade. Between 1865 and 1870, the council built sewers after more than 800 people died in a [cholera](7591.md) epidemic; according to a [by-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-law), any house within 100 feet (30 m) of a sewer had to be connected to it.[^cite_note-history3-11] By 1871 the population had risen to 100,000,[^cite_note-history2-27] and the national census listed Portsmouth's population as 113,569.[^cite_note-history3-11] A working-class suburb was constructed in the 1870s, when about 1,820 houses were built, and it became [Somerstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerstown,_Hampshire).[^cite_note-history3-11] Despite public-health improvements, 514 people died in an 1872 [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox) epidemic.[^cite_note-history3-11] On 21 December of that year, the [*Challenger* expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_expedition) embarked on a 68,890-nautical-mile (127,580 km) circumnavigation of the globe for scientific research.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERice199927–48-88][^cite_note-89]

When the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) was at its height of power, covering a quarter of Earth's total land area and 458 million people at the turn of the 20th century, Portsmouth was considered "the world's greatest naval port".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201324-90] In 1900, Portsmouth Dockyard employed 8,000 people – a figure which increased to 23,000 during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War).[^cite_note-history2-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201391-91] The whole of Portsea Island came united under the control of Portsmouth borough council in 1904.[^cite_note-portsenc-92]

In 1906, [HMS *Dreadnought*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Dreadnought_\(1906\)) was launched from Portsmouth Dockyard.[^cite_note-93] The ship revolutionised naval warfare and began an arms race with Germany. The ship's entry into service in 1906 represented such an advance in naval technology that her name came to be associated with an entire generation of battleships.

### 1913 terrorist attack



A fire started by [suffragettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragettes) at the [semaphore tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semaphore_tower), [Portsmouth dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_dockyard), in December 1913, killed two men.

A major [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) incident occurred in the city in 1913, which led to the deaths of two men. During the [suffragette bombing and arson campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragette_bombing_and_arson_campaign) of 1912–1914, [militant](310790.md) [suffragettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragettes) of the [Women's Social and Political Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Social_and_Political_Union) carried out a series of politically motivated bombing and arson attacks nationwide as part of their campaign for [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage).[^cite_note-94] In one of the more serious suffragette attacks, a fire was purposely started at [Portsmouth dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_dockyard) on 20 December 1913, in which two sailors were killed after it spread through the industrial area.[^cite_note-Portsmouth-95][^cite_note-Webb-96][^cite_note-Bearman-97] The fire spread rapidly as there were many old wooden buildings in the area, including the historic semaphore tower which dated back to the eighteenth century, which was completely destroyed.[^cite_note-Webb-96] The damage to the dockyard area cost the city £200,000 in damages, equivalent to £23,600,000 today.[^cite_note-Webb-96] In the midst of the firestorm, a battleship, [HMS *Queen Mary*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Queen_Mary), had to be towed to safety to avoid the flames.[^cite_note-Webb-96] The two victims were a pensioner and a signalman.[^cite_note-Webb-96]

The attack was notable enough to be reported on in the press in the [United States](3434750.md), with the *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* reporting on the disaster two days after with the headline "Big Portsmouth Fire Loss".[^cite_note-Portsmouth-95] The report also disclosed that at a previous police raid on a suffragette headquarters, "papers were discovered disclosing a plan to fire the yard".[^cite_note-Portsmouth-95]

### First and Second World Wars

In this photograph, King George VI is inspecting the crew of the Norwegian ship HNoMS Draug, which was docked in Portsmouth sometime during the war.

[George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI) inspecting the crew of the [HNoMS *Draug*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNoMS_Draug_\(1908\)) in Portsmouth during the Second World War

On 1 October 1916, Portsmouth was bombed by a [Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeppelin) airship.[^cite_note-nazi1-98] Although the [Oberste Heeresleitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberste_Heeresleitung) (German Supreme Army Command) said that the town was "lavishly bombarded with good results", there were no reports of bombs dropped in the area.[^cite_note-99] According to another source, the bombs were mistakenly dropped into the harbour rather than the dockyard.[^cite_note-nazi1-98] About 1,200 ships were refitted in the dockyard during the war, making it one of the empire's most strategic ports at the time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201391-91]

Portsmouth's boundaries were extended onto the mainland of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) between 1920 and 1932 by incorporating [Paulsgrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulsgrove), [Wymering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wymering), [Cosham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosham), [Drayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drayton,_Hampshire) and [Farlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farlington,_Hampshire) into Portsmouth.[^cite_note-portsenc-92] Portsmouth was granted [city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) in 1926 after a long campaign by the borough council.[^cite_note-portsenc-92] The application was made on the grounds that it was the "first naval port of the kingdom".[^cite_note-100] In 1929, the city council added the [motto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motto) "Heaven's Light Our Guide" to the medieval coat of arms. Except for the celestial objects in the arms, the motto was that of the [Star of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Star_of_India) and referred to the troopships bound for [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) which left from the port.[^cite_note-blu-101] The crest and [supporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporter) are based on those of the [royal arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_arms_of_the_United_Kingdom), but altered to show the city's maritime connections: the lions and unicorn have fish tails, and a [naval crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_crown) and a representation of the Tudor defensive boom which stretched across Portsmouth Harbour are around the unicorn's neck.[^cite_note-boom-50][^cite_note-blu-101]

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the city (particularly the port) was bombed extensively by the [Luftwaffe](17885.md) in the Portsmouth Blitz.[^cite_note-history3-11] Portsmouth experienced 67 air raids between July 1940 and May 1944, which destroyed 6,625 houses and severely damaged 6,549.[^cite_note-history2-27] The air raids caused 930 deaths and wounded almost 3,000 people,[^cite_note-guild-102][^cite_note-103] many in the dockyard and military establishments.[^cite_note-104] On the night of the city's heaviest raid (10 January 1941), the Luftwaffe dropped 140 tonnes of high-explosive bombs which killed 171 people and left 3,000 homeless.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013151-105] Many of the city's houses were damaged, and areas of [Landport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landport) and Old Portsmouth destroyed; the future site of [Gunwharf Quays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunwharf_Quays) was razed to the ground.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013186-106] The [Guildhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Guildhall) was hit by an incendiary bomb which burnt out the interior and destroyed its inner walls,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013147-107] although the civic plate was retrieved unharmed from the vault under the front steps.[^cite_note-guild-102] After the raid, Portsmouth mayor Denis Daley wrote for the *Evening News*:

> We are bruised but we are not daunted, and we are still as determined as ever to stand side by side with other cities who have felt the blast of the enemy, and we shall, with them, persevere with an unflagging spirit towards a conclusive and decisive victory.

— Sir Denis Daley, January 1941[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013146-108]

Portsmouth Harbour was a vital military embarkation point for the 6 June 1944 D-Day landings. [Southwick House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwick_House), just north of the city, was the headquarters of Supreme Allied Commander [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013155,_156-109][^cite_note-110] A [V-1 flying bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb) hit Newcomen Road on 15 July 1944, killing 15 people.[^cite_note-history2-27]

### 1945 to present

Much of the city's housing stock was damaged during the war. The wreckage was cleared in an attempt to improve housing quality after the war; before permanent accommodations could be built, Portsmouth City Council built [prefabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefabs_in_the_United_Kingdom) for those who had lost their homes. More than 700 prefab houses were constructed between 1945 and 1947, some over bomb sites.[^cite_note-history2-27] The first permanent houses were built away from the city centre, in new developments such as Paulsgrove and [Leigh Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leigh_Park);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013160-111][^cite_note-Leigh_Park_history-112] construction of council estates in Paulsgrove was completed in 1953. The first Leigh Park housing estates were completed in 1949, although construction in the area continued until 1974.[^cite_note-history2-27] Builders still occasionally find [unexploded bombs](406804.md), such as on the site of the destroyed Hippodrome Theatre in 1984.[^cite_note-113] Despite efforts by the city council to build new housing, a 1955 survey indicated that 7,000 houses in Portsmouth were unfit for human habitation. A controversial decision was made to replace a section of the central city, including Landport, Somerstown and Buckland, with council housing during the 1960s and early 1970s. The success of the project and the quality of its housing are debatable.[^cite_note-history2-27]

Her Majesty's Yacht Britannia is docked in Portsmouth Harbour for the 50th anniversary of the D-Day Landings in 1994. More modern Royal Navy ships are docked in behind her, and the masts of the HMS Victory can be seen in the far background.

[Her Majesty's Yacht *Britannia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMY_Britannia) in Portsmouth Harbour during the 50th anniversary of the D-Day Landings in 1994. The masts of [HMS *Victory*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Victory) can be seen in the background.

Portsmouth was affected by the decline of the British Empire in the second half of the 20th century. Shipbuilding jobs fell from 46 per cent of the workforce in 1951 to 14 per cent in 1966, drastically reducing manpower in the dockyard. The city council attempted to create new work; an industrial estate was built in Fratton in 1948, and others were built at Paulsgrove and Farlington during the 1950s and 1960s.[^cite_note-history2-27] Although traditional industries such as brewing and corset manufacturing disappeared during this time, electrical engineering became a major employer. Despite the cutbacks in traditional sectors, Portsmouth remained attractive to industry. [Zurich Insurance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Insurance_Group) moved their UK headquarters to the city in 1968, and [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) relocated their European headquarters in 1979.[^cite_note-history2-27] Portsmouth's population had dropped from about 200,000 to 177,142 by the end of the 1960s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013161-114] Defence Secretary [John Nott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nott) decided in the early 1980s that of the four home dockyards, Portsmouth and [Chatham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Dockyard) would be closed. The city council won a concession, however, and the dockyard was downgraded instead to a naval base.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201392-115]

In 1956, the Soviet cruiser [Ordzhonikidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_cruiser_Irian) docked in Portsmouth harbour on a diplomatic mission that had taken head of state [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev) and [Nikolai Bulganin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Bulganin) to Britain. Naval intelligence was interested in the design of the ship and [MI6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MI6) recruited diver [Lionel Crabb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Crabb) to collect intelligence on the ship particularly its propulsion. After diving into the harbour Crabb was never seen again. This led to a diplomatic incident with the Soviet Union and scandal in British domestic politics.[^cite_note-116]

On 2 April 1982, Argentine forces invaded two British territories in the South Atlantic: the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md) and [South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Georgia_and_the_South_Sandwich_Islands). The British government's response was to dispatch a [naval task force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_logistics_in_the_Falklands_War), and the aircraft carriers [HMS *Hermes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Hermes_\(R12\)) and [HMS *Invincible*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Invincible_\(R05\)) sailed from Portsmouth for the South Atlantic on 5 April. The successful outcome of the war reaffirmed Portsmouth's significance as a naval port and its importance to the defence of British interests.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201393-117] In January 1997, [Her Majesty's Yacht *Britannia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMY_Britannia) embarked from the city on her final voyage to oversee the handover of Hong Kong; for many, this marked the end of the empire.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrendon2007660-118][^cite_note-119] She was decommissioned on 11 December of that year at Portsmouth Naval Base in the presence of [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II), the [Duke of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Philip,_Duke_of_Edinburgh), and twelve senior members of the royal family.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

Redevelopment of the naval shore establishment [HMS *Vernon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Vernon_\(shore_establishment\)) began in 2001 as a complex of retail outlets, clubs, pubs, and a shopping centre known as Gunwharf Quays.[^cite_note-history2-27] Construction of the 552-foot-tall (168 m) [Spinnaker Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinnaker_Tower), sponsored by the [National Lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lottery_\(United_Kingdom\)), began at Gunwharf Quays in 2003.[^cite_note-122] The [Tricorn Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricorn_Centre), called "the ugliest building in the UK" by the BBC, was demolished in late 2004 after years of debate over the expense of demolition and whether it was worth preserving as an example of 1960s [brutalist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist_architecture).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClark2009-124] Designed by [Owen Luder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Luder) as part of a project to "revitalise" Portsmouth in the 1960s, it consisted of a shopping centre, market, nightclubs, and a [multistorey car park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multistorey_car_park).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013164-125] Portsmouth celebrated the 200th anniversary of the [Battle of Trafalgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trafalgar) in 2005, with Queen Elizabeth II present at a [fleet review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_review) and a mock battle.[^cite_note-history2-27] The naval base is home to two-thirds of Britain's surface fleet.[^cite_note-RN1-126] The city also hosted international commemorations for 50th, 75th and 80th anniversaries of the D-Day landings, these were attended by international leaders and remaining veterans.

## Geography

An aerial view of western side of Portsmouth (including Gunwharf Quays, the dockyard and the Spinnaker tower), the harbour itself, and the town of Gosport

Aerial view of Portsmouth and Portsmouth Harbour



England population density and low elevation coastal zones. Portsmouth is particularly vulnerable to [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise).

Portsmouth is 73.5 miles (118.3 km) by road from central London, 49.5 miles (79.7 km) west of [Brighton](49796.md), and 22.3 miles (35.9 km) east of [Southampton](7920751.md).[^cite_note-GM-127] It is located primarily on [Portsea Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsea_Island) and is the United Kingdom's only island city, although the city has expanded to the mainland.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201314,_16-128] [Gosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosport) is a town and borough to the west.[^cite_note-GM-127] Portsea Island is separated from the mainland by [Portsbridge Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsbridge_Creek),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVine1990-129] which is crossed by three road bridges (the [M275 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M275_motorway), the [A3 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_road), and the [A2030 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2030_road)), a railway bridge, and two footbridges.[^cite_note-130] Portsea Island, part of the [Hampshire Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_Basin),[^cite_note-131] is low-lying; most of the island is less than 3 metres (9.8 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_above_sea_level).[^cite_note-rising-132][^cite_note-133] The island's highest natural elevation is the Kingston Cross road junction, at 21 feet (6.4 m) above ordinary spring tide.[^cite_note-highpoint-134]

[Old Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Portsmouth), the original town, is in the south-west part of the island and includes [Portsmouth Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Point) (nicknamed Spice Island).[^cite_note-135] The main channel entering Portsmouth Harbour, west of the island,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVine1990-129] passes between Old Portsmouth and Gosport.[^cite_note-GM-127] Portsmouth Harbour has a series of lakes, including Fountain Lake (near the commercial port), Portchester Lake (south central), Paulsgrove Lake (north), Brick Kiln Lake and Tipner (east), and Bombketch and Spider Lakes (west). Further northwest, around Portchester, are Wicor, Cams, and Great Cams Lakes.[^cite_note-GM-127] The large tidal inlet of [Langstone Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langstone_Harbour) is east of the island. The [Farlington Marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farlington_Marshes), in the north off the coast of [Farlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farlington,_Hampshire), is a 125 hectares (310 acres) [grazing marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazing_marsh) and saline lagoon. One of the oldest [local reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_nature_reserve) in the county, built from reclaimed land in 1771, it provides a habitat for migratory wildfowl and [waders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wader).[^cite_note-136]

A high aerial view of Portsea Island (the island which Portsmouth is situated on), and neighbouring Hayling Island

Portsea Island and Hayling Island

South of Portsmouth are [Spithead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spithead), the [Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solent), and the [Isle of Wight](15102.md). Its southern coast was fortified by the [Round Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Tower_\(Portsmouth\)), the [Square Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_Tower), Southsea Castle, [Lumps Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumps_Fort) and [Fort Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Cumberland_\(England\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPatterson1985-137] Four Palmerston Forts were built in the Solent: [Spitbank Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitbank_Fort), [St Helens Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Helens_Fort), [Horse Sand Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_Sand_Fort) and [No Man's Land Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Man's_Land_Fort).

The resort of [Southsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea) is on the central southern shoreline of Portsea Island,[^cite_note-pissea-138] and [Eastney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastney) is east.[^cite_note-eastney-139] Eastney Lake covered nearly 170 acres (69 hectares) in 1626.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWebb198911-140] North of Eastney is the residential [Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton,_Portsmouth) and an area of reclaimed land known as Milton Common (formerly Milton Lake),[^cite_note-GM-127] a "flat scrubby land with a series of freshwater lakes".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELong2007188-141] Further north on the east coast is [Baffins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baffins), with the Great Salterns recreation ground and golf course around [Portsmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_College).[^cite_note-GM-127]

The [Hilsea Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilsea_Lines) are a series of defunct fortifications on the island's north coast, bordering Portsbridge Creek and the mainland.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201380-142][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMitchell1988-143] [Portsdown Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsdown_Hill) dominates the skyline in the north, and contains several large Palmerston Forts[^cite_note-144] such as [Fort Fareham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Fareham), [Fort Wallington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wallington), [Fort Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Nelson,_Hampshire), [Fort Southwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Southwick), [Fort Widley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Widley), and [Fort Purbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Purbrook).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPatterson1985-137][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201379,_80-145] Portsdown Hill is a large band of [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk); the rest of Portsea Island is composed of layers of [London Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Clay) and [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand) (part of the [Bagshot Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagshot_Formation)), formed principally during the [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene).[^cite_note-146]

Northern areas of the city include [Stamshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamshaw), [Hilsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilsea) and [Copnor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copnor), [Cosham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosham), [Drayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drayton,_Hampshire), [Farlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farlington,_Hampshire), Paulsgrove and [Port Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Solent).[^cite_note-147] Other districts include [North End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_End,_Hampshire) and Fratton.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] The west of the city contains [council estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_housing_in_the_United_Kingdom), such as [Buckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckland,_Portsmouth), [Landport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landport), and Portsea, which replaced Victorian terraces destroyed by Second World War bombing.[^cite_note-history2-27] After the war, the 2,000-acre (810 ha) [Leigh Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leigh_Park) estate was built to address the chronic housing shortage during post-war reconstruction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013160-111] Although the estate has been under the jurisdiction of [Havant Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Havant) since the early 2000s, Portsmouth City Council remains its landlord (the borough's largest landowner).[^cite_note-Leigh_Park_history-112]

The city's main station, [Portsmouth and Southsea railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_and_Southsea_railway_station),[^cite_note-150] is in the city centre near the [Guildhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Guildhall) and the [Civic Offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Civic_Offices).[^cite_note-guild-102][^cite_note-151] South of the Guildhall is Guildhall Walk, with a number of pubs and clubs.[^cite_note-152] The city's other railway station, [Portsmouth Harbour railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Harbour_railway_station), is located on a pier at the harbour's edge, near Old Portsmouth.[^cite_note-153] Edinburgh Road contains the city's Roman Catholic cathedral and [Victoria Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Park,_Portsmouth), a 15-acre (6.1 ha) park which opened in 1878.[^cite_note-vic-154]

### Climate

Portsmouth has a mild [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), with more sunshine than most of the British Isles.[^cite_note-155] Frosts are light and short-lived and snow is quite rare in winter, with temperatures rarely dropping below freezing.[^cite_note-rising-132] The average maximum temperature in January is 10 °C (50 °F), and the average minimum is 5 °C (41 °F). The lowest recorded temperature is −8 °C (18 °F).[^cite_note-metextreme-156] In summer, temperatures sometimes reach 30 °C (86 °F). The average maximum temperature in July is 22 °C (72 °F), and the average minimum is 15 °C (59 °F). The highest recorded temperature is 35 °C (95 °F).[^cite_note-metextreme-156] The city gets about 645 millimetres (25.4 in) of rain annually, with a minimum of 1 mm (0.04 in) of rain reported 103 days per year.[^cite_note-weather-157]

## Demography



Population pyramid of Portsmouth (unitary authority) in 2021

Portsmouth is the second-most densely populated city in the [United Kingdom](31717.md), after [London](17867.md).[^cite_note-PortsmouthCouncil-161][^cite_note-OffNatStat-162] In the [2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%932022_United_Kingdom_censuses), the city had 208,100 residents.[^cite_note-auto-9] The city used to be even more densely populated, with the 1951 census showing a population of 233,545.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDickinson1998390-163][^cite_note-164] In a reversal of that decrease, its population has been gradually increasing since the 1990s.[^cite_note-165] With about 860,000 residents, [South Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Hampshire) is the [fifth-largest urban area in England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_the_United_Kingdom) and the largest in [South East England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_England) outside London; it is the centre of one of the United Kingdom's most-populous metropolitan areas.[^cite_note-166]

The city is predominantly white (85.3% of the population). However, Portsmouth's long association with the Royal Navy ensures some diversity.[^cite_note-hant-167] Some large, well-established non-white communities have their roots in the Royal Navy, particularly the [Chinese](372983.md) community from [British Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong).[^cite_note-hant-167][^cite_note-168] Portsmouth's long industrial history with the Royal Navy has drawn many people from across the British Isles (particularly Irish Catholics) to its factories and docks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaly201127-169][^cite_note-172] According to the 2021 census, Portsmouth's population was 78% [White British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_British), 6.8% [other White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Other_White), 2.3% [Bangladeshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Bangladeshis), 2.6% [mixed race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_\(United_Kingdom_ethnicity_category\)), 1.5% [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indians), 1.0% [Chinese](1255834.md), 1.8% [other](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_census), 0.5% [Black African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_British_people), 0.5% [white Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_people_in_Great_Britain), 1.8% [other Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Asians), 0.3% [Pakistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pakistanis), 2.6% [Black Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_African-Caribbean_people) and 0.4% other Black.[^cite_note-\:0-173]

## Politics

A front-facing view of Portsmouth Guildhall and the surrounding civic offices

The neo-classical [Portsmouth Guildhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Guildhall) and surrounding [Civic Offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Civic_Offices) are the centre of government.



[Portsmouth North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_North)&#x20;



[Portsmouth South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_South)&#x20;



The 14 electoral wards of Portsmouth

The city is administered by [Portsmouth City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_City_Council), a [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority) which is responsible for local affairs. Portsmouth was granted its first market town charter in 1194.[^cite_note-179] In 1904, its boundaries were extended to all of Portsea Island and were later expanded onto the mainland of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) between 1920 and 1932 by incorporating Paulsgrove, [Wymering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wymering), [Cosham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosham), [Drayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drayton,_Hampshire) and [Farlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farlington,_Hampshire) into Portsmouth.[^cite_note-portsmouth1-180] Portsmouth was granted [city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) on 21 April 1926.[^cite_note-portsmouth1-180]

On 1 April 1974, it formed the second tier of local government (below [Hampshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_County_Council));[^cite_note-181] Portsmouth and Southampton became administratively independent of Hampshire with the creation of the unitary authority on 1 April 1997.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201318-182]

The city is divided into two parliamentary constituencies, [Portsmouth South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_South) and [Portsmouth North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_North), represented in the [House of Commons](13828.md) by [Stephen Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Morgan_\(British_politician\)) and [Amanda Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Martin), both of the [Labour Party](19279158.md).[^cite_note-183] The two Parliamentary constituencies each contain 7 electoral wards, giving an overall total of 14 electoral wards. Portsmouth's inner city centre is located in the Portsmouth South constituency.

Portsmouth City Council has 14 electoral wards, each ward returns three councillors, making 42 in total.[^cite_note-ward-184] Each councillor serves a four-year term.[^cite_note-185] Following the [May 2018 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_Kingdom_local_elections), the [Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)) formed a minority administration, they have run the city since then. The leader of the council is the Liberal Democrat, [Gerald Vernon-Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Vernon-Jackson). The lord mayor usually has a one-year term.[^cite_note-186]

The council is based in the [Civic Offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Civic_Offices), which house the tax support, housing-benefits, resident-services, and municipal-functions departments.[^cite_note-187] They are in Guildhall Square, with the [Portsmouth Guildhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Guildhall) and Portsmouth Central Library. The Guildhall, a symbol of Portsmouth, is a cultural venue. It was designed by [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds)-based architect [William Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hill_\(English_architect\)), who began it in the [neo-classical style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism) in 1873 at a cost of £140,000.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013146-108][^cite_note-188] It was opened to the public in 1890.[^cite_note-189]

In March 2026, the government announced that it would abolish the existing city council in 2028 as part of [local government reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England) across Hampshire.[^cite_note-190] These proposals were withdrawn in September 2026.[^cite_note-191]

### Minister for Portsmouth

Between January 2014 and July 2016, Portsmouth uniquely had a dedicated government minister, the [Minister for Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Portsmouth), a position created in response to the loss of 900 jobs from BAE Systems within HMNB Portsmouth. The minister was charged with bringing economic growth to the city.[^cite_note-192]

## Economy

In this photograph, many large containers and other cargo are lined up in the city's ferry port. A ferry can be seen docked in the background.

[Portsmouth International Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_International_Port) is a major employer.

Ten per cent of Portsmouth's workforce is employed at [Portsmouth Naval Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNB_Portsmouth), which is linked to the city's biggest industry, defence; the headquarters of [BAE Systems Surface Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Surface_Ships) is in the city.[^cite_note-193] BAE's Portsmouth shipyard received construction work on the two new [*Queen Elizabeth*-class aircraft carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth-class_aircraft_carrier).[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195][^cite_note-tel-196] A £100 million contract was signed to develop needed facilities for the vessels.[^cite_note-tel-196] However defence shipbuilding was ended in the city in favour of Glasgow during the 2014 Scottish Independence referendum. The Government was accused of making the decision to keep the more antiquated Glasgow shipyard over Portsmouth for political reasons as part of the pro union campaign. Ministers at the time did state shipbuilding would return to the city if Scotland left the UK. A minister for Portsmouth was established to help deal with the economic fallout of the decision.[^cite_note-197] A ferry port handles passengers and cargo,[^cite_note-198] and a fishing fleet of 20 to 30 boats operates out of Camber Quay, [Old Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Portsmouth); most of the catch is sold at the quayside fish market.[^cite_note-fish-199]

The city is host to [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM)'s UK headquarters and Portsmouth was also the UK headquarters of [Zurich Financial Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Financial_Services) until 2007.[^cite_note-history2-27][^cite_note-200] City shopping is centred on Commercial Road and the 1980s Cascades Shopping Centre.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202] The shopping centre has 185,000 to 230,000 visitors weekly.[^cite_note-203] Redevelopment has created new shopping areas, including the [Gunwharf Quays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunwharf_Quays) (the repurposed [HMS *Vernon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Vernon_\(shore_establishment\)) shore establishment,[^cite_note-guns-204] with stores, restaurants and a cinema) and the Historic Dockyard, which caters to tourists and holds an annual Victorian [Christmas market](1340652.md).[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-gun-206] Ocean Retail Park, on the north-eastern side of Portsea Island, was built in September 1985 on the site of a former metal-box factory.[^cite_note-207]

A view of some shops in the Gunwharf Quays shopping centre.

[Gunwharf Quays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunwharf_Quays) shopping centre

Development of Gunwharf Quays continued until 2007, when the 330-foot-tall (101 m) No. 1 Gunwharf Quays residential tower was completed.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209] The development of the former Brickwoods Brewery site included the construction of the 22-storey Admiralty Quarter Tower, the tallest in a complex of primarily low-rise residential buildings.[^cite_note-210] Number One Portsmouth, a proposed 25-storey 330 feet (101 m) tower opposite Portsmouth & Southsea station, was announced at the end of October 2008.[^cite_note-211] In August 2009, internal demolition of the existing building had begun.[^cite_note-212] A high-rise student dormitory, nicknamed "The Blade", has begun construction on the site of the [swimming baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_pool) at the edge of Victoria Park. The 300-foot (91 m) tower will be Portsmouth's second-tallest structure, after the Spinnaker Tower.[^cite_note-213]

In April 2007, Portsmouth F.C. announced plans to move from Fratton Park to a new stadium on reclaimed land next to the Historic Dockyard. The £600 million mixed-use development, designed by [Herzog & de Meuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzog_&_de_Meuron), would include shops, offices and 1,500 harbourside apartments.[^cite_note-SAA-214][^cite_note-215] The scheme was criticised for its size and location, and some officials said that it would interfere with harbour operations.[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217] The project was rejected by the city council due to the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis).[^cite_note-218]

A Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carrier docked in Scotland. This ship is one of two aircraft carriers, Portsmouth is its home port.

Portsmouth is the home port of the two *Queen Elizabeth*-class aircraft carriers.

Portsmouth's two Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carriers, [HMS *Queen Elizabeth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Queen_Elizabeth_\(R08\)) and [HMS *Prince of Wales*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Prince_of_Wales_\(R09\)), were ordered by defence secretary [Des Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Browne) on 25 July 2007.[^cite_note-219] They were built in the Firth of Forth at [Rosyth Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosyth_Dockyard) and [BAE Systems Surface Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Surface_Ships) in Glasgow, [Babcock International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babcock_International) at Rosyth, and at HMNB Portsmouth.[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] The government announced before the [2014 Scottish independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Scottish_independence_referendum) that military shipbuilding would end in Portsmouth, with all UK surface-warship construction focused on the two older BAE facilities in Glasgow.[^cite_note-end-222] The announcement was criticised by local politicians as a political decision to aid the referendum's "No" campaign.[^cite_note-223]

## Culture

Portsmouth has several theatres. The [New Theatre Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Theatre_Royal) in Guildhall Walk, near the city centre, specialises in professional drama.[^cite_note-224] The restored Kings Theatre in Southsea features amateur musicals and national tours.[^cite_note-225] The Groundlings Theatre, built in 1784, is housed at the Old Beneficial School in Portsea.[^cite_note-226] New Prince's Theatre and Southsea's Kings Theatre were designed by Victorian architect [Frank Matcham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Matcham).[^cite_note-227]

The city has three musical venues: the Guildhall,[^cite_note-228] [the Wedgewood Rooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wedgewood_Rooms) (which includes Edge of the Wedge, a smaller venue),[^cite_note-229] and [Portsmouth Pyramids Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Pyramids_Centre).[^cite_note-230] [Portsmouth Guildhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Guildhall) is one of the largest venues in [South East England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_England), with a [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of 2,500.[^cite_note-guild-102][^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232] A concert series is presented at the Guildhall by the [Bournemouth Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth_Symphony_Orchestra).[^cite_note-233] The [Portsmouth Sinfonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Sinfonia) approached classical music from a different angle during the 1970s, recruiting players with [no musical training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outsider_music) or who played an instrument new to them.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235] The Portsmouth Summer Show is held at [King George's Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George's_Fields). The 2016 show held during the last weekend of April, featured [cover bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_band) such as the Silver Beatles, the Bog Rolling Stones, and Fleetingwood Mac.[^cite_note-236]

A number of musical works are set in the city. *[H.M.S. Pinafore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.M.S._Pinafore)* is a comic opera in two acts set in Portsmouth Harbour, with music by [Arthur Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Sullivan) and libretto by [W.S. Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W.S._Gilbert).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStedman1996157–158-237] [*Portsmouth Point*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Point_\(Walton\)) is a 1925 [overture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overture) for orchestra by English composer [William Walton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Walton), inspired by [Thomas Rowlandson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Rowlandson)'s etching of Portsmouth Point in Old Portsmouth.[^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239] The overture has been recorded many times,[^cite_note-240] and often played at the BBC [Proms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Proms).[^cite_note-241] [John Cranko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cranko)'s 1951 ballet *[Pineapple Poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple_Poll)*, which features music from [Gilbert and Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_and_Sullivan) operettas and is loosely based on Gilbert's poem *The Bumboat Woman's Story*, is also set in Portsmouth.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243]

Portsmouth hosts yearly remembrances of the D-Day landings, attended by veterans from Allied and Commonwealth nations.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245] The city played a major role in the 50th D-Day anniversary in 1994; visitors included US President [Bill Clinton](3356.md), Australian Prime Minister [Paul Keating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Keating), King [Harald V of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_V_of_Norway), French President [François Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand), New Zealand Prime Minister [Jim Bolger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Bolger), Canadian Prime Minister [Jean Chrétien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chr%C3%A9tien), Prime Minister [John Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Major), the Queen, and the Duke of Edinburgh.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247] The 75th Anniversary of D-Day was similarly commemorated in the city. Prime Minister [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May) led the event, and was joined by leaders of the US, Canada, Australia, France and Germany.[^cite_note-Mohan-Hickson-248]

The annual Portsmouth International Kite Festival, organised by the city council and the Kite Society of Great Britain, celebrated its 25th anniversary in 2016.[^cite_note-249]

[Victorious Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorious_Festival), the biggest metropolitan music festival in the UK takes place on Southsea Seafront in Portsmouth. It has been an annual event at this location since 2014. It is a large family-friendly music festival and has featured headliners including [Stereophonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereophonics), [Noel Gallagher's High Flying Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Gallagher's_High_Flying_Birds), [The Prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prodigy), and other prominent household names.[^cite_note-charity-250]

Portsmouth is frequently used as a filming location for television and film productions, especially the Historic Dockyard. Productions include [*Tommy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_\(1975_film\)), *[Tomorrow Never Dies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomorrow_Never_Dies)* (1997), the 2026 feature film [*Project Hail Mary*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Hail_Mary_\(film\)), [*Mansfield Park*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_Park_\(1999_film\)) and the Hollywood adaptation of *[Les Miserables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Miserables)*.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252]

In 2005, Portsmouth featured in the first series of ITV's *Britain's Toughest Towns*.[^cite_note-253] As this documentary also indicated, Portsmouth has issues with gangs and anti-social behaviour.[^cite_note-News_May_2021-254][^cite_note-News_June_2021...-255][^cite_note-News_June_2021-256][^cite_note-HL_June_2020-257][^cite_note-News_Sept_2020-258]

Portsmouth is home to Paul Stone, the street artist known as [My Dog Sighs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Dog_Sighs).[^cite_note-259] In 2021, for his INSIDE exhibition, he transformed a derelict ballroom into an immersive world inhabited by creatures that he called 'Quiet Little Voices'.[^cite_note-260] In 2023 he celebrated 20 years as a street artist by hiding £30,000 of art across the city in a [Free Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifted_art) giveaway.[^cite_note-261] He has been awarded an honorary doctorate by [University of Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Portsmouth).[^cite_note-262]

Portsmouth City Council made a bid on 28 November 2025 to become [UK City of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_City_of_Culture) 2029.[^cite_note-263]

## Literature



This statue to [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens) in Portsmouth is one of only three statues to the historic writer in the world. Dickens wrote in his will that he did not want such statues built in his honour.

Portsmouth is the hometown of Fanny Price, the main character of [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen)'s novel *[Mansfield Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_Park)*, and most of its closing chapters are set there.[^cite_note-264] Nicholas and Smike, the main protagonists of [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens)' novel *[The Life and Adventures of Nicholas Nickleby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_and_Adventures_of_Nicholas_Nickleby)*, make their way to Portsmouth and become involved with a theatrical troupe.[^cite_note-265] Portsmouth is most often the port from which Captain Jack Aubrey's ships sail in [Patrick O'Brian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_O'Brian)'s seafaring historical [Aubrey-Maturin series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aubrey-Maturin_series).[^cite_note-266] Portsmouth is the main setting of [Jonathan Meades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Meades)'s 1993 novel *Pompey*.[^cite_note-267] Since the novel was published, Meades has presented a TV programme documenting Victorian architecture in Portsmouth Dockyard.[^cite_note-268]

Victorian novelist and historian [Sir Walter Besant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Walter_Besant) documented his 1840s childhood in *By Celia's Arbour: A Tale of Portsmouth Town*, precisely describing the town before its defensive walls were removed.[^cite_note-269] Southsea (as Port Burdock) features in *[The History of Mr Polly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_History_of_Mr_Polly)* by [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells), who describes it as "one of the three townships that are grouped around the Port Burdock naval dockyards".[^cite_note-270] The resort is also the setting of the [graphic novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_novel) *[The Tragical Comedy or Comical Tragedy of Mr. Punch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tragical_Comedy_or_Comical_Tragedy_of_Mr._Punch)* by [high fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_fantasy) author [Neil Gaiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Gaiman), who grew up in Portsmouth. A Southsea street was renamed The Ocean at the End of the Lane by the city council in honour of Gaiman's [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ocean_at_the_End_of_the_Lane).[^cite_note-Flood-271][^cite_note-272]

Crime novels set in Portsmouth and the surrounding area include [Graham Hurley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Hurley)'s D.I. Faraday/D.C. Winter novels[^cite_note-graham-273] and [C. J. Sansom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._J._Sansom)'s Tudor crime novel, *Heartstone*; the latter refers to the warship *Mary Rose* and describes Tudor life in the town.[^cite_note-274] *Portsmouth Fairy Tales for Grown Ups*, a collection of short stories, was published in 2014.[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276] The collection, set around Portsmouth, includes stories by crime novelists William Sutton and Diana Bretherick.[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278]

## Education

See also: [List of schools in Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Portsmouth)

A side-facing view of the Park Building, one of the buildings which make up the University of Portsmouth

Park Building, University of Portsmouth

The [University of Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Portsmouth) was founded in 1992 as a [new university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_university) from Portsmouth Polytechnic; in 2016, it had 20,000 students.[^cite_note-279] The university was ranked among the world's top 100 modern universities in April 2015.[^cite_note-Gurney-280][^cite_note-uopnews-281] In 2013, it had about 23,000 students and over 2,500 staff members.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013198-282] Several local colleges also award [Higher National Diplomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_National_Diploma), including [Highbury College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highbury_College) (specialising in vocational education),[^cite_note-283] and Portsmouth College (which offers academic courses).[^cite_note-284] [Admiral Lord Nelson School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Lord_Nelson_School) and [Miltoncross Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miltoncross_Academy) were built in the late 1990s to meet the needs of a growing school-age population.[^cite_note-285][^cite_note-286]

After the cancellation of the national building programme for schools, redevelopment halted.[^cite_note-287] Two schools in the city were judged "inadequate", and 29 of its 63 schools were considered "no longer good enough" by [Ofsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofsted) in 2009.[^cite_note-288] Before it was taken over by [Ark Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ark_\(charity\)) and became [Ark Charter Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ark_Charter_Academy), St Luke's Church of England secondary school was one of England's worst schools in GCSE achievement. It was criticised by officials for its behavioural standards, with students reportedly throwing chairs at teachers.[^cite_note-289] Since it became an academy in 2009, the school has improved; 69 per cent of its students achieved five GCSEs with grades of A\* to C, including English and mathematics.[^cite_note-290] The academy's intake policy is for a standard comprehensive school, drawing from the community rather than by religion.[^cite_note-291]

[Portsmouth Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Grammar_School), the city's oldest independent school, was founded in 1732.[^cite_note-292][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013200-293] Other independent schools include [Portsmouth High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_High_School,_Southsea),[^cite_note-294] and [Mayville High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayville_High_School,_Southsea) (founded in 1897).[^cite_note-295]

## Landmarks

A view of the port side of HMS Warrior alongside Portsmouth Harbour. The Spinnaker Tower can be seen to the far left.

[HMS *Warrior*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Warrior_\(1860\)) (right) and the [Spinnaker Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinnaker_Tower) are two of Portsmouth's main attractions.

Many of Portsmouth's former defences are now museums or event venues. Several Victorian-era forts on Portsdown Hill are tourist attractions;[^cite_note-296] [Fort Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Nelson,_Hampshire), at its summit, is home to the Royal Armouries museum.[^cite_note-297] Tudor-era Southsea Castle has a small museum, and much of the seafront defences leading to the [Round Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Tower_\(Portsmouth\)) are open to the public. The castle was withdrawn from active service in 1960, and was purchased by Portsmouth City Council.[^cite_note-298] The southern part of the Royal Marines' [Eastney Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastney_Barracks) is now the [Royal Marines Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Marines_Museum), and was opened to the public under the [National Heritage Act 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Heritage_Act_1983).[^cite_note-299] The museum received a £14 million grant from the National Lottery Fund, and was scheduled to relocate to Portsmouth Historic Dockyard in 2019.[^cite_note-300] The birthplace of [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens), at Mile End Terrace,[^cite_note-dickens-301][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201373-302] is the [Charles Dickens' Birthplace Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens'_Birthplace_Museum); the four-storey red brick building became a Grade I listed building in 1953.[^cite_note-303] Other tourist attractions include the Blue Reef Aquarium (with an "underwater safari" of British aquatic life)[^cite_note-304] and the Cumberland House Natural History Museum, housing a variety of local wildlife.[^cite_note-305][^cite_note-306]

A picture of HMS Victory, the world's oldest commissioned naval ship, situated in Portsmouth's dry dock. The ship itself is missing its figurehead in this photo, but retains its original sails.

[HMS *Victory*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Victory) at Portsmouth Historic Dockyard, the world's oldest naval ship still in commission, is one of the city's most popular tourist attractions.

Most of the city's landmarks and tourist attractions are related to its naval history. They include [the D-Day Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_D-Day_Story) in Southsea, which contains the 83-metre-long (272 ft) [Overlord Embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overlord_Embroidery).[^cite_note-307][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013156-308] Portsmouth is home to several well-known ships; Horatio Nelson's flagship [HMS *Victory*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Victory), the world's oldest naval ship still in commission, is in the [dry dock](213739.md) of [Portsmouth Historic Dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Historic_Dockyard). The *Victory* was placed in a permanent dry dock in 1922 when the [Society for Nautical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Nautical_Research) led a national appeal to restore her,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201339-85] and 22 million people have visited the ship.[^cite_note-309] The remains of Henry VIII's flagship, *Mary Rose*, was rediscovered on the seabed in 1971.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201337-52] She was raised and brought to a purpose-built structure in Portsmouth Historic Dockyard in 1982.[^cite_note-rose-310] Britain's first iron-hulled warship, [HMS *Warrior*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Warrior_\(1860\)), was restored and moved to Portsmouth in June 1987 after serving as an oil fuel pier at [Pembroke Dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke_Dock) in [Pembrokeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembrokeshire) for fifty years.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201342-311][^cite_note-312][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWinton19875-313] The [National Museum of the Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_Royal_Navy), in the dockyard, is sponsored by a charity that promotes research into the Royal Dockyard's history and archaeology.[^cite_note-314] The dockyard hosts the Victorian Festival of Christmas, featuring [Father Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_Christmas) in a traditional green robe, each November.[^cite_note-315][^cite_note-316]

Portsmouth's long association with the armed forces is demonstrated by a large number of war memorials, including several at the Royal Marines Museum[^cite_note-317] and a large collection of memorials related to the Royal Navy in [Victoria Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Park,_Portsmouth).[^cite_note-vic-154] The [Portsmouth Naval Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Naval_Memorial), in Southsea Common, commemorates the 24,591 British sailors who died during both World Wars and have no known grave.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013135-318] Designed by Sir [Robert Lorimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lorimer), it was unveiled by [George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI) (then [Duke of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_York)) on 15 October 1924.[^cite_note-319] In the city centre, the [Guildhall Square Cenotaph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guildhall_Square_Cenotaph) contains the names of the fallen and is guarded by stone sculptures of machine gunners by [Charles Sargeant Jagger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Sargeant_Jagger).[^cite_note-320] The west face of the memorial reads:

> This memorial was erected by the people of Portsmouth in proud and loving memory of those who in the glorious morning of their days for England's sake lost all but England's praise. May light perpetual shine upon them.[^cite_note-321]

The city has three cemeteries: Kingston, Milton Road, and Highland Road. Kingston Cemetery, opened in 1856, is in east Fratton. At 52 acres (21 ha), it is Portsmouth's largest cemetery and has about 400 burials a year.[^cite_note-cemeteries-322] In February 2014, a ceremony celebrating the 180th anniversary of Portsmouth's Polish community was held at the cemetery.[^cite_note-323] The approximately 25-acre (10 ha) Milton Road Cemetery, founded on 8 April 1912, has about 200 burials per year. There is a [crematorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crematorium) in [Portchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portchester).[^cite_note-cemeteries-322]

Portsmouth Central Library in Guildhall Walk was built in 1976 in the [brutalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist_architecture) style, designed by city architect Ken Norrish; it is also known as the Norrish Central Library.[^cite_note-324][^cite_note-325][^cite_note-326] In 2026 it was chosen by Poet Laureate [Simon Armitage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Armitage) as one of the venues for his annual library tour which in that year visited libraries in places with initials N-P, celebrating the 50th anniversary of the library and the centenary of Portsmouth's city status.[^cite_note-327]

## Gunwharf Quays

Main article: [Gunwharf Quays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunwharf_Quays)

A view of the Spinnaker Tower from the ground at Gunwharf Quays. The tower itself resembles a sail, reflecting Portsmouth's maritime history.

The Spinnaker Tower, seen from the waterfront at Gunwharf Quays

The naval shore establishment [HMS *Vernon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Vernon_\(shore_establishment\)) contained the Royal Navy's arsenal; weapons and ammunition which would be taken from ships at its 'Gun Wharf' as they entered the harbour, and resupplied when they headed back to sea. The 1919 *Southsea and Portsmouth Official Guide* described the establishment as "the finest collections of weapons outside the Tower of London, containing more than 25,000 rifles".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013185,_186-328] During the early nineteenth century, the 'Gunwharf' supplied the fleet with a "grand arsenal" of cannons, mortars, bombs, and ordnance. Although gunpowder was not provided due to safety concerns, it could be obtained at Priddy's Hard (near Gosport).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen2015123-329] An armoury sold small arms to soldiers, and the [stone frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_frigate) also had blacksmith and carpenter shops for armourers. It was run by three officers: a *viz* (storekeeper), a clerk, and a foreman. By 1817, Gunwharf reportedly employed 5,000 men and housed the world's largest naval arsenal.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen2015123,_124-330]

HMS *Vernon* was closed on 1 April 1996[^cite_note-vernon-331] and was redeveloped by Portsmouth City Council as Gunwharf Quays,[^cite_note-guns-204] a mixed residential and retail site with [outlet stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlet_store), restaurants, pubs, cafés and a cinema.[^cite_note-332] Construction of the [Spinnaker Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinnaker_Tower) began in 2001, and was completed in the summer of 2005. The project exceeded its budget and cost £36 million, of which Portsmouth City Council contributed £11 million.[^cite_note-333][^cite_note-334][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013232-335] The 560-foot (170 m) tower is visible at a distance of 23 miles (37 km) in clear weather, and its viewing platforms overlook the Solent (towards the Isle of Wight), the harbour and Southsea Castle.[^cite_note-336][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013233-337] The tower weighs over 33,000 tonnes (32,000 long tons; 36,000 short tons).[^cite_note-338][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013233-337]and has the largest glass floor in Europe.

## Southsea

Main article: [Southsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea)

A view of the Southsea Promenade, which contains arcades, restaurants, cinemas and a pier (which cannot be seen in this photograph)

Southsea Promenade, which includes the Clarence Pier amusement park

The Portsmouth Naval Memorial in Southsea: a large stone pillar and a plaque commemorating the fallen sailors of both World Wars

[Portsmouth Naval Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Naval_Memorial) in Southsea

Southsea is a [seaside resort](293990.md) and residential area of Portsmouth located at the southern end of Portsea Island. Its name originates from Southsea Castle, a seafront castle built in 1544 by Henry VIII to help defend the [Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solent) and Portsmouth Harbour.[^cite_note-339] The area was developed in 1809 as Croxton Town; by the 1860s, the suburb of Southsea had expanded to provide working-class housing.[^cite_note-pissea-138] Southsea developed as a seaside and bathing resort.[^cite_note-pissea-138] A pump room and baths were built near the present-day [Clarence Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Pier), and a complex was developed which included vapour baths, showers, and card-playing and assembly rooms for holiday-goers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuail200016–17-340]

Clarence Pier, opened in 1861 by the [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VII) and [Princess of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_of_Denmark), was named after Portsmouth military governor [Lord Frederick FitzClarence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Frederick_FitzClarence) and was described as "one of the largest amusement parks on the south coast".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013140-341] [South Parade Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Parade_Pier) was built in 1878, and is among the United Kingdom's 55 remaining private piers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013138-342][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuail200046-343] Originally a terminal for ferries travelling to the Isle of Wight, it was soon redeveloped as an entertainment centre. The pier was rebuilt after fires in 1904, 1967 and 1974 (during the filming of *[Tommy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_\(1975_film\))*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013138-342][^cite_note-pissea-138] Plans were announced in 2015 for a Solent Eye at Clarence Pier: a £750,000, 24-gondola [Ferris wheel](175541.md) similar to the [London Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Eye).[^cite_note-344]

Southsea is dominated by Southsea Common, a 480-acre (190 ha) grassland created by draining the marshland next to the vapour baths in 1820. The common met the demands of the early-19th-century military for a clear [firing range](1237973.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuail200019–20-345] and parallels the shore from Clarence Pier to Southsea Castle.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuail200019–20-345] A popular recreation area, it hosts a number of annual events which include carnivals, Christmas markets, and Victorian festivals.[^cite_note-346][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013136-347] The common has a large collection of mature [elm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elm) trees, believed to be the oldest and largest surviving in Hampshire and which have escaped [Dutch elm disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_elm_disease) due to their isolation. Other plants include the [Canary Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Island) date palms (*[Phoenix canariensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_canariensis)*), some of Britain's largest, which have recently produced viable seed.[^cite_note-348]

The resort of Southsea previously had its own dedicated light railway line; the [Southsea Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea_Railway) and its own terminus, [East Southsea railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Southsea_railway_station). The Southsea Railway and station were closed in 1914, with the station's name merged into that of Portsmouth's main railway station name in 1925.

## Religion

See also: [List of places of worship in Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_places_of_worship_in_Portsmouth)

A front facing view of Portsmouth's Roman Catholic cathedral, St John the Evangelist. The cathedral itself is made of brick and has a large chancel and nave at the front. Stained windows are also seen above the front door.

St John the Evangelist, the Roman Catholic cathedral built in 1882, is one of the city's two cathedrals.

Portsmouth has two cathedrals: the [Anglican Cathedral of St Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Cathedral) in [Old Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Portsmouth) and the Roman Catholic [Cathedral of St John the Evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St_John_the_Evangelist,_Portsmouth). The city is one of 34 British settlements with a [Roman Catholic](606848.md) cathedral.[^cite_note-cathhh-170][^cite_note-349] Portsmouth's first chapel, dedicated to [Thomas Becket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Becket), was built by Jean de Gisors in the second half of the 12th century.[^cite_note-350][^cite_note-chapel2-351] It was rebuilt and developed into a [parish church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_church) and an Anglican cathedral.[^cite_note-chapel2-351][^cite_note-352] Damaged during the 1642 [Siege of Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Portsmouth), its tower and [nave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nave) were rebuilt after [the Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Restoration).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnowles200621-353] Significant changes were made when the Diocese of Portsmouth was founded in 1927.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt201344-354] It became a cathedral in 1932 and was enlarged, although construction was halted during the Second World War. The cathedral was re-consecrated before [Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_The_Queen_Mother) in 1991.[^cite_note-355]

The [Royal Garrison Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domus_Dei) was founded in 1212 by [Peter des Roches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_des_Roches), [Bishop of Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester). After centuries of decay, it became an ammunition store in 1540. The 1662 marriage of Charles II and [Catherine of Braganza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Braganza) was celebrated in the church, and large receptions were held there after the defeat of [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) at the 1813 [Battle of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Leipzig). In 1941, a firebomb fell on its roof and destroyed the nave.[^cite_note-garr-60] Although the church's chancel was saved by servicemen shortly after the raid, replacing the roof was deemed impossible due to the large amounts of salt solution absorbed by the stonework.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewitt2013150-356]

The Cathedral of St John the Evangelist was built in 1882 to accommodate Portsmouth's increasing Roman Catholic population, and replaced a chapel built in 1796 to the west. Before 1791, Roman Catholic chapels in towns with [borough status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) were prohibited. The chapel opened after the [Roman Catholic Relief Act 1791](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Relief_Act_1791) was passed, and was replaced by the cathedral.[^cite_note-catholic-357] It was constructed in phases; the nave was completed in 1882; the [crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_\(architecture\)) in 1886, and the [chancel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancel) by 1893. During the blitz, the cathedral was badly damaged when Luftwaffe bombing destroyed Bishop's House next door; it was restored in 1970, 1982, and 2001.[^cite_note-catholic-357] The [Roman Catholic Diocese of Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Portsmouth) was founded in 1882 by [Pope Leo XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII).[^cite_note-359] Smaller places of worship in the city include St Jude's Church in Southsea,[^cite_note-360] [St Mary's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Church,_Portsea) in Portsea,[^cite_note-361] [St Ann's Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Ann's_Church,_HMNB_Portsmouth) in the naval base[^cite_note-362] and the [Portsmouth and Southsea Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_and_Southsea_Synagogue), one of Britain's oldest.[^cite_note-363] Other places of worship include the Immanuel Baptist Church, Southsea; Trinity Methodist Church, Highland Road; Buckland United Reformed Church; The Oasis Centre Elim Penteostal Church; Jubilee Pentecostal Church, Somers Road; Kings Church Assemblies of God (St Peter's Somers Road); Family Church; Christ Central Church, John Pounds Centre; The Jami Mosque, Bradford Junction; The Sikh Gurudwara, Margate Road.

## Sport

Fratton Park football stadium at night, home of Portsmouth F.C. The pitch is lit by floodlights.

Fratton Park, home of Portsmouth F.C.

[Portsmouth F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C.) play their home games at [Fratton Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fratton_Park). They have won two [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League) titles (1949 and 1950),[^cite_note-364][^cite_note-rsssf-365] and won the [FA Cup](11237.md) in 1939 and 2008.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeasom198421-366][^cite_note-367] The club returned to the [Premier League](11250.md) in 2003.[^cite_note-368] They were relegated to the [Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Championship) in 2010 and, experiencing serious financial difficulties in February 2012,[^cite_note-369] were relegated again to [League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_One). The club was relegated the following year to [League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_Two), the fourth tier of [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_England#Men's_League_system).[^cite_note-370] Portsmouth F.C. was purchased in April 2013 by the Pompey Supporters Trust, becoming the largest fan-owned club in English Football history.[^cite_note-371][^cite_note-372] In May 2017, as League Two champions, they were promoted to [League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_One) for the 2017–18 season. They won promotion back to the Championship as Champions of League One in May 2024.

[Moneyfields F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneyfields_F.C.) have played in the [Wessex Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wessex_Football_League) Premier Division since 1998.[^cite_note-373] [United Services Portsmouth F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Services_Portsmouth_F.C.) (formerly known as Portsmouth Royal Navy) and [Baffins Milton Rovers F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baffins_Milton_Rovers_F.C.) compete in Wessex League Division One; United Services was founded in 1962,[^cite_note-374] and Baffins Milton Rovers in 2011.[^cite_note-375] The [rugby](25405.md) teams [United Services Portsmouth RFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Services_Portsmouth_Rugby_Football_Club) and [Royal Navy Rugby Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy_Rugby_Union) play their home matches at the [United Services Recreation Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Services_Recreation_Ground). Royal Navy Rugby Union play in the annual [Army Navy Match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Navy_Match) at [Twickenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twickenham).[^cite_note-376]

Portsmouth began hosting [first-class cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_cricket) at the United Services Recreation Ground in 1882,[^cite_note-377] and [Hampshire County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_County_Cricket_Club) matches were played there from 1895 to 2000. In 2000, Hampshire moved their home matches to the new [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(cricket_ground\)) cricket ground in [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End,_Hampshire).[^cite_note-PRO-378] Portsmouth is home to two [hockey](10886.md) clubs: Portsmouth Hockey Club, based at the [Admiral Lord Nelson School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Lord_Nelson_School) and United Services Portsmouth Hockey Club, based on Burnaby Road.[^cite_note-379] Great Salterns Golf Club, established in 1926,[^cite_note-380] is an 18-hole parkland course with two holes played across a lake;[^cite_note-381] there are coastal courses at [Hayling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayling_Golf_Club?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the Gosport and Stokes Bay Golf Club.[^cite_note-GM-127] Boxing was a popular sport between 1910 and 1960, and a monument commemorating the city's boxing heritage was built in 2017.[^cite_note-382]

### Ferries

A view of various ferries, cargo and military vessels moving out of Portsmouth Harbour. This photograph was taken from the viewing deck of the Spinnaker Tower.

Ferries and cargo and military vessels in Portsmouth Harbour

[Portsmouth Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Harbour_railway_station) has passenger-ferry links to [Gosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosport) and the [Isle of Wight](15102.md),[^cite_note-385] with car-ferry service to the Isle of Wight nearby.[^cite_note-386] [Hovertravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovertravel), Britain's longest-standing commercial [hovercraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovercraft) service, begun in the 1960s, runs from near Clarence Pier in [Southsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea) to [Ryde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryde), Isle of Wight.[^cite_note-387] [Portsmouth International Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_International_Port) has links to [Caen](92443.md), [Cherbourg-Octeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherbourg-Octeville), [St Malo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Malo) and [Le Havre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Havre) in France,[^cite_note-388][^cite_note-389] [Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander,_Cantabria) and [Bilbao](68029.md) in Spain,[^cite_note-bit-390] and the [Channel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands).[^cite_note-chan-391] Ferry services from the port are operated by [Brittany Ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany_Ferries) and [DFDS Seaways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFDS_Seaways).[^cite_note-392]

On 18 May 2006, [Trasmediterranea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trasmediterranea) began service to Bilbao in competition with [P\&O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\&O_Ferries)'s service. Its ferry, *Fortuny*, was detained in Portsmouth by the [Maritime and Coastguard Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_and_Coastguard_Agency) for a number of safety violations.[^cite_note-393] They were quickly corrected and the service was cleared for passengers on 23 May that year.[^cite_note-394] Trasmediterránea discontinued its Bilbao service in March 2007, citing a need to deploy the *Fortuny* elsewhere.[^cite_note-395] P\&O Ferries ended their service to [Bilbao](68029.md) on 27 September 2010 due to "unsustainable losses".[^cite_note-396][^cite_note-397] The second-busiest ferry port in the UK (after [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover)), Portsmouth handles about three million passengers per year.[^cite_note-398][^cite_note-399]

Portsmouth has four railway stations on Portsea Island: [Hilsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilsea_railway_station), [Fratton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fratton_railway_station), [Portsmouth & Southsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_&_Southsea_railway_station)[^cite_note-402] and [Portsmouth Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Harbour_railway_station),[^cite_note-403] with a fifth station at [Cosham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosham_railway_station) in the northern mainland suburb of [Cosham, Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosham). Portsmouth previously had additional stations at [Southsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Southsea_railway_station), [Farlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farlington_Halt_railway_station) and [Paulsgrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulsgrove_Halt_railway_station), but these were closed at various periods of the twentieth century.

The city of Portsmouth is on two direct [South Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Western_Railway_\(train_operating_company\)) routes to [London Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Waterloo_railway_station), via [Guildford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guildford_railway_station) and via [Basingstoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basingstoke_railway_station).[^cite_note-404] There is a South Western Railway stopping service to [Southampton Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Central_railway_station) and [Great Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Western_Railway_\(train_operating_company\)) service to [Cardiff Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_Central_railway_station) via Southampton, [Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury_railway_station), [Bath Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath_Spa_railway_station) and [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Temple_Meads_railway_station).[^cite_note-405] [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_\(train_operating_company\)) has service to [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_railway_station), [Gatwick Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatwick_Airport_railway_station), [Croydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Croydon_railway_station) and [London Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Victoria_railway_station).[^cite_note-406]

#### Closed stations

Southsea once had its own branch line, the [Southsea Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southsea_Railway), which opened in 1885 between [Southsea railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Southsea_railway_station) and Fratton; it was closed in 1914 due to competition from tram services.[^cite_note-407]

[Farlington Halt railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farlington_Halt_railway_station) was built to serve Portsmouth Park [racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseracing_in_the_United_Kingdom), opening as Farlington Race Course on 26 June 1891.[^cite_note-Butt-408] The racecourse was closed during [World War One](4764461.md), but the station was retained to serve the ammunition dump put in its place.[^cite_note-409] The station closed in 1917.[^cite_note-Butt-408] Re-opened in 1922 until 1927.[^cite_note-Butt-408] Under the [Southern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Railway_\(UK\)), it re-opened as a general public [halt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_halt) in 1928 named *Farlington Halt*;[^cite_note-Butt-408] however, this was short-lived as the station closed due to insufficient customers on 4 July 1937.[^cite_note-Butt-408]

[Paulsgrove Halt railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulsgrove_Halt_railway_station)[^cite_note-410] was a railway station opened in 1928 to serve the adjacent Portsmouth [Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racecourse), a pony racing stronghold.[^cite_note-411] The station was formerly located between Cosham and Portchester stations. Paulsgrove Halt was closed along with the racecourse when the land was acquired by the military in 1939, at the outbreak of [World War II](32927.md).

### Air

[Portsmouth Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Airport_\(Hampshire\)), with a grass runway, was in operation from 1932 to 1973. After it closed, housing (Anchorage Park) and industry were built on the site.[^cite_note-412][^cite_note-413] The nearest airport is [Southampton Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Airport) in the [Borough of Eastleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Eastleigh), 19.8 miles (31.9 km) away.[^cite_note-GM-127] It has a [South Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Western_Railway_\(train_operating_company\)) rail connection, requiring a change at [Southampton Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Central_railway_station) or [Eastleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastleigh_railway_station).[^cite_note-414] [Heathrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Airport) and [Gatwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatwick_Airport) are 65 miles (105 km) and 75 miles (121 km) away, respectively. Gatwick is linked by Southern train service to London Victoria station and Heathrow is linked by coach to [Woking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woking), which is on both rail lines to London Waterloo and the [London Underground](17839.md).[^cite_note-415] Heathrow is linked to Portsmouth by National Express coaches.[^cite_note-416]

### Former canal



A map of the planned route of Portsmouth and Arundel Canal across Portsea Island from 1815

The [Portsmouth and Arundel Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_and_Arundel_Canal) ran between the towns and was built in 1823 by the Portsmouth & Arundel Navigation Company. Never financially successful, and found to be contaminating Portsea Island fresh water wells,[^cite_note-417] it was abandoned in 1855 and the company was wound up in 1888.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreen200618–19-418] The canal was part of a larger scheme for a secure inland canal route from London to Portsmouth, allowing boats to avoid the [English Channel](9230.md). It had three sections: a pair of ship canals (one on Portsea Island and one to [Chichester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichester)) and a barge canal from [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford,_West_Sussex) on the [River Arun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Arun) to [Hunston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunston,_West_Sussex), where it joined the canal's Chichester section.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmstrong1971144–145-419]

The route through Portsea Island began from a basin formerly located on Arundel Street and cut through [Landport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landport), Fratton and [Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton,_Portsmouth), ending at the eastern end of Locksway Road in Milton (where a set of lock gates accessed [Langstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langstone_Harbour) and [Chichester Harbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichester_Harbour). After the island route was closed, the drained canal-bed sections through Landport and Fratton were reused for the [Portsmouth Direct line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Direct_line), or filled-in to surface level to form a new main road route to Milton, named Goldsmith Avenue.

The brick-lined canal walls are clearly visible between the Fratton and Portsmouth & Southsea railway stations. The canal lock entrance at Locksway Road in Milton is east of the Thatched House [pub](24578.md).[^cite_note-420]

## Media

Portsmouth, Southampton and their adjacent towns are served primarily by programming from the [Rowridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowridge_transmitting_station) and [Chillerton Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chillerton_Down_transmitting_station) transmitters on the [Isle of Wight](15102.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPawley1972-421] although the transmitter at [Midhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midhurst_transmitting_station) can substitute for Rowridge. Portsmouth was one of the first cities in the UK to have a local TV station (MyTV), although the Isle of Wight began local television broadcasting in 1998.[^cite_note-422] In November 2014, [That's Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That's_Solent) was introduced as part of a nationwide roll-out of local [Freeview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeview_\(UK\)) channels in south-central England.[^cite_note-423] The stations broadcast from Rowridge.[^cite_note-424]

[BBC](19344654.md) local radio station that broadcast to the city is [BBC Radio Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Solent) on 96.1 FM. According to [RAJAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAJAR), popular radio stations include regional [Greatest Hits Radio South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits_Radio_South) and Global Radio's [Heart South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_South) and [Capital South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_South). [Easy Radio South Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easy_Radio_South_Coast) broadcasts from Southampton to the city on 107.4 MHz,[^cite_note-425] and the non-profit community station, Express FM, broadcasts on 93.7.[^cite_note-426] Patients at [Queen Alexandra Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Alexandra_Hospital) (Portsmouth's primary hospital) receive local programming from Portsmouth Hospital Broadcasting, which began in 1951.[^cite_note-427] When the first local commercial radio stations were licensed during the 1970s by the [Independent Broadcasting Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Broadcasting_Authority) (IBA), [Radio Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Victory) received the first licence and began broadcasting in 1975. In 1986, the IBA increased the Portsmouth licence to include Southampton and the Isle of Wight. The new licence went to Ocean Sound (later known as Ocean FM), with studios in [Fareham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fareham); Ocean FM became [Heart Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_Hampshire). For the city's 800th birthday in 1994, Victory FM broadcast for three 28-day periods over 18 months.[^cite_note-428] It was purchased by TLRC, who relaunched the station in 2001 as the Quay;[^cite_note-429] Portsmouth Football Club became a stakeholder in 2007, selling it in 2009.[^cite_note-430]

Portsmouth's daily newspaper is *[The News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_News_\(Portsmouth\))*, founded in 1873 and previously known as the *Portsmouth Evening News*. *The Journal*, a free weekly newspaper, is published by *News* publisher [Johnston Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnston_Press).[^cite_note-431][^cite_note-432]

## Notable people

See also: [List of people from Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_from_Portsmouth)

Portsmouth has been home to a number of famed authors; [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens), whose works include *[A Christmas Carol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol)*, *[Great Expectations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Expectations)*, *[Oliver Twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Twist)* and *[A Tale of Two Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Tale_of_Two_Cities)*, was born there.[^cite_note-433] [Arthur Conan Doyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Conan_Doyle), author of the [Sherlock Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes) stories, practised medicine in the city and played in goal for the amateur Portsmouth Association Football Club.[^cite_note-434] [Rudyard Kipling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudyard_Kipling) (poet and author of *[The Jungle Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jungle_Book)*)[^cite_note-blue-435] and [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells), author of *[The War of the Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_War_of_the_Worlds)* and *[The Time Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_Machine)*, lived in Portsmouth during the 1880s.[^cite_note-436] Novelist and historian [Walter Besant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Besant), author of *By Celia's Arbour, A Tale of Portsmouth Town*, was born in Portsmouth.[^cite_note-437][^cite_note-438] Historian [Frances Yates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Yates), known for her work on Renaissance [esotericism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_esotericism), was born in the city.[^cite_note-439][Francis Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Austen), brother of [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen), briefly lived in the area after graduating from Portsmouth Naval Academy.[^cite_note-440] Contemporary literary figures include social critic, journalist and author [Christopher Hitchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Hitchens), who was born in Portsmouth.[^cite_note-441] [Nevil Shute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevil_Shute) moved to the city in 1934 when he relocated his aircraft company, and his former home is in Southsea.[^cite_note-442] Fantasy author [Neil Gaiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Gaiman) grew up in [Purbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purbrook) and Southsea.[^cite_note-Flood-271][^cite_note-Bowlby-443]

[Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) engineer [Isambard Kingdom Brunel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isambard_Kingdom_Brunel) was born in Portsmouth.[^cite_note-444][^cite_note-445] His father, [Marc Isambard Brunel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Isambard_Brunel), worked for the Royal Navy and developed the world's first [production line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_line) to mass-produce [pulley blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_\(sailing\)) for ship rigging.[^cite_note-royal-78] [James Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Callaghan), British prime minister from 1976 to 1979, was born and raised in Portsmouth.[^cite_note-446][^cite_note-447] Son of a Protestant Northern Irish petty officer in the Royal Navy, Callaghan was the only person to hold all four [Great Offices of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Offices_of_State): foreign secretary, home secretary, chancellor and prime minister.[^cite_note-448] [John Pounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pounds), the founder of [ragged schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragged_school) (which provided free education to working-class children), lived in Portsmouth and founded England's first ragged school there.[^cite_note-449]

Comedian and actor [Peter Sellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sellers) was born in Southsea,[^cite_note-450] and [Arnold Schwarzenegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger) briefly lived and trained in Portsmouth.[^cite_note-451] Other actors who were born or lived in the city include *[EastEnders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EastEnders)* actresses [Emma Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Barton)[^cite_note-452] and [Lorraine Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorraine_Stanley), comedienne[^cite_note-453] and singer [Audrey Jeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audrey_Jeans),[^cite_note-454] and Bollywood actress [Geeta Basra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geeta_Basra).[^cite_note-455] Internationally-renowned street artist [My Dog Sighs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Dog_Sighs) lives in the city and his murals can be seen on many walls in the city.[^cite_note-20-years-456] Cryptozoologist Jonathan Downes was born in Portsmouth, and lived there for a time.[^cite_note-457] [Ant Middleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant_Middleton), former SBS, current television presenter and author was born in Portsmouth.[^cite_note-458] [Helen Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Duncan), the last person to be imprisoned under the 1735 Witchcraft Act, was arrested in Portsmouth.[^cite_note-459]

Notable sportspeople include Commonwealth Games gold medalist [Michael East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_East_\(athlete\)),[^cite_note-460] Olympic medallist in cycling [Rob Hayles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Hayles),[^cite_note-461] former British light-heavyweight boxing champion [Tony Oakey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Oakey),[^cite_note-462] Olympic medallist [Alan Pascoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Pascoe) as well as professional footballer [Mason Mount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason_Mount).[^cite_note-463] Single-handed yachtsman [Alec Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Rose),[^cite_note-464] [2003 World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_World_Aquatics_Championships) gold medallist [Katy Sexton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Sexton),[^cite_note-465] and Olympic medallist [Roger Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Black) were also born in the city.[^cite_note-466] [Jamshid bin Abdullah of Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamshid_bin_Abdullah_of_Zanzibar), the last constitutional monarch of the island state, lived in exile in Portsmouth with his wife and six children,[^cite_note-467] prior to resettling in his ancestral land of [Oman](22316.md).[^cite_note-468]
