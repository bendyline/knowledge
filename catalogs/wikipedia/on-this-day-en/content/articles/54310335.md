**Ana Brnabić**[^cite_note-1] (born 28 September 1975) is a Serbian politician serving as acting [President of Serbia](774382.md) since 28 September 2026 and [president of the National Assembly of Serbia](23179864.md) since 2024. A member of the [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md), she previously served as [Prime Minister of Serbia](606320.md) from 2017 to 2024. She was the [first woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_elected_and_appointed_female_heads_of_state_and_government), [first openly lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_first_openly_LGBTQ_holders_of_political_offices), and longest-serving person to hold the office of prime minister.[^cite_note-Surk-2][^cite_note-3]

She entered [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_cabinet_of_Aleksandar_Vu%C4%8Di%C4%87) as the [minister of state administration and local self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_State_Administration_and_Local_Self-Government_\(Serbia\))[^cite_note-4] from 11 August 2016 until 29 June 2017, under prime minister [Aleksandar Vučić](13349267.md) and acting prime minister [Ivica Dačić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivica_Da%C4%8Di%C4%87). In this role, Brnabić initiated reforms of central government services in Serbia.

After Vučić was inaugurated as the president of Serbia on 31 May 2017, he proposed Brnabić as his successor in June 2017.[^cite_note-5] Brnabić and her [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_cabinet_of_Ana_Brnabi%C4%87) were voted into office on 29 June 2017 by a majority of 157 out of 250 members of the [National Assembly of Serbia](619753.md). Elected as a [non-partisan](2172378.md) politician, she joined the ruling [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md) in 2019, and was subsequently elected as vice president in 2021. The National Assembly re-elected her into office after the [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Serbian_parliamentary_election) and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Serbian_general_election) elections. She was elected president of the National Assembly after the [2023 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Serbian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-\:0-6]

In 2019, Brnabić was ranked by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine as the 88th [most powerful woman in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_The_World's_100_Most_Powerful_Women) and the 19th most powerful female political and policy leader.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Some observers believe that she had no [political power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_power) in line with the constitutional role of chief of the [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), arguing instead that Vučić wielded power in his capacity as the president.

## Early and personal life

Brnabić was born in [Belgrade](55904.md). Her father Zoran was born in [Užice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%C5%BEice) in 1950 and finished his studies in Belgrade, where the family lived. Her paternal grandfather Anton Brnabić, an ethnic [Croat](69801.md) Yugoslav military officer, was born in [Stara Baška](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stara_Ba%C5%A1ka) on the [Croatian island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Croatia) of [Krk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krk), in the [Kingdom of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia), (present-day [Croatia](5573.md)). He fought with the [Yugoslav Partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia) and was ranked [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel) after the war. Her maternal grandparents are from [Babušnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babu%C5%A1nica), southeastern Serbia. Brnabić identifies as a [Serb](59512.md).[^cite_note-9]

Brnabić is a [lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian), the second female [LGBTQ](78174325.md) head of government in the world following [Jóhanna Sigurðardóttir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3hanna_Sigur%C3%B0ard%C3%B3ttir) ([Iceland 2009–2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Iceland)), and fifth [openly LGBTQ head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_first_openly_LGBTQ_holders_of_political_offices) overall following Jóhanna, [Elio Di Rupo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elio_Di_Rupo) ([Belgium 2011–2014](204622.md)), [Xavier Bettel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavier_Bettel) ([Luxembourg 2013–2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Luxembourg)), and [Leo Varadkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Varadkar) ([Ireland 2017–2020, 2022–2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach)). In 2017, she became the first head of government from the Balkan region to attend a gay pride march when she attended [Belgrade Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Pride).[^cite_note-10]

In 2019, her partner Milica Đurđić gave birth to a boy; Brnabić is the first openly [gay](5171128.md) prime minister whose partner gave birth while the prime minister was in office.[^cite_note-11]

## Education and business career

Brnabić was raised in Belgrade, Serbia, where she attended the Belgrade Fifth Gymnasium. In addition to her Serbian education, Brnabić holds a [Bachelor of Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Business_Administration) (BBA) diploma of [Northwood University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwood_University), Michigan, USA, and an [MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) of the [University of Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hull), England, UK, and worked for over a decade with international organizations, foreign investors, local self-government units, and the public sector in Serbia.[^cite_note-EWB-12]

Prior to Brnabić's appointment to the Government of Serbia, she was director of Continental Wind Serbia,[^cite_note-13] where she worked on the implementation of the investment of €300 million into a windpark in [Kovin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovin).[^cite_note-14] She was a member of the managing board of the non-profit foundation Peksim.[^cite_note-15]

She has been engaged in different US consulting companies that implemented [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAID)-financed projects in Serbia. She was deputy manager of the Serbia Competitiveness Project, the expert on the Local Self-government Reform Program in Serbia and the senior coordinator of the Program of Economic Development of Municipalities.[^cite_note-EWB-12] She was active in the foundation of the [National Alliance for Local Economic Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Alliance_for_Local_Economic_Development) (NALED) in 2006.[^cite_note-RTS-16] During that engagement, she participated in the introduction of the concept of local economic development in Serbia and building of potentials of municipalities to improve the business environment at the local level with active promotion of investments. She became a member, and thereafter the president, of the managing board of NALED.[^cite_note-RTS-16]

## Politics



Brnabić alongside Croatian Prime Minister [Andrej Plenković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrej_Plenkovi%C4%87), 16 June 2018

In August 2016, she was appointed the [Minister of Public Administration and Local Self-Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Public_Administration_and_Local_Self-Government_\(Serbia\)). In addition, she is the president of the Council for Innovative Entrepreneurship and Information Technologies of the [Government of Serbia](608371.md), as well as of the Republic Council for National Minorities and the vice president of the Republic Council for Public Administration Reform.

Brnabić described herself as a [pro-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-European) and [technocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocratic) prime minister.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] She explained that the priorities for her government are [modernization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernization_theory), [education reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_reform) and [digitization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digitization).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] [^cite_note-21] On the other hand, Brnabić has been criticised because she is the head of a [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative) and [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist) government which also includes openly [anti-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Western_sentiment) and [pro-Russian](2541825.md) ministers.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]



Brnabić with Bulgarian President [Rumen Radev](51575780.md) during his official state visit in [Belgrade](55904.md), 21 June 2018

In May 2018, Brnabić took over the [Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(Serbia\)) until the new Minister was appointed, following the resignation of [Dušan Vujović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Vujovi%C4%87).[^cite_note-VujovicHandover-26] On 29 May 2018, she appointed [Siniša Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sini%C5%A1a_Mali) as Vujović's successor on that position.[^cite_note-27] On 26 July 2018, Brnabić hosted a ceremony at the [United States Congress](31756.md) in Washington, which was held to mark the 100th anniversary of raising the [Serbian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Serbia) in front of the [White House](33057.md).[^cite_note-28]

In October 2019, the Prime Minister confirmed she had joined the ruling [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md).[^cite_note-29] On 25 October 2019, Brnabić signed a Free Trade Agreement between Serbia and the member states of the [Eurasian Economic Union](27290106.md) (EAEU), extending the list of Serbian products that can be exported to the EAEU territory.[^cite_note-30]



Brnabić with Vice-President of the European Commission [Maroš Šefčovič](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maro%C5%A1_%C5%A0ef%C4%8Dovi%C4%8D) in Brussels, on 4 July 2023

After the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) spread [to Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Serbia) in March 2020, Brnabić was appointed for the head of the Health Crisis Committee.[^cite_note-31] After president Vučić declared a [state of emergency](298608.md) on 15 March, the government issued regulations on measures during a state of emergency with the aim of suppressing the consequences of the outbreak.[^cite_note-32] A [curfew](297681.md) was introduced for the first time in Serbia since [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-33] Brnabić was elected as vice president of SNS in November 2021.[^cite_note-34] Her [third cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_cabinet_of_Ana_Brnabi%C4%87) was elected on 26 October 2022.[^cite_note-35]

She was elected president of the National Assembly of Serbia on 20 March 2024.[^cite_note-\:0-6] As first deputy prime minister, Dačić assumed the role of acting prime minister until the election of a new government.

On 28 September she bacame acting [President of Serbia](774382.md) after resignation of [Aleksandar Vučić](13349267.md).[^cite_note-36]

### Debate on constitutional roles



Brnabić with Russian Prime Minister [Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) during his visit to Serbia, 19 October 2019

Political scientist Krzysztof Zuba listed Brnabić as an example of head of government with extensive political dependence on a leader of the governing party.[^cite_note-Zuba-37] He defined a situation in which the Prime Minister does not have their own political position as the chief of the executive as a “surrogate government”, explaining that a distribution of power that is contrary to constitutional determinants is a characteristic of non-democratic systems.[^cite_note-Zuba-37]

In February 2019, [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) reported that Serbia's [status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_in_the_World) declined from *Free* to *Partly Free* due to deterioration in the conduct of elections, continued attempts by the government and allied media outlets to undermine independent journalists through legal harassment and smear campaigns, and Vučić's accumulation of executive powers that conflict with his constitutional role.[^cite_note-38] Opposition leaders and some observers describe her as a mere [puppet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_ruler) of Vučić, whose presidency, according to the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Serbia), is largely ceremonial with no significant executive power.[^cite_note-Surk-2][^cite_note-Zuba-37][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Brnabić never denied this, and even said that Vučić should act as a "mentor" of the prime minister.[^cite_note-41]

### Kosovo

See also: [Political status of Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Kosovo)



Brnabić with Japanese Prime Minister [Fumio Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida), 28 September 2022

In December 2018, commenting on the announced transformation of the [Kosovo Security Force](21731596.md) into the Kosovo Armed Forces, Brnabić said: "I hope we won’t have to use our military, but at the moment, that's one of the options on the table because one cannot witness a new [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) of the Serbs and new [Storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Storm) — although [Edi Rama](277805.md) is calling for them. When someone knows you have a strong army, then they have to sit down and talk to you."[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

In May 2019, Kosovo's Foreign Minister, [Behgjet Pacolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behgjet_Pacolli), stated his refusal to permit the entry of Brnabić into Kosovo, citing her alleged adherence to a perceived racist ideology. Brnabić, during the handover of a [European Commission](9974.md) 2019 progress report, said: "[Haradinaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramush_Haradinaj), [Thaçi](3626980.md) and [Veseli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadri_Veseli) are competing to see who the biggest [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist) and [chauvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvinist) is. What scares me most is that we are dealing with irrational people, the worst kind of [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist), people who literally walked out of the woods."[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] This was met with strong criticism, particularly by Albanian Twitter users, who campaigned with the hashtag *#literallyjustemergedfromthewoods* in order to mock the Prime Minister.[^cite_note-46]

On 20 January 2020, the governments of Serbia and Kosovo agreed to restore flights between their capitals for the first time in more than two decades.[^cite_note-flight1-47][^cite_note-flight2-48] The deal came after months of diplomatic talks by [Richard Grenell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Grenell), the [United States ambassador to Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_the_United_States_to_Germany), who was named special envoy for [Serbian-Kosovar relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo%E2%80%93Serbia_relations) by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) the year before.[^cite_note-flight1-47]

### Srebrenica genocide denial

In an interview on 14 November 2018 with the German public broadcaster [Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle), Brnabić [denied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_genocide_denial) that the July 1995 [massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre) of Bosniaks by [Bosnian Serb forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska) in [Srebrenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica) had been an act of genocide.[^cite_note-Rudicde1-49] Two weeks later, the [European Parliament](9581.md) adopted a resolution saying that the parliament regretted the continuing denial of the Srebrenica genocide by parts of the Serbian authorities and recalled that full cooperation with the [ICTY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Tribunal_for_the_former_Yugoslavia) and its successor mechanism included accepting its judgements.[^cite_note-50] The Hague Court criticised Brnabić for denial of the Srebrenica genocide.[^cite_note-Rudicde1-49][^cite_note-Rudicden2-51]

### LGBT rights

See also: [LGBT rights in Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Serbia)



Brnabić confronted at the 2019 [Belgrade Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Pride) parade by a participant holding a sign that says: "Prime Minister, what is it like to live with all the privileges?"

After she was appointed prime minister, Brnabić said that she did not want to be branded Serbia's gay prime minister and that she did not plan "to push LGBT legal reforms at this stage" because she wanted to prioritise other policy reforms.[^cite_note-52] In September 2017, Brnabić took part in [the pride parade in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Pride) and became the first Serbian prime minister to attend a pride parade. At the event, Brnabić said: "The government is here for all citizens and will secure the respect of rights for all citizens."[^cite_note-53]

Brnabić says that she advocates [inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inheritance) rights of same-sex couples.[^cite_note-54] In February 2019, Milica Đurđić, Brnabić's partner, gave birth to a son named Igor, but [same-sex marriage](92656.md) is constitutionally banned and [LGBT parenting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_parenting) is not regulated in Serbia. Some journalists and LGBT activists have concluded that Brnabić has failed to advocate for LGBT equality in Serbia.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

## Awards and other activities

She has been awarded a number of plaudits for the development projects on which she worked, for the promotion of socially accountable business operation and tolerance.[^cite_note-57] She has been awarded the [Order of the Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Republika_Srpska).[^cite_note-58] Brnabić was named the honorary citizen of [Šabac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0abac) in 2024.[^cite_note-59]
