**Cuautla** (Nahuatl pronunciation: [\[ˈkʷaʍt͡ɬa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Nahuatl), meaning "where the eagles roam"), officially **Heroica e Histórica Ciudad de Cuautla de Morelos** (*Heroic and Historic City of Cuautla of Morelos*) or **H. H. Cuautla, Morelos**, is a city and [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Mexico) in the [Mexican state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_state) of [Morelos](195827.md), about 104 kilometers south of [Mexico City](18987.md). In the 2010 census the city population was 154,358.[^cite_note-3] The municipality covers 153.651 km<sup>2</sup> (59.325 sq mi). Cuautla is the third most populous city in the state, after [Cuernavaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuernavaca) and [Jiutepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiutepec). The city was founded on April 4, 1829.[^cite_note-inafed-4] The 2020 population figures were 187,118 inhabitants for the municipality and 157,336 inhabitants for the city of Cuatula.[^cite_note-censo2020-1]

The Cuautla Metropolitan Area, the second largest in [Morelos](195827.md), comprises the municipalities of Cuautla, Yautepec, Ayala, [Yecapixtla](732223.md), [Atlatlahucan](731990.md), and [Tlayacapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlayacapan). It covers 1,037.30 km<sup>2</sup> (400.50 sq mi), which represents 21.26% of the state's total area. The metropolitan population (2010) is 434,187.[^cite_note-5]

## History

### Prehispanic history

The [Olmec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec) group who lived in [Chalcatzingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcatzingo) (southeast of Cuautla) founded settlements in Cuautla, Tepalcingo, Jonacatepec (Las Pilas), Olintepec, Atlihuayan, Huaxtepec, Gualupita de Cuernavaca, Tlayacapan, etc. (Piña Chan y Plancarte).

Five years after the conquest of [*Cuahunahuác* (Cuernavaca)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuernavaca) in (1379 CE),[^cite_note-gob-6] [Moctezuma Ilhuicamina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moctezuma_Ilhuicamina) conquered Huaxtepec ([Oaxtepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaxtepec)), Yautepec, Tlayacapan and other towns of Morelos and Guerrero. With Huaxtepec, which was the prehispanic and colonial capital of the peoples of the *Plan de Amilpas*, its 25-human settlements including *Cuauhtlán*, had to pay a tribute of 400 cotton blankets, 400 two-color valances, 400 bedspreads, 800 thin cotton blankets, 400 pairs of shorts (*patees*), 200 women's shirts, and 1,200 veils (*mantillas*) every 80 days. In addition, they were required to contribute labor.

### Spanish conquest and colonial period

The *Plan de Amilpas* including Cuautla was conquered by Captain [Gonzalo de Sandoval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_de_Sandoval) on March 14, 1521. In 1543 [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain) was organized into four provinces: Michoacán, México, Coatzacoalcos, and Las Mixtecas; the present territory of Morelos was part of the Mexico province.[^cite_note-gob-6]

The church and monastery of [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1) were built between 1560 and 1580. The church is relatively small and austere, although there is a beautiful cupola on the south side. The [façade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facade) of the church is made of pink [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) with well-defined bases, columns, and [cornices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornice). The small, two-level belltower is square and simple. The monetary is high and formal; it may have been built after the church. The church is unique in that it faces the east, there is no real [atrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrium_\(architecture\)), and there are neither *[capillas posas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillas_posa?action=edit\&redlink=1)* nor an open chapel.[^cite_note-convent-7] Construction of the church of [Santo Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Dominic) also began in the middle of the 16th century, but there is little information on it.[^cite_note-convent-7]

After the conquest, Indigenous lands were confiscated, mostly for sugar cane plantations and mills (*trapiches*). Despite Indigenous protests, Viceroy [Gaspar de Zúñiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_de_Z%C3%BA%C3%B1iga,_5th_Count_of_Monterrey) ruled in favor of the colonists on July 5, 1603. In 1646 the province of México became the *[Real Audiencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Audiencia);* Cuautla became an *alcaldia* (mayoralty) belonging to the [Intendencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendant_\(government_official\)) of Puebla.[^cite_note-gob-6]

### Independence & 19th century

After a number of successful battles, General [José María Morelos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Morelos) arrived in Cuautla in December 1811. On February 19, 1812, Spanish General [Félix María Calleja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Mar%C3%ADa_Calleja) began the 72-day [Siege of Cuautla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Cuautla). Morelos was accompanied by [Leonardo Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Bravo_\(general_officer\)), [Mariano Matamoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Matamoros), [Hermenegildo Galeana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermenegildo_Galeana), [Nicolás Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Bravo), and [Manuel de Ordiera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_de_Ordiera). 12-year-old [Narciso Mendoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narciso_Mendoza?action=edit\&redlink=1), known as the *Niño Artillero* (Child Gunner) is remembered for stopping an advance of royalist troops by setting off a cannon.[^cite_note-8] Morelos and his army were able to break out on May 2, 1812.

Cuautla was designated a *Heroic City* (*Heroica Ciudad de Morelos*) on April 4, 1829, while Felipe B. Montero was *Presidente Municipal* (mayor).[^cite_note-inafed-4]

An 1865 photograph shows the construction of the second floor of the *Palacio Municipal* (city hall) in 1865. The building was burned during the Revolution.[^cite_note-HHHCuautla-9]

The railroad station was opened on June 18, 1881, next to the *Convento de San Diego*.[^cite_note-HHHCuautla-9] [Tourist trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_train) continue to operate along the line.[^cite_note-tren-10]

### Revolution & 20th century

As a young man, [Emiliano Zapata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiliano_Zapata) was concerned about land seizures in [Anenecuilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anenecuilco), his hometown. In May [1911](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_in_Mexico) he answered [Francisco I. Madero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_I._Madero)'s call to arms against President [Porfirio Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porfirio_D%C3%ADaz). After minor battles in [Chietla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chietla_Municipality), [Izúcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iz%C3%BAcar_de_Matamoros), [Metepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metepec) [Atlixco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlixco), Yautepec, and Jonacatepec, he arrived at Cuautla on May 11, 1911. With 4,000 or 5,000 troops he surrounded the city, and the week-long [Battle of Cuautla (1911)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cuautla_\(1911\)) began. The battle was a major turning point in the [Mexican Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Revolution), as Porfirio Díaz said the battle convinced him to sign the [Treaty of Ciudad Juárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ciudad_Ju%C3%A1rez) and resign.[^cite_note-zap-11]

Nearly seven years later, in April [1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_in_Mexico), Colonel Jesus Guajardo assassinated Zapata, apparently under orders of President [Venustiano Carranza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venustiano_Carranza) in [Hacienda Chinameca, Ayala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinameca,_Morelos). His body was placed on a mule and left on the street before he was brought to Cuautla for burial.[^cite_note-zap-11]

The city hall was burned during the Battle of Cuautla in 1911; the bell *Nuestra Señora de Dolores* was transferred from the Church of Guadalupita on February 19, 2017 when the city hall was restored.[^cite_note-inafed-4]

### 21st century

Six people, including an 11-year-old child, were killed in the September 17, [2017 Puebla earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Puebla_earthquake).[^cite_note-12]

Jesus Corona Damian of the coalition called *Juntos Haremos Historia* (Together we will make history) was elected Presidente Municipal (mayor) in the election of July 1, 2018.[^cite_note-13]

[Tetelcingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetelcingo) was scheduled to become an autonomous municipality on January 1, 2019,[^cite_note-14] but Cuautla authorities objected.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

As of May 4, 2020, there were 505 infections and 59 deaths in the state of Morelos and 62 confirmed infections from the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Mexico) in Cuautla.[^cite_note-17] Schools and many businesses were closed from mid March until June 1.[^cite_note-18] On June 2, Cuautla reported 224 confirmed cases and 34 deaths from the virus; the reopening of the state was pushed back until at least June 13.[^cite_note-19] Cuautla reported an increase of 137 new cases from January 12 to 14, 2021, making 1,602 cases in all, second highest in the state.[^cite_note-20] On March 8 Cuautla became the third municipality in Morelos to vaccinate senior citizens (60+).[^cite_note-21]

Irving Eduardo Solano Vera ("El Profe"), said to be the leader of the [Jalisco New Generation Cartel](35006872.md) (CJNG), was arrested in Colonia Santa Rosa on February 18, 2021.[^cite_note-22]

### Leather, crafts, & textiles

- Cuautla is distinguished by its *huarachería* (leather sandals).

- Tetelcingo is known for the elaboration of wire birdcages, bread boxes, and lace napkins. Women in Tetelcingo wear traditional Indigenous dress, consisting of a navy blue [huipil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huipil) (skirt) and a *chincuete* that is girded at the waist with a belt loom weaved by them. There are several workshops that are dedicated to saddlery, the manufacture of huaraches, belts, saddles, and all the necessary equipment for riding.

### Food

Rich stews with green or red *[guacamole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guacamole)*, the green mole of *pipián* with [tamales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale), and [cecina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecina_\(meat\)#Mexico). Also *[huitlacoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huitlacoche)*, *[molotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molote)*, a squash flower soup (*[sopa de flor de calabaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopa_de_flor_de_calabaza)*), or a [mushroom cream soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cream_of_mushroom_soup), or [sopa azteca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopa_azteca?action=edit\&redlink=1). Also available is [pollo morelense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollo_morelense?action=edit\&redlink=1), chicken with [peanut sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut_sauce), flower pancakes, and broken beans (*[frijoles quebrados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frijoles_quebrados?action=edit\&redlink=1)*).[^cite_note-HHHCuautla-9]

### Art

*Motivos sobre el agua*, (Motifs on water) is a fresco painted by [Olga Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Costa) and [José Chávez Morado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Ch%C3%A1vez_Morado), hidden inside the [Agua Hedionda Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agua_Hedionda_Spa). There is a group of plump mermaids playing musical instruments at the bottom of the sea. One carries the zither, another the cello, and one a type of shell or tambourine. They are surrounded by fish, shells, corals, and starfish. It was made in 1952.[^cite_note-HHHCuautla-9]

### Dance

*Las Tetelcingas* is a traditional [dance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J4A9glrwCN8) dance from the indigenous town of [Tetelcingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetelcingo).[^cite_note-HHHCuautla-9]

Typical dress: the authentic suit is that of *Tetelcinga*, which consists of a [huipil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huipil) (tunic) and a tangle of thick dark blue cloth, tied at the waist with wide folds by means of a blue and red sash, a blue skirt, sandals, with a headband made of flowers. The women's hair is worn long, either loose or braided.[^cite_note-29] Eliseo Aragón said that half a century ago some women used to color their hair blue, red, or green, in the [Olmec style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec_influences_on_Mesoamerican_cultures). It is also said that drinking cups were used on the head as an ornament. The men's costume consists of white cotton pants tied with a red sash, a long-sleeved cotton shirt, a straw *sombrero* (hat), a bandana, and sandals; however, there are photographs in which the natives of Tetelcingo wear a kind of *jorongo* (sleeveless poncho) and short leather pants.

## Economy

Among the most important activities in Cuautla you will find:[^cite_note-30]

- Agriculture (corn, beans)

- Livestock (beef, pork)

- Nursery (production of ornamental plants and fruit trees)

- Aquaculture (production of aquatic species such as mojarras, prawns, and catfish)

- Agroindustry (sugar cane)

- General Commerce (financial, administrative and real estate services, premises such as stationery, grocery, food, beverages, etc.)

- Services (hotel, restaurant, professional, technical, and personal)

- Construction.

- Manufacturing.

The Cuautla Industrial Park, located in [Ayala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Ayala), covers 130 hectares (320 acres) and is the second largest in Morelos. Most of the companies are related to the automotive industry. The largest employers are *Saint-Gobainl*, (glass), *Sekurit* (windshields), and *Temic* (industrial parts).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

## Architecture

The vast majority of buildings in Cuautla are made of brick or cinder blocks, framed by [reinforced concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforced_concrete) [posts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_\(structural\)) which in turn support reinforced concrete [beams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_\(structure\)), reinforced concrete [floors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floor) and [roofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roof). Also, many homes in this city, are made with only cinder blocks, cement, and a tin roof. Many of these homes also lack electricity and running water. Nearly all homes have a "tinaco",[^cite_note-47] which is a large plastic [water tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_tank) on the roof as well as a concrete *[cisterna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern)* below ground. Both are refilled regularly at the same time by the city. When necessary the water is [pumped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump) from the *cisterna* to the *Tinaco*. These 1,100-liter (290 U.S. gal; 240 imp gal)[^cite_note-48] water tanks let water out into [pipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_\(fluid_conveyance\)) which have [valves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valve) that are opened as needed to wash clothes and dishes or to bathe or flush the toilet. A single valve is located at the *tinaco*to be closed if necessary.

### Climate

The city is quite warm year-round. In the winter, there is a slight decrease in both the daytime and nighttime temperatures, and because of Cuautla's relative proximity south of the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer) and its altitude (about 4,500 feet or 1,400 meters above sea level), the nighttime temperatures year-round usually average about 57 °F (14 °C). On the other hand, because Cuautla is somewhat close to the [Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator), temperatures year-round tend to reach into the mid-80s to lower 90s°F (upper 20s°C to the lower 30s°C) even during the winter, and in spring on many days the daytime temperatures may reach well into the upper 90s°F (lower 30s°C).[^cite_note-54]
