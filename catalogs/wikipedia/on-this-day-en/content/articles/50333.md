**Utrecht** ([/ˈjuːtrɛkt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*YOO-trekt*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Dutch: [\[ˈytrɛxt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f7/260_Utrecht.ogg/260_Utrecht.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f7/260_Utrecht.ogg/260_Utrecht.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Utrecht dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrechts-Alblasserwaards): *Ut(e)reg* [\[ˈyt(ə)ʁɛχ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)) is the [fourth-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_Netherlands_by_province) of the [Netherlands](21148.md), as well as the capital and the most populous city of the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_the_Netherlands) of [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_\(province\)). The [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) of Utrecht is located in the eastern part of the [Randstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randstad) [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation), in the very centre of mainland Netherlands, and includes [Haarzuilens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haarzuilens), [Vleuten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vleuten) and [De Meern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Meern). It has a population of 378,140 as of January 2026.[^cite_note-10]

Utrecht's ancient city centre features many buildings and structures, several dating as far back as the [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages). It has been the religious centre of the Netherlands since the 8th century. It received city rights in 1122. In 1579, the [Union of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Utrecht) was signed in the city to lay the foundations for the [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic).

Utrecht’s historic centre is characterised by the Dom Tower and a medieval canal system with wharves and cellars below street level. Today, Utrecht is a major national railway hub and a centre for higher education, research, healthcare and culture. The municipality contains the [Rietveld Schröder House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rietveld_Schr%C3%B6der_House) and components of the [Lower German Limes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Germanic_Limes) and the [Dutch Water Defence Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Water_Defence_Lines), all of which are UNESCO World Heritage properties. In 2025, [Lonely Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonely_Planet) selected Utrecht as one of 25 destinations featured in its Best in Travel 2026 guide.

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Utrecht).

### Origins (before 650 CE)



[Willem Blaeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Blaeu)'s 1652 map of Utrecht (here *Traiectum Wttrecht*) with its canals and fortifications with nurseries in green on the outside. North is to the left.

Although there is some evidence of earlier inhabitation in the region of Utrecht, dating back to the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age) (app. 2200 [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCE)) and settling in the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) (app. 1800–800 BCE),[^cite_note-11] the founding date of the city is usually related to the construction of a [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) [fortification](204118.md) (*[castellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castellum)*), probably built in around 50 [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_Era). A series of such fortresses were built after the [Roman emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperor) [Claudius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius) decided the empire should not expand further north. To consolidate the border, the [Limes Germanicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limes_Germanicus) defense line was constructed[^cite_note-20eUtrecht-12] along the main branch of the river [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), which at that time traversed a more northern route (now known as the [Kromme Rijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kromme_Rijn), *Crooked Rhine*) compared to today's Rhine flow. These fortresses were designed to house a [cohort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohort_\(military_unit\)) of about 500 Roman soldiers. Near the fort, settlements grew that housed [artisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisan), traders and soldiers' wives and children.

In Roman times, the name of the Utrecht fortress was simply *[Traiectum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traiectum_\(Utrecht\))*, denoting its location at a possible Rhine crossing. Traiectum became Dutch Trecht; with the U from [Old Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dutch) "uut" (downriver) added to distinguish U-trecht from [Maas-tricht](20125.md),[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] on the river [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse). In 11th-century official documents, it was Latinized as Ultra Traiectum. Around the year 200, the wooden walls of the fortification were replaced by sturdier [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff) stone walls,[^cite_note-15] remnants of which are still to be found below the buildings around Dom Square.

From the middle of the 3rd century, [Germanic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_Tribes) regularly invaded the Roman territories. After around 275 the Romans could no longer maintain the northern border, and Utrecht was abandoned.[^cite_note-20eUtrecht-12] Little is known about the period from 270 to 650. Utrecht is first spoken of again several centuries after the Romans left. Under the influence of the growing realms of the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks), during [Dagobert I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagobert_I)'s reign in the 7th century, a church was built within the walls of the Roman fortress.[^cite_note-20eUtrecht-12] In ongoing border conflicts with the [Frisians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frisians), this first church was destroyed.

### Centre of Christianity in the Netherlands (650–1579)



The [Dom Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Tower_of_Utrecht) seen from the Stadhuisbrug (Town Hall Bridge) in the city centre, 2013. The remaining section of the [Cathedral of Saint Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Martin's_Cathedral,_Utrecht) is not connected to the tower (in the background) since the collapse of the [nave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nave) due to the [1674 derecho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1674_derecho).

Main article: [Prince-Bishopric of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Utrecht)

By the mid-7th century, British, English and Irish [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionaries) set out to convert the [Frisians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frisians). [Pope Sergius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Sergius_I) appointed their leader, Saint [Willibrordus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willibrordus), as bishop of the Frisians. The tenure of Willibrordus is generally considered to be the beginning of the [Bishopric of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Utrecht_\(695%E2%80%931580\)).[^cite_note-20eUtrecht-12] In 723, the Frankish leader [Charles Martel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Martel) bestowed the fortress in Utrecht and the surrounding lands as the base of the bishops. From then on Utrecht became one of the most influential seats of power for the Catholic Church in the Netherlands. The archbishops of Utrecht were based at the uneasy northern border of the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire). In addition, the city of Utrecht had competition from the nearby trading centre [Dorestad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorestad).[^cite_note-20eUtrecht-12] After the fall of Dorestad around 850, Utrecht became one of the most important cities in the Netherlands.[^cite_note-16] The importance of Utrecht as a centre of Christianity is illustrated by the election of the Utrecht-born [Adriaan Florenszoon Boeyens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Adrian_VI) as [pope](23056.md) in 1522 (the last non-Italian pope before [John Paul II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_II)).

#### Prince-bishops

Main article: [Archdiocese of Utrecht (695–1580)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Utrecht_\(695%E2%80%931580\))

When the Frankish rulers established the system of [feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism), the [Bishops](4092.md) of Utrecht came to exercise worldly power as [prince-bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-bishop).[^cite_note-20eUtrecht-12] The territory of the bishopric not only included the modern province of Utrecht (Nedersticht, 'lower [Sticht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sticht)') but also extended to the northeast. The feudal conflict of the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) heavily affected Utrecht. The prince-bishopric was involved in almost continuous conflicts with the Counts of [Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Holland) and the Dukes of [Guelders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelders).[^cite_note-17] The [Veluwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veluwe) region was seized by Guelders, but large areas in the modern province of [Overijssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overijssel) remained as the Oversticht.

#### Religious buildings

Several churches and monasteries were built inside, or close to, the city of Utrecht. The most dominant of these was the [Cathedral of Saint Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Martin,_Utrecht), inside the old Roman fortress. The construction of the present [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) building was begun in 1254 after an earlier [romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture) construction had been badly damaged by fire. The [choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choir_\(architecture\)) and [transept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transept) were finished from 1320 and were followed then by the ambitious [Dom tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Tower_of_Utrecht).[^cite_note-20eUtrecht-12] The last part to be constructed was the central [nave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nave), from 1420. By that time, however, the age of the great cathedrals had come to an end and declining finances prevented the ambitious project from being finished, the construction of the central nave being suspended before the planned [flying buttresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_buttress) could be finished.[^cite_note-20eUtrecht-12] Besides the cathedral there were four [collegiate churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_church) in Utrecht: [St. Salvator's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Salvator's_Church) (demolished in the 16th century), on the Dom square, dating back to the early 8th century.[^cite_note-18] Saint [John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist) (Janskerk), originating in 1040;[^cite_note-19] [Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieterskerk,_Utrecht), building started in 1039[^cite_note-20] and [Saint Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_\(mother_of_Jesus\))'s church building started around 1090 (demolished in the early 19th century, cloister survives).[^cite_note-21] Besides these churches, the city housed [St. Paul's Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul's_Abbey,_Utrecht),[^cite_note-22] the 15th-century [beguinage of St. Nicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begijnhof,_Utrecht), and a 14th-century chapter house of the [Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Knights).[^cite_note-Karel_V-23]

Besides these buildings which belonged to the bishopric, an additional four [parish churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_church) were constructed in the city: the [Jacobikerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobikerk) ([dedicated to Saint James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James,_son_of_Zebedee)), founded in the 11th century, with the current Gothic church dating back to the 14th century;[^cite_note-24] the Buurkerk (Neighbourhood-church) of the 11th-century parish in the centre of the city; Nicolaichurch (dedicated to [Saint Nicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas)), from the 12th century,[^cite_note-25] and the 13th-century Geertekerk (dedicated to Saint [Gertrude of Nivelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_of_Nivelles)).[^cite_note-26]

#### City of Utrecht

Its location on the banks of the river Rhine allowed Utrecht to become an important trade centre in the Northern Netherlands. The growing town was granted [city rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_rights_in_the_Netherlands) by [Henry V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) at Utrecht on 2 June 1122. When the main flow of the Rhine moved south, the old bed which still flowed through the heart of the town became ever more [canalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal); and the wharf system was built as an inner city harbour system.[^cite_note-27] On the wharfs, storage facilities (*werfkelders*) were built, on top of which the main street, including houses, was constructed. The wharves and the cellars are accessible from a platform at water level with stairs descending from the street level to form a unique structure.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The relations between the bishop, who controlled many lands outside of the city, and the citizens of Utrecht was not always easy.[^cite_note-20eUtrecht-12] The bishop, for example, dammed the [Kromme Rijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kromme_Rijn) at [Wijk bij Duurstede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wijk_bij_Duurstede) to protect his estates from flooding. This threatened shipping for the city and led the city of Utrecht to commission a canal to ensure access to the town for shipping trade: the Vaartse Rijn, connecting Utrecht to the [Hollandse IJssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollandse_IJssel) at [IJsselstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IJsselstein).

#### The end of independence

In 1528 the bishop lost secular power over both Neder- and Oversticht, which included the city of Utrecht, to [Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). Charles V combined the [Seventeen Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventeen_Provinces) (the current [Benelux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benelux) and the northern parts of France) as a personal union. This ended the prince-bishopric of Utrecht, as the secular rule was now the [lordship of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordship_of_Utrecht), with the religious power remaining with the bishop, although Charles V had gained the right to appoint new bishops. In 1559 the bishopric of Utrecht was raised to archbishopric to make it the religious centre of the Northern [ecclesiastical province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province) in the Seventeen Provinces.

The transition from independence to a relatively minor part of a larger union was not easily accepted. To quell uprisings, Charles V struggled to exert his power over the city's citizens who had struggled to gain a certain level of independence from the bishops and were not willing to cede this to their new lord. The heavily fortified castle [Vredenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vredenburg_\(castle\)) was built to house a large garrison whose main task was to maintain control over the city. The castle would last less than 50 years before it was demolished in an uprising in the early stages of the [Dutch Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Revolt).

### Republic of the Netherlands (1579–1806)



[Lambert de Hondt (II)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambert_de_Hondt_\(II\)?action=edit\&redlink=1): the Surrender of Utrecht on 30 June 1672 to the French king [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV) on a white horse, 1672, [Centraal Museum Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centraal_Museum). The city keys are proffered to him on a cushion.



[Joost Cornelisz. Droochsloot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joost_Cornelisz_Droochsloot): The Disbanding of the 'Waardgelders' (Mercenaries in the Pay of the Town Government) by [Maurice, Prince of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice,_Prince_of_Orange) on Neude Square, Utrecht, 31 July 1618. Painting 1625.

In 1579 the northern seven provinces signed the [Union of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Utrecht) treaty (Dutch: Unie van Utrecht), in which they decided to join forces against Spanish rule. The Union of Utrecht is seen as the beginning of the [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic). In 1580, the new and predominantly Protestant state abolished the bishoprics, including the archbishopric of Utrecht. The [stadtholders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadtholder) disapproved of the independent course of the Utrecht bourgeoisie and brought the city under much more direct control of the republic, shifting the power towards its dominant province [Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland). This was the start of a long period of stagnation of trade and development in Utrecht. Utrecht remained an atypical city in the new republic being about 40% Catholic in the mid-17th century, and even more so among the elite groups, who included many rural nobility and gentry with town houses there.[^cite_note-30]

The fortified city temporarily fell to the French invasion in 1672 (the [Disaster Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampjaar), Dutch: Rampjaar). The French invasion was stopped just west of Utrecht at the [Old Hollandic Waterline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollandic_Water_Line#Old_Hollandic_Waterline). In 1674, only two years after the French left, the centre of Utrecht was struck by a catastrophic [derecho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1674_derecho). The halt to building before construction of flying buttresses in the 15th century now proved to be the undoing of the cathedral of St Martin church's central section which collapsed, creating the current Dom square between the tower and choir. In 1713, Utrecht hosted one of the first international peace negotiations when the [Treaty of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Utrecht_\(1713\)) settled the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession). Beginning in 1723, Utrecht became the centre of the non-Roman [Old Catholic Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Catholic_Church).

### Modern history (1815–present)



People celebrating the [liberation of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Day_\(Netherlands\)) at the end of [World War II](32927.md) on 7 May 1945. [Amersfoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amersfoort)?



Zadelstraat (Saddlers' Street), Utrecht, with Dom Tower in the background, 2009



Contemporary map of Utrecht with the new suburb [Leidsche Rijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leidsche_Rijn) ([De Meern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Meern)) to the left, 2014

In the early 19th century, the role of Utrecht as a fortified town had become obsolete. The fortifications of the [Nieuwe Hollandse Waterlinie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Hollandse_Waterlinie) were moved east of Utrecht. The town walls could now be demolished to allow for expansion. The moats remained intact and formed an important feature of the Zocher plantsoen, an [English style landscape park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_garden) that remains largely intact today. Growth of the city increased when, in 1843, a railway connecting Utrecht to Amsterdam was opened. After that, Utrecht gradually became the main hub of the [Dutch railway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_routes_in_the_Netherlands). With the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) finally gathering speed in the Netherlands and the ramparts taken down, Utrecht began to grow far beyond its medieval centre. When the Dutch government allowed the bishopric of Utrecht to be reinstated by [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) in 1853, Utrecht became the centre of Dutch Catholicism once more. From the 1880s onward, neighbourhoods such as Oudwijk, [Wittevrouwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wittevrouwen?action=edit\&redlink=1), Vogelenbuurt to the east, and Lombok to the west were developed. New middle-class residential areas, such as Tuindorp and [Oog in Al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oog_in_Al), were built in the 1920s and 1930s. During this period, several [Jugendstil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugendstil) houses and office buildings were built, followed by [Rietveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrit_Rietveld) who built the [Rietveld Schröder House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rietveld_Schr%C3%B6der_House) (1924), and [Dudok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Marinus_Dudok)'s construction of the city theatre (1941).



The [Winkel van Sinkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winkel_van_Sinkel), the first department store in the Netherlands. Oudegracht, Utrecht, 2010

During [World War II](32927.md), Utrecht was held by German forces until the general German surrender of the Netherlands on 5 May 1945. [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_army) and [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Army) troops that had surrounded the city entered it after that surrender, on 7 May 1945. Following the end of World War II, the city grew considerably when new neighbourhoods such as [Overvecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overvecht), [Kanaleneiland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanaleneiland), [Hoograven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoograven?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Lunetten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunetten) were built. Around 2000, the [Leidsche Rijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leidsche_Rijn) area was developed as an extension of the city to the west.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

&#x20;The area surrounding [Utrecht Centraal railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Centraal_railway_station) and the station itself were developed following modernist ideas of the 1960s, in a [brutalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist_architecture) style. This development led to the construction of the shopping mall [Hoog Catharijne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoog_Catharijne?action=edit\&redlink=1), the music centre Vredenburg ([Hertzberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Hertzberger), 1979), and conversion of part of the ancient canal structure into a highway ([Catherijnebaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catharijnesingel)). Protest against further modernisation of the city centre followed even before the last buildings were finalised. In the early 21st century, the whole area is undergoing change again. The redeveloped music centre TivoliVredenburg opened in 2014 with the original Vredenburg and Tivoli concert and rock and jazz halls brought together in a single building.&#x20;

### Religion

Utrecht has been the religious centre of the Netherlands since the 8th century. Currently it is the see of the Metropolitan [Archbishop of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Utrecht), the most senior Dutch Roman Catholic leader.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] His [ecclesiastical province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province) covers the whole kingdom.

Utrecht is also the see of the archbishop of the [Old Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Catholic_Church), titular head of the [Union of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Utrecht_\(Old_Catholic\)), and the location of the offices of the [Protestant Church in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Church_in_the_Netherlands), the main Dutch Protestant church.

As of 2013, the largest religion is Christianity with 28% of the population being Christian, followed by Islam with 9.9% in 2016 and Hinduism with 0.8%.

**Abstede** (Utrecht-Oost), **Catharijne** (Utrecht-Binnenstad), **Lauwerecht** (Utrecht-Noordoost) and **Tolsteeg** (Utrecht-Zuid) were separate municipalities between 1818 and 1823, when they were merged with Utrecht. **Zuilen** (Utrecht-Noordwest) was a separate municipality until 1954, when it was merged with Utrecht and the former municipality of Maarssen. Former municipalities **Oudenrijn** (nowadays part of Utrecht), **Jutphaas** (nowadays part of Nieuwegein) and **Achttienhoven** (nowadays part of De Bilt) partially merged with Utrecht in 1954. In 2001, the former municipality of **Vleuten-De Meern** (a merger between the former municipalities of Vleuten, Haarzuilens, Veldhuizen and Oudenrijn in 1954) merged with Utrecht to become a city part (**wijk**) of it.[^cite_note-43]

Utrecht is the centre of a densely populated area, a fact which makes concise definitions of its agglomeration difficult, and somewhat arbitrary. The smaller Utrecht agglomeration of continuously built-up areas counts some 420,000 inhabitants and includes [Nieuwegein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwegein), [IJsselstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IJsselstein) and [Maarssen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maarssen). It is sometimes argued that the close by municipalities [De Bilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Bilt), [Zeist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeist), [Houten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houten), [Vianen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vianen) ([Vijfheerenlanden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijfheerenlanden)), [Driebergen-Rijsenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driebergen-Rijsenburg) ([Utrechtse Heuvelrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrechtse_Heuvelrug)), and [Bunnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunnik) should also be counted towards the Utrecht agglomeration, bringing the total to 640,000 inhabitants. The larger region, including slightly more remote cities such as [Woerden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woerden) and [Amersfoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amersfoort), counts up to 820,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-CBS_statline_2007-44]

## Cityscape



Panorama of Utrecht from the west with Dom Tower and Buurkerk, 2008



[Oudegracht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudegracht) (the 'Old Canal') in central Utrecht, 2006



The Oudegracht with Hamburgerbrug (bridge) with Dom Tower seen from the south, Utrecht, in the 1890s



View of the Oudegracht from the Dom Tower, 2006



Aerial view of Utrecht from the Dom Tower, 2009

Utrecht's cityscape is dominated by the [Dom Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Tower_of_Utrecht), the tallest belfry in the Netherlands and originally part of the [Cathedral of Saint Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Martin,_Utrecht).[^cite_note-46] An ongoing debate is over whether any building in or near the centre of town should surpass the Dom Tower in height (112 m \[367 ft]). Nevertheless, some tall buildings are now being constructed that will become part of the skyline of Utrecht. The second-tallest building of the city, the [Rabobank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabobank)-tower, was completed in 2010 and stands 105 m (344 ft) tall.[^cite_note-47] Two antennas will increase that height to 120 m (394 ft). Two other buildings were constructed around the [Nieuw Galgenwaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Galgenwaard) stadium (2007). These buildings, the 'Kantoortoren Galghenwert' and 'Apollo Residence', stand 85.5 m (280.5 ft) and 64.5 m (211.6 ft) high, respectively. The former Utrecht Main Post Office, built in 1924, is still in the city centre at Neude square, but is now serving as library, see also [Utrecht Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Post_Office).

Another landmark is the old centre and the canal structure in the inner city. The [Oudegracht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudegracht) is a curved canal, partly following the ancient main branch of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine). It is lined with the unique wharf-basement structures that create a two-level street along the canals.[^cite_note-48] The inner city has largely retained its medieval structure,[^cite_note-49] and the moat ringing the old town is largely intact.[^cite_note-50] In the 1970s part of the moat was converted into a motorway. It was then converted back into a waterway, the work being finished in 2020.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

Because of the role of Utrecht as a [fortified city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortified_city), construction outside the medieval centre and its city walls was restricted until the 19th century. Surrounding the medieval core there is a ring of late-19th- and early-20th-century neighbourhoods, with newer neighbourhoods positioned farther out.[^cite_note-53] The eastern part of Utrecht remains fairly open. The [Dutch Water Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Water_Line), moved east of the city in the early 19th century, required open lines of fire, thus prohibiting all permanent constructions until the middle of the 20th century on the east side of the city.[^cite_note-54]

Due to the past importance of Utrecht as a religious centre, several monumental churches were erected, many of which have survived.[^cite_note-55] Most prominent is the [Dom Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Martin,_Utrecht). Other notable churches include the romanesque [St Peter's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieterskerk,_Utrecht) and St John's churches; the gothic churches of St James and St Nicholas; and the Buurkerk, now converted into a [museum for automatically playing musical instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Speelklok).

## Transport

### Public transport

Because of its central location, Utrecht is well connected to the rest of the Netherlands and has a well-developed public transport network.

#### Heavy rail



Utrecht Centraal railway station. The renovated hall with its warped roof, 2015.

[Utrecht Centraal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Centraal) is the main railway station of Utrecht and is the largest in the country. There are regular intercity services to all major Dutch cities, including direct services to [Schiphol Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schiphol_railway_station). Utrecht Centraal is a station on the [night service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_the_Netherlands#Night_service), providing an all-night service to (among others) Schiphol Airport, Amsterdam and Rotterdam, seven days a week. International [InterCityExpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCityExpress) (ICE) services to Germany through [Arnhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem) call at Utrecht Centraal. Regular local trains to all areas surrounding Utrecht also depart from Utrecht Centraal; and service several smaller stations: [Utrecht Lunetten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Lunetten_railway_station); [Utrecht Vaartsche Rijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Vaartsche_Rijn_railway_station); [Utrecht Overvecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Overvecht_railway_station); [Utrecht Leidsche Rijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Leidsche_Rijn_railway_station); [Utrecht Terwijde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Terwijde_railway_station); [Utrecht Zuilen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Zuilen_railway_station) and [Vleuten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vleuten_railway_station). A former station [Utrecht Maliebaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Maliebaan_railway_station) closed in 1939 and has since been converted into the [Dutch Railway Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Railway_Museum).

Utrecht is the location of the headquarters of [Nederlandse Spoorwegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Spoorwegen) (English: *Dutch Railways*), the largest rail operator in the Netherlands, and [ProRail](168969.md), the state-owned company responsible for the construction and maintenance of the country's rail infrastructure.

#### Light rail

The [Utrecht sneltram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_sneltram) is a [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) system with three routes connecting Utrecht Centraal railway station to the suburbs of [IJsselstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IJsselstein) and [Nieuwegein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwegein), as well as the [Utrecht Science Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Science_Park) district. The sneltram began operations in 1983 and is currently operated under the U-OV brand by the private transport company [Qbuzz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qbuzz). The system has a total length of 18.3 km and 54 trainsets, carrying over 9 million riders in 2023.[^cite_note-TD-2024-06-19-56]

Utrecht is the only city among the four largest in the Netherlands (the others being [Amsterdam](844.md), [The Hague](30269.md), and [Rotterdam](26049.md)) to award a public transportation concession by tender. Qbuzz will hold the current concession until December 2025, after which [Transdev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transdev) will assume operations until 2035.[^cite_note-TD-2024-06-19-56]

#### Bus transport

Utrecht Centraal railway station also serves local and regional buses at its west side (Centrumzijde) and at its east side (Jaarbuursplein), where both sides have connections to the Utrecht sneltram. Fifty local [bus](4146.md) routes are operated by [Qbuzz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qbuzz) under the U-OV brand until December 2025 when Transdev will take over the concession.[^cite_note-TD-2024-06-19-56] The local bus fleet is one of Europe's cleanest, using only buses compliant with the [Euro-VI standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_emission_standards#Emission_standards_for_trucks_and_buses)[^cite_note-Thomsen-2024-57] as well as electric buses for inner-city transport. The plan is that all buses be zero-emission by 2028.[^cite_note-TD-2024-06-19-56] Regional buses from the city are operated by [Arriva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arriva).

The Utrecht Centraal railway station is also served by [FlixBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlixBus). It acts as departure and arrival place of many coach companies serving holiday resorts in Spain and France—and during winter in [Austria](26964606.md) and [Switzerland](26748.md).

### Cycling

Like most Dutch cities, Utrecht has an extensive network of [cycle paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_path), making cycling safe and popular. 51% of journeys within the city are by bicycle, more than any other mode of transport.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] Bicycles are used by young and old people, and by individuals and families. They are mostly traditional, upright, steel-framed bicycles, with few gears. There are also bucket bikes for carrying cargo such as groceries or small children. Thanks in part to the access provided by bicycles, 100% of the population lives in a [15-minute city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15-minute_city) and more than 90% can get to the major destination types within 10 minutes.[^cite_note-60]

In 2014, the city council decided to build the world's largest [bicycle parking station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_parking_station), near the [Central Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Centraal_railway_station).[^cite_note-61] This three-floor construction cost over €30 million and can hold 12,500 bicycles. The bicycle parking station was built in stages, with the first part opening in August 2017, and the final section (after some delay) being opened on 19 August 2019.[^cite_note-62]

### Road transport

Utrecht is well-connected to the Dutch road network. Two of the most important major roads serve the city of Utrecht: the [A12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A12_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) and [A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) motorways connect [Amsterdam](844.md), [Arnhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem), [The Hague](30269.md) and [Maastricht](20125.md), as well as Belgium and Germany. Other major motorways in the area are the [Almere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almere)–[Breda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breda) [A27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A27_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) and the Utrecht–[Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groningen_\(city\)) [A28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A28_motorway_\(Netherlands\)).[^cite_note-63] Due to the increasing traffic and the ancient city plan, traffic congestion is a common phenomenon in and around Utrecht, causing elevated levels of [air pollutants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollutant). This has led to a passionate debate in the city about the best way to improve the city's air quality.

### Shipping

Utrecht has an industrial port located on the [Amsterdam-Rijnkanaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam-Rijnkanaal).[^cite_note-64] The container terminal has a capacity of 80,000 containers a year. In 2003, the port facilitated the transport of four million tons of cargo; mostly sand, gravel, fertiliser and fodder.[^cite_note-65] Additionally, some tourist boat trips are organised from various places on the Oudegracht; and the city is connected to touristic shipping routes through sluices.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

## Economy



'[De Inktpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Inktpot?action=edit\&redlink=1)' ("The Inkpot") with artificial [UFO](31814.md) sculpture by Marc Ruygrok, 1999. Photo 2003

[Production industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) constitutes a small part of the economy of Utrecht. The economy of Utrecht depends for a large part on the several large institutions located in the city. It is the centre of the Dutch railway network and the location of the head office of [Nederlandse Spoorwegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Spoorwegen). [ProRail](168969.md) is headquartered in *[De Inktpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Inktpot?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (The Inkwell), the largest brick building in the Netherlands[^cite_note-69] (the "UFO" featured on its façade stems from an art programme in 2000). [Rabobank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabobank), a large bank, has its headquarters in Utrecht.[^cite_note-70]

Utrecht is also informally considered the "capital" of the [Dutch games industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games_in_the_Netherlands).[^cite_note-71] It was named by [Business Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Finland) in 2023 as one of several capitals for the European games industry as a whole.[^cite_note-72] Utrecht's influence in this field was caused by video game development courses at its universities, which were the first such courses in Europe when launched in 2002. Since 2008 Utrecht has also been home to the studio incubator programme [Dutch Game Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Game_Garden), which has launched a number of studios in the area.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] By 2014 the programme had created 200 jobs.[^cite_note-75] Utrecht is also home to [Nixxes Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixxes_Software) (a [PlayStation Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Studios) subsidiary) as well as [Sokpop Collective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokpop_Collective).

## Education



View on the Science Park campus of [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) with the university library (centre), 2004

Utrecht hosts several large institutions of higher education. The most prominent of these is [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) (est. 1636), the largest university in the [Netherlands](21148.md) with 30,449 students (as of 2012). The university is partially based in the inner city as well as in the [Utrecht Science Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Science_Park) campus area, on the east side of the city. According to [Shanghai Jiaotong University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Jiaotong_University)'s university ranking in 2014, it is the 57th best university in the world.[^cite_note-76] Utrecht also houses the much smaller [University of Humanistic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Humanistic_Studies), which houses about 400 students.[^cite_note-77]

Utrecht is home of one of the locations of [TIAS School for Business and Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TIAS_School_for_Business_and_Society), focused on post-experience management education and the largest management school of its kind in the Netherlands. In 2008, its executive [MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBA) program was rated the 24th best program in the world by the *[Financial Times](136566.md)*.[^cite_note-78]

Utrecht is also home to two other large institutions of higher education: the vocational university [Hogeschool Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Professional_Education_Utrecht) (37,000 students),[^cite_note-79] with locations in the city and the Uithof campus; and the [HKU Utrecht School of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_School_of_the_Arts) (3,000 students).

There are many schools for [primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_education#Netherlands) and secondary education, allowing parents to select from different philosophies and religions in the school, as is inherent in the [Dutch school system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Netherlands).

## Culture



[Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy) statue by Henk Evertzen, Nijntjepleintje (Miffy small square), Utrecht, 2006



The [Rietveld Schröder House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rietveld_Schr%C3%B6der_House) from 1924, photo 2010



[Caryatids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caryatid) on the façade of the Winkel van Sinkel, Oudegracht, Utrecht, 2008

The city of Utrecht has an active cultural life, and in the Netherlands is second only to Amsterdam.[^cite_note-Umon-80] There are several theatres and theatre companies. The 1941 main city theatre was built by [Dudok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Marinus_Dudok). In addition to theatres, there is a large number of cinemas, including three arthouse cinemas. Utrecht is host to the international [Early Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_Oude_Muziek) (Festival Oude Muziek, for music before 1800) and the [Netherlands Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Film_Festival). The city has an important classical music hall, Vredenburg (1979 by [Herman Hertzberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Hertzberger)). Its acoustics are considered among the best of the 20th-century original music halls. The original Vredenburg music hall has been redeveloped as part of the larger station area redevelopment plan and in 2014 gained additional halls that allowed its merger with the rock club Tivoli and the SJU jazzpodium. There are several other venues for music throughout the city. Young musicians are educated in the [conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_or_university_school_of_music), a department of the [Utrecht School of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_School_of_the_Arts). There is a specialised [museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Speelklok) of automatically playing musical instruments.



Prins Clausbrug ([Prince Claus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Claus_of_the_Netherlands) Bridge) across the [Amsterdam-Rhine Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam-Rhine_Canal), Utrecht, 2005

There are many art galleries in Utrecht. There are also several foundations to support art and artists. Training of artists is done at the [Utrecht School of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_School_of_the_Arts). The [Centraal Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centraal_Museum) has many exhibitions on the arts, including a permanent exhibition on the works of Utrecht resident illustrator [Dick Bruna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Bruna), who is best known for creating [Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy) ("Nijntje", in Dutch). BAK, \[Dutch: "Basis voor Actuele Kunst," Basis for Contemporary Art] offers contemporary art exhibitions and public events, as well as a Fellowship program for practitioners involved in contemporary arts, theory and activisms. Although street art is illegal in Utrecht, the Utrechtse Kabouter, a picture of a gnome with a red hat, became a common sight in 2004.[^cite_note-81] Utrecht also houses one of the landmarks of modern architecture, the 1924 [Rietveld Schröder House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rietveld_Schr%C3%B6der_House), which is listed on UNESCO's [World Heritage Sites](44940.md).

Every Saturday, a paviour adds another letter to *[The Letters of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Letters_of_Utrecht)*, an endless poem in the cobblestones of the Oude Gracht in Utrecht. With the *Letters*, Utrecht has a [social sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sculpture) as a growing monument created for the benefit of future people.

To promote culture, Utrecht city organizes cultural Sundays. During a thematic Sunday, several organisations create a program which is open to everyone without, or with a substantially reduced, admission fee. There are also initiatives for [amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur) artists. The city subsidises an organisation for amateur education in arts aimed at all inhabitants (Utrechts Centrum voor de Kunsten), as does the university for its staff and students. Additionally there are also several private initiatives. The city council provides coupons for discounts to inhabitants who receive welfare to be used with many of the initiatives.

In 2017, Utrecht was named a UNESCO [City of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Literature). In 2025 the national [literature museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literatuurmuseum) will move from the Hague to Utrecht.

### Sports



[Triton rowing club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triton_rowing_club?action=edit\&redlink=1) team pauses with their coach by the Muntbrug, a rotating bridge built in 1887.

Utrecht is home to the premier league (professional) [football](10568.md) club [FC Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Utrecht), which plays in [Stadium Nieuw Galgenwaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Galgenwaard). It is also the home of Kampong, the largest (amateur) sportsclub in the Netherlands (4,500 members), SV Kampong.[^cite_note-82] Kampong features [field hockey](10886.md), association football, [cricket](25675557.md), [tennis](29773.md), [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\)) and [boules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boules). Kampong's men and women top hockey squads play in the highest Dutch hockey league, the Rabohoofdklasse. Utrecht is also home to baseball and softball club UVV, which plays in the highest Dutch baseball league: de Hoofdklasse. The [rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_football) culture in Utrecht is carried by the [USRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USRS?action=edit\&redlink=1), that has been playing in the highest leagues of the Dutch rugby pyramid since 1967. Utrecht's waterways are used by several rowing clubs. Viking is a large club open to the general public, and the student clubs Orca and Triton compete in the [Varsity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varsity_\(rowing_regatta\)) each year.

In July 2013, Utrecht hosted the [European Youth Olympic Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Youth_Olympic_Festival), in which more than 2,000 young athletes competed in nine Olympic sports. In July 2015, Utrecht hosted the Grand Départ and first stage of the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France).[^cite_note-83]

### Music and events

The city has several music venues, such as [TivoliVredenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TivoliVredenburg), [Tivoli De Helling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivoli_\(Utrecht\)), [ACU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACU_\(Utrecht\)), [Moira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moira_\(Utrecht\)), EKKO, dB's and RASA. Utrecht hosts the yearly Utrecht Early Music Festival ([Festival Oude Muziek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_Oude_Muziek)).[^cite_note-92] Several events in the [Thunderdome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderdome_\(music_festival\)) series, a large [gabber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabber) music event, have been held in [Jaarbeurs Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaarbeurs_Utrecht). The city also hosts [Trance Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trance_Energy) there. Every summer there used to be the *Summer Darkness* festival, which celebrated [goth culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goth_subculture) and [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_music).[^cite_note-Brandon_Hartley-93] In November the [Le Guess Who?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Guess_Who%3F) festival, focused on [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock), [art rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_rock) and [experimental rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_rock), takes place in many of the city's venues.

### Theatre

There are two main theatres in the city, the [Theater Kikker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_Kikker?action=edit\&redlink=1)[^cite_note-94] and the [Stadsschouwburg Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadsschouwburg_Utrecht?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-95] De Parade, a travelling theatre festival, performs in Utrecht in summer. The city also hosts the yearly [Festival aan de Werf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_aan_de_Werf) which offers a selection of contemporary international theatre, together with visual arts, public art and music. Another theatre, the Beatrix Theater, located in the *Beurskwartier*, has hosted musical productions including *[Mamma Mia!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamma_Mia!_\(musical\))* (2018-19).[^cite_note-96]
