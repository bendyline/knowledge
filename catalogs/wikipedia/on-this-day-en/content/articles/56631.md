**Bujumbura** (French pronunciation: [\[buʒumbuʁa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); Kirundi pronunciation: [\[buʒuᵐbuɾa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)), formerly **Usumbura**, is the economic capital, former political capital, largest city and main port of [Burundi](21490998.md). It ships most of the country's chief export, [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), as well as [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) and [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) ore. Bujumbura was formerly the country's political capital. In late December 2018, Burundian president [Pierre Nkurunziza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Nkurunziza) announced that he would follow through on a 2007 promise to return [Gitega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitega) its former political capital status, with Bujumbura remaining as economical capital and center of commerce. A vote in the [Parliament of Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Burundi) made the change official on 16 January 2019, and as of late 2025, the transition is still ongoing, having been estimated to end in 2022.[^cite_note-3]

## History

Bujumbura grew from a small village after it became a military post in [German East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa) in 1889. After [World War I](4764461.md) it was made the administrative center and *de facto* capital of the [Belgian](3343.md) [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) mandate of [Ruanda-Urundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruanda-Urundi). The name was changed from Usumbura to Bujumbura upon Burundi's independence in 1962.[^cite_note-4] Since independence, Bujumbura was the scene of frequent fighting between the country's two main ethnic groups, with [Hutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu) militias opposing the [Tutsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutsi)-dominated [Burundi Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_Force_\(Burundi\)) during the [Burundian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundian_Civil_War) in the early 90s.

During the 1990s civil war, battles between Burundi's two main ethnic groups destroyed hospitals, administrative buildings, the University of Burundi, and other schools in the city, severely crippling the local economy. The Belgian colonial period left a significant architectural mark on the city centre, with buildings constructed in styles popular in Europe during the first half of the 20th century, including elements of Modernism and Art Deco, many of which remain visible in the historical centre today.[^cite_note-5]

## Geography



[Lake Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tanganyika)

Bujumbura is on the north-eastern shore of [Lake Tanganyika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tanganyika), the second [deepest lake in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_by_depth) after [Lake Baikal](56865.md). The city also lies at the mouth of the [Ruzizi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruzizi_River) and the smaller [Mutimbuzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutimbuzi_River) River, [Ntahangwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ntahangwa_River), [Muha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muha_River) and [Kanyosha Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanyosha_River).

### Climate

Bujumbura has a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Aw*)[^cite_note-Climate-Data.org-6] bordering on [hot semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate#Hot_semi-arid_climates) (*BSh*). There are distinct [wet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) and [dry seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_seasons?action=edit\&redlink=1); the wet season being from October to April. Being close to the equator, average temperatures in the city vary little over the year. However due to the moderate altitude, temperatures generally are not as high as expected for a city close to the equator. The high temperature is around 29 °C (84 °F) and the low around 19 °C (66 °F).

## Economy



View of Bujumbura Market

See also: [Markets in Bujumbura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markets_in_Bujumbura) and [Economy of Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Burundi)

The [Port of Bujumbura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Bujumbura) is the largest port on Lake Tanganyika.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBurundi_Port_of_Bujumbura_Logistics_Cluster-13] Burundi depends on neighboring countries for access to the ocean. Goods may be carried by road via Rwanda, or by the lake and then by road or rail via Tanzania, Congo or Zambia. 80% of Burundi's external trade is carried via the last three routes, using the Port of Bujumbura.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEExploitation_du_Port_de_Bujumbura2-14] The port manages receipt and delivery of exports and imports, whether carried by ship or by truck. As of 2011 more than 90% of cargo handled was imports, of which about 60% entered by ship and 40% by truck.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStudy_of_Master_Plan_for_Port_Sector8-15]

[Bujumbura Central Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bujumbura_Central_Market) was in the city centre, along Rwagasore Avenue. During the [1993 ethnic violence in Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_ethnic_violence_in_Burundi), citizens became less likely to travel far from the city centre, and markets in neighbouring communities lost their business to the central market. At dawn on 27 January 2013 a serious fire ravaged the central market.[^cite_note-16] Due to the poor emergency response, the fire lasted for hours, resulting in a serious blow to local exchanges. Hundreds of vendors, local and foreign, lost their goods to the fire and the reported [looting](209811.md).[^cite_note-17]

## Sports

Bujumbura is the location for the city's multisport [Intwari stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_du_Prince_Louis_Rwagasore). Mainly used for [football](10568.md) matches, it is the country's largest stadium with 22,000 seats.

The city is also home to many [basketball](3921.md) and [tennis](29773.md) courts, as well as a multitude of indoor and outdoor swimming pools.

The stadium was refurbished under a FIFA Forward-funded project and officially inaugurated on 31 January 2026.[^cite_note-21] FIFA said the renovated venue’s capacity was 15,000 and that it now has an artificial pitch and floodlights.[^cite_note-22]

## Transport



Bujumbura International Airport

The [Bujumbura International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bujumbura_International_Airport) is situated on the outskirts of the city.

Public transport in Bujumbura mainly consists of taxis and mini-buses, locally known as the [Hiace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_HiAce). Public transport vehicles are generally white and blue.



Central Market minibus station

Bujumbura's taxis are abundant all over the city, and are considered the safest form of transportation. There are taxi-motos (motorcycle taxis) and taxis-vélos (bicycle taxis), although they are only available in certain parts of the city.

For long-distance travel, locals prefer to take the many Hiace full-size vans, which travel regularly across Burundi. Bujumbura's main bus terminal is located by the Central Market.

## Culture

Bujumbura's main attractions are its many museums, parks and monuments. Museums in the city include the [Burundi Museum of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundi_Museum_of_Life) and the [Burundi Geological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burundi_Geological_Museum?action=edit\&redlink=1). Other nearby attractions are the [Rusizi National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusizi_National_Park), the [Livingstone-Stanley Monument at Mugere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingstone-Stanley_Monument,_Burundi) (where [David Livingstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Livingstone) and [Henry Morton Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morton_Stanley) visited 14 days after their first historic meeting at [Ujiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujiji) in Tanzania), the presidential palace and the [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_\(river_or_stream\)) of the southernmost [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the [Nile](21244.md), described locally as the source of the Nile.

Bujumbura was also home of the independent weekly radio programme *Imagine Burundi*, the country's first locally produced English-language programme that focused on stories about life in the region. The show was broadcast from September 2010 to August 2013, and recordings are archived on the show's website at imagineburundi.com.[^cite_note-24]
