[Capital punishment](5902.md) is a legal penalty for numerous offences in [Syria](7515849.md): the Syrian government holds a retentionist view of capital punishment.[^cite_note-1]

Current laws allow the death penalty for [treason](31292.md); [espionage](10501.md); [murder](20171.md); [arson](184695.md) resulting in death; attempting a death-eligible crime; recidivism for a felony punishable by forced labor for life; political acts and military offences such as bearing arms against Syria in the ranks of the enemy, [desertion](488824.md) of the armed forces to the enemy, [insubordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insubordination), acts of incitement under martial law or in wartime; violent [robbery](54849.md); [terrorism](30636.md); subjecting a person to [torture](47702.md) or barbaric treatment during the commission of gang-[robbery](54849.md); [rape](7980471.md); membership in the [Muslim Brotherhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Brotherhood_of_Syria); joining the [Islamic State](9087364.md); [drug trafficking](218449.md) of narcotics; [political dissidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_dissent) and [falsification of material evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_evidence) resulting in a third party being convicted for a drug offense and sentenced to death.[^cite_note-2]

On 11 August 2026, a Syrian court sentenced deposed President [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md) and his brother [Maher al-Assad](3139640.md) to death in absentia, as part of a ruling against several former regime officials.[^cite_note-3] These sentences were also passed down to multiple former public officials, such as [Atef Najib](32651945.md), [Fahd Jassem al-Freij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahd_Jassem_al-Freij), and Mohamed Ayman Ayoush. In total, nine people received death sentences. [^cite_note-4]
