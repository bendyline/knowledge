**Assam**[^cite_note-15] is a state in [Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India), located south of the [Eastern Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Himalayas) along the [Brahmaputra Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_Valley) and [Barak River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_River) valleys. It covers an area of 78,438 km<sup>2</sup> (30,285 sq mi), and is the second largest state in northeastern India by area. As per the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India), the state had a population of 31.2 million, and is the largest by population in the region. It shares international borders with [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan) to the north and with [Bangladesh](3454.md) to the southwest. It borders the Indian states of [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh) to the north; [Nagaland](408677.md) and [Manipur](422642.md) to the east; [Meghalaya](326019.md), [Tripura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripura), and [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram) to the south; and [West Bengal](34040.md) to the west. It is connected to the rest of the India to the west via the [Siliguri Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siliguri_Corridor) (also known as the chicken neck),[^cite_note-16] a 22 km (14 mi) wide strip of land. [Dispur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispur) is the capital of Assam while [Dibrugarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibrugarh) is the second capital.[^cite_note-17] [Guwahati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guwahati) is the largest city and urban area in the state.

[Archaeological record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_record) from the region indicate early settlements from the second to first century BCE. Though the region finds mention in the Hindu epic *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)*, there is a lack of substantial archaeological evidence regarding its ancient history. The region is described as one of the frontiers of the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) in fourth century [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era). It was ruled by [Varmanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varman_dynasty) (c. 350–650 CE), [Mlechchhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlechchha_dynasty) (c.655–900 CE) and [Kamarupa-Palas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_dynasty_\(Kamarupa\)) (c. 900–1100 CE) later. The [Ahom kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom) was established in the 13th century CE, and ruled till the early 19th century CE. The period saw [various conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom%E2%80%93Mughal_conflicts) with the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughals). After the [Moamoria rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moamoria_rebellion) and [Burmese invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_invasions_of_Assam) weakened the Ahom kingdom, the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) started establishing tea estates in the region in the 19th century CE. The region became part of the [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency), and the [Assam Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Province) was established in 1912. The [Assam Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Legislative_Assembly) was formed in 1937. The province became part of Independent India on 15 August 1947, and was recognised as a state after India became a republic on 26 January 1950.

The state is organised into 35 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Assam) across five [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Assam). [Assamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_language) and [Bodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo_language) are the official languages of the state with [Meitei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language) recognised as an additional official language in three districts of the Barak Valley and [Hojai district](50128072.md), and [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) in the Barak Valley. The state is known for its namesake [Assam silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_silk) and [Assam tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_tea),[^cite_note-18] recognised as [Geographical Indications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_geographical_indications_in_India) in India. [Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crude_Oil) was discovered in the region in 1867, and [oil drilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_well) started in 1889. [Wildlife tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_tourism) is a major contributor to the [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Assam) of the state, aided by the presence of various [protected areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_areas_of_India) including the [Kaziranga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaziranga_National_Park) and [Manas National Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manas_National_Park), which are [World Heritage Sites](44940.md). More than half of the extant wild population of the one-horned [Indian rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rhinoceros) occurs in the state. The state is a rich [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) zone consisting of various ecosystems including [tropical rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforests), [deciduous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous_forests), riverine [grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland), [orchards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchards), and [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetlands). It receives copious amount of rainfall, which feeds the [Brahmaputra River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River) system, whose [tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) and [oxbow lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbow_lake) form a distinctive hydro-[geomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphic) environment.

## Etymology

Main article: [Etymology of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Assam)

The first dated mention of the region comes from *[Periplus of the Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea)* (1st century) and [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy)'s [Geographia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographia_\(Ptolemy\)) (2nd century), which calls the region *Kirrhadia*, apparently after the [Kirata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirata) population.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] In the classical period and up to the 12th century, the region east of the [Karatoya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karatoya_River), largely congruent to present-day Assam, was called [Kamarupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarupa), and alternatively, [Pragjyotisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragjyotisha).[^cite_note-21] Though a western portion of Assam as a region continued to be called [Kamrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamrup_region), the [Ahom kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom) that emerged in the east, and which came to dominate much of the [Brahmaputra Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_Valley), was called Assam (e.g. Mughals used *Asham*); and the [British province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Assam) too was called Assam. Though the precise [etymology of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Assam) is not clear, the name Assam, first used by the British in the [Treaty of Yandabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Yandabo) in 1826, is associated with the [Ahom people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_people), originally called *Shyam* ([Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_people)).[^cite_note-22]

## History

Main article: [History of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Assam)

### Pre-history

Further information: [People of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_of_Assam)

Assam and adjoining regions have evidences of human settlement from the beginning of the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age).[^cite_note-23] The hills at the height of 1,500 to 2,000 feet (460–615 m) were popular habitats probably due to availability of exposed dolerite basalt, useful for tool-making.[^cite_note-HKBarpujariCHOA-24] [Ambari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambari) site in Guwahati has revealed [Shunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunga_Empire)-[Kushana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushana) era artefacts including flight of stairs and a water tank which may date from 1st century BCE and may be 2,000 years old. Experts speculate that another significant find at Ambari is [Roman era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_era) [Roman roulette pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_pottery) from the 2nd century BCE. Gupta explains that while Guwahati, formerly known as Pragjyotishpur in ancient times, is mentioned in epics like the Mahabharata and the Puranas, there is a lack of substantial archaeological evidence regarding its ancient history, especially before the 7th century AD.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

### Legend

Further information: [Danava dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danava_dynasty), [Bhauma dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhauma_dynasty), and [Asura kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asura_kingdom)

According to a late text, [Kalika Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalika_Purana) (c. 9th–10th century [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era)), the earliest ruler of Assam was Mahiranga Danava of the [Danava dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danava_dynasty), which was removed by [Narakasura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narakasura) of [Mithila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithila_\(region\)) and established the [Bhauma dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhauma_dynasty). The last of these rulers, also Narakasura, was slain by [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna). Naraksura's son [Bhagadatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagadatta) became the king, who (it is mentioned in the Mahabharata) fought for the [Kauravas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaurava) in the [battle of Kurukshetra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurukshetra_War) with an army of [kiratas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiratas), [chinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinas) and dwellers of the eastern coast. At the same time towards the east in central Assam, [Asura kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asura_kingdom) was ruled by another line of kings.[^cite_note-27]

### Ancient era

Further information: [Kamarupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarupa)



[Kamarupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarupa) kingdom at its height



[Deopahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deopahar) ruins

Evidence indicates presence of civilisation in Assam around 2nd century BCE, a rock cut stupa at [Sri Surya Pahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Surya_Pahar) has been dated to 200 BCE contemporary with rock cut [Karle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karla_Caves) and [Bhaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaja_Caves) caves of Maharashtra. The site is located in a hilly terrain where several rock-cut [shivalingas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivalinga), votive stupas and the deities of [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus), [Buddhist](3267529.md) and [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain) pantheon are scattered.[^cite_note-28] [Samudragupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudragupta)'s 4th-century-CE [Allahabad pillar inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayaga_prashasti) mentions [Kamarupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarupa)[^cite_note-29] and [Davaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davaka) (Central Assam)[^cite_note-30] as frontier kingdoms of the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire). Davaka was later absorbed by Kamarupa, which grew into a large kingdom that spanned from Karatoya River to near present [Sadiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiya) and covered the entire Brahmaputra Valley, [North Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bengal), parts of [Bangladesh](3454.md) and, at times [Purnea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purnea) and parts of [West Bengal](34040.md).[^cite_note-31] The kingdom was ruled by three dynasties who traced their lineage from a mleccha or Kirata Naraka; the [Varmanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varman_dynasty) (c. 350–650 CE), the [Mlechchha dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlechchha_dynasty) (c.655–900 CE) and the [Kamarupa-Palas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_dynasty_\(Kamarupa\)) (c. 900–1100 CE), from their capitals in present-day [Guwahati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guwahati) ([Pragjyotishpura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragjyotishpura)), Tezpur ([Haruppeswara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haruppeswara)) and [North Gauhati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Gauhati) ([Durjaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durjaya)) respectively. All three dynasties claimed descent from [Narakasura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narakasura). In the reign of the Varman king, [Bhaskaravarman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskaravarman) (c. 600–650 CE), the Chinese traveller [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang) visited the [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamrup_region) and recorded his travels. Later, after weakening and disintegration (after the Kamarupa-Palas), the Kamarupa tradition was extended to c. 1255 CE by the Lunar I (c. 1120–1185 CE) and Lunar II (c. 1155–1255 CE) dynasties.[^cite_note-HKBarpujariCHOA-24]

### Medieval era

Further information: [Ahom kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom), [Chutia kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_kingdom), [Kamata kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamata_kingdom), [Kachari kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachari_kingdom), and [Baro-Bhuyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baro-Bhuyan)

Following the decline of [Kamarupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarupa), political authority in the Brahmaputra valley became divided among a number of regional states and chiefly confederacies. The [Kamata kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamata_kingdom) emerged in the west, while the [Chutia kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_kingdom) and [Kachari kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachari_kingdom) developed in eastern and southern Assam, respectively; the [Baro-Bhuyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baro-Bhuyan) controlled several areas of central Assam, and the [Ahom kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom) arose in Upper Assam. Over the following centuries, these states competed for territory and political supremacy, while the region also experienced repeated interventions from the Bengal Sultanate and, later, the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire). In the early 19th century, the [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_invasions_of_Assam) invaded the region, which led to its annexation.[^cite_note-32]

#### Chutia kingdom

Main article: [Chutia kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_kingdom)

See also: [Chutia-Ahom conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia-Ahom_conflicts)

The [Chutia kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_kingdom), ruled by [Chutia people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_people) of [Bodo-Kachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo-Kachari_people) origin, was the principal state of eastern Assam. Its territories extended on both banks of the Brahmaputra, broadly eastwards from [Vishwanath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biswanath_Chariali) on the north bank and the [Buridihing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihing_River) on the south, and included adjoining areas of present-day [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh). [Sadiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiya) formed its principal political and religious centre, while [Habung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habung) was among its western dependencies before the Ahom expansion.[^cite_note-33]

Competition with the expanding Ahom kingdom intensified in the early 16th century and resulted in the [Chutia-Ahom conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia-Ahom_conflicts). After a series of campaigns between 1512 and 1523–24, the Chutia royal state was defeated and its principal territories were incorporated into the Ahom kingdom. Chutia nobles, soldiers, artisans and administrative elements were subsequently absorbed into the expanding Ahom state, although armed Chutia resistance continued intermittently after the conquest.

#### Kamata kingdom

Main articles: [Kamata kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamata_kingdom) and [Koch dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_dynasty)

See also: [Koch-Ahom conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch-Ahom_conflicts)

The [Kamata kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamata_kingdom) emerged in the western part of the former Kamarupa kingdom during the 13th century, with its political centre eventually shifting to [Kamatapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamatapur).[^cite_note-34] Its last major ruling house, the [Khen dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khen_dynasty), was overthrown in 1498 by [Alauddin Hussain Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alauddin_Hussain_Shah) of Bengal, though Bengal's control over the region proved short-lived. In the early 16th century, [Biswa Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biswa_Singha) consolidated Koch power (another [Bodo-Kachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo-Kachari) group) and established the [Koch dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_dynasty) in the former Kamata domain. Under [Nara Narayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_Narayan) and his brother [Chilarai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilarai), the Koch kingdom became one of the principal powers of the region. It later divided into the western [Koch Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooch_Behar_State) and eastern [Koch Hajo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_Hajo), whose subsequent struggles with the Mughals and the Ahoms shaped the politics of western Assam in the 17th century.[^cite_note-35]

#### Kachari kingdom

Main article: [Kachari kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachari_kingdom)

The [Dimasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimasa_people) or Kachari kingdom was another major [Bodo-Kachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo-Kachari_people) state that ruled from the 13th century until 1854, with an early political centre at [Dimapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimapur). Its sphere of influence extended across the [Dikhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikhow_River), [Doyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doyang)-[Dhansiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhansiri) and [Kapili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopili_River) valley, including parts of central and southern Assam, and its relations with the Ahoms alternated between conflict, tributary arrangements and political accommodation.

During the reign of [Suhungmung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhungmung), Ahom expansion into the Dikhou-Dhansiri region led to repeated warfare with the Kacharis, culminating in the capture of Dimapur in the 16th century. The Dimasa rulers subsequently shifted their political centre southwards to [Maibang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maibang) in the North Cachar Hills and later to [Khaspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaspur) (after a marriage alliance with the Khaspur state), where the kingdom continued to remain an important regional power before its eventual absorption into British territory.

#### Ahom kingdom

Main article: [Ahom kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom)

See also: [Sukaphaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukaphaa), [Suhungmung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhungmung), [Rudra Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudra_Singha), and [Ahom-Mughal conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom-Mughal_conflicts)

According to the traditional chronology, the [Ahoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_people), a [Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_peoples) group, entered the Brahmaputra valley under [Sukaphaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukaphaa) in 1228 and established their political centre at [Charaideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charaideo) in 1253. This chronology has, however, been questioned by modern scholarship, which argues, based on Tai and Chinese sources, that the Ahom polity may instead have been founded during the 14th century.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] For the first three centuries, the polity remained concentrated mainly in the southeastern part of Upper Assam[^cite_note-38] and developed gradually from a relatively small Tai political formation into a territorial state.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

The major territorial expansion began under [Suhungmung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhungmung) in the early 16th century. The Ahoms annexed the [Chutia kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_kingdom), subdued a number of [Baro-Bhuyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baro-Bhuyan) chiefs, expanded into Kachari territory and extended their authority westwards. These conquests substantially enlarged the kingdom and brought new populations, administrative traditions and specialised occupational groups into the Ahom state.([Baruah 1986](#CITEREFBaruah1986)\:220–234)



[Garchuk Lachit Garh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garchuk_Lachit_Garh) a 17th-century mud fort ruins

In the 17th century, the expansion of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) into former Koch territories brought the Mughals into direct conflict with the Ahoms. The resulting [Ahom–Mughal conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom%E2%80%93Mughal_conflicts) continued intermittently from 1615 to 1682 and included victories and reverses on both sides. [Mir Jumla II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Jumla_II) occupied [Garhgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garhgaon) in 1662, but the Ahoms recovered Guwahati in 1667, defeated the Mughal forces at the [Battle of Saraighat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Saraighat) in 1671, and finally expelled them from Guwahati at the [Battle of Itakhuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Itakhuli) in 1682. The [Manas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manas_River) thereafter became the effective western boundary between the Ahom and Mughal spheres.[^cite_note-43]



Royal court of [Siva Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siva_Singha) and [Ambika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambika,_Bar_Raja) by Badha Ligira, c. 1736.

The kingdom reached another period of political and cultural strength under rulers such as [Rudra Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudra_Singha) in the late 17th and early 18th centuries. From the late 18th century, however, the prolonged [Moamoria rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moamoria_rebellion) weakened the state, and subsequent factional struggles facilitated the [Burmese invasions of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_invasions_of_Assam). The Burmese occupation ended with the [Treaty of Yandabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Yandabo) in 1826, after which the East India Company established control over Assam.([Gogoi 2017](#CITEREFGogoi2017)\:20–26)

### Colonial era

Further information: [Colonial Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Assam) and [Assam Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Province)



Map of [Eastern Bengal and Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bengal_and_Assam) under British rule, 1907–1909.

In the later part of the 18th century, religious tensions and atrocities by the nobles led to the [Moamoria rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moamoria_rebellion) (1769–1805), resulting in tremendous casualties of lives and property. The rebellion was suppressed but the kingdom was severely weakened by the civil war. Political rivalry between Prime Minister Purnananda [Burhagohain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burhagohain) and Badan Chandra [Borphukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borphukan), the [Ahom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_people) Viceroy of Western Assam, led to an invitation to the Burmese by the latter,[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] in turn leading to three successive [Burmese invasions of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_invasions_of_Assam). The reigning monarch [Chandrakanta Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudingphaa) tried to check the Burmese invaders but he was defeated after fierce resistance, which led to the Burmese occupation of Assam.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

A reign of terror was unleashed by the Burmese on the Assamese people,[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] who fled to neighbouring kingdoms and British-ruled [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] The Burmese reached the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company)'s borders, and the [First Anglo-Burmese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Burmese_War) ensued in 1824. The war ended under the [Treaty of Yandabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Yandabo)[^cite_note-57] in 1826, with the Company taking control of Western Assam and installing [Purandar Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purandar_Singha) as king of Upper Assam in 1833. The arrangement lasted until 1838 and thereafter the British gradually annexed the entire region. Thereafter the court language and medium of instruction in educational institutions of Assam was made [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), instead of Assamese. Starting from 1836 until 1873, this imposition of a foreign tongue created greater unemployment among the [People of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_of_Assam) and Assamese literature naturally suffered in its growth.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]



Showing a historical incident at Kanaklata Udyan, [Tezpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tezpur)

The discovery of *[Camellia sinensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camellia_sinensis)* in 1834 in Assam was followed by testing in 1836–37 in London.[^cite_note-60] The British allowed companies to rent land from 1839 onwards. Thereafter tea plantations proliferated in Eastern Assam,[^cite_note-Roy-61] where the soil and the climate were most suitable. Problems with the imported Han Chinese labourers from China and hostility from native Assamese resulted in the migration of forced labourers from central and eastern parts of India. After initial trial and error with planting the Chinese and the Assamese-Chinese hybrid varieties, the planters later accepted the local *[Camellia assamica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camellia_assamica)* as the most suitable variety for Assam. By the 1850s, the industry started seeing some profits. The industry saw initial growth, when in 1861, investors were allowed to own land in Assam and it saw substantial progress with the invention of new technologies and machinery for preparing processed tea during the 1870s.

Despite the commercial success, tea labourers continued to be exploited, working and living under poor conditions. Fearful of greater government interference, the tea growers formed the [Indian Tea Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Tea_Association) in 1888 to lobby to retain the status quo. The organisation was successful in this, but even after India's independence, conditions of the labourers have improved very little.[^cite_note-62]

The *Assam Postage Circle* was established by 1873 under the headship of the Deputy Post Master General.[^cite_note-63]

Initially, Assam was made a part of the [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency), then in 1906 it was made a part of [Eastern Bengal and Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bengal_and_Assam) province, and in 1912 it was reconstituted into a chief commissioners' province. In 1913, a legislative council and, in 1937, the Assam Legislative Assembly, were formed in Shillong, the erstwhile capital of the region. The British tea planters imported labour from central India adding to the demographic canvas.

The Assam territory was first separated from Bengal in 1874 as the 'North-East Frontier' [non-regulation province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-regulation_province), also known as the Assam Chief-Commissionership. It was incorporated into the new province of [Eastern Bengal and Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bengal_and_Assam) in 1905 after the partition of Bengal (1905–1911) and re-established in 1912 as [Assam Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Province).[^cite_note-64]

At the turn of the 20th century, British India consisted of eight provinces that were administered either by a governor or a lieutenant-governor. Assam Province was one among the major eight provinces of British India.&#x20;

After a few initially unsuccessful attempts to gain independence for Assam during the 1850s, anti-colonial Assamese joined and actively supported the [Indian National Congress](149333.md) against the British from the early 20th century, with [Gopinath Bordoloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopinath_Bordoloi) emerging as the preeminent nationalist leader in the Assam Congress. Bordoloi's major political rival in this time was Sir Saidullah, who was representing the [Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League), and had the backing of the influential Muslim cleric [Maulana Bhasani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Bhasani).[^cite_note-65]

With the partition of India in 1947, Assam became a constituent state of India. The [Sylhet District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_District) of Assam (excluding the Karimganj subdivision) was given up to East Pakistan, which later became Bangladesh.

### Modern history

See also: [Undivided Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undivided_Assam) and [Assam separatist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_separatist_movements)



Assam till the 1950s; The new states of Nagaland, Meghalaya and Mizoram formed in the 1960–1970s. The capital of Assam was shifted to Dispur, a suburb of Guwahati. After the Indo-China war in 1962, Arunachal Pradesh was also separated out.

[Separatist groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_separatist_movements) began forming along ethnic lines, and demands for autonomy and sovereignty grew, resulting in the fragmentation of Assam. In 1961, the government of Assam passed legislation making use of the [Assamese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_language) compulsory. It was withdrawn later [under pressure from Bengali speaking people in Cachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Language_Movement_\(Barak_Valley\)). The government of India, which has the unilateral powers to change the borders of a state, divided Assam into several states within the borders of what was then Assam. In 1963, the Naga Hills district became the 16th state of India under the name of [Nagaland](408677.md). Part of Tuensang was added to Nagaland. In 1970, in response to the demands of the Khasi, Jaintia and Garo people of the [Meghalaya Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbi-Meghalaya_plateau), the districts containing the Khasi Hills, Jaintia Hills, and Garo Hills were formed into an autonomous state within Assam; in 1972 this became a separate state under the name of Meghalaya. In 1972, Arunachal Pradesh (the [North East Frontier Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_Frontier_Agency)) and Mizoram (from the Mizo Hills in the south) were separated from Assam as union territories; both became states in 1986.[^cite_note-Bhattacharyya1995-66] Since the restructuring of Assam after independence, communal tensions and violence remain.&#x20;

In the 1980s the [Brahmaputra Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_Valley) saw a six-year [Assam Agitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Agitation)[^cite_note-Hazarika-67] triggered by the discovery of a sudden rise in registered voters on electoral rolls. It tried to force the government to identify and deport foreigners illegally migrating from neighbouring [Bangladesh](3454.md) and to provide constitutional, legislative, administrative and cultural safeguards for the indigenous Assamese majority, which they felt was under threat due to the increase of migration from Bangladesh. The agitation ended after an accord (Assam Accord 1985) between its leaders and the Union Government, which remained unimplemented, causing simmering discontent.[^cite_note-SinhaSKillmig-68]

The post 1970s experienced the growth of armed separatist groups such as the [United Liberation Front of Asom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Liberation_Front_of_Asom) (ULFA)[^cite_note-Hazarika-67] and the [National Democratic Front of Bodoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Front_of_Bodoland) (NDFB). In November 1990, the [Government of India](553883.md) deployed the [Indian army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_army), after which low-intensity military conflicts and political homicides have been continuing for more than a decade. In recent times, ethnically based militant groups have grown. The [Panchayati Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_Raj) Act has been applied in Assam, after agitation of the communities due to the sluggish rate of development and general apathy of successive state governments towards Indigenous Assamese communities.

Deadly floods hit the state in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Assam_floods) and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Assam_floods).[^cite_note-69]

## Geography

Main article: [Physical geography of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_geography_of_Assam)

See also: [Tourism in North East India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_North_East_India)



Environs: Assam, dissected hills of the South Indian Plateau system and the Himalayas all around its north, north-east and east.

A significant geographical aspect of Assam is that it contains three of six physiographic divisions of India – The Northern Himalayas (Eastern Hills), The Northern Plains (Brahmaputra plain) and Deccan Plateau (Karbi Anglong). As the Brahmaputra flows in Assam the climate here is cold and there is rainfall most of the month. Geomorphic studies conclude that the Brahmaputra, the life-line of Assam, is an [antecedent river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antecedent_river) older than the Himalayas, which has [entrenched itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrenched_river) since they started rising. The river with steep [gorges](78478.md) and rapids in Arunachal Pradesh entering Assam, becomes a [braided river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braided_river) (at times 10 mi/16 km wide) and with tributaries, creates a flood plain (Brahmaputra Valley: 50–60 mi/80–100 km wide, 600 mi/1000 km long).[^cite_note-RLSinghIndia-70] The hills of [Karbi Anglong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbi_Anglong), [North Cachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Cachar) and those in and close to Guwahati (also Khasi-Garo Hills) now eroded and dissected are originally parts of the South Indian Plateau system.[^cite_note-RLSinghIndia-70] In the south, the [Barak River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_River) originating in the Barail Range (Assam-Nagaland border) flows through the [Cachar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachar_district) with a 25–30 miles (40–50 km) wide valley and enters Bangladesh with the name [Surma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surma_River).

Urban centres include [Guwahati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guwahati), one of the 100 fastest growing cities in the world.[^cite_note-71] Guwahati is also referred to as the "Gateway to North-East India". [Silchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silchar) (in the Barak valley) is the second most populous city in Assam and an important centre of business. Other large cities include [Dibrugarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibrugarh), an oil and natural gas industry centre.[^cite_note-72]

### Climate

With the [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate), Assam is temperate (summer max. at 95–100 °F or 35–38 °C and winter min. at 43–46 °F or 6–8 °C) and experiences heavy rainfall and high humidity.[^cite_note-RLSinghIndia-70][^cite_note-73] The climate is characterised by heavy monsoon downpours reducing summer temperatures and affecting foggy nights and mornings in winters, frequent during the afternoons. Spring (March–April) and autumn (September–October) are usually pleasant with moderate rainfall and temperature. Assam's agriculture usually depends on the south-west monsoon rains.

#### Flooding

See also: [Brahmaputra floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_floods)

Every year, rivers like the Brahmaputra and Barak overflow due to heavy rainfall, causing widespread flooding across Assam. The rising water levels submerge nearby areas, washing away houses, livestock, and damaging infrastructure such as bridges, railway tracks, and roads, leading to communication breakdowns in many regions. This natural disaster also results in numerous fatalities throughout the state.[^cite_note-Assam_Flood_Toll_Rises_to_13-74][^cite_note-Flood_situation_in_Assam_worsens-75]

### Fauna

See also: [Biodiversity of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_of_Assam)

Rhinoceros unicornis

An [Indian rhino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rhinoceros) at [Kaziranga National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaziranga_National_Park)

Golden langur

An endangered [golden langur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_langur)

Assam is one of the richest [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) zones in the world and consists of tropical [rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest),[^cite_note-76] [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous) forests, riverine [grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasslands),[^cite_note-77] [bamboo](39029.md)[^cite_note-78] orchards and numerous [wetland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland)[^cite_note-79] ecosystems; Many are now protected as national parks and reserved forests.

Assam has wildlife sanctuaries, the most prominent of which are two UNESCO [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites)[^cite_note-80]-the [Kaziranga National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaziranga_National_Park), on the bank of the [Brahmaputra River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River), and the [Manas Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manas_Wildlife_Sanctuary), near the border with Bhutan. The Kaziranga is a refuge for the fast-disappearing Indian one-horned rhinoceros. The state is the last refuge for numerous other endangered and threatened species including the [white-winged wood duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-winged_wood_duck) or *deohanh*, [Bengal florican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_florican), [black-breasted parrotbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-breasted_parrotbill), [red-headed vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-headed_vulture), [white-rumped vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-rumped_vulture), [greater adjutant](3566768.md), [Jerdon's babbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerdon's_babbler), [rufous-necked hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufous-necked_hornbill), [Bengal tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_tiger), [Asian elephant](379035.md), [pygmy hog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmy_hog), [gaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaur), [wild water buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_water_buffalo), [Indian hog deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_hog_deer), [hoolock gibbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoolock_gibbon), [golden langur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_langur), [capped langur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capped_langur), [barasingha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barasingha), [Ganges river dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_river_dolphin), [Barca snakehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channa_barca), [Ganges shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_shark), [Burmese python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_python), [Brahminy river turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahminy_river_turtle), [black pond turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_pond_turtle), [Asian forest tortoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_forest_tortoise), and [Assam roofed turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_roofed_turtle). Threatened species that are extinct in Assam include the [gharial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharial), a critically endangered fish-eating crocodilian, and the [pink-headed duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink-headed_duck) (which may be extinct worldwide). For the state bird, the white-winged wood duck, Assam is a globally important area.[^cite_note-81] In addition to the above, there are three other National Parks in Assam namely Dibru Saikhowa National Park, Nameri National Park and the Orang National Park.

Assam has conserved the one-horned [Indian rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rhinoceros) from near extinction, along with the [pygmy hog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmy_hog), tiger and numerous species of birds, and it provides one of the last wild habitats for the [Asian elephant](379035.md). [Kaziranga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaziranga) and [Manas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manas_National_Park) are both [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites). The state contains [Sal tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorea_robusta) forests and forest products, much depleted from earlier times. A land of high rainfall, Assam displays greenery. The Brahmaputra River tributaries and [oxbow lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbow_lake) provide the region with hydro-[geomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphic) environment.

The state has the largest population of the [wild water buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_water_buffalo) in the world.[^cite_note-82] The state has the highest diversity of birds in India with around 820 species.[^cite_note-83] With subspecies the number is as high as 946.[^cite_note-84] The mammal diversity in the state is around 190 species.[^cite_note-85]

### Flora



Blooming of [Kopou Orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhynchostylis_retusa) marks the beginning of the festive season of *[Bihu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihu)* in Assam.

Assam is remarkably rich in [Orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchidaceae) species and the [Foxtail orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhynchostylis_retusa) is the state flower of Assam.[^cite_note-86] The recently established Kaziranga National Orchid and Biodiversity Park boasts more than 500 of the estimated 1,314 orchid species found in India.

### Geology

Assam has [petroleum](23195.md), natural gas, coal, [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) and other minor minerals such as [magnetic quartzite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartzite), [kaolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaolin), [sillimanites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sillimanite), [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) and [feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar).[^cite_note-NEDFiNICminerals-87] A small quantity of iron ore is available in western districts.[^cite_note-NEDFiNICminerals-87] Discovered in 1867, a [USGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USGS) estimate shows 399 million barrels (63,400,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of oil, 1,178 billion cubic feet (3.34×10<sup>10</sup> m<sup>3</sup>) of gas and 67 million barrels (10,700,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of [natural gas liquids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_liquids) in the Assam Geologic Province.[^cite_note-Oil-88][^cite_note-89]

The region is prone to natural disasters like annual floods and frequent mild earthquakes. Strong earthquakes were recorded in 1869, [1897](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1897_Assam_earthquake), and [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Assam%E2%80%93Tibet_earthquake).

Traditionally, Assamese was the language of the common folk in the ancient [Kamarupa kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarupa_kingdom) and in the medieval kingdoms of [Dimasa Kachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachari_kingdom), [Chutiya Kachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutiya_kingdom), Borahi Kachari, [Ahom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom) and [Kamata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamata_kingdom) kingdoms. Traces of the language are found in many poems by Luipa, Sarahapa, and others, in [Charyapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charyapada) (c. 7th–8th century CE). Modern dialects such as [Kamrupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamrupi_dialects) and [Goalpariya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goalpariya_dialects) are remnants of this language, which blend into the [Rajbanshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajbanshi_language_\(Nepal\)) and [Rangpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpuri_language) lects spoken in North Bengal which have the same origin. Moreover, Assamese in its traditional form was used by the ethno-cultural groups in the region as lingua-franca, which spread during the stronger kingdoms and was required for economic integration. Localised forms of the language still exist in Nagaland and Arunachal Pradesh.

Linguistically modern Assamese traces its roots to the version developed by the American Missionaries based on the local form used near [Sivasagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sivasagar) (Xiwôxagôr) district. Assamese (*Ôxômiya*) is a rich language due to its hybrid nature and unique characteristics of pronunciation and softness. The presence of [Voiceless velar fricative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_velar_fricative) in Assamese makes it a unique among other similar [Indo-Aryan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages).[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

[Bodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boro_language_\(India\)) is spoken largely in [Western Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Assam). It is official language of the [Bodoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodoland_Territorial_Region) territorial region and co-official language of the state of Assam. It is also one of twenty-two languages listed in the Eighth Schedule of the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India). Spatial distribution patterns of the ethno-cultural groups, cultural traits and the phenomenon of naming all the major rivers in the [North East Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-East_India) with Bodo-Kachari words (e.g. [Dihing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihing), Dibru, [Dihong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihong), D/[Tista](1365385.md), and Dikrai) reveal that it was more widely-spoken in ancient times. Other languages of Tibeto-Burman origin and related to Bodo-Kachari are [Deori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deori_language), [Mising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_language), [Karbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbi_language), [Rabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabha_language), and [Tiwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwa_\(Lalung\)).

There are approximately 590,000 [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language) speakers spread all over the state forming about 1.98% of Assam's total population according to 2011 census.

There are speakers of [Tai languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_languages) in Assam. A total of six Tai languages were spoken in Assam. Two are now extinct.[^cite_note-Morey-143]

- [Tai Phake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phake_language)

- [Tai Aiton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiton_language)

- [Khamti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamti_language)

- [Khamyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamyang_language) (critically endangered)

- [Ahom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_language) (extinct)

- [Turung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turung_language) (extinct)

There are also speakers of Dravidian [Kurukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurukh_language)[^cite_note-144] and Austroasiatic [Khasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_language) speakers scattered across Assam.

### Subdivisions

The administrative districts are further subdivided into 54 "Subdivisions" or *[Mahakuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakuma)*.[^cite_note-RDGoA-147] Every district is administered from a district headquarters with the office of the [Deputy Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Collector), District Magistrate, Office of the District Panchayat and usually with a [district court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Courts_of_India).

The [local governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Governance_in_India) system is organised under the *jila-parishad* (District Panchayat) for a district, *[panchayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_Raj)* for group of or individual rural areas and under the urban local bodies for the towns and cities. There are now 2489 village panchayats covering 26247 villages in Assam.[^cite_note-DIPRGoA-150] The 'town-committee' or *nagar-somiti* for small towns, 'municipal board' or *pouro-sobha* for medium towns and [municipal corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporation) or *pouro-nigom* for the cities consist of the urban local bodies.

For revenue purposes, the districts are divided into revenue circles and *mouza*s; for the development projects, the districts are divided into 219 'development-blocks' and for law and order these are divided into 206 police stations or *thana*.

[Guwahati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guwahati) is the largest [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) and [urban conglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_conglomeration) administered under the highest form of [urban local body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_local_body) – [Guwahati Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guwahati_Municipal_Corporation) in Assam. The Corporation administers an area of 216.79 km<sup>2</sup> (83.70 sq mi).[^cite_note-151] Apart from [Guwahati Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guwahati_Municipal_Corporation) and [Dibrugarh Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibrugarh_Municipal_Corporation) All other urban centres are managed under [Municipal Boards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagar_Palika).

## Disputes and social issues

### Inter-state dispute



According to Assam Government, Assam has border dispute with four states namely Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh.[^cite_note-The_Times_of_India-166]

#### Assam-Mizoram dispute

[Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram) used to be a district of Assam as [Lushai hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lushai_hills) before being carved out as a separate union territory and later, becoming another state in 1987. Because of the history, the district's borders did not really matter for locals for a long time. Mizoram shares a border with the districts [Cachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachar), [Hailakandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailakandi) and [Karimganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimganj) which comes under [Barak valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_valley) region of Assam. Over time, the two states started having different perceptions about where the demarcation should be. While Mizoram wants it to be along an [Inner Line Permit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Line_Permit) notified in 1875 to protect tribals from outside influence, which Mizos feel is part of their historical homeland, Assam wants it to be demarcated according to district boundaries drawn up much later.[^cite_note-The_Times_of_India-166][^cite_note-167]

#### Assam-Meghalaya dispute

[Meghalaya](326019.md) has identified close to a dozen areas on which it has a dispute with Assam about the state's borders. The chief ministers of the two states, [Himanta Biswa Sarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himanta_Biswa_Sarma) and Megahalya's [Conrad Sangma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_Sangma), recently held the first-ever meeting on inter-state border dispute. Both the states have agreed to individually assess the claims for all 12 areas flagged by Meghalaya in the past. A second round of discussion between the two state CMs will be held next month of August. On the question of the role the Union Government is playing in redressing the inter-State border dispute in the country, minister of state for home affairs [Nityanand Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nityanand_Rai) said, "The approach of the Central Government has consistently been that inter-state disputes can be resolved only with the cooperation of the State Governments concerned and that the Central Government acts only as a facilitator for amicable settlement of the dispute in the spirit of mutual understanding."[^cite_note-The_Times_of_India-166]

#### Assam-Nagaland dispute

The border dispute between the two states has been going on since the formation of Nagaland in 1963. The two states lay claim to Merapani, a small village next to the plains of Assam's Golaghat district. There have been reports of violent clashes in the region since the 1960s.[^cite_note-The_Times_of_India-166][^cite_note-168]

#### Assam-Arunachal Pradesh dispute

Assam shares an 804.10 km inter-state boundary with [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh). The state of Arunachal Pradesh, created in 1987, claims some land that traditionally belonged to its residents has been given to Assam. A tripartite committee had recommended that certain territories be transferred from Assam to Arunachal. The two states have since been battling it out in the [Supreme court of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_court_of_India) over the issue. Some incidents of local violence have been reported from the borders.[^cite_note-The_Times_of_India-166][^cite_note-169]

### Separate statehood demand within Assam

#### Ahomland

[Upper Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Assam_division)'s various [Tai-Ahom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_people) organisations like "Ahom Tai Mangoliya Rajya Parishad" (ATMRP), has been demanding a separate Ahomland state since 1967, comprising erstwhile Un-divided Sivasagar and Lakhimpur districts (today's [Upper Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Assam) and [North Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Assam) divisions) respectively.[^cite_note-170] On 2023, "Tai Ahom Yuba Parishad, Assam" (TAYPA) have organised a protest at Chachal and have demanded separate Ahomland state.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

#### Barak state

Main article: [Barak state movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_state_movement)



Barak Valley

The [Barak Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_Valley) of Assam comprising the present districts of [Cachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachar), [Karimganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimganj_district) and [Hailakandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailakandi_district) is contiguous to [Sylhet](577594.md) (Bengal plains), where the Bengalis, according to historian J.B. [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language).[^cite_note-Cachar-174] Migrations to Cachar increased after the British annexation of the region.[^cite_note-Cachar-174] The native [Bengali people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Hindus) of Southern Assam demanded separate state for themselves within the Bengali majority areas of Assam particularly Bengali majority [Barak valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_valley) comprising three districts: [Cachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachar_district), [Hailakandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailakandi_district), [Karimganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimganj_district) along with [Dima Hasao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dima_Hasao_district) and parts of [Hojai](50128072.md) was also demanded to meet the criteria for creating a separate state for themselves by carving out from Assam's Assamese majority [Brahmaputra Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_Valley) post [NRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Citizens_for_Assam).[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178] [Silchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silchar) is the proposed capital of Barak state.[^cite_note-179] Barak valley is the most neglected part of Assam in terms of its infrastructure development, tourism sector, educational institutions, hospitals, IT industries, G.D.P, H.D.I etc. which is still lagging behind in comparison to the Assam's mainland [Brahmaputra Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_Valley) which have access to all of those facilities mentioned above.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184] In fact, the Assam's Southern part have an overall indigenous Bengali majority population, particularly [Hojai](50128072.md) have overall (54%) Bengali-speaking population,[^cite_note-185] [Barak Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_Valley) region have an overwhelming Bengali majority of about 80.3%, while [Dima Hasao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dima_Hasao_district) have approximately 30.2% significant Bengali plurality on certain pockets specially in the urban areas of the district.[^cite_note-censusindia.gov.in-133]

#### Bodoland

Main article: [Bodoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodoland)



Bodoland district map

The agitation for the creation of a separate [Bodoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodoland) state resulted in an agreement between the Indian Government, the Assam state government and the [Bodo Liberation Tigers Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo_Liberation_Tigers_Force). According to the agreement made on 10 February 2003, the [Bodoland Territorial Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodoland_Territorial_Council), an entity subordinate to the government of Assam, was created to govern four districts covering 3082 [Bodo Kachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo_people)-majority villages in Assam.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187] Elections to the council were held on 13 May 2003, and [Hagrama Mohilary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagrama_Mohilary) was sworn in as the chief of the 46-member council on 4 June.[^cite_note-188] Demographic wise, the Indigenous Bodo tribe constitutes half of the region's population, along with the region have also significant large number of other ethnic minorities which includes: Assamese, Koch Rajbangshi, Garo, Rabha tribe, Adivasis, Nepalis, Tea tribes, Bengalis, Biharis, Marwaris and Muslims.[^cite_note-censusindia.gov.in-133]

#### Dimaraji

Main article: [Dimaraji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimaraji)



Map of Dimaraji state

The [Dimasa people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimasa_people) of northeast India have been demanding a separate state called [Dimaraji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimaraji) or "Dimaland" for several decades. It would comprise the [Dimasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimasa_people)-[Kachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimasa_people) inhabited areas, namely [Dima Hasao district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dima_Hasao_district), [Cachar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachar_district), parts of [Barak Valley, Nagaon district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaon_district), [Hojai district](50128072.md) and [Karbi Anglong district](1698656.md) in Assam together with part of [Dimapur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimapur_district) in [Nagaland](408677.md).

#### Karbiland

See also: [Karbi Anglong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbi_Anglong)



[Karbi Anglong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbi_Anglong) is one of the 35 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Assam) of Assam. Karbi Anglong was previously known as Mikir Hills. It was part of the Excluded Areas and Partially Excluded Areas (the present North East India) in British India. The British Indian government had never included this area under their government's jurisdiction. Thereby, no government development work or activity were done, nor any tax levied from the hills including Karbi Anglong. The first memorandum for a [Karbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbi_people) homeland was presented to Governor Reid on 28 October 1940 by [Semsonsing Ingti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semsonsing_Ingti) and Khorsing Terang at Mohongdijua.[^cite_note-189] The Karbi leaders were then, a part of the All Party Hill Leaders' Conference (APHLC) which was formed on 6 July 1960.[^cite_note-190] The movement again gained momentum when the Karbi Anglong District Council passed a resolution demanding a Separate State in 1981. Then again from 1986 through the leadership of Autonomous State Demand Committee (ASDC), demanded Autonomous statehood of Karbi Anglong and Dima Hasao under Article 244(A). In 2002, the Karbi Anglong Autonomous Council passed another resolution to press for the demand of statehood. Several other memoranda were submitted at different times by several organisations. The demand for a separate state turned violent on 31 July 2013 when student demonstrators set government buildings on fire. Following the incident, the elected leaders of Karbi Anglong jointly submitted a memorandum to the [Prime Minister of India](24452.md) demanding a separate State. Demographic wise, more than half of the Karbi Anglong population is made up of Indigenous Karbi tribe with significant migrants from other parts of India.[^cite_note-censusindia.gov.in-133]

### Migration from Bangladesh

Assam has been a major site of migration since the [Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) of the subcontinent, with the first wave being composed largely of [Bengali Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Hindu) [refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengali_refugees) arriving during and shortly after the establishment of India and Pakistan (current-day Bangladesh was originally part of Pakistan, known as [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan)) in 1947–1951. Between the period of first patches (1946–1951), around 274,455 Bengali Hindu refugees arrived from what is now called Bangladesh (former [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan)) in various locations of Assam as permanent settlers; in the second patches between 1952 and 1958, around 212,545 Bengali Hindus from Bangladesh took shelter in various parts of the state permanently.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] After the [1964 East Pakistan riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_East_Pakistan_riots) many Bengali Hindus poured into Assam as refugees, and the number of Hindu migrants in the state rose to 1,068,455 in 1968 (sharply after 4 years of the riot).[^cite_note-193] The fourth patches, numbering around 347,555, arrived after the [Bangladesh liberation war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_liberation_war) of 1971 as refugees; most of them, being Bengali-speaking Hindus, decided to stay in Assam permanently afterwards.[^cite_note-194] Though the governments of India and Bangladesh made agreements for the repatriation of certain groups of refugees after the second and third waves, a large presence of refugees and other migrants and their descendants remained in the state. Nevertheless, still people of Bangladesh have been immigrating to Assam on a regular basis. As per reports, about 635 of Bangladeshi people, mostly Hindus,immigrate to Assam daily.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196]

Besides migration caused by displacement, there is also a large and continual unregulated movement between Assam and neighbouring regions of Bangladesh with an exceptionally porous border. The situation is called a risk to Assam's as well as India's security.[^cite_note-197] The continual [illegal entry](7124412.md) of people into Assam, mostly from [Bangladesh](3454.md), has caused economic upheaval and social and political unrest.[^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199] During the [Assam Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Movement) (1979–1985), the [All Assam Students Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Assam_Students_Union) (AASU) and others demanded that government stop the influx of [immigrants](4599312.md) and [deport](412990.md) those who had already settled.[^cite_note-Illegal_Migration_into_Assam-200] During this period, 855 people (the AASU says 860) died in various conflicts with migrants and police.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202] The 1983 Illegal Migrants (Determination by Tribunal) Act, applied only to Assam, decreed that any person who entered the Assam after Bangladesh declared independence from Pakistan in 1971 and without authorisation or travel documents is to be considered a foreigner, with the decision on foreigner status to be carried out by designated tribunals. In 1985, the [Indian Government](553883.md) and leaders of the agitation signed the [Assam accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_accord) to settle the conflict.[^cite_note-Illegal_Migration_into_Assam-200]

The 1991 census made the changing demographics of border districts more visible.[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-Illegal_Migration_into_Assam-200] Since 2010, the Indian Government has undertaken the updating of the [National Register of Citizens for Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Citizens_for_Assam), and in 2018 the 32.2 million residents of Assam were subject to a review of their citizenship.[^cite_note-204] In August 2019, India released the names of the 2 million residents of Assam that had been determined to be non-citizens and whose names had therefore been struck off the Register of Citizens, depriving them of rights and making them subject to action, and potentially leaving some of them stateless, and the government has begun deporting non-citizens, while detaining 1,000 others that same year.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207]

In January 2019, the Assam's peasant organisation [Krishak Mukti Sangram Samiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishak_Mukti_Sangram_Samiti) (KMSS) claimed that there are around 20 [lakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakh) [Hindu Bangladeshis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Bangladesh) in Assam who would become Indian citizens if the [Citizenship (Amendment) Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_\(Amendment\)_Act,_2019) is passed. [BJP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BJP), however claimed that only eight lakh Hindu Bangladeshis will get citizenship.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210] According to various sources, the total number of illegal Hindu Bangladeshis is hard to ascertain.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-FP-212] According to the [census data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_of_India), the number of Hindu immigrants have been largely exaggerated.[^cite_note-FP-212]

In February 2020, the Assam Minority Development Board announced plans to segregate illegal Bangladeshi Muslim immigrants from the indigenous Muslims of the state, though some have expressed problems in identifying an indigenous Muslim person. According to the board, there are 1.4 [crore](121420.md) Muslims in the state, of which 1 crore are of Bangladeshi origin.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215] A report reveals that out of total 33 districts in Assam, Bangladeshis dominate almost 15 districts of Assam.[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218]

### Floods

Main article: [Brahmaputra floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_floods)

In the rainy season every year, the Brahmaputra and other rivers overflow their banks and flood adjacent land. Flood waters wash away property including houses and livestock. Damage to crops and fields harms the agricultural sector. Bridges, railway tracks, and roads are also damaged, harming transportation and communication, and in some years requiring food to be air-dropped to isolated towns. Some deaths are attributed to the floods.[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220]

### Unemployment

[Unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment) is a chronic problem in Assam. It is variously blamed on poor [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure), limited connectivity, and government policy;[^cite_note-221] on a "poor work culture";[^cite_note-222] on failure to advertise vacancies;[^cite_note-223] and on government hiring candidates from outside Assam.[^cite_note-224]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Assam)



Per capita income of Assam since 1950

Assam's economy is based on agriculture and oil. Assam produces more than half of India's tea.[^cite_note-241] The Assam-Arakan basin holds about a quarter of the country's oil reserves, and produces about 12% of its total petroleum.[^cite_note-242] According to the recent estimates,[^cite_note-government2005-243] Assam's per capita GDP is [₹](490783.md)6,157 at constant prices (1993–94) and [₹](490783.md)10,198 at current prices; almost 40% lower than that in India.[^cite_note-244] According to the recent estimates,[^cite_note-government2005-243] per capita income in Assam has reached [₹](490783.md)6756 (1993–94 constant prices) in 2004–05, which is still much lower than India's.

### Macro-economy

Between 1951 and 1979, Assam registered an average level of [real gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_gross_domestic_product) growth compared to the national average.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAhmedBaruahBhuyan200622-245] However, the state's real GDP growth lagged behind the national average after 1979 due to socio-political instability.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAhmedBaruahBhuyan200619-246] Between 1981 and 2001, Assam's GSDP increased 3.3 per cent annually compared to the national average of 6 per cent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAhmedBaruahBhuyan200622-245] Since the 2000s, Assam has registered higher levels of real gross domestic product growth. [GSDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSDP) at [constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_gross_domestic_product) (1999-2000) prices increased 5.33 per cent annually between 2002 and 2007.[^cite_note-The-Hindu-247][^cite_note-The-Telegraph-248] Real GSDP increased 6.78 per cent annually between 2007 and 2012.[^cite_note-The-Telegraph-248] Real GSDP at constant (2011-12) prices increased approximately 7.6 per cent annually between 2012 and 2016.[^cite_note-Ministry-of-Statistics-and-Programme-Implementation-249] Real GSDP at constant (2011-12) prices increased approximately 7.0 per cent annually between 2016 and 2025.[^cite_note-Ministry-of-Statistics-and-Programme-Implementation-249]

### Employment

Unemployment is one of the major problems in Assam. This problem can be attributed to overpopulation and a faulty education system. Every year, large numbers of students obtain higher academic degrees but because of non-availability of proportional vacancies, most of these students remain unemployed.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251] A number of employers hire over-qualified or efficient, but under-certified, candidates, or candidates with narrowly defined qualifications. The problem is exacerbated by the growth in the number of technical institutes in Assam which increases the unemployed community of the State. The reluctance on the part of the departments concerned to advertise vacancies in vernacular language has also made matters worse for local unemployed youths particularly for the job-seekers of Grade C and D vacancies.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253]

Reduction of the unemployed has been threatened by [illegal immigration](7124412.md) from Bangladesh. This has increased the workforce without a commensurate increase in jobs. Immigrants compete with local workers for jobs at lower wages, particularly in construction, domestics, Rickshaw-pullers, and vegetable sellers.[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255] The government has been identifying (via [NRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Citizens_for_Assam)) and deporting illegal immigrants. Continued immigration is exceeding deportation.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257]

### Agriculture



Assamese women busy planting paddy seedlings in their agricultural field in Pahukata village in the Nagaon district of Assam



A paddy field in Assam

In Assam among all the productive sectors, agriculture makes the highest contribution to its domestic sectors, accounting for more than a third of Assam's income and employs 69% of workforce.[^cite_note-258] Assam's biggest contribution to the world is [Assam tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_tea). It has its own variety, *Camellia sinensis* var. *assamica*. The state produces rice, [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed), [mustard seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_seed), [jute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jute), potato, sweet potato, banana, [papaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya), [areca nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areca_nut), sugarcane and [turmeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmeric).

Assam's agriculture is yet to experience modernisation in a real sense. With implications for food security, per capita food grain production has declined in the past five decades.[^cite_note-AHDR2003III-259] Productivity has increased marginally, but is still low compared to highly productive regions. For instance, the yield of rice (a staple food of Assam) was just 1531 kg per hectare against India's 1927 kg per hectare in 2000–01[^cite_note-AHDR2003III-259] (which itself is much lower than [Egypt](8087628.md)'s 9283, US's 7279, South Korea's 6838, Japan's 6635 and China's 6131 kg per hectare in 2001[^cite_note-260]). On the other hand, after having strong domestic demand, and with 1.5 million hectares of inland water bodies, numerous rivers and 165 varieties of fishes,[^cite_note-261] fishing is still in its traditional form and production is not self-sufficient.[^cite_note-AHDR2003IV-262]

&#x20;Floods in Assam greatly affect the farmers and the families dependent on agriculture because of large-scale damage of agricultural fields and crops by flood water.[^cite_note-Assam_Flood_Toll_Rises_to_13-74][^cite_note-Flood_situation_in_Assam_worsens-75] Every year, flooding from the Brahmaputra and other rivers deluges places in Assam. The water levels of the rivers rise because of rainfall resulting in the rivers overflowing their banks and engulfing nearby areas. Apart from houses and livestock being washed away by flood water, bridges, railway tracks and roads are also damaged by the calamity, which causes communication breakdown in many places. Fatalities are also caused by the natural disaster in many places of the state.[^cite_note-Assam_Flood_Toll_Rises_to_132-263][^cite_note-Flood_situation_in_Assam_worsens2-264]

See also: [2016 Assam floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Assam_floods)

### Infrastructure

On 30 August 2023, [Maligaon Flyover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maligaon_Flyover) was inaugurated. The flyover is Assam's longest flyover, spanning 2.63 kilometres and connecting Maligaon Chariali to Kamakhya Gate in Guwahati.[^cite_note-265]

### Industry

Handlooms and handicrafts are traditional industries that continue to survive, especially among rural women, in the state.[^cite_note-266]

Assam's proximity to some neighbouring countries such as Bangladesh, Nepal and Bhutan, benefits its trade. The major [Border checkpoints](288329.md) through which border trade flows to Bangladesh from Assam are : Sutarkandi (Karimganj), Dhubri, Mankachar (Dhubri) and Golokanj. To facilitate border trade with Bangladesh, Border Trade Centres have been developed at Sutarkandi and [Mankachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankachar). It has been proposed in the 11th five-year plan to set up two more Border Trade Center, one at [Ledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ledo,_Assam) connecting China and other at [Darrang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darrang) connecting Bhutan. There are several Land Custom Stations (LCS) in the state bordering Bangladesh and Bhutan to facilitate border trade.[^cite_note-267]

The government of India has identified some thrust areas for industrial development of Assam:[^cite_note-268]

- Petroleum and natural gas-based industries

- Industries based on locally available minerals

- Processing of plantation crops

- Food processing industries

- Agri-Horticulture products

- Herbal products

- Biotech products

- Pharmaceuticals

- Chemical and plastic-based industries

- Export oriented industries

- Electronic and IT base industries including services sector

- Paper making industries

- Textiles and sericulture

- Engineering industries

- Cane and bamboo-based industries

- Other handicrafts industry

Although, the region in the eastern periphery of India is landlocked and is linked to the mainland by the narrow [Siliguri Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siliguri_Corridor) (or the Chicken's Neck) improved transport infrastructure in all the three modes – rail, road and air – and developing urban infrastructure in the cities and towns of Assam are giving a boost to the entire industrial scene. The [Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokpriya_Gopinath_Bordoloi_International_Airport) at Guwahati, with international flights to [Bangkok](56615.md) and [Singapore](27318.md) offered by [Druk Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druk_Air) of [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), was the 12th busiest airport of India in 2012.[^cite_note-269] The cities of Guwahati[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271] in the west and [Dibrugarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibrugarh)[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273] in the east with good rail,[^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275] road and air connectivity are the two important nerve centres of Assam, to be selected by Asian Development Bank for providing \$200 million for improvement of urban infrastructure.[^cite_note-Asian_Development_Bank-276][^cite_note-277]

Assam is a producer of [crude oil](23195.md) and it accounts for about 15% of India's crude output,[^cite_note-278] exploited by the Assam Oil Company Ltd.,[^cite_note-279] and natural gas in India. Oil was discovered in [Makum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makum) in 1867, and [oil drilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_well) started in 1889.[^cite_note-Oil-88] Most of the oilfields are located in the Eastern Assam region. Assam has four oil refineries in [Digboi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digboi) (Asia's first and world's second refinery), Guwahati, [Bongaigaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongaigaon) and [Numaligarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numaligarh) and with a total capacity of 7 million metric tonnes (7.7 million [short tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_ton)) per annum. Asia's first refinery was set up at Digboi and discoverer of Digboi oilfield was the Assam Railways & Trading Company Limited (AR\&T Co. Ltd.), a registered company of London in 1881.[^cite_note-280] One of the biggest public sector oil company of the country [Oil India Ltd.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_India_Ltd.) has its plant and headquarters at [Duliajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duliajan).

There are several other industries, including a chemical [fertiliser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertiliser) plant at [Namrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namrup), [petrochemical](67728.md) industries in Namrup and Bongaigaon, paper mills at [Jagiroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiroad), [Hindustan Paper Corporation Ltd. Township Area Panchgram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Paper_Corporation_Ltd._Township_Area_Panchgram) and [Jogighopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogighopa), sugar mills in Barua Bamun Gaon, Chargola, Kampur, cement plants in Bokajan and [Badarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badarpur,_Assam), and a cosmetics plant of [Hindustan Unilever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Unilever) (HUL) at [Doom Dooma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_Dooma). Moreover, there are other industries such as jute mill, textile and yarn mills, [Assam silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_silk), and silk mills. Many of these industries are facing losses and closure due to lack of infrastructure and improper management practices.[^cite_note-281]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Assam)

See also: [Assamese cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_cinema)



Moran [bihu dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihu_dance) in the traditional attire of the [Moran people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moran_people) on the occasion of [Bohag Bihu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohag_Bihu)

[Assamese Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_Culture) is described as [hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybridity) and [syncretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretic) in nature due to the assimilation of numerous ethnic groups and cultural practices of [Austroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic), [Tibeto-Burman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman), [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples) and [Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_peoples) inhabitants. Therefore, both local elements or the local elements in Sanskritised forms are distinctly found.[^cite_note-BKakatiAIFD-282] The major milestones in the evolution of Assamese culture are:



Dakhinpat Satra of [Majuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majuli)

- Assimilation in the [Kamarupa kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamarupa) for almost 800 years ([Varman dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varman_dynasty) for 300 years, [Mlechchha dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlechchha_dynasty) for 250 years and the [Pala dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_dynasty_\(Kamarupa\)) for 200 years.

  [^cite_note-HKBarpujariCHOA-24]

- Establishment of the [Chutia kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_kingdom) in the 12th century in eastern Assam and assimilation for next 400 years.

  [^cite_note-HKBarpujariCHOA-24]

- Establishment of the [Ahom kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahom_kingdom) in the 13th century CE and assimilation for next 600 years.

  [^cite_note-HKBarpujariCHOA-24]

- Assimilation in the [Koch kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_kingdom) (15th–16th century CE) of western Assam and Kachari kingdom (12th–18th century CE) of central and southern Assam.

  [^cite_note-HKBarpujariCHOA-24]

- [Neo-Vaishanavite (Ekasarana Dharma)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekasarana_Dharma) Movement led by [Srimanta Shankardeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srimanta_Shankardeva) (*Xongkordeu*) made an enormous impact on the socio-cultural and religious sphere of Assam. This 15th century religio-cultural movement under the leadership of [Srimanta Sankardeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srimanta_Sankardeva) (Xonkordeu) and his disciples have provided another dimension to Assamese culture. A renewed Hinduisation in local forms took place, which was initially greatly supported by the Koch and later by the Ahom kingdoms. The resultant social institutions such as *[namghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namghar)* and *[sattra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sattra)* (the Vaishnav Monasteries) have become an integral part of the Assamese way of life. The movement contributed greatly towards language, literature, and performing and fine arts.. It was also an egalitarian reform movement as it broke away with the old caste barriers of Brahmanical Hinduism and converted into its fold people of all castes, ethnicity and religions (including [Islam](6037917.md)).



Presenting *[Gayan Bayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gayan_Bayan)* in [Majuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majuli), the Neo-Vaishnavite cultural heritage of Assam

The modern culture has been influenced by events in the British and the post-British era. [Assamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_language) language was standardised by American [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist) [Missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) such as [Nathan Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Brown_\(missionary\)), [Dr. Miles Bronson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Bronson_and_the_Noctes) and local pundits such as [Hemchandra Barua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemchandra_Barua) with the dialect spoken in undivided [Sibsagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibsagar) district (the centre of the Ahom kingdom) forming the standardised dialect.

Increasing efforts of standardisation in the 20th century alienated the localised forms present in different areas and with the less-assimilated [ethno-cultural groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups) (many source-cultures). However, Assamese culture in its hybrid form and nature is one of the richest, still developing and in true sense is a '[cultural system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_system)' with sub-systems. Many source-cultures of the Assamese cultural-system are still surviving either as sub-systems or as sister entities, e.g. the; [Bodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo_people) or [Karbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbi_people) or [Mishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_people). It is important to keep the broader system closer to its roots and at the same time focus on development of the sub-systems.

Some of the common and unique cultural traits in the region are peoples' respect towards [areca-nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betel_nut) and [betel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betel) leaves, symbolic ([gamosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamosa), arnai, etc.), [traditional silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_silk) garments (e.g. [mekhela chador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekhela_chador), traditional dress of Assamese women) and towards forefathers and elderly. Moreover, great hospitality and bamboo culture are common.

### Symbols

See also: [List of Assam state symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Assam_state_symbols) and [Jaapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaapi)



A [Mising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_people) woman using a handloom to weave a traditional [Mekhela chador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekhela_chador) dress.



A decorative Assamese *[Jaapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaapi)* laid over a Gamosa

Symbolism is an ancient cultural practice in Assam and is still a very important part of the Assamese way of life. Various elements are used to represent beliefs, feelings, pride, identity, etc.

**Tamulpan** (areca nut and betel leaves), **[Xorai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xorai)** and **[Gamosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamosa_of_Assam)** are three important symbolic elements in Assamese culture. *Tamulpan* or *guapan* (gua from *kwa*) are considered along with the Gamosa (a typical woven cotton or silk cloth with embroidery) as the offers of devotion, respect and friendship. The Tamulpan-tradition is an ancient one and is being followed since time-immemorial with roots in the aboriginal Austric culture. *[Xorai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xorai)* is a traditionally manufactured bell-metal article of great respect and is used as a container-medium while performing respectful offers. Moreover, symbolically many ethno-cultural groups use specific clothes to portray respect and pride.



Singhasan of manikut in a [Namghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namghar)

&#x20;There were many other symbolic elements and designs, but are now only found in literature, art, sculpture, architecture, etc. or in use today for only religious purposes. The typical designs of *Assamese-lion*, *dragon* (ngi-ngao-kham), and *flying-lion* (Naam-singho) are used for symbolising various purposes and occasions. The archaeological sites such as the [Madan Kamdev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madan_Kamdev) (c. 9th–10th centuries CE) exhibits mass-scale use of lions, dragon-lions and many other figures of demons to show case power and prosperity.



Jhumura

&#x20;The Vaishnavite monasteries ([Sattras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satra_\(Ekasarana_Dharma\))) and many other architectural sites of the late medieval period display the use of lions and dragons for symbolic effects.

See also: [Domahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domahi), [Bohag Bihu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohag_Bihu), [Magh Bihu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magh_Bihu), and [Bwisagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bwisagu)

There are diversified important traditional festivals in Assam. Bihu is the most important festival of Assam and is celebrated all over the state.The Assamese new year (Ek Bohag) is celebrated in April of the Gregorian calendar.



Husori in Bihu

**[Bihu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihu)** is described as the soul and life of Assam. It is a series of three prominent festivals each associated with a certain stage during the cultivation of paddy. Primarily a secular festival celebrated to mark the seasons and the significant points of a cultivator's life over yearly cycle. Three Bihus, *rongali* (in the month of *bohag*), celebrated with the coming of spring and the beginning of the sowing season; *kongali* or *kati*, the barren bihu when the fields are lush but the barns are empty and *[bhogali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhogali_Bihu)* (in the month of *magh*), the thanksgiving when the crops have been harvested and the granaries are full. Bihu songs and [Bihu dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihu_dance) are associated with *rongali* and *bhogali* bihu. The day before the each bihu is known as the day of **Uruka**. The first day of 'Rongali bihu' is called 'Goru bihu' (the bihu of the cows), when the cows are taken to the nearby rivers or ponds to be bathed with special care. In recent times the form and nature of celebration has changed with the growth of urban centres.



Traditional Bwisagu dance

**[Bwisagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bwisagu)** is one of the most popular seasonal festivals of the [Bodos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo_people). **Baisagu** is a Boro word which originated from the word "Baisa" which means year or age, and "Agu" meaning starting or beginning. Bwisagu marks the beginning of the new year. It is celebrated at the beginning of the first month of the Boro year, around mid-April in the Gregorian Calendar. It has remarkable similarities to the festival of Rongali Bihu, also celebrated at the same time in Assam. The worship of Bathow is done on the second day of the festival.



[Mising girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_people) dancing during [Ali Ai Ligang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Ai_Ligang) (Spring Festival) to the tunes of [Oi\:Nitom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oi_Nitom)

**[Ali-Aye-Ligang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Ai_Ligang)** or Ali-Ai-Ligang is a spring festivital associated with agriculture celebrated by the indigenous [Mising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_people) of Assam and other Northeast Indian states. It marks the beginning of the Ahu paddy cultivation in the farms. The term "Ali" denotes legumes, "Aye" means seed and "Ligang" is 'to sow'. The festival is celebrated on a Wednesday of the month of Fagun of the Assamese calendar and in the month of February in English calendar. The *gumrag* dance is associated with this festival.



[Dimasa women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimasa_people) performing Baidima, the traditional dance of the Dimasa people.

**[Bushu Dima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimasa_people#Festivals)** or simply Bushu is a major harvest festival of the [Dimasa people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimasa_people). This festival is celebrated during the end of January. Officially 27 January has been declared as the day of Bushu Dima festival. The Dimasa people celebrate by playing musical instruments- khram (a type of drum), muri (a kind of huge long flute). The people dance to the different tunes of "murithai" and each dance has its own unique name, the most prominent being the "Baidima" There are three types of Bushu celebrated by the Dimasas- Jidap, Surem and Hangsou.

**[Me-Dam-Me-Phi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me-Dam-Me-Phi)** is the day of the [veneration of the dead ancestors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration_of_the_dead#Assam) for the [Tai-Ahom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai-Ahom) community. It bears striking similarity in the concept of ancestor worship that the Tai-Ahoms share with other peoples originating from the [Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_peoples) stock. The word ‘Me’ means offerings, ‘Dam’ means ancestors and ‘Phi’ means gods. According to the Buranjis, Lengdon (God of thunder), the king of Mong Phi ([The heavenly kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian)), sent two of his grandsons Khunlung and Khunlai to Mong Ri Mong Ram (present day [Xishuangbanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xishuangbanna), China) and at that moment Ye-Cheng-Pha, the God of knowledge, advised them to perform Umpha, Phuralong, Mae Dam Mae Phi and Rik-khwan rituals in different months of the year on different occasions to pay respect to the [Phi-Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_folk_religion) (Ancestral Spirit) and Khwan elements. Since that day till now Mae Dam Mae Phi has been observed by the Tai-Ahoms. It is celebrated on 31 January every year according to the Gregorian calendar.



[Rongker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongker) is celebrated every year by the [Karbi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbi_people)

**[Rongker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongker)** also called **[Dehal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongker)** is an annual winter festival of merriment celebrated by the [Karbi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbi_people) of Assam. It is observed in order to appease the local deities associated with the welfare of the village and the harvest of crops and also to get rid of all evil spirits. Although the festival does have a specific time it is usually observed at the beginning of the Karbi New year (Thang thang) which falls on February of the Gregorian calendar.



Doul Mohutsav (Holi) in [Barpeta Satra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barpeta_Satra)

**Doul Mohutsav**, also called **Fakuwa** or Doul Utsav is a festival of colours and happiness popular in [Lower Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Assam) and especially in [Barpeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barpeta_district). It is synonymous with the festival of [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi) celebrated in Northern India. Holigeets of Barpeta are sung which is incredibly popular and enthralls the heart of every Assamese. These holigeets are the exquisite compositions in praise of [Lord Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Krishna). People from different parts of the state visit Barpeta Satra to experience this colourful and joyful festival.

**Chavang Kut** is a post harvesting festival of the [Kuki people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_people). The festival is celebrated on the first day of November every year. Hence, this particular day has been officially declared as a Restricted Holiday by the Assam government. In the past, the celebration was primarily important in the religio-cultural sense. The rhythmic movements of the dances in the festival were inspired by animals, agricultural techniques and showed their relationship with ecology. Today, the celebration witnesses the shifting of stages and is revamped to suit new contexts and interpretations. The traditional dances which form the core of the festival is now performed in out-of-village settings and are staged in a secular public sphere. In Assam, the Kukis mainly reside in the two autonomous districts of Dima Hasao and Karbi Anglong.

**Beshoma** is a festival of [Deshi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deshi_people) (one of the indigenous [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) groups of Assam).[^cite_note-283] It is a celebration of sowing crop. The Beshoma starts on the last day of Chaitra and goes on until the sixth of Baisakh. With varying locations it is also called *Bishma* or *Chait-Boishne.*[^cite_note-284]

Among the new generation, [Zubeen Garg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zubeen_Garg), [Angaraag Mahanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angaraag_Mahanta) and [Joi Barua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joi_Barua). There is an award given in the honour of [Bishnu Prasad Rabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnu_Prasad_Rabha) for achievements in the cultural/music world of Assam by the state government.

Contemporary Assamese music also saw the rise of [Zubeen Garg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zubeen_Garg), one of the most popular modern singers of the region. His Assamese song "Mayabini Raatir Bukut" has achieved iconic status and is widely regarded as an anthem in Assam.[^cite_note-286]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Assamese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_cuisine)



Assamese Thali

Typically, an Assamese meal consists of many things such as *[bhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhat_\(food\))* ([rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice)) with *dayl/ daly* ([lentils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lentils)), *masor jool* ([fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) stew), *mangxô* ([meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat) [stew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stew)) and stir fried [greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf_vegetable) or [herbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbs) and [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetables).[^cite_note-287]



[Bhut jolokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhut_jolokia) (ghost pepper) is used in preparation of traditional meat stews

The two main characteristics of a traditional meal in Assam are *[khar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_cuisine#Khar)* (an Alkali, named after its main ingredient) and *tenga* (preparations bearing a characteristically [rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavoring) and [tangy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pungent) flavour). *Khorika* is the smoked or fire grilled meat eaten with meals. Pitika (mash) is another delicacy of Assam. It includes alu pitika (mashed potatoes), bilahi (tomatoes), bengena (brinjals) or even masor pitika (fish). Commonly consumed varieties of [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat) include [Mutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat_meat), [fowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fowl), [duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck)/[goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose), [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish), [pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeon), [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork) and [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef) (among [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) and [Christian](5211.md) indigenous Assamese ethnic groups). [Grasshoppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasshoppers), [locusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locusts), [silkworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silkworms), [snails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snails), [eels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eels), [bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat) wild [fowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fowl), [squab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squab) and other birds, and [venison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venison) are also eaten, albeit in moderation.



A [Mising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_people) girl roasting fish and pork in bamboo tubes (*bahor sungha*) in an ethnic festival

*Khorisa* (fermented [bamboo shoots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_shoots)) are used at times to flavour curries while they can also be preserved and made into pickles. *Koldil* ([banana flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_flower)) and [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(plant\)) are also used in popular culinary preparations.[^cite_note-288]



[Rice beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apo_\(drink\)) is consumed on festivals by the various indigenous ethnic communities of Assam

A variety of different rice cultivars are grown and consumed in different ways, viz., roasted, ground, boiled or just soaked.

Fish curries made of [free range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_range) [wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife) [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) as well as *Bôralí*, *rôu*, *illish*, or *sitôl* are the most popular.

Another favourite combination is *[luchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luchi)* (fried flatbread), a curry which can be vegetarian or non-vegetarian.

Many indigenous Assamese communities households still continue to brew their traditional alcoholic [beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverages); examples include: Laupani, Xaaj, Paniyo, Jou, Joumai, Hor, [Apong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apo_\(drink\)), Sujen etc. Such beverages are served during traditional festivities. Declining them is considered socially offensive.

Assamese food is generally served in traditional [bell metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_metal) dishes and platters like *Kanhi*, *[Maihang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maihang)* and so on.

### Literature

Main article: [Assamese literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_literature)



[Lakshminath Bezbaroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshminath_Bezbaroa), one of the foremost figures of [Assamese literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_literature)



Imaginary portrait of [Srimanta Sankardeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankardev) by [Bishnu Prasad Rabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnu_Prasad_Rabha)

[^cite_note-289]

Assamese literature dates back to the composition of [Charyapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charyapada), and later on works like [Saptakanda Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saptakanda_Ramayana) by [Madhava Kandali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhava_Kandali), which is the first translation of the [Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana) into an [Indo-Aryan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages), contributed to Assamese literature.[^cite_note-290][^cite_note-291][^cite_note-292] [Sankardeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankardev)'s [Borgeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borgeet), [Ankia Naat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankia_Naat), [Bhaona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaona) and [Satra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satra_\(Ekasarana_Dharma\)) tradition backed the 15th-16th century Assamese literature.[^cite_note-293][^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295][^cite_note-296] Written during the Reign of [Ahoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahoms), the [Buranjis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buranji) are notable literary works which are prominently historical manuscripts.[^cite_note-297] Most literary works are written in Assamese although other local language such as [Bodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo_language) and [Dimasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimasa_language) are also represented. In the 19th and 20th century, Assamese and other literature was modernised by authors including [Lakshminath Bezbaroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshminath_Bezbaroa), [Birinchi Kumar Barua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birinchi_Kumar_Barua), [Hem Barua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hem_Barua), [Dr. Mamoni Raisom Goswami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamoni_Raisom_Goswami), [Bhabendra Nath Saikia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhabendra_Nath_Saikia), [Birendra Kumar Bhattacharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birendra_Kumar_Bhattacharya), [Hiren Bhattacharyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiren_Bhattacharyya), [Homen Borgohain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homen_Borgohain), [Bhabananda Deka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhabananda_Deka), [Rebati Mohan Dutta Choudhury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebati_Mohan_Dutta_Choudhury), [Mahim Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahim_Bora), [Lil Bahadur Chettri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lil_Bahadur_Chettri), [Syed Abdul Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Abdul_Malik), [Surendranath Medhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surendranath_Medhi), [Hiren Gohain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiren_Gohain) etc.

### Fine arts

Main article: [Painting of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting_of_Assam)

The archaic [Mauryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Empire) [Stupas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupas) discovered in and around [Goalpara district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goalpara_district) are the earliest examples (c. 300 BCE to c. 100 CE) of ancient art and architectural works. The remains discovered in Daparvatiya (*Doporboteeya*) archaeological site with a beautiful doorframe in Tezpur are identified as the best examples of artwork in ancient Assam with influence of [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath) School of Art of the late [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_empire) period.

Painting is an ancient tradition of Assam. [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang) (7th century CE) mentions that among the Kamarupa king Bhaskaravarma's gifts to [Harshavardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harshavardhana) there were paintings and painted objects, some of which were on Assamese silk. Many of the manuscripts such as [Hastividyarnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastividyarnava) (A Treatise on Elephants), the *Chitra Bhagawata* and in the Gita Govinda from the Middle Ages bear excellent examples of traditional paintings.

### Traditional crafts

Main articles: [Traditional crafts of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_crafts_of_Assam) and [Bell and brass metal crafts of Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_and_brass_metal_crafts_of_Assam)

See also: [Assam silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_silk)

Assam has a rich tradition of [crafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafts), [Cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caning_\(furniture\)) and bamboo craft, bell metal and [brass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass_instrument) craft, silk and cotton [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving), toy and [mask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mask) making, [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery) and [terracotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta) work, wood craft, [jewellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewellery) making, and musical instruments making have remained as major traditions.[^cite_note-298]

Cane and bamboo craft provide the most commonly used utilities in daily life, ranging from household utilities, weaving accessories, fishing accessories, furniture, musical instruments, construction materials, etc. Utilities and symbolic articles such as *Sorai* and *Bota* made from bell metal and brass are found in every Assamese household.[^cite_note-299][^cite_note-300] [Hajo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajo) and [Sarthebari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarthebari) (*Sorthebaary*) are the most important centres of traditional bell-metal and brass crafts. Assam is the home of several types of silks, the most prestigious are: Muga – the natural golden silk, Pat – a creamy-bright-silver coloured silk and Eri – a variety used for manufacturing warm clothes for winter. Apart from [Sualkuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sualkuchi) (*Xualkuchi*), the centre for the traditional silk industry, in almost every parts of the Brahmaputra Valley, rural households produce silk and silk garments with excellent embroidery designs. Moreover, various ethno-cultural groups in Assam make different types of cotton garments with unique embroidery designs and wonderful colour combinations.

Moreover, Assam possesses unique crafts of toy and mask making mostly concentrated in the Vaishnav Monasteries, pottery and [terracotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta) work in western Assam districts and wood craft, iron craft, jewellery, etc. in many places across the region.

## Media

Print media include Assamese dailies *[Amar Asom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amar_Asom)*, *[Asomiya Khabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asomiya_Khabar)*, *[Asomiya Pratidin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asomiya_Pratidin)*, *[Dainik Agradoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainik_Agradoot)*, *[Dainik Janambhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainik_Janambhumi)*, *[Dainik Asam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainik_Asam)*, *[Gana Adhikar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gana_Adhikar)*, *[Janasadharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janasadharan)* and *[Niyomiya Barta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niyomiya_Barta)*. *Asom Bani*, *Sadin* and *Janambhumi* are Assamese weekly newspapers. The English dailies of Assam include *[The Assam Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Assam_Tribune)*, *[The Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sentinel_\(Guwahati\))*, *[The Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Telegraph_\(Calcutta\))*, *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India)*, *[The North East Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_North_East_Times)*, *Eastern Chronicle* and *The Hills Times*. *[Thekar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thekar)*, in the Karbi language has the largest circulation of any daily from Karbi Anglong district. *Bodosa* has the highest circulation of any Bodo daily from BTR. *[Dainik Jugasankha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainik_Jugasankha)* is a Bengali daily with editions from Dibrugarh, Guwahati, Silchar and Kolkata. *Dainik Samayik Prasanga*, *Dainik Prantojyoti*, *Dainik Janakantha* and *Nababarta Prasanga* are other prominent Bengali dailies published in the Barak Valley towns of Karimganj and Silchar. Hindi dailies include *Purvanchal Prahari*, *Pratah Khabar* and *Dainik Purvoday*.

Broadcasting stations of [All India Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio) have been established in 22 cities across the state. Local news and music are the main priority for those stations. Assam has three public service broadcasting service stations of state-owned [Doordarshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DD_Assam) at Dibrugarh, Guwahati and Silchar. The Guwahati-based satellite news channels include [Assam Talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Talks), [DY 365](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DY_365), [News Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Live_\(Indian_TV_channel\)), [News18 Assam North East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News18_Assam_North_East), [North East Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_Live), [Prag News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prag_News) and [Pratidin Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratidin_Time).

## Geographical indications

### Boka Chaul

[Boka Chaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boka_Chaul) was awarded the [Geographical Indication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_geographical_indications_in_India) (GI) status tag from the [Geographical Indications Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_Indication_Registry_\(India\)), under the [Union Government of India](553883.md), on 30 July 2018 and is valid until 24 July 2026.[^cite_note-\:0-301][^cite_note-302]

Lotus Progressive Centre (LPC) and Centre for Environment Education (CEE) from Guwahati, proposed the GI registration of Boka Chaul.[^cite_note-303] After filing the application in July 2018, the rice was granted the GI tag in 2023 by the Geographical Indication Registry in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), making the name "Boka Chaul" exclusive to the rice grown in the region. It thus became the second rice variety from Assam after [Joha rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joha_rice) and the 7th type of goods from Assam to earn the GI tag.[^cite_note-304]

The GI tag protects the rice from illegal selling and marketing, and gives it legal protection and a unique identity.
