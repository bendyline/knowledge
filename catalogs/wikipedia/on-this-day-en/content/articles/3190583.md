The **Japan women's national football team** ([Japanese](15606.md): サッカー日本女子代表, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Sakkā Nippon Joshi Daihyō*), commonly known as **[Nadeshiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_nadeshiko) Japan** (なでしこジャパン),[^cite_note-3] represents [Japan](15573.md) in [women's association football](191466.md) and is run by the [Japan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Football_Association) (JFA). One of the two countries to win every [FIFA](11049.md) competition and the most successful women's national team in the Asian Football Confederation, its highest ranking in the [FIFA Women's World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Rankings) is 3rd, achieved in December 2011.[^cite_note-4]

Japan participated at every [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) in history, reaching the final twice; they defeated the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_soccer_team) in the [2011 FIFA Women's World Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_Final), claiming their first World Cup title, and becoming the first Asian team to do so and only the fourth women's world champions.[^cite_note-5] It won silver medals at the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics) and the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup), making it the only Asian team to have three combined medals from international championships.[^cite_note-6] Japan were the [Asian Cup champions](2428648.md) three times, in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup) and [2026](67991141.md). They also won gold medals at the [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Asian_Games) in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2010_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2018_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament), [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament), and [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2026_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament), and at the [EAFF Football Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EAFF_E-1_Football_Championship_\(women\)) in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_EAFF_Women's_Football_Championship), [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_EAFF_Women's_Football_Championship), [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_EAFF_E-1_Football_Championship_\(women\)) and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_EAFF_E-1_Football_Championship_\(women\)).

## History

### 1970s and 1980s

During the 1970s, the number of women football players and teams increased in Japan, and teams made up regional leagues in various parts of Japan. In 1977, a team representing Japan participated in its first international tournament, the [1977 AFC Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_AFC_Women's_Championship). But this team was not actually a national team, since the [Japan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Football_Association) dispatched a club team, [FC Jinnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan_FC_Ladies) as its representative.[^cite_note-jw-7][^cite_note-wfa-8] In 1980, "[All-Japan Women's Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Japan_Women's_Football_Championship)" was held. In 1981, the Japan Football Association formed the first national team for the [1981 AFC Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_AFC_Women's_Championship)[^cite_note-9] and [Seiki Ichihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiki_Ichihara) managed as the first Japan national team manager.[^cite_note-JFA-2] The first match against [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_women's_national_football_team) on 7 June at this tournament was the first match for a Japan national team.[^cite_note-JFA-2] In 1984, a national team was gathered for the first time in three years for a China expedition, with [Takao Orii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takao_Orii) managing the squad.[^cite_note-JFA-2]

In January 1986, [Ryohei Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryohei_Suzuki_\(footballer\)) became the first full-time manager for the national team. In December, Japan won 2nd place at the [1986 AFC Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_AFC_Women's_Championship). In 1989, the "[Japan Women's Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._League)" (abbreviated to "L. League") was established, and the women's national team qualified for the "[1991 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup)" in China.

### Verge of decline (1990s)

Japan women's national football team attended various championship tournaments such as the [1996 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Summer_Olympics) and the [1995 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) which had made the national team and the L.League very popular. However, in 1999, Japan failed to qualify for the [2000 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics), and along with economic stagnation ([Lost Decade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Decade_\(Japan\))) this helped cause the withdrawal of a series of teams from the L. League. Japanese women's football was on the verge of decline.

### Regeneration (2002–2008)

In August 2002, the [Japan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Football_Association) appointed [Eiji Ueda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiji_Ueda), who had been coach for the [Macau national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau_national_football_team), as the new head coach. Officials expected a revitalization of women's football and planned a team reorganization, aiming for the [2004 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics). The team at first went through a losing streak, but Ueda gradually improved the team, and it eventually gained wide support in Japan. In particular, a game against Korea DPR, which decided who would participate in the 2004 Olympics, not only made fans rush to the National Stadium but also was widely watched on TV.

Following the increase in public interest in women's football in Japan, the JFA organized a public contest to select a nickname for the team. "Nadeshiko Japan" was chosen from among about 2,700 entries and was announced on 7 July 2004. "Nadeshiko", a kind of [dianthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianthus), comes from the phrase "[Yamato Nadeshiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_Nadeshiko)" (大和撫子, "ideal Japanese woman").

#### 2003 and 2007 World Cup

Japan faced [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_women's_national_soccer_team) and [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_women's_national_football_team) for the [2003 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup). Beginning with a 6–0 thrashing of newcomer Argentina, Japan subsequently fell 0–3 to the eventual champions Germany, followed by a loss of 1–3 to Canada, the team that ultimately finished 4th.

In the [2007 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) held in China, they again faced Germany and Argentina, with [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_women's_national_football_team) making up the group. They started with a 2–2 draw against England, before beating Argentina 1–0. A 0–2 defeat to reigning champion Germany again eliminated Japan from the group stage.

#### 2008 Summer Olympics

After qualifying from the preliminary round, Nadeshiko faced the United States, Norway and New Zealand in Group C of the Women's Olympic Football Tournament in Beijing.

In the first match, they drew 2-2 with the Football Ferns of New Zealand and then lost 1-0 to the Americans in the second match. However, as the two best third-placed teams progressed, Japan secured qualification for the quarter-finals with a 5-1 win over Norway in the final match of the group phase.

In the quarter-finals, the Japanese eliminated the host China with a 2-0 victory thanks to goals from [Homare Sawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homare_Sawa) and [Yuki Nagasato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Nagasato). It was the first time that Japan had advanced beyond the quarter-finals in any international women's football competition.

At the semifinals, Japan met the United States again, but just like in the group stage, the Americans came out on top, winning 4-2. In the bronze medal match, Nadeshiko ended up being defeated by Germany 2-0.

#### 2010 AFC Women's Asian Cup

Like the 2008 Women's Olympic Football Tournament, the 2010 AFC Women's Asian Cup was also held in China. Japan was drawn in Group A with North Korea, Thailand and Myanmar.

After two thrashings against Myanmar (7-0) and Thailand (4-0), Nadeshiko finished the group stage as leaders, beating North Korea 2-1. In the semi-finals, however, they were defeated by Australia thanks to a goal by [Kate Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Gill) before the end of the first half. The Matildas would go on to win the tournament.

In the third-place play-off, Japan defeated hosts China 2-0 and secured the last Asian spot in the 2011 FIFA Women's World Cup (Australia and North Korea also qualified as finalists in that tournament).

After finishing third in the [2010 AFC Women's Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup), Japan qualified for the FIFA Women's World Cup 2011 [finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) in Germany. There were certain doubts whether Nadeshiko would play in that tournament due to the [2011 Tohoku earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Tohoku_earthquake_and_tsunami) which happened a couple of months before, but the participation was confirmed at the last minute.

After finishing second in their group behind [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_women's_national_football_team), Japan shocked the world beating two-time defending champion and host nation [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_women's_national_football_team) 1–0 in the quarterfinals, before easily defeating [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_women's_national_football_team) 3–1 to reach the final.

After the final game finished 2–2 after extra time, Japan beat the United States 3–1 in a penalty shootout, becoming the first Asian team to win the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md), and the first Asian team to win a senior [FIFA](11049.md) title.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] It came right after [men's team](993546.md) won the [2011 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup), marked their most successful year in Japanese football.

#### 2012 Summer Olympics

Japan qualified for the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) by finishing first in the [Asian qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_Asian_Qualifiers) in September 2011, only 6 weeks after winning the Women's World Cup. At the Olympics, after finishing second in their group behind [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_women's_national_football_team), *Nadeshiko Japan* defeated [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_women's_national_football_team) 2–0 in the quarterfinals, followed by a 2–1 victory over [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_women's_national_football_team), whom Nadeshiko had lost to in a friendly match right before the Olympics, to reach the final.

In a rematch of the World Cup final, Japan was defeated in the Olympic final by a score of 1–2 against the United States, allowing two goals to [Carli Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carli_Lloyd) in the 8th and 54th minutes. [Yūki Ōgimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Nagasato) scored the lone goal for Japan.[^cite_note-14]

The [Japan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Football_Association) (JFA) was accused of [sexism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexism), after flying the men's Olympic team on [business class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_class) and the women's team on [economy class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_class).[^cite_note-15]

#### 2014 AFC Women's Asian Cup

Despite having won a [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) in 2011, Japan entered the [2014 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup) having never previously won the tournament. They were drawn with defending champions [Australia](30864460.md), host [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team) and newcomer [Jordan](1090065.md). Their first match in the group stage of the tournament resulted in a 2–2 draw against the Matildas.[^cite_note-16] Also in the group stage, Japan upset host Vietnam by a 4–0 win before defeating Jordan with a 7–0 win to finish first with a higher goal difference.

In the semi-final, Japan beat eight-time champions [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team) 2–1 after 120'. In the final, they met Australia once again and successfully earned a 1–0 win with [Azusa Iwashimizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azusa_Iwashimizu)'s goal. This marked the first time for Japan to become "Queen of Asia". They became the first Asian team to subsequently win both the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) and [AFC Women's Asian Cup](2428648.md). Because of their top placement in the tournament, Japan, Australia, China, [South Korea](1018627.md) and newcomer [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_national_football_team) secured their spot at the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) to be played in Canada the following year.[^cite_note-17]

#### 2015 World Cup



The national teams of Japan and the United States at the 2015 FIFA Women's World Cup

Japan, then fourth in the world, was drawn into Group C for the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup), with tournament debutants Ecuador, Switzerland, and Cameroon. Japan won all three games, securing passage into the Round of 16, where they drew yet another tournament debutant in the Netherlands. Saori Ariyoshi and Mizuho Sakaguchi scored goals for Japan, and they ultimately survived a couple of nervy moments to get into the quarterfinals.

In a all-Asian quarterfinal against Australia, Japan once again used their technical possession game to frustrate the Matildas and negate their speed. Mana Iwabuchi notched the only goal of the game three minutes from time to send Japan to the semifinals.

Against England in the semifinals, Nadeshiko Japan was able to survive against the Lionesses, as the two teams traded goals from the penalty spot (Aya Miyama for Japan, Fara Williams for England). Deadlocked from the 40th minute on, Japan got abreak as English centre back Laura Bassett, in trying to clear out a Japan cross, ended up scoring an own-goal at the death. This set up a rematch with the United States from the 2011 Women's World Cup.

Unfortunately for Japan, the Americans came out flying and scored four goals in the first 16 minutes of the match, with American midfielder [Carli Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carli_Lloyd) scoring a hat trick in the process. Yuki Ogimi brought Japan one back in the 27th minute, and an own goal from [Julie Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Johnston) halved the American lead, but [Tobin Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobin_Heath) put the final touch on the United States' third Women's World Cup victory.

### Asian domination and brief decline (2016–2022)

#### 2018 AFC Women's Asian Cup

Defending champions Japan won in 2014 and played in the continental competition held in Jordan in a group with then-runners-up Australia, South Korea and Vietnam. In Group C, Nadeshiko beat the Vietnamese 4-0 and drew the following two games against the South Koreans and the Australians. As Japan, Australia and South Korea each finished with 5 points, the tiebreaker was goal difference and the Japanese finished second behind Australia (the goal difference was also decisive for Japan to secure a direct spot in the 2019 FIFA Women's World Cup over South Korea).

In the semi-finals, Japan defeated again China by 3–1 with a goal from [Mana Iwabuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mana_Iwabuchi) and two goals from [Kumi Yokoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumi_Yokoyama). The final match in Amman was a repeat of the 2014 final between Japan and Australia, in which Nadeshiko defeated the Matildas again thanks to a goal scored by Yokoyama in the 84th minute. It was Japan's second continental title and their second title in a row.

#### 2019 World Cup

While Japan remains on a title and a runners-up spot at the last two editions of the World Cup as well as a runner-up spot at the [2012 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament), it failed to qualify for the [2016 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) while the [World Cup 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) sees the country of the Rising Sun being knocked out pretty quickly. *Nadeshiko Japan*, who no longer benefit from the surprise effect unlike previous tournaments, passed the first round without glory, with a narrow victory over [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_women's_national_football_team) (2–1) a goalless draw against [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_women's_national_football_team) (0–0) as well as a defeat against [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_women's_national_football_team) (0–2) in the last group match for first place in [group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_Group_D). The Japanese find the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_women's_national_football_team) at the same stage of the competition as 4 years earlier, but the confrontation this time turns to the advantage of the *Oranje* who take their revenge and eliminated Japan by the same score by which they had lost in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) (1–2).

#### Olympic Games in 2021 at home

Japan is encountering the same difficulties during the [2020 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) organized at home and postponed by a year due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). In front of their public, Japan passed the first round with difficulty, with a victory snatched in extremis on the edge of the last quarter of an hour of the game on the last day against [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_women's_national_football_team) (1–0) to finish among the 2 best 3rd in the group, having had a few scares with a header from the Chilean [Francisca Lara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisca_Lara) having rebounded on the goal line without crossing it just after hitting the bar,[^cite_note-18] and this shortly before the saving Japanese goal. Japan, which had in the meantime struggled against the other top names in the group (1–1 draw against [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_women's_national_soccer_team) after being behind and missing a penalty, 0–1 defeat against [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain_women's_Olympic_football_team)), confirms its difficulties against to the big teams like this first round and the 2019 World Cup by falling in the next round against [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_women's_national_football_team) (1–3).

#### 2022 AFC Women's Asian Cup

Still suffering from the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic, Japan was seeking its third consecutive continental title in India. In the first phase, Nadeshiko beat Myanmar (5-0) and Vietnam (3-0) and drew 1-1 with South Korea, finishing first in the group.

In the quarterfinals, they secured a direct spot in the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup by beating Thailand 7-0. However, their dream of a third consecutive championship ended when they were defeated by China in the semifinals on penalties by 4-3 after a 2-2 draw that lasted until 120 minutes of extra time.

### The new era (2023–2024)

#### 2023 World Cup



Japan playing against [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_women's_national_football_team) at the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup](45271353.md) in [Wellington](33804.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md)

Japan finished first in [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_Group_C) with Spain, Zambia and Costa Rica, winning all three group games, scoring 11 goals and conceded none. Japan's technical mastery made a strong impression in particular in her 4–0 victory against [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_women's_national_football_team), the eventual champion. That was a landmark match that broke long-held FIFA records. Japan women's 23% of possession is the lowest for a winning side at the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) since 2011. This is often compared to Japan 2-1 Spain in [2022 FIFA World Cup Group E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E), which Japan's 18% of possession is the lowest for a winning side at the FIFA World Cup since 1966.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-espn_japan-20][^cite_note-TA_Japan-21] Match statistics reflected Japan women was more incisive and efficient, with only 23% possession, their expected goal (xG) was 0.9 vs 0.7, shots attempted were 7 vs 10, shots on target were 5 vs 2, and goals scored were 4 vs 0.[^cite_note-22]

In the knockout stage, Japan was able to beat [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_women's_national_football_team) 3–1[^cite_note-23] and advanced to the quarter-finals for the first time since [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup). Facing [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_women's_national_football_team), another Scandinavian in the quarter-finals, Japan was hailed as favorite due to their four consecutive wins at this World Cup, but, unfortunately for the *Nadeshiko*, they were unable to create as many chances as they did against Norway. Japan found themselves trailing by 2 goals to nothing at halftime, and [Honoka Hayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoka_Hayashi)'s goal in the 87th minute ended as the only effort Japan could effectively turn into a goal. Losing by 2–1, they were eliminated from the World Cup, and the second time in succession that Japan fell to Sweden in a major competition, as they were also eliminated at the quarter-finals of the Tokyo Olympics just two years prior, on 2021.

#### 2024 Summer Olympics

After eliminating [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_women's_national_football_team) in the third round of the [2024 AFC Women's Olympic Qualifying Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_AFC_Women's_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament), the Nadeshiko Japan returned to qualify for an Olympic Games for the first time since London 2012 (they failed to qualify for the Rio 2016 Games). In the first match against [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_women's_national_football_team), they opened the score with a beautiful free-kick shoot from [Aoba Fujino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aoba_Fujino), but the Spanish came back by winning 2-1 with the goals of [Aitana Bonmatí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitana_Bonmat%C3%AD) and [Mariona Caldentey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariona_Caldentey). Against [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_women's_national_football_team) in the second match, the Nadeshiko was losing the match by 1-0 until the final stoppage time when a penalty kick of [Saki Kumagai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saki_Kumagai) and a 40-meter goal shot from [Momoko Tanikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momoko_Tanikawa) turned the match around and prevented Japan from being eliminated in the group stage until then.

In the last match of the group phase against [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_women's_national_football_team), the Japanese qualified to the quarter-finals winning the Nigerians by 3-1 with goals from [Maika Hamano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maika_Hamano), [Mina Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mina_Tanaka) and a free-kick from [Hikaru Kitagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikaru_Kitagawa). Although they made a great match against [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_soccer_team) in the quarter-finals, Japan was eliminated losing to the Americans by 1-0 due to a goal of [Trinity Rodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Rodman) in extra time. At the end, Nadeshiko Japan end the Paris campaign in the fifth place, the best of the non-semifinalists.[^cite_note-24]

### Nils Nielsen era (2025–2026)

In mid-December 2024, JFA head coach Futoshi Ikeda stepped down, and Greenland-born Danish coach [Nils Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nils_Nielsen) took over as head coach for the Nadeshikos. Nielsen also brought in Australian U-20 coach [Leah Blayney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leah_Blayney) as one of the assistant managers of the JFA. His style of play included bringing out the ruthlessness needed from the Japanese to win games.

#### 2025 SheBelieves Cup and first title won

Japan was invited alongside [Australia](30864460.md) and [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_women's_national_football_team) to the 2025 SheBelievesCup in the United States. As the tournament reverted back to the round-robin style format, the top-placed team was the winner. Japan started against Australia and easily dominated the game with a 4-0 thrashing of the Matildas with goals by [Mina Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mina_Tanaka) (2), [Maika Hamano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maika_Hamano), and [Moeka Minami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moeka_Minami).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Japan went on to win against Colombia 4-1 with an instant super goal from [Momoko Tanikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momoko_Tanikawa) eighteen seconds into the game. Mina Tanaka then scored the second from a corner kick by Tanikawa. Japan conceded from a counterattack by the Colombians, however in the second half, a goal from Maika Hamano and a penalty kick finished by Mina Tanaka sealed it for the Nadeshikos.[^cite_note-27]

Japan headed into the final game of the tournament with the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_soccer_team). With their goal difference higher than the Americans, a draw was needed for them to win the competition. One minute into the game, [Yuka Momiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%ABka_Momiki) scored the first goal, however in the 14th minute, [Ally Sentnor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ally_Sentnor) scored the equalizer which leveled the game. After half-time, [Aoba Fujino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aoba_Fujino) won a foul outside the American box, and [Yui Hasegawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yui_Hasegawa) took the subsequent free kick, which the American goalkeeper initially saved, but then [Toko Koga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dko_Koga) followed up and puts the loose ball into the net. Nielsen's Japan sees out the game with the win, lifting their first SheBelieves Cup trophy.[^cite_note-28] They defeated the United States for the first time since [2013 Algarve Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Algarve_Cup), and ended their 22-game unbeaten run.

### Mid-year drop of form

#### First set of international friendlies

Fresh from their SheBelievesCup win, Japan faced against Colombia in two back-to-back games, drawing against them 1-1 in the first, and then winning against them in an unofficial match 6-1. However, their form started to drop when they lost two back-to-back matches against Brazil, whose pace and power dominated the Japanese. They also played a send-off game against Spain for the 2025 UEFA Women's Euros, where despite Mina Tanaka scoring the opening goal, the Spanish turned the game around, with the final score being 1-3 to Spain.

#### 2025 EAFF E-1 Women's Football Championship

With the EAFF E-1 Football Championship upcoming, Japan fielded a rotated squad of entirely WE League players. They easily win against Chinese Taipei 4-0, before suffering two consecutive draws against China and South Korea 1-1 and 0-0 respectively. Despite Japan having a higher goal difference than the other teams, they ended up in third place, since they had lower head-to-head goals than China and South Korea.

#### Last four friendlies

In October Japan played two friendlies against European opposition namely Italy and Norway. They were behind Italy after half-time, but Yui Hasegawa scored a chip which ties the game and eventually the game ended 1-1. Against Norway, they lost 2-0 with Norway's [Signe Gaupset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signe_Gaupset) scoring a brace.[^cite_note-29]

Japan ended their winless drought against [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_women's_national_soccer_team) in two back-to-back games, which saw them win 3-0 and 1-0 in both of those games.[^cite_note-30]

#### 2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup

Finishing top three in the previous tournament, Japan automatically qualified for the [2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup](67991141.md) which took place in Australia as the favorites to win the title and was placed in a relatively easy Group C along with [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_women's_national_football_team), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_women's_national_football_team) and [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_women's_national_football_team). They beat Taiwan 2–0 in their game, as their opponents played with a very low block which the Japanese broke through in the 61st minute with a goal from [Momoko Tanikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momoko_Tanikawa) and another one later by [Kiko Seike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiko_Seike). In their next game, the Nadeshikos dismantle India, putting eleven past the net, which was the match with the most goals scored in the tournament including hat-tricks from [Riko Ueki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riko_Ueki) and [Hinata Miyazawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinata_Miyazawa). Their final group stage game against Vietnam had them cruise to a 4–0 victory, topping their group and sending them to the quarter-finals.[^cite_note-31]

In the quarter-finals, Japan eliminated the [Philippines](5514753.md) with a dominating 7–0 victory, automatically booking their ticket to the [2027 FIFA Women's World Cup](60983910.md).[^cite_note-32] Japan advanced to the semi-finals against [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_football_team), where they won 4–1 and conceded their first and only goal of the tournament.

Japan faced off against Australia in [the final](82560603.md), whose team eliminated North Korea and then previous champions China from the tournament. In front of a record crowd of mainly Australian fans, the Japanese scored the only goal of the match with a shot outside of the box from [Maika Hamano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maika_Hamano). After a cagey battle and repelling a last-ditch Australian attack in the last minutes, Japan lived up to the expectations and reclaimed their title, winning their third continental trophy.[^cite_note-33] They were by all means the best team of the tournament, with 29 goals scored across all six games, six wins and five clean sheets.

## Team image

### Nicknames

The Japan women's national football team has been known or nicknamed as the "*[Nadeshiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_nadeshiko) Japan*". The word Nadeshiko has several meanings.

The term “[Yamato nadeshiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_nadeshiko)” (which came the expression) was an old expression often used to describe the traditional Japanese woman as a woman who was beautiful and delicate on the outside but strong and determined on the inside, although she was often submissive to men. In fact, the word [Yamato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_people) is the name given to the race and ethnicity of people from which Japan was formed (as it is also the name of the Japanese imperial dynasty that rules the country to this day). In modern times, the term “Yamato Nadeshiko” has been updated to mean “a modest woman” who is both strong, mature and determined, as well as beautiful, delicate and graceful.

The nickname was coined by the [Japan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Football_Association) in 2004 after a fan vote during the [Athens 2004 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympic_Games), which was intended to convey that the Japanese female player was strong and brave like an athlete, feminine like a woman and Japanese with both a patriotic and nationalistic character. At first, it was not widely accepted by the public and the media until the 2011 Women’s World Cup. After the 2011 World Cup title, the expression Nadeshiko completely changed to “the female football player from Japan”.[^cite_note-34]

### Rivalries

#### China

In the 1970s, Nadeshiko Japan and China’s Steel Roses became the first Asian continental powerhouses in women's football. While the Steel Roses dominated in the 1980s, 1990s, and 2000s, Nadeshiko Japan began dominating the rivalry in the early 2010s. It is the biggest women's football rivalry in Asia.

#### Australia

Main article: [Australia–Japan football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93Japan_football_rivalry)

Although the first match between both national teams started in 1984, since the Australians came to AFC in 2006 both Nadeshiko Japan and the Matildas created the most significant women's football rivalry in Asia with a lot of [AFC Women's Asian Cup](2428648.md) title matches and a [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) quarterfinal.

#### North Korea

Another big rival of Nadeshiko Japan, the Azaleas from North Korea created this rivalry at the same time than the rivalry of Japan with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_women's_national_football_team). Although the North Koreans don't play a lot of recently matches against the Japanese in professional terms, in the U-20 and U-17 competitions is the biggest rivalry of the continent.

#### South Korea

Main article: [Japan–South Korea football rivalry § Women's matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93South_Korea_football_rivalry#Women's_matches)

Unlike men's football, the rivalry between Japan and [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_football_team) in women's football is still recent (created in the 1990s). But it's not a rivalry below due to the history of the two countries.

#### United States

Main article: [Japan–United States women's soccer rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93United_States_women's_soccer_rivalry)

Outside Asia, the biggest rival of Nadeshiko Japan is [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_soccer_team). Both teams created the rivalry in the 1980s and both teams have always faced each other in decisive instances, including two consecutive [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) finals recently in 2011 and 2015.

### Algarve Cup

The Algarve Cup was an invitational [international tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_competitions_in_women's_association_football), hosted by the [Portuguese Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Football_Federation) (FPF). ### Tournament of Nations

The **Tournament of Nations** was a global invitational [tournament for national teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_competitions_in_women's_association_football) in non-World Cup and non-Olympic years hosted by the [United States Soccer Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Soccer_Federation) (USSF) in several [American cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-58] The inaugural tournament was held in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Tournament_of_Nations).

The 2021 edition would have been a pre-Olympics tournament due to the rescheduling of the [Tokyo Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-59] On May 6, 2021, however, the USSF announced that it would no longer hold Tournament of Nations because recent changes in international windows by [FIFA](11049.md) made a round-robin tournament unfeasible.[^cite_note-60]
