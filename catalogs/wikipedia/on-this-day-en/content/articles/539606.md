**Central Africa** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Afrique centrale*; [Spanish](26825.md): *África central*; [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *África Central*) is a [subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subregion) of the [African continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_continent) comprising various countries according to different definitions. **Middle Africa** is an analogous term used by the United Nations in its [geoscheme for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme_for_Africa) and consists of the following countries: [Angola](701.md), [Cameroon](5447.md), [Central African Republic](5478.md), [Chad](5488.md), [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), [Equatorial Guinea](9366.md), [Gabon](12027.md), [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md), and [São Tomé and Príncipe](20026306.md). The United Nations Office for Central Africa also covers [Burundi](21490998.md) and [Rwanda](25645.md), although both are classified under [East Africa](260987.md) in the geoscheme.[^cite_note-\:0-1] These eleven countries are or were members of the [Economic Community of Central African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_Central_African_States) (ECCAS).[^cite_note-\:0-1] Six of those countries (Cameroon, Central African Republic, Chad, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, and Republic of the Congo) are also members of the [Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_Central_African_States#Economic_and_Monetary_Community_of_Central_Africa) (CEMAC) and share a common currency, the [Central African CFA franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_CFA_franc).[^cite_note-2]

## Background



Membership of [ECCAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_Central_African_States)

The [Central African Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Federation) (1953–1963), also called the [Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Rhodesia_and_Nyasaland), was made up of what are now the nations of [Malawi](19088.md), [Zambia](34415.md), and [Zimbabwe](34399.md). Similarly, the Anglican [Church of the Province of Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Province_of_Central_Africa) covers dioceses in [Botswana](3464.md), Malawi, Zambia, and Zimbabwe, while the [Church of Central Africa, Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Central_Africa,_Presbyterian) has synods in Malawi, Zambia, and Zimbabwe. These states are now typically considered part of East or [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa).[^cite_note-4]

### Prehistory

Main article: [Prehistoric Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Central_Africa)

[Archeological finds in Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_Central_Africa) have been made which date back over 100,000 years.[^cite_note-5] According to Zagato and Holl, there is evidence of iron smelting in the Central African Republic that may date back to 3000 to 2500 BCE.[^cite_note-6] Extensive walled settlements have recently been found in Northeast Nigeria, approximately 60 km (37 mi) southwest of Lake Chad dating to the first millennium BCE.[^cite_note-7]

Trade and improved agricultural techniques supported more sophisticated societies, leading to the early civilizations of West Africa: [Sao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sao_civilization), [Kanem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem_Empire), [Bornu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornu_Empire), [Shilluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilluk_Kingdom), [Baguirmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Baguirmi), and [Wadai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadai_Empire).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAppiahGates2010254-8]

Around 2500 BCE, [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples) migrants had reached the Great Lakes Region in Central Africa. Halfway through the first millennium BCE, the Bantu had also settled as far south as what is now [Angola](701.md).

### Ancient history

#### Sao civilization

Main article: [Sao civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sao_civilization)

The West African [Sao civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sao_civilization) flourished from ca. the 6th century BCE to as late as the 16th century CE in northern Central Africa. The Sao lived by the Chari River south of Lake Chad in territory that later became part of Cameroon and Chad. They are the earliest people to have left clear traces of their presence in the territory of modern [Cameroon](5447.md). Today, several ethnic groups of northern Cameroon and southern Chad but particularly the [Sara people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_people) claim descent from the civilization of the Sao. Sao artifacts show that they were skilled workers in [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze), copper, and iron.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFanso198919-9] Finds include bronze sculptures and terra cotta statues of human and animal figures, coins, funerary urns, household utensils, jewelry, highly decorated pottery, and spears.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFanso198919HudgensTrillo19991051-10] The largest Sao archaeological finds have been made south of Lake Chad.

### Kanem Empire



The Kanem and Bornu Empires in 1810

Main article: [Kanem Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem_Empire)

The West-Central African kingdom of [Kanem–Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem%E2%80%93Bornu_Empire) was centered in the Lake [Chad Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Basin). It was known as the [Kanem Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem_Empire) from the 9th century CE onward and lasted as the independent kingdom of [Bornu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornu_Empire) until 1900. At its height it encompassed an area covering not only much of [Chad](5488.md), but also parts of modern eastern [Niger](21373.md), northeastern [Nigeria](21383.md), northern [Cameroon](5447.md) and parts of [South Sudan](32350676.md). The history of the Empire is mainly known from the Royal Chronicle or *[Girgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girgam)* discovered in 1851 by the German traveler [Heinrich Barth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Barth).[^cite_note-11] Kanem rose in the 8th century in the region to the north and east of Lake Chad. The Kanem empire went into decline, shrank, and in the 14th century was defeated by [Bilala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilala_people) invaders from the [Lake Fitri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Fitri) region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFalola200826-12]

### Bornu Empire

Main article: [Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornu_Empire)



[Galadima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galadima) of Bornu receiving French officer [Parfait-Louis Monteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parfait-Louis_Monteil), 1891

The [Kanuri people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_people) of West Africa led by the Sayfuwa migrated to the west and south of the lake, where they established the [Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornu_Empire). By the late 16th century the Bornu empire had expanded and recaptured the parts of Kanem that had been conquered by the Bulala.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFalola200827-13] Satellite states of Bornu included the [Damagaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Damagaram) in the west and [Baguirmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bagirmi) to the southeast of Lake Chad.

### Shilluk Kingdom

Main article: [Shilluk Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilluk_Kingdom)

The [Shilluk Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilluk_Kingdom) was centered in [South Sudan](32350676.md) from the 15th century from along a strip of land along the western bank of White Nile, from [Lake No](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_No) to about 12° north [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude). The capital and royal residence were in the town of [Fashoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashoda). The kingdom was founded during the mid-15th century CE by its first ruler, [Nyikang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyikang). During the 19th century, the Shilluk Kingdom faced decline following military assaults from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and later British and Sudanese colonization in [Anglo-Egyptian Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan).

### Baguirmi Kingdom

Main article: [Baguirmi Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguirmi_Kingdom)

The Kingdom of Baguirmi existed as an independent state during the 16th and 17th centuries southeast of West-Central Africa [Lake Chad](59314.md) region in what is now the country of Chad. Baguirmi emerged to the southeast of the [Kanem–Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem%E2%80%93Bornu_Empire). The kingdom's first ruler was [Mbang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Baguirmi) Birni Besse. Later in his reign, the [Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornu_Empire) conquered and made the state a tributary.

### Wadai Empire



[Abéché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ab%C3%A9ch%C3%A9), capital of Wadai, in 1918 after the French had taken over

Main article: [Wadai Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadai_Empire)

The [Wadai Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadai_Empire) was centered in Chad from the 17th century. The [Tunjur people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunjur_people) founded the Wadai Kingdom to the east of Bornu in the 16th century. In the 17th century, there was a revolt of the [Maba people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maba_people) who established a Muslim dynasty. At first, Wadai paid tribute to Bornu and Durfur, but by the 18th century, Wadai was fully independent and had become an aggressor against its neighbors.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAppiahGates2010254-8]

### Lunda Empire

Main article: [Lunda Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunda_Empire)

Further information: [List of rulers of the Lunda Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_the_Lunda_Empire)



Lunda town and dwelling

Following the [Bantu Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_Migration) from Western Africa, Bantu kingdoms and empires began to develop in southern Central Africa. In the 1450s, a [Luba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luba_Empire) from the royal family [Ilunga Tshibinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilunga_Tshibinda) married [Lunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunda_people) queen Rweej and united all Lunda peoples. Their son Mulopwe Luseeng expanded the kingdom. His son Naweej expanded the empire further and is known as the first Lunda emperor, with the title *Mwata Yamvo* (*mwaant yaav*, *mwant yav*), the "Lord of Vipers". The Luba political system was retained, and conquered peoples were integrated into the system. The *mwata yamvo* assigned a *cilool* or *kilolo* (royal adviser) and tax collector to each state conquered.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShillington2005141Davidson1991161-14]

Numerous states claimed descent from the Lunda. The [Imbangala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imbangala) of inland Angola claimed descent from a founder, Kinguri, brother of Queen Rweej, who could not tolerate the rule of *mulopwe* Tshibunda. *Kinguri* became the title of kings of states founded by Queen Rweej's brother. The [Luena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luena_people) (Lwena) and [Lozi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozi_people) (Luyani) in Zambia also claim descent from Kinguri. During the 17th century, a Lunda chief and warrior called [Mwata Kazembe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwata_Kazembe) set up an [Eastern Lunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Lunda) kingdom in the valley of the [Luapula River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luapula_River). The Lunda's western expansion also saw claims of descent by the [Yaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaka_people) and the [Pende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pende_people). The Lunda linked Central Africa with the western coast trade. The kingdom of Lunda came to an end in the 19th century when it was invaded by the [Chokwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chokwe_people), who were armed with guns.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavidson1991161Shillington2005139,_141-15]

### Kongo Kingdom

Main article: [Kingdom of Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kongo)

Further information: [List of rulers of Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Kongo)



Kongo in 1711

By the 15th century CE, the farming [Bakongo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_people) (*ba* being the plural prefix) were unified as the [Kingdom of Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kongo) under a ruler called the *[manikongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manikongo)*, residing in the fertile [Pool Malebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo) area on the lower [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River). The capital was [M'banza-Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M'banza-Kongo). With superior organization, they were able to conquer their neighbors and extract tribute. They were experts in metalwork, pottery, and weaving raffia cloth. They stimulated interregional trade via a tribute system controlled by the *manikongo*. Later, maize (corn) and [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) (manioc) would be introduced to the region via trade with the Portuguese at their ports at [Luanda](18402.md) and [Benguela](1126618.md). The maize and cassava would result in population growth in the region and other parts of Africa, replacing [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet) as the main staple.

By the 16th century, the *manikongo* held authority from the Atlantic in the west to the [Kwango River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwango_River) in the east. Each territory was assigned a *mani-mpembe* (provincial governor) by the *manikongo*. In 1506, [Afonso I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_I_of_Kongo) (1506–1542), a Christian, took over the throne. Slave trading increased with Afonso's wars of conquest. About 1568 to 1569, the [Jaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaga_\(people\)) invaded Kongo, laying waste to the kingdom and forcing the *manikongo* into exile. In 1574, Manikongo [Álvaro I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_I_of_Kongo) was reinstated with the help of Portuguese mercenaries. During the latter part of the 1660s, the Portuguese tried to gain control of Kongo. Manikongo [António I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_I_of_Kongo) (1661–1665), with a Kongolese army of 5,000, was destroyed by an army of Afro-Portuguese at the [Battle of Mbwila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mbwila). The empire dissolved into petty polities, fighting among each other for war captives to sell into slavery.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECollinsBurns2007185–188Shillington2005196–198Davidson1991156–157-16]

Kongo gained captives from the [Kingdom of Ndongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ndongo) in wars of conquest. Ndongo was ruled by the *ngola*. Ndongo would also engage in slave trading with the Portuguese, with [São Tomé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Tom%C3%A9) being a transit point to Brazil. The kingdom was not as welcoming as Kongo; it viewed the Portuguese with great suspicion and as an enemy. The Portuguese in the latter part of the 16th century tried to gain control of Ndongo but were defeated by the [Mbundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mbundu_people). Ndongo experienced depopulation from slave raiding. The leaders established another state at [Matamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Matamba), affiliated with [Queen Nzinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nzinga_of_Ndongo_and_Matamba), who put up a strong resistance to the Portuguese until coming to terms with them. The Portuguese settled along the coast as trade dealers, not venturing on conquest of the interior. Slavery wreaked havoc in the interior, with states initiating wars of conquest for captives. The [Imbangala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imbangala) formed the slave-raiding state of [Kasanje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasanje_Kingdom), a major source of slaves during the 17th and 18th centuries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShillington2005198,_199Davidson1991158-17]

### Modern history

Further information: [Decolonisation of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa), [Postcolonial Africa § Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcolonial_Africa#Central_Africa), and [Neocolonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neocolonialism)

See also: [Neocolonialism § Françafrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neocolonialism#Fran%C3%A7afrique), [Central African CFA franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_CFA_franc), and [Status of forces agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_forces_agreement)



French explorer [Paul Du Chaillu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Du_Chaillu) confirmed the existence of [Pygmy peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmy_peoples) of central Africa.

During the [Conference of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_of_Berlin) in 1884–85 Africa was divided up between the European colonial powers, defining boundaries that are largely intact with today's post-colonial states.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarlow2003139-18] On 5 August 1890 the British and French concluded an agreement to clarify the boundary between [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa) and what would become [Nigeria](21383.md). A boundary was agreed along a line from [Say](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Say,_Niger) on the [Niger](21437.md) to Barruwa on [Lake Chad](59314.md), but leaving the [Sokoto Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoto_Caliphate) in the British sphere.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHirshfield197926-19] [Parfait-Louis Monteil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parfait-Louis_Monteil) was given charge of an expedition to discover where this line actually ran.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHirshfield197937–38-20] On 9 April 1892 he reached [Kukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kukawa) on the shore of the lake.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELengyel2007170-21] Over the next twenty years a large part of the Chad Basin was incorporated by treaty or by force into [French West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Africa). On 2 June 1909, the Wadai capital of [Abéché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ab%C3%A9ch%C3%A9) was occupied by the French.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMazenot2005352-22] The remainder of the basin was divided by the British in Nigeria, who took [Kano](508782.md) in 1903,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFalola2008105-23] and the Germans in Cameroon.

The countries of the basin regained their independence between 1956 and 1962, retaining the colonial administrative boundaries. [Chad](5488.md), [Gabon](12027.md), the [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md), and the [Central African Republic](5478.md) became autonomous states with the dissolution of [French Equatorial Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Equatorial_Africa) in 1958, gaining full independence in 1960. The [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) also gained independence from [Belgium](3343.md) in 1960, but quickly devolved into a period of political upheaval and conflict known as the [Congo Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Crisis) (1960–1965) which ended with the installment of [Joseph Mobutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobutu_Sese_Seko) as president and renamed the country [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire) in 1971. [Equatorial Guinea](9366.md) gained independence from [Spain](26667.md) in 1968, leading to the election of [Francisco Macías Nguema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Mac%C3%ADas_Nguema), now widely regarded as one of the most brutal dictators in history. In 1961, [Angola](701.md) became involved in the [Portuguese Colonial War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Colonial_War), a 13-year-long struggle for independence in [Lusophone Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese-speaking_African_countries). It gained independence only in 1975, following the 1974 [Carnation Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution) in [Lisbon](18091.md). [São Tomé and Príncipe](20026306.md) also gained independence in 1975 in the aftermath of the Carnation Revolution. In 2011, [South Sudan](32350676.md) gained its independence from the [Republic of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Sudan) after [over 50 years of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sudanese_Civil_War).

In the 21st century, many jihadist and Islamist groups began to operate in the Central African region, including the [Seleka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleka) and the [Ansaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaru).

Over the course of the 2010s, the internationally unrecognized secessionist state called [Ambazonia](841514.md) gained increasing momentum in its home regions, resulting in the ongoing [Anglophone Crisis](57170868.md) in Cameroon.[^cite_note-24]

## Economy



Fishing in Central Africa

The main economic activities of Central Africa are farming, herding and fishing. At least 40% of the rural population of northern and eastern Central Africa lives in poverty and routinely face chronic food shortages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenmore2004220-25] Crop production based on rain is possible only in the southern belt. [Slash-and-burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash-and-burn) agriculture is a common practice.[^cite_note-26] Flood recession agriculture is practiced around Lake Chad and in the riverine wetlands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERangeleyThiamAndersonLyle199449-27] Nomadic herders migrate with their animals into the grasslands of the northern part of the basin for a few weeks during each short rainy season, where they intensively graze the highly nutritious grasses. When the dry season starts they move back south, either to grazing lands around the lakes and floodplains, or to the savannas further to the south.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenmore2004230-28]

In the 2000–01 period, fisheries in the Lake Chad basin provided food and income to more than 10 million people, with a harvest of about 70,000 tons.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenmore2004220-25] Fisheries have traditionally been managed by a system where each village has recognized rights over a defined part of the river, wetland or lake, and fishers from elsewhere must seek permission and pay a fee to use this area. The governments only enforced rules and regulations to a limited extent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenmore2004215-29] Local governments and traditional authorities are increasingly engaged in [rent-seeking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent-seeking), collecting license fees with the help of the police or army.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKenmore2004218-30]

Oil is also a major export of the countries of northern and eastern Central Africa, notably making up a large proportion of the GDPs of Chad and South Sudan.

## Demographics

Further information: [Demographics of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Africa), [List of African countries by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_population), and [List of ethnic groups of Africa § Central Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_of_Africa#Central_Africa)



[Kinshasa](45773.md) is a [megacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacity) with more than 15 million inhabitants.



[Pygmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Pygmies) hunter-gatherers in the [Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Basin)

Following the [Bantu Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_Migration), Central Africa is primarily inhabited by [Native African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_L_\(mtDNA\)) or [Bantu peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples) and [Bantu languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages) predominate. These include the [Mongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongo_people), [Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_people) and [Luba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luba_people) peoples. Central Africa also includes many [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan) and [Niger-Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger-Congo) [Ubangian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubangian) communities: in north western Central Africa the Nilo-Saharan [Kanuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_people)[^cite_note-Factbook:_Nigeria-31][^cite_note-Factbook:_Chad-32] predominate. Most of the [Ubangian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubangian) speakers in Africa (often grouped with Niger-Congo) are also found in Central Africa, such as the [Gbaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gbaya_people),[^cite_note-Factbook:_Central_African_Republic-33] [Banda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_people)[^cite_note-Factbook:_Central_African_Republic-33] and [Zande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zande_people),[^cite_note-Factbook:_South_Sudan-34][^cite_note-Factbook:_Central_African_Republic-33] in northern Central Africa.

Notable Central African supra-regional organizations include the [Lake Chad Basin Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Chad_Basin_Commission) and the [Economic Community of Central African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_Central_African_States).

The predominant religions of Central Africa are [Christianity](5211.md) and [traditional faiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Traditional_Religion). [Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Chad) is the only country in the region where [Islam](6037917.md) in the majority religion. Islam is also common in [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Cameroon), being practiced by about 30% of the population. Smaller Muslim communities exist in the other countries too.

Due to common historical processes and widespread demographic movements between the countries of Central Africa before the Bantu Migration into much of southern Central Africa, the cultures of the region evidence many similarities and interrelationships. Similar cultural practices stemming from common origins as largely Nilo-Saharan or Bantu peoples are also evident in Central Africa including in music, dance, art, body adornment, initiation, and marriage rituals.
