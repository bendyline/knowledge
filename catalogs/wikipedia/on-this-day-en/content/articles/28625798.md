The **Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly** is the unicameral legislature of Indian union territory of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md). Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly is the legislative arm of the [Government of Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Jammu_and_Kashmir). At present, it consists of 95 [members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly_\(India\)), directly elected from [95 constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_constituencies_of_the_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Legislative_Assembly). The tenure of the Legislative Assembly is five years unless dissolved sooner.

Prior to 2019, the [State of Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Jammu_and_Kashmir) had a bicameral legislature with a legislative assembly (lower house) and a legislative council (upper house). The [Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Reorganisation_Act,_2019), passed by the [Parliament of India](410238.md) in August 2019, replaced this with a unicameral legislature while also re-organising the state into a [union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory).[^cite_note-3] The [current assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Assembly_\(union_territory\)) was elected in September and October 2024.

## History

### Praja Sabha

The first legislature of the [princely state of Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)), called the *Praja Sabha*, was established by the government of the [Maharaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_of_Kashmir) [Hari Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Singh) in 1934.[^cite_note-B-4] It had 33 elected seats, 30 nominated members and 12 *ex-officio* members.[^cite_note-5]

The [first election in 1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Praja_Sabha_election) saw the Liberal Group headed by Pandit Ram Chander Dubey emerge as the largest party and the [Muslim Conference](512306.md) as the second largest (with 14 seats).[^cite_note-6] Further elections were held in 1938 and 1947.

In 1939, the Muslim Conference party renamed itself to [National Conference](512306.md) under the leadership of [Sheikh Abdullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Abdullah) and opened its membership to people of all religions. It launched a [Quit Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quit_Kashmir) movement in 1946 and boycotted the 1947 election.[^cite_note-7]

### Post-accession

After the accession of the [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)) to the Union of India in 1947, the Maharaja ceded powers to a popular government headed by Sheikh Abdullah. [Elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Constituent_Assembly_election) for a [constituent assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_Jammu_and_Kashmir) were held in 1951, in which Abdullah's National Conference won all 75 seats.

In 1957, a new constitution was adopted by the constituent assembly, which established a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) legislature consisting of an upper house, the [Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Legislative_Council) and a lower house, the Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly.[^cite_note-\:1-8] [Wali Mohammad Itoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wali_Mohammad_Itoo) of the National Conference was speaker of the assembly between 7 July 1983 and 31 July 1984.[^cite_note-Malhotra-9]

### Union Territory (with legislation)

In 2019, [Article 370 of the Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_370_of_the_Constitution_of_India), which gave special status to Jammu and Kashmir, was [abrogated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revocation_of_the_special_status_of_Jammu_and_Kashmir)[^cite_note-10] and [Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Reorganisation_Act,_2019) was passed to reconstitute the state of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\)) into union territories of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) and [Ladakh](303611.md) with effect from 31 October 2019.[^cite_note-\:3-11] The union territory of Jammu and Kashmir has a [unicameral](204304.md) Legislative Assembly. The [Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Legislative_Council) was formally abolished on 31 October 2019.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

In March 2020, a three-member Delimitation Commission was formed, chaired by retired Justice [Ranjana Prakash Desai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjana_Desai), for the delimitation of the union territory of Jammu and Kashmir.[^cite_note-dComissionFormed-14] The commission published its interim report in February 2022.[^cite_note-\:0-15] The final delimitation report was released on 5 May 2022[^cite_note-The_Hindu-16] and it came into force from 20 May 2022.[^cite_note-17]

The first elections to the reconstituted assembly with 90 elected seats were held in September 2024.&#x20;

## Composition

### Initial Composition

The Legislative Assembly was initially composed of 100 members, later increased to 111 by the then [Constitution of Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Jammu_and_Kashmir) (Twentieth Amendment) Act of 1988.[^cite_note-\:1-8] Of these, 24 seats were designated for the territorial constituencies of the state that came under Pakistani control in 1947.[^cite_note-\:1-8] These seats remained officially vacant as per section 48 of the then state constitution and now also in The Constitution of India.[^cite_note-\:1-8] These seats were not taken into account for reckoning the total membership of the assembly, especially for deciding [quorum](237014.md) and voting majorities for legislation and government formation.[^cite_note-\:1-8] Hence the total contestable and filled seats of the assembly were 87 seats. The [Kashmir Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Valley) region had 46 seats, the [Jammu region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_Division) had 37 seats, and [Ladakh](303611.md) region had 4 seats.

### State Reorganisation and Delimitation

[Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Reorganisation_Act,_2019) was passed to reconstitute the state of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\)) into union territories of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) and [Ladakh](303611.md).[^cite_note-\:3-11] In March 2020, Delimitation Commission was formed for the delimitation of the union territory of Jammu and Kashmir prior to the [2024 Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Jammu_and_Kashmir_Legislative_Assembly_election).[^cite_note-dComissionFormed-14] The delimitation report added additional 6 seats to the [Jammu division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_division) and 1 seat to [Kashmir division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_division). After delimitation, the total seats in the assembly rose to a total of 119 seats with 114 elected seats, out of which 24 seats are designated for areas that fall under [Pakistan-administered Kashmir](2745.md) and 5 seats are to be nominated by the Lt. Governor of UT. Out of the remaining 90 seats, 43 seats are in Jammu division and 47 seats are in the Kashmir division. With this, hindu majority Jammu with a population of 53 lakh (43% of the total population of 1.22 crore) will have 47% seats, while muslim majority Kashmir which has a population of 68 lakh (56%) will have 52% of the seats.[^cite_note-The_Hindu-16]

#### Reservation for SC/STs

The parliament passed the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill 2023 which provides for reservation of 7 seats for the Scheduled Castes and 9 seats for the Scheduled Tribes.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### Provisions for Nominated Members

[Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Reorganisation_Act), 2019 provides for nomination of 2 members to the Legislative Assembly by the [Lieutenant Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Jammu_and_Kashmir) if women are not adequately represented in the house.[^cite_note-20] Following amendment to the Act in 2023, the Lieutenant Governor may also nominate two representatives of Kashmiri migrant families (one seat reserved for woman) and one member to represent the migrants from [Pakistan-administered Kashmir](2745.md),[^cite_note-21] presently making the total number of nominated members maximum five.

## Tenure and functions

Members of the Legislative Assembly were elected for a six-year term up to 2019 and five-year term thereafter. The seats are filled by [direct election](652563.md) from single member constituencies using the [first past the post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_past_the_post) method. The assembly may be dissolved before the completion of the full term by the Lieutenant Governor upon the advice of the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chief_Ministers_of_Jammu_and_Kashmir). The Lieutenant Governor may also convene [special sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_session) of the legislative assembly.
