**Rogoźno** ([\[rɔˈɡɔʑnɔ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Rogasen*) is a town in west-central [Poland](22936.md), in [Greater Poland Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_Voivodeship), about 40 kilometres (25 mi) north of [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84).[^cite_note-TERYT-2] Its population is 10,959 (2021). It is the seat of the administrative district ([gmina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmina)) called [Gmina Rogoźno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmina_Rogo%C5%BAno).

## History



Death of King [Przemysł II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemys%C5%82_II) in Rogoźno, 19th-century painting by [Jan Matejko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Matejko)

Rogoźno dates back to a [Lechitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lechites) (proto-Polish) [stronghold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gord_\(archaeology\)) from the 8th and 9th centuries. Its name is of [Old Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Polish) origin, and comes from the word *rogoża*, a type of plant. The oldest known mention comes from 1192. From the mid-12th century it was a seat of a [castellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castellan). In 1280 it was granted [town rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_rights)[^cite_note-3] by [Przemysł II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemys%C5%82_II). King [Przemysł II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemys%C5%82_II) of Poland was murdered in or near Rogoźno in 1296. According to one tradition he was kidnapped while staying at the town, but was so badly wounded in the process that he was unable to continue the journey, and was killed by his captors at [Sierniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierniki,_Oborniki_County) a few miles to the east. Rogoźno was a [royal town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city_in_Poland) of Poland, administratively located in the Poznań County in the [Poznań Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84_Voivodeship_\(14th_century_to_1793\)) in the [Greater Poland Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_Province,_Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland).[^cite_note-4]

The town was annexed by [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) in the [Second Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Partition_of_Poland) in 1793. After the successful [Greater Poland uprising of 1806](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_uprising_\(1806\)), it was regained by Poles and included within the short-lived [Duchy of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Warsaw). The 10th Polish Infantry Regiment was formed in Rogoźno in 1806.[^cite_note-5] In 1815 it was reannexed by Prussia, and from 1871 it was also part of Germany. During the time of Prussian administration, the town was subjected to [Germanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanisation_of_Poles_during_the_Partitions) policies. In 1906–1907, local [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) school children protested against Germanisation.[^cite_note-6] According to the 1910 census, the population of the town was 5,624, of whom 3,203 (57%) reported German as their sole mother tongue, while 2,326 (41%) reported Polish; the Jewish population was 515 (9%).[^cite_note-7]

In November 1918, Poland regained independence and the [Greater Poland uprising (1918–19)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_uprising_\(1918%E2%80%9319\)) broke out, which goal was to reintegrate the town and region with the reborn state. The town was captured by Polish insurgents on December 31, 1918,[^cite_note-8] and thereafter became part of the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic).

During the joint German-Soviet [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) in September 1939, which started [World War II](32927.md), the town was invaded by Germany and then [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poland_\(1939%E2%80%931945\)) until 1945. Polish residents were subjected to arrests, massacres and [expulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Poles_by_Nazi_Germany). Some inhabitants of Rogoźno were among Poles massacred by the Germans in September 1939 in the nearby villages of [Podlesie Kościelne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podlesie_Ko%C5%9Bcielne) and [Międzylesie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%C4%99dzylesie,_Oborniki_County).[^cite_note-9] In late 1939, around 900 Poles were expelled from the town and its surroundings.[^cite_note-10] The Germans also established and operated a Nazi prison in the town.[^cite_note-11] Nine graduates from the local teachers' college, as well as a local doctor and a police officer were murdered by the Russians in the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre) in 1940.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The [Polish resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II) was active in Rogoźno. Local Polish scouts reproduced a [secret Polish newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_underground_press), originally published in nearby [Oborniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oborniki).[^cite_note-16]
