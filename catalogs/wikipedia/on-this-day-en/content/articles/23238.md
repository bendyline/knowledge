The [People's Republic of China](5405.md) is the second [most-populous country in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_\(United_Nations\)) with a population exceeding 1.4 billion, only surpassed by [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India). China was the world's [most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population)[^cite_note-7] until it was surpassed by India in 2023.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In 2025, China's population officially stood at 1.404 billion, which was three million less than the previous year, with the lowest recorded birthrate since at least the [proclamation of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) in 1949.[^cite_note-\:6-10] China's population has a relatively small youth component, partially a result of the strict enforcement of China's [one-child policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-child_policy) that was in effect from 1979 until 2015, which limited urban families to one child and rural families to two. As of 2025, the country's [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) was below 1.0, one of the lowest in the world alongside [South Korea](27022.md), [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Taiwan), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Hong_Kong) and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Singapore).[^cite_note-\:8-11] Since 2022, deaths have outstripped births in the country.[^cite_note-\:6-10]

According to the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census), 91.11% of the population was [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), and 8.89% were [minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China). China's population growth rate is -0.10%.[^cite_note-\:9-12][^cite_note-13] China conducted its [sixth national population census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Chinese_census) in 2010,[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] and its [seventh census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census) was completed in late 2020, with data released in May 2021.[^cite_note-16]

During 1960–2015, the population grew to nearly 1.4 billion. Under [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong), China nearly doubled in population from 540 million in 1949 to 969 million in 1979. This growth slowed because of the one-child policy instituted in 1979.[^cite_note-Banister-17] The 2022 data shows a declining population for the first time since 1961.[^cite_note-18]

China's population reached 1 billion in 1982, making it the first country to reach this milestone.[^cite_note-19]

### Censuses in China

Main article: [Census in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_in_China)

The People's Republic of China conducted censuses in [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Chinese_census), [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Chinese_census), [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Chinese_census), [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Chinese_census), [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Chinese_census), [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Chinese_census), and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census). In 1987, the government announced that the fourth national census would take place in 1990 and that there would be one every ten years thereafter. The 1982 census (which reported a total population of 1,008,180,738) is generally accepted as significantly more reliable, accurate, and thorough than the previous two.[^cite_note-20] Various international organizations eagerly assisted the Chinese in conducting the 1982 census, including the [United Nations Fund for Population Activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Fund_for_Population_Activities), which donated US\$100.0 million between 1980 and 1989 for a variety of projects, one of which being the 1982 census.[^cite_note-21]

China was the world's most populous nation until being surpassed by India in 2023.[^cite_note-22]

By the seventh census in 2020, the total population had reached to 1,419,933,142, with the mainland having 1,411,778,724, Hong Kong having 7,474,200, and Macau having 683,218. However, this number is disputed by [obstetrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetrics) researcher [Yi Fuxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Fuxian), who argues that data related to population growth is inflated by local governments to obtain financial subsidies from the central government.[^cite_note-23]

### Population density and distribution



Map of China's population density across third-level administrative divisions. Based on the results of the 2020 census.



General topographic map of the most populous part of China, as per 2024 (Click to enlarge)

China is the second most populous country in the world and its overall population density is similar to [those](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_density) of countries like [Denmark](76972.md) (excluding Greenland) or [Ghana](12067.md). However, the country's population density contains major regional variations. In 2002, about 94% of the population lived east of the [Heihe–Tengchong Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heihe%E2%80%93Tengchong_Line); although this eastern area comprises only 43% of China's total land area, its population density, at roughly 280/km<sup>2</sup>, is comparable to that of Japan.[^cite_note-24]

Broadly speaking, the population concentrates east of the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau) and south of the [northern steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian%E2%80%93Manchurian_grassland). The most densely populated areas included the [Yangtze River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_Valley) (of which the delta region was the most populous), [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin), [North China Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_Plain), [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta), and the industrial area around the city of [Shenyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenyang) in the northeast.[^cite_note-25]

Population is most sparse in the mountainous, desert, and grassland regions of the northwest and southwest. In the [Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region](55064.md), portions are completely uninhabited, and only a few sections have populations denser than ten people per km<sup>2</sup>. The Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang, and [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) autonomous regions and [Qinghai](173818.md) and [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) comprise 55% of the country's land area but in 1985 contained only 5.7% of its population.

Birth rate in China

Initially, China's post-1949 leaders were ideologically disposed to view a large population as an asset. But soon afterwards, the leaders changed course. For one year, starting in August 1956, vigorous support was given to the Ministry of Public Health's mass [birth control](18978770.md) efforts. These efforts, however, had little impact on fertility. After the interval of the [Great Leap Forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Leap_Forward), Chinese leaders again saw rapid population growth as an obstacle to development, and their interest in birth control revived. In the early 1960s, schemes somewhat more muted than during the first campaign, emphasized the virtues of late marriage. Birth control offices were set up in the central government and some provincial-level governments in 1964. The second campaign was particularly successful in the cities, where the birth rate was cut in half during the 1963–66 period.

The [one-child policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-child_policy) enjoyed much greater success in urban than in rural areas. Even without state intervention, there were compelling reasons for urban couples to limit the family to a single child. Raising a child required a significant portion of family income, and in the cities a child did not become an economic asset until he or she entered the work force at age sixteen. Couples with only one child were given preferential treatment in housing allocation. In addition, because city dwellers who were employed in state enterprises received pensions after retirement, the sex of their first child was less important to them than it was to those in rural areas.[^cite_note-32]

Observers suggested that an accurate assessment of the one-child program would not be possible until all women who came of childbearing age in the early 1980s passed their fertile years. As of 1987 the one-child program had achieved mixed results. In general, it was very successful in almost all urban areas but less successful in rural areas.[^cite_note-33]

Rapid fertility reduction associated with the one-child policy has potentially negative results. For instance, in the future the elderly might not be able to rely on their children to care for them as they have in the past, leaving the state to assume the expense, which could be considerable. Based on United Nations and Chinese government statistics, it was estimated in 1987 that by 2000 the population 60 years and older (the retirement age is 60 in urban areas) would number 127 million, or 10.1% of the total population; the projection for 2025 was 234 million elderly, or 16.4%. According to projections based on the 1982 census, if the one-child policy were maintained to the year 2000, 25% of China's population would be age 65 or older by 2040. In 2050, the number of people over 60 is expected to increase to 430 million.[^cite_note-34] Even though China has already adopted a [two-child policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-child_policy#China) since 2016, data shows that the second-child policy cannot stop the problem of an aging population. China needs to find an appropriate birth policy to optimize the demographic dividend, which refers to the proportion of labor-age population.[^cite_note-35] On the other hand, higher house prices plays an important role on the influence of marriage and fertility. The increasing house prices leads to lower marriage rates and cause other serious social problems in China.[^cite_note-36]

In 2024, [United Nations](31769.md) researchers forecast China's population to fall to 639 million by 2100.[^cite_note-\:4-37] The same year, researchers from [Victoria University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_\(Australia\)) and the [Shanghai Academy of Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Academy_of_Social_Sciences) forecast that China's population will fall to approximately 525 million by 2100 at current rates.[^cite_note-\:2-38][^cite_note-\:4-37] This revision, reducing the population estimate to 525 million from a previous forecast of 597 million by 2100, indicates a sharper decline than previously anticipated.[^cite_note-\:2-38] Demographer [Yi Fuxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Fuxian) estimated that China's population may fall to as low as 330 million by 2100.[^cite_note-39]

### Pro-natalist policies

In the 2020s, pro-[natalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalism) policies have been put in place to encourage people to have more children.[^cite_note-40] Specific policies have included lengthening of paid maternity leave, cash payments to mothers in some regions who have their second and third children,[^cite_note-\:11-41][^cite_note-\:53-42] government-provided children's clothing and toys, increased child allowances, reduced taxes, government-subsidized fertility and pregnancy treatment,[^cite_note-43] and new childcare facilities.[^cite_note-\:2025MesaLago-44]<sup>: 163 </sup> Local government family-planning committees, previously used to enforce the one-child policy, are deployed for pro-natalist policies such as calling women to check on their [menstrual cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstrual_cycle).[^cite_note-\:10-45][^cite_note-\:53-42][^cite_note-\:5-46]

In July 2025, China announced an annual childcare subsidy of 3,600 yuan (about \$500) until age three; this subsidy is estimated to impact about 20 million families.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] In December 2025, China announced an end to the value-add tax exemption on contraceptives[^cite_note-49] and reimbursement for out-of-pocket childbirth expenses.[^cite_note-50] In February 2026, the [Cyberspace Administration of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberspace_Administration_of_China) mandated that social media platforms [censor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_China) content deemed as spreading "fear of marriage" or "anxiety about childbirth."[^cite_note-51]

### Total fertility rate by region

According to the 2000 census, the [TFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) was 1.22 (0.86 for cities, 1.08 for towns and 1.43 for villages/outposts). Beijing had the lowest [TFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) at 0.67, while [Guizhou](173815.md) had the highest at 2.19. The [Xiangyang district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangyang_District,_Jiamusi) of [Jiamusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiamusi) city ([Heilongjiang](173816.md)) has a TFR of 0.41, which is the lowest TFR recorded anywhere in the world in recorded history. Other extremely low TFR counties are: 0.43 in the [Heping district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heping_District,_Tianjin) of [Tianjin city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianjin_city) ([Tianjin](50821.md)), and 0.46 in the [Mawei district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawei_district) of [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou) city ([Fujian](88585.md)). At the other end TFR was 3.96 in [Geji County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geji_County) ([Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region)), 4.07 in [Jiali County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiali_County) (Tibet), and 5.47 in [Baqing County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baqing_County) (Tibet).[^cite_note-56]

The 2010 census reported a TFR of 1.18 (0.88 in cities, 1.15 in townships, and 1.44 in rural areas).[^cite_note-iussp.org-57] The five regions with the lowest fertility rates were Beijing (0.71), Shanghai (0.74), [Liaoning](155516.md) (0.74), [Heilongjiang](173816.md) (0.75), and [Jilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin) (0.76). The five regions with the highest fertility rates were [Guangxi](47836950.md) (1.79), [Guizhou](173815.md) (1.75), [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) (1.53), [Hainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan) (1.51), and [Anhui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhui) (1.48).[^cite_note-iussp.org-57]

The 2020 census reported a TFR of 1.301, with 1.118 in cities, 1.395 in townships, and 1.543 in rural areas.[^cite_note-58] The shift of fertility rate recorded in 2020 census compared to that in the previous 2 censuses may not be an actual recovery, but rather due to the low quality and massive underreporting of lower-age groups in the 2000 and 2010 censuses, while the [population control policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-child_policy) in China back then may also give families an incentive to hide their children, which is largely relieved as the policy changed in 2010s. This can be demonstrated by the much lower number of population aged 0\~4 and 5\~9 in the 2000 and 2010 censuses[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] when compared to the corresponding age groups in 2020.[^cite_note-61]

Total fertility rate by ethnic group (2010 census): [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) (1.14), [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people) (1.59), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) (1.48), [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people) (1.18), [Uyghur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_people) (2.04), [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people) (1.82), [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people) (1.82), [Tujia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people) (1.74), [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) (1.60), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) (1.26).[^cite_note-62]

In 2025, China's population decreased by 3.39 million, with 7.92 million births outpaced by 11.31 million deaths. The country's population stands at 1.4 billion, still the world's second-largest after India. The birth rate fell to 5.63 births per 1,000 people, down from 6.39 in 2023, continuing a steady decline since 2016. Despite a slight uptick in 2024, the trend suggests no reversal. The decline follows years of strict "one-child" policy until its end in 2016 and mirrors patterns seen in other countries like Japan and South Korea, where rising education levels, changing attitudes toward marriage, urbanization, and high child-rearing costs contribute to falling birth rates.[^cite_note-63] Chinese demographer Liang Zhongtang estimated that China's total fertility rate in 2024 fell to 0.7, who stated that the country's population figures had been manipulated for decades.[^cite_note-\:7-64]

### Fertility and mortality



Traffic in Beijing

See also: [Aging of China](60912277.md)

Beginning in the early 1950s, [mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rate) steadily declined; it continued to decline through 1978 and remained relatively constant through 1987. One major fluctuation was reported in a computer reconstruction of China's population trends from 1953 to 1987 produced by the [United States Bureau of the Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bureau_of_the_Census). The computer model showed that the crude death rate increased dramatically during the famine years associated with the [Great Leap Forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Leap_Forward) (1958–60).[^cite_note-69]

China exhibits a serious [gender imbalance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_preference_in_China). Census data obtained in 2000 revealed that 119 boys were born for every 100 girls, and among China's "floating population" the ratio was as high as 128\:100. These situations led the government in July 2004 to ban selective abortions of female fetuses. It is estimated that this imbalance will rise until 2025–2030 to reach 20% then slowly decrease.[^cite_note-70]

In the 2020s, cash incentives have been offered to increase birth rates.[^cite_note-\:11-41][^cite_note-\:53-42] Local government family-planning committees, previously used to enforce the one-child policy, are deployed for pro-natalist policies such as calling women to check on their [menstrual cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstrual_cycle).[^cite_note-\:10-45][^cite_note-\:53-42][^cite_note-\:5-46]

#### Censorship of data

Although the [National Bureau of Statistics of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Statistics_of_China) reported 9.02 million births in 2023,[^cite_note-\:22-71] the state-owned *Mother and Infant Daily* newspaper reported there were 7.88 million births for the same year.[^cite_note-\:3-72] The report was promptly [censored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_China) following publication.[^cite_note-\:3-72]

### Labor force

In 2012, for the first time, according to the [National Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Statistics_of_China) in January 2013, the number of people theoretically able to enter the Chinese labor force (individuals aged 15 to 59), shrank slightly to 937.27 million, a decrease of 3.45 million from 2011. This trend, resulting from a [demographic transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_transition), is anticipated to continue until at least 2030.[^cite_note-CNBC011913-73] The [World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_Factbook) estimated the 2019 active labor force was 774.71 million.[^cite_note-74]

### Gender balance

Future challenges for China will be the gender disparity. According to the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census), males account for 51.24% of China's 1.41 billion people, while females made up 48.76% of the total. The sex ratio (the number of males for each female in a population) at birth was 118.06 boys to every 100 girls (54.14%) in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Chinese_census), higher than the 116.86 (53.89%) of 2000, but 0.53 points lower than the ratio of 118.59 (54.25%) in 2005.[^cite_note-75]

## Ethnic groups

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of ethnic groups in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_China).

The People's Republic of China (PRC) officially recognizes 56 distinct ethnic groups, the largest of which are [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), who constitute 91.51% of the total population in 2010. Ethnic minorities constitute 8.49% or 113.8 million of China's population in 2010. During the past decades ethnic minorities have experienced higher growth rates than the majority [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) population, because they are not under the one-child policy. Their proportion of the population in China has grown from 6.1% in 1953, to 8.04% in 1990, 8.41% in 2000, and 8.49% in 2010. Large ethnic minorities (data according to the 2000 census) include the [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people) (16 million, 1.28%), [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people) (10 million, 0.84%), [Uyghur](31783.md) (9 million, 0.78%), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) (9 million, 0.71%), [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people) (8 million, 0.71%), [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people) (7 million, 0.61%), [Tujia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people) (5.75 million, 0.63%), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China) (5 million, 0.46%), [Tibetan](249458.md) (5 million, 0.43%), [Buyei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouyei_people) (3 million, 0.23%), and [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans) (2 million, 0.15%). Over 126,000 Westerners from [Canada](5042916.md), the [US](3434750.md) and [Europe](9239.md) are living in mainland China.[^cite_note-76]

According to [Barry Sautman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Sautman), the minority population has increased faster than the national population in recent decades.[^cite_note-\:02-77]<sup>: 10 </sup> In 2020, 8.9% of Chinese were ethnic minorities, up from 6.7% in 1982.[^cite_note-\:02-77]<sup>: 10 </sup>

## Migration

Main articles: [Immigration to China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_China), [Migration in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_in_China), and [Chinese emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_emigration)

China's immigration policy, governed by the 2012 Exit-Entry Law, focuses on strict, merit-based, and security-driven controls, actively discouraging low-skilled immigration while restricting long-term permanent residency for most foreigners.[^cite_note-116] In 2004, a system was introduced allowing immigrants to obtain [permanent residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Permanent_Resident_ID_Card); however, its implementation has been carried out on a case-by-case basis. Between 2004 and 2023, approximately 12,000 individuals were granted this status. Naturalization rates have been even lower.[^cite_note-117]

[Internal migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_migration) in the People's Republic of China is one of the most extensive in the world according to the [International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization).[^cite_note-Labour_migration-118] In fact, research done by Kam Wing Chan of the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) suggests that "In the 30 years since 1979, China's urban population has grown by about 440 million to 622 million in 2009. Of the 440 million increase, about 340 million was attributable to net migration and urban reclassification. Even if only half of that increase was migration, the volume of rural-urban migration in such a short period is likely the largest in human history."[^cite_note-119] Migrants in China are commonly members of a [floating population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_population), which refers primarily to migrants in China without local household registration status through the Chinese [Hukou system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukou_system).[^cite_note-Liang_2004_467–488-120] In general, rural-urban migrant workers are most excluded from local educational resources, citywide social welfare programs and many jobs because of their lack of hukou status.[^cite_note-121]

In 2011 a total of 252.78 million [migrant workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrant_worker) (an increase of 4.4% compared to 2010) existed in China. Out of these, migrant workers who left their hometown and worked in other provinces accounted for 158.63 million (an increase of 3.4% compared to 2010) and migrant workers who worked within their home provinces reached 94.15 million (an increase of 5.9% compared to 2010).[^cite_note-statistics-122] Estimations are that Chinese cities will face an influx of another 243 million migrants by 2025, taking the urban population up to nearly 1 billion people.[^cite_note-WSJ-123] This population of migrants would represent "almost 40 percent of the total urban population," a number which is almost three times the current level.[^cite_note-WSJ-123][^cite_note-124] While it is often difficult to collect accurate statistical data on migrant floating populations, the number of migrants is undoubtedly quite large. "In China's largest cities, for instance, it is often quoted that at least one out of every five persons is a migrant."[^cite_note-125] China's government influences the pattern of urbanization through the [Hukou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukou_system) permanent residence registration system, land-sale policies, infrastructure investment and the incentives offered to local government officials. The other factors influencing migration of people from rural provincial areas to large cities are employment, education, business opportunities and higher standard of living.[^cite_note-126]

The mass emigration known as the Chinese diaspora,[^cite_note-127] which occurred from the 19th century to 1949, was mainly caused by wars and [starvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_famines_in_China) in [mainland China](55079.md), invasion from various foreign countries, as well as the problems resulting from political corruption. Most immigrants were illiterate peasants and manual labourers, called "[coolies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolies)" by analogy to the same pattern of immigration from India, who emigrated to work in countries such as the Americas, Australia, South Africa and Southeast Asia.

[Shandong](105032.md): The province is traditionally a stronghold of Confucianism and is the area of origin of many folk religious sects and Confucian churches of the modern period, including the Universal Church of the Way and its Virtue, the [Way of the Return to the One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiyidao) (皈依道 *Guīyīdào*), the [Way of Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiguandao) (一貫道 *Yīguàndào*), and others. Alex Payette (2016) testifies the rapid growth of Confucian groups in the province in the 2010s.<sup>[\[96\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPayette2016-100)</sup> According to the Chinese General Social Survey of 2012,<sup>[\[97\]](#cite_note-101)</sup> about 2.2% of the total population of China (around 30 million people) claims membership in the folk religious sects, which have likely maintained their historical dominance in central-northern and northeastern China.
