**Conservatism in Sweden** refers to the political philosophy of [conservatism](6675.md) as it has developed in [Sweden](5058739.md) throughout the last two centuries. Today it is primarily represented by two major parties, namely the liberal-conservative [Moderate Party](202985.md) and the nationalist [Sweden Democrats](65873.md).

## History

### Early beginnings

In the early 19th century, Swedish conservatism developed alongside [Swedish Romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Romantic_literature). The historian [Erik Gustaf Geijer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Gustaf_Geijer), an exponent of [Gothicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothicism), glorified the [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age) and the [Swedish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire),[^cite_note-1] and the idealist philosopher [Christopher Jacob Boström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Jacob_Bostr%C3%B6m) became the chief ideologue of the official state doctrine, which dominated Swedish politics for almost a century.[^cite_note-2] Other influential Swedish conservative Romantics were [Esaias Tegnér](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esaias_Tegn%C3%A9r) and [Per Daniel Amadeus Atterbom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Daniel_Amadeus_Atterbom).

### Introduction of democracy

Early parliamentary conservatism in Sweden was explicitly elitist. The [Conservative Party](202985.md) was formed in 1904 with one major goal in mind: to stop the advent of [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage), which they feared would result in socialism. Yet, it was a Swedish admiral, the conservative politician [Arvid Lindman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvid_Lindman), who first extended democracy by enacting [male suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Male_suffrage), despite the protests of more traditionalist voices, such as the later prime minister, the arch-conservative and authoritarian statesman [Ernst Trygger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Trygger), who railed at progressive policies such as the abolition of the [death penalty](5902.md).[^cite_note-3]

Once a democratic system was in place, Swedish conservatives sought to combine traditional elitism with modern populism. Sweden's most renowned political scientist, the conservative politician [Rudolf Kjellén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Kjell%C3%A9n), coined the terms [geopolitics](163225.md) and [biopolitics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biopolitics) in relation to his [organic theory of the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organicism#In_politics_and_sociology).[^cite_note-4] He also developed the [corporatist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_corporatism)-[nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_conservatism) concept of *[Folkhemmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folkhemmet)* ('the people's home'), which became the single most powerful political concept in Sweden throughout the 20th century, although it was adopted by the [Social Democratic Party](202978.md) who gave it a more socialist interpretation.[^cite_note-5]

### World War II

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_in_World_War_II), all parties from [Left](18499.md) to [Right](56522.md), with the exception of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Party_\(Sweden\)), formed a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition) in the [Hansson III cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansson_III_cabinet). Around 3000 people, mostly communists, were deemed enemies of the state and sent to [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_camps_in_Sweden_during_World_War_II) during the war.[^cite_note-6]

### Post-war era

In the post-war era, the centre-right parties struggled to cooperate due to their ideological differences: the agrarian populism of the [Centre Party](217370.md), the urban liberalism of the [Liberal People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberals_\(Sweden\)), and the liberal-conservative elitism of the [Moderate Party](202985.md) (the old Conservative Party). Furthermore, the Social Democrats became very popular at this time as a major centre-left party, but as it moved further to the left under [Olof Palme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olof_Palme) it disenchanted many of its previous voters. In [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4lldin_I_cabinet) and in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A4lldin_II_cabinet), the three centre-right parties formed a government under [Thorbjörn Fälldin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorbj%C3%B6rn_F%C3%A4lldin)—and again in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bildt_cabinet) under aristocrat [Carl Bildt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bildt) and with support from the newly founded [Christian Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democrats_\(Sweden\)), the most conservative party in contemporary Sweden.[^cite_note-7]

### Contemporary politics

In modern times, [mass immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_immigration) from distant cultures caused a large populist dissatisfaction, which was not channeled through any of the established parties, who generally espoused [multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism).[^cite_note-8] Instead, the 2010s saw the rise of the right-wing populist [Sweden Democrats](65873.md), who were surging as the largest party in the polls on several occasions.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Due to its fascist roots, the party was ostracised by the other parties until 2019 when Christian Democrat leader [Ebba Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebba_Busch) reached out for collaboration, after which the Moderate Party followed suit.[^cite_note-11] In [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Swedish_general_election), the centre-right parties formed a [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristersson_cabinet) with support from the Sweden Democrats as the largest party.[^cite_note-12] The subsequent [Tidö Agreement](72007581.md), negotiated in [Tidö Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tid%C3%B6_Castle), incorporated authoritarian policies such as a stricter stance on immigration and a harsher stance on [law and order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_and_order_\(politics\)).[^cite_note-13]

The late 2010s also saw the emergence of new smaller conservative parties such as [Alternative for Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_for_Sweden) and [Citizens' Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens'_Coalition). A new conservative think-tank, [Oikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oikos_\(Swedish_think-tank\)), was founded by Sweden Democrat [Mattias Karlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattias_Karlsson_\(politician,_born_1977\)) in 2020.[^cite_note-14]

A resurgence of interest in conservatism has been particularly notable among the younger generation. In the 2022 school elections in [Sweden](5058739.md), in which 80% percent of all Swedish teenagers participated, 9% voted for [left-wing](18499.md) parties, 21% voted for [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics) parties, 8% voted for a [centrist](39619035.md) party, 41% voted for [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics) parties, and 21% voted for a right-wing party.[^cite_note-15]
