In Australia, there is an ongoing debate over the possibility of a ban on the wearing of [burqa](165826.md) and [niqab](1322219.md), conservative forms of dress for Muslim women. There are currently 14 nations that have banned the burqa and niqab, including [Austria](26964606.md), [France](5843419.md), [Belgium](3343.md), [Denmark](76972.md), [Switzerland](26748.md), [Netherlands](21148.md), [Portugal](23033.md),[^cite_note-1] [Latvia](17514.md),[^cite_note-2] [Bulgaria](3415.md),[^cite_note-3] [Cameroon](5447.md), [Chad](5488.md), [Congo](19599929.md), and [Gabon](12027.md).

Senator [Pauline Hanson](69926.md) has [worn a burqa into parliament](81683761.md) multiple times as a form of protest.[^cite_note-4]

## Background

Following the [Moscow theatre hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_theatre_hostage_crisis) in 2002, Australian politician [Fred Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Nile) asked whether the then state minister of police would consider banning full-body coverings like those worn by the [Chechen](6095.md) terrorists from parliament and public gathering places in order to prevent the carriage of weapons or explosive devices. On 23 June 2010, Nile introduced a bill into the Legislative Council to criminalize the public wearing of any face covering which prevents the identification of the wearer,[^cite_note-5] including the [burqa](165826.md) and [niqab](1322219.md). In 2014 Nile introduced another bill that would ban the burqa and niqab.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

In 2010, Senator [Cory Bernardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cory_Bernardi) wrote an opinion piece calling for a ban on wearing the [burqa](165826.md) in public.[^cite_note-\:3-8]

In September 2014, Senator [Jacqui Lambie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqui_Lambie) announced plans to introduce a [private member's bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_member's_bill) aimed at banning the [burqa](165826.md) in Australia.[^cite_note-9] In February 2017, she introduced a [private member's bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_member's_bill) which would amend the Criminal Code Act 1995 to make it illegal to wear full-face coverings in public places when a terrorism threat declaration is in force, unless it was necessary for certain purposes.[^cite_note-10]

## Arguments

One main argument for a burqa ban is security, especially in government areas such as [Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Canberra). It has been suggested that rather than a complete ban, people wearing face coverings be required to show their face for 'security and identification purposes' in these places exclusively.[^cite_note-11]

Some who support a ban have argued that Islamic women who wear burqas are forced to do so by men rather than by [Islam](6037917.md), and that Islam does not require women to cover their faces. Some Islamic women say they feel 'naked' walking out without wearing a burqa, and that a ban would effectively force them to stay at home.

The proposed ban has been criticised for conflicting with Section 116 of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia), which prohibits the federal government from making any law 'prohibiting the free exercise of religion'.[^cite_note-12]

## State-level enforcement

In September 2011, [Australia](4689264.md)'s most populous state, [New South Wales](21654.md), passed the *Identification Legislation Amendment Act 2011* requiring a person to remove a face covering if asked by a state official. The law is viewed as a response to a court case in 2011 where a woman in Sydney was convicted of falsely claiming that a traffic policeman had tried to remove her *niqab*.[^cite_note-13]

## Political positions

Politicians such as [Cory Bernardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cory_Bernardi),[^cite_note-14] [George Christensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Christensen), [Jacqui Lambie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqui_Lambie), [Mark Latham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Latham), [Pauline Hanson](69926.md) and [Tony Abbott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Abbott)[^cite_note-15] have openly advocated for a ban on the burqa, either in public places or in all settings (depending on the person). Several political parties also officially support a ban on the burqa, including the [Australian Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Conservatives) and [One Nation](83013.md).[^cite_note-16]

## Public opinion

A 2017 poll of 2,832 Australian residents conducted by media outlet [Sky News Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News_Australia) and [ReachTEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ReachTEL) found that more than half of respondents either supported or strongly supported banning the burqa in public spaces.[^cite_note-survey-17]&#x20;
