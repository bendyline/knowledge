On June 15, 2026, a [United States Air Force](32090.md) [Boeing B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md) [strategic bomber](214209.md) crashed shortly after takeoff near [Edwards Air Force Base](107530.md) in [Edwards, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwards,_California), United States.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] All eight crew members were killed.[^cite_note-CNN-3]

The accident was the first crash of a B-52 since 2016, when one crashed at [Andersen Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andersen_Air_Force_Base) in [Guam](11974.md), injuring the seven crew members.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] It is also the deadliest crash of a B-52 since 1982, when one crashed at [Mather Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mather_Air_Force_Base) in [Sacramento County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_County,_California), killing all nine crew members.[^cite_note-6]

## Background

### Aircraft

The aircraft involved in the accident was 60-0061, a [Boeing B-52H Stratofortress](18933037.md#Variants) operated by the [United States Air Force](32090.md), and assigned to the [412th Test Wing.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/412th_Test_Wing)[^cite_note-7] 60-0061 was operated by the [307th Bomb Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/307th_Bomb_Wing) in the mid-2010s, where it was nicknamed *[The Spirit of Aggieland II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_of_Aggieland)*.[^cite_note-8] In December 2025, Edwards Air Force Base said the aircraft had flown from [Port San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_San_Antonio) to the base after receiving an upgraded radar system as part of an ongoing modernization program.[^cite_note-9]

### Passengers and crew

The aircraft was carrying eight people, including military personnel, civilians and contractors, on a routine test mission.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] [Boeing](18933266.md) confirmed that two of its employees were among the occupants.[^cite_note-Sky-12][^cite_note-13] The flight was intended to support an [active electronically scanned array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_electronically_scanned_array) (AESA) radar modernization program.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Accident



The crash generated a large plume of smoke visible on satellite imagery ([CIRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_Institute_for_Research_in_the_Atmosphere) image pictured)

The crash was reported at 11\:20 a.m. [PDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone).[^cite_note-16] Photos from the scene showed little recognizable wreckage after a post-crash fire, and video footage showed a large plume of black smoke rising from the crash site.[^cite_note-Sky-12][^cite_note-17]

## Aftermath

Shortly before 1\:00 p.m., the base announced that the airfield was closed and all inbound aircraft were being diverted. All non-commercial visitor passes for the base were suspended in order to minimize interference with the emergency response.[^cite_note-18] The crash was later deemed "not survivable",[^cite_note-19] and [Air Force Materiel Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Materiel_Command) Commander Lieutenant general [Linda Hurry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Hurry) confirmed the death of all 8 personnel on board the aircraft.[^cite_note-20] In a separate statement, [Chief of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force) of the Air Force General [Kenneth S. Wilsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_S._Wilsbach) expressed his sorrows and thoughts with the bomber and testing community, saying that he is "keeping the families, friends, and loved ones affected in my prayers."[^cite_note-21]

## Investigation

The cause of the crash was not immediately known. [Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_\(United_States\)) James Hayes, Deputy Commander of the [412th Test Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/412th_Test_Wing), said the investigation could take an estimated six months.[^cite_note-22]
