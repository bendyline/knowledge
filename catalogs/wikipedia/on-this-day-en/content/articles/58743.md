**Marburg virus disease** (**MVD**), formerly known as **Marburg hemorrhagic fever** (**MHF**), is a [viral hemorrhagic fever](1719309.md) in human and non-human [primates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate) caused by either of the two [Marburgviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburgvirus): [Marburg virus](33134774.md) (MARV) and [Ravn virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravn_virus) (RAVV).[^cite_note-Spickler-3] Its clinical symptoms are very similar to those of [Ebola virus disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_disease) (EVD).[^cite_note-cdcmar-1]

[Egyptian fruit bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_fruit_bat) are believed to be the normal carrier in nature and Marburg virus [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA) has been isolated from them.[^cite_note-Kortepeter2020-4]

## Signs and symptoms

The most detailed study on the frequency, onset, and duration of MVD [clinical signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_sign) and [symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symptom) was performed during the 1998–2000 mixed MARV/RAVV disease outbreak.[^cite_note-Bausch2006-5] A skin [rash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rash), red or purple spots (e.g. [petechiae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petechia) or [purpura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purpura)), [bruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruises), and [hematomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematoma) (especially around needle injection sites) are typical hemorrhagic manifestations. However, contrary to popular belief, hemorrhage does not lead to [hypovolemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypovolemia) and is not the cause of [death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death) (total blood loss is minimal except during [labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childbirth)). Instead, death occurs due to [multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_organ_dysfunction_syndrome) due to fluid redistribution, [hypotension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotension), [disseminated intravascular coagulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disseminated_intravascular_coagulation), and focal [tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_\(biology\)) [necroses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necrosis).[^cite_note-Bausch2006-5][^cite_note-Martini1968-6][^cite_note-Stille1968-7][^cite_note-Martini1971-8][^cite_note-9]

Clinical phases of Marburg hemorrhagic fever's presentation are described below. Note that phases overlap due to variability between cases.

1. Incubation: 2–21 days, averaging 5–9 days.

   [^cite_note-clini-10]

2. Generalization Phase: Day 1 up to Day 5 from the onset of clinical symptoms. MHF presents with a high fever 104 °F (\~40˚C) and a sudden, severe headache, with accompanying chills, fatigue, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, pharyngitis, maculopapular rash, abdominal pain, [conjunctivitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunctivitis), and [malaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaise).

   [^cite_note-clini-10]

3. Early Organ Phase: Day 5 up to Day 13. Symptoms include prostration, [dyspnea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyspnea), [edema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edema), [conjunctival injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunctival_injection), viral [exanthema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exanthema), and CNS symptoms, including encephalitis, confusion, delirium, apathy, and aggression. Hemorrhagic symptoms typically occur late and herald the end of the early organ phase, leading either to eventual recovery or worsening and death. Symptoms include bloody stools, [ecchymoses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecchymoses), blood leakage from [venipuncture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venipuncture) sites, mucosal and visceral hemorrhaging, and possibly [hematemesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematemesis).

   [^cite_note-clini-10]

4. Late Organ Phase: Day 13 up to Day 21+. Symptoms bifurcate into two constellations for survivors and fatal cases. Survivors will enter a convalescence phase, experiencing [myalgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myalgia), [fibromyalgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibromyalgia), [hepatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis), [asthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthenia), ocular symptoms, and [psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosis). Fatal cases continue to deteriorate, experiencing continued fever, [obtundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtundation), [coma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coma), [convulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convulsions), diffuse [coagulopathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coagulopathy), metabolic disturbances, [shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_\(circulatory\)) and death, with death typically occurring between days 8 and 16.

   [^cite_note-clini-10]

The WHO also writes that at the phase of gastrointestinal symptoms' predomination, "the appearance of patients...has been described as showing 'ghost-like' drawn features, deep-set eyes, expressionless faces, and extreme lethargy."[^cite_note-11]

MVD is caused by two viruses; [Marburg virus (MARV)](33134774.md) and [Ravn virus (RAVV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravn_virus), family Filoviridae.[^cite_note-12]<sup>: 458 </sup>

Marburgviruses are endemic in [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid) [woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland) of [equatorial Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Africa).[^cite_note-Peterson2004-13][^cite_note-Pinzon2005-14][^cite_note-Peterson2006-15] Most marburgvirus infections were repeatedly associated with people visiting natural [caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave) or working in [mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining). In 2009, the successful isolation of infectious MARV and RAVV was reported from healthy [Egyptian fruit bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_fruit_bat) caught in caves.[^cite_note-Kortepeter2020-4][^cite_note-Towner2009-16] This isolation strongly suggests that [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World) [fruit bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabat) are involved in the natural maintenance of marburgviruses and that visiting bat-infested caves is a risk factor for acquiring marburgvirus infections. Further studies are necessary to establish whether Egyptian rousettes are the actual hosts of MARV and RAVV or whether they get infected via contact with another animal and therefore serve only as intermediate hosts. Another risk factor is contact with nonhuman primates, although only one outbreak of MVD (in 1967) was due to contact with infected monkeys.[^cite_note-Siegert1967-17]

Contrary to [Ebola virus disease (EVD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_disease), which has been associated with heavy [rains](19009110.md) after long periods of dry weather,[^cite_note-Pinzon2005-14][^cite_note-18] triggering factors for spillover of marburgviruses into the human population have not yet been described.

## Transmission

The details of the initial transmission of MVD to humans remain incompletely understood. Transmission most likely occurs from [Egyptian fruit bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_fruit_bat) or another natural host, such as [non-human primates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-human_primate) or through the consumption of [bushmeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushmeat), but the specific routes and body fluids involved are unknown. Human-to-human transmission of MVD occurs through direct contact with infected bodily fluids such as blood.[^cite_note-Kortepeter2020-4] Transmission events are relatively rare – there have been only 11 recorded outbreaks of MARV between 1975 and 2011, with one event involving both MARV and RAVV.[^cite_note-19]

## Diagnosis



Marburg virus liver injury

MVD is clinically indistinguishable from [Ebola virus disease (EVD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_disease), and it can also easily be confused with many other diseases prevalent in [Equatorial Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Africa), such as other [viral hemorrhagic fevers](1719309.md), [falciparum malaria](20423.md), [typhoid fever](31596.md), [shigellosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigellosis), [rickettsial diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickettsia) such as [typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhus), [cholera](7591.md), [gram-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram-negative_bacteria) [sepsis](158400.md), [borreliosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borreliosis) such as [relapsing fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relapsing_fever) or [EHEC enteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verotoxin-producing_Escherichia_coli). Other infectious diseases that ought to be included in the [differential diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_diagnosis) include [leptospirosis](649760.md), [scrub typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrub_typhus), [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_\(disease\)), [Q fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q_fever), [candidiasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidiasis), [histoplasmosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histoplasmosis), [trypanosomiasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trypanosomiasis), [visceral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visceral) [leishmaniasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leishmaniasis), hemorrhagic [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox), [measles](58911.md), and fulminant [viral hepatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_hepatitis). Non-infectious diseases that can be confused with MVD are [acute promyelocytic leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_promyelocytic_leukemia), [hemolytic uremic syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemolytic_uremic_syndrome), [snake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake) [envenomation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envenomation), [clotting factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coagulation) deficiencies/platelet disorders, [thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrombotic_thrombocytopenic_purpura), [hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_hemorrhagic_telangiectasia), [Kawasaki disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawasaki_disease), and even [warfarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warfarin) intoxication.[^cite_note-Gear1989-20][^cite_note-Gear1978-21][^cite_note-Grolla2005-22][^cite_note-Bogomolov1998-23]

The most important indicator that may lead to the suspicion of MVD at clinical examination is the [medical history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_history) of the patient, in particular the travel and occupational history (which countries and caves were visited) and the patient's exposure to wildlife (exposure to bats or bat excrements). MVD can be confirmed by isolation of marburgviruses from or by detection of marburgvirus antigen or genomic or subgenomic RNAs in patient [blood](3997.md) or [serum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_serum) samples during the acute phase of MVD. Marburgvirus isolation is usually performed by [inoculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inoculation) of [grivet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grivet) kidney epithelial [Vero E6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vero_cell) or MA-104 [cell cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_culture) or by inoculation of human adrenal carcinoma SW-13 cells, all of which react to infection with characteristic [cytopathic effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytopathic_effect).[^cite_note-Hofmann1968-24][^cite_note-25] Filovirions can easily be visualized and identified in cell culture by [electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscopy) due to their unique filamentous shapes, but electron microscopy cannot differentiate the various filoviruses alone despite some overall length differences.[^cite_note-Geisbert1995-26] [Immunofluorescence assays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunofluorescence) are used to confirm marburgvirus presence in cell cultures. During an outbreak, virus isolation and electron microscopy are most often not feasible options. The most common diagnostic methods are therefore [RT-PCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_transcription_polymerase_chain_reaction)[^cite_note-Gibb2001-27][^cite_note-Drosten2002-28][^cite_note-Weidmann2004-29][^cite_note-Zhai2007-30][^cite_note-Weidmann2007-31] in conjunction with [antigen-capture ELISA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELISA),[^cite_note-Saijo2005-32][^cite_note-Saijo2006-1-33][^cite_note-Saijo2006-2-34] which can be performed in field or mobile hospitals and laboratories. [Indirect immunofluorescence assays (IFAs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunofluorescence) are not used for diagnosis of MVD in the field anymore.

### Classification

Marburg virus disease (MVD) is the official name listed in the [World Health Organization](33583.md)'s [International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Classification_of_Diseases_and_Related_Health_Problems) 10 (ICD-10) for the human disease caused by any of the two marburgviruses; [Marburg virus](33134774.md) (MARV) and [Ravn virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravn_virus) (RAVV). In the scientific literature, Marburg hemorrhagic fever (MHF) is often used as an unofficial alternative name for the same disease. Both disease names are derived from the [German](11867.md) city [Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg), where MARV was first discovered.[^cite_note-Siegert1967-17]

## Prevention

Main article: [Prevention of viral hemorrhagic fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevention_of_viral_hemorrhagic_fever)

Marburgviruses are highly [infectious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infection), but not very [contagious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contagious_disease). They do not get transmitted by [aerosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol) during natural MVD outbreaks. Due to the absence of an approved vaccine, prevention of MVD therefore relies predominantly on [quarantine](25237.md) of confirmed or high probability cases, proper [personal protective equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment), and [sterilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterilization_\(microbiology\)) and [disinfection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinfection).

### Vaccine development

Main article: [Marburg vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg_vaccine)

There are currently no [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md)-approved [vaccines](32653.md) for the prevention of MVD. Many candidate vaccines have been developed and tested in various animal models.[^cite_note-Garbutt2004-35][^cite_note-Daddario-DiCaprio2006-36][^cite_note-Swenson2008-37] Of those, the most promising ones are [DNA vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_vaccination)[^cite_note-Riemenschneider2003-38] or based on [Venezuelan equine encephalitis virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_equine_encephalitis_virus) [replicons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replicon_\(genetics\)),[^cite_note-Hevey1998-39] [vesicular stomatitis Indiana virus (VSIV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicular_stomatitis_virus)[^cite_note-Daddario-DiCaprio2006-36][^cite_note-Jones2005-40] or [filovirus-like particles (VLPs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus-like_particle)[^cite_note-Swenson2008-37] as all of these candidates could protect nonhuman primates from marburgvirus-induced disease. DNA vaccines have entered clinical trials.[^cite_note-NIAIDVaccineDevelopment-41]

There is not yet an approved vaccine, because of [economic factors in vaccine development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics_of_vaccines), and because filoviruses killed few before the 2010s.[^cite_note-42]

### Endemic zones

The natural maintenance hosts of marburgviruses remain to be identified unequivocally. However, the isolation of both MARV and RAVV from [bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat) and the association of several MVD outbreaks with bat-infested mines or caves strongly suggests that bats are involved in Marburg virus transmission to humans. Avoidance of contact with bats and abstaining from visits to caves is highly recommended, but may not be possible for those working in mines or people dependent on bats as a food source.

### During outbreaks

Since marburgviruses are not spread via aerosol, the most straightforward prevention method during MVD outbreaks is to avoid direct (skin-to-skin) contact with patients, their [excretions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excretion) and [body fluids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_fluids), and any possibly [contaminated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contamination) materials and utensils. Patients should be isolated, but still are safe to be visited by family members. Medical staff should be trained in and apply strict barrier nursing techniques (disposable face mask, gloves, goggles, and a gown at all times). Traditional [burial](177497.md) rituals, especially those requiring [embalming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embalming) of bodies, should be discouraged or modified, ideally with the help of local [traditional healers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_healer).[^cite_note-43]

### In the laboratory

Marburgviruses are [World Health Organization](33583.md) Risk Group 4 Pathogens, requiring [Biosafety Level 4-equivalent containment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosafety),[^cite_note-BMBL5-44] laboratory researchers have to be properly trained in BSL-4 practices and wear proper personal protective equipment.

## Treatment

There is currently no effective marburgvirus-specific [therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therapy) for MVD. Treatment is primarily supportive in nature and includes minimizing invasive procedures, balancing fluids and [electrolytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolyte) to counter [dehydration](155056.md), administration of [anticoagulants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticoagulants) early in infection to prevent or control [disseminated intravascular coagulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disseminated_intravascular_coagulation), administration of [procoagulants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coagulation) late in infection to control [hemorrhaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeding), maintaining [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) levels, [pain management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_management), and administration of [antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibacterial) or [antifungals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifungal_medication) to treat secondary infections.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

## Prognosis

Although supportive care can improve survival chances, Marburg virus disease is fatal in the majority of cases. The [case fatality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_fatality_rate) has been estimated to be 61.9%.[^cite_note-lid-47]

### 1967 outbreak

Main article: [1967 Marburg virus disease outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Marburg_virus_disease_outbreak)

MVD was first documented in 1967, when 31 people became ill in the [German](11867.md) towns of [Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg) and [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main), and in [Belgrade](55904.md), [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). The outbreak involved 25 primary MARV infections and seven deaths, and six nonlethal secondary cases. The outbreak was traced to infected [grivets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grivet) (species *Chlorocebus aethiops*) imported from an undisclosed location in [Uganda](31816.md) and used in developing [poliomyelitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliomyelitis) [vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccines). The monkeys were received by Behringwerke, a Marburg company founded by the first winner of the [Nobel Prize in Medicine](52502.md), [Emil von Behring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_von_Behring). The company, which at the time was owned by [Hoechst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoechst_AG), was originally set up to develop [sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_plasma) against [tetanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetanus) and [diphtheria](58937.md). Primary infections occurred in Behringwerke [laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory) staff while working with grivet tissues or tissue cultures without adequate [personal protective equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment). Secondary cases involved two [physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician), a [nurse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurse), a post-mortem attendant, and the wife of a [veterinarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterinarian). All secondary cases had direct contact, usually involving blood, with a primary case. Both physicians became infected through accidental skin pricks when drawing blood from patients.[^cite_note-Kissling1968-68][^cite_note-Bonin1969-69][^cite_note-Jacob1971-70][^cite_note-Stojkovic1971-71]

### 1975 cases

In 1975, an Australian tourist became infected with MARV in [Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesia) (today [Zimbabwe](34399.md)). He died in a hospital in [Johannesburg](58893.md), [South Africa](17416221.md). His girlfriend and an attending nurse were subsequently infected with MVD, but survived.[^cite_note-Gear1975-72][^cite_note-Gear1977-73][^cite_note-Conrad1978-74]

### 1980 cases

A case of MARV infection occurred in 1980 in [Kenya](188171.md). A French man, who worked as an electrical engineer in a sugar factory in Nzoia (close to [Bungoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungoma)) at the base of [Mount Elgon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Elgon) (which contains [Kitum Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitum_Cave)), became infected by unknown means and died on 15 January shortly after admission to [Nairobi](21482.md) Hospital.[^cite_note-75] The attending physician contracted MVD, but survived.[^cite_note-Smith1982-76] A popular science account of these cases can be found in [Richard Preston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Preston)'s book *[The Hot Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hot_Zone)* (the French man is referred to under the [pseudonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym) "Charles Monet", whereas the physician is identified under his real name, Shem Musoke).[^cite_note-Preston1994-77]

### 1987 case

In 1987, a single lethal case of RAVV infection occurred in a 15-year-old Danish boy, who spent his vacation in [Kisumu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisumu), [Kenya](188171.md). He had visited [Kitum Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitum_Cave) on [Mount Elgon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Elgon) prior to travelling to [Mombasa](200152.md), where he developed clinical signs of infection. The boy died after transfer to [Nairobi](21482.md) Hospital.[^cite_note-Johnson1996-78] A popular science account of this case can be found in [Richard Preston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Preston)'s book *[The Hot Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hot_Zone)* (the boy is referred to under the [pseudonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym) "Peter Cardinal").[^cite_note-Preston1994-77]

### 1988 laboratory infection

In 1988, researcher Nikolai Ustinov infected himself lethally with MARV after accidentally pricking himself with a syringe used for inoculation of [guinea pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_pig). The accident occurred at the Scientific-Production Association "Vektor" (today the [State Research Center of Virology and Biotechnology "Vektor"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Research_Center_of_Virology_and_Biotechnology_VECTOR)) in [Koltsovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koltsovo,_Novosibirsk_Oblast), [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) (today [Russia](25391.md)).[^cite_note-Beer1999-79] Very little information is publicly available about this MVD case because Ustinov's experiments were classified. A popular science account of this case can be found in [Ken Alibek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Alibek)'s book *Biohazard*.[^cite_note-Alibek1999-80]

### 1990 laboratory infection

Another laboratory accident occurred at the Scientific-Production Association "Vektor" (today the [State Research Center of Virology and Biotechnology "Vektor"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Research_Center_of_Virology_and_Biotechnology_VECTOR)) in [Koltsovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koltsovo,_Novosibirsk_Oblast), [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR), when a scientist contracted MARV by unknown means.[^cite_note-Nikiforov1994-81]

### 1998–2000 outbreak

A major MVD outbreak occurred among illegal [gold miners](1291393.md) around Goroumbwa mine in [Durba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durba,_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) and [Watsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watsa), [Democratic Republic of Congo](76762.md) from 1998 to 2000, when co-circulating MARV and RAVV caused 154 cases of MVD and 128 deaths. The outbreak ended with the flooding of the mine.[^cite_note-Bausch2006-5][^cite_note-Bertherat1999-82][^cite_note-Bausch2003-83]

### 2004–2005 outbreak

In early 2005, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) began investigating an outbreak of [viral hemorrhagic fever](1719309.md) in [Angola](701.md), which was centered in the northeastern [Uíge Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%C3%ADge_Province) but also affected many other provinces. The Angolan government had to ask for international assistance, as there were only approximately 1,200 doctors in the entire country and provinces that had few as two. Health care workers also complained about a shortage of basic [personal protective equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment). [Médecins Sans Frontières](20498.md) (MSF) reported that when their team arrived at the provincial hospital at the center of the outbreak, they found it operating without water and electricity. [Contact tracing](1724851.md) was complicated by the fact that the country's roads and other infrastructure were devastated after nearly three decades of [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_War_of_Independence) and the countryside remained littered with [land mines](18172.md).[^cite_note-Roddy2010-84]

Americo Boa Vida Hospital in the Angolan capital, [Luanda](18402.md), set up a special isolation ward to treat patients from the countryside. Due to the high fatality rate of MVD, some people came to be suspicious of and hostile towards hospitals and medical workers. For instance, a specially-equipped isolation ward at the provincial hospital in Uíge was reported to be empty during much of the epidemic, even though the facility was at the center of the outbreak. WHO was forced to implement what it described as a "harm reduction strategy" by distributing disinfectants to affected families who refused hospital care. Of the 252 people who contracted MVD, 227 died.[^cite_note-Roddy2010-84][^cite_note-Hovette2005-85][^cite_note-Ndayimirije2005-86][^cite_note-Towner2006-87][^cite_note-Jeffs2007-88][^cite_note-Roddy2007-89][^cite_note-Roddy2009-90]

### 2007 cases

In 2007, four [miners](219353.md) became infected with marburgviruses in [Kamwenge District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamwenge_District), [Uganda](31816.md). The first case, a 29-year-old man, became symptomatic on July 4, 2007, was admitted to a hospital on July 7, and died on July 13. Contact tracing revealed that the man had had prolonged close contact with two colleagues (a 22-year-old man and a 23-year-old man), who experienced clinical signs of infection before his disease onset. Both men had been admitted to hospitals in June and survived their infections, which were proven to be due to MARV. A fourth, 25-year-old man, developed MVD clinical signs in September and was shown to be infected with RAVV. He also survived the infection.[^cite_note-Towner2009-16][^cite_note-Adjemian2011-91]

### 2008 cases

On July 10, 2008, the [Netherlands National Institute for Public Health and the Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_National_Institute_for_Public_Health_and_the_Environment) reported that a 41-year-old Dutch woman, who had visited Python Cave in [Maramagambo Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maramagambo_Forest) during her holiday in [Uganda](31816.md), had MVD due to MARV infection, and had been admitted to a hospital in the [Netherlands](21148.md). The woman died under treatment in the [Leiden University Medical Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University_Medical_Centre) in [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden) on July 11. The Ugandan Ministry of Health closed the cave after this case.[^cite_note-Timen2009-92] On January 9 of that year an infectious diseases physician notified the Colorado Department of Public Health and the Environment that a 44-year-old American woman who had returned from [Uganda](31816.md) had been hospitalized with a [fever of unknown origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fever_of_unknown_origin). At the time, serologic testing was negative for [viral hemorrhagic fever](1719309.md). She was discharged on January 19, 2008. After the death of the Dutch patient and the discovery that the American woman had visited Python Cave, further testing confirmed the patient demonstrated MARV [antibodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibodies) and [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA).[^cite_note-CDC2009-93]

### 2017 Uganda outbreak



Kween District in Uganda

Main article: [2017 Uganda Marburg virus outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Uganda_Marburg_virus_outbreak)

In October 2017 an outbreak of Marburg virus disease was detected in [Kween District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kween_District), Eastern Uganda. All three initial cases (belonging to one family – two brothers and one sister) had died by 3 November. The fourth case – a health care worker – developed symptoms on 4 November and was admitted to a hospital. The first confirmed case traveled to [Kenya](188171.md) before the death. A close contact of the second confirmed case traveled to [Kampala](56689.md). It is reported that several hundred people may have been exposed to infection.[^cite_note-who2017-94][^cite_note-newsweek2017-95]

### 2021 Guinean cases

Main article: [2021 Guinea Marburg virus disease outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Guinea_Marburg_virus_disease_outbreak)

In August 2021, two months after the re-emergent Ebola epidemic in the [Guéckédou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%A9ck%C3%A9dou) prefecture was declared over, a case of the Marburg disease was confirmed by health authorities through laboratory analysis.[^cite_note-mvdwho-57] Other potential case of the disease in a contact awaits official results. This was the first case of the Marburg hemorrhagic fever confirmed to happen in West Africa. The case of Marburg also has been identified in [Guéckédou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%A9ck%C3%A9dou_Prefecture).[^cite_note-reuters-56] During the outbreak, a total of one confirmed case, who died ([CFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_fatality_rate)=100%), and 173 contacts were identified, including 14 high-risk contacts based on exposure.[^cite_note-\:0-96] Among them, 172 were followed for a period of 21 days, of which none developed symptoms. One high-risk contact was lost to follow up.[^cite_note-\:0-96] Sequencing of an isolate from the Guinean patient showed that this outbreak was caused by the Angola-like Marburg virus.[^cite_note-97] A colony of [Egyptian rousettus bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_fruit_bat) ([reservoir host](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_reservoir) of [Marburg virus](33134774.md)) was found in close proximity (4.5 km) to the village, where the Marburg virus disease outbreak emerged in 2021.[^cite_note-\:1-98] Two sampled fruit bats from this colony were PCR-positive on the Marburg virus.[^cite_note-\:1-98]

### 2022 Ghanaian cases

Main article: [2022 Ghana Marburg virus disease outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ghana_Marburg_virus_disease_outbreak)

In July 2022, preliminary analysis of samples taken from two patients – both deceased – in [Ghana](12067.md) indicated the cases were positive for Marburg. However, per standard procedure, the samples were sent to the [Pasteur Institute of Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasteur_Institute_of_Dakar) for confirmation.[^cite_note-99] On 17 July 2022 the two cases were confirmed by Ghana,[^cite_note-100] which caused the country to declare a Marburg virus disease outbreak.[^cite_note-101] An additional case was identified, bringing the total to three.[^cite_note-102]

### 2023 Equatorial Guinea outbreak

Main article: [2023 Equatorial Guinea Marburg virus disease outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Equatorial_Guinea_Marburg_virus_disease_outbreak)

A disease outbreak was first reported in [Equatorial Guinea](9366.md) on 7 February 2023, and on 13 February 2023, it was identified as being Marburg virus disease. It was the first time the disease was detected in the country.[^cite_note-103] Neighbouring [Cameroon](5447.md) detected two suspected cases of Marburg virus disease on 13 February 2023,[^cite_note-104] but they were later ruled out.[^cite_note-105] On 25 February, a suspected case of Marburg was reported in the Spanish city of [Valencia](63861.md),[^cite_note-Spain-106] however this case was subsequently discounted.[^cite_note-107] As of 4 April 2023, there were 14 confirmed cases and 28 suspected cases, including ten confirmed deaths from the disease in Equatorial Guinea.[^cite_note-BNO-108][^cite_note-cidrap1-61] On 8 June 2023, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) declared the outbreak over.[^cite_note-109] In total, 17 laboratory-confirmed cases and 12 deaths were recorded. All the 23 probable cases reportedly died. Four patients recovered from the virus and have been enrolled in a survivors programme to receive psychosocial and other post-recovery support.[^cite_note-110]

### 2023 Tanzania outbreak

Main article: [2023 Tanzania Marburg virus disease outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Tanzania_Marburg_virus_disease_outbreak)

A Marburg virus disease outbreak in [Tanzania](30118.md) was first reported on 21 March 2023 by the Ministry of Health of Tanzania.[^cite_note-111] This was the first time that Tanzania had reported an outbreak of the disease. On 2 June 2023, Tanzania declared the outbreak over.[^cite_note-112] There were 9 total infections, resulting in 6 total deaths.[^cite_note-tanz-63][^cite_note-tanz2-64]

### 2024 Rwanda outbreak

Further information: [Rwanda Marburg virus disease outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda_Marburg_virus_disease_outbreak)

On September 27, 2024, an outbreak of the [Marburg virus](33134774.md) was confirmed in [Rwanda](25645.md). As of September 29, 2024, six deaths and twenty cases had been confirmed. The Rwandan Minister of Health, [Sabin Nsanzimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabin_Nsanzimana), confirmed that the infected were mostly healthcare workers and that [contact tracing](1724851.md) had been initiated in the country.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

## Research

Experimentally, recombinant [vesicular stomatitis Indiana virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicular_stomatitis_virus) (VSIV) expressing the glycoprotein of MARV has been used successfully in nonhuman primate models as post-exposure prophylaxis.[^cite_note-115] A vaccine candidate has been effective in nonhuman primates.[^cite_note-116] Experimental therapeutic regimens relying on [antisense technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisense_therapy) have shown promise, with [phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morpholino) (PMOs) targeting the MARV genome[^cite_note-117] New therapies from [Sarepta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarepta_Therapeutics)[^cite_note-118] and Tekmira[^cite_note-119] have also been successfully used in humans as well as primates.
