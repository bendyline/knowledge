The Philippines is one of the state opponents of the militant group, the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL), more commonly referred to by the local media as the Islamic State of Iraq and Syria (ISIS).

The local [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) groups through which ISIL maintains operations include the [Maute group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maute_group), [Abu Sayyaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sayyaf), the [Bangsamoro Islamic Freedom Fighters](39963003.md) and [Ansar Khalifa Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_Khalifa_Philippines). They follow the school of thought of [Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Abd_al-Wahhab), commonly referred to as [Wahhabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The groups pledged allegiance to ISIL in 2014 or the following years.

ISIL has been linked to increased suicide bombings by Filipino nationals in 2018 and 2019. This method has rarely been carried out in the Philippines; the few bombings successfully carried out were done by foreigners. The group was suspected to be involved in the [2018 Lamitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Lamitan_bombing), [2019 Jolo Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Jolo_Cathedral_bombings) and [Indanan bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indanan_bombings).[^cite_note-suicide-9]

## Main events timeline

Main article: [Timeline of the Moro conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Moro_conflict)

### 2014

On July 23, Abu Sayyaf leader [Isnilon Hapilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isnilon_Hapilon) pledged allegiance to ISIL through a video posted on [YouTube](3524766.md).[^cite_note-10] This is an indication of ISIL presence in the Philippines.[^cite_note-11]

### 2015

In April, the Wahhabi Maute group pledged allegiance to [ISIL](9087364.md) along with the [Ansar Khalifa Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_Khalifa_Philippines) terrorist organization, vowing to provide support for each other.[^cite_note-12] The Maute Group was a strong manifestation of the rise of family terrorism in the Philippines.[^cite_note-13]

On November 16, when the APEC Summit was to be held in Manila, a video of men in masks with ISIL black flag behind them is posted on Facebook, claiming "ISIL in Mindanao" will attack the summit.[^cite_note-14]

### 2017

**May 23**

A video discovered on a cellphone seized by [AFP](23448.md) during a raid on a safe house in Marawi shows the militants including [Hapilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isnilon_Hapilon) and Maute brothers were planning an attack on [Marawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marawi_City).[^cite_note-15] The attack was the 4th step for them to gain the approval of the ISIL leadership: "*requires the conduct of widespread atrocities and uprisings all across Mindanao.*"[^cite_note-16]

**June 1**

Eight foreign militants had been killed in Marawi, five of which they have identified as [Malaysian](3607937.md), [Indonesian](14579.md), [Saudi Arabian](349303.md), [Yemeni](350939.md) and a [Chechen](6095.md).[^cite_note-17]

October 16

[Isnilon Hapilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isnilon_Hapilon) and [Omar Maute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Maute) was reportedly killed.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

**October 19**

The Malaysian terrorist [Mahmud Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Ahmad) who helped finance the Marawi siege and recruit foreign fighters[^cite_note-20] was killed.[^cite_note-21]

### 2023

**September 21**

Three farmers were killed in a gun attack in Kauswagan, Lanao del Norte.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

**December 3**

ISIS claimed responsibility for the [Mindanao State University bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao_State_University_bombing).[^cite_note-24]

### 2024

**April 19**

Philippine forces killed an Islamic State militant, who had been implicated in past beheadings, [including of 10 Filipino marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Basilan_beheading_incident) and two kidnapped Vietnamese, in a clash in the southern Philippines.

**December 17**

Three members of the Intelligence Service of the Armed Forces of the Philippines (ISAFP) were killed in an ambush in Basilan province, authorities said Tuesday.[^cite_note-25]

### 2026

**January 30**

On January 30, 2026, the [Islamic State – East Asia Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sayyaf) claimed an ambush that killed 4 soldiers. This is the group's first claimed attack since October 2024.[^cite_note-26]

## ISIL's support

In March 2016, training manuals, bandanas with ISIL inscriptions, and other documents for militants under the ISIL were recovered after the military captured a Maute group camp, indicating that the group may be trying to link up with ISIL.[^cite_note-27]

On June 21, 2016, ISIL released a video entitled "The Solid Structure", which recognized Abu Sayyaf leader Hapilon as the [mujahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) authorized to lead the [jihadists](7826589.md) in the Philippines, and designated him as the [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) for [Southeast Asia](28741.md). The video also urged aspiring members who can't go to the Middle East to fight for ISIL in the Philippines instead.[^cite_note-28]

In August 2017, another video released by ISIL asked would-be fighters to go to the Philippines, especially to Marawi City, where militants were [under siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marawi) from government forces.[^cite_note-29]

Involvement of Filipino citizens in ISIL has been reported as early as 2014.[^cite_note-30] According to the *[Daily Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mail)* citing undisclosed Kurdish sources, a Filipino national was among the ISIL members who appeared in a beheading video of American aid worker [Peter Kassig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kassig) and 18 Syrian soldiers uploaded to [YouTube](3524766.md). The Philippine military said that the report could not be verified and said that there was no ISIL recruitment in the Philippines at that time. The [Department of Foreign Affairs](4718612.md) during this time had been receiving unverified reports of Filipinos training to fight for ISIL in Syria.[^cite_note-suspected-31]

In June 2016, ISIL released a video where three of its members, a Filipino, an Indonesian and a Malaysian urged aspiring members who can't go to the Middle East to fight for ISIL in the Philippines instead. In January 2017, Rappler reported that the Filipino member was identified as Mohammad Reza Kiram, a 21-year-old who was the first verified member in ISIL fighting in Syria.[^cite_note-32]

## Public opinion on ISIL

In a poll conducted between February 16 to May 8, 2017, the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) said that 70% of Filipinos questioned view ISIL as a major threat to the Philippines ahead of [global climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_climate_change) (65%) and [cyberattacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberattacks) (64%).[^cite_note-34]
