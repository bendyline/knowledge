The **Minister of National Defense** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 국방부장관) of [South Korea](27019.md) is the leader and chief executive of the [Ministry of National Defense](5985967.md). The minister is responsible for implementing South Korea's national defense policy, and is a member of the [State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_\(South_Korea\)). The minister of national defense is appointed by the [president](98630.md) and is confirmed upon a hearing by the [National Assembly](601423.md).

Under the principle of [civil control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_control_of_the_military), Article 87 of the [South Korean Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korean_Constitution) mandates that no current member of the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_forces_of_South_Korea) can become a member of the State Council.[^cite_note-1] However, [South Korean law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korean_law) does not instate a required period of hiatus between retirement from military service and appointment to the position of defense minister, resulting in a majority of past ministers being 'quasi-civilians' who were appointed after recent relief from active duty.[^cite_note-2]<sup>: 125 </sup>

The current minister of national defense is [Ahn Gyu-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahn_Gyu-back), who was appointed by president [Lee Jae Myung](50305655.md) on June 24, 2025. He succeeded former defense minister [Kim Yong-hyun](78510434.md), who resigned on 5 December 2024 following his involvement in the [2024 South Korean martial law crisis](78508419.md).[^cite_note-3] Ahn is considered the first civilian defence minister since the [May 16 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_16_coup) in 1961.[^cite_note-4]
