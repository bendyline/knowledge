**Novi Sad** ([Serbian Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Cyrillic_alphabet): Нови Сад, pronounced [\[nôʋiː sâːd\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Serbo-Croatian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/37/NoviSad.ogg/NoviSad.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/37/NoviSad.ogg/NoviSad.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; see [§ Etymology](#Etymology) for other names) is the [second-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Serbia) in Serbia and the administrative center of the autonomous province of [Vojvodina](176801.md). It is located in the southern portion of the [Pannonian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Plain) on the border of the [Bačka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%8Dka) and [Syrmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrmia) geographical regions, lying on the banks of the [Danube](21209639.md) river, and facing the northern slopes of [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora). According to the 2022 census, the population of Novi Sad city proper stands at 260,438, its contiguous urban area has 325,511 inhabitants, and the population of its administrative area totals 372,136 people.[^cite_note-Municipalities_and_regions_of_the_Republic_of_Serbia_2025-1]<sup>: 18 </sup> It is the fifth largest [city on the Danube river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_on_the_river_Danube) and the largest that is not a national capital.

Novi Sad was founded in 1694, when [Serb](59512.md) merchants formed a colony across the Danube from the [Petrovaradin Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin_Fortress), a strategic [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg) military post. In subsequent centuries, it became an important trading, manufacturing and cultural centre, and has historically been dubbed *the Serbian Athens*.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The city was heavily devastated in the [1848 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848_in_the_Habsburg_areas), but was subsequently rebuilt and restored. Today, along with the Serbian capital city of Belgrade, Novi Sad is an industrial and financial center important to the [Serbian economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Serbia).

Novi Sad was the [European Youth Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Youth_Capital) in 2019 and a [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) in 2022.[^cite_note-5] It became a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [Creative City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) of [Media Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Media_Arts) in 2023.[^cite_note-UnescoCCN-6][^cite_note-euronewsCCN-7]

## Etymology

The name *Novi Sad* means "new plantation" in [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language). Its Latin name, stemming from the establishment of Habsburg city rights, is *Neoplanta*. The official names of Novi Sad in local administration are:[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

- [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): Нови Сад / *Novi Sad*

- [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language): *Újvidék*

- [Slovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_language): *Nový Sad*

- [Pannonian Rusyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Rusyn_language): Нови-Сад

In both [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language) and [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language), which are official in provincial administration, the city is called *Novi Sad*. Historically, the city was called *Neusatz* and *Neusatz an der Donau* (translated as 'Novi Sad on the Danube') in German.

In its [wider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensu_lato) meaning, the name *new plantation* refers to the "City of Novi Sad", one of the city-level [administrative units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Serbia) of [Serbia](29265.md), which includes Novi Sad proper on the left bank of the Danube, the towns of Sremska Kamenica and Petrovaradin on the right bank and the extensive suburbs of the left bank. *Novi Sad* can also refer [strictly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensu_stricto) to only the urban areas of the city (Novi Sad proper and the towns of Sremska Kamenica and Petrovaradin), or only to the historical core on the left bank, i.e. Novi Sad proper excluding Sremska Kamenica and Petrovaradin.

Human habitation in the territory of present-day Novi Sad has been traced as far back as the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age). Several settlements and [necropolises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necropolis) dating to 5000 BC were unearthed during the construction of a new boulevard in [Avijatičarsko Naselje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avijaticarsko_Naselje).[^cite_note-13] A settlement was also identified on the right bank of the river [Danube](21209639.md) in present-day [Petrovaradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin).

In antiquity, the region was inhabited by [Celtic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_tribes), most notably the [Scordisci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scordisci). Celts had been present in the area since the 4th century BC and founded the first fortress on the right bank of the Danube. Later, in the 1st century BC, the region was conquered by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome). During Roman rule, a larger fortress was built in the 1st century, named *Cusum*, and included in the [Roman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Pannonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia).

In the 5th century, Cusum was devastated by [Hunnic invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns). By the end of the century, the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) had rebuilt the town and called it *Petrikon* or *Petrikov* ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Πέτρικον) after [Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter). [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) tribes such as the [Severians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severians), the [Obotrites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obotrites) and the [Serbs](59512.md) (including the subtribes of the [Braničevci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brani%C4%8Devci) and the [Timočani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timo%C4%8Dani)) settled the region around Novi Sad, mainly in the 6th and 7th centuries.[^cite_note-Sava_S_1998,_page_36-14] The Serbs absorbed the aforementioned Slavic groups as well as the [Paleo-Balkanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Southeastern_Europe) peoples of the region.[^cite_note-Sava_S_1998,_page_36-14]

In the Middle Ages, the area was controlled by the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths), [Gepids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gepids), [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Avars), [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks), [West Slavic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavs), again by the Byzantines, and finally by the [Hungarians](17106358.md). It was a part of the medieval [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) from its foundation in 1000 until the Ottoman invasion in the 16th century. Hungarians began to settle in the area, which before that time had been mostly populated by Slavs. The earliest known mention was as the Hungarian variant *Peturwarad* or *Pétervárad* (Serbian: *Petrovaradin*/Петроварадин), derived from the Byzantine variant, found in documents from 1237. That year, several other settlements were mentioned as existing in the territory of modern-day urban Novi Sad.

From the 13th century to the 16th century, the following settlements existed in the urban territory of the modern-day Novi Sad:[^cite_note-Curcin-15][^cite_note-Borovszky-16]

- on the right bank of the Danube: *Pétervárad* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *Petrovaradin*) and *Kamanc* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *Kamenica*).

- on the left bank of the Danube: *Baksa* or *Baksafalva* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *Bakša, Bakšić*), *Kűszentmárton* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *Sent Marton*), *Bivalyos* or *Bivalo* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *Bivaljoš, Bivalo*), *Vásárosvárad* or *Várad* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *Vašaroš Varad, Varadinci*), *Zajol I* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *[Sajlovo I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gornje_Livade,_Novi_Sad), Gornje Sajlovo, Gornje Isailovo*), *Zajol II* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *[Sajlovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sajlovo) II, Donje Sajlovo, Donje Isailovo*), *Bistritz* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *[Bistrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bistrica_\(Novo_Naselje\))*). Some other settlements existed in the suburbs of Novi Sad: *Mortályos* (Serbian: *Mrtvaljoš*), *Csenei* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *[Čenej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cenej)*), *Keménd* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *[Kamendin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamendin,_Sirig)*), *Rév* ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *Rivica*).

An etymology of settlement names reveals that some designations are of [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages) origin, which indicates that the areas were initially inhabited by Slavs, particularly the [West Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavs). For example, Bivalo (Bivaljoš) had a large Slavic settlement dating from the 5th–6th centuries.[^cite_note-Curcin-15] Other names are of [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language) origin (for example Bélakút, Kűszentmárton, Vásárosvárad, Rév), indicating that the settlements were inhabited by Hungarians before the [Ottoman invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_invasion?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the 16th century.[^cite_note-Borovszky-16] Some settlement names are of uncertain origin.

Tax records from 1522 show a mix of Hungarian and Slavic names among the inhabitants of these villages, including Slavic names like Bozso (Božo), Radovan, Radonya (Radonja), Ivo, etc. Following the Ottoman invasion in the 16th–17th centuries, some of these settlements were destroyed. Most of the surviving Hungarian inhabitants retreated from the area. Some of the settlements persisted under Ottoman rule and were populated by ethnic Serbs.

Between 1526 and 1687, the region was under [Ottoman](22278.md) rule. In 1590, the population of all villages in the territory of present-day Novi Sad numbered 105 houses, inhabited exclusively by Serbs. Ottoman records mention only those who paid taxes, so the number of Serbs who lived in the area (for example, those that served in the [Ottoman army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Ottoman_Empire)) was likely larger than was recorded.[^cite_note-17]

### Founding of the city



The [Battle of Petrovaradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Petrovaradin) (1716) was a major clash between [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) and Ottoman forces, significant for halting [Ottoman expansion](22278.md) into Central Europe and strengthening [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) control in the region.



[Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) prohibited Orthodox Christians to settle in [Petrovaradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin). That policy pushed Serbs to form the *Serb city* which later became Novi Sad. The city was proclaimed to be a free royal city by [Maria Theresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa) by 1748.

Habsburg rule was aligned with the Roman Catholic doctrine and, as it took over this area near the end of the 17th century, the government prohibited people of [Orthodox faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) from residing in [Petrovaradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin). Unable to build homes there, the Serbs of the area founded a new settlement in 1694 on the left bank of the Danube. They initially called it the 'Serb city' ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): *Srpski Grad*, [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *[Ratzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rascians) Stadt*). Another name used for the settlement was Petrovaradinski Šanac. In 1718, the inhabitants of the village of [Almaš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma%C5%A1) were resettled to Petrovaradinski Šanac, where they founded [Almaški Kraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma%C5%A1ki_Kraj) ('the Almaš quarter').

According to 1720 data, the population of Ratzen Stadt was composed of 112 Serbian, 14 German, and 5 Hungarian houses. The settlement officially gained the present names *Novi Sad* and *Újvidék* (*Neoplanta* in Latin) in 1748 when it became a 'free royal city', in German language it was called Neusatz.

The edict that made Novi Sad a 'free royal city' was proclaimed on 1 February 1748. The edict reads:

> ' We, [Maria Theresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa_of_Austria), by the grace of God [Holy Roman Empress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empress),\
> Queen of [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia), [Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravia), [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia), [Croatia](5573.md), [Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonia), [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prozor-Rama), [Serbia](29265.md), [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Central-Eastern_Europe\)), [Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodomeria), [Carinthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Carinthia), \[...]\
> cast this proclamation to anyone, whom it might concern... so that the renowned Petrovaradinski Šanac, which lies on the other side of the Danube in the [Bačka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%8Dka) province on the [Sajlovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sajlovo) land, by the might of our divine royal power and prestige...make this town a [Free Royal City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_imperial_city) and to fortify, accept and acknowledge it as one of the free royal cities of our [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) and other territories, by abolishing its previous name of Petrovaradinski Šanac, renaming it Neoplanta ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin)), Új-Vidégh ([Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language)), Neusatz (German) and Novi Sad ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language)) '

In the 18th century, the Habsburg monarchy recruited Germans from the southern principalities of the Holy Roman Empire to relocate to the Danube valley. They wanted both to increase the population and to redevelop the river valley for agriculture, which had declined markedly under the Ottomans. To encourage such settlement, the government ensured that the German communities could practice their religion (mostly Catholicism) and use their original German dialect.

### Habsburg monarchy



[Petrovaradin fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin_fortress) known as the "Gibraltar on the [Danube](21209639.md)," is a massive 18th-century, 112-hectare [fortification in Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fortifications_in_Serbia), renowned as one of Europe's best-preserved and second-largest, crucial for halting [Ottoman expansion into the Europe in 1716](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Turkish_War_\(1716%E2%80%931718\)).

For much of the 18th and 19th centuries, Novi Sad remained the largest city inhabited by Serbs. The reformer of the Serbian language, [Vuk Stefanović Karadžić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuk_Stefanovi%C4%87_Karad%C5%BEi%C4%87), wrote in 1817 that Novi Sad was the 'largest Serb municipality in the world'. It was a cultural and political centre for Serbs (see also [Serbian Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Revival)), who did not have their own [national state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state) at the time. Due to its cultural and political influence, the city became known as the 'Serbian [Athens](1216.md)' (*Srpska Atina* in Serbian). According to 1843 data, Novi Sad had 17,332 inhabitants, of whom 9,675 were [Orthodox Christians](10186.md), 5,724 [Catholics](606848.md), 1,032 [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants), 727 Jews, and 30 adherents of the [Armenian church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church). The largest [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) in the city were Serbs, and the second largest were [Germans](152735.md).

During the [Revolution of 1848–49](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848), Novi Sad was part of [Serbian Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Vojvodina), a Serbian [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_area) within the Austrian Empire. [In 1849](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Petrovaradin), the Hungarian garrison led by general [Pál Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1l_Kiss), located at the Petrovaradin Fortress, bombarded and devastated the city, which lost much of its population. According to the 1850 census, there were only 7,182 citizens left in the city, compared to 17,332 in 1843. [Marija Trandafil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marija_Trandafil) and her husband paid for some of the rebuilding including two churches.[^cite_note-nreview-18] Between 1849 and 1860, Novi Sad was part of a separate Austrian crownland known as the [Voivodeship of Serbia and Banat of Temeschwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeship_of_Serbia_and_Banat_of_Temeschwar). After the abolishment of this province, the city was included into the [Batsch-Bodrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A1cs-Bodrog_County) County. The post office was opened in 1853. Following the [compromise of 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compromise_of_1867), Novi Sad was located within the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), the *[Transleithania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transleithania)*, which comprised half of the new [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary). During this time, the [Magyarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyarization) policy of the Hungarian government drastically altered the demographic structure of the city as the formerly predominantly Serbian population became one with a more mixed character. In 1880, 41.2% of the city's inhabitants used the [Serbian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language) most frequently and 25.9% employed Hungarian. In the following decades, the percentage of Serbian-speakers decreased, while the number of Hungarian-speakers increased. According to the 1910 census, the city had 33,590 residents, of whom 13,343 (39.72%) spoke Hungarian, 11,594 (34.52%) Serbian, 5,918 (17.62%) German and 1,453 (4.33%) Slovak. It is not certain whether Hungarians or Serbs were the larger ethnic group in the city in 1910, since the various ethnic groups ([Bunjevci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunjevci), [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people), Jews, other [South Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Slavs) people, etc.) were classified in census results only according to the language they spoke.[^cite_note-19]

Similar demographic changes can be seen in the religious structure: in 1870, the population of Novi Sad included 8,134 Orthodox Christians, 6,684 Catholics, 1,725 Calvinists, 1,343 Lutherans, and others.[^cite_note-20] In 1910, the population included 13,383 [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholics) and 11,553 Orthodox Christians, while 3,089 declared themselves as [Lutheran](23371382.md), 2,751 as [Calvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinist), and 2,326 as Jewish.[^cite_note-21]

### Serbia and Yugoslavia



The [Prince Tomislav Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Tomislav_Bridge) in Novi Sad was a key [Danube](21209639.md) crossing built in the early 20th century and destroyed in 1941 during [World War II](32927.md).



[Trams in Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Novi_Sad) operated in the city from 1911 to 1958

On 25 November 1918, the [Assembly of Serbs, Bunjevci and other Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_People's_Assembly_of_Serbs,_Bunjevci_and_other_Slavs_in_Banat,_Ba%C4%8Dka_and_Baranja) of Vojvodina in Novi Sad proclaimed the union of the region of Vojvodina with the [Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia). From 1 December 1918, Novi Sad was part of the [Kingdom of Serbs, Croats, and Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia); and in 1929, it became the capital of the [Danube Banovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Banovina), a province of the newly named Kingdom of Yugoslavia. In 1921, the population of Novi Sad numbered 39,122 inhabitants, 16,293 of whom spoke the Serbian language, 12,991 Hungarian, 6,373 German, 1,117 Slovak, etc.[^cite_note-22]

In 1941, Yugoslavia was invaded and partitioned by the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers), and its northern parts, including Novi Sad, were annexed by [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_during_World_War_II). During [World War II](32927.md), about 5,000 citizens were murdered and many others were resettled. During the three days of the [Novi Sad raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1942_raid_in_Novi_Sad) (21–23 January 1942) alone, Hungarian police killed 1,246 citizens, among them more than 800 Jews, and threw their corpses into the icy waters of the Danube.

The total death toll of the raid was around 2,500.[^cite_note-Cesarani-23][^cite_note-Sajti-24] Citizens of all nationalities—Serbs, Hungarians, Slovaks, and others—fought together against the Axis authorities.[^cite_note-Sajti-24] In 1975 the whole city was awarded the title [People's Hero of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_People's_Hero).

The [Yugoslav Partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans) of [Syrmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrmia) and [Bačka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%8Dka) entered the city on 23 October 1944. During the military administration of [Banat, Bačka and Baranja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat,_Ba%C4%8Dka_and_Baranja) (17 October 1944 – 27 January 1945), the Partisans [killed tens of thousands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944%E2%80%931945_killings_in_Vojvodina), mostly Serbs, Germans, and Hungarians, who were perceived as opponents to the new regime.[^cite_note-25]

Novi Sad became part of the new [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). Since 1945, Novi Sad has been the capital of [Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Autonomous_Province_of_Vojvodina), a province of the [Republic of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Serbia). The city went through rapid industrialization and its population more than doubled in the period between World War II and the breakup of Yugoslavia in the early 1990s.



[*The Family*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_victims_of_the_raid_in_Novi_Sad) by [Jovan Soldatović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jovan_Soldatovi%C4%87), Monument dedicated to the victims of the [Novi Sad raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_raid), which took place during the Hungarian occupation in WWII.

After 1992, Novi Sad became a part of the [Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). Devastated by [NATO bombardment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NATO_bombing_of_Yugoslavia) during the [Kosovo War](16760.md) of 1999, [Novi Sad was left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NATO_bombing_of_Novi_Sad) without any of its three Danube bridges ([Žeželj Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDe%C5%BEelj_Bridge), [Varadin Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varadin_Bridge) and [Liberty Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Bridge,_Novi_Sad)), communications, water, and electricity. Residential areas were cluster-bombed several times while the [oil refinery](195137.md) was bombarded daily, causing severe pollution and widespread ecological damage. In 2003, FR Yugoslavia was transformed into the [state union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_union) of [Serbia and Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro). These two states separated in June 2006 (following the May 2006 [Montenegrin independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrin_independence_referendum,_2006)), leaving Novi Sad part of the [Republic of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Serbia).

On 1 November 2024, the canopy of the [main railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_railway_station) in Novi Sad collapsed, [killing sixteen people](78262640.md). The incident sparked a series of [mass protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_Serbian_anti-corruption_protests) against government corruption.[^cite_note-26]

During 2025, one million square meters of residential space were under construction simultaneously in Novi Sad, making the city the largest construction site in Serbia.[^cite_note-27]

## Geography

The city lies on the [meander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meander) of the river Danube, which is only 350 meters wide beneath the marking stones of Petrovaradin.[^cite_note-GeoNS-28] A section of the [Danube-Tisza-Danube Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube-Tisa-Danube_Canal) marks the northern edge of the wider city centre. The main part of the city lies on the left bank of the Danube in the region of [Bačka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%8Dka), while the smaller settlements of [Petrovaradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin) and [Sremska Kamenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sremska_Kamenica) lie on the right bank, in the region of [Srem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srem) (Syrmia). The section situated on the left bank of the river lies on one of the southernmost and lowest parts of the [Pannonian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Plain), while [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora) on the right bank is a [horst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horst_\(geology\)) mountain. [Alluvial plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial_plain) along the Danube are well-formed, especially on the left bank, and in some parts 10 kilometres (6 miles) from the river. A large part of Novi Sad lies on a [fluvial terrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluvial_terrace) with an elevation of between 80 and 83 metres (262 and 272 feet). The northern part of Fruška Gora is composed of massive [landslide](37208.md) zones, although they are largely inactive with the exception of the [Ribnjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribnjak,_Novi_Sad) neighbourhood between [Sremska Kamenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sremska_Kamenica) and [Petrovaradin Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin_Fortress).[^cite_note-UrbNS-29]

The total land area of the city is 699 square kilometres (270 sq mi), while its urban area spans 129.7 km<sup>2</sup> (50 sq mi).[^cite_note-GeoNS-28]

### Climate

Novi Sad has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*)[^cite_note-Climate-Data.org-30] closely bordering on [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dfa*) with a January mean of 0.7 °C (33.3 °F). The city experiences four distinct seasons. Autumn is drier than spring, with long sunny and warm periods. Winter is not so severe, with an average of 22 days of complete sub-zero temperature, and averages 22 days of snowfall. January is the coldest month, with an average low of −2.5 °C (27.5 °F). Spring is usually short and rainy, while summer arrives abruptly. The coldest temperature ever recorded in Novi Sad was −30.7 °C (−23.3 °F) on 24 January 1963, and the hottest temperature ever recorded was 41.6 °C (106.9 °F) on 24 July 2007.



Panoramic view from the [Petrovaradin Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin_Fortress)

The east-southeasterly wind, known as [Košava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%A1ava_\(wind\)), blows from the [Carpathians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains) and brings clear and dry weather. It mostly blows in autumn and winter, in 2 to 3-day intervals. The [average speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity) of Košava is 25 to 43 km/h (16 to 27 mph), but gusts can sometimes reach up to 130 km/h (81 mph). In wintertime, accompanied by [snow storms](244878.md), the winds can cause large snow-drifts.

## Settlements

See also: [List of buildings in Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_buildings_in_Novi_Sad)



Residential towers in Liman district

Novi Sad is a typical Central European town in terms of its architecture. The Town Hall and the Court House were built by Emmerich Kitzweger (1868–1917).[^cite_note-andora-35] During the [1848/1849 revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848_in_the_Habsburg_areas), the town was heavily bombed. One third of all houses were destroyed and the city's population was reduced to half.[^cite_note-36] Architecture from the 19th century dominates the [city centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stari_Grad,_Novi_Sad).[^cite_note-andora-35] Small, older houses used to surround the centre of town, but they are now being replaced by modern, multi-story buildings.[^cite_note-live-37]

During the socialist period, new city blocks with wide streets and multi-story buildings were constructed around the city core. However, not many [communist-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plattenbau) high-rise buildings were erected.[^cite_note-live-37] The total number of [apartment buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartment_building), with ten or more floors, remained at about 50, the rest having mostly three to six floors. From 1962 to 1964, a new [boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard), today called *Bulevar oslobođenja*, was cut through the older neighbourhoods, establishing major communication lines.[^cite_note-uns-38] Several more boulevards were subsequently built in a similar manner, creating an [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonality) network which replaced the primarily radial structure of the old town. These interventions paved the way for a relatively unhampered growth of the city, which has almost tripled in population since the 1950s.[^cite_note-database-39] Despite a huge increase in car ownership, [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) is still relatively mild, apart from a few major arteries.

Some of the oldest neighbourhoods in the city are [Stari Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stari_Grad,_Novi_Sad) (Old Town), [Rotkvarija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotkvarija_\(%C5%BDitni_Trg\)), [Podbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podbara), and [Salajka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salajka). The areas of [Sremska Kamenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sremska_Kamenica) and [Petrovaradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin), located on the right bank of the Danube, were separate towns in the past, but today belong to the urban area of Novi Sad. [Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liman,_Novi_Sad), as well as [Bistrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bistrica_\(Novo_Naselje\)), are neighbourhoods built during the 1960s, 1970s and 1980s, with contemporary style buildings and wide boulevards ([Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liman,_Novi_Sad) was divided into four sections, numbered I–IV).[^cite_note-database-39]

New neighbourhoods, like [Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liman,_Novi_Sad), [Detelinara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detelinara), and [Bistrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bistrica,_Novi_Sad), emerged from the fields and forests surrounding the city. Following World War II, tall residential buildings were constructed to house the huge influx of people leaving the country side. Many old houses in the city centre, from the Rotkvarija and [Bulevar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulevar) neighbourhoods, were torn down in the 1950s and 1960s, to be replaced by multi-story buildings.[^cite_note-database-39] Since the city has experienced a major construction boom in the last 10 years, some neighbourhoods have completely been transformed.[^cite_note-database-39]

Neighbourhoods with single-family homes are mostly located away from the city centre. [Telep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telep), situated in the southwest, and [Klisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klisa,_Serbia), in the north, are the oldest such neighborhoods. [Adice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adice) and [Veternik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veternik), both located west of the downtown area, have significantly expanded during the last 15 years, partly due to the influx of Serbian refugees fleeing the [Yugoslav wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_wars).

### Suburbs

Some 43,000 inhabitants or 11.7% of the administrative city's total population resides in the 11 rural settlements, outside its urban area.[^cite_note-census-40] [Ledinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ledinci), [Stari Ledinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stari_Ledinci), and [Bukovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovac,_Novi_Sad) are all located on [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora) slopes, with Stari Ledinci being the most isolated and least populated village. Towns and villages in the adjacent municipalities of [Sremski Karlovci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sremski_Karlovci), [Temerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temerin), and [Beočin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beo%C4%8Din) share the same public transportation system and are economically tied to Novi Sad.

Novi Sad is the second largest city in Serbia, after [Belgrade](55904.md), and the largest city in [Vojvodina](176801.md). Its contiguous urban area (which include Novi Sad proper and adjacent settlements of [Petrovaradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin), [Sremska Kamenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sremska_Kamenica), [Veternik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veternik), and [Futog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futog)) has population of 325,551 inhabitants. Population of the administrative area of the city of Novi Sad stands at 368,967 inhabitants.

Since its founding, the population of the city has been constantly increasing, and in the last three decades in particular, it experienced rapid population growth. According to the 2022 census, 50.8% of the population of Novi Sad was not native to the city: 30.3% migrated to Novi Sad from rest of Serbia, 17.1% came from countries of former Yugoslavia (predominately Serbs from [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), [Croatia](5573.md), and [Montenegro](20760.md)), while some 3.4% came from other countries.

## Culture



[Matica Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matica_Srpska)



[Serbian National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_National_Theatre)

In the 19th and early 20th century, Novi Sad was the capital of [Serbian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_culture), earning it the nickname *Serbian Athens*.[^cite_note-44] During that time, many Serbian novelists, poets, jurists, and publishers had lived or worked in Novi Sad at some point in their career, including [Vuk Stefanović Karadžić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuk_Stefanovi%C4%87_Karad%C5%BEi%C4%87), [Mika Antić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mika_Anti%C4%87), [Đura Jakšić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90ura_Jak%C5%A1i%C4%87) and [Jovan Jovanović Zmaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jovan_Jovanovi%C4%87_Zmaj), among others. [Matica srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matica_srpska), the oldest cultural-scientific institution in Serbia, was moved from [Budapest](36787.md) to Novi Sad in 1864, and now contains the second-largest library in the country, the Library of Matica srpska, with over 3.5 million volumes. The [Serbian National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_National_Theatre), one of the oldest professional theatre among the [South Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Slavs), was founded in Novi Sad in 1861.[^cite_note-45]

Today, Novi Sad is the second largest cultural centre in [Serbia](29265.md), after [Belgrade](55904.md). Municipal officials have made the city more attractive with numerous cultural events and music concerts. Since 2000, Novi Sad is home to the [EXIT festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_\(festival\)), one of the biggest music summer festivals in Europe. Other important cultural events include the [Sterijino pozorje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterijino_pozorje) theatre festival, [Zmaj Children Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zmaj_Children_Games), [International Novi Sad Literature Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Novi_Sad_Literature_Festival), [Novi Sad Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_Jazz_Festival), and many others.[^cite_note-46] Novi Sad also hosts a fashion show twice a year, attracting local and international designers. Called [Serbia Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_Fashion_Week), the event also features the works of applied artists, musicians, interior decorators, multimedia experts and architects.[^cite_note-47] In addition to the [Serbian National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_National_Theatre), other prominent playhouses consist of the [Novi Sad Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_Theatre), [Novi Sad Youth Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_Youth_Theatre), and the Cultural Centre of Novi Sad. The [Novi Sad Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_Synagogue) also houses many cultural events. Other [cultural institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_institutions) include the Detachment of the Serbian Academy of Science and Art, Library of Matica Srpska, Novi Sad City Library and [Azbukum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azbukum). The city is also home to the [Archives of Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_of_Vojvodina).[^cite_note-48] dating back to 1565.

Novi Sad has several folk song societies, which are known as *kulturno-umetničko društvo* or KUD. The best known societies in the city are: KUD Svetozar Marković, AKUD Sonja Marinković, SKUD Željezničar, FA Vila and the oldest SZPD [Neven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEVEN_Serbian_Craftsmen_Singing_Society), established in 1892.

Ethnic minorities express their own traditions, folklore and songs through various societies such as the Hungarian MKUD Petőfi Sándor, Slovak SKUD Pavel Jozef Šafárik, and Pannonian Rusyn RKC Novi Sad.

Novi Sad was chosen to be the [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) for 2021,[^cite_note-49] however its mandate was moved to 2022 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-sustain-50] From this mandate, the industrial zone in Liman neighborhood was repurposed as an artist quarter known as *Distrikt*. Due to the continued efforts of local artists, the city became a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [Creative City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) of [Media Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Media_Arts) in 2023.[^cite_note-UnescoCCN-6][^cite_note-euronewsCCN-7]

### Tourism



River cruise ship on the [Danube](21209639.md)



[Exit festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_\(festival\))

Since the early 2000s, the number of tourists visiting Novi Sad has been steadily rising each year. During the annual [EXIT music festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_\(festival\)), the city is full of young people from all over Europe. In 2017, over 200,000 visitors from 60 countries came to the festival, attending about 35 concerts.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

Other events include shows and congresses organized by [Novi Sad Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_Fair), a local management company, bringing in many businesspersons and entrepreneurs to the city. Novi Sad is home to the largest [agricultural show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_show) in the region, having attracted 600,000 attendees in 2005.[^cite_note-57] The tourist port, near [Varadin Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varadin_Bridge) in the city centre, welcomes cruise boats from across Europe that travel the Danube.

The most recognized structure in Novi Sad is the [Petrovaradin Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin_Fortress), which dominates the skyline and also offers scenic views of the city. The nearby historic neighbourhood of [Stari Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stari_Grad,_Novi_Sad) has many monuments, museums, cafes, restaurants and shops. Also in the vicinity, is the [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora) National Park, approximately 20 km (12 mi) from the city centre.

A total of 234,708 tourist arrivals with 567,926 overnight stays were recorded in 2023.

### Cuisine

Typical [Serbian food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_food) can be found in Novi Sad, including traditional dishes like [ćevapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%86evapi), [burek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burek), [kajmak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajmak), [kiseli kupus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_sour_cabbage), [kiflice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kifli) and [pasulj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasulj), as well as fish dishes, local cheeses and charcuterie.[^cite_note-coolhunting.com-58] Restaurants and farmsteads offer fresh produce from local farmers and also [regional vintages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_wine) from [Fruska Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruska_Gora)'s wineries.[^cite_note-coolhunting.com-58][^cite_note-59] Modern alternatives are available at some of the city's top restaurants, which prepare traditional fare with an updated twist.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] Pastry shops serve local specialties such as layered cakes made from ground nuts and cream, referred to as 'torta' in Serbian. Desserts also often include raspberries, one of the region's largest exports, and historic Dunavska Street is home to many ice cream parlors.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

Index sandwich, named after university student index books, is a popular local street food since the 1980s.[^cite_note-64] It is made out of a bread roll stuffed with melted cheese, ham, mushrooms and lashings of sauce.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

### Green areas and recreation

The parks and green areas of Novi Sad form an important part of the city's urban and natural landscape, encompassing landscaped city parks, promenades, and protected natural areas. Among the most notable urban parks are [Danube Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Park), Futoški Park, the Liman parks, and Kamenički Park, which play a significant recreational, ecological, and cultural role within the city.[^cite_note-68]



[Fruška Gora National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora), the oldest [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park) in Serbia, represents the primary recreational and [outdoor sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outdoor_sports) area for the residents of Novi Sad. In the image: the [Lake Ledinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ledinci) area.

A distinct group of green spaces is located in the Petrovaradin area, including the parks and landscaped zones surrounding the [Petrovaradin Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrovaradin_Fortress). These areas function as promenades and viewpoints overlooking the Danube and the city. Adjacent to the fortress is Molinarijev Park, a smaller green space closely connected to the historical setting of the fortification.

The wider area of Novi Sad also includes several valuable natural sites outside the urban core. The [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora) National Park, located south of the city, represents a major natural and recreational area known for its forest ecosystems, vineyards, protected plant and animal species, and cultural and historical heritage. To the north and east of the city lies the [Koviljsko-Petrovaradinski Rit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koviljsko-Petrovaradinski_Rit?action=edit\&redlink=1) Special Nature Reserve, a wetland area along the [Danube](21209639.md) that is of exceptional importance for biodiversity conservation, characterized by rich flora and fauna, particularly wetland bird species, fish, and floodplain habitats. Together, the city parks, Petrovaradin green areas, [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora), and the Koviljsko-Petrovaradinski Rit form the distinctive [green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt) of Novi Sad.

The inhabitants of Novi Sad engage in a wide range of recreational and leisure activities. With regards to [team sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_sport), [football](10568.md) and basketball have the highest numbers of participants. Cycling is also popular due to the city's flat terrain and the extensive off-road network, found in nearby mountainous [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora). Hundreds of commuters cycle the roads, [bike lanes and bike paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segregated_cycle_facilities) daily.

Proximity to the [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora) National Park attracts many city dwellers on the weekends. They enjoy the numerous hiking trails, restaurants and monasteries located in and around the mountain area. Occurring on the first weekend of every May, the *[Fruška Gora Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1kogorski_maraton)* lets hikers, runners and cyclists take advantage of the many hiking trails.[^cite_note-69] During the summer months, citizens from Novi Sad visit [Lake Ledinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ledinci) in Fruška Gora, as well as the numerous beaches situated along the [Danube](21209639.md), the largest being [Štrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0trand) in the [Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liman,_Novi_Sad) neighbourhood. There are also several recreational marinas bordering the river.

## Economy

Novi Sad is the economic centre of [Vojvodina](176801.md), the most fertile agricultural region in [Serbia](29265.md). The city also represents one of the largest economic and cultural hubs in Serbia.

Novi Sad had always been a developed city within the former [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). In 1981, its GDP per capita was 172% of the Yugoslav average.[^cite_note-70] During the 1990s, the city, like the rest of Serbia, was severely affected by an internationally imposed [trade embargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embargo) and hyperinflation of the [Yugoslav dinar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_dinar). The embargo, along with economic mismanagement, led to a decay or demise of once important industrial combines, such as *Novkabel* (electric cable industry), *Pobeda* (metal industry), *Jugoalat* (tools), *Albus* and *HINS* (chemical industry). Practically the only viable large facilities remaining today are the oil refinery, located northeast of the city, and the [thermal power plant](2549543.md).

The economy of Novi Sad has mostly recovered from that period and grown strongly since 2001, shifting from an industry-driven economy to the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy). The processes involved in [privatizing](24661.md) state and society-owned enterprises, as well as strong private incentives, have increased the share of privately owned companies to over 95% in the district, with small and medium-size enterprises dominating the city's [economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_development).[^cite_note-pkns2-71]

The significance of Novi Sad as a financial centre is already proven, by being home to the national headquarters of numerous banks, such as [Erste Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erste_Bank_Novi_Sad), [Vojvođanska banka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojvo%C4%91anska_banka_a.d.), and [Crédit Agricole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9dit_Agricole_Srbija);[^cite_note-72] as well as the third largest insurance company in Serbia, [DDOR Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDOR_Novi_Sad). Furthermore, the city is home to major energy companies like [Naftna Industrija Srbije](7562798.md) oil company and [Srbijagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srbijagas) gas company. It is also the seat of many farms for wheat production and trade.

## Politics

Main articles: [Politics of Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Novi_Sad) and [List of mayors of Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Novi_Sad)



[Novi Sad City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_City_Hall), seat of the mayor and city government



[Banovina Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banovina_Palace_\(Novi_Sad\)), seat of the provincial assembly and government

Novi Sad is the administrative centre of the [Autonomous Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Serbia) of [Vojvodina](176801.md), and as such, home to Vojvodina's [Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Vojvodina) and [Provincial Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Vojvodina).

The city's administrative bodies include the city [assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deliberative_assembly) as the representative body, as well as the mayor and [city government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government) defining the executive bodies. The mayor and city assembly members are chosen through direct elections. The city assembly has 78 seats,[^cite_note-75] while the city government consists of 11 members. The mayor and members of the city's assembly are elected to four-year terms. The city government is elected by the city assembly at the proposal of the mayor.

As of 2025, the mayor of Novi Sad is [Žarko Mićin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDarko_Mi%C4%87in) of the [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md).[^cite_note-\:0-76] While his party holds the majority of seats in the city assembly, the [Socialist Party of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Serbia), the [Democratic Party of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_of_Serbia), as well as other parties and groups, are also represented.

The city of Novi Sad is divided into 47 [local communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_local_communities_in_Novi_Sad).[^cite_note-77]

## Society

### Education

Main article: [Education in Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Novi_Sad)



Rectorate of the [University of Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Novi_Sad)



Student Cultural Center of the University of Novi Sad

Novi Sad is one of the most important centres of higher education and research in Serbia, with four universities overall and numerous professional, technical, and private colleges and [research institutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_institute), including a law school with its own publication.[^cite_note-79] The largest educational institution in the city is the [University of Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Novi_Sad), a public school established in 1960. As of 2012, it has 14 faculties, 9 of which are located on the main [university campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_campus_in_Novi_Sad).[^cite_note-uni-80] It is attended by more than 50,000 students and has a total staff of nearly 5,000.[^cite_note-uni-80]

Business Academy University and EducoNS University are private schools also located in the city.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] Other educational institutions include [Novi Sad Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_Open_University), offering professional courses in [adult education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_education), and the [Protestant Theological Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_Theological_College).

As of 2022, there are 37 elementary schools (33 public and 4 private) with about 26,000 students.[^cite_note-stat-83] The [secondary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_\(upper_secondary\)) system consists of 25 vocational schools (12 public and 13 private) and 4 [gymnasiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_\(school\)) with almost 18,000 students.[^cite_note-stat-83]

### Media

Novi Sad has one major daily newspaper, *[Dnevnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnevnik_\(Novi_Sad\))*, and among the periodicals, the monthly magazine *Vojvođanski magazin* stands out. The city is also home to the regional public broadcaster, *[Radio Television of Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Television_of_Vojvodina) (RTV)*, and municipal public broadcaster, *Novosadska televizija*,[^cite_note-84] as well as a few commercial TV stations such as *Kanal 9*,[^cite_note-85] *Panonija*[^cite_note-86] and *RTV Most*.[^cite_note-87] Major local commercial radio stations include *[Radio AS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_AS?action=edit\&redlink=1) FM* and *[Radio 021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_021?action=edit\&redlink=1)*.[^cite_note-88]

Novi Sad is also known as a publishing centre. The most important publishing houses are *Matica srpska*, *Stilos*, and *Prometej*. Well-known journals, in literature and art, include *[Letopis Matice srpske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letopis_Matice_srpske)*, the oldest Serbian journal, *Polja*,[^cite_note-89] which is issued by the [Cultural Center of Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Center_of_Novi_Sad), and *Zlatna greda*, published by the Association of Writers of Vojvodina.[^cite_note-90]

The city hosts an annual literature conference, [Book Talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_Talk).[^cite_note-91]

### Sports



[SPENS Sports Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPC_Vojvodina)



[Karađorđe Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara%C4%91or%C4%91e_Stadium)

Founded in 1790, the *'City Marksmen Association'* became the first sporting organization in Novi Sad. Founded on 28 March 1885, [VK Danubius 1885](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VK_Danubius_1885) is the oldest rowing club in former Yugoslavia.[^cite_note-92] A more widespread interest in competitive sports developed after the *Municipal Association of Physical Culture* was created in 1959 and when the [SPENS Sports Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPC_Vojvodina) was built in 1981. Nowadays, about 220 sports organizations are active in Novi Sad.

Professional sports in Novi Sad mostly revolve around the [Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD_Vojvodina) multi-sport club. Having won two championships in 1966 and 1989, the [FK Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Vojvodina) football club represents the 3rd all-time best team in Serbia, right behind its two Belgrade rivals, [Red Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_Belgrade) and [Partizan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Partizan). With 13 championship titles, [OK Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OK_Vojvodina) is the top volleyball team in the country. As for handball, [RK Vojvodina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RK_Vojvodina) has won the national championship on multiple occasions.[^cite_note-93]

Athletes from Novi Sad had the honour of participating in the first [Olympic Games](48644.md) in Athens. The largest number of Novi Sad competitors, to participate in the Olympics, was at the [Atlanta Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics). Eleven athletes won 6 medals there. Three also competed at the 1980 [Moscow Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_at_the_1980_Summer_Olympics), while two participated in the 1976 [Montreal Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics) and the 1956 [Melbourne Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_at_the_1956_Summer_Olympics).

Many national and international competitions are held in the city. Novi Sad played host to the European and World Championships in table tennis in 1981[^cite_note-Најзначајније_приредбе-94] and the [29th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Olympiad) in 1990. It also welcomed the European and World Championships in [sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambo_\(martial_art\)), the Balkan and European Championships in judo, the 1987 final match of the [Saporta Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saporta_Cup) in European basketball,[^cite_note-Најзначајније_приредбе-94][^cite_note-Cup_Winners’_Cup_1986-87-95] and the final tournament of the European volleyball cup.[^cite_note-Најзначајније_приредбе-94] Furthermore, Novi Sad co-hosted the [2005 European Basketball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_2005), as well as hosting the 2017 [Volleyball World League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volleyball_World_League) matches.[^cite_note-Најзначајније_приредбе-94][^cite_note-wkf.net-96] The year 2018 saw the city welcome the Senior European Fencing Championships and the European Senior Karate Championships.[^cite_note-Најзначајније_приредбе-94][^cite_note-wkf.net-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

The city also holds traditional sporting events such as the Novi Sad marathon, international swimming competitions and many other events. The very first 'MTB Petrovaradin Fortress Cup' took place in 2018, allowing national and regional cyclists to compete. It is also the first mountain bike competition to be held in Serbia.[^cite_note-99]

## Transportation

Main article: [Transport in Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Novi_Sad)



[Liberty Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Bridge,_Novi_Sad)

Air transport

Novi Sad currently does not have its own civil airport. The city is roughly a one-hour drive from [Belgrade Nikola Tesla Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Nikola_Tesla_Airport), which connects it with capitals across Europe. The small [Čenej Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_Airfield) to the north of the city is used for sporting and agricultural purposes. There are plans to upgrade it to serve for cargo and small-scale public transport,[^cite_note-100] but the future of this initiative is uncertain.

Urban transport



City bus

The main public transportation system in Novi Sad consists of bus lines, operated by the public company [JGSP Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JGSP_Novi_Sad). There are twenty-one urban lines and thirty-five suburban lines, with the main bus terminal being at the northern end of the [Liberation Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulevar) (Bulevar oslobođenja) next to the [Novi Sad railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_railway_station), in addition to a smaller terminal in the town center. There are numerous taxi companies serving the city.

The city used to have [a tram system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_tram_system), but it was decommissioned in 1957.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

Rail and road transport

Novi Sad lies on branch B of the [Pan-European Corridor X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_X). The [A1 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_motorway_\(Serbia\)) connects the city with [Subotica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subotica) and [Hungary](13275.md) to the north and the capital city of [Belgrade](55904.md) to the south.

It runs parallel to the [Budapest–Belgrade railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest%E2%80%93Belgrade_railway), which connects it to major European cities. On 19 March 2022 the "Soko" (meaning "falcon") high-speed line between Novi Sad and Belgrade opened and runs with 18 departures daily. Its maximum speed is 200 km/h and the 75 kilometres between Belgrade and Novi Sad are covered in 35 minutes. Between Belgrade and Novi Sad there are a total of 60 departures per day. There are three types of trains in total. "Intercity" (SOKO), "Regio-ekspres" and "Regio". On 8 October 2025 the "Soko" high-speed line between Belgrade (via Novi Sad) and Subotica opened and runs with 9 departures daily. Its maximum speed is 200 km/h and the 108 kilometres between Novi Sad and Subotica are covered in 45 minutes.

Novi Sad is connected with [Zrenjanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zrenjanin) and [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara) on the northeast and [Ruma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruma) on south with a regional highway; there are plans to upgrade it to a motorway or an expressway, with a tunnel under the [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora) shortcutting the *Iriški Venac* mountain pass.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]



[Danube–Tisa–Danube Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube%E2%80%93Tisa%E2%80%93Danube_Canal)

Three bridges cross the Danube in Novi Sad (as of 2020): [Liberty Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Bridge,_Novi_Sad) (Most Slobode) connects [Sremska Kamenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sremska_Kamenica) with the city proper. [Varadin Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varadin_Bridge) (Varadinski most) and [Žeželj Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDe%C5%BEelj_Bridge) (Žeželjev most), connects Petrovaradin with city centre, and used for railway and heavy truck traffic. Many bridges also span the Danube-Tisa-Danube canal, running north of the city centre. Currently, two bridges over the Danube are being built, along with two new railway bridges over the Danube-Tisa-Danube canal.

Water transport

The [Port of Novi Sad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Novi_Sad) is located on the outskirts of the city on [Danube](21209639.md) river. Since May 2019 it has been owned by DP WORLD from the UAE. With over a million tonnes of load turnover, it is the largest cargo port in Serbia.[^cite_note-105]
