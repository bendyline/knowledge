**Jijel** ([Kabyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabyle_language): *Ǧiǧel*, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جِيْجَل), the [classical](252905.md) **[Igilgili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igilgili)**, is the capital of [Jijel Province](1469900.md) in north-eastern [Algeria](358.md). It is flanked by the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) in the region of [Corniche Jijelienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corniche_Jijelienne) and had a population of 131,513 in 2008.

Jijel is the administrative and trade center for a region specialising in [cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_\(material\)) processing, [leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather) [tanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanning_\(leather\)) and [steelmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steelmaking). Local [crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop) include [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus) and [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain). Fishing is also of great importance. Tourists (mainly Algerians) are attracted to Jijel for its landscapes and fine sand beaches. Being a [resort town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_town), there are many hotels and restaurants. There are [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians) tombs nearby.

## Geography

Jijel is situated 30 km from [Taza National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taza_National_Park); this [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park) and other vicinity features support a variety of flora and fauna. It is an important [habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat) for the endangered [Barbary macaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_macaque), *Macaca sylvanus*.[^cite_note-4]

### Climate

Jijel has a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) (*Csa* in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)).

## History

Main article: [Igilgili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igilgili)

Originally Phoenician, [Igilgili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igilgili) passed to the [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginian_Empire), the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) and [Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), the [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandal_Kingdom), the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), the [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate), the [Genovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa), and the [Ottomans](22278.md). It was conquered for the last time in the 16th century by [Hayreddin Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayreddin_Barbarossa).

In the book [Al-Istibsar fi 'agaib al-Amsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Istibsar_fi_'agaib_al-Amsar?action=edit\&redlink=1), written in the late 12th century, the author describes the city as:[^cite_note-6]

> "It is an ancient city on the sea, with an old wall that extends into the sea, and it has great strategic significance. The city is abundant in grapes, apples, and fruits. From it, fruits, grapes, and [debes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_honey) are carried to the city of [Béjaïa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9ja%C3%AFa). On this city stands Mount Ketama, known as Mount Zaldwa, a very fertile area with many Berber tribes. It was here that the call of Abdullah al-Da‘i took place. Between Jijel and Béjaïa, on the coast, there is a place called Al-Mansuriyya, with a great mountain above it."

In July 1664, the [French took the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djidjelli_expedition). Resistance was organised under the direction of [Shaban Aga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaban_Aga?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the French were driven out in October of the same year. Jijel remained a [corsair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_corsairs) stronghold until recaptured by the French in 1839. Strong local resistance, finally subdued in 1851, resulted in the construction of three forts along its southern fringe as well as minimal colonisation. The original town was devastated by an earthquake in 1856.

## Communications

Due to the rugged landscape, Jijel is slightly isolated. However, it is connected by road to large cities like [Bejaïa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja%C3%AFa) (90 km west), [Setif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setif) (135 km southwest) and [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine,_Algeria) (150 km southeast). The city also has its own airport [Jijel Ferhat Abbas Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jijel_Ferhat_Abbas_Airport).

## Infrastructure

Jijel is built along modern patterns with wide streets framed by trees. The surroundings consist largely of dense cork-oak forest. A [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) lies right out from the coast and there is a [citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel) to the north. There is a hospital, previously a Catholic church (which was demolished), mosques and [University of Jijel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jijel).

## Port

A relatively new port has been built at [Djen Djen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djen_Djen?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-port-7] approximately 7 miles east of Jijel, which can handle large bulk carriers having a draft up to 18.2m. Presently, the port is mainly used by car carriers and break bulk vessels.
