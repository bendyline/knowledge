**Randa Abdel-Fattah** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): رندة عبد الفتاح; born 6 June 1979) is an Australian sociologist, lawyer and writer. She is an advocate for human rights, particularly for [Palestinian people](23267.md). Much of her work focuses on [identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_\(social_science\)) and what it means to be [Muslim in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Australia). Her [debut novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debut_novel), *[Does My Head Look Big in This?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Does_My_Head_Look_Big_in_This%3F)*, was published in 2005. Her 2025 novel, *[Discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discipline_\(novel\))*, came to public attention after Abdel-Fattah was disinvited from the 2026 [Adelaide Writers' Week](2029835.md), leading to a [boycott by most of the scheduled participants](82076037.md) and the subsequent cancellation of the event.

## Early life and education

Randa Abdel-Fattah was born on 6 June 1979[^cite_note-21st_Century_Learning_Conference-2020-1] in [Sydney](27862.md), New South Wales,[^cite_note-austlit-2] to a [Palestinian](23267.md) father and an [Egyptian](31912046.md) mother.[^cite_note-RanABC-3][^cite_note-Dow-2012-4] Although often referred to as [Palestinian Australian](31839311.md),\[^cite_note-\:0-5]\[^cite_note-[www.bbc.com-2026-6](http://www.bbc.com-2026-6/)] Abdel-Fattah has said "I don't think I ever identified as or even felt 'Palestinian in Australia' Sometimes I felt a hybrid of Australian and Palestinian. Sometimes I felt neither Australian nor Palestinian, tired of identity politics \[...] As for my cultural consciousness, my mother is Egyptian and I grew up among her family in Australia and probably absorbed more Egyptian culture as a child."[^cite_note-g287-7] In an essay on *[Mondoweiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondoweiss)* in January 2026, she described herself as a Palestinian.[^cite_note-Mondoweiss-8]

She grew up in [Melbourne](17306237.md), Victoria, and attended a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) primary school and then King Khalid Islamic College.[^cite_note-austlit-2] She wrote her first novel, based on [Roald Dahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roald_Dahl)'s *[Matilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilda_\(novel\))*, when she was in sixth grade.[^cite_note-21st_Century_Learning_Conference-2020-1] She started writing the first draft of a semi-autobiographical novel at age 15, which was completed and published ten years later as *Does My Head Look Big in This?*[^cite_note-Dow-2012-4]

In 2002, Abdel-Fattah gained [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) and [LLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Laws) degrees from the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne).[^cite_note-austlit-2]\[^cite_note-[www.all4palestine.org-9](http://www.all4palestine.org-9/)] During this time, she was the media liaison at the [Islamic Council of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Council_of_Victoria), writing about [Muslims in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims_in_Australia).[^cite_note-RanABC-3]

In 2016, Abdel-Fattah obtained her PhD from the Department of Sociology at [Macquarie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macquarie_University).\[^cite_note-[www.all4palestine.org-9](http://www.all4palestine.org-9/)] Her thesis, "[Islamophobia](162173.md) and Everyday Multiculturalism in Australia", was later published by [Routledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Routledge) in 2018.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Career

### Law

Abdel-Fattah worked as a [solicitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitor) for various firms, including [Slater & Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slater_&_Gordon) (2001–2003), Lander & Rogers (2003–2006), Thompson Playford Lawyers (2006–2009) and Hicksons Lawyers (2009–2012).\[^cite_note-[www.all4palestine.org-9](http://www.all4palestine.org-9/)]

As of September 2025, she is a lawyer of the [Supreme Court of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_New_South_Wales). She is patron of the Racial Justice Centre, the first Australian Community Legal Service focused on racial justice.[^cite_note-profile2025-12]

### Writing and research

In 2018, Abdel-Fattah received two research fundings from the [Australian Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Research_Council) in the form of their Discovery Early Career Research Award, for studying the sociological conditions of modern Muslim and non-Muslim youth. Her three-year project was based at Macquarie University with Amanda Wise as her collaborator.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Her findings were published by [NewSouth Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_South_Wales_Press) in 2021 as *Coming of Age in the War on Terror*, and as a paper in the *[Journal of Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Sociology)* in 2024.[^cite_note-Abdel-Fattah-2024-15]

Abdel-Fattah has published research articles in the *Journal of Sociology*,[^cite_note-16] the *[Sociological Research Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociological_Research_Online)*,[^cite_note-17] and the *Journal of Intercultural Studies*.[^cite_note-18]

She has written books for children and young adults. She created a series aimed at early readers, called *Our Stories*, of books written and illustrated by diverse authors and illustrators. Her research inspired another project for young children, leading to Australia's first [Black-Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-Palestinian_solidarity) [picture book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picture_book) collaboration, *11 Words For Love* (2023).[^cite_note-profile2025-12]

She has also published novels. Her latest novel *[Discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discipline_\(novel\))* was published in August 2025.[^cite_note-discipline-19] Set in [Sydney](27862.md) during the [Israel-Gaza War](74998519.md), its main characters are a young journalist of Palestinian descent and an academic of Egyptian Australian descent.[^cite_note-haydar2025-20][^cite_note-heath2025-21]

She has written articles for *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald)*, *[The Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Age)*, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, *[Overland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overland_\(magazine\))*, *[Meanjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meanjin)*, *[Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English)*, *[Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde), [New Matilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Matilda),* and *[The New Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Arab)*.\[^cite_note-[www.all4palestine.org-9](http://www.all4palestine.org-9/)][^cite_note-RanABC-3]

As of September 2025 Abdel-Fattah is a Future Fellow in the Department of Sociology at Macquarie University. Her research areas cover Islamophobia, race, Palestine, the [war on terror](13425800.md), youth identities, and social movement activism.[^cite_note-profile2025-12]

## Views and media appearances

Abdel-Fattah describes herself as a [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist) and has written critical pieces on [women's rights in Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_rights_in_Saudi_Arabia), stating that women should retain the right to wear what they want.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-music2015-23] She no longer discusses the [hijab](68301.md), on the basis that it constitutes [flogging a dead horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flogging_a_dead_horse) and detracts from the discussion of other issues.[^cite_note-music2015-23]

On Australian television, she has appeared on *[Insight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insight_\(Australian_TV_program\))* ([SBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Broadcasting_Service)), *[First Tuesday Book Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_Club)* ([ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Broadcasting_Corporation)), *[Q\&A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q+A_\(Australian_talk_show\))* ([ABC TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_TV_\(Australian_TV_channel\))),[^cite_note-RanABC-3] *[Sunrise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrise_\(Australian_TV_program\))* ([Seven Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Network)), and *[9am with David & Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9am_with_David_&_Kim)* ([Network 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_10)).[^cite_note-24]

In 2022, the [Australian Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Research_Council) (ARC) granted Abdel-Fattah their Future Fellowship[^cite_note-Cassidy-2025-25] on a research project titled "Arab/Muslim Australian Social Movements since the 1970s: A hidden history". Abdel-Fattah is the primary chief investigator, contracted to work on the project from 2023 to 2027.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

During the [Gaza War](74998519.md) starting in 2023, she has been a vocal critic of the Israeli government,[^cite_note-28] accusing it of committing a "[Palestinian holocaust](75668427.md)" in Gaza.[^cite_note-29] At an academic symposium organised by the [Queensland University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_University_of_Technology)'s Carumba Institute, Abdel-Fattah spoke of how, instead of organising an academic conference as mandated by her ARC grants, she used the funds to run a workshop for women from multicultural backgrounds.[^cite_note-30] Because of the fund diversion remarks, the education minister, [Jason Clare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Clare), wrote to the ARC board on 31 January 2025 asking it to investigate Abdel-Fattah. On 27 February, the ARC announced that the Abdel-Fattah's grant was suspended and that it was the responsibility of [Macquarie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macquarie_University) to resolve the issue[^cite_note-Cassidy-2025-25][^cite_note-31] and, if the grant had been misused, to refund the entire amount.[^cite_note-32] After a 10-month investigation led by two independent academics, the university announced on 23 December 2025 that "there is no basis for any further investigation of the concerns raised by the ARC." The ARC restored the funding.[^cite_note-33]

Abdel-Fattah's social media post from March 2024 which stated that [Zionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist) "have no claim or right to cultural safety" was widely reported and used against her. In an essay on *Mondoweiss* in January 2026, she wrote of her "fight against Zionism" and restated that Zionists, as she understands the term, are not entitled to claim the right to a culturally safe space.[^cite_note-Mondoweiss-8]

In the same essay Abdel-Fattah also defended changing her [Facebook](7529378.md) cover to a picture of a [paraglider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragliding) in the colours of the [Palestinian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_flag) on 8 October 2023, one day after the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md). She wrote that, at the time, she was not aware that armed paragliders had landed at the music festival and shot at civilians. She wrote: "The last short-lived mass breakout was in January 2008 \[...] Images of these items symbolise freedom 'and escape from imprisonment'" and "Palestinians in the diaspora were posting the image as an iconic symbol of an imprisoned, besieged population breaking free." Her profile picture remained unchanged for five months, which she attributed to the fact she does not regularly use Facebook. She wrote in January 2026 that "an image that represented a moment of freedom is not something I will apologise for".[^cite_note-Mondoweiss-8]

In August 2025, Abdel-Fattah cancelled her scheduled presentation at the [Bendigo Writers Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendigo_Writers_Festival) in response to a [code of conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_conduct) imposed by the festival two days before the festival was due to begin. Many other scheduled presenters also [boycotted the festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendigo_Writers_Festival_boycott) in support of Abdel-Fattah and in protest at the code of conduct.[^cite_note-34] She sued [Bendigo City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_Bendigo) and [La Trobe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trobe_University) in [VCAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Civil_and_Administrative_Tribunal) in July 2026, stating that she had suffered reputational damage and was discriminated against because of her political beliefs or activity.[^cite_note-35]

### 2026 Adelaide Writers' Week

Main article: [Adelaide Writers' Week boycott](82076037.md)

On 8 January 2026, the [Adelaide Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide_Festival) board announced that Abdel-Fattah's scheduled appearance at [Adelaide Writers' Week](2029835.md) in February to March 2026 had been cancelled due to concerns over "[cultural sensitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_sensitivity)" following the [2025 Bondi Beach shooting](81839099.md).[^cite_note-sensitivity-36] There was a backlash from writers and the public to the decision, with most of the participants announcing [a boycott of the event](82076037.md).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-muller-38][^cite_note-sensitivity-36] Abdel-Fattah decried the move as [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship).[^cite_note-AP_News-2026-39] Due to the controversy, [Louise Adler](22153705.md), the director of Adelaide Writer's Week who had scheduled her appearance, resigned from her position,[^cite_note-40]\[^cite_note-[www.bbc.com-2026-6](http://www.bbc.com-2026-6/)] and Writers' Week was cancelled.[^cite_note-AP_News-2026-39] The board resigned and a new board was appointed which apologised to Abdel-Fattah.\[^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-41] The new board also invited her to the 2027 edition of the event.[^cite_note-42]

### Newcastle Writers' Festival 2026

Abdel-Fattah appeared at [Newcastle Writers' Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_Writers'_Festival) in [Newcastle, New South Wales](52665.md), in March 2026, with two sessions sold out and a third added owing to demand, despite NSW Premier [Chris Minns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Minns) previously opining that her inclusion was "crazy".[^cite_note-43]

## Other roles and activities

Abdel-Fattah is a [human rights advocate](4358082.md) and stood at the [1998 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Australian_federal_election) as a member of the [Unity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_Party_\(Australia\)) (with the slogan "Say No to [Pauline Hanson](69926.md)"). She has also been interested in [interfaith dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interfaith_dialogue) and has been a member of various interfaith networks. She has volunteered time with human rights and migrant resource organisations, including the Australian Arabic Council, the Victorian Migrant Resource Centre, the Islamic Women's Welfare Council, and the [Asylum Seeker Resource Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum_Seeker_Resource_Centre).[^cite_note-21st_Century_Learning_Conference-2020-1][^cite_note-RanABC-3] Abdel-Fattah has also been a member of the Palestinian Human Rights Committee and the New South Wales Young Lawyers for Human Rights Committee.[^cite_note-austlit-2]

## Personal life

As of 2022 Abdel-Fattah was living in [Sydney](27862.md) with her husband and four children.[^cite_note-stella-54]

She is an observant Muslim.[^cite_note-Dow-2012-4]
