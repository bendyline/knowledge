The **Sudan People's Liberation Movement** (**SPLM**; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الحركة الشعبية لتحرير السودان, *Al-Ḥarakat ash-Shaʿbiyyat liTaḥrīr as-Sūdān*) is a political party in [South Sudan](32350676.md). It was initially founded as the political wing of the [Sudan People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_People's_Liberation_Army) (SPLA; a key belligerent of the [Second Sudanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sudanese_Civil_War)) in 1983. On January 9, 2005 the SPLA, the SPLM and the [Government of Sudan](5698926.md) signed the [Comprehensive Peace Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Peace_Agreement), ending the civil war. SPLM then obtained representation in the Government of Sudan, and was the main constituent of the [Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_South_Sudan) of the then semi-autonomous [Southern Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Sudan_Autonomous_Region_\(2005%E2%80%932011\)).

When [South Sudan](32350676.md) became a sovereign state on 9 July 2011, SPLM became the ruling party of the new republic. SPLM branches in Sudan separated themselves from SPLM, forming the [Sudan People's Liberation Movement–North](30884032.md). Further factionalism appeared as a result of the 2013–2014 [South Sudanese Civil War](41382446.md), with President [Salva Kiir](2363203.md) leading the **SPLM-Juba** and former Vice President [Riek Machar](2407248.md) leading the [Sudan People's Liberation Movement-in-Opposition](42792943.md).

Although the party has not adhered to one consistent ideology since the independence of South Sudan and many factions exist, it has been described as "being on the [left of centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_of_centre) ideological\[ly] speaking",[^cite_note-10] and section IV.2 of the party manifesto states support for a [regulated market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulated_market),[^cite_note-11] with similar elements to [social democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy).

The SPLM was formed as a [Marxist–Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist%E2%80%93Leninist),[^cite_note-fit1-12] [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism)[^cite_note-13] rebel movement on 16 May 1983, after the Government of Sudan's abandonment of the [Addis Ababa Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addis_Ababa_Agreement_\(1972\)) signed between the government of [Gaafar Nimeiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaafar_Nimeiry) and the [Anyanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyanya) leader [Joseph Lagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Lagu), who had first introduced the southern Sudanese to the effective political, economic, social, educational, and religious situations they would face after Sudan's independence. The movement published a manifesto setting out its positions and attracted a group of rebellious southern Sudanese soldiers of the Sudanese Army based in [Bor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bor,_South_Sudan), [Pochalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pochalla), and [Ayod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayod) (first called the Bor Mutiny).[^cite_note-14] These joined remnants of the Anyanya rebels of the [First Sudanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sudanese_Civil_War) based in [Ethiopia](187749.md). Founders of the SPLA (the military wing of the movement) included Captain [Salva Kiir Mayardit](2363203.md), Samuel Abu John Khabas, Major [William Nyuon Bany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Nyuon_Bany), Major [Kerubino Kuanyin Bol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerubino_Kuanyin_Bol) and many other southern Sudanese officers of the [Sudanese Armed Forces](27429.md). [Joseph Oduho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Oduho) was made chairman of the SPLM and Colonel [John Garang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Garang), a [Dinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinka_people) army officer, was made commander of the SPLA. Oduho was later deposed by Garang, who made himself overall leader of the combined movement (SPLA/M).[^cite_note-15]

The Government of Sudan has been associated with [Islam](6037917.md) and [Arab](2185.md) descent and culture since then, in deep contrast with the SPLA, associated instead with Africanism, indigenous beliefs, African culture and, to a degree [Christianity](5211.md).[^cite_note-16] It fought against the governments of [Gaafar Nimeiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaafar_Nimeiry), [Sadiq al-Mahdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiq_al-Mahdi) and [Omar Hasan Ahmad al-Bashir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Hasan_Ahmad_al-Bashir) in what is now called the [Second Sudanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sudanese_Civil_War). SPLA/M's declared aim was to establish a democratic Sudan with it as the leading party in control of the southern areas. The war has been largely described in [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_war) and [ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_conflict) terms, and also as a struggle for control of the water and oil resources located in southern and western Sudan. In the late 1980s, Marxism–Leninism was abandoned in favor of a broadly Socialist platform.[^cite_note-fit1-12]

In 1991, the [SPLA-Nasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPLA-Nasir) faction led by [Riek Machar](2407248.md) and [Lam Akol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lam_Akol) attempted to overthrow chairman Garang. The attempt failed but led to widespread fighting in the south and the formation of other rebel groups, such as [Kerubino Kuanyin Bol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerubino_Kuanyin_Bol)'s SPLA [Bahr-al-Ghazal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahr_el_Ghazal_\(region_of_South_Sudan\)) faction. These internal divisions hampered negotiations with the government. SPLA-Nasir renamed itself SPLA-United and then transformed itself, with substantial personnel changes, into the [South Sudan Independence Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sudan_Independence_Movement)/Army. Several smaller factions signed the [Khartoum Peace Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum_Peace_Agreement_of_1997) with the government in April 1997 and formed the United Democratic Salvation Front (UDSF). In July 1992, a government offensive seized southern Sudan, and captured the SPLA headquarters in [Torit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torit).[^cite_note-DeRouen4-17]

The Sudanese government accused [Uganda](31816.md) and [Eritrea](17238590.md) of supporting the SPLA/M, who were alleged to have operated on the Ugandan side of the Sudanese border with Uganda at the southern limit of Sudan. In 2005, a treaty between the SPLA/M and the Sudanese government led to the formal recognition of [Southern Sudanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Sudan_Autonomous_Region_\(2005%E2%80%932011\)) autonomy. SPLM joined the government as part of the 2005 peace agreement, gaining about one-third of government positions. On 11 October 2007, the SPLM withdrew from the government, alleging violations of the peace agreement; this raised concerns about the future of the agreement.[^cite_note-18] In 2012, as a consequence of [South Sudanese independence](13265409.md), SPLM became the new country's governing political party and the SPLA the country's army. The Sudan branch separated from the movement and formed [SPLM-N](30884032.md) to carry out anti-government activities in Sudan.

### Factionalism

- [SPLM-DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Change_\(South_Sudan\)) (Democratic Change, 2009–present)

- [SPLM–N](30884032.md) (North, 2011–present)

- SPLM-IG (In Government, 2013–present)

- [SPLM-IO](42792943.md) (In-Opposition, 2013–present)

- SPLM-FD (Freed Detainees, 2015–present)

- R-SPLM

As a result of the [South Sudanese Civil War](41382446.md) in 2013–2014, the SPLM factionalised into the SPLM-Juba, led by President Salva Kiir,[^cite_note-19] and SPLM-IO (in opposition), led by former Vice President Riek Machar.[^cite_note-20]
