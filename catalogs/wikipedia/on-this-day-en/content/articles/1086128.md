**Mahdia** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): المهدية *[al-Mahdīyah](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5a/Mahdia.wav/Mahdia.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>*) is a [Tunisian](30188.md) coastal city with 76,513 inhabitants, south of [Monastir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastir,_Tunisia) and southeast of [Sousse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sousse).

Mahdia is a provincial centre north of [Sfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sfax). It is important for the associated fish-processing industry, as well as weaving. It is the capital of [Mahdia Governorate](4981961.md).

## History

### Antiquity

The old part of Mahdia corresponds to the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) city called **Aphrodisium** and, later, called **Africa** (a name perhaps derived from the older name),[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] or **Cape Africa**.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-Tissot-6] The [Catholic Church](606848.md)'s list of [titular sees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see) includes a no longer residential [bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric) called Africa[^cite_note-7] and, since there is no record of an episcopal see in [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) times called by either of these names (nor by that of Alipota, another Roman town that Charles Tissot suggested tentatively might be represented by present-day Mehdia),[^cite_note-Tissot-6] it is supposed that the [episcopal see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_see) of Africa was established when the city was held by the [Kingdom of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily), as a part of the [Kingdom of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Africa) (1147–1160) and when [Pope Eugene III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Eugene_III) consecrated a bishop for it in 1148. An inventory of movable property of the church of Africa (*inventarium thesauri Africani*) exists in an archive of the [Cappella Palatina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappella_Palatina) of [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) in Sicily.[^cite_note-8] Salim Dev identified Mahdia instead with ancient *Ruspae* or *[Ruspe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruspe)*,[^cite_note-9] which is more commonly taken to have been at Henchir Sbia (or just Sbia), north of Mahdia, or at the ruins known as Ksour Siad.[^cite_note-10] The most illustrious bishop of this see was [Fulgentius of Ruspe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulgentius_of_Ruspe). The Catholic Church's list of titular sees, which identifies the see of Africa as Mahdia, identifies Ruspe/Ruspae as Henchir Sbia.[^cite_note-11]

The [Mahdia shipwreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdia_shipwreck) – a sunken ship found off Mahdia's shore, containing Greek art treasures – is dated to about 80 BC, the early part of Roman rule in this region.

### Islamic era

Muslim Mahdia was founded by the [Fatimids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimids) under the [Caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph) [Abdallah al-Mahdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdallah_al-Mahdi) and made the capital of [Ifriqiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifriqiya).[^cite_note-TN-12] As the then-newly-created Fatimid Caliphate was a [Shi'a](26961.md) regime supported by a [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) [Kutama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutama) military, the caliph may have been motivated to move his capital here so as to put some distance between his power base and the predominantly [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) city of [Kairouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairouan) (the traditional capital of Ifriqiya up to that point).[^cite_note-\:8-13] Construction began in 916 and the new city was officially inaugurated on 20 February 921, although some construction continued afterward.[^cite_note-\:8-13] In addition to its heavy fortified walls, the city included the Fatimid palaces, an artificial harbor, and a [congregational mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregational_mosque) (the [Great Mosque of Mahdia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Mahdiya)). Most of the Fatimid city has not survived to the present day. The mosque, however, is one of the most well-preserved Fatimid monuments in the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb), although it has been extensively damaged over time and was in large part reconstructed by [archeologists](18951655.md) in the 1960s.[^cite_note-\:8-13] Fragments of mosaic pavements from the palaces have also been discovered from modern excavations.[^cite_note-\:8-13]

The [Zirid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirid_dynasty), which succeeded the Fatimids in the Maghreb, moved their capital here in 1057. In 1087, [the town was attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdia_campaign_of_1087) by raiding ships from [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) and [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa) who burned the Muslim fleet in the harbor. The attack played a critical role in Christians' seizure of control of the Western Mediterranean, which allowed the [First Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade) to be supplied by sea.[^cite_note-14] Their rule was brought to an end by the [Norman conquest of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Kingdom_of_Africa) in 1148. In 1160 the city came under [Almohad rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_conquest_of_Norman_Africa).[^cite_note-15]

[al-Bakri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bakri), in the 11th century, wrote about the city:[^cite_note-16]

> "The city of Mahdia is named after [Ubayd Allah al-Mahdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_Allah_al-Mahdi_Billah), who built it, as mentioned in historical records. It is sixty miles from [Kairouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairouan). The sea surrounds it on three sides, and it can only be entered from the western side. It has a large suburb known as Zwaila, where markets, public baths, and the homes of its inhabitants are located. [Al-Mu‘izz ibn Badis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mu'izz_ibn_Badis) built a wall around this suburb. The city itself is about two miles long, with its width varying—it is never as wide as its full length. All its buildings are made of stone. The city has iron gates with no wood in them, each gate weighing a thousand quintals. Each gate is thirty spans long, with each nail in them weighing six pounds. The gates are decorated with depictions of animals. \[..] Mahdia serves as a harbor for ships from Alexandria, the Levant, Sicily, al-Andalus, and other places. Its port is carved into solid rock and can accommodate thirty ships."

The role of the capital was taken over by [Tunis](57659.md) in the 12th century during the Almohad era, which it remained during the [Hafsid Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafsid_Dynasty). Later the city was subject to many raids. In 1390 it was the target of the [Barbary Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_Crusade), when a French army laid siege to the city but failed to take it.

The city was [captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Mahdiye_\(1550\)) by the Spaniards in 1550. A Spanish garrison remained there until 1553. [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) then offered the charge of the town to the [Order of Saint John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller) who [ruled Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Malta_under_the_Order_of_Saint_John) but they refused it deeming it too expensive.[^cite_note-17] The emperor ordered the Viceroy of Sicily, [Juan de Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Vega), to dismantle Mahdia despite it being a strategically important stronghold. The demolition tasks were carried out by Hernando de Acuña. Shortly after Mahdia was reoccupied by the Ottomans, but only to live by fishing and oil-works, and the town lost its logistic and commercial importance. It remained under Turkish rule until the [19th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_conquest_of_Tunisia).

During the [Nazi Occupation of Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Occupation_of_Tunisia) in [World War II](32927.md), Mahdia was the site where [Khaled Abdelwahhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Abdelwahhab) hid approximately two dozen persecuted [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_Jews).[^cite_note-18]

## Transport

[Gare Mahdia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_Mahdia) forms the southern terminus of the [metre-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre-gauge) [Sahel Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel_Metro) railway line, which runs from [Sousse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sousse) and [Monastir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastir,_Tunisia).[^cite_note-SNCFT-19]
