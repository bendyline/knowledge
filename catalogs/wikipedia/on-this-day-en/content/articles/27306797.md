**Heidi Alexander** (born 17 April 1975) is a British politician who has served as [Secretary of State for Transport](354558.md) since November 2024.[^cite_note-\:2-2] A member of the [Labour Party](19279158.md), she has been the [Member of Parliament](3776360.md) (MP) for [Swindon South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swindon_South) since 2024. Previously she was the MP for [Lewisham East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewisham_East_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) from 2010 to 2018.

Born in Swindon, Alexander studied at Churchfields Comprehensive School (now [Lawn Manor Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawn_Manor_Academy)) and [New College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Swindon) before going on to gain a BA degree in geography and an MA degree in European Urban and Regional Change from [Grey College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_College,_Durham). Alexander then consecutively served as a parliamentary researcher for MP [Joan Ruddock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Ruddock) and as a campaign manager for the charity Clothes Aid.

Alexander was elected to the [Lewisham London Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewisham_London_Borough_Council) for [Evelyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_\(ward\)) in 2004 and was elected the MP for [Lewisham East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewisham_East_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) in the [2010 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election). Alexander was appointed [parliamentary private secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_private_secretary) to [Shadow Environment Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Secretary_of_State_for_Environment,_Food_and_Rural_Affairs) [Mary Creagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Creagh) before becoming an [opposition whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_whip) in 2012. Promoted to Deputy [Shadow Minister for London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Minister_for_London) and a senior opposition whip in 2013, she became [Shadow Secretary of State for Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Secretary_of_State_for_Health_and_Social_Care) following the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Labour_Party_leadership_election_\(UK\)) of [Jeremy Corbyn](415036.md) to the Labour leadership. In 2016 she was part of the coordinated resignation from the Shadow Cabinet [^cite_note-3] ostensibly because of Corbyn's response to the [EU referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum) and the dismissal of [Hilary Benn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilary_Benn) for encouraging fellow Shadow Cabinet members to resign.[^cite_note-4]

In 2018 Alexander resigned her seat to become Deputy Mayor of London for Transport where she remained until 2021. In the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election) Alexander was elected the MP for [Swindon South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swindon_South). Alexander then served as Minister of State for Courts and Legal Services until she was appointed Transport Secretary in November 2024, replacing [Louise Haigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Haigh).

## Early life and career

Alexander was born in [Swindon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swindon), [Wiltshire](51231.md) to Malcolm, an electrician, and Elaine Alexander ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Lanham). She was educated at [Churchfields Comprehensive School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchfields_School) and New College Sixth Form. Alexander studied at [Grey College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_College,_Durham),[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] where she received a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in geography and an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts#United_Kingdom_and_Ireland) degree in European Urban and Regional Change.[^cite_note-7]

Alexander had a 6-month placement in the office of [Cherie Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherie_Blair) at [10 Downing Street](226294.md) in 1998.[^cite_note-\:0-8] She worked as a Parliamentary researcher for Lewisham MP [Joan Ruddock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Ruddock) from 1999 to 2005, and as campaigns manager for the charity Clothes Aid from 2005 to 2006.[^cite_note-9]

## Political career

### Local government

Alexander served as a member of [Lewisham London Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewisham_London_Borough_Council) for [Evelyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_\(ward\)) from a by-election in 2004 until [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Lewisham_London_Borough_Council_election). She was Deputy [Mayor of Lewisham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Lewisham) and Cabinet Member for Regeneration from 2006 to 2010. Alexander was selected as the Labour candidate for [Lewisham East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewisham_East_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) in October 2009, and elected to Parliament at the [2010 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election).[^cite_note-10]

### House of Commons

Shortly after her election to Parliament, Alexander was appointed [parliamentary private secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_private_secretary) to [Mary Creagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Creagh), then the [shadow environment secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_environment_secretary). She became an [opposition whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_whip) in 2012,[^cite_note-11] and was promoted to Deputy Shadow [Minister for London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_London) and a senior Opposition whip in 2013.[^cite_note-\:0-8] She served as a member of the [Communities and Local Government Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communities_and_Local_Government_Committee) from 2010 to 2012 and [Health Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Select_Committee) from 2016 to 2017.

Following [Jeremy Corbyn](415036.md)'s election as Labour leader in September 2015, Alexander joined the [shadow cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_cabinet) as [shadow secretary of state for health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_secretary_of_state_for_health).[^cite_note-Morris-12]

Heidi Alexander became the first shadow cabinet minister to resign in June 2016,[^cite_note-13] calling for a new party leader following the EU referendum and dismissal of [Hilary Benn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilary_Benn).[^cite_note-14] In an opinion piece for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, Alexander wrote "I loved being the shadow health secretary. But I hated being part of the shadow cabinet...because it was entirely dysfunctional" and "so inept, so unprofessional, so shoddy".[^cite_note-15]

### Deputy Mayor of London

In May 2018, Alexander [resigned her seat in Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Lewisham_East_by-election) to become [Deputy Mayor of London for Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Mayor_of_London_for_Transport) under [Sadiq Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiq_Khan).[^cite_note-guardian-20180508-16] She served as Deputy Chair of [Transport for London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_for_London) in her role, and remained on the body's board until the opening of [Crossrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossrail).

During her time in the role, she was tasked with maintaining London transport during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and leading several rounds of government bailout negotiations.[^cite_note-\:1-17] She notably worked to tackle delays to the opening of Crossrail and re-opening of [Hammersmith Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammersmith_Bridge), and took up cycling to promote that method of transport. Initially planning to step down at the end of Khan's first term until the pandemic, she departed her role in late 2021 to "consider her next career move".[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-\:1-17]

### Return to Parliament

In June 2022, Alexander announced her intention to seek selection as the Labour candidate for [Swindon South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swindon_South).[^cite_note-19] She was selected in July 2022 as [prospective parliamentary candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospective_parliamentary_candidate) for the seat,[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] and was successful at the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election). Alexander was [subsequently appointed](77280613.md) [Minister of State for Courts and Legal Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_for_Courts_and_Legal_Services) at the [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(United_Kingdom\)).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

#### Appointment to Cabinet

Alexander was appointed [Secretary of State for Transport](354558.md) in November 2024, succeeding [Louise Haigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Haigh).[^cite_note-\:2-2] She was appointed to the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_\(United_Kingdom\)), giving her the [honorific title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorific) of [The Right Honourable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Right_Honourable) for life.[^cite_note-24]

## Political views

Alexander supported [Andy Burnham](414818.md) in the [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Labour_Party_leadership_election_\(UK\)) and [2015 Labour leadership elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Labour_Party_leadership_election_\(UK\)),[^cite_note-25] and [Owen Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Smith) in the [2016 Labour leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Labour_Party_leadership_election_\(UK\)).[^cite_note-26] She chaired [Sadiq Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiq_Khan)'s campaign for the [2016 London mayoral election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_London_mayoral_election).[^cite_note-guardian-20180425-27]

Alexander opposed the triggering of [Article 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_50) following the EU referendum, proposing a "reasoned amendment" in January 2017 to throw out the article.[^cite_note-theguardianwalkerlabourmpsputforward-28] She co-founded the Labour Campaign for the Single Market in 2017, and is a supporter of the pro-EU group [Open Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Britain).[^cite_note-open-britain-20170623-29][^cite_note-guardian-20180425-27][^cite_note-guardian-20180208-30]

In November 2024, Alexander voted in favour of the [Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminally_Ill_Adults_\(End_of_Life\)_Bill), which proposes to legalise [assisted suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_suicide_in_the_United_Kingdom) for terminally ill adults in the UK.[^cite_note-31]

## Personal life

Alexander married Martin Ballantyne in 2011.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]
