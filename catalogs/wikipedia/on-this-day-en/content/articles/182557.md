**Jewish fundamentalism** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): פונדמנטליזם יהודי) refers to [fundamentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamentalism) in the context of [Judaism](15624.md). The term *fundamentalism* was originally used in reference to [Christian fundamentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_fundamentalism), a [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) movement which emphasizes a belief in [biblical literalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_literalism). Today, it is commonly used in reference to movements that oppose [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism), [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism), and ecumenical tendencies within societies, as well as modernist, liberal, and ecumenical tendencies within specific [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion), and it is often coupled with extremist ideologies and/or political movements. The use of this definition is important in a Jewish context because the two movements which are most commonly associated with Jewish fundamentalism, [Religious Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism) and [Haredi Judaism](53835.md),[^cite_note-brit1-1] stray far from biblical literalism due to the importance of the [Oral Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_Law#Judaism) within Judaism.

## Overview

Like other fundamentalist movements, fundamentalist Judaism usually presents itself as the only valid form of Judaism, [Jewish culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_culture), and truth. However, [Religious Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism) and [Haredi Judaism](53835.md), the two movements which are most broadly associated with Jewish fundamentalism, differ in significant ways, and historically, they have sometimes opposed each other (but recently, there has been more overlap, partly due to the rise of the [Hardal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardal) movement). [Religious Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism) is more associated with political extremism, while [Haredi Judaism](53835.md) is associated with men studying Torah every day and making sure all actions are in line with the will of God.[^cite_note-2]

## Haredi Judaism

Main article: [Haredi Judaism](53835.md)

Haredi Judaism consists of groups within [Orthodox Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) that are characterized by their strict adherence to *[halakha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halakha)* (Jewish law) and traditions, in opposition to modern values and practices.[^cite_note-\:3-3][^cite_note-4] Its members are usually referred to as **ultra-Orthodox** in English; however, the term "ultra-Orthodox" is considered pejorative by many of its adherents, who prefer terms like **strictly Orthodox** or **Haredi**.[^cite_note-Shafran-5] Haredi Jews regard themselves as the most religiously authentic group of Jews,[^cite_note-DumovaFiordo2011-6][^cite_note-7] but other [movements of Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_religious_movements) disagree.[^cite_note-Rubel2010-8]

## Religious Zionism

Main article: [Religious Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism)

[Religious Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionism) is an ideology that combines [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) and [Orthodox Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism). It primarily began with the teachings of Rabbi [Abraham Isaac Kook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Isaac_Kook) (1865–1935), who saw Zionism as a part of a divine scheme to [return Jews to their ancestral homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_of_Israel), and eventually bring about the coming of the [Messiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messiah#Judaism). Religious Zionism gained a new momentum after the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, when Israel conquered the [West Bank](33209.md), a territory which is rich in [Biblical Jewish history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historicity_of_the_Bible). The [Gush Emunim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gush_Emunim) movement took off under the leadership of [Zvi Yehuda Kook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvi_Yehuda_Kook), and it also spearheaded the proliferation of [Israeli settlements](15123.md) in the newly conquered territory.

Religious Zionism is still a relatively broad term which encompasses both moderate and extreme elements. The extremist elements are frequently associated with [anti-Arab racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Arab_racism), [anti-Palestinianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Palestinianism), and [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence); frequently, they are associated with ideological inspiration from [Kahanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahanism). They have also been associated with [terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_extremist_terrorism) against [Palestinians](23267.md), and in some cases, they have also been associated with terrorism against the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md). The [Hilltop Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilltop_Youth) movement is especially associated with the most extreme forms of Religious Zionism.[^cite_note-9]

## Study

Jewish fundamentalism was ignored for much of the 20th century, and it was only when it began to have an effect on [Israeli politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_politics) and international relations that scholars began to study it in earnest.[^cite_note-10]
