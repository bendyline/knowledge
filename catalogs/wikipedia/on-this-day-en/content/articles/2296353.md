**Birbhum district** (Bengali pronunciation: [\[biːrbʰuːm\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Bengali)) is an [administrative unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District#India) in the [Indian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_state) of [West Bengal](34040.md). It is the northernmost district of [Burdwan division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burdwan_division)—one of the five [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_West_Bengal) of West Bengal. The district headquarters is in [Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suri,_Birbhum). Other important cities are [Rampurhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampurhat), [Bolpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolpur) and [Sainthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainthia).[^cite_note-blocdir-4][^cite_note-admin-5] [Jamtara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamtara_district), [Dumka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumka_district) and [Pakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakur_district) districts of the state of [Jharkhand](197225.md) lie at the western border of this district; the border in other directions is covered by the districts of [Bardhaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardhaman_district) of [Purba Bardhaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purba_Bardhaman_district), [Paschim Bardhaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paschim_Bardhaman_district) and [Murshidabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murshidabad_district) of West Bengal.

Often called "the land of red soil",[^cite_note-paschim5-6] Birbhum is noted for its topography and its cultural heritage which is somewhat different from the other districts in West Bengal. The western part of Birbhum is a bushy region, a part of the [Chota Nagpur Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Plateau). This region gradually merges with the fertile alluvial farmlands in the east.[^cite_note-paschim2-7]

This district saw many cultural and religious movements in history. [Tarapith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapith_Temple) is a [Hindu pilgrimage site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_pilgrimage_sites_in_India) located at 9km distance from [Rampurhat city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampurhat), which is one of the prominent places in Birbhum where every day a large number of pilgrims from across the country, neighboring states, and even abroad gather.This town is particularly known for the [Tarapith Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapith_Temple) and its adjoining Hindu [crematory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crematory) ground. The [Visva Bharati University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visva_Bharati_University) at [Santiniketan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiniketan), established by [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore), is one of the places Birbhum is known for.[^cite_note-8] Many festivals are celebrated in the district, including [Poush Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poush_Mela).[^cite_note-paschim8-9]

Birbhum is primarily an agricultural district with around 75.09% of the population being dependent on agriculture.[^cite_note-paschim4-10] Principal industries of the district include cotton and silk harvesting and weaving, rice and oilseed milling, [lac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lac_\(resin\)) harvesting, stone mining and metalware and pottery manufacture.[^cite_note-paschim1-11] [Bakreshwar Thermal Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakreshwar_Thermal_Power_Station) is the only [heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry) in the district.[^cite_note-pramanik-12]

## Etymology

The name Birbhum comes probably from the term 'land' ('bhumi') of the 'brave' ('bir') or [Bir king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_king)').[^cite_note-gazeteer1-13][^cite_note-paschim6-14] Another theory says that the district bears the name of [Bagdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagdi_caste) king [Bir Malla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Malla), who ruled in the area from 1501 to 1554 CE.[^cite_note-gazeteer1-13][^cite_note-paschim6-14] *Bir* in the [Santali language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santali_language) means forests; therefore, Birbhum could also mean a land of forests.[^cite_note-gazeteer1-13][^cite_note-paschim6-14]

## Geography



Map of Birbhum district showing CD blocks and municipal areas

Situated between 23° 32' 30" (right above the [tropic of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_cancer)) and 24° 35' 0" north latitude and 87° 5' 25" and 88° 1' 40" east longitudes, and about 4,545 square kilometres (1,755 sq mi) in area, this district is triangular. River Ajay forms the southern base and the apex of the triangle points north. The river forms the boundary between the districts of Birbhum and Bardhaman. The state of [Jharkhand](197225.md) is at the northern and the western border of Birbhum and Murshidabad is at the east.[^cite_note-admin-5][^cite_note-paschim1-11][^cite_note-gazeteer1-13]

Geographically, this area lies at the northeastern end of the [Chota Nagpur Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Plateau), as it slopes down and merges with the alluvial plains of the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges). In the district there is only hilly area of natural rock formation situated near [Dubrajpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrajpur) town called [Mama Bhagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mama_Bhagne) *Pahar* (Hills). The western portion of the district is historically known as *[Vajjabhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajjabhumi)* or *Bajrabhumi*.[^cite_note-paschim2-7][^cite_note-gazeteer2-15] It is an undulating upland that is generally barren. The comparatively more fertile eastern portion, constituting the northeastern Rarh region, merges with the Gangetic plain. *Vajjabhumi* is included in the Rarh region, and the rest of Rarh is called *Sumha* to differentiate it from *Vajjabhumi*.[^cite_note-paschim2-7][^cite_note-gazeteer2-15]

### Climate



Rivers and towns of Birbhum

The climate on the western side is dry and extreme but is relatively milder on the eastern side. During summer, the temperature can shoot well above 40 °C (104 °F) and in winters it can drop to around 6 °C (43 °F).[^cite_note-paschim1-11] Rainfall is higher in the western areas as compared to the eastern areas. The annual average rainfall in Rajnagar is 1,405 millimetres (55.3 in) and in Nanoor it is 1,212 millimetres (47.7 in), mostly in the monsoon months (June to October).[^cite_note-paschim2-7][^cite_note-gazeteer1-13]

### Rivers

A number of rivers flow across Birbhum. Some of the most notable include [Ajay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajay_River), [Mayurakshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayurakshi_River), [Kopai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopai_River), [Bakreshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakreshwar_River), [Brahmani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmani_River_\(Birbhum\)), [Dwarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarka_River), [Hinglo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinglo_River), and [Bansloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bansloi_River).[^cite_note-admin-5][^cite_note-paschim1-11] Almost all the rivers originate higher up on the Chota Nagpur plateau and flow across Birbhum in a west–east direction. During the [monsoon](57630.md) season, the rivers flow high and swift compared to the dry summer season, resulting in a cycle of floods and droughts.[^cite_note-paschim2-7][^cite_note-paschim4-10] The Mayurakshi provides irrigation for about 2,428 square kilometres (937 sq mi).[^cite_note-Selim-17]

## History

### Prehistory



Terracota design depicting *Ramayana* war at Jayadev Kenduli



Statue of Rabindranath Tagore by [K P Krishnakumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_P_Krishnakumar) at Amar Kutir

The area now known as Birbhum was inhabited from pre-historic times. Some of the archaeological sites related to [Pandu Rajar Dhibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandu_Rajar_Dhibi) of [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) remains are located in Birbhum.[^cite_note-18] Stone age implements have been found at several places in the district.[^cite_note-19]

According to the old [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) book *[Acaranga Sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acaranga_Sutra)*, the last (24th) great [Tirthankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirthankara) [Mahavira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavira) had wandered through this land, referred to as the "pathless country of [Ladha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarh_region) in *Vajjabhumi* and *[Subbhabhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subbhabhumi)* (probably [Suhma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhma_kingdom))" in the fifth century BCE.[^cite_note-admin-5][^cite_note-gazeteer2-15][^cite_note-20] According to some historians, the spread of [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) and [Buddhism](3267529.md) in the Rarh region was part of the process of Aryanisation of the area.[^cite_note-21] Based on *Divyabdan*, a [Buddhist](3267529.md) text, Dr. Atul Sur has inferred that [Gautam Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautam_Buddha) probably traversed this area to go to [Pundravardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pundravardhana) and [Samatata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samatata).[^cite_note-atulsur-22]

The [Rarh region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarh_region), once a part of the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) was later included in the empires of the [imperial Guptas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire), [Shashanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashanka) and [Harshavardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harshavardhana). After dismemberment of Harshavardhana's empire, the region was ruled by the [Palas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire), under whose rule Buddhism, particularly the [Vajrayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajrayana) cult, flourished.[^cite_note-23] In the seventh century CE, the Chinese traveller [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang) described some of the monasteries he visited in the region.[^cite_note-gazeteer2-15][^cite_note-atulsur-22] In the 12th century CE, the [Sena dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_dynasty) took control of the area.[^cite_note-admin-5]

### Medieval age



Krishna travelling to Mathura in a 17th-century painting from Birbhum

The region was ruled by Muslims in the 13th century CE, although their control over the western parts of the district appears to have been minimal, with true power resting in the hands of the local Hindu chiefs, known as the Bir Rajas (Bagdi Malla dynasty).[^cite_note-admin-5] The towns of Hetampur, Birsinghpur and Rajnagar contain their relics.[^cite_note-Gupta-24] [Hetampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetampur) and [Rajnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajnagar,_Birbhum) kingdoms ruled most of Birbhum and parts of Burdwan, Maldah and [Jharkhand](197225.md) from [Dubrajpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrajpur) (meaning two kingdoms – Hetampur & Rajnagar). [Minhaj-i-Siraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minhaj-i-Siraj), the author of the *Tabaqat-i-Nasiri*, mentions Lakhnur as the *thanah* (headquarters) of the Rarh *wing* of the Muslim rule and an important frontier post. The location of Lakhnur, though not yet identified, falls in Birbhum.[^cite_note-admin-5][^cite_note-gazeteer2-15]

### Colonial era



1779 map of the [Jungle Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_Terry) District.

The area which is now Birbhum district was badly impacted by the [Great Bengal famine of 1770](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Bengal_famine_of_1770). Many villages were wiped out entirely, and even in large towns, over three quarters of households perished.[^cite_note-25]

The administrative unit called Birbhum was first formed in 1787 under the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company), as "District Beerbhoom". The area had previously been administered as a part of Murshidabad district. The original district was much larger than its modern incarnation.[^cite_note-admin-5][^cite_note-gazeteer2-15]

In the late 18th century, Birbhum and surrounding areas were affected by more famines followed by a peasants' insurrection. Although the insurrection primarily targeted [zamindars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamindar) and the government, trading and manufacturing centres were also looted. In June 1789, the manufacturing town of [Ilambazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilambazar) was sacked, although it recovered and became a magnet for manufacturing and trade.[^cite_note-26]

Until 1793, Birbhum included "Bishenpore" or [Bishnupur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnupur,_West_Bengal), which is now part of the Bankura district. Until the [1857 Sepoy Mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rebellion_of_1857), the [Santhal Parganas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhal_Parganas) was part of Birbhum; the district thus sprawled up to [Deoghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deoghar) in the west. The immediate reason then for separating the western tribal majority areas was the [Santhal rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhal_rebellion) of 1855–56.[^cite_note-admin-5][^cite_note-gazeteer2-15] In 1872, Birbhum district comprises 1,344 square miles (3,480 km<sup>2</sup>) area with 695,921 inhabitants.[^cite_note-27]

## Economy



A common Birbhum village scene



Product display of *Amar Kutir*

Birbhum is primarily an agricultural district with around 75% of the people dependent on agriculture.[^cite_note-paschim4-10] While 159.3 km<sup>2</sup> (61.5 sq mi) of land is occupied by forests, 3,329.05 km<sup>2</sup> (1,285.35 sq mi) of land is used for agricultural purposes.[^cite_note-paschim1-11] 91.02% of the population live in villages.[^cite_note-paschim4-10] Out of total 4,50,313 farmers(holding 3,20,610 hectares of land), 3,59,404 are marginal farmers(holding 1,41,813 hectares altogether), 63,374 are small farmers(holding 95,144 hectares altogether), 26,236 are semi-medium farmers(holding 76,998 hectares altogether), 1,290 are medium farmers(holding 6,215 hectare altogether), and 9 are large farmers (holding 440 hectares of land). The average size of land holding per farmer is 0.71 hectares. 6,07,172 people work as agricultural labourers in Birbhum.[^cite_note-birbhum.gov.in-16] Major crops produced in the district include rice, legumes, wheat, corn (maize), potatoes and sugar cane.[^cite_note-paschim4-10] The district has thirteen cold storages.[^cite_note-paschim1-11] Land with irrigation facilities in 2001-02 totalled 2,763.9 km<sup>2</sup> (1,067.1 sq mi).[^cite_note-paschim4-10] There are five barrages, providing irrigation support. [Canada Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayurakshi_River#Massanjore_Dam) on the Mayurakshi river at Massanjore lies close to the border of Birbhum and the [Dumka district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumka_district) in Jharkhand. Further down the Mayurakshi is the Tilpara Barrage at Suri.[^cite_note-paschim4-10]

Birbhum is a major centre of [cottage industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottage_industries). Perhaps the most notable cottage industry is a non-profit rural organisation named [Amar Kutir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amar_Kutir). Other main industries in Birbhum are agriculture-based industries, textiles, forestry, arts and crafts. Sriniketan is noted for its dairy industry and as a forestry centre. Some of the notable forms of cottage industries of Birbhum include textile—especially cotton and locally harvested tussar silk, jute works, batik, kantha stitch, macramé (weaving by knotting threads), leather, pottery and terracotta, solapith, woodcarving, bamboo and cane craft, metal works and tribal crafts.[^cite_note-paschim1-11] There are 8,883 small and medium scale industries. Principal industries of the district include cotton and silk harvesting and weaving, rice and oilseed milling, lac harvesting, and metalware and pottery manufacture.[^cite_note-paschim1-11] [Bakreshwar Thermal Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakreshwar_Thermal_Power_Station) (210 MW x 3 + 210 MW x 2 under construction) is the only heavy industry in the district.[^cite_note-pramanik-12]

[Sainthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainthia) is known as business capital of Birbhum and an economically important city. Sainthia is the major centre for export and import of cottage industries. Sainthia is known as the business capital of Birbhum and an economically important city. Sainthia is a major center for the export and import of cottage industries. As the business epicenter of Birbhum, Sainthia was home to a prominent coconut oil manufacturer established by the Chandra family. 'Lion (সিংহ) Marka' (Lion Brand) coconut oil, which was manufactured and processed in a factory located in main Sainthia. This iconic brand not only gained statewide and national recognition but also became a symbol of the Sainthia's industrial prowess, significantly contributing to its economic growth. The factory's operations, which spanned from the 1950s to nearly 45 years, were a cornerstone of Sainthia's prominence until they ceased in 1992. Today, plenty of large, semi-large scale businesses run in this city, credit to its well connected logistics and rail network. Moreover, the economy stands tall credit to sales of agricultural based products. [^cite_note-28] [^cite_note-29] [^cite_note-30]

In 2006 the [Ministry of Panchayati Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Panchayati_Raj) named Birbhum one of the country's 250 [most backward districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_India) (out of a total of [640](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India)).[^cite_note-brgf-31] It is one of the eleven districts in West Bengal currently receiving funds from the Backward Regions Grant Fund Programme (BRGF).[^cite_note-brgf-31]

## Political and administrative divisions

The district comprises three subdivisions: [Suri Sadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suri_Sadar_subdivision), [Bolpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolpur_subdivision) and [Rampurhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampurhat_subdivision).[^cite_note-blocdir-4] [Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suri,_Birbhum) is the district headquarters. There are 26 police stations, 19 development blocks, 6 municipalities and 167 [gram panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_panchayat) in this district.[^cite_note-blocdir-4][^cite_note-adminsetup-32] Other than municipality area, each subdivision contains community development blocs which in turn are divided into rural areas and census towns. There are seven urban units: six municipalities and one [census town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_town).[^cite_note-adminsetup-32][^cite_note-33] The latest amongst the urban units to have a municipality was Nalhati in 2000.[^cite_note-34]

Before delimitation the district was divided into 12 [assembly constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_constituency) (AC):[^cite_note-curAC-35] [Nanoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoor_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #283), [Bolpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolpur_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #284), [Labhpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labpur_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #285), [Dubrajpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrajpur_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #286), [Rajnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajnagar,_Birbhum_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #287), [Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suri_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #288), [Mahammad Bazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahammad_Bazar_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #289), [Mayureswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayureswar_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #290), [Rampurhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampurhat_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #291), [Hansan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansan_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #292), [Nalhati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalhati_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #293) and [Murarai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murarai_\(Vidhan_Sabha_constituency\)) (AC #294). The constituencies of Nanoor, Rajnagar, Mayureswar and Hansan were reserved for [Scheduled Castes (SC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_castes_and_scheduled_tribes) candidates.[^cite_note-curAC-35] [Vidhan Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly) was elected in [2006 assembly elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_West_Bengal_state_assembly_election), which took place before the rearrangement of parliamentary and assembly constituencies as per order of the [Delimitation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delimitation_Commission) in respect of the [delimitation of constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delimitation_of_constituencies) in the West Bengal. Delimitation was made effective for all elections in the state of West Bengal that was held on or after 19 February 2008.[^cite_note-36] [2009 Indian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Indian_general_election) was based on the newly formed parliamentary constituencies and the newly formed assembly constituencies have representatives in the 2011 elections of West Bengal.

## Transport



Katwa to Ahmedpur Narrow gauge railway

The [Panagarh–Morgram Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagarh%E2%80%93Morgram_Highway) runs through the district. All the towns and villages are connected by roads. The total length of roads in the district are: surfaced–2,413 km (1,499 mi) and unsurfaced–4,674 km (2,904 mi). Against this the total length of rail track in the district is 201.32 km (125.09 mi), including 26.55 km (16.50 mi) of the [Ahmadpur-Katwa line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadpur-Katwa_line), laid in 1917.[^cite_note-paschim1-11] The [Sahibganj Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahibganj_Loop) of the [Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Railway_\(India\)), laid in 1862, passes through this district. [Rampurhat Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampurhat_Junction_railway_station) is the most busiest railway station of this district. There is a junction at Nalhati for the connection to [Azimganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaganj_Azimganj) in Murshidabad district. The [Andal-Sainthia Branch Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal-Sainthia_Branch_Line) connects it to the main Howrah-Delhi main line at Andal.[^cite_note-paschim1-11]

According to the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India) Birbhum district has a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India) of 3,502,404,[^cite_note-districtcensus-40] roughly equal to the nation of [Lithuania](17675.md)[^cite_note-cia-41] or the US state of [Connecticut](6466.md).[^cite_note-42] This gives it a ranking of 84th in India (out of a total of [640](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India)).[^cite_note-districtcensus-40] The district has a population density of 771 inhabitants per square kilometre (2,000/sq mi).[^cite_note-districtcensus-40] Its [population growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning_in_India) over the decade 2001-2011 was 16.15%.[^cite_note-districtcensus-40] Birbhum has a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) of 956 [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_India) for every 1000 males,[^cite_note-districtcensus-40] and a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 70.9%. 12.83% of the population lives in urban areas. [Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes) made up 29.50% and 6.92% of the population respectively.[^cite_note-districtcensus-40][^cite_note-43] Other than those speaking the local dialect of [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), there are tribal [Santhals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhal_people) and ten other [tribal communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adivasi) in Birbhum with some presence, amongst whom [Koda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kora_\(tribe\)), [Mahali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahali) and [Oraons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oraon_people) are more common.[^cite_note-44]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Birbhum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Birbhum)



Baul singers at Santiniketan

The [bauls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baul) of Birbhum, their philosophy and their songs form a notable representation of the folk culture of the district. Birbhum has been home to *kabiyal*s, *kirtaniya*s and other folk culture groups.[^cite_note-paschim8-9][^cite_note-52]

The numerous [fairs in Birbhum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairs_in_Birbhum) start with *Poush Mela* at Santiniketan and follows through the Bengali month of [Poush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poush) until [Makar Sankranti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makar_Sankranti). Particularly lively is the fair at [Jaydev Kenduli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaydev_Kenduli).[^cite_note-paschim8-9] Festivities are organised across the seasons.[^cite_note-paschim12-53] People of Birbhum patronise folk entertainment programmes such as [jatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatra_\(Bengal\)), [kavigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavigan), [raibenshe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raibenshe) and [alkap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkap).[^cite_note-paschim7-54]

Many poets were born in this district, as for example, [Chandidas (Rami)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandidas).[^cite_note-paschim7-54] In addition to being a confluence of [Vaishnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnavism), [Shakta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaktism) and [Saiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaivism) cultures, Birbhum villages observe ancient customs like worship of gramdevta (*gram* means village and *devta* means deity) in many forms.[^cite_note-Mitra-55][^cite_note-paschim10-56]

Amongst the major attractions of Birbhum are [Bakreshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakreshwar), [Tarapith Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapith_Temple), [Dwarbasini temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarbasini) at [Chondrapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chondrapur), [Fullara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullara) at [Labpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labpur_\(community_development_block\)) and [Patharchapuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patharchapuri). Birbhum has many old temples, such as the ones at Jaydev Kenduli, Surul and Nanoor, with delicate decorative tiles made of [terra cotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_cotta) (burnt clay).[^cite_note-paschim9-57]

## Flora and fauna

The eastern area of Birbhum is a part of the rice plains of West Bengal, and the vegetation includes usual characteristics of rice fields in Bengal, such as species of [Aponogeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aponogeton), [Utricularia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utricularia), [Drosera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosera), [Philcoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philcoxia), [Scrophulariaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrophulariaceae) and similar aquatic or [palustrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palustrine) genera.[^cite_note-gazeteer1-13][^cite_note-admingeo-58] In the drier western region of the district, the characteristic shrubs and herbs include species of [Wendlandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendlandia), [Convolvulaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convolvulaceae), [Stipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stipa), [Tragus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragus_\(plant\)), [Spermacoce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermacoce), [Ziziphus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziziphus), [Capparis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capparis) and similar plants that grows on laterite soil.[^cite_note-admingeo-58] [Mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), [palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae), and [bamboo](39029.md) are among common trees in Birbhum.[^cite_note-admingeo-58] Other common species of plants include [jackfruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackfruit), [arjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminalia_arjuna), [sal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorea_robusta), [guava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guava), kend and [mahua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhuca_longifolia).[^cite_note-admingeo-58]

Other than [feral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feral) dogs and domestic cattle, the most frequently encountered non-human mammal is the [hanuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semnopithecus_entellus), a long tailed [grey langur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_langur) prevalent in the [Gangetic plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangetic_plain). Some wild [boars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boars) and [wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_wolf) may still be spotted in the small forests of Chinpai, Bandarsol and Charicha.[^cite_note-admingeo-58] Leopards and bears are not to be seen any more in the wild.[^cite_note-admingeo-58] Sometimes during the season when mahua trees bloom, wild [Asiatic elephants](379035.md) from Jharkhand come in trampling crops and threatening life and property.[^cite_note-admingeo-58] Birds of Birbhum include a mix of hilly and plain-land dwelling species like [partridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partridge), [pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeon), green pigeon, [water fowls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_fowl), [doyel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doyel), [Indian robin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_robin), [drongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drongo), [hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawk), [cuckoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuckoo), [koel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koel), [sunbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunbird), [Indian roller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_roller), [parrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parrot), [babbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World_babbler), and some [migratory birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migratory_birds).[^cite_note-admingeo-58]

[Ballabhpur Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballabhpur_Wildlife_Sanctuary) near Santiniketan was declared a sanctuary in 1977.[^cite_note-ballavpur-59] Economically important trees are planted here and [blackbucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackbuck), [spotted deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_deer), jackals, foxes and a variety of water birds live in its 2 km<sup>2</sup> (0.8 mi<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-ballavpur-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-parks-61]

According to the 2011 census Birbhum district had a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 70.9%.[^cite_note-districtcensus-40] up from 62.16% in 2001. In 1951 census, the literacy rate was 17.74%. It increased to 48.56% in 1991.[^cite_note-paschim3-62]

The growth of literacy in the last decade of the twentieth century was particularly remarkable with special emphasis on the eradication of illiteracy. While it is feared that Birbhum may not be able to fulfill the national objective of sending all children in the age group 6–14 years to school by 2010, efforts are on in that direction.[^cite_note-paschim3-62]

The district has 127 libraries supported by the government, one private library and one district library.[^cite_note-paschim1-11]

## Sports

One of the most popular outdoor sports in the villages of Birbhum used to be [danguli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilli-danda) (literal meaning "ball and stick").[^cite_note-paschim12-53] However, in recent times [cricket](25675557.md) has replaced some of its popularity. A major cricket event named as: *Dream Gold Cup* is organised in Labpur every year in the month of January. Cricket teams from all over the state comes for participation. It is played in the *Stadium field* in *Sasthinagar.* A sport that can be played outdoors and indoors and is popular among children is [marbles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_\(toy\)), which involves projecting a striker bead with fingers to hit an ensemble of black or green glass beads.

In addition to [cricket](25675557.md), the other most popular outdoor sports of this district are [football](10568.md) and [kabadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaddi) and [volleyball](32558.md).[^cite_note-paschim12-53]
