**Washington County** is a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(United_States\)) located in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Tennessee](30395.md). As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population was 133,001.[^cite_note-QF-2] Its [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) is [Jonesborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonesborough,_Tennessee).[^cite_note-GR6-3] The county's largest city and a regional educational, medical and commercial center is [Johnson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_City,_Tennessee). Washington County is Tennessee's oldest county, having been established in 1777 when the state was still part of [North Carolina](21650.md).[^cite_note-4] Washington County is part of the Johnson City, TN [Metropolitan Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_City_metropolitan_area), which is a component of the Johnson City–[Kingsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsport,_Tennessee)–[Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol,_Tennessee), TN-[VA](32432.md) [Combined Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Statistical_Area), commonly known as the "[Tri-Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-Cities,_Tennessee)" region.

## History

### Watauga and the Washington District

Washington County is rooted in the [Watauga settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watauga_Association), which were established in the early 1770s in the vicinity of what is now [Elizabethton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabethton,_Tennessee), in adjacent Carter County. At the outbreak of the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) in 1776, the Wataugans organized the "Washington District", which was governed by a [committee of safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_safety_\(American_Revolution\)). North Carolina initially refused to recognize the settlements as legal, but finally agreed to annex the district after the settlers thwarted an invasion by hostile Cherokees. The settlements were governed as the [Washington District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_District,_North_Carolina), which originally included all of what is now Tennessee. The district was reorganized as Washington County in 1777.[^cite_note-tehc-5]

### Washington County, North Carolina and Franklin

From 1777 until 1784, North Carolina held nominal control over the county, but did little for the residents, at least in their eyes. So the area citizens formed, in 1784, the [State of Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Franklin) to meet their needs. Franklin was an early attempt to create a fourteenth state prior to [Kentucky](16846.md) and [Vermont](32578.md)'s admissions into the union. The county reverted to North Carolina control, however, following the failure of the Franklin state government in 1788.

### Part of Tennessee

In 1790 the area became part of [Southwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Territory), and afterward it was admitted to the Union in 1796 as the 16th state.[^cite_note-tehc-5] [Jonesboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonesborough,_Tennessee), the county seat of Washington County, is Tennessee's oldest town. With many buildings restored, it comprises one of the nation's most authentic historic districts of the period 1790–1870.

Washington County was divided between pro-Union and pro-secession sentiments at the outset of the Civil War. In Tennessee's Ordinance of Secession referendum on June 8, 1861, Washington Countians voted 1,445 to 1,022 in favor of remaining in the Union.[^cite_note-6] One of the bridges targeted by the [East Tennessee bridge-burners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee_bridge_burnings) in November 1861 was located in what is now [Watauga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watauga,_Tennessee) near the Washington-Carter county line.[^cite_note-7] [Landon Carter Haynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landon_Carter_Haynes), a Confederate senator, hailed from Washington County.[^cite_note-tehc-5]

Johnson City, originally known as Johnson's Depot, was a major [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) center for the southeastern states, connecting the region for freight transportation and passengers. It was the headquarters for both the standard-gauge Carolina, Clinchfield, and Ohio ([Clinchfield Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinchfield_Railroad)), which required the excavation and blasting of 17 tunnels during its construction; and the narrow-gauge [East Tennessee and Western North Carolina Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee_and_Western_North_Carolina_Railroad) (Tweetsie). Significant restoration is underway, as well as publicizing the railroad heritage of the Johnson's Depot Historic District. Other historic properties are being restored as representative of Johnson City's late nineteenth and early twentieth-century era as a railway center.

## Geography



Farm near [Limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone,_Tennessee)



Rainbow and railroad tracks near [Telford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telford,_Tennessee)

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau), the county has a total area of 330 square miles (850 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 326 square miles (840 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 3.3 square miles (8.5 km<sup>2</sup>) (1.0%) is water.[^cite_note-GR1-8] The western portion of the county is situated in the [Ridge-and-Valley Appalachians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge-and-Valley_Appalachians), which are characterized by long, narrow ridges roughly oriented northeast-to-southwest. The county's most prominent Ridge-and-Valley features rise in the vicinity of its northwestern border with [Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawkins_County,_Tennessee) and [Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sullivan_County,_Tennessee) counties. The eastern portion of the county lies within the [Blue Ridge Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Mountains), specifically the [Bald Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_Mountains) (south of the [Nolichucky River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolichucky_River)) and the [Unaka Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unaka_Range) (north of the Nolichucky).[^cite_note-9] Buffalo Mountain, a long ridge that straddles much of Washington's eastern boundary, contains the county's highest point, 3,520-foot (1,070 m) Pinnacle Knob. The [Cherokee National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee_National_Forest) protects much of the extreme eastern part of the county. Sampson Mountain, which rises in the southeastern part of the county, is home to a designated [wilderness area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wilderness_Preservation_System).

The [Nolichucky River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolichucky_River) flows through the southern part of Washington County. The [Watauga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watauga_River) flows the northern part of the county, and forms part of the county's border with [Sullivan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sullivan_County,_Tennessee). The lower section of the Watauga River is part of [Boone Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boone_Dam).

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the county had a population of 133,001, 55,428 households, and 33,838 families residing in the county.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-25]

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the median age was 40.7 years. 19.0% of residents were under the age of 18 and 19.2% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 94.9 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 92.7 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-25]

The racial makeup of the county was 85.7% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans), 4.2% [Black or African American](2154.md), 0.3% [American Indian and Alaska Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 1.5% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), <0.1% [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) and [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander), 2.1% from some other race, and 6.1% from [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans). [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) residents of any race comprised 4.6% of the population.[^cite_note-Census2020PL-26]

74.2% of residents lived in urban areas, while 25.8% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-27]

There were 55,428 households in the county, of which 25.5% had children under the age of 18 living in them. Of all households, 45.1% were married-couple households, 19.9% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 28.1% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 31.0% of all households were made up of individuals and 12.4% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-25]

There were 60,546 housing units, of which 8.5% were vacant. Among occupied housing units, 64.5% were owner-occupied and 35.5% were renter-occupied. The homeowner vacancy rate was 1.6% and the rental vacancy rate was 7.7%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-25]

### 2000 census

As of the [census](6889.md)[^cite_note-GR8-28] of 2000, there were 107,198 people, 44,195 households, and 29,478 families residing in the county. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 328 people per square mile (127 people/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 47,779 housing units at an average density of 146 units per square mile (56/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the county was 93.72% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 3.82% [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)) or [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 0.24% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 0.73% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 0.02% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 0.51% from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), and 0.97% from two or more races. 1.38% of the population were [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)) of any race.

There were 44,195 households, out of which 28.20% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 52.60% were [married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) living together, 10.50% had a female householder with no husband present, and 33.30% were non-families. 27.80% of all households were made up of individuals, and 9.70% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.33 and the average family size was 2.85.

In the county, the population was spread out, with 21.30% under the age of 18, 10.80% from 18 to 24, 30.00% from 25 to 44, 24.00% from 45 to 64, and 13.90% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 37 years. For every 100 females, there were 94.80 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 91.70 males.

The median income for a household in the county was \$33,116, and the median income for a family was \$41,162. Males had a median income of \$30,874 versus \$21,485 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the county was \$19,085. About 10.20% of families and 13.90% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 16.80% of those under age 18 and 14.20% of those age 65 or over.

## Politics

Like most of East Tennessee, Washington County has been a [Republican](32070.md) stronghold since the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). No national [Democratic](5043544.md) candidate has won the county since [1856](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1856_United_States_presidential_election_in_Tennessee),[^cite_note-31] although [Lyndon Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Johnson) came within 359 votes of [Barry Goldwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater) in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election_in_Tennessee) and [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) came within 819 votes of [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford) in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election_in_Tennessee). [Franklin Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Roosevelt) is the only other Democrat to even cross the 40 percent mark. The only time the Republicans have failed to win the county since [1868](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1868_United_States_presidential_election_in_Tennessee), the first election it competed in Tennessee, came in [1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_United_States_presidential_election_in_Tennessee), when the [Bull Moose Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Party_\(United_States,_1912\)) divided the Republican vote and won a plurality.
