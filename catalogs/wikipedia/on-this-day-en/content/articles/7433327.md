The **Ukrainian Ground Forces** (**SVZSU**, [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Сухопутні війська Збройних сил України, **СВЗСУ**, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Sukhoputni viiska Zbroinykh syl Ukrainy*), also referred to as the **Ukrainian Army** is the land force, and one of the eight [branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md). It was formed from Ukrainian units of the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Army) after [Ukrainian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine), and its ancestry is traced back to the 1917–22 army of the [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic).

After the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991, Ukraine retained its [Soviet-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet-era) army equipment. The Armed Forces were systematically downsized and underinvested in after 1991. As a result, the Ukrainian army had very little of its Soviet equipment in working order by July 2014, and most systems had become antiquated. Personnel numbers had shrunk and training, command, and support functions needed improvement.[^cite_note-krarmyJuly14-3] After the start of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)) in April 2014 in [eastern Ukraine](13065940.md), Ukraine embarked on a program to enlarge and modernise its armed forces.[^cite_note-krarmyJuly14-3][^cite_note-Tsirw-4][^cite_note-Uaupg24816-5] Personnel in the Ukrainian Armed Forces overall climbed from 129,950 in March 2014[^cite_note-6] to 204,000 active personnel in May 2015,[^cite_note-2015sUa-7] with 169,000 soldiers in the Ground Forces branch as of 2016.[^cite_note-Archived_copy-8] In 2016, 75% of the army consisted of contract servicemen.[^cite_note-2016Uar-9] Since 2014, Ukraine's ground forces have also been equipped with increasingly modern tanks, APCs, and many other types of combat equipment.[^cite_note-10]

## History



[Ukrainian People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Army) soldiers in front of [St. Michael's Golden-Domed Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael's_Golden-Domed_Monastery) in [Kyiv](585629.md), 1918

The Ukrainian Ground Forces traces its ancestry to the [Ukrainian People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Army) and the [Ukrainian Galician Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Galician_Army) of 1917–21. It fought in the [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence) (the [Ukrainian-Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian%E2%80%93Soviet_War)), the [Southern Front of the Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Front_of_the_Russian_Civil_War), the [Polish–Ukrainian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Ukrainian_War), and the [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Soviet_War)[–](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Ukrainian_War)Soviet War.

Since 2015, with the adoption of the [Defenders Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenders_Day_\(Ukraine\)) holiday, certain traditions of the [Ukrainian Insurgent Army](1045217.md) of [World War II](32927.md) have been incorporated into the ethos and culture of the Ground Forces.

### Collapse of the USSR



Ukrainian soldiers on a [military exercise](1225714.md) in 1998

The [August 1991 Soviet coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) began the process of splitting the Soviet military. [Leonid Kravchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kravchuk), the head of the [Ukrainian Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet_of_the_Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), declared on 24 August 1991 the formation of the Ukrainian Armed Forces, the subordination of Soviet military units in Ukraine, and the creation of the [Ministry of Defence](14284651.md) of Ukraine. On 3 September 1991 the [Soviet Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Force) major general [Kostyantyn Morozov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostyantyn_Morozov) was appointed the first Minister of Defense of Ukraine by the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md), the new parliament. In October the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_\(Ukrainian_State\)) declared that a Ukrainian army would be created with 450,000 troops and the Ukrainian parliament adopted several laws that created the framework for the creation of Ukrainian ground, naval, and air forces, as well as a national guard. The Soviet defense ministry was opposed to this initially, but by early November they started talks with the Ukrainian defense ministry to manage the division of Soviet forces in Ukraine.[^cite_note-strategic15-11]

The Ground Forces were officially established on 6 December 1991 as part of the armed forces, with a presidential decree on 12 December - from then on marked as Ground Forces Day - being the first that designated the Soviet Army's Ukrainian formations as the ground component of the new force.

After their establishment, in 1992 the Ukrainian Ground Forces included approximately 245,000 personnel[^cite_note-strategic53-12] and 6,500 tanks.[^cite_note-13]

### Creation of the Ground Forces



Ukrainian army Soldier in Iraq, 2003

The Ukrainian Ground Forces were the second largest army in Europe at the time.[^cite_note-strategic15-11] Following the [declaration of Ukrainian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine) in 1991, among those formations gained by the new Ukrainian Ground Forces by inheritance from the old Soviet Army were the [1st Guards Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Guards_Army_\(Soviet_Union\)), the [13th Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Army_\(Soviet_Union\)), the [38th Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38th_Army_\(Soviet_Union\)), two tank armies (the [6th Guards Tank Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Guards_Tank_Army) and the [8th Tank Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Tank_Army)), and the [32nd Army Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_Army_Corps_\(Ukraine\)) at [Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol). The [28th Guards Motor Rifle Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28th_Mechanized_Brigade_\(Ukraine\)) and the [180th Rifle Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/180th_Rifle_Division) were left in Ukraine, having been previously under the [14th Guards Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Guards_Army) headquartered at [Tiraspol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiraspol) in the [Moldovan SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). The post of commander of ground troops was designated in early 1992. By the end of 1992, the [Kyiv Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Military_District) was disbanded, and Ukraine used its structures as the basis for the Ministry of Defence and the [General Staff](17313362.md).[^cite_note-14]

The government made an effort to get all troops to take an oath of allegiance to Ukraine to prevent a possible coup. All personnel were required to either take the oath, or to retire or return to their home republic. The Ukrainian oath of loyalty that was administered was not based on ethnicity or linguistics but on a civic identity, and turned the Soviet Army in Ukraine into the modern Ukrainian Ground Forces. As of February 1992, about 80% of personnel had taken the oath, according to Defense Minister Morozov. Laws establishing regulations the personnel and technical basis for the military were passed in 1992, which included keeping the Soviet rank structure, with the exception of the rank of marshal, which was replaced with [general of the army of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_of_the_army_of_Ukraine). It was planned that the restructuring of the entire Armed Forces would take place until 1995.[^cite_note-strategic15-11]

Between June and August 1993, the first redesignation of armies to army corps appears to have taken place.[^cite_note-15] While the post of Chief of Ground Forces had been created in early 1992, it was over two years before the first holder, Colonel General [Vasily Sobkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Sobkov?action=edit\&redlink=1), was appointed on 7 April 1994.[^cite_note-16]

The creation of the Ground Forces as a separate branch of the young AFU was formalised by Presidential Decree 368/96 of 23 May 1996, 'On the Ground Forces of Ukraine.'[^cite_note-17] That year both the Ground Forces Command was formed and the [1st Army Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Army_Corps_\(Ukraine\)) was reorganised as the Northern Territorial Operational Command (which became the Northern Operational Command in 1998). In 1997 the Carpathian Military District was reorganised as [Operational Command West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_Command_West).

From 1992 to 1997, the forces of the Kyiv MD were transferred to the Odesa MD, and the Odesa MD's headquarters moved to [Donetsk](562951.md).[^cite_note-18]

In a December 1996 speech, President [Leonid Kuchma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kuchma) revealed that as many as 191 mechanised infantry and tank battalions were rated not ready, adding,"This is especially dangerous in the forward-based units securing the nation's borders."[^cite_note-19]

### Reform



Ukrainian and [US Army](32087.md) soldiers during the 2011 [Rapid Trident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_Trident) exercise

Under a plan promulgated in 2000, the Ground Forces were to reduce the number of troops from 300,000 to 240,000 by 2015, and an ultimate change from a partial [conscript](5735.md)-based force to a fully professional military.[^cite_note-20] The armed forces received little more than half of the [Hr](722696.md) 68 million it was promised for reform in 2001, but managed to disband nine [regiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment) and close 21 local military bases.[^cite_note-21]

In 2005–06, the Northern Operational Command was reorganised as [Territorial Directorate "North"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Directorate_%22North%22). It was tasked with territorial defence, mobilisation training, and preparation of reserves.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-24]

From 1991, the Ukrainian Ground Forces bought its military equipment only from [Russia](25391.md) and other [CIS](36870.md) states, as well as locally producing some of their own equipment.[^cite_note-krarmyJuly14-3][^cite_note-Tsirw-4] Until 2014 and the start of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)), the [defence industry in Ukraine](61177145.md) produced equipment mostly for export.[^cite_note-Ukraine_crisis_timeline_BBC-25][^cite_note-krarmyJuly14-3]

### Russian occupation of Crimea

Main article: [Annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation)



Ukrainian troops in 2013, a year before the annexation of Crimea

In the aftermath of the [2014 Ukrainian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity), Russian [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spetsnaz) in [unmarked uniforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_green_men_\(Russo-Ukrainian_War\)) began surrounding Ukrainian military bases on the Crimean peninsula before capturing them individually using a mixture of attrition and threats.[^cite_note-26] Over the following weeks the [Russian Armed Forces](25709.md) consolidated control of the peninsula and established road blocks to cut off the possibility of Ukraine sending reinforcements from the mainland.[^cite_note-27] The takeover of Crimea was largely bloodless, as the Ukrainian soldiers there did not fight back.[^cite_note-28] By the end of March, all remaining Ukrainian troops were ordered to pull out of Crimea.[^cite_note-29]

The Ukrainian army was considered to be in a poor state during and after the annexation, with only 6,000 of its troops ready for combat and many of its vehicles lacking batteries.[^cite_note-wsj31115-30] After [Russia's annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) only 6,000 of the 20,300 Ukrainian soldiers stationed in Crimea before the annexation left the peninsula. The rest stayed in Crimea and defected to Russia.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

### Russo-Ukrainian War

#### War in Donbas (2014–2022)

Main article: [War in Donbas (2014–2022)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\))



Ukrainian soldier in the aftermath of the [Siege of Sloviansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sloviansk) on 9 July 2014.&#x20;

In the early months of the war in Donbas that erupted in 2014 the Armed Forces were widely criticised for their poor equipment and inept leadership, forcing Internal Affairs Ministry forces like the [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_of_Ukraine) and the [territorial defence battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_defence_battalion_\(Ukraine\)) to take on the brunt of the fighting in the first months of the war.[^cite_note-KP23215bb-33][^cite_note-NR9MAYbb-34]

By February 2018 the Ukrainian Armed Forces were larger and better equipped, numbering 200,000 active-service military personnel. Most of the volunteer soldiers of the territorial defence battalions were integrated into the Ukrainian army.[^cite_note-35]

Within the reporting period of 16 November 2017 to 15 February 2018 a [United Nations](31769.md) [OHCHR](22463.md) monitoring mission documented 115 cases of credible allegations of human rights abuses committed by Russia and its proxy forces.[^cite_note-Feb2018h37udj18-36] The nature of the crimes ranges from enforced disappearances, looting of civilian property, torture, rape and sexual violence up to political repression and extrajudicial killings.[^cite_note-Feb2018h37udj18-36]

#### Full-scale Russian invasion (2022–present)



Ukrainian soldier in a trench during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022

Main article: [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md)

On [24 February 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Russia began a [full-scale invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-37] The Ground Forces have been participants in most of the land combat actions of the ongoing war. The influx of Western material and supplies to the branch before and during the conflict as well as mobilisation efforts have resulted in a massive expansion of the force, in addition to ongoing force modernisation.&#x20;

## Military training and education centres

See also: [Ukraine–NATO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine%E2%80%93NATO_relations)



[Ukrainian special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces_of_Ukraine) soldiers during an exercise in November 2015



Ukrainian soldier and Canadian soldier conversing with each other during the 2014 Rapid Trident exercise in Yavoriv, Ukraine

Training in 2006 was aimed at developing mobility and combat readiness of the forces.[^cite_note-WB06-38] The Ukrainian Armed Forces took advantage of the opportunities provided by UN exercises and exercises where Ukraine, [NATO](21133.md) members, and other partners participated.[^cite_note-WB06-38][^cite_note-39]

Training resulted in 6,000 combat-ready troops in the spring of 2014 of Ukraine's (then) 129,950 active military personnel.[^cite_note-wsj31115-30][^cite_note-40] In 2016 the Ukrainian army had more than 200,000 combat-ready soldiers of its 260,000 active personnel.[^cite_note-2015sUa-7][^cite_note-KPUA14S16-41]

In 2015 Ukraine, the [United States](3434750.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and [Canada](5042916.md) established the Joint Multinational Training Group – Ukraine (JMTG-U), setting up three new training sites in [Khmelnytskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmelnytskyi,_Ukraine), [Kamianets-Podilskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamianets-Podilskyi), and [Yavoriv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yavoriv).[^cite_note-KPUA14S16-41] The latter, known as the [International Centre for Peacekeeping and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yavoriv_military_base) or the Yavoriv Combat Training Centre, was hit by eight Russian missiles in March 2022.[^cite_note-42]

### Mechanized forces

Main article: [Mechanized Infantry (Ukraine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanized_Infantry_\(Ukraine\))



A Ukrainian soldier in a [KrAZ Spartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STREIT_Group_Spartan) preparing to engage the opposition force during an air assault at Exercise Rapid Trident 16 July 3, 2016



A Ukrainian army [T-64BM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-64) during a training exercise

[Mechanized infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanized_infantry) brigades constitute the largest and primary components of the Ukrainian Ground Forces. Their primary objectives in the case of wartime operations are: capturing and holding targets, maintaining positions, defending against enemy attacks, penetrating enemy lines, and defeating enemy forces on either defensive or offensive operations.

The mechanized infantry forces are equipped with a combination of Soviet-made (part of them modernized), more modern Ukrainian-made, and increasingly Western-made armored vehicles, including variants of the [T-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-80), [T-64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-64),[^cite_note-45] (T-64BV Model 2017, T-64BV), [T-64BM "Bulat"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-64)[^cite_note-46] and [T-72UA1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72) main battle tanks,[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] [BTR-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-4), [BTR-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-60), [BTR-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-70), and [BTR-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-80) wheeled armored personnel carriers, and [BMP-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-1), [BMP-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-2), and [BMD-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMD-2) tracked infantry fighting vehicles.

In the years after the fall of the Soviet Union, a large number of the former Soviet mechanized infantry and armored formations on Ukrainian soil were disbanded – the [IISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies) said the numbers dropped from 14 divisions in 1992, to two divisions, six [brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade), and one independent regiment in 2008.[^cite_note-50]

The mechanized infantry brigades, together with the newer motorized, mountain, rifle infantry and assault infantry brigades, constitute the Infantry Corps of the Ukrainian Ground Forces. The Armored Forces also constitute their own corps. Established in 1991, these two corps are the oldest combat arms of the Ukrainian Ground Forces.

From 2014 until the mid-2020s, the brigade constituted the principal operational echelon for mechanised and armoured formations in the Ukrainian Ground Forces, with higher-level formations largely organized as temporary operational groupings rather than permanent corps or divisions.[^cite_note-51]

### Army Aviation



[Mil Mi-24](384591.md) helicopters of the Ukrainian Army Aviation

The [Ukrainian Army Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Army_Aviation) provides [reconnaissance](146911.md), tactical fire support and air transport for the Ukrainian Ground Forces in support of its paramount responsibilities to the nation. As of 2017 Ukraine's army fields four Army Aviation brigades in an Army Aviation Command directly subordinated to the Ground Forces HQ, each in support of operational commands of the UGF:

-  [11th Army Aviation Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Army_Aviation_Brigade_\(Ukraine\)), [Kherson](1025861.md)

-  [12th Army Aviation Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Army_Aviation_Brigade_\(Ukraine\)), [Novyi Kalyniv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novyi_Kalyniv)

-  [16th Army Aviation Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_Army_Aviation_Brigade_\(Ukraine\)), [Brody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brody)

-  [18th Army Aviation Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Army_Aviation_Brigade_\(Ukraine\)), [Poltava](38581.md)

The Army Aviation's maintenance facility is the 57th Aviation Base in [Brody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brody). The service's equipment includes [Mi-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-2), [Mi-8](957233.md), and [Mi-24](384591.md) helicopters.

### Rocket Forces and Artillery

Main article: [Rocket Forces and Artillery (Ukraine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Forces_and_Artillery_\(Ukraine\))



Ukrainian [BM-30 Smerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-30_Smerch) heavy [multiple rocket launchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_rocket_launcher) on parade in Kyiv

The RF\&FA (Rocket Forces and Field Artillery) Corps constitute one of the oldest combat support corps of the Ukrainian Ground Forces. Established 1991 on the basis of Soviet Army artillery divisions assigned to the AFU and the field artillery of the UGF's infantry and armored divisions, units of this corps provide artillery fires support to formations of the Ground Forces in combat operations and in wartime operational support of other branches of the AFU and other uniformed organizations in fulfillment of its missions to the nation.

### Ground Forces Air Defence Missile Artillery

The Army Anti-Air Defence Missile Artillery regiments and brigade-level battalions or regiments in the infantry and armoured brigades are responsible for protecting troops against enemy air attacks anywhere on the battlefield, and while in combat or in static protection of UGF facilities. The army air defence branch is equipped with a variety of effective [surface-to-air](182664.md) missile systems of the air defence regiments and [anti-aircraft missile](146640.md) and [artillery](2508.md) complexes under the brigades' air defence battalions or regiments. Brigade level units are characterized by their high rate of fire, vitality, maneuverability, and capability of action under all conditions of modern combat arms operations. Surface-to-air missile systems and complexes of operational command level are characterised by their long range and firepower and are equipped with surface-to-air missile complexes [Osa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K33_Osa), [Kub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2K12_Kub), and [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_missile_system). Anti-aircraft missile and artillery complexes that are of brigade level are equipped with various ex-Soviet and Western systems like the [Tunguska-M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2K22_Tunguska), [Igla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K38_Igla) [MANPADS](1389714.md) system, [Strela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K35_Strela-10), and Shilka anti-aircraft missile systems.[^cite_note-53]

Formed in 1992, it is also one of the oldest combat support corps of the Ground Forces.

The war in Donbas caused a radical reform of the Ukrainian Armed Forces in general and the Ukrainian Ground Forces in particular; it built and expanded on the 2011 structure.[^cite_note-54] In 2022 the structure was composed of four Operational Commands: West, North, South, and [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Directorate_of_the_Territorial_Defense_Forces_%22East%22).[^cite_note-Сухопутні_війська-55]

The full-scale invasion in 2022 led to rapid expansion with the number of military personnel swelling from 250,000 to 980,000 by January 2025.[^cite_note-56] Since early 2025, the Armed Forces of Ukraine have been transitioning to a corps-based command structure, with corps becoming the primary operational-level formations, replacing brigade-centric organization and ad hoc wartime operational groupings.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

The reform aims to establish large operational formations integrating multiple brigades with supporting elements, including artillery, unmanned systems, logistics, and intelligence, under a unified command. This structure is intended to improve coordination across an extended фронт and enhance the speed of operational decision-making. The introduction of corps reflects the scale of the war, enabling more effective command and control by grouping numerous brigades into higher-level formations with dedicated headquarters and planning capabilities.[^cite_note-59]

The UGF Reserve Corps ([Ukrainian:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Language) *Корпус резерву*) - as it had been then named upon its foundation - was raised in 2016 and was directly subordinated to the General Staff via the Commander of the Ground Forces. It is also called the Army Strategic Reserve Corps. Its main function when it was founded is to prepare and provide administrative support and control over [reservists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservist) of the ground forces. According to plans the Reserve Corps was to be fully operational by 2020 with reserve servicemen in three separate categories:[^cite_note-67]

- **Operational Reserve of the First Line** (*оперативний резерв першої черги*) – includes about 50,000 reserve servicemen with extensive combat training (60 days of combat training every two years) in the reserve companies and batteries of the operational army brigades and regiments, with those reservists to become casualty replacements in wartime

- **Operational Reserve of the Second Line** (*оперативний резерв другої черги*) – includes reserve servicemen with combat training of 30 days every two years in Territorial Defence Forces brigades. In addition, the command personnel will undergo 10 days training cycles yearly. The XI Army Corps by that time was to also act as the pool formation for those territorial brigades and transfer them to the ground forces' operational commands in wartime as needed.

- **Mobilisation Reserve** (*мобілізаційний резерв*) – includes all Ukrainian citizens, who are eligible for [mobilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilization) in case of war, but do not belong to the first or the second line operational reserve. They can be used to form support units or to boost the service numbers of the territorial brigades as casualty replacements.

In that organisation, the XI Army Reserve Corps - as it has been currently named since 2024 - is currently engaged in the ongoing Russian invasion of Ukraine and its reservists have fought in every ground operation of the conflict.

The Ukrainian army unveiled its new uniforms on 24 August 2016 ([Independence Day of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_of_Ukraine)).[^cite_note-nuUaWP-68] The new uniforms are modeled on British military styles, having a modern [pixelated digital camouflage pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-scale_camouflage), called the [MM-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MM-14) pattern.[^cite_note-nuUaWP-68] They also incorporate details from the uniforms worn by the Ukrainian People's Army.[^cite_note-nuUaWP-68] The new cap includes an insignia of a Ukrainian Cossack grasping a cross.[^cite_note-nuUaWP-68]

### Armour

The majority of tanks and armoured vehicles in Ukrainian service as of 2022 were of Soviet origin, such as the [T-64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-64), [T-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-80), and [T-72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72) tank families, the [BMP-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-1) and [BMP-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-2) infantry fighting vehicle, and the [BTR-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-80) armoured personnel carrier.[^cite_note-\:0-71] Tank donations from the West to Ukraine have been mostly [T-72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72) variants, with the majority of Western tanks being from the [Leopard 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard_1) and [Leopard 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard_2) families.[^cite_note-72] Western armoured vehicles donated to Ukraine include the [Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley_Fighting_Vehicle) and [Marder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marder_\(infantry_fighting_vehicle\)) infantry fighting vehicles, the [Stryker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryker), [M113](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M113_armored_personnel_carrier), and [Roshel Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roshel_Senator) armoured personnel carriers, and the [Humvee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humvee) family of light military vehicles. The Ukrainian military, as of 2024, also operates a number of captured Russian [T-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-90) main battle tanks and [BMP-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-3) IFVs.[^cite_note-73]

### Artillery

Before Western artillery systems started being donated in 2022, Ukraine operated an artillery park that mostly consisted of older Soviet-designed equipment. Soviet self-propelled guns in Ukrainian service include the [2S1 Carnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2S1_Gvozdika), the [2S3 Akatsiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2S3_Akatsiya), the [2S7 Pion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2S7_Pion), and the [2S19 Msta-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2S19_Msta-S).[^cite_note-\:0-71][^cite_note-\:1-74] The Ukrainian army also operates the Ukrainian produced [2S22 Bohdana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2S22_Bohdana) self propelled howitzer and the [BM-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-27_Uragan) and [BM-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-30_Smerch) rocket artillery systems, with the [D-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/152_mm_towed_gun-howitzer_M1955_\(D-20\)) and [Msta-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/152_mm_howitzer_2A65_Msta-B) towed guns being used primarily by motorised infantry formations.[^cite_note-\:1-74] Western artillery donated to Ukraine consists mostly of the [M777](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M777_howitzer) towed howitzer, the [M119](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M119_howitzer)[^cite_note-75] and [L119](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L118_light_gun) towed field guns, the [M109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M109_howitzer), [PzH 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panzerhaubitze_2000) and [AHS Krab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AHS_Krab) self-propelled howitzers, and the [HIMARS](147011.md) and [M270](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_Multiple_Launch_Rocket_System) rocket artillery systems.[^cite_note-76]

### Aviation

[Ukrainian Army Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Army_Aviation) operates three families of Soviet-designed helicopters: the [Mi-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-2) for training, and the [Mi-8](957233.md) and [Mi-24](384591.md) for transport and attack.[^cite_note-77]

## Deployment outside of Ukraine

### Bosnia-Herzegovina



Ukrainian [IFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFOR) peacekeepers at a checkpoint outside Sarajevo, 1996

On 15 July 1992, Ukrainian peacekeepers formed into the 240th battallion arrived in Bosnia, at the time engulfed by a [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War). In what became the first [peacekeeping](84617.md) mission in the history of independent Ukraine, the soldiers were initially based in [Sarajevo](26786.md). Since 1993 Ukrainian soldiers were deployed in [Goražde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gora%C5%BEde) and [Žepa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDepa), which functioned as [enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclave) inside of the [Bosnian Serb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Serb)-held territory. In May 1995 Bosnian Serbs troops attacked two Ukrainian checkpoints in Sarajevo, attempting to get control over the peacekeepers' weapons and taking several Ukrainians hostage. The captured peacekeepers were later released and moved to [Zagreb](55906.md).[^cite_note-vijesti-78]

Another group of Ukrainian peacekeepers, which was based in Žepa, suffered from a [blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_%C5%BDepa) by Serb forces. Following the [fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Srebrenica) of nearby [Srebrenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica), a number of survivors of the [massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre) perpetrated by the Serbs managed to reach the Ukrainian positions. Following negotiations, Serb forces agreed to allow the evacuation of civilians from Žepa under protection of Ukrainian peacekeepers. As a result, between 5 and 10 thousand people, both civilians and members of the Bosnian Muslim army, could escape the village, with 36 soldiers being captured by the Serbs and 116 civilians murdered.[^cite_note-vijesti-78]

[European Union](9317.md) envoy [Carl Bildt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Bildt) and [British Army](4887.md) general [Rupert Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Smith) positively evaluated the actions of Ukrainian peacekeepers during the evacuation of Žepa. Following the operation, [Ukrainian defence minister](14284651.md) [Valeriy Shmarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriy_Shmarov) received an award from the country's president [Leonid Kuchma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kuchma). However, the peacekeepers themselves received no awards, officially due to "disciplinary grounds", but possibly because of pressure from [Russia](25391.md), whose authorities were sympathetic to the Serb side of the conflict and didn't want information on the events in Žepa to reach publicity.[^cite_note-vijesti-78]

### Kosovo

Ukrainian forces have also been deployed to [Kosovo](17391.md) since 2000 as part of the 600 man [Polish–Ukrainian Peace Force Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Ukrainian_Peace_Force_Battalion). In August 2014, Ukraine ended its mission to Kosovo due to the [2014 Russian invasion of Ukraine](42085878.md).[^cite_note-79]

### Afghanistan

Between 2001 and 2021, Ukraine allowed US military cargo planes to fly over and refuel on Ukrainian soil on their way to Afghanistan. In 2007 Ukraine deployed a detachment of the 143rd De-Mining Centre of the Armed Forces of Ukraine to Afghanistan. Ukraine had kept a team of soldiers deployed to Afghanistan as part of the ISAF from 2007 to 2021, which mostly consisted of pilots, medical officers, and bomb disposal experts.[^cite_note-Ukraine_Ministry_of_Defense-80]

Ukrainian pilots were responsible for training pilots of the [Afghan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force) on the operation of several aircraft as Afghan forces consisted of mostly Soviet designed aircraft such as the [Mi-17](1493374.md), which Ukrainian troops were very familiar with. In 2013, the contingent of troops in Afghanistan totaled 26 troops. In 2014 the Ukrainian contingent was further drawn down and the team included 8 bomb disposal experts and several medical officers.[^cite_note-Ukraine_Ministry_of_Defense-80]

### Iraq

Further information: [Ukrainian involvement in the Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_involvement_in_the_Iraq_War)



Henadii Lachkov, commander of the Ukrainian contingent in Iraq, kissing his country's flag

Ukraine deployed a sizable contingent of troops to [Iraq](7515928.md) as part of the [Iraq War](5043324.md), which were stationed near [Kut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kut). Ukraine's troop deployment was the second largest of all those from former Soviet states besides [Georgia](48768.md) and Ukraine deployed more soldiers to Iraq than many other NATO members such as Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania. Ukraine also suffered the fifth highest casualty toll during the war, with only Polish, Italian, British, and US forces suffering heavier losses.[^cite_note-81]

From 2003 to 2005 over 1,700 Ukrainian soldiers were deployed to Iraq, making up the third-largest contingent at the time. They were designated as the [5th Mechanised Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Mechanized_Brigade_\(Ukraine\)). Much as in Ukraine's mission to Kosovo, the troops deployed were contract soldiers and not conscripts. Ukraine began to severely draw down its troop levels in Iraq in 2005 due to mounting casualties and the political toxicity of the conflict. By 2005 only 876 soldiers, or roughly half of the original contingent were deployed, and by year's end troop levels dropped to below 100. In 2008, one year before the official end of the US military mission, President [Viktor Yushchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yushchenko) ordered all remaining troops in Iraq to return home, marking an official end of Ukraine's mission.[^cite_note-82]



Ukrainian [Mi-8](957233.md) helicopters of the [United Nations Mission in Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mission_in_Liberia) (UNMIL)

### Africa

Ukrainian peacekeeping forces have been deployed to the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), [Liberia](17791.md), [Sudan](27421.md) and [South Sudan](32350676.md), and [Côte d'Ivoire](57744.md). Ukrainian forces have also been requested to take a more active role in the [Northern Mali Conflict](36856978.md) of 2012 in battling Islamic militants. One of the largest deployments is the 18th Separate Helicopter Unit of the Ukrainian Armed Forces which consisted of 160 servicemen and four [Mi-24](384591.md)P helicopters and was deployed to the DRC in 2011.[^cite_note-83]

## Veterans

Ukraine provides combat veterans with various benefits. Ukrainians who have served in World War II, the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), or as [liquidators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_liquidators) at the [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) are eligible for benefits such as a monthly allowance, a discount on medical and pharmacy services, free use of public transportation, additional vacation days from work, having priority for retention in case of work layoffs, easier loan access and approval process, preference when applying for security related positions, priority when applying to vocation school or trade school, and electricity, gas, and housing subsidies. Veterans are also eligible to stay at military sanatoriums, provided there is available space.[^cite_note-Харькова_Тимохов-84]

Since gaining independence, Ukraine has deployed troops to conflicts in Kosovo, Iraq, and Afghanistan, which has created a new generation of veterans separate from those who served in the Soviet forces. Most recently the government passed a law extending veteran benefits to Ukrainian troops participating in the war in Donbas. Veterans from other nations who move to or reside in Ukraine may be eligible for some of the listed benefits. This provision was likely made to ensure that World War II, Chernobyl, and Afghanistan veterans from other Soviet states who moved to Ukraine received similar benefits. As Ukraine has participated in numerous NATO-led conflicts since its independence, it is unclear if NATO veterans would be extended these benefits.[^cite_note-Харькова_Тимохов-84]

Veteran groups are not as developed as in the United States, which has numerous well known national organisations such as the [Veterans of Foreign Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_of_Foreign_Wars). World War II veterans, and even persons who have lived through the war are generally treated with the highest respect. Other veterans are not as well known. Ukrainian veterans from the Soviet war in Afghanistan are strikingly similar to the Vietnam veterans of the United States, although the Soviet Union generally kept the public in the dark throughout the war, unlike in Vietnam, where coverage was very high. Afghanistan is often labeled as a mistake by the Soviet Union and its successor states, but the lack of media coverage, and the censorship through the war have ensured that many still remain unaware of their nation's involvement in the conflict.[^cite_note-85] Despite Ukraine having had the 3rd largest contingent of troops in Iraq in 2004, few Ukrainians realize today that their nation also is home to many veterans of the Iraq War. Soldiers who took part in the war in Donbas can receive free land plots.[^cite_note-86] On 22 November 2018, the [Ministry for Veterans Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_for_Veterans_Affairs_\(Ukraine\)) was officially established.[^cite_note-UGF10145168MVA-87]
