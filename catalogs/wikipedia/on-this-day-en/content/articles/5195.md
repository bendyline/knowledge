[Canada](5042916.md) has an advanced and diversified [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy). The nation relies upon its [abundant natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Canada#Natural_resources) and well-developed [international trade networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_trading_partners_of_Canada).[^cite_note-econ-31][^cite_note-32] As of 2026, it is the [eleventh-largest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) in nominal terms. Its [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) in [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) (PPP) terms ranks third among G7 countries.[^cite_note-World_Bank-7][^cite_note-33] Among OECD members, Canada has a highly efficient and strong [social security system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_programs_in_Canada); social expenditure stood at [roughly 23.1% of GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state#Effects). The country's average household [disposable income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_income) per capita is "well above" the OECD average.[^cite_note-34] The [Government of Canada](616255.md) formulates economic policy while the [Bank of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Canada) functions as its [central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) and implements monetary policy.

The Canadian economy ranks low in [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Canada) and [income disparity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality) while ranking highly on economic freedom and stability.[^cite_note-cpi-35][^cite_note-36] Canada ranks among the lowest of the most developed countries for [housing affordability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing_in_Canada).[^cite_note-37] It maintains a highly developed banking sector with the [Toronto Stock Exchange](205584.md) ranked the [tenth-largest stock exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stock_exchanges) in the world by [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization).[^cite_note-38] Its [cooperative banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_banking) sector is pronounced with the world's highest per-capita membership in [credit unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_union).[^cite_note-39] Among [G20](2206608.md) members, Canada has the second-largest [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) to GDP ratio and the lowest net debt-to-GDP ratio in the G7.[^cite_note-w479-40]

## Contemporary era overview

Main article: [Economic history of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_Canada)

Since the early 20th century, the growth of Canada's manufacturing, mining, and service sectors has transformed the nation from a largely rural economy to an urbanized, industrial one.[^cite_note-41] The Canadian economy is dominated by the [service industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy), which employs about three-quarters of the country's workforce.[^cite_note-42] Among developed countries, Canada has an unusually important [primary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector_of_the_economy), of which the [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry_in_Canada) and [petroleum industries](4071088.md) are the most prominent components.[^cite_note-SueyoshiGoto2018-43] Many towns in northern Canada, where agriculture is difficult, are sustained by nearby mines or sources of timber.[^cite_note-44] Canada spends [around 1.70% of GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_by_research_and_development_spending) on advanced [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) across various sectors of the economy.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-unctad-46]

Canada's economic integration with the United States has increased significantly since [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-47] The [Automotive Products Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_Products_Trade_Agreement) of 1965 opened Canada's borders to trade in the automobile manufacturing industry.[^cite_note-48] In the 1970s, concerns over energy self-sufficiency and foreign ownership in the manufacturing sectors prompted the federal government to enact the [National Energy Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Energy_Program) (NEP) and the [Foreign Investment Review Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Investment_Review_Agency) (FIRA).[^cite_note-49] The government abolished the NEP in the 1980s and changed the name of FIRA to [Invest in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invest_in_Canada) to encourage foreign investment.[^cite_note-50] The [Canada – United States Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_%E2%80%93_United_States_Free_Trade_Agreement) (FTA) of 1988 eliminated tariffs between the two countries, while the [North American Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Free_Trade_Agreement) (NAFTA) expanded the free-trade zone to include [Mexico](3966054.md) in 1994 (later replaced by the [Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement](58620732.md)).[^cite_note-51] As of 2023, [Canada is a signatory to 15 free trade agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_agreements_of_Canada) with 51 countries.[^cite_note-GAC_2020-52]

Canada is one of the few developed nations that are net exporters of energy.[^cite_note-SueyoshiGoto2018-43][^cite_note-53] [Atlantic Canada possesses vast offshore deposits of natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_drilling_in_Atlantic_Canada),[^cite_note-54] and Alberta hosts the fourth-largest oil reserves in the world.[^cite_note-Monga_2022-55] The vast [Athabasca oil sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athabasca_oil_sands) and other oil reserves give Canada 13% of global oil reserves, constituting the [world's third or fourth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_proven_oil_reserves).[^cite_note-56] Canada is additionally one of the world's largest suppliers of [agricultural products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Canada); the [Canadian Prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Prairies) are one of the most important global producers of wheat, [canola](71016451.md), and other grains.[^cite_note-57] The [country is a leading exporter](46644724.md) of zinc, uranium, gold, nickel, [platinoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_group), aluminum, steel, [iron ore](145352.md), [coking coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coking_coal), lead, copper, molybdenum, cobalt, and cadmium.[^cite_note-OEC_-_The_Observatory_of_Economic_Complexity-58][^cite_note-The_Atlas_of_Economic_Complexity-59] Canada has a sizeable heavy manufacturing sector centred in southern Ontario and Quebec, with automobiles and [aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics) representing particularly important industries.[^cite_note-60] The [country's fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry_in_Canada) and [tourism industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Canada) are also key contributors to the economy.[^cite_note-61]

## Monetary policy

The mandate of the central bank—the [Bank of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Canada)—is to conduct monetary policy that manages [inflation](38286.md) to ensure the Canadian dollar's long-term [purchasing power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-Monetary_Policy_Report_2015_07-63]

Under the [inflation-targeting](2683415.md) monetary policy that has been the cornerstone of Canada's monetary and fiscal policy since the early 1990s, the Bank of Canada sets an inflation target.[^cite_note-Monetary_Policy_Report_2015_07-63][^cite_note-inflation-targeting_2010-64] The inflation target was set at 2%, which is the midpoint of an inflation range of 1 to 3%. They established a set of inflation-reduction targets to keep inflation "low, stable and predictable" and to foster Canada's sustained economic growth, employment gains and improved standard of living.[^cite_note-Monetary_Policy_Report_2015_07-63]

Following the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession), the narrow focus of inflation-targeting as a means of providing stable growth in the Canadian economy was revised.[^cite_note-beyond_inflation-targeting_2011-65] By 2011, the then-Bank of Canada Governor [Mark Carney](13571049.md) argued that monetary policy would allow for a more flexible inflation-targeting in specific situations, specifically those favorable to employment or economic gains.[^cite_note-beyond_inflation-targeting_2011-65]

The Bank of Canada reassessed their mandate 15 years later in 2026 in response to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Canada) and a [trade war with the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_United_States_trade_war_with_Canada).[^cite_note-\:10-66] Their framework was adjusted to prioritize reducing inflation, rising housing costs, and protection against [supply shocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_shock).[^cite_note-\:10-66]

### Real estate, rental, and leasing

Main articles: [Affordable housing in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing_in_Canada), [Public housing in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_housing_in_Canada), and [Condominiums in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominiums_in_Canada)

See also: [Canadian property bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_property_bubble), [Rent regulation in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent_regulation_in_Canada), [Canadian property law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_property_law), and [Homelessness in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_in_Canada)

Canada's real estate market is the backbone of the economy, comprising over 13% of the GDP by sector in 2024. In 2024, housing investment accounted for 25% of national wealth,[^cite_note-68] up from 21% in 2021.[^cite_note-69] It is difficult to discern the actual contribution of other sectors (such as construction, Investment and financial services, manufacturing, and forestry) to real estate, rental and leasing, as these industries are intrinsically linked in a complex economy. The Bank of Canada has increased its mortgage bond holdings to target 50% of the fixed-rate primary issuances.[^cite_note-70]

### Defence

See also: [Category\:Defence companies of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Defence_companies_of_Canada)

Canada's defence sector is a high-tech, export-driven industry, comprising nearly 600 firms that contribute over \$9.6 billion in GDP. Centered heavily on aerospace, simulation, advanced manufacturing and cyber technology, the industry exports close to \$8 billion annually.[^cite_note-71]

### Service sector

See also: [Category\:Service industries in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Service_industries_in_Canada)

The service sector in Canada is vast and multifaceted, employing about three quarters of Canadians and accounting for 70% of GDP.[^cite_note-auto-72] The largest employer is the [retail](183515.md) sector, employing almost 12% of Canadians.[^cite_note-73] The retail industry is concentrated mainly in a small number of chain stores clustered together in [shopping malls](235723.md). In recent years, there has been an increase in the number of [big-box stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big-box_store), such as [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart) (of the United States), [Real Canadian Superstore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Canadian_Superstore), and [Best Buy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Buy) (of the United States). This has led to fewer workers in this sector and the migration of retail jobs to the suburbs.



The [Financial District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_District,_Vancouver) in [Downtown Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Vancouver). Canadian business services are largely concentrated in large urban areas of Canada.

The second-largest portion of the service sector is the business service, and it employs only a slightly smaller percentage of the population.[^cite_note-74] This includes [financial services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_services), [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate), and communications industries. This portion of the economy has been rapidly growing in recent years. It is largely concentrated in the major urban centres, especially [Toronto](64646.md), [Montreal](7954681.md) and [Vancouver](32706.md).

The education and health sectors are two of Canada's largest, but both are primarily under the influence of the government. The health care industry has been quickly growing and is the third-largest in Canada.

Canada has an important [high tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech) industry,[^cite_note-75] and a burgeoning film, television, and entertainment industry creating content for local and international consumption (see [Media in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Canada)).[^cite_note-76] [Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Canada) attracts millions of visitors and supports approximately 10% of the national labor force. In recent years, statistics show that Canada has received over 20 million international tourists annually.[^cite_note-n066-77] The summer months are especially popular for travelers both domestically and internationally.[^cite_note-l240-78] Tourism and supporting industries contributed over \$100 billion to the Canadian national economy in 2024. The sector supports nearly 1.8 million Canadians working in tourism-related fields.[^cite_note-n066-77]

#### Banking and finance

Main articles: [Banking in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_in_Canada), [List of banks and credit unions in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_banks_and_credit_unions_in_Canada), and [List of insurance companies in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_insurance_companies_in_Canada)

This section is an excerpt from [Banking in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Banking_in_Canada\&action=edit)]



The towers at [Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Street) and [King Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Street_\(Toronto\)) in Toronto are home to four of Canada's five largest banks.

The banking sector of [Canada](5042916.md) is a large component of the [nation's private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companies_of_Canada) and the broader Canadian economy. It is dominated by a small number of large banks, with the six largest combining for 93% of the banking assets.[^cite_note-79] The two largest, the [Royal Bank of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bank_of_Canada) and the [Toronto Dominion Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Dominion_Bank) are among the world's 25 largest banks.[^cite_note-Banking_in_Canada_SP-80] It has been considered to be one of the safest and soundest banking systems in the world.[^cite_note-Banking_in_Canada_WEF-81]

Canada's banks have high service levels and investments in technology. A report released by the office of the [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_\(Canada\)) in 2002 states "Canada has the highest number of ATMs per capita in the world and benefits from the highest penetration levels of electronic channels such as [debit cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debit_cards), [Internet banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_banking) and [telephone banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_banking)".[^cite_note-Banking_in_Canada_fingcca-82] More recent data published by the [World Bank](36752.md) shows that as of 2017 Canada has 227.82 ATMs per 100,000 adults, which ranks the country third worldwide.[^cite_note-83]

[Education in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Canada) is for the most part provided [publicly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school), funded and overseen by [federal](616255.md), [provincial](75763.md), and [local governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government).[^cite_note-84] Education is within provincial jurisdiction and the curriculum is overseen by the province.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] Education in Canada is generally divided into [primary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_education), followed by secondary education and post-secondary. Education in both English and French is available in most places across Canada.[^cite_note-87] Canada has a large number of universities, almost all of which are publicly funded.[^cite_note-88] Established in 1663, [Université Laval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_Laval) is the oldest post-secondary institution in Canada.[^cite_note-Education_in_Canada_BlakeKeshen2017p249-89] The largest university is the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) with over 85,000 students.[^cite_note-90] Four universities are regularly ranked among the top 100 world-wide, namely the University of Toronto, [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia), [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), and [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University), with a total of [18 universities ranked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rankings_of_universities_in_Canada) in the top 500 worldwide.[^cite_note-91]

According to a 2022 report by the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)](33853117.md), Canada is the most educated country in the world;[^cite_note-Education_in_Canada_Statistics_Canada_2022_n626-92][^cite_note-Education_in_Canada_OECD_2023_t514-93] the country ranks first worldwide in the percentage of adults having [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education), with over 57 per cent of Canadian adults having attained at least an undergraduate college or university degree.[^cite_note-Education_in_Canada_Education_c106-94] Canada spends an average of about 5.3 per cent of its GDP on education.[^cite_note-Education_in_Canada_TheGlobalEconomy.com_1971_w626-95] The country invests heavily in tertiary education (more than US\$20,000 per student).[^cite_note-96] As of 2022, 89 per cent of adults aged 25 to 64 have earned the equivalent of a high-school degree, compared to an OECD average of 75 per cent.[^cite_note-Education_in_Canada_OECD_2023_t514-93]

The [mandatory education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education) age ranges between 5–7 to 16–18 years,[^cite_note-97] contributing to an adult literacy rate of 99 per cent.[^cite_note-Education_in_Canada_cia-98] Just over 60,000 children are [homeschooled in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling_in_Canada) as of 2016. The [Programme for International Student Assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_for_International_Student_Assessment) indicates Canadian students perform well above the OECD average, particularly in mathematics, science, and reading,[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] ranking the overall knowledge and skills of Canadian 15-year-olds as the sixth-best in the world, although these scores have been declining in recent years. Canada is a well-performing OECD country in reading literacy, mathematics, and science, with the average student scoring 523.7, compared with the OECD average of 493 in 2015.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

#### Healthcare

Main article: [Healthcare in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Canada)

This section is an excerpt from [Healthcare in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Healthcare_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

[Healthcare in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Canada) is delivered through the provincial and territorial systems of [publicly funded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicly_funded_health_care) [single-payer healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-payer_healthcare), informally called [Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_\(Canada\)).[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_AaseWaring2017-103][^cite_note-104] It is guided by the provisions of the *[Canada Health Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Health_Act),&#x20;*&#x61;dopted during 1984,[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_Bégin1988-105] and is [universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care).[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_LeattMapa2003-106]<sup>: 81 </sup> The 2002 [Royal Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_the_Future_of_Health_Care_in_Canada), known as the Romanow Report, revealed that Canadians consider universal access to publicly funded health services as a "fundamental value that ensures national health care insurance for everyone wherever they live in the country".[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_Romanow_Report_2002-107][^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_Romanow_Recommendations_2002-108]

Canadian Medicare provides coverage for approximately 70 percent of Canadians' healthcare needs, and the remaining 30 percent is paid for through the private sector.[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_CIHI_2019-109][^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_CIHI_2005-110] The 30 percent typically relates to services not covered or only partially covered by Medicare, such as [prescription drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescription_drug), eye care, [medical devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_device), [gender care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_health_care), [psychotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychotherapy), [physical therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_therapy) and [dentistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dentistry_in_Canada).[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_CIHI_2019-109][^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_CIHI_2005-110] About 65-75 percent of Canadians have some form of supplementary [health insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance) related to the aforementioned reasons; many receive it through their employers or use secondary [social programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_programs_in_Canada) related to extended coverage for families receiving [social assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_protection#Social_assistance) or vulnerable demographics, such as seniors, minors, and those with disabilities.[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_WaPo_20170701-111]

According to the [Canadian Institute for Health Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Institute_for_Health_Information) (CIHI), by 2019, Canada's [aging population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging_population) represents an increase in healthcare costs of approximately one percent a year, which is a modest increase. In a 2020 [Statistics Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Canada) Canadian Perspectives Survey Series (CPSS), 69 percent of Canadians self-reported that they had excellent or very good physical health—an improvement from 60 percent in 2018.[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_statcan_Findlay_20200424-112] In 2019, 80 percent of Canadian adults self-reported having at least one major risk factor for chronic disease: [smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking_in_Canada), physical inactivity, unhealthy eating, or excessive [alcohol](5026194.md) use.[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_GregoryStephens2019-113] Canada has [one of the highest rates of adult obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity_in_Canada) among [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](33853117.md) (OECD) countries attributing to approximately 2.7 million cases of [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes) (types 1 and 2 combined).[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_GregoryStephens2019-113] Four chronic diseases—[cancer](105219.md) (a leading cause of death), [cardiovascular diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease), [respiratory diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_disease) and diabetes account for 65 percent of deaths in Canada.[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_GregoryStephens2019-113] There are approximately 8 million individuals aged 15 and older with one or more [disabilities in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability_in_Canada).[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_k471-114]

In 2021, the [Canadian Institute for Health Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Institute_for_Health_Information) reported that [healthcare spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_spending_as_percent_of_gross_domestic_product_\(GDP\)_by_country) reached \$308 billion, or 12.7 percent of Canada's GDP for that year.[^cite_note-115] In 2022 Canada's per-capita spending on [health expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_total_health_expenditure_per_capita) ranked 12th among [healthcare systems in the OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_total_health_expenditure_per_capita).[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_OECD-barcharts-116] Canada has performed close to the average on the majority of OECD health indicators since the early 2000s,[^cite_note-117] and ranks above average for [access to care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care), but the number of doctors and hospital beds are considerably below the OECD average.[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_OECD_2017-118] The [Commonwealth Funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Fund) 2021 report comparing the healthcare systems of 11 high-income countries ranked Canada second-to-last.[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_auto3-119] Identified weaknesses of Canada's system were comparatively higher infant mortality rate, the prevalence of chronic conditions, long wait times, poor availability of after-hours care, and a lack of prescription drugs coverage.[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_Prescription_for_Canada-120] An increasing problem in Canada's health system is a shortage of [healthcare professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_professional) and hospital capacity.[^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_Duong_Vogel_2023_pp._E309–E310-121][^cite_note-Healthcare_in_Canada_Thiessen_Partner_Organization_Ma_2023_j399-122]

#### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Canada)

This section is an excerpt from [Tourism in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tourism_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

[Tourism in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Canada) is a major economic driver in the service sector, attracting millions of visitors and supporting approximately 10% of the national labor force. In recent years, statistics show that Canada has received over 20 million international tourists annually.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_n066-123] The summer months are especially popular for travellers both domestically and internationally.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_l240-124] Tourism and supporting industries contributed over \$100 billion to the Canadian national economy in 2024. The sector supports nearly 1.8 million Canadians working in tourism-related fields.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_n066-123]

Domestic tourism in Canada is the principal economic driver of the sector, with statistics indicating that, in 2024, about 70% of all tourism spending came from Canadian residents exploring their own country.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_n066-123] Domestic statistics also indicate that travel within provinces is prevalent, with residents supporting local businesses and attractions.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_n066-123] [Land border crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border_crossings) by United States citizens has historically been the largest source of inbound tourists, followed by [international airline travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_airports_in_Canada) from the United Kingdom and other European countries.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_f677-125] Visitors from Asia have become more popular in recent decades.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_l299-126] Passport holders from over 50 visa-exempt countries and nationals who require a visa can visit Canada for up to six months at a time.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_r896-127]

Federal, provincial, municipal and local government initiatives aimed at promoting sustainable tourism and attracting foreign tourists play a major role in the industry.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_u976-128] Canada is known for its safety and security,[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_k500-129] attracting tourists through its natural features (e.g., [Niagara Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_Falls)), [festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_festivals_in_Canada) (e.g., [Calgary stampede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_stampede)), [arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_art) (e.g., [Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival)), [heritage sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Canada), (e.g., [Quebec city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_city)) sporting events (e.g.,[Grey Cup](230107.md)), amusement parks (e.g., [Canada's Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada's_Wonderland)) and its diverse culture (e.g., [Caribbean Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribana)).

[Canada's national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_of_Canada) like [Banff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banff_National_Park) and [Jasper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasper_National_Park) see millions of visitors annually, drawn by their landscapes and outdoor activities.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_s746-130] [Provincial parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_park) including [Algonquin Provincial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquin_Provincial_Park), [Bow Valley Provincial variety of international and domestic tourist throughout the year.[^cite_note-Tourism_in_Canada_g459-132] The country is home to numerous historical and cultural [national ### Manufacturing

Main pages: [Technological and industrial history of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_and_industrial_history_of_Canada) and [Category\:Manufacturing in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Manufacturing_in_Canada)



[Ford](30433662.md)'s [Oakville Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakville_Assembly) in the [Greater Toronto Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Toronto_Area). Central Canada is home to several auto factories of the major American and Japanese automakers.

The [general pattern of development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-sector_model) for wealthy nations was a transition from a raw material production-based economy to a manufacturing-based economy and then to a service-based economy. At its World War II peak in 1944, Canada's manufacturing sector accounted for 29% of GDP,[^cite_note-mfggdp-135] declining to 10.37% in 2017.[^cite_note-industry_GDP-67] Canada has not suffered as greatly as most other rich, industrialized nations from the pains of the relative decline in the importance of manufacturing since the 1960s.[^cite_note-mfggdp-135] A 2009 study by [Statistics Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Canada) also found that, while manufacturing declined as a relative percentage of GDP from 24.3% in the 1960s to 15.6% in 2005, manufacturing volumes between 1961 and 2005 kept pace with the overall growth in the volume index of GDP.[^cite_note-136] Manufacturing in Canada declined significantly during the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession). As of 2017, manufacturing accounts for 10% of Canada's GDP,[^cite_note-industry_GDP-67] a relative decline of more than 5% of GDP since 2005.

Central Canada is home to [branch plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_plant) to all the major American and Japanese automobile makers and many parts factories owned by Canadian firms such as [Magna International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_International) and [Linamar Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linamar).

#### Automotive

Main article: [Automotive industry in Canada](23560776.md)

This section is an excerpt from [Automotive industry in Canada](23560776.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Automotive_industry_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

The [automotive industry in Canada](23560776.md) consists primarily of assembly plants of foreign [automakers](163778.md), most with headquarters in the United States or Japan, along with hundreds of manufacturers of [automotive parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_auto_parts) and systems.

Canada is currently the [thirteenth-largest auto-producing nation in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_motor_vehicle_production), and seventh largest auto exporter by value, producing 1.4 million vehicles and exporting \$32 billion worth of vehicles in 2020.[^cite_note-137] Canada's highest rankings ever were the second-largest producer in the world between 1918 and 1923 and third-largest after World War II.

Automotive manufacturing is one of Canada's largest industrial sectors, accounting for 10% of manufacturing GDP and 23% of manufacturing trade. Canada produces passenger vehicles, trucks and buses, auto parts and systems, truck bodies and trailers, as well as tires and machine, tools, dies and molds (MTDM). The auto industry directly employs more than 125,000 people in vehicle assembly and auto parts manufacturing, and another 380,000 in distribution and aftermarket sales and service.

#### Steel



[ArcelorMittal Dofasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dofasco), view from Burlington Street

Canada was the world's nineteenth-largest steel exporter in 2018. In year-to-date 2019 (through March), further referred to as YTD 2019, Canada exported 1.39 million metric tons of steel, a 22 percent decrease from 1.79 million metric tons in YTD 2018. Based on available data, Canada's exports represented about 1.5 percent of all steel exported globally in 2017. By volume, Canada's 2018 steel exports represented just over one-tenth the volume of the world's largest exporter, China. In value terms, steel represented 1.4 percent of the total goods Canada exported in 2018. The growth in exports in the decade since 2009 has been 29%. The largest producers in 2018 were [ArcelorMittal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal), [Essar Steel Algoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essar_Steel_Algoma), and the first of those alone accounted for roughly half of Canadian steel production through its two subsidiaries. The top two markets for Canada's exports were its [NAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAFTA) partners, and by themselves accounted for 92 percent of exports by volume. Canada sent 83 percent of its steel exports to the United States in YTD 2019. The gap between domestic demand and domestic production increased to −2.4 million metric tons, up from −0.2 million metric tons in YTD 2018. In YTD 2019, exports as a share of production decreased to 41.6 percent from 53 percent in YTD 2018.[^cite_note-usdoc-139]

In 2017, [heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry) accounted for 10.2% of Canada's greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-\:1-140]

### Energy

Main article: [Energy in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Canada)

See also: [Renewable energy in Canada](21730962.md)

Canada has access to cheap sources of energy because of its geography. This has enabled the creation of several important industries, such as the large [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium) industries in British Columbia[^cite_note-159] and Quebec.[^cite_note-160] Canada is also one of the world's highest per capita consumers of energy.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

#### Electricity

Main article: [Electricity sector in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_Canada)



Share of electricity generation in Canada – percentage share

The [electricity sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector) in [Canada](5042916.md) has played a significant role in the economic and political life of the country since the late 19th century. The sector is organized along provincial and territorial lines. In a majority of provinces, large [government-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government-owned_company) integrated public utilities play a leading role in the [generation](9540.md), [transmission](38824.md) and [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_distribution) of electricity. [Ontario](22218.md) and [Alberta](717.md) have created [electricity markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_market) in the last decade in order to increase investment and competition in this sector of the economy. In 2017, the electricity sector accounted for 10% of total national greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-\:0-163] Canada has substantial electricity trade with the neighbouring [United States](3434750.md) amounting to 72 TWh exports and 10 TWh imports in 2017.

[Hydroelectricity](381399.md) accounted for 59% of all electric generation in Canada in 2016,[^cite_note-164] making Canada the world's second-largest producer of hydroelectricity after China.[^cite_note-165] Since 1960, large hydroelectric projects, especially in [Quebec](7954867.md), [British Columbia](3392.md), [Manitoba](18926.md) and [Newfoundland and Labrador](21980.md), have significantly increased the country's generation capacity.

The second-largest single source of power (15% of the total) is nuclear power, with several plants in Ontario generating more than half of that province's electricity and one generator in [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Lepreau_Nuclear_Generating_Station). This makes Canada the world's sixth-largest electricity producer generated by nuclear power, producing 95 TWh in 2017.[^cite_note-166]

Fossil fuels provide 19% of Canadian electric power, about half as coal (9% of the total), and the remainder a mix of natural gas and oil. Only five provinces use coal for electricity generation. Alberta, Saskatchewan, and Nova Scotia rely on coal for nearly half of their generation, while other provinces and territories use little or none. Alberta and Saskatchewan also use a substantial amount of natural gas. Remote communities, including all of Nunavut and much of the Northwest Territories, produce most of their electricity from diesel generators at high economic and environmental costs. The federal government has set up initiatives to reduce dependence on diesel-fired electricity.[^cite_note-167]

Non-hydro renewables are a fast-growing portion of the total, at 7% in 2016.

#### Oil and gas

Main articles: [Petroleum industry in Canada](4071088.md), [Fracking in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fracking_in_Canada), and [Pipelines in Canada](60372560.md)

See also: [History of the petroleum industry in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_petroleum_industry_in_Canada)



[Syncrude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncrude)'s Mildred Lake plant site at the [Athabasca oil sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athabasca_oil_sands) in [Alberta](717.md)

Canada possesses extensive oil and gas resources centred in Alberta, and the Northern Territories but is also present in neighboring [British Columbia](3392.md) and [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan). The vast [Athabasca oil sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athabasca_oil_sands) give Canada the world's third-largest reserves of oil after Saudi Arabia and Venezuela, according to [USGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USGS). The oil and gas industry represents 27% of Canada's total [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md), an increase of 84% since 1990, mostly due to the development of the oil sands.[^cite_note-\:0-163]

Historically, an important issue in Canadian politics is the interplay between the oil and energy industry in [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada) and the industrial heartland of Southern Ontario. Foreign investment in Western oil projects has fueled [Canada](5042916.md)'s rising dollar. This has raised the price of Ontario's manufacturing exports and made them less competitive, a problem similar to the [decline of the manufacturing sector in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_disease).[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169]

The [National Energy Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Energy_Policy) of the early 1980s attempted to make Canada oil-sufficient and to ensure equal supply and price of oil in all parts of Canada, especially for the eastern manufacturing base.[^cite_note-canadian_eclopedia-170] This policy proved deeply divisive as it forced Alberta to sell low-priced oil to eastern Canada.[^cite_note-National_Post_2012-171] The policy was eliminated 5 years after it was first announced amid a collapse of oil prices in 1985. The new Prime Minister [Brian Mulroney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Mulroney) had campaigned against the policy in the [1984 Canadian federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Canadian_federal_election). One of the most controversial sections of the [Canada–United States Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93United_States_Free_Trade_Agreement) of 1988 was a promise that Canada would never charge the United States more for energy than fellow Canadians.[^cite_note-\:1-140]

### Agriculture, fishing, livestock and forestry

Main articles: [Agriculture in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Canada), [Aquaculture in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture_in_Canada), [Dairy farming in Canada](29012710.md), [Fishing industry in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry_in_Canada), and [Forestry in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry_in_Canada)

See also: [Dairy and poultry supply management in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_and_poultry_supply_management_in_Canada)



An inland grain terminal along the [Yellowhead Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowhead_Highway) in [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan)

Canada is one of the world's largest suppliers of agricultural products, particularly wheat and other grains.[^cite_note-172] Canada is a major exporter of agricultural products, to the United States and Asia. As with all other developed nations, the proportion of the population and GDP devoted to agriculture fell dramatically over the 20th century. The agriculture and agri-food manufacturing sector created \$49.0 billion to Canada's GDP in 2015, accounting for 2.6% of total GDP.[^cite_note-173] This sector also accounts for 8.4% of Canada's Greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-\:1-140]

The Canadian agriculture industry receives significant government subsidies and support as with other developed nations.[^cite_note-174] However, Canada has strongly supported reducing market influencing subsidies through the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization). In 2000, Canada spent approximately CDN\$4.6 billion on support for the industry. \$2.32 billion was classified under the WTO designation of "green box" license, meaning it did not directly influence the market, such as money for research or disaster relief. All but \$848.2 million were subsidies worth less than 5% of the value of the crops they were provided for.

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Canada)

See also: [Roads in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_in_Canada), [List of Canadian railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Canadian_railways), [List of airports in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Canada), [List of airlines of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airlines_of_Canada), and [Category\:Ports and harbours of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Ports_and_harbours_of_Canada)

This section is an excerpt from [Transportation in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Transportation_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

Canada, the world's [second-largest country in total area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area), is dedicated to having efficient, high-capacity multimodal [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation) spanning often vast distances between [natural resource extraction sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Canada), [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Canada) and [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_census_metropolitan_areas_and_agglomerations_in_Canada). Canada's transportation system includes more than 1,400,000 kilometres (870,000 mi) of roads, 10 major international airports, 300 smaller airports, 72,093 km (44,797 mi) of functioning railway track, and more than 300 commercial ports and harbours that provide access to the [Pacific](23070.md), [Atlantic](698.md) and [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean) oceans as well as the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) and the [St. Lawrence Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_Seaway).[^cite_note-175] In 2005, the transportation sector made up 4.2% of Canada's GDP, compared to 3.7% for Canada's mining and oil and gas extraction industries.[^cite_note-Transportation_in_Canada_Trans_Sector_05-176]

[Transport Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Canada) oversees and regulates most aspects of transportation within federal jurisdiction, including interprovincial transport. This primarily includes rail, air and maritime transportation. Transport Canada is under the direction of the federal government's [Minister of Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Transport_\(Canada\)). The [Transportation Safety Board of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Safety_Board_of_Canada) is responsible for maintaining transportation safety in Canada by investigating accidents and making safety recommendations.

### Water supply and sanitation

Main article: [Water supply and sanitation in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_supply_and_sanitation_in_Canada)

This section is an excerpt from [Water supply and sanitation in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_supply_and_sanitation_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Water_supply_and_sanitation_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

[Canada](5042916.md) has nearly universal and generally good quality access to water, but a lack of clean drinking water in many First Nations communities remains a problem.[^cite_note-177] Water use in [Canada](5042916.md) is high compared to Europe, since water tariffs are low and 44% of users are not [metered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_metering).

Despite a commitment by the federal government to promote increased cost recovery, only 50% of the cost of maintaining and operating water infrastructure is actually being recovered from users through tariffs, the rest being financed through taxes.

## Science and technology

Main article: [Science and technology in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Canada)

See also: [Canadian industrial research and development organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_industrial_research_and_development_organizations) and [Artificial intelligence industry in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_industry_in_Canada)

This section is an excerpt from [Science and technology in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Science_and_technology_in_Canada\&action=edit)]



The Canadian-built [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) robotic arm (left), known as [Canadarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadarm), transferred the [P5 truss segment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Truss_Structure) over to the Canadian-built [space station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_station) robotic arm, known as [Canadarm2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadarm2).

In 2019, [Canada](5042916.md) spent approximately CA\$40.3 billion on domestic [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development), of which over \$7 billion was provided by the federal and provincial governments.[^cite_note-178] In 2018, Canada spent approximately C\$34.5 billion on domestic [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development), of which around \$2 billion was spent directly by the federal government in-house and an additional \$5.7 billion was provided by provincial and federal sources in the form of grants.[^cite_note-179] This investment corresponds to about 1.57% of Canada's [gross domestic product](12594.md), a decline from 1.72% in 2014.[^cite_note-Science_and_technology_in_Canada_UNESCO_Science_Report_2021-180] Canada was ranked 17th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

As of 2020, the country has produced fifteen [Nobel laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_by_country) in [physics](52497.md), [chemistry](25416893.md), and [medicine](52502.md),[^cite_note-183] and was ranked fourth worldwide for scientific research quality in a major 2012 survey of international scientists.[^cite_note-184] It is furthermore home to the headquarters of a number of global technology firms.[^cite_note-185] Canada [has one of the highest levels of Internet access in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_Internet_users), with over 33 million users, equivalent to around 94 percent of its total 2014 population.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189]

Some of the most notable scientific developments in Canada include the creation of the modern [alkaline battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaline_battery)[^cite_note-190] and the [polio vaccine](192198.md)[^cite_note-Science_and_technology_in_Canada_Farrell-191] and discoveries about the interior structure of the [atomic nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus).[^cite_note-Science_and_technology_in_Canada_Katz-192] Other major Canadian scientific contributions include the [artificial cardiac pacemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_cardiac_pacemaker), mapping the [visual cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_cortex),[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-Science_and_technology_in_Canada_topten-194] the development of the [electron microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscope),[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196] [plate tectonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics), [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning), [multi-touch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-touch) technology and the identification of the first [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole), [Cygnus X-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_X-1).[^cite_note-197] Canada has a long history of discovery in genetics, which include [stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell), [site-directed mutagenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site-directed_mutagenesis), [T-cell receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-cell_receptor) and the identification of the genes that cause [Fanconi anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanconi_anemia), [cystic fibrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cystic_fibrosis) and [early-onset Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early-onset_Alzheimer's_disease), among numerous other diseases.[^cite_note-Science_and_technology_in_Canada_topten-194][^cite_note-198]

The [Canadian Space Agency](328618.md) operates a highly active space program, conducting deep-space, planetary, and aviation research, and developing rockets and satellites.[^cite_note-199] Canada was the third country to design and construct a satellite after the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_space_program) and the United States, with the 1962 [Alouette 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alouette_1) launch.[^cite_note-Science_and_technology_in_Canada_Angelo2009s-200] Canada is a participant in the [International Space Station](15043.md) (ISS), and is a pioneer in space robotics, having constructed the [Canadarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadarm), [Canadarm2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadarm2) and [Dextre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dextre) robotic manipulators for the ISS and NASA's [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle).[^cite_note-Science_and_technology_in_Canada_Bidaud2012-201] Since the 1960s, Canada's aerospace industry has designed and built numerous marques of satellite, including [Radarsat-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radarsat-1) and [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radarsat-2), [ISIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISIS_\(satellite\)) and [MOST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOST_\(spacecraft\)).[^cite_note-202] Canada has also produced one of the world's most successful and widely used [sounding rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sounding_rocket), the [Black Brant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Brant_\(rocket\)); over 1,000 Black Brants have been launched since the rocket's introduction in 1961.[^cite_note-Science_and_technology_in_Canada_Godefroy2017-203]

### Political issues

#### Canada–United States trade relations

Main article: [Canada–United States trade relations](17206485.md)

Canada and the United States share a common trading relationship. Canada's job market continues to perform well along with the US, reaching a 30-year low in the unemployment rate in December 2006, following 14 consecutive years of employment growth.[^cite_note-207]



Flags of Canada and the United States

The United States is by far Canada's largest trading partner, with more than \$1.7 billion [CAD](101846.md) in trade per day in 2005.[^cite_note-208] In 2009, 73% of Canada's exports went to the United States, and 63% of Canada's imports were from the United States.[^cite_note-209] Trade with Canada makes up 23% of the United States' exports and 17% of its imports.[^cite_note-210] By comparison, in 2005 this was more than U.S. trade with all countries in the European Union combined,[^cite_note-211] and well over twice U.S. trade with all the countries of [Latin America](18524.md) combined.[^cite_note-212] Just the two-way trade that crosses the [Ambassador Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_Bridge) between [Michigan](18859.md) and [Ontario](22218.md) equals all U.S. exports to [Japan](15573.md). Canada's importance to the United States is not just a border-state phenomenon: Canada is the leading export market for 35 of 50 U.S. states, and is the United States' largest foreign supplier of energy.

Bilateral trade increased by 52% between 1989, when the [U.S.–Canada Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Canada_Free_Trade_Agreement) (FTA) went into effect, and 1994, when the [North American Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Free_Trade_Agreement) (NAFTA) superseded it.[^cite_note-213] Trade has since increased by 40%. NAFTA continues the FTA's moves toward reducing trade barriers and establishing agreed-upon trade rules. It also resolves some long-standing bilateral irritants and liberalizes rules in several areas, including agriculture, services, energy, financial services, investment, and government procurement. NAFTA forms the largest trading area in the world, embracing the 405 million people of the three North American countries.

The largest component of U.S.–Canada trade is in the energy sector.

The U.S. is Canada's largest agricultural export market, taking well over half of all Canadian food exports.[^cite_note-214] Nearly two-thirds of Canada's forest products, including [pulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_\(paper\)) and [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper), are exported to the United States; 72% of Canada's total [newsprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsprint) production also is exported to the U.S.

At [\$73.6](https://web.archive.org/web/20060411203756/http://www.nrcan.gc.ca/statistics/energy/default.html) billion in 2004, U.S.-Canada trade in [energy](533487.md) is the largest U.S. energy trading relationship, with the overwhelming majority (\$66.7 billion) being exports from Canada. The primary components of U.S. energy trade with Canada are [petroleum](23195.md), [natural gas](22131.md), and [electricity](9550.md). Canada is the United States' largest oil supplier and the fifth-largest energy producing country in the world. Canada provides about 16% of U.S. oil imports and 14% of total U.S. consumption of natural gas. The United States and Canada's national electricity grids are linked, and both countries share [hydropower](14073.md) facilities on the western borders.

While most of U.S.-Canada trade flows smoothly, there are occasionally bilateral trade disputes, particularly in the agricultural and cultural fields. Usually these issues are resolved through bilateral consultative forums or referral to [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) (WTO) or NAFTA dispute resolution. In May 1999, the U.S. and Canadian governments negotiated an agreement on [magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magazine) that provides increased access for the U.S. [publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publishing) industry to the Canadian market. The United States and Canada also have resolved several major issues involving [fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery). By common agreement, the two countries submitted a [Gulf of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Maine) boundary dispute to the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) in 1981; both accepted the court's October 12, 1984, ruling which demarcated the territorial sea boundary. A current issue between the United States and Canada is the ongoing [softwood lumber dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93United_States_softwood_lumber_dispute), as the U.S. alleges that Canada unfairly subsidizes its forestry industry.

In 1990, the United States and Canada signed a bilateral Fisheries Enforcement Agreement, which has served to deter [illegal fishing](6346579.md) activity and reduce the risk of injury during fisheries enforcement incidents. The U.S. and Canada signed a [Pacific Salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Salmon) Agreement in June 1999 that settled differences over implementation of the 1985 Pacific Salmon Treaty for the next decade.[^cite_note-215]

Canada and the United States signed an [aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation) agreement during [Bill Clinton](3356.md)'s visit to Canada in February 1995, and air traffic between the two countries has increased dramatically as a result. The two countries also share in operation of the [St. Lawrence Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_Seaway), connecting the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) to the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md).[^cite_note-216]

The U.S. remains Canada's largest foreign investor and the most popular destination for Canadian foreign investments. In 2018, the stock of U.S. direct investment in Canada totaled \$406 billion, while the stock of Canadian investment in the U.S. totaled \$595 billion, or 46% of the overall CDIA stock for 2018.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218] This made Canada the second largest investing country in the U.S. for 2018[^cite_note-219] US investments are primarily directed at Canada's [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) and [smelting](28716.md) industries, petroleum, chemicals, the manufacture of machinery and transportation equipment, and finance, while Canadian investment in the United States is concentrated in manufacturing, wholesale trade, real estate, petroleum, finance, insurance and other services.[^cite_note-220]

#### Corruption

This section is an excerpt from [Corruption in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Corruption_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

[Corruption in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Canada) is the [use of political power for private gain](66220.md) by Canadian government officials.

Canada had the distinction of being the least corrupt government in [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Americas) in 2023, as measured by the [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index).[^cite_note-221] [Conflicts of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflicts_of_interest) within government, tax evasion, and the prevalence of [money laundering](19390.md) in areas such as [British Columbia](3392.md)[^cite_note-222] are among the leading factors of corruption in Canada.

Canada ranks at the bottom of the bribery-fighting rankings with "little or no enforcement of anti-bribery measures".[^cite_note-Corruption_in_Canada_canada_at_bottom_in_fighting_bribery-223] The 2014 [Ernst & Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_&_Young) global fraud survey found that "twenty percent of Canadian executives believe bribery and corruption are widespread in this country".[^cite_note-Corruption_in_Canada_canada_not_immune-224]

#### Immigration

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Economic impact of immigration to Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_immigration_to_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Economic_impact_of_immigration_to_Canada\&action=edit)]

The [economic impact of immigration to Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_immigration_to_Canada) is dominated by two conflicting narratives: higher immigration levels increase GDP[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226] but decrease GDP per capita or living standards for the resident population[^cite_note-227][^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229] and lead to [diseconomies of scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diseconomies_of_scale) in terms of overcrowding of hospitals, schools and recreational facilities, deteriorating environment, increase in cost of services, and an increase in cost of housing.[^cite_note-230] A commonly supported argument is that impact of immigration on GDP is not an effective metric for immigration.[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-Economic_impact_of_immigration_to_Canada_Portes_2019_pp._12–17-232] Another narrative regarding immigration is the replacement of the aging workforce.[^cite_note-233] However, some economists note that increasing immigration rates is not an entirely effective strategy to counter it.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235] Increased immigration numbers and the resulting surge in housing prices significantly contributed to the rise of inflation in 2021 to an 18-year high, while the immigration caps introduced in 2025 have been linked to increasing housing affordability through a positive drop in average asking monthly rents, ranging from 2 to 8 percent in several major cities.[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239]

Canada is one of the top Western countries in terms of per capita immigrant acceptance.[^cite_note-Economic_impact_of_immigration_to_Canada_Reality_Check_team_2019-240] The per capita immigration rate to Canada has been relatively constant since the 1950s. However, in the first and second decades of the 21st century, there was a steady increase in the education and skill level of immigrants to Canada. This was due to the focus on higher average productivity-based applicants, resulting in immigrants to Canada being, on average, better educated than Canadians.[^cite_note-Economic_impact_of_immigration_to_Canada_CBC_2017-241][^cite_note-Economic_impact_of_immigration_to_Canada_How_Canada_Performs_2016-242] This trend was enhanced for income redistribution in the third decade of the 21st century by opening low-skilled immigrant pathways with minimal immigration score requirements to reach a target of 400,000 immigrants annually. This has cemented a new narrative on immigration: immigration is to fill low-skilled jobs and alleviate competitive labor market pressures faced by businesses that use cheap labor.[^cite_note-243][^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245] Starting in 2022, the Trudeau government has set immigration targets influenced by the [Century Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_Initiative)'s lobbying. Over a million immigrants were targeted in 2022, followed by 465,000 immigrants in 2023, 485,000 immigrants in 2024, and a projection of 500,000 immigrants in 2025.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248] Across Canada, people have been asking the government to match [affordable housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing_in_Canada) to the set immigration levels, while the government annually welcomes 500,000 new permanent residents, and more than 800,000 foreign nationals into the country on study visas, as asylum seekers, and on temporary work visas.[^cite_note-249] A Canadian journalist highlighted the poor preparedness for receiving immigrants in swathes, pointing to Canada's historic need for a "servant class" and "cheap labor" for the bourgeoisie and the owning class. Furthermore, the journalist observed that Canada's failure to address or neutralize the social and professional barriers for immigrants suggests it is not as welcoming as it purports to be. This policy of inaction silently [perpetuates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_justification_theory) the creation of a servant class within the country's diverse [mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_mosaic). As a part of the process, by default, the government systematically forces a majority of immigrants into vulnerable positions and economic disenfranchisement.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251]

An article by an ex-policy maker states that Canada is rooting for the low-wage-low-productivity model of competitiveness that it has been locked in since the mid-1980s with the immigration targets, a problematic approach according to [Paul Krugman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Krugman) in the long term, and which the ex-policy maker also endorses by stating that throwing more cheap labor at problems without a significant increase in productivity will affect a country's ability to improve its standard of living over time.[^cite_note-252] Some economists also warn that sustaining low productivity and the permanent integration of low-skilled temporary residents or foreign workers would lead to forced tax increases to manage associated government costs.[^cite_note-253] The article further observes that it is imperative that Canada and the "government for the people" require a fundamental re-commitment to pre-1970s dominating national objective and efforts for a steady improvement in raising the living standard of Canadians by embracing the supply and demand concept of labor economics, and improving the economic efficiency of the system.[^cite_note-254] A former director from Quebec's Ministry of Immigration observed that the government needs to treat people better, as their lives and families' futures are at stake.[^cite_note-255]

#### Taxation

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Taxation in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Taxation_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

[Taxation in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Canada) involves the assessment and collection of [taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax) at each level of government, including [federally](616255.md), [provincially and territorially](75763.md), and [locally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Canada). The collection of federal, provincial and territorial income taxes is mainly administered by the federal government through the [Canada Revenue Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Revenue_Agency), which then remits the provincial and territorial shares to the relevant provinces and territories. The exception is [Quebec](7954867.md), where [Revenu Québec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenu_Qu%C3%A9bec) administers the collection of provincial income tax.

Taxes are levied on various income sources, including income, payroll, property, sales, capital gains, dividends, imports, among others; various fees are additionally imposed. As of 2024, taxes collected at the federal, provincial and territorial, and local levels of government amounted to 34.9% of the country's total [GDP](12594.md), above the [OECD](33853117.md) average of 34.1% of GDP.[^cite_note-256]

The [income tax policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax_in_Canada) of Canada is based on the [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_tax) model of taxation, with individuals and businesses paying a higher percentage of tax as the amount of income earned increases.[^cite_note-257]

This section is an excerpt from [Minimum wage in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Minimum_wage_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

Under the [Constitution of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada), the responsibility for enacting and enforcing [labour laws](18657.md), including the [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage), rests primarily with the ten [Provinces of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Canada). The three [Territories of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_Canada) have a similar power, delegated to them by federal legislation. Some provinces allow lower wages to be paid to liquor servers and other gratuity earners or to inexperienced employees.

The [Government of Canada](616255.md) has the constitutional authority to set minimum wages only for employees within [federal jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_federalism), such as federal public servants and workers in industries that are under federal regulatory jurisdiction, such as banks, airlines and interprovincial railways. The federal government earlier set its own minimum wage rates for workers under its jurisdiction. In 1996, however, the [federal minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Labour_Code#Part_3:_Employment_Standards_Act) was redefined to be the general adult minimum wage rate of the province or territory where the work is performed. Following the [2021 budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_federal_budget), the Government of Canada reestablished a federal minimum wage for federally regulated industries on December 29, 2021.[^cite_note-259]

This section is an excerpt from [Poverty in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Poverty_in_Canada\&action=edit)]



Unemployed people lining up outside a community [soup kitchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup_kitchen) in [Quebec City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City)

[Poverty in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Canada) refers to the state or condition in which a person or household lacks essential resources—financial or otherwise—to maintain a modest standard of living in their community.

Researchers and governments have used different metrics to measure [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty) in Canada including Low-Income Cut-Off (LICO), Low Income Measure (LIM), and Market Basket Measure (MBM).[^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_HRSDC_2008-260] In November 2018, [Employment and Social Development Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment_and_Social_Development_Canada) announced the establishment of Canada's first Official Poverty Line to be based on the MBM. The MBM considers the cost of a basket of basic goods and services needed by a family of two adults and two children to maintain a modest standard of living.[^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_Opportunity_for_all-261][^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_Canadian_Income_Survey,_2017-262][^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_Canadian_Income_Survey,_2020-263]

There was an increase in poverty in Canada and many other industrial nations in the 1980s.[^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_Kazemipur_20000301-264] By 2008, Canada's poverty rate was among the highest of [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development) (OECD) member nations—the wealthiest countries in the world.[^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_OECD2008-265]

The number of people living below the official poverty line decreased substantially from 14.5% in 2015 to 10.1% in 2019,[^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_Canadian_Income_Survey,_2019-266] and 6.4% in 2020.[^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_Canadian_Income_Survey,_2020-263]

[Child poverty in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_poverty_in_Canada) declined since 2015, with the number of children who were living in poverty decreasing 71% by 2020. Child poverty has a disproportionately high effect on Indigenous households in Canada.[^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_Beedie_20190624-267] According to a 2019 study by researchers at the [Assembly of First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_First_Nations) and the [Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Centre_for_Policy_Alternatives) (CCPA), nearly 50% of Indigenous children in Canada—both on and off reserve—were living in poverty.[^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_Beedie_20190624-267]

As of 2020, about 2.4 million Canadians, or 6.4% of the population, lived below the poverty line, according to [Statistics Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Canada) Canadian Income Survey, 2020 released on March 23, 2022.[^cite_note-Poverty_in_Canada_Canadian_Income_Survey,_2020-263]

#### Canadian government debt

Main article: [Canadian public debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_public_debt)

[Canadian government debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_public_debt), also called Canada's public debt, is the liabilities of the government sector. For 2019 (the fiscal year ending March 31, 2020), total financial liabilities or gross debt was \$2.434 trillion for the consolidated Canadian general government (federal, provincial, territorial, and local governments combined). This corresponds to 105.3% as a ratio of GDP (GDP was \$2.311 trillion).[^cite_note-SC_largeDeficit-268] Of the \$2.434 trillion, \$1.146 trillion or 47% was federal (central) government liabilities (49.6% as a ratio of GDP). Provincial government liabilities comprise most of the remaining liabilities.[^cite_note-SC_largeDeficit-268]

#### Household debt

[Household debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_debt), the amount of money that all adults in the household owe financial institutions, includes [consumer debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_debt) and [mortgage loans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgage_loan). In March 2015, the International Monetary Fund reported that Canada's high household debt was one of two vulnerable domestic areas in Canada's economy; the second is its [overheated housing market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_property_bubble).[^cite_note-IMF_2015_CA-269]

According to Statistics Canada, total household credit as of July 2019 was CAD\$2.2 trillion.[^cite_note-Statistics_Canada-270] According to Philip Cross of the [Fraser Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Institute), in May 2015, while the Canadian household debt-to-income ratio is similar to that in the US, however lending standards in Canada are tighter than those in the United States to protect against high-risk borrowers taking out unsustainable debt.[^cite_note-271]

## Green economy

This section is an excerpt from [Green economy policies in Canada](53634914.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Green_economy_policies_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

[Green economy policies in Canada](53634914.md) are policies that contribute to transitioning the Canadian economy to a more environmentally sustainable one. The [green economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_economy) can be defined as an economy, "that results in improved human well-being and social equity, while significantly reducing environmental risks and ecological scarcities."[^cite_note-Green_economy_policies_in_Canada_\:8-272] Aspects of a green economy would include stable growth in income and employment that is driven by private and public investment into policies and actions that reduce carbon emissions, pollution and prevent the [loss of biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss).[^cite_note-Green_economy_policies_in_Canada_\:8-272]



Wind turbines in Nova Scotia

Green economy policies can be defined as legislation or actions put forth by public institutions with the main intent of further establishing an environmentally sustainable system. Some of these policies would include investments into green energy sectors aimed at reducing carbon emissions, and aiding the growth of renewable energy resources.[^cite_note-273] Green economy policies can also be considered on an international scale, with agreements such as the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol) and the [Paris Agreement](30242372.md). Policies on a national level include, agreements such as the [Western Climate Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Climate_Initiative), and the Boreal Forest Agreement among many others.

Green economy policies have become popular across various public jurisdictions in Canada. There has been an implementation of policies and initiatives across various levels of government. Due to the rise in frequency of these green economy policies in Canada, the socio-technical landscape is changing. More Canadians are supportive of green energy policies and a transition into a green economy.[^cite_note-274]
