The **Joint List** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): القائمة المشتركة, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-Qa'imah al-Mushtarakah*; [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): הָרְשִׁימָה הַמְּשֻׁתֶּפֶת, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Hebrew): *HaReshima HaMeshutefet*) is a [political alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_alliance) of [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab-Israeli)-majority political parties in Israel. It was originally formed by [Hadash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash), [Balad](44494596.md), [Ra'am](39385583.md) and [Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta'al) ahead of the [2015 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Israeli_legislative_election).

The alliance was the third-largest faction in the [Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_twentieth_Knesset) after the [2015 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Israeli_legislative_election), estimated to have received 82% of the Arab vote.[^cite_note-20] In January 2019, [Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta'al) split from the alliance, and the remaining coalition was dissolved on 21 February 2019.[^cite_note-21] The Joint List was reestablished on 28 July for the [September 2019 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2019_Israeli_legislative_election),[^cite_note-22] in which it won 13 seats and was again the third-largest faction.[^cite_note-23] In the [2020 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Israeli_legislative_election), the Joint List increased its seats from 13 to 15, described by *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* as "an unprecedented showing."[^cite_note-24] In the buildup to the [2021 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israeli_legislative_election), the Islamic conservative-leaning Ra'am left the Joint List due to ideological disagreements and ran on its own, gaining four seats, while the seats held by the Joint List fell to six. The alliance broke up prior to the [2022 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Israeli_legislative_election), with Hadash and Ta'al running together and Balad running alone.

Hadash, Ta'al and Balad reached an agreement, following extensive negotiations, on 19 August 2026 to run together in the [2026 Israeli legislative election](71244652.md). The slate is set to be headed by Hadash chairman [Yousef Jabareen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousef_Jabareen), followed by Ta'al chairman [Ahmad Tibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Tibi) and Balad chairman [Sami Abu Shehadeh](61848927.md), while Ra'am is expected to run separately.

### Initial formation in 2015





The party logos in 2015.



The Joint List during the [consultation process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law:_The_Government) at [President](153049.md) [Reuven Rivlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuven_Rivlin)'s official residence, after the [2015 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Israeli_legislative_election)&#x20;

When formed ahead of the [2015 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Israeli_legislative_election), the alliance was temporarily known as Wamab.[^cite_note-25]

The agreement between the parties, which included [Balad](44494596.md), [Hadash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash), [Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta'al), and [Ra'am](39385583.md) was signed on 22 January,[^cite_note-TOI-26] marking the first time the major Arab parties had run as a single list.[^cite_note-TOI1-27] Balad, Hadash, and Ra'am had run separately for elections since the 1990s (Balad and Hadash ran together in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Israeli_general_election)), whilst Ta'al had run in alliance with all three during the 1990s and 2000s. However, the raising of the [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) from 2% to 3.25% led to the parties creating an alliance to increase their chances of crossing the threshold,[^cite_note-TOI-26] as both Hadash and Balad received less than 3% of the vote in the [2013 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Israeli_legislative_election). Initially, the parties mulled running as two blocs (Hadash with Ta'al, and Balad with the Islamic Movement), but party representatives said pressure from the Arab public pushed them to join forces.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The northern branch of the Islamic Movement denounced the entire electoral project.[^cite_note-30]

A reconciliation committee was formed, which created the electoral list ahead of the election.[^cite_note-31] The alliance's list for the 2015 elections was headed by [Ayman Odeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_Odeh), the newly-elected leader of Hadash, followed by [Masud Ghnaim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masud_Ghnaim) ([Ra'am](39385583.md)), [Jamal Zahalka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Zahalka) (Balad), and [Ahmad Tibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Tibi) ([Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta'al)), with the following places alternating between Hadash, the Islamic Movement, and Balad. The 12th to 14th places were subject to rotation agreements between the parties.[^cite_note-32]

The Joint List won 13 seats in the [2015 Knesset elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Israeli_legislative_election) with 10.6% of the total vote, becoming the third-largest party in the [20th Knesset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Knesset).[^cite_note-cec_20th-33] Odeh stated that he intended for the alliance to work on shared issues with center-left Jewish opposition parties and seek membership of key parliamentary committees.[^cite_note-34]

One of the party's first actions after the elections was to trade the two seats that, as the third-largest faction, it was entitled to on the [Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_and_Defense_Committee) for two more seats on the [Finance Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_Committee_\(Israel\)), primarily to better address its constituents' financial and housing concerns.[^cite_note-35]

Hadash's [Dov Khenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dov_Khenin) was the only Jewish Knesset member representing the Joint List.[^cite_note-b303-36]

In late 2015, asked by Army Radio host [Razi Barkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razi_Barkai) if the [murder of Eitam and Na'ama Henkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Eitam_and_Na'ama_Henkin) in the West Bank is part of the "popular struggle," Odeh said that "the Palestinian people choose how to fight against the occupation. I have no doubt that the popular way is the right way and definitely not armed struggle." He said that he has rejected armed struggle in the past but said an occupied nation has a "right to struggle" for a Palestinian state based on the 1967 borders. Regarding throwing rocks, he said he supported the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada), which was "fully justified" because of the [occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Palestine): "I cannot tell the nation how to struggle, where and which target to throw the rock. I do not put red lines on the Arab Palestinian nation," he continued. Odeh also argued he did not view waving the [Palestinian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_flag) as a provocation, saying it is the national flag of the Palestinian Arab nation and "does not threaten anyone. Every nation has a flag."[^cite_note-v024-37]

In December 2016, Hadash Secretary General [Adal Amar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Amar?action=edit\&redlink=1) came under criticism for praising the "unification of [Aleppo](159244.md)" after [Assad regime](57134800.md) forces [took control of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleppo_offensive_\(November%E2%80%93December_2016\)). Some Hadash MKs voiced support for the Assad government. After the April 2017 [Khan Shaykhun chemical attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Shaykhun_chemical_attack) carried out by Assad's forces in a rebel area of Syria, Odeh made a statement condemning the attack, but Hadah vetoed a stronger List statement naming Assad.[^cite_note-b303-36][^cite_note-j867-38]

In February 2019 ahead of the [April 2019 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2019_Israeli_legislative_election), the Joint List dissolved amidst disagreements over internal party representation within the alliance. The four member parties instead ran two separate lists, [Hadash–Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash%E2%80%93Ta'al) and [Ra'am](39385583.md)–[Balad](44494596.md).[^cite_note-39]

### Re-formation in 2019

Due to low Arab [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) in the April 2019 election, the member parties once again discussed potentially re-establishing the Joint List, which officially re-formed on 20 June.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] Members hope to improve turnout and consolidate the Arab vote ahead of the [September 2019 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2019_Israeli_legislative_election).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

On 22 September after the election, the Joint List endorsed [Benny Gantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Gantz) for prime minister, the first time that an Arab party endorsed anyone for prime minister since [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin) in 1992.[^cite_note-44] However, Israeli President [Reuven Rivlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuven_Rivlin) announced on 23 September that the Joint List's three [Balad](44494596.md) [MKs](74656.md) had abstained from endorsing a candidate, thus putting Gantz behind incumbent Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) in total MK recommendations,[^cite_note-gantzbehind-45] though the Joint List's endorsement of Gantz did account for 10 of the political bloc's 13 MKs.[^cite_note-gantzbehind-45]



Joint List 2020 logo (Arabic)

The Joint List won 15 seats with 12.67% of the vote in the [2020 Knesset elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Israeli_legislative_election), remaining as the third-largest party in the Knesset until [Yesh Atid](35515608.md) split off from [Blue and White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_and_White_\(political_alliance\)) to lead the opposition. This set a new record for percentage of the vote and number of seats for an Arab party,[^cite_note-46] with it performing strongly in the [Northern District](324281.md), Arab cities and villages in the Galilee and also due to the high turnout in the Arab community.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] This was in part due to an increase in support from the Jewish left, as [Ayman Odeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_Odeh)'s campaigning in Jewish areas helped draw those voters away from the declining establishment left-wing parties.[^cite_note-Economist-49]

After the election, the Joint List unanimously supported Gantz.[^cite_note-jpost15march-50] However, after the formation of a unity government, it remained in the opposition.

The Joint List ran in the [2021 Knesset election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israeli_legislative_election) without [Ra'am](39385583.md), which had withdrawn from it several months prior to the vote; it won 4.81% of votes and six seats, a sharp decline compared to previous elections. Such decline was mainly due to the fact that Ra'am ran separately from the List and to the partial resurgence of the [Israeli Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Labor_Party) and [Meretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meretz), who increased their votes and seats.[^cite_note-51]

Former alliance member Ra'am decided to engage with Netanyahu.[^cite_note-Broke-52] Ta'al also left the alliance but later rejoined.[^cite_note-Ta'al-53] During the election campaign and after party lists had been submitted, the [Ma'an party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Democratic_Party_\(Israel\)) withdrew its candidacy and endorsed the Joint List, becoming a new member of the alliance.[^cite_note-Ma'an2-54]

Hadash's [Ofer Cassif](60517491.md) was the only Jewish MK representing the Joint List.[^cite_note-ToI_Zelensky-55]

Members of the Joint List took varying stances on the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md). Ra’am’s [Walid Taha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walid_Taha) attended a speech via Zoom by Ukrainian President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) at the Knesset, Ra’am leader [Mansour Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_Abbas) was speaking at a conference in Haifa at the same time as the speech, Ta'al's MKs watched the televised version rather than joining the Zoom call, and Hadash and Balad did not attend. Hadash's Ofer Cassif said: “Very sad that good leftists are being deceived after false propaganda — and that they even expect my friends and me to toe the line with the lies being fed to us. I do not take sides in unnecessary wars that harm innocent civilians, strengthen those in power, and enrich the lords of war.”[^cite_note-ToI_Zelensky-55]

The alliance broke up when [Balad](44494596.md) submitted a separate candidate list for the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Israeli_legislative_election). Balad had objected to Hadash's position that the alliance should support a prime ministerial candidate following the election, and wanted to rework the rotational system of seat sharing employed by the alliance.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] The Balad list received 2.9% of the vote, failing to pass the [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold).[^cite_note-58] A [Hadash–Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash%E2%80%93Ta'al) list was formed, which received 3.75% of votes and won five seats.[^cite_note-59]

### Third formation in 2026

Main article: [2026 Israeli legislative election](71244652.md)

In July 2025, amidst popular frustration over the [Gaza war](74998519.md), [Hadash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash) revealed that it was in talks with Ra'am to revive the Joint List to increase the political influence of [Palestinian citizens of Israel](492331.md). According to a poll from [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University), 73.3% of Arab respondents supported the inclusion of Arab parties into the next government coalition, while voter turnout among Arabs was expected to hit 57%.[^cite_note-60]

In August 2025, the parties reportedly were negotiating a new Joint List.[^cite_note-61] On 22 January 2026, the leaders of Hadash, Ta'al, Balad and Ra'am signed an agreement to work toward reviving the Joint List.[^cite_note-\:02-62] Ra'am leader [Mansour Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_Abbas) supported joining a technical alliance only if each party preserved its political independence after the election.[^cite_note-27Jan2-63]

On 10 June 2026, Hadash announced that it was moving forward with re-establishing the Joint List with Ta'al and Balad, while leaving open the possibility of Ra'am joining later.[^cite_note-\:12-64] According to [Hadash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadash) [secretary general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_\(title\)) [Amjad Shbita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amjad_Shbita), the other parties accommodated Ra'am's request for a "joint technical list", but Ra'am was unwilling for all parties to jointly endorse a political platform.[^cite_note-65] Ra'am indicated in an interview the next month that it would run on its own in the election.[^cite_note-66] Negotiations between the Joint List parties stalled in August after Ta'al chairman [Ahmad Tibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Tibi) rejected the proposed allocation of places on the slate.[^cite_note-67] [A Place for Us All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Place_for_Us_All) tried to join the alliance but the negotiations failed and the party withdrew from the election.[^cite_note-68]

Ra'am differs with other Arab parties on how to engage with Israel; it is willing to engage in [electoral politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_politics) with Zionist anti-Netanyahu parties to gain "concrete" concessions while the other parties view working with a Zionist government as allowing themselves to become a "safety net" for it. This divergence makes it more difficult for the parties to reunite in one list.[^cite_note-69]

On 19 August 2026, Hadash, Ta'al and Balad reached an agreement to run together in the upcoming election, re-establishing the Joint List without Ra'am.[^cite_note-toi-joint-run-2026-1] According to *[The Jerusalem Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jerusalem_Post)*, the agreement was signed after Tibi withdrew his opposition to a framework proposed by a reconciliation committee, though the deal remains subject to formal approval by the parties' institutions. The proposed list will place Jabareen first, Tibi second, Abu Shehadeh third, [Jafar Farah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jafar_Farah?action=edit\&redlink=1) fourth, Bakr Awawdeh fifth and [Ofer Cassif](60517491.md) sixth.[^cite_note-jpost-reestablished-2026-70] Farah was replaced by Faten Ghattas following the submission of the list.[^cite_note-71] Tibi is also expected to serve as chairman of the parliamentary faction.[^cite_note-maariv-reestablished-2026-72] Jabareen suggested that same month that the Joint List could provide a "parliamentary safety net" to establish an "alternative" to the Netanyahu government.[^cite_note-73]

[Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) announced on 14 September that it planned to file a disqualification petition with the [Central Elections Committee](4456077.md) against the Joint List.[^cite_note-74] The following week, [Adalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adalah_\(legal_center\)) filed a challenge in favor of the list.[^cite_note-75] The Central Elections Committee barred the list on 23 September from contesting the election, with the final decision up to the [Supreme Court of Israel](1666510.md).[^cite_note-76] The court unanimously ruled on 2 October against the disqualification of the Joint List, allowing it to run in the election.[^cite_note-77]

## Politics and ideology

The list was ideologically diverse, and included communists, socialists, feminists, Islamists, and Arab nationalists.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-TOI1-27] After having united parties with various political agendas, Odeh met with Jewish Hadash activists and former Knesset speaker [Avraham Burg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avraham_Burg) (who had endorsed Hadash), in an attempt to allay concerns that the new alliance would dilute the party's principles, such as gender equality.[^cite_note-80]

The alliance's 2015 election campaign focused on preventing [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) from forming a government and helping the [Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Labor_Party)–led [Zionist Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist_Union) do so instead.[^cite_note-TOI1-27][^cite_note-81]

The Joint List was not united in terms of support for Jewish–Arab co-operation, supported mainly by Hadash. In March 2015 (after the Zionist Union had signed a [vote-sharing agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagenbach-Bischoff_system) with [Meretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meretz), and [Kulanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulanu) with [Israel Beytenu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Beytenu)), officials from the Zionist Union, Meretz, and [Yesh Atid](35515608.md) explored the idea that the Zionist Union and Meretz revoke their agreement so that the Zionist Union could share surplus votes with Yesh Atid, and Meretz with the Joint List, to potentially strengthen the dovish bloc in the Knesset.[^cite_note-82] However, the offer caused intra-list tension; Hadash (including [Dov Khenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dov_Khenin) and Joint List chief Odeh) and Ra'am supported the partnership with Meretz, but the Islamic Movement and especially Balad opposed it.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] According to [Nahum Barnea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahum_Barnea), most of the List, including [Jamal Zahalka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Zahalka) of Balad, supported the agreement, but [Qatar](8238258.md), which reportedly funds Balad, sided with the extremist elements within Balad and had the party come out against it.[^cite_note-86] After the Joint List announced it would not share votes with any party, Meretz officials declared that the List had chosen nationalism and separatism over Jewish–Arab solidarity.[^cite_note-87] A post-election analysis showed that no agreement between these left-of-center parties would have made a difference to the final result.[^cite_note-88]
