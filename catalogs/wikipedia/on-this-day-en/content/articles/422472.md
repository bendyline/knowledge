**Langkawi**, officially known as **Langkawi, the Jewel of Kedah** ([Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Langkawi Permata Kedah*), is a [duty-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duty-free) island and an [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) of 99 islands (plus five small islands visible only at low tide in the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca)) located some 30 kilometres (19 mi) off the coast of northwestern [Malaysia](3607937.md) and a few kilometres south of [Ko Tarutao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_Tarutao), adjacent to the [Thai border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia%E2%80%93Thailand_border). Politically, it is an administrative [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_in_Malaysia) of [Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedah), with [Kuah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuah) as its largest town. Langkawi was developed as a [tourist destination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Malaysia) in the 1980s, and Pantai Cenang is the island's most popular beach and tourist area.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Etymology



Dataran Helang (Eagle Square)

The name *Langkawi* is thought to have existed by the early 15th century, although in the 16th century the island of Langkawi was also marked on maps variously as Langa, Langka, Lansura, and Langapura.[^cite_note-legends1-5]

There are many suggestions for the origin of the name of Langkawi. According to one interpretation, *Langkawi* means island of the reddish-brown [eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle), a [brahminy kite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahminy_kite) in colloquial [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language).[^cite_note-HughesMurphy2010-6] The Malay word for eagle is *helang* (colloquially shortened to *lang*), and *kawi* is a red stone used as a chalk to mark goods.[^cite_note-legends1-5] This interpretation was used to create the landmark sculpture of an eagle as the symbol of Langkawi at Dataran Helang (Eagle Square) in [Kuah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuah).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Some believed that Langkawi is the same as, or related to, the [Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanka) or Langkapuri mentioned in Indian sources such as the *[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana)*.[^cite_note-9] This ancient name Lanka (or Lankapura and Lankapuri) is found in Indian literature from an early period (named in *Ramayana* as the city of the king [Ravana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravana)), although the identification of the original Lanka is not certain.[^cite_note-10] *Puri* or *puram* in Sanskrit means a town or city. The name Langkawi is also thought to be related to [Langkasuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkasuka), an old kingdom believed to have links with Kedah.[^cite_note-legends-11] Some also thought that Langkawi means "many beautiful islands", *langka* being a Sanskrit word meaning "beautiful" while *wi* means "many".[^cite_note-legends1-5]

In 2008, the Sultan [Abdul Halim of Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Halim_of_Kedah), conferred the title of *Langkawi Permata Kedah* (meaning 'Langkawi, the Jewel of Kedah') upon the island as part of his [golden jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_jubilee) as an affirmation of Kedah's ownership over the island.[^cite_note-12]

## History

Langkawi had long been at the periphery of, but closely associated with, the domain of the [Kedah Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedah_Sultanate). Legend tells of a great snake *ular besar*, the custodian of the Langkawi Islands, to which a new king of Kedah must sacrifice a virgin daughter whenever he ascended the throne, or when war was declared with another state.[^cite_note-13]



The [Mao Kun map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Kun_map) from *[Wubei Zhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wubei_Zhi)*, derived from the 15th century navigation maps of [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He), shows Langkawi (龍牙交椅) next to [Penang Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang_Island) (檳榔嶼)

The island of Langkawi was recorded in history by various travellers to the region. It was called *Lóngyápútí* (龍牙菩提) in the 14th century by the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) traveller [Wang Dayuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Dayuan). When the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) admiral [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He) visited the region, the island was marked as 龍牙交椅, *Lóngyájiāoyǐ*, on his map. In the 15th century, it was known to the [Acehnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acehnese_people) as *Pulau Lada* ('Pepper Island'). In 1691, the French general [Augustin de Beaulieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_de_Beaulieu) recorded going to the island of "Lancahui" (Langkawi) to buy pepper,[^cite_note-legends-11] and de Beaulieu was required to obtain a license from Kedah's heir apparent in [Perlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perlis) before the *[penghulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penghulu)* or chief of Langkawi would sell pepper to him.[^cite_note-14]

Langkawi was historically home to [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples), such as the [Orang Laut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Laut), originally from the southern part of the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula) and [Malay people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_people). It had been thought to be cursed for a couple of centuries. According to local legend, in the late 18th century, a woman named [Mahsuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahsuri) was wrongfully accused of adultery and put to death. Before she died, she placed a curse on the island that would last for seven generations.[^cite_note-15] Not long after Mahsuri's death, in 1821, the Siamese army invaded Kedah and attacked Langkawi. In the first attack, the locals burned down the granary at [Padang Matsirat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padang_Matsirat) to starve the Siamese army. The Siamese nevertheless captured the island in May 1822, killed its leaders, and took many islanders as slaves, while others fled.[^cite_note-16] Before the Siamese invasion, there was an estimated island population of 3,000–5,000, but only a small proportion was left after the invasion.[^cite_note-17]

The island was recaptured from the Siamese in 1837. In 1840–1841, the Sultan of Kedah, who went into exile after the Siamese attacks, was allowed to return by the Siamese. The Langkawi islands' population recovered afterwards. However, the Orang Laut who fled after the Siamese attacks did not return.[^cite_note-18] In 1909, the islands came under British rule following the [Anglo-Siamese Treaty of 1909](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Siamese_Treaty_of_1909). The middle of the channel between [Tarutao National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarutao_National_Park) and Langkawi became the [Siamese border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia%E2%80%93Thailand_border). During [World War II](32927.md), Siam took control briefly as [British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya) fell to the Japanese.

Langkawi was a haven for pirates who attacked [junks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junks) in the northern part of the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca). In a series of operations, between December 1945 and March 1946, the British cleared the pirates' land bases on Langkawi and Tarutao.[^cite_note-19] The British continued to rule until Malaya gained its independence in 1957.

Langkawi remained a quiet backwater until 1986, when Prime Minister [Mahathir Mohamad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahathir_Mohamad) transformed it into a major tourist resort, helping to plan many of the islands' buildings himself.[^cite_note-Bound_for_Glory-20] Mahsuri's seven-generation curse was said to have been lifted after a seventh generation descendant of Mahsuri was born in the Thai [province of Phuket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuket_Province).[^cite_note-21] The island rapidly grew as a tourist destination, and by 2012, it received over three million tourists a year.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

## Geography



Map of Langkawi

Langkawi, a cluster of 99 islands separated from mainland Malaysia by the Strait of Malacca, is a district of the state of Kedah in northern Malaysia and lies approximately 51 kilometres (32 mi) west of Kedah. The total landmass of the islands is 47,848 hectares (118,230 acres). The main island is about 25 kilometres (16 mi) from north to south and slightly more from east to west. The coastal areas consist of flat, [alluvial plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial_plain) punctuated with [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) [ridges](714908.md). Two-thirds of the island is dominated by forest-covered mountains, hills, and natural vegetation.[^cite_note-24]

The island's oldest geological formation, the Machinchang Formation, was the first part of [Southeast Asia](28741.md) to rise from the [seabed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabed) in the [Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian) over half a billion years ago. The oldest part of the formation is observable at Teluk Datai to the northwest of the island, where the exposed [outcrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outcrop) consists of mainly [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) ([quartzite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartzite)) in the upper parts and [shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale) and [mudstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudstone) in the lower parts of the sequence.[^cite_note-25] The best exposure of Cambrian rocks (541 to 485 [Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mya_\(unit\))) in Malaysia is the Machinchang Formation, composed of [quartzose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartzose) [clastic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clastic_rock) formations, in Langkawi; the other known example, the Jerai Formation, emerges near the west coast of Kedah on the mainland.[^cite_note-26] Geologically, all these rocks are in the Western Belt of Peninsular Malaysia, which is thought to be part of the [Shan–Thai terrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan%E2%80%93Thai_terrane).

## Climate



[Parasailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasailing) at dawn

Langkawi receives more than 2,400 mm (94 in) of rain annually. Langkawi features a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Am*). Langkawi has a short [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season) from December until February. March to November is a long [rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainy_season). September is the wettest month, when it can receive more than 500 mm (20 in).

Only four of the 99 islands are inhabited: Langkawi (*Pulau Langkawi*, the main island), Tuba, Rebak and Dayang Bunting. The population is approximately 99,000, around 65,000 of them in Langkawi, of which 90% are [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Malays).[^cite_note-30] The other [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups) consist mainly of [Chinese](372983.md), [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-resident_Indian_and_person_of_Indian_origin), and [Thais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_people).

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Malaysia) is practised primarily by ethnic Malays. Other major religions are [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Malaysia) (mainly among [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-resident_Indian_and_person_of_Indian_origin)), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Malaysia) (mainly among Chinese and [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_people)), and [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Malaysia) (mostly Chinese).

[Standard Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_language) is the official language. [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) is widely spoken and understood by the locals. Most natives speak a Langkawi variant of [Kedah Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedah_Malay), with minorities also speaking [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) and [Siamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Thai).

## Tourism



Islands of Langkawi

On 1 June 2007, Langkawi Island was given a [World Geopark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Network_of_Geoparks) status by [UNESCO](21786641.md).[^cite_note-32] Three of its main conservation areas in Langkawi Geopark are Machincang Cambrian Geoforest Park, Kilim Karst Geoforest Park, and Dayang Bunting Marble Geoforest Park (Island of the Pregnant Maiden Lake). These three parks are the most popular tourism area within Langkawi Geopark. In 2014, UNESCO issued a "yellow card" warning that the park could lose its status as a geopark.[^cite_note-33]

### Cable car and Sky Bridge



The [Langkawi Sky Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_Sky_Bridge) above the rainforest canopy

The [Langkawi Cable Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_Cable_Car) takes visitors up to the peak of Gunung Mat Chinchang, where the [Langkawi Sky Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_Sky_Bridge) is located. The Sky Bridge was closed in 2012 for maintenance and upgrading but reopened in February 2015. An [inclined lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclined_lift) called SkyGlide that would take visitors from the top station to the Sky Bridge was completed in late 2015.[^cite_note-skyglide-34]

The start of the cable car ride is located in the Oriental Village where there are several attractions, including a 3-Dimensional art museum known as Art in Paradise.[^cite_note-35]

### Seven Wells Waterfall

The [Seven Wells Waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Wells_Waterfall) lies 1.5 kilometres (0.93 mi) north of the Oriental Village and comprises natural rock pools and a 90-metre waterfall.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### The Kilim Karst Geoforest Park (The Kilim River)

The Kilim Karst Geoforest Park is a mangrove forest park which consists of limestone caves and three interconnected river estuaries that stretch approximately 10 km from Kisap village to Tanjung Rhu.[^cite_note-38] Bats, crocodiles, eagles, kingfishers, monitor lizards, macaques, otters, snakes and tree crabs are some of the most commonly found wildlife in the park.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### MAHA Tower Langkawi

MAHA Tower is a tower in [Kuah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuah), it is the latest addition, also part of the Langkawi City's project.[^cite_note-LANGKAWI_CITY-41] The tower is approximately 1.40 km from Dataran Lang.[^cite_note-Tower-42]

### Relic of Mahsuri

Relic of Mahsuri in Wings by Croske Resort Langkawi at [Padang Matsirat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padang_Matsirat), recorded the ancient history and story of Langkawi, is part of the State Government of Kedah's Safe Langkawi project. The hand crafted featured stone wall is 200 meters from [Langkawi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_International_Airport) .

### Durian Perangin Waterfall

Durian Perangin Waterfall is a cascading waterfall located on the northeastern side of Langkawi Island, Malaysia. It is named after the durian trees that grow in the surrounding rainforest. The waterfall consists of multiple tiers, with natural pools at the base of each cascade.[^cite_note-43]

## Transportation



Boarding flight at Langkawi International Airport

The island of Langkawi can be reached by sea and air. The Langkawi Jetty Point connects the island to main destinations like [Kuala Perlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Perlis), [Kuala Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Kedah) and Tamalang. There's also ferry service to [Satun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satun) town and to [Ko Lipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_Lipe) island in Thailand.[^cite_note-44] The ferry between Langkawi and Ko Lipe operates from October until June. The departure/arrival point in Langkawi is at Kuah Jetty, Langkawi and Telaga Harbour, Langkawi. The departure/arrival point in Ko Lipe is at Pattaya Beach. As there is no pier in Ko Lipe, transfers to the beach are done with local [long-tail boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-tail_boat). The journey takes about 1 hour and 30 minutes.[^cite_note-45]

[Langkawi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkawi_International_Airport) is one of seven international [airports in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_in_Malaysia) and connects the island to [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md), [Singapore](27318.md), [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang) and [Subang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subang,_Selangor).
