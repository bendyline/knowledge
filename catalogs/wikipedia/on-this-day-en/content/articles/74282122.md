Early general elections were held in the [Netherlands](21148.md) on 29 October 2025 to elect the members of the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Netherlands\)).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The elections had been expected to be held in 2028, but a [snap election](462915.md) was called after the [Schoof cabinet](77133656.md) collapsed due to the [Party for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Freedom) (PVV) withdrawing from the coalition. The margin of votes between the first and second placed parties, [Democrats 66](172352.md) (D66) and PVV, was the smallest since 1956.[^cite_note-nos56-4]

And for the first time since the size of Parliament was last enlarged in 1956, those two leading parties, the [social liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_liberal) D66 and the [right-wing populist](8354382.md) PVV, tied in seat counts, each winning 26 seats in the House. D66 achieved their best-ever result in a general election. The parties that were part of the Schoof cabinet—the PVV, the [People's Party for Freedom and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_for_Freedom_and_Democracy) (VVD), [New Social Contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Social_Contract) (NSC), and the [Farmer–Citizen Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmer%E2%80%93Citizen_Movement) (BBB)—all lost seats, with NSC receiving a mere fraction of its last vote count and losing all 20 of its seats. The [Christian Democratic Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Appeal) (CDA) and [JA21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JA21) made significant gains, with the CDA gaining 13 seats and JA21 gaining 8.[^cite_note-5]

## Background

See also: [2023–2024 Dutch cabinet formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%932024_Dutch_cabinet_formation) and [Schoof cabinet](77133656.md)

The [2023 Dutch general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dutch_general_election) resulted in losses for all parties in the [fourth Rutte cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Rutte_cabinet). The [Party for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Freedom) (PVV) became the largest party in the House of Representatives for the first time and then [formed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%932024_Dutch_cabinet_formation) a coalition with the [People's Party for Freedom and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_for_Freedom_and_Democracy) (VVD), [New Social Contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Social_Contract) (NSC) and the [Farmer–Citizen Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmer%E2%80%93Citizen_Movement) (BBB). The [Schoof cabinet](77133656.md) with the independent [Dick Schoof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Schoof) as [prime minister](273726.md) was sworn in on 2 July 2024. On 3 June 2025, PVV left the coalition due to disagreements over asylum policy;[^cite_note-6] this led to Schoof submitting the resignation of the cabinet.[^cite_note-7]

A snap election was called for 29 October 2025;[^cite_note-8] prior to this, the next general election had been scheduled for 15 March 2028.[^cite_note-9] On 23 August 2025, NSC also left the coalition, citing disagreements over sanctions against [Israel](9282173.md) for the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-10] On 12 June 2025, the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Netherlands\)) and [GroenLinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GroenLinks) agreed to participate with one shared candidate list again and to merge their parties in 2026.[^cite_note-11]

Three current parliamentary parties had a change in their [lead candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_candidate) since the 2023 election. [Jimmy Dijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Dijk) replaced [Lilian Marijnissen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilian_Marijnissen) as [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(Netherlands\)) (SP) leader less than a month after the election,[^cite_note-12] while [Thierry Baudet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Baudet) stepped down for [Lidewij de Vos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidewij_de_Vos) to be the lead candidate of [Forum for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_for_Democracy) (FvD),[^cite_note-13] and [Eddy van Hijum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddy_van_Hijum) was chosen as lead candidate of NSC after [Pieter Omtzigt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Omtzigt) and [Nicolien van Vroonhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolien_van_Vroonhoven) both stepped down.[^cite_note-14]

## Electoral system

Main article: [Elections in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_the_Netherlands)



Voting ballot for the elections in Delft

Pursuant to articles C.1, C.2 and C.3 of the electoral law, elections for the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Netherlands\)) take place every four years in March, unless a [snap election](462915.md) is called. The 150 members of the House of Representatives are elected through [semi-open lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-open_lists) under [proportional representation](23298.md) in a single nationwide constituency. The number of seats per list is determined using the [D'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method). A list must receive a number of votes equal to or exceeding the [Hare quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare_quota) (1 full seat) in order to qualify for seat distribution, meaning there is an [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) of 0.67%.[^cite_note-15]

Voters have the option to cast a preferential vote. The seats won by a list are first allocated to the candidates who, in preferential votes, have received at least 25% of the Hare quota (effectively ¼ of a seat or 0.17% of the total votes), regardless of their placement on the electoral list. If multiple candidates from a list pass this threshold, their ordering is determined based on the number of votes received. Any remaining seats are allocated to candidates according to their position on the electoral list.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Campaign

According to polls, the housing market, immigration, and healthcare were the main topics in the months prior to the election.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] [GroenLinks–PvdA](73305628.md) (GL/PvdA), [Democrats 66](172352.md) (D66), the [Christian Democratic Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Appeal) (CDA), the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(Netherlands\)) (SP), the [Christian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Union_\(Netherlands\)) (CU), and [Volt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt_Netherlands) included the reduction of the mortgage interest deduction in their party manifestos. That was opposed by the [Party for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Freedom) (PVV), the [People's Party for Freedom and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_for_Freedom_and_Democracy) (VVD), [New Social Contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Social_Contract) (NSC), the [Farmer–Citizen Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmer%E2%80%93Citizen_Movement) (BBB), and [Forum for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_for_Democracy) (FvD).[^cite_note-41]

According to the [Bureau for Economic Policy Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_for_Economic_Policy_Analysis), GL/PvdA and BBB wanted to keep the halving of the [deductible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductible) for [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_the_Netherlands), whereas VVD, CDA, Volt, and [JA21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JA21) wanted to increase it to €440. GL-PvdA, D66, CU, and Volt wanted more [wealth tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_tax) and profit tax, in contrast to VVD, BBB, and JA21, all of which did not want that and wanted to cut back on [development aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_aid). VVD, NSC, and the [Reformed Political Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Political_Party) (SGP) did not want additional measures to reach climate goals, and BBB and JA21 wanted to emit more CO<sub>2</sub>.[^cite_note-42]



Wilders attending a protest against an asylum seeker center in [Helmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmond) in July 2025

PVV leader [Geert Wilders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geert_Wilders) attended protests against asylum seeker centers in Helmond and Zwolle, urging municipalities to defy the [Dispersal Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispersal_Act), and making these protests a central part of his election campaign.[^cite_note-43] This approach drew criticism from local politicians and the [Association of Netherlands Municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Netherlands_Municipalities), who accused Wilders of improper interference in local politics and of inciting unnecessary fear and division.[^cite_note-44] In August 2025, a discrimination hotline and Muslim organisations filed complaints against Wilders over a tweet comparing a young blonde woman with PVV and an older woman wearing a headscarf with the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Netherlands\)) (PvdA). The tweet drew a record amount of complaints to the hotline and was compared to [Nazi propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_in_Nazi_Germany).[^cite_note-45]

A ban against political advertisement on platforms of [Meta](62420226.md) started on 6 October 2025. In the month leading up to the ban, FvD and D66 spent the most money on advertisements on [Facebook](7529378.md) and [Instagram](31591547.md).[^cite_note-46] On 10 October, Wilders was named as a target of a terror cell, for which three men were arrested a day earlier. The [National Coordinator for Security and Counterterrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coordinator_for_Security_and_Counterterrorism) did not expect any remaining threat; however, citing bad feelings, Wilders suspended his campaign.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] On 15 October, he resumed his campaign.[^cite_note-49]

### Possible coalitions

Multiple parties, including GL/PvdA, VVD, and CDA, stated they would not want to form a government with PVV. Wilders stated he wanted to govern, possibly in a [minority government](271434.md).[^cite_note-50] GL/PvdA and VVD both stated they would rather not govern with each other but did not rule out working together in a government.[^cite_note-51] VVD preferred a centre-right coalition that included D66, BBB, CDA, and JA21, while D66 opposed any right-wing coalition and instead favored a [national unity government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_unity_government), involving both left-wing and right-wing parties.[^cite_note-52] JA21 did not rule out any party to govern with, although party leader [Joost Eerdmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joost_Eerdmans) stated he did not see JA21 in a cabinet with GL/PvdA or D66.[^cite_note-53]

## Opinion polls

Main article: [Opinion polling for the 2025 Dutch general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_2025_Dutch_general_election)

After rising in the polls in the first months after the [2023 Dutch general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dutch_general_election), the [Party for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Freedom) (PVV) declined from March 2024 onwards; however, it continued to lead. [GroenLinks–PvdA](73305628.md) (GL/PvdA) consistently polled in second place, between 22 and 30 seats. The [Christian Democratic Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Appeal) (CDA) rose consistently in the polls, at first at the expense of [New Social Contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Social_Contract) (NSC) and later of the [People's Party for Freedom and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_for_Freedom_and_Democracy) (VVD).[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] In the final months before the election, [JA21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JA21) also rose significantly in the polls, as did [Democrats 66](172352.md) (D66).[^cite_note-71]

## Results

[Democrats 66](172352.md) (D66), led by [Rob Jetten](58754918.md), narrowly beat [Geert Wilders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geert_Wilders)'s [Party for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Freedom) (PVV), winning 16.9% of the vote to PVV's 16.7%, and tying with PVV at 26 seats in the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Netherlands\)). D66 gained 17 seats compared to the 2023 election, while the PVV lost 11 seats. The [People's Party for Freedom and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_for_Freedom_and_Democracy) (VVD) of [Dilan Yeşilgöz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilan_Ye%C5%9Filg%C3%B6z) came third with 14.2% of the vote and 22 seats, losing 2 compared to the 2023 election, followed by the [GroenLinks–PvdA](73305628.md) (GL/PvdA) alliance, led by [Frans Timmermans](9524458.md), which obtained 12.8% of the vote and 20 seats, losing 5. The [Christian Democratic Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Appeal) (CDA) under [Henri Bontenbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bontenbal) achieved a notable resurgence, securing 11.8% of the vote and 18 seats, gaining 13 since 2023.[^cite_note-72]

Among smaller parties, JA21 rose to win 6.0% of the vote and 9 seats, an increase of 8 from the 2023 election, while the [Forum for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_for_Democracy) (FvD) gained 4.5% of the vote and 7 seats, an increase of 4. The [Farmer–Citizen Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmer%E2%80%93Citizen_Movement) (BBB) saw a sharp decline to 2.7% and 4 seats, a loss of 3.[^cite_note-73] [DENK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denk_\(political_party\)), the [Reformed Political Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Political_Party) (SGP), the [Party for the Animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_the_Animals) (PvdD), and the [Christian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Union_\(Netherlands\)) (CU) each remained at 3 seats, with the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(Netherlands\)) (SP) reduced to the same number, losing 2 seats. [50PLUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50Plus) returned to parliament with two seats, while [Volt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt_Netherlands) lost one of two seats. The [New Social Contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Social_Contract) (NSC) faced the largest defeat, losing all of their 20 seats.[^cite_note-74]

[Party for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Freedom) leader [Geert Wilders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geert_Wilders) shared messages on [X](9988187.md) that suggested [electoral fraud](382165.md) in [Maastricht](20125.md), [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden) and [Zaanstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaanstad). The three municipalities denied any wrongdoing. The posts were regarded as an unprecedented step from Wilders' side, which some political scientists compared to [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s attempt to discredit the [2020 American presidential election](48410011.md).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

## Aftermath

Main article: [2025–2026 Dutch cabinet formation](81512169.md)

The election produced a fragmented outcome, with [Democrats 66](172352.md) (D66) attaining the lowest result for the largest party in Dutch history, although it is D66's best-ever result in a general election, breaking its record of 24 seats of the [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Dutch_general_election) and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Dutch_general_election) elections. The [Party for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Freedom) (PVV) lost seats like the other parties of the [Schoof cabinet](77133656.md): the [People's Party for Freedom and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_for_Freedom_and_Democracy) (VVD), [New Social Contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Social_Contract) (NSC), and the [Farmer–Citizen Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmer%E2%80%93Citizen_Movement) (BBB). VVD achieved its joint-worst result since 22 seats in the [2006 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Dutch_general_election), although the party had been projected to perform worse in several opinion polls leading up to the election. NSC lost all of its 20 seats, the biggest loss for a Dutch party since the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Netherlands\)) (PvdA) lost 29 seats in the [2017 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Dutch_general_election). [JA21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JA21) achieved its best-ever result in general elections with 9 seats. The [Forum for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_for_Democracy) (FvD) nearly regained all of the support that the party had lost in the [2023 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dutch_general_election), winning 7 seats. The [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(Netherlands\)) (SP) got its worst results since its entry into the parliament following the [1994 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Dutch_general_election). [50Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50Plus) re-entered parliament with 2 seats, having been shut out in 2023. Following the release of the exit poll, [Frans Timmermans](9524458.md) announced that he would resign from the leadership of [GroenLinks–PvdA](73305628.md) (GL/PvdA).[^cite_note-auto-82][^cite_note-83]

[Rob Jetten](58754918.md), leader of D66, considered GL-PvdA a logical coalition partner along with VVD and the [Christian Democratic Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Appeal) (CDA).[^cite_note-84] [Geert Wilders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geert_Wilders) conceded that PVV was unlikely to be part of the next government.[^cite_note-auto-82] On 4 November, [Wouter Koolmees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wouter_Koolmees) was selected as [scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_\(Dutch_cabinet_formation\)), after being nominated by the largest party, D66.[^cite_note-85] On his advice, D66 and CDA started substantial negotiations guided by informateurs [Hans Wijers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Wijers) and [Sybrand Buma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sybrand_Buma).[^cite_note-86] Wijers subsequently stepped down after a private message of his was leaked where he criticized VVD leader [Dilan Yeşilgöz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilan_Ye%C5%9Filg%C3%B6z).[^cite_note-87]

The [2025 Speaker of the Dutch House of Representatives election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Speaker_of_the_Dutch_House_of_Representatives_election) was held on 18 November 2025, with incumbent speaker [Martin Bosma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Bosma) (PVV) seeking re-election, before being defeated by [Thom van Campen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_van_Campen) (VVD).[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]
