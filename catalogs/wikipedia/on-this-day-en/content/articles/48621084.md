**Masour Ousmane Dembélé**[^cite_note-3] (born 15 May 1997) is a French professional [footballer](10568.md) who plays as a [forward](428681.md) and [right winger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_winger) for [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) club [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) and the [France national team](250197.md). Regarded as one of the best players in the world,[^cite_note-\:6-4][^cite_note-\:7-5][^cite_note-\:8-6][^cite_note-7] he is one of eleven players to have won the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), [UEFA Champions League](44220.md), and [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d'Or) during their career.

Dembélé began his professional career at [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Rennais_FC), where he was named [Ligue 1 Young Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1_Young_Player_of_the_Year) in his one season with the first team. In 2016, he transferred to the German club [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund), winning the [DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFB-Pokal) in his debut season and scoring in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_DFB-Pokal_final). Regarded as one of the most promising young talents in world football, he joined the Spanish club [Barcelona](68187.md) in 2017 for an initial fee of €105 million, making him at the time the [joint-second most expensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_association_football_transfers) player in history, alongside [Paul Pogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pogba) and behind [Neymar](15326144.md). Although Dembélé did not fully meet the expectations placed on him at Barcelona, he won multiple domestic honours with the club, including three [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) titles, two [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_del_Rey) titles, and two [Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a) titles.

Dembélé returned to France in 2023, signing with Paris Saint-Germain for a fee of €50.4 million. In the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), he experienced a resurgence in his career, contributing 33 goals and 15 assists in 49 appearances as PSG won a [continental treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_treble). He was named the [Ligue 1 Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1_Player_of_the_Year) and the 2025 [UEFA Champions League Player of the Season](44220.md#Player_of_the_Season), as well as finishing as the [Ligue 1 top scorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ligue_1_top_scorers). His performances earned him the [2025 Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Ballon_d'Or). In the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), Dembélé was again named the Ligue 1 Player of the Year, and won another [Champions League](74127997.md) and [league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Ligue_1) title.

After winning 20 caps and scoring five goals at youth level, Dembélé made his senior international debut for France in 2016. He was a member of the France squad that won the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md), also featuring at [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md) (finishing as [runners-up](70432805.md)), [Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024), and the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md).

## Early life and career

Masour Ousmane Dembélé[^cite_note-Legion-8] was born on 15 May 1997[^cite_note-9] in [Vernon, Eure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernon,_Eure), France.[^cite_note-10] His mother is Mauritanian-Senegalese from [Waly Diantang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waly_Diantang,_Mauritania), while his father is [Malian](19127.md).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Dembélé began playing football between the ages of 12 and 13 in nearby [Évreux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89vreux), first at ALM Évreux and then at [Évreux FC 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89vreux_FC_27).[^cite_note-france3-13][^cite_note-evreux-14]

## Club career

### Rennes



Dembélé with [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Rennais_F.C.) in 2015

Dembélé made his senior debut for [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Rennais_F.C.)' reserve team in the [Championnat de France Amateur](3248285.md), on 6 September 2014, coming on as a 78th-minute substitute for [Zana Allée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zana_All%C3%A9e). He effectively set up Alseny Kourouma for the second goal of a 2–0 home win over the reserve team of Breton rivals [Guingamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_Avant_Guingamp).[^cite_note-15] On 9 November, he scored his first career goal, again coming off the bench in a game at the Stade de la Piverdière, this time against the reserves of [Laval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Lavallois).[^cite_note-16] He totalled 13 goals in 18 games in his first season, including a [hat-trick](411541.md) on 16 May 2015 in a 6–1 win over [Hérouville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_H%C3%A9rouville).[^cite_note-17]

On 6 November 2015, Dembélé made his professional debut for Rennes' first team in [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) against [Angers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angers_SCO), replacing [Kamil Grosicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamil_Grosicki) for the last five minutes of the game.[^cite_note-18] On 22 November, he scored his first Ligue 1 goal for the first team against [Bordeaux](1135285.md), opening a 2–2 draw at [Roazhon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roazhon_Park).[^cite_note-19] On 9 January 2016, Dembélé found the net again for Rennes, as they came from 0–2 down to draw 2–2 against regional rivals [Lorient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Lorient) at home.[^cite_note-20] On 6 March, he scored his first Ligue 1 [hat-trick](411541.md) in a 4–1 victory over [Nantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Nantes) in the [Derby Breton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_Breton).[^cite_note-21]

On 18 March, he scored his tenth goal of the season in a 5–2 win against [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille) at the [Stade Vélodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_V%C3%A9lodrome).[^cite_note-22] He ended his debut season with twelve goals from 29 matches, after scoring twice in a 3–1 win against [Stade Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Reims) on 2 April.[^cite_note-23]

Rennes' sporting director [Mikaël Silvestre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mika%C3%ABl_Silvestre) compared Dembélé to [Cristiano Ronaldo](623737.md), whom he had seen arrive at [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) around the same age.[^cite_note-france3-13]

### Borussia Dortmund



Dembele training with [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund) in 2017

On 12 May 2016, Dembélé signed a five-year contract with German club [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund), effective 1 July.[^cite_note-24] On 14 August, he made his debut in a 2–0 defeat against [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) in the [DFL-Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_DFL-Supercup).[^cite_note-25] He scored his first goal for Dortmund on 20 September, in a [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) encounter against [VfL Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg), which Dortmund won 5–1 at the [Volkswagen Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Arena).[^cite_note-26] On 22 November, he scored the first [Champions League](44220.md) goal of his career as the German club defeated [Legia Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legia_Warsaw) 8–4 in a group stage meeting.[^cite_note-27]

On 26 April 2017, in the [DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_DFB-Pokal) semi-final against Bayern Munich, Dembélé assisted [Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Emerick_Aubameyang)'s 69th-minute equaliser and scored the winning goal in the 74th minute, helping Dortmund reach the [2017 DFB-Pokal Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_DFB-Pokal_Final).[^cite_note-28] On 27 May, in the decisive match against [Eintracht Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eintracht_Frankfurt) at the [Berlin Olympiastadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiastadion_\(Berlin\)), he scored the first goal of a 2–1 victory as Dortmund clinched their first major title in five years. Dembélé was subsequently named [man of the match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_of_the_match).[^cite_note-29]

After scoring six goals and recording 13 assists during [2016–17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Bundesliga), Dembélé was named to the Bundesliga Team of the Season and awarded the league's Rookie of the Season award.[^cite_note-ToS-30][^cite_note-RtS-31]

### Barcelona

On 25 August 2017, [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) side [Barcelona](68187.md) announced that they had reached an agreement to sign Dembélé for €105 million plus a reported €40 million add-ons.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] On 28 August, he had his medical and signed a five-year contract, with his [buyout clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyout_clause) set at €400 million.[^cite_note-34] Barcelona had sold [Neymar](15326144.md) to [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) (PSG) for €222 million, so the deal meant that Dembélé became the [joint-second most expensive player (in euros)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_association_football_transfers), along with [Paul Pogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pogba). Rennes received a reported €20 million from Borussia Dortmund as a result of the sale,[^cite_note-35] and Évreux 27 were also due part of the fee.[^cite_note-evreux-14] He was handed the number 11 shirt previously worn by Neymar.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

#### 2017–18: First season and domestic double

Dembélé made his debut on 9 September as a 68th-minute substitute for [Gerard Deulofeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Deulofeu) in a 5–0 [Derbi barceloní](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbi_barcelon%C3%AD) win over [Espanyol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCD_Espanyol) at the [Camp Nou](222488.md), assisting the final goal by [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_\(Uruguayan_footballer\)).[^cite_note-38] In his first league start eight days later at [Getafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getafe_CF), he injured his hamstring and was ruled out for four months.[^cite_note-39] He was given the medical all-clear on 2 January 2018,[^cite_note-40] but a couple weeks later, he again injured himself against [Real Sociedad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Sociedad) and was ruled out for up to four weeks.[^cite_note-41]

On 14 March 2018, Dembélé scored his first goal for Barcelona, netting the second goal in a 3–0 second leg win in the [Champions League](44220.md) round of 16 against [Chelsea](7473.md).[^cite_note-42] On 17 April, he scored his first La Liga goal, the opening goal in an eventual 2–2 draw with [Celta Vigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_Celta_de_Vigo).[^cite_note-43] On 9 May, Dembélé scored twice, marking the first brace of his Barcelona career, in a 5–1 home victory over [Villarreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villarreal_CF).[^cite_note-44] Dembélé won both the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_del_Rey) and La Liga winners' medals in his first season in Spain, with the 20-year-old scoring four goals in 24 appearances across all competitions.[^cite_note-1718season-45]

#### 2018–19: Supercopa de España and second La Liga title



Dembélé playing for [Barcelona](68187.md) in 2018

On 12 August 2018, Dembélé scored the winning goal against [Sevilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla_FC) in the [Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a), in an eventual 2–1 victory to win Barcelona their 13th [Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a) title.[^cite_note-46] He opened his [La Liga season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_La_Liga)'s goal tally by scoring the only goal of the game against [Real Valladolid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Valladolid), on 25 August, away at the [Estadio José Zorrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Jos%C3%A9_Zorrilla).[^cite_note-47] On 18 September, Dembélé scored his first Champions League goal of [the season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Champions_League), helping Barcelona beat [PSV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSV_Eindhoven) 4–0 at the Camp Nou.[^cite_note-48] On 4 November, he inspired Barcelona to a 3–2 comeback away against [Rayo Vallecano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayo_Vallecano), scoring the 2–2 equaliser with a half-volley in the 87th minute.[^cite_note-49] On 11 December, he scored a remarkable solo goal against [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) outpacing many defenders, finishing with a cool left footed shot past [Hugo Lloris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Lloris). He won the UEFA Champions League Goal of the Week for that goal.[^cite_note-50] After the match, Barcelona manager [Ernesto Valverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Valverde) praised the youngster, saying "He has made a great goal, within reach of players with his talent."[^cite_note-51] He finished the season with 14 goals, 8 of them in the league, as his club managed to win another La Liga title.[^cite_note-52]

#### 2019–21: Recurring injuries

Dembélé injured his left hamstring in the opening match of the [2019–20 La Liga season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_La_Liga), a 0–1 defeat against [Athletic Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao), and was expected to miss five weeks.[^cite_note-53] He returned as a half-time substitute in Barcelona's 2–1 home win over Villarreal on 24 September. He scored his first goal of the season in a 4–0 win over Sevilla on 6 October, but was later sent off for dissent.[^cite_note-54] On 27 November, in a [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_UEFA_Champions_League) [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_UEFA_Champions_League_group_stage) match against his former club Borussia Dortmund, Dembélé suffered an injury which led to his substitution after 24 minutes.[^cite_note-55] It was later confirmed by Barcelona that he would miss the following ten weeks with a thigh injury.[^cite_note-56]

In February 2020, on his return to training, he suffered a serious hamstring tear which ended his season.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

Dembélé returned to the field for the first time in ten months as a 70th-minute substitute for [Ansu Fati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansu_Fati) in Barcelona's [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_La_Liga) fixture against Villarreal on 27 September 2020.[^cite_note-59] He scored his first goal in 380 days in the team's first [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Champions_League) fixture of the [season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_FC_Barcelona_season) against [Ferencváros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferencv%C3%A1ros) on 20 October.[^cite_note-60]

#### 2021–23: Hamstring surgery and La Liga assist leader

Dembélé suffered a hamstring injury when playing with France national team in the delayed [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) in 2021. His injury required surgery, and was operated on in [Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku), Finland, by surgeon Lasse Lempainen in late June 2021.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] Dembélé was also operated on by Lempainen and [Sakari Orava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakari_Orava) in 2017 and 2020.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

On 10 May 2022, Dembélé provided two assists in a 3–1 home victory over Celta Vigo. It was his 10th and 11th league assists in 2022.[^cite_note-65] He finished the [2021–22 La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_La_Liga) season as the league's top playmaker with thirteen assists.[^cite_note-66]

On 14 July 2022, Dembélé extended his contract with Barcelona until 30 June 2024.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] His release clause was set to €50 million, and later raised to €100 million on 1 August 2023.[^cite_note-69]

### Paris Saint-Germain

#### 2023–24: First season in Paris and domestic double

On 12 August 2023, Dembélé joined Ligue 1 club Paris Saint-Germain on a contract until June 2028, for a fee of €50.4 million.[^cite_note-\:0-70][^cite_note-\:1-71][^cite_note-\:2-72] He was initially assigned the number 23 jersey, but switched to the number 10 following Neymar's departure from the club.[^cite_note-\:3-73][^cite_note-\:4-74]

On 20 August, Dembélé made his PSG debut, coming on as a substitute in a 1–1 draw away to [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse_FC).[^cite_note-75] On 24 September, he got his first goal involvement for PSG, providing the assist for [Gonçalo Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gon%C3%A7alo_Ramos)'s first goal in a 4–0 [Le Classique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Classique) win over [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille).[^cite_note-76] On 24 November, Dembélé scored his first goal for the club in a 5–2 win at home over [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC).[^cite_note-77] On 10 April 2024, he scored his first Champions League goal with PSG in a 3–2 home defeat against his former club Barcelona in the quarter-final first leg.[^cite_note-78] In the second leg six days later, he scored PSG's first goal in a 4–1 away victory, helping secure his club's qualification to the semi-finals and taking home the player of the match award.[^cite_note-79] At the end of the [2023–24 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Ligue_1), Dembélé won the Ligue 1 title with PSG.[^cite_note-psg_teams-80] He also scored the opening goal in PSG's 2–1 win over [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais) in the [2024 Coupe de France final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Coupe_de_France_final).[^cite_note-81]

#### 2024–25: Continental treble win and individual accolades



Dembélé playing for [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) in 2025

On 5 January 2025, Dembélé scored a stoppage-time winner as PSG defeated [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) 1–0 in the [2024 Trophée des Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Troph%C3%A9e_des_Champions).[^cite_note-82] On 22 January, he scored Paris's first goal in a 4–2 comeback victory over [Manchester City](165813.md) in the Champions League.[^cite_note-83] On 29 January, he scored a hat-trick in a 4–1 Champions League victory over [VfB Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfB_Stuttgart), his second career hat-trick and first in European competitions, helping PSG qualify to the [knockout phase play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_phase#Knockout_phase_play-offs).[^cite_note-84] Three days later, Dembélé scored another hat-trick in a 5–2 league win away to [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Brestois_29), becoming the first PSG player to have scored consecutive hat-tricks in official matches.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] In the second leg of the Champions League round of 16 on 11 March, Dembélé scored the equalizing goal on aggregate against [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.).[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] He scored in the ensuing [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) as PSG advanced to the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] On 29 April, he scored PSG's only goal in a 1–0 away win over [Arsenal](2174.md) in the Champions League semi-finals first leg.[^cite_note-92] He assisted [Achraf Hakimi](52004386.md)'s goal in the second leg, helping PSG advance to the final with a 2–1 home victory and setting a new record for the most goal contributions by a player at a French club in a single Champions League campaign, with twelve.[^cite_note-93]

Dembélé ended the season as joint-top scorer in Ligue 1 with 21 goals, having helped PSG to their 13th league title.[^cite_note-lequipe_1-94] He was also named the [Ligue 1 Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1_Player_of_the_Year) for the first time, and was included in the Ligue 1 Team of the Year.[^cite_note-\:5-95] On 31 May 2025, Dembélé won the Champions League after PSG beat [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan) 5–0 in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Champions_League_final), a match in which he provided two assists.[^cite_note-96] After the final, his statistics for the season were of 33 goals and 15 assists in 49 appearances in all competitions,[^cite_note-97] leading to him being cited as one of the leading candidates for the [2025 Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Ballon_d'Or),[^cite_note-98] which he ultimately won.[^cite_note-\:9-99] At the [2025 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup), after missing the group stage due to injury, Dembélé scored a goal in a 2–0 win over Bayern Munich in the quarter-finals and recorded a goal and assist in a 4–0 win over [Real Madrid](26413.md) in the semi-finals. PSG went on to finish as runners-up following a 3–0 final defeat to Chelsea.[^cite_note-100]

#### 2025–26: Back-to-back Champions League titles

Dembélé won the [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup) with PSG on 13 August, scoring in a 4–3 penalty shoot-out after [a 2–2 draw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Super_Cup) against [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.). His stoppage-time assist for [Gonçalo Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gon%C3%A7alo_Ramos)'s header and his successful penalty in the shoot-out were among the contributions that earned him the Player of the Match award.[^cite_note-101] On 30 August 2025, Dembélé scored his first two goals of the season, two penalties in a 6–3 win away to [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse_FC),[^cite_note-102] but while on international duty in early September, he suffered a hamstring injury that would keep him out of action for six weeks.[^cite_note-103] That same month, he won the 2025 Ballon d'Or.[^cite_note-\:9-99]

On 22 October, Dembélé returned from injury for PSG, scoring in a 7–2 victory over [Bayer Leverkusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_04_Leverkusen) in the Champions League.[^cite_note-104] On 4 November, in a match against Bayern Munich, he suffered another injury, this time to the calf, keeping him out of action for "several weeks."[^cite_note-105] On 16 December, Dembélé was named [The Best FIFA Men's Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_FIFA_Football_Awards) of the year.[^cite_note-106] On 8 January 2026, he scored a goal in a 2–2 draw against Marseille in the [2025 Trophée des Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Troph%C3%A9e_des_Champions), an eventual victory on penalties for PSG.[^cite_note-107] On 14 April, he scored twice in a 2–0 away victory against Liverpool in the [Champions League quarter-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_phase) second leg, sealing progression to the semi-finals with a 4–0 aggregate win.[^cite_note-108]

On 11 May, Dembélé was named the [UNFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troph%C3%A9es_UNFP_du_football) [Ligue 1 Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1_Player_of_the_Year) for the second consecutive season.[^cite_note-109] He became only the fifth player in the history of the award to win in back-to-back years, joining a list that includes [Zlatan Ibrahimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatan_Ibrahimovi%C4%87) and [Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md). Despite a season interrupted by injuries, he finished the domestic campaign with 10 goals and 6 assists, playing a pivotal role in [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) securing their 12th league title in 14 years.[^cite_note-110] Later that month, on 30 May, he scored the equalising penalty in a 1–1 draw against [Arsenal](2174.md) in the [UEFA Champions League final](76987191.md), before Paris Saint-Germain prevailed 4–3 in the penalty shootout to retain the trophy.[^cite_note-111]

## International career



Dembélé after winning the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) with [France](250197.md)

Dembélé was called up to the senior [France](250197.md) squad for the first time in August 2016 to face [Italy](362466.md) and [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_national_football_team) in [friendlies](977090.md) after [Alexandre Lacazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Lacazette) and [Nabil Fekir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabil_Fekir) withdrew through injury.[^cite_note-112] He made his debut on 1 September against the former at the [Stadio San Nicola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_San_Nicola), replacing [Antoine Griezmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Griezmann) for the final 27 minutes of a 3–1 win.[^cite_note-113] On 13 June 2017, Dembélé scored his first goal for France in a 3–2 friendly victory against [England](9904.md).[^cite_note-114]

On 17 May 2018, Dembélé was named in the 23-man French squad for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia.[^cite_note-115] He featured in four matches during the tournament, including all group stage matches and appearing as a late substitute in the quarter-final against [Uruguay](679782.md).[^cite_note-116] On 15 July, he was an unused substitute as France beat [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) 4–2 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_final).[^cite_note-WC2018-117]

On 18 May 2021, Dembélé was selected in the French squad for the postponed [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020).[^cite_note-118] In the second group stage match against [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team), he sustained a rupture of the right hamstring tendon at the knee level which ruled him out for the remainder of the tournament.[^cite_note-119]

Dembélé was called up to the French squad for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar, where he played all games as France finished the tournament as runner-ups.[^cite_note-120] Dembélé started the [final](70432805.md) but conceded a penalty in the first half and was substituted in the 41st minute.[^cite_note-121]

On 18 November 2023, Dembélé scored a goal in a [14–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_v_Gibraltar_\(UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying\)) win over [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_national_football_team) during the [UEFA Euro 2024 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying), which was the largest win in the history of the France national team.[^cite_note-122] At the [final tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) in Germany, he was named [man of the match](906698.md) in France's quarter-final victory against [Portugal](576328.md).[^cite_note-123]



Dembélé with France at the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md)

On 14 May 2026, Dembélé was selected in the 26-man squad for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-124] In France's second group match on 22 June, he assisted [Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md)'s second goal and later scored his team's third in a 3–0 victory over [Iraq](1039652.md), which saw his side qualify for the knockout stages.[^cite_note-125] On 26 June, Dembélé scored a [hat-trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_hat-tricks) and was named Man of the Match in a 4–1 victory against [Norway](203277.md) in the final group stage match,[^cite_note-126] becoming the third French player to achieve this feat after [Just Fontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Fontaine) and [Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md).[^cite_note-127] In addition, he became the first player to score a first-half hat-trick since [Oleg Salenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Salenko) in 1994 and the second-fastest hat-trick from the start of the match after [Erich Probst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Probst) in 1954.[^cite_note-128] In the round of 32 on 30 June, he set-up Mbappé's first goal in a 3–0 win over [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team).[^cite_note-129] In the quarter-finals against [Morocco](1024865.md), he scored in a 2–0 victory, reaching five goals in a single World Cup edition and becoming only the third French player to achieve the feat after Just Fontaine and Kylian Mbappé.[^cite_note-130] While Dembele scored in the third-place match against [England](9904.md), the French lost the match 4–6.[^cite_note-131]

## Player profile



Dembélé playing for [France](250197.md) at the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md)

Dembélé is a [winger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winger_\(association_football\)) who can play on either flank, due to his ability to use both feet, and to utilise his technical ability, speed, and [agility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agility) in order to get past opponents or beat [defenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defender_\(association_football\)) in one-on-one situations. Dembélé can also operate as a left or right-sided [attacking midfielder](548981.md#Attacking_midfielder) in a [4–4–2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_\(association_football\)#4%E2%80%934%E2%80%932) or [3–5–2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_\(association_football\)#3%E2%80%935%E2%80%932) formation. His clinical finishing and eye for goal also allow him to be deployed in a more offensive role as a [striker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striker_\(association_football\)). Dembélé also has great quality in terms of his shooting ability from distance.

Often described as one of the best players in the world,[^cite_note-\:6-4][^cite_note-\:7-5][^cite_note-\:8-6] Dembélé has received praise from former team captain [Andrés Iniesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta) for his game-changing qualities.[^cite_note-132] He is well known for his [ability to use either foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambidexterity);[^cite_note-133] a highly skilful player, his [dribbling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dribbling#Association_football) skills and ability to perform elaborate moves allow him to cut through from the left or right wing in order to score or create goalscoring opportunities for his teammates. The Frenchman is also an excellent [crosser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_\(association_football\)) of the ball; moreover, his creativity is exceptional when in possession. Furthermore, his pace and intelligent runs make him a major offensive threat during [counterattacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterattack).

## Personal life

Dembélé is a [Muslim](19541.md)[^cite_note-134] and observes [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan), which requires him to [fast from dawn until sunset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasting_during_Ramadan) for roughly a month, including on training and match days.[^cite_note-135]

In 2021, Dembélé married a Moroccan woman in a traditional Moroccan ceremony in France.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] They have a daughter, born in September 2022.[^cite_note-139] Dembélé's fatherhood has been credited with him settling down, as he was previously one of Barcelona's most-fined players due to disciplinary issues.[^cite_note-140]

In July 2021, video footage of Dembélé and teammate [Antoine Griezmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Griezmann) circulated online, in which Dembélé makes racial comments towards Japanese technicians in his hotel room.[^cite_note-141] As the technicians appear to be troubleshooting the room's television, Dembélé says to Griezmann in French: "All these ugly faces, just so you can play *[PES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFootball)*, aren't you ashamed? ... What the fuckin language?". As the video zooms in on the technicians' faces, Dembélé laughs and comments, "Are you technologically advanced in your country or not?".[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]
