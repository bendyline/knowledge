**Ghanaians** are people originating from the [Ghanaian Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(region\)).[^cite_note-29] Ghanaians predominantly inhabit the Republic of [Ghana](12067.md) and are the predominant cultural group and residents of Ghana, numbering 34 million people as of 2024, making up 85% of the population.[^cite_note-Facts_About_Ghana_-_2014_Ghana_pop-28][^cite_note-A_Journey_Through_Islam-30] The word "Ghana" means "warrior king".[^cite_note-31] An estimated [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora) population of 4 million people worldwide are of Ghanaian descent.[^cite_note-Jamaica_National_launches_new_Ghana_money_transfer_brand-32] The term ethnic Ghanaian may also be used in some contexts to refer to a group of related ethnic groups native to the Gold Coast.[^cite_note-33]

The [ethnogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnogenesis) of Ghanaians is traced back to [nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) migration from [Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia) along the [Sahara](325363.md) desert then south to the Gold Coast, and the Ghanaian ethnogenesis taking place on the Ghanaian [Gold Coast region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(region\)) from the 10th to 16th century AD.[^cite_note-34] Early Ghanaians were involved in a lucrative trade with gold bars and other natural minerals to the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) in 1471; these Ghanaian states were among the wealthiest on the African continent from the 17th century onwards, following successful further expansion of lucrative Ghanaian gold bars trading to the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) and [Scandinavians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) from the 16th century through to the 20th century.[^cite_note-Chronology_of_world_history-35]

Early Ghanaians established a number of powerful kingdoms and empires from the 10th to 17th century, some of which became great powers in the west African region.[^cite_note-Chronology_of_world_history-35] By 1902, the kingdoms had been annexed by the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) to the [Gold Coast colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)) following a series of Anglo-Ghanaian conflicts in the 19th century.[^cite_note-Chronology_of_world_history-35] Ghanaians gained their independence from [British colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonial) rule in 1957, and renamed their [sovereign state](23604120.md) "Ghana (Warrior King)" because the various Ghanaian kingdoms were a warrior-based society according to contemporary and historical historiography.[^cite_note-etymology-36] The Republic of Ghana was the [first African country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_and_dependent_territories_in_Africa) to gain independence from European colonial rule.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

## Demographics and genetics

Main article: [Demographics of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Ghana)

More than 90% of the Ghanaian citizens in Ghana live in [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area)—a figure higher than the world average. The rate of Ghana's population growth is at the world average.[^cite_note-Ghana_–_2010_Population_and_Housing_Census-26] Most Ghanaians move to urban areas seeking well-paid jobs. Ghanaians have high level of education in science, [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology), mathematics and vocational studies. However, the rural areas have large productivity in agricultural produce. Ethnic groups in Ghana include the [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people) (47.3%), the [Mole-Dagbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mole-Dagbon_people) (18.5%), the [Ewe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewe_people) (13.9%), the [Ga-Dangme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga-Dangme_people) (7.4%), the [Gurma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurma_people) (5.7%) and the [Guan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guang_people) (3.7%).

According to a 2005 [Y-DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-DNA) study, indigenous Ghanaians in Ghana carry 61% [E1b1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E1b1a_\(Y-DNA\)).[^cite_note-Wood_2005-39][^cite_note-40] Indigenous Ghanaians also belong to [paternal lineages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrilineality) at 2.2% [E1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E1a_\(Y-DNA\)).[^cite_note-Wood_2005-39] Indigenous Ghanaians in Ghana are 1.1% [E1b1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E1b1b_\(Y-DNA\)) [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade) bearers, a [haplogroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup) that is most common in [North Africa](21714.md) and the [Horn of Africa](62142.md). [West Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) haplogroup [R1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1b) is present in 1.1% of the population.[^cite_note-Wood_2005-39]

## National identity and citizenship in Ghana

The inhabitants of Ghana possessing [Ghanaian passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_passport) are 20 million persons, including an additional 3‒4 million persons abroad. Ghana has a diverse population that reflects its colorful history and the peoples who have populated the region from ancient times to the present, with the historic amalgam of the main groups forming the basis of Ghana's current demographics. [Native West Africans](67393.md) make up 98% of the population.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] There is also a new population of [Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people), [Middle Easterners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_diaspora), [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Africans_of_European_ancestry) and other recent [immigrants](4599312.md).[^cite_note-44]

To obtain [Ghanaian nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_nationality_law), one must be [naturalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalization) after seven years of [Ghana Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Card) [permanent residency](1284324.md). The [Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people), Middle Easterners and [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europeans) who have lived in Ghana for most of their lives have acquired Ghanaian citizenship, which is granted without any discrimination. Two thirds, or 67%, of Ghanaians speak English.[^cite_note-Ghana_–_2010_Population_and_Housing_Census-26][^cite_note-45] There are more than 100 ethnic groups, each with its own distinct language.[^cite_note-46] However, languages that belong to the same ethnic group are usually mutually intelligible. There are nine [language family groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Ghana) and 11 languages from these groups are officially sponsored by the government: [Akuapem Twi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akuapem_dialect), [Asante Twi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asante_dialect), [Ewe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewe_language), [Fante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fante_dialect), [Ga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga_language), [Dangme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangme_language), [Dagbani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagbani_language), [Nzema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nzema_language), Ahanta language (Ahantas) [Dagaare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagaare_language), [Gonja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonja_language) and [Kasena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasena_language).[^cite_note-RG-47]

During the colonial era, a number of Europeans intermarried with Africans and had offspring, who include such notable Gold Coasters as [Carel Hendrik Bartels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carel_Hendrik_Bartels) and [James Bannerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bannerman). Most European settlers left the Gold Coast after it won independence. Currently, the most significant immigrant populations in Ghana are Africans from other countries on the continent, Asians ([Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_the_New_York_City_metropolitan_area) and [Chinese](372983.md)), some of them Europeans (Britons, [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), French, [Italians](385155.md), Latin Americans, Poles, [Scandinavians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), and [Germans](152735.md)), and Middle Easterners, particularly Lebanese and Syrians.

## Nationalism, independence and transformation to republic

See also: [Ghanaian Nationalism in Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aborigines'_Rights_Protection_Society), [Ghanaian Aborigines' Rights Protection Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Ghana), and [Ghana](12067.md)

[Universal Newsreel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Newsreel) about the independence of Ghana in 1957.

The Ghanaian [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) was suspended by the [Ghanaian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Ghana) during the time of [World War II](32927.md), but was resumed in 1945.[^cite_note-IN1-48] The Ghanaians allied with the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) in World War II.[^cite_note-IN1-48] The [Fifth Pan-African Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-African_Congress#5th_Pan-African_Congress:_Manchester_\(1945\)) held in October 1945, served to form the support for the [liberalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalization) of Ghanaian colonial domination on 4 August 1947.[^cite_note-IN1-48][^cite_note-IN2-49] On 12 June 1949, [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah), formed the first governing party in the history of the Gold Coast, which refused to cooperate with the colonial authorities and which led to the achievement of Ghanaian independence and the opposition to the [1951 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ghana), in which Nkrumah was incarcerated together with his collaborators.[^cite_note-IN2-49]

On 8 February 1951, the first elections in the history of the Gold Coast were held; Nkrumah's win was confirmed on 12 February 1951.[^cite_note-IN2-49] Ghanaian nationalism was initiated in organisation with the Ghanaian [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) movement, the [Big Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Six_\(Ghana\)) and through the [Gold Coast Aborigines' Rights Protection Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Aborigines'_Rights_Protection_Society); then strikes and mass riots were formed on the streets of the Gold Coast by its natives for Gold Coast independence, the colonial governor at the time, the [Earl of Listowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hare,_5th_Earl_of_Listowel), proclaimed Gold Coast's independence on 6 March 1957.[^cite_note-50] Nkrumah became the first [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Ghana).[^cite_note-PID-51] On 1 July 1960, Nkrumah drew up the first [Constitution of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ghana); the British monarch ceased to be [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heads_of_state_of_Ghana), and Ghana became a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic).[^cite_note-PID-51]

## Subgroups

### Ghanaian Arabs

Main article: [Ghanaian Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_Arabs)

Ghanaian Arabs are Ghanaians and [citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_nationality_law) of [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) origin or descent. Ghanaian Arabs are mainly from [Lebanon](17771.md), [Syria](7515849.md) and [Arab Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb). Ghana has the largest Arab population in western Africa.

### Ghanaian Indians

Main article: [Ghanaian Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_Indian)

Ghanaian Indians are Ghanaians and citizens of [Indian](7564733.md) origin or descent. Many Ghanaian Indians are descendants of those who migrated from [India](14533.md) following [India's partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947.[^cite_note-Ghana's_unique_African-Hindu-54]

### Ghanaian Surinamese and Guyanese

Main articles: [Ndyuka people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndyuka_people), [Afro-Surinamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Surinamese), and [Afro-Guyanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Guyanese)

Ndyuka (also spelled "Djuka") or Aukan or Okanisi sama, are a Ghanaian [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people) subgroup who live in Eastern [Suriname](26828.md) and west of [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) and speak the [Ndyuka language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndyuka_language), a sub-language of the [Akan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_language). They were shipped as imported labourers slaves from the [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(region\)) (modern-day Ghana) to Suriname about 300 years ago to work on Dutch-owned plantations. Ndyukas or Aukans are subdivided into the *Opu*, who live upstream of the [Tapanahony River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapanahony_River) of southeastern Suriname, and the *Bilo*, who live downstream of that river. They further subdivide themselves into 14 [matrilinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilinear) kinship groups called *lo*.

## Culture

See also: [Social conduct in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conduct_in_Ghana), [Culture of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Ghana), [Ghanaian name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_name), [Ghana's material cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana's_material_cultural_heritage), and [Ghanaian museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Ghana)

Ghana's cultural diversity is most evident in cuisine, arts, literature, heritage, music, dance, clothing, and sports.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_African_art-56][^cite_note-57]

[Kente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kente) is a Ghanaian ceremonial cloth traditionally used as the national costume. Kente is hand-woven on a horizontal [treadle loom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loom) in strips measuring about 4 inches wide, which are sewn together into larger pieces of cloth. Cloths come in various colours, sizes and designs, which have different meanings, and are worn on important social occasions.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_African_art-56] During the 13th century, Ghanaians developed their unique art of *[adinkra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adinkra_symbols)* printing.

Notable Ghanaian authors include novelists [Ayi Kwei Armah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayi_Kwei_Armah) (*[The Beautyful Ones Are Not Yet Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beautyful_Ones_Are_Not_Yet_Born)*), [Ama Ata Aidoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ama_Ata_Aidoo) (*[Our Sister Killjoy: or Reflections from a Black-eyed Squint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Sister_Killjoy:_or_Reflections_from_a_Black-eyed_Squint)*) and [J. E. Casely Hayford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._E._Casely_Hayford), author of *[Osiris Rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osiris_Rising)*. In addition to novels, other literary genres such as theatre and poetry have been well developed at a national level.

[Ghanaian music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Ghana) incorporates several distinct types of instruments, including [talking drums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_drum), the [atenteben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atenteben) and koloko lute, the atumpan, and log [xylophones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylophone) used in asonko music. The most well-known genre to come from Ghana is [highlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlife).[^cite_note-58] Highlife originated in the late 19th century and early 20th century. In the 1990s, a new genre of music, [hiplife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiplife), was created through the combination of highlife, Afro-reggae, [dancehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancehall) and [hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop_music).[^cite_note-Female_Song_Tradition-59] Hiplife is the most popular Ghanaian music,[^cite_note-60] followed by the other genre of Ghanaian music, [highlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlife).[^cite_note-61] Ghanaian dance is globally well known and performed worldwide.[^cite_note-Female_Song_Tradition-59] The dances are varied and may involve complex and co-ordinated movement of the arms, torso, hips, feet and head, performed to different Ghanaian music forms for entertainment, celebrating at festivals, and other occasions. Some popular dances include Adowa and [Azonto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azonto).[^cite_note-Dance_-_Ghana-62] Other traditional dances from Ghana are [Kpanlogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kpanlogo), Klama and Bamaya.[^cite_note-Dance_-_Ghana-62]

[Sports in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Ghana) is dominated by association football represented by the [Ghana Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Premier_League) and the [Ghana national football team](1029620.md).[^cite_note-63] The rich culture in Ghana led to the annual festival held at the capital region, Greater Accra at the James Town township which is celebrated along with the Homowo festival. This new festival called [Chale Wote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chale_Wote_Street_Art_Festival)[^cite_note-\:1-64] has caught the eyes of many who seek to experience the true Ghanaian culture and festival for themselves.

## Women

Further information: [Women's Manifesto for Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Manifesto_for_Ghana), [Women in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Ghana), and [Education in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Ghana)



Young Ghanaian women in [kente clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kente_cloth) at a cultural ceremony

In Ghanaian society [polygyny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygyny) – marriages in which men are permitted to have more than one wife at the same time[^cite_note-cs-65] – has been traditionally practised, especially among well-to-do Ghanaian men.[^cite_note-cs-65] Among [matrilineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilineal) groups, such as the [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people), married women continued to reside at their maternal homes.[^cite_note-cs-65] Meals prepared by the wife would be carried to the husband at his maternal house.[^cite_note-cs-65] In polygynous situations, visitation schedules would be arranged.[^cite_note-cs-65] The separate living patterns reinforced the idea that each spouse is subject to the authority of a different household head, and because spouses are always members of different lineages, each is ultimately subject to the authority of the senior men of his or her lineage.[^cite_note-cs-65] The wife, as an outsider in the husband's family, would not inherit any of his property, other than that granted to her by her husband as gifts in token appreciation of years of devotion.[^cite_note-cs-65] The children from this matrilineal marriage would be expected to inherit from their mother's family.[^cite_note-cs-65] Today, the percentage of women in polygynous marriages in Ghana's rural areas (23.9%) is almost double that of women in Ghana's urban areas (12.4%).[^cite_note-MICS-66] The age group with the most women in polygynous marriages is 45–49, followed by the 15–19 age group and the 40–44 group.[^cite_note-MICS-66] Rates of polygynous marriages decrease as education level and wealth level increase.[^cite_note-MICS-66]

During 2008–2012, the national literacy rate for women aged 15–24 was 83.2%, only slightly lower than that for males of the same age group (88.3%).[^cite_note-UNICEF_Stats-67] However, literacy rates fluctuate across Ghana country and socioeconomic statuses.[^cite_note-MICS-66][^cite_note-UNICEF_Stats-67] By [regions of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Ghana), literacy rates for females range from 44% to 81%.[^cite_note-MICS-66] Women living at the highest socioeconomic status exhibit the highest literacy rates at 85%, while only 31% of women living at the lowest socioeconomic status are literate.[^cite_note-MICS-66] Over the timespan of 2008–12, there were 4% more females enrolled in [preschool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool) than males.[^cite_note-UNICEF_Stats-67] Net enrolment and attendance ratios for [primary school](25058.md) were both about the same for males and females, net enrollment standing at about 84% and net attendance at about 73%.[^cite_note-UNICEF_Stats-67] Enrolment in [secondary school](554992.md) for females was slightly lower than for males (44.4% vs. 48.1%), but female attendance was higher by about the same difference (39.7% vs. 43.6%).[^cite_note-UNICEF_Stats-67]

As of 2011, women made up 66.9% of economically active population in Ghana.[^cite_note-equality_and_poverty-68] Within the informal sector, women usually work in personal services.[^cite_note-equality_and_poverty-68] There are distinct differences in artisan apprenticeships offered to women and men, as well.[^cite_note-equality_and_poverty-68] Men are offered a much wider range of apprenticeships, such as carpenters, masons, blacksmiths, mechanics, painters, repairers of electrical and [electronic appliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_engineering), upholsters, metal workers, car sprayers, etc.[^cite_note-equality_and_poverty-68] In contrast, most female artisans are involved in either hairdressing or dressmaking.[^cite_note-equality_and_poverty-68] Women generally experience a disparity in earnings, receiving a daily average of 6,280 cedis compared to 8,560 cedis received by men, according to the Ghana Living Standards Survey.[^cite_note-equality_and_poverty-68] Women are flourishing in teaching professions.[^cite_note-cs-65]



[Ghana Armed Forces](12075.md) (GAF) Military Female [Sergeant](219424.md) at a GAF military exercise, 2013 in Ghana.

Early 1990s' data showed that about 19% of the instructional staff at the nation's three universities in 1990 was female.[^cite_note-cs-65] Of the teaching staff in specialized and diploma-granting institutions, 20% was female; elsewhere, corresponding figures were 21% at secondary-school level; 23% at middle-school level, and as high as 42% at primary-school level.[^cite_note-cs-65] Women also dominated the secretarial and nursing professions in Ghana.[^cite_note-cs-65] When women were employed in the same line of work as men, they were paid equal wages, and they were granted maternity leave with pay.[^cite_note-cs-65] However, women in [research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research) professions report experiencing more difficulties than men in the same field, which can be linked to restricted professional networks for women because of lingering traditional familial roles.[^cite_note-gender_and_science-69]

Feminist organizing has increased in Ghana as women seek to obtain a stronger role in the nation's democratic government.[^cite_note-Interview-70] In 2004, a coalition of women created the [Women's Manifesto for Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Manifesto_for_Ghana), a document that demands economic and political equality as well as reproductive health care and other rights.[^cite_note-Interview-70] The National Council for Women and Development (NCWD) is fervent in its stance that the social and economic well-being of women, who compose slightly more than half of the nation's population, cannot be taken for granted.[^cite_note-cs-65] The Council sponsored a number of studies on women's work, education, and training, and on family issues that are relevant in the design and execution of policies for the improvement of the condition of women.[^cite_note-cs-65] Among these considerations the NCWD stressed family planning, child care, and female education as paramount.[^cite_note-cs-65][^cite_note-Mainstreaming-71]

In 2007, the government of Ghana took legal proceedings to prosecute men who abuse their women.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

## Republic of Ghana (1957–present)



President of the Republic of Ghana and Commander-in-Chief of the Ghana Armed Forces: [Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah), [Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Rawlings), [Kufuor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kufuor), [Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Evans_Atta_Mills) and [Mahama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dramani_Mahama).

In 1966, Nkrumah was deposed, after which Ghana entered a period of military rule. On 31 December 1981, the regime led by [Flight lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_lieutenant) [Jerry John Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Rawlings) installed the [Provisional National Defence Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_National_Defence_Council) (PNDC), of which he became chairman. In 1992, Rawlings retired from the military and set up the [National Democratic Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Congress_\(Ghana\)) (NDC), and was subsequently elected for two terms as president.

On January 7, 2001, [John Agyekum Kufuor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kufuor) succeeded Rawlings as [Ghanaian head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ghana) until the year 2008. Kufuor was replaced as Ghanaian head of state by [John Atta Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Atta_Mills) until his death in July 2012.[^cite_note-swear_in_John_Mahama_as_president-80] In July, 2012, [John Dramani Mahama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dramani_Mahama) succeeded Mills as the Republic of Ghana President and Commander-in-Chief of the Ghana Armed Forces.[^cite_note-swear_in_John_Mahama_as_president-80]

On 7 January 2017, [Nana Akufo-Addo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nana_Akufo-Addo) took office as President.[^cite_note-81] On 7 January 2025, John Dramani Mahama took office once again as President of the Republic of Ghana.
