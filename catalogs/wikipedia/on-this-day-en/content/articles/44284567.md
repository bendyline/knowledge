**Jamaat-ul-Ahrar** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جماعة الأحرار, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Jamā‘at ul-Āḥrār*, "Assembly of the Free," abbreviated as **JuA**) is an [al-Qaeda](1921.md)-linked[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-afp4sep-11] splinter faction of the [Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan](18953699.md) (TTP, or Pakistani Taliban) that was formed in August 2014.[^cite_note-afp4sep-11][^cite_note-\:1-12] [Omar Khalid Khorasani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Khalid_Khorasani), the JuA's founding leader, had close links to al-Qaeda and its leader [Ayman al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahiri).[^cite_note-\:2-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-\:1-12] The organization is internationally designated as a terrorist group.[^cite_note-15]

The JuA came to prominence after it claimed responsibility for the [2014 Wagah border suicide attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Wagah_border_suicide_attack). Relations between JuA and the TTP have long been contentious and marked by recurring rifts.[^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-16] In August 2014, Omar Khalid Khorasani split from the Pakistani Taliban and announced the formation of Jamaat ul-Ahrar.[^cite_note-\:2-13][^cite_note-\:1-12] In September 2014, JuA issued a statement denouncing the Pakistani Taliban leadership and declared its support to the [Islamic State](9087364.md) militant group.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-afp4sep-11]

JuA initiated a reconciliation process with the TTP in 2015 and remerged with the Pakistani Taliban in August 2020.[^cite_note-rejoin-4][^cite_note-\:4-19] Starting from 2023, amid growing rifts with the TTP's Central Shura Council and leadership disputes with Mufti [Noor Wali Mehsud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_Wali_Mehsud), the group has carried out attacks independently and has resumed operating separately from the [Pakistani Taliban](18953699.md) since 2025.[^cite_note-\:5-20][^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-\:6-21] In May 2026, intense clashes between the TTP and JuA erupted in the [Kurram](5053207.md) region, in which 19 JuA fighters and 3 TTP members were killed.[^cite_note-\:7-22][^cite_note-\:8-23]

## History

### Roots and development

In September 2014, Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan chief Fazlullah ousted Mohmand Agency chief Omar Khalid Khorasani (former leader of [Ahrar-ul-Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar-ul-Hind)). Omar Khalid Khorasani and his associates in Mohmand Agency had accused the TTP leadership of deviating from the TTP ideology, leading to the formation of splinter group TTP Jamaat-ul-Ahrar.

The [Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrik-i-Taliban_Pakistan) was effectively divided into two factions. The original TTP is headed by [Maulana Fazlullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulana_Fazlullah), who was elected in November 2013 following the killing of ex-chief [Hakimullah Mehsud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakimullah_Mehsud) in a U.S. drone strike. In February 2014, [Ahrar-ul-Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar-ul-Hind), headed by Umar Qasmi (former leader in the [Lashkar-e-Jhangvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashkar-e-Jhangvi)) was formed after TTP opened peace talks with the Pakistani government. It later merged into Jamaat-ul-Ahrar, a second splinter group that broke away from Tehreek-i-Taliban in Pakistan on 4 September 2014, and named Omar Khalid Khorasani as its commander.[^cite_note-24]

### Relations with TTP

The group had announced they would no longer recognize or obey Mullah Fazlullah as their Emir.[^cite_note-dawn.com-25] In March 2015, the group's spokesman announced that it was considering to swear loyalty to main TTP leadership again.[^cite_note-26]

JuA initiated a reconciliation process with the TTP in 2015 and remerged with the Pakistani Taliban in August 2020.[^cite_note-rejoin-4][^cite_note-\:4-19] Starting from 2023, amid growing rifts with the TTP's Central Shura Council and leadership disputes with Mufti [Noor Wali Mehsud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_Wali_Mehsud), the group has carried out attacks independently and has resumed operating separately from the [Pakistani Taliban](18953699.md) since 2025.[^cite_note-\:5-20][^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-\:6-21] In May 2026, intense clashes between the TTP and JuA erupted in the [Kurram](5053207.md) region, in which 19 JuA fighters and 3 TTP members were killed.[^cite_note-\:7-22][^cite_note-\:8-23]

## Designation as terrorist organization

On 6 July 2017, the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council)'s [1267 Sanctions Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1267_Sanctions_Committee) approved the addition of Jamaat-ul-Ahrar in the list of entities and individuals subject to the assets freeze, travel ban and arms embargo. Pakistan had proposed this listing. It was designated as a [Global Terrorist Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specially_Designated_Global_Terrorist) under the [SDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specially_Designated_Nationals_and_Blocked_Persons_List) by the [United States Department of the Treasury](53667.md)'s [Office of Foreign Assets Control](231045.md), with addresses in [Mohmand District](4557955.md), [Bajaur District](4461891.md), [Khyber District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_District), [Orakzai District](3578580.md), [Charsadda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charsadda), [Peshawar](66230.md), [Swat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swat,_Pakistan), [Punjab Province, Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Province,_Pakistan).[^cite_note-27]

## Split

Jamaat-ul-Ahrar was split into two groups, with a video statement on 12 November 2017 announcing that the Hizbul Ahrar group, formed in [Nangarhar province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangarhar_province) of Afghanistan on 11 November, will be headed by militant commander Mukarram Khan. Khan had previously served as an important commander and spokesman of JuA. The statement said that Khan left JuA because of differences with its chief over "attacks against minority [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_Christians), killing civilians, extortion, kidnapping for ransom and other acts he deemed un-Islamic".[^cite_note-50] Since then, Hizbul Ahrar has claimed numerous attacks against police officers and army men, the modus operandi of the group is mainly targeted assassinations and IED blasts.[^cite_note-51]
