The **premier of Quebec** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *premier ministre du Québec* [\[pʁəmje ministʁ dy kebɛk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) (masculine) or *première ministre du Québec* [\[pʁəmjɛʁ-\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) \[feminine]) is the [first minister](183220.md) and [head of government](37570.md) of the [Canadian province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Canada) of [Quebec](7954867.md). The current [premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier) of Quebec is [Christine Fréchette](71918639.md), who took office on April 15, 2026.

## Selection and qualifications

The premier of Quebec is appointed as president of the [Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_Quebec) by the [lieutenant governor of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_of_Quebec), the viceregal representative of the [King in Right of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_in_Quebec). The premier is most usually the head of the party winning the most seats in the [National Assembly of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Quebec) and is normally a sitting member of the National Assembly. An exception to this rule occurs when the winning party's leader fails to win a riding. In that case, the premier would have to attain a seat by winning a by-election. This has happened, for example, to [Robert Bourassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bourassa) in 1985.

The role of the premier of Quebec is to set the legislative priorities on the opening speech of the National Assembly. The premier represents the leading party and must have the confidence of the assembly, as expressed by votes on budgets and other matters considered as confidence votes.

The term "premier" is used in English, while French employs "premier ministre", which translates directly to "prime minister". In at least one instance, the term "prime minister of the Province of Quebec" was used in an English-language advertisement.[^cite_note-3] The term is also used for the Podium Ceremony of the annual Formula One Grand Prix du Canada in Montreal.
