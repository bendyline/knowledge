# Multicab

A **multi cab** is a small [light truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_truck) or [kei truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_truck) in the [Philippines](23440.md) that is usually used as [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport). Like [jeepneys](884965.md), they usually have fixed routes, although there are multicabs that serve as [taxicabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab) to take passengers where they want, as a [motorized tricycle](29843385.md) might.[^cite_note-1] Aside from being a mode of mass transportation, it can also be customized for other purposes such as a pickup truck or a private van.[^cite_note-everythingcebu-2]

Multicabs can be found throughout the Philippines. It can be found in urban areas such as [Metro Manila](72340.md),[^cite_note-3] [Metro Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Cebu),[^cite_note-everythingcebu-2][^cite_note-4] and [Metro Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Davao).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] A multicab is lightweight, narrow and small and can navigate through narrow streets. With seating capacity of around 11 to 13,[^cite_note-8] the passenger space also tends to be cramped relative to a van. There are also multicabs that have a seating capacity less than 11.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Small multicabs typically have [three-cylinder engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-cylinder_engine).[^cite_note-11] In [Tacloban](746499.md), there are plans to convert the multicab engines into rechargeable batteries for [sustainable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy).[^cite_note-12]

A multicab is about as comfortable as a jeepney, and usually has a cleaner and less noisy engine.[^cite_note-silentgarden-13] A group travelling by multicab may hire a multicab for a day (e.g., for family use) and pay the daily fee, while optionally buying food and drink for the driver.[^cite_note-silentgarden-13] A multicab is typically assembled in a factory in the Philippines with surplus parts from [Japan](15573.md) and [South Korea](27019.md),[^cite_note-everythingcebu-2] in contrast to jeepneys, which are usually hand-made. Popular makes are [Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki) models such as the [Suzuki Carry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki_Carry), or more its rugged cousin, the [Autozam/Mazda Scrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autozam_Scrum) (locally known as "Suzuki" Scrum, due to interchangeability of parts between these two vehicles). Multicab models sometimes also using [Daihatsu Hijet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daihatsu_Hijet) and [Mitsubishi Minicab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Minicab).&#x20;

As part of the [Public Utility Vehicle Modernization Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Utility_Vehicle_Modernization_Program), multicabs operating as public transport must be built according to the Class 1 standard. Such microbuses are often airconditioned, feature a side door (both automatic and manually opened), a much taller ceiling (150 centimeters), and can typically seat 9 to 13 passengers.
