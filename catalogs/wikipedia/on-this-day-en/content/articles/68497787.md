The **supreme leader of Afghanistan**[^cite_note-4] ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): د افغانستان مشر, [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Pashto): *Də Afġānistān Məshar*, [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): رهبر افغانستان, romanised: *Rahbar-i Afghānistān*), officially the **supreme leader of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan**[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-11] and also referred to by his religious title ***Amir al-Mu'minin*** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), lit. '**Commander of the Faithful**'), is the [absolute ruler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_ruler), [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), and national religious leader of [Afghanistan](737.md), as well as the leader of the [Taliban](30635.md).[^cite_note-supreme_leader-12][^cite_note-head_of_state-13][^cite_note-VoA_March_2023-14] The supreme leader wields unlimited authority and is the ultimate source of all law.[^cite_note-VoA_March_2023-14][^cite_note-UNSC_May_2022-15][^cite_note-16]

The first supreme leader, [Mullah Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah_Omar), ruled Afghanistan from 1996 to 2001 before his government was overthrown by the United States and he was forced into exile. The current supreme leader is [Hibatullah Akhundzada](50633776.md), who assumed office in exile during the [Taliban insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgency) on 25 May 2016, upon being chosen by the [Leadership Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_Council_of_Afghanistan), and came to power on 15 August 2021 with the Taliban's [victory over Afghan government forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive) in 2021. Since coming to power, Akhundzada has issued [numerous decrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_decrees_by_Hibatullah_Akhundzada) that have profoundly reshaped government and daily life in Afghanistan by implementing his strict interpretation of the [Hanafi school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi_school) of [Sharia law](28840.md).

The supreme leader appoints and manages the activities of the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Afghanistan) and other members of the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Afghanistan), as well as judges and [provincial](412343.md) and local leaders.[^cite_note-VoA_March_2023-14]

## History

The office was established by [Mullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah) [Mohammed Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah_Omar), who founded both the [Taliban](30635.md) and the [original Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)) in the 1990s. On 4 April 1996, in [Kandahar](17260.md), followers of Omar bestowed upon him the title [Amir al-Mu'minin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_al-Mu'minin) (أمير المؤمنين), meaning "Commander of the Faithful", as Omar had held [a cloak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloak_of_Muhammad) taken from [its shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirka_Sharif) in the city, asserted to be that of the [Islamic prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophets_and_messengers_in_Islam) [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_in_Islam).[^cite_note-NYT_Omar-17][^cite_note-18] The Taliban [seized control of Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1992%E2%80%931996\)#Taliban_take-over) on 27 September 1996, ousting President [Burhanuddin Rabbani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burhanuddin_Rabbani) and installing Omar as the country's head of state.[^cite_note-19]

The Taliban views the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) as its constitution. However, it approved a *dastur*, a document akin to a [basic law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_law), in 1998, which proclaimed Omar supreme leader but did not outline a succession process. In 1996 interview, [Wakil Ahmed Muttawakil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakil_Ahmed_Muttawakil) stated that the Amir al-Mu'minin is "only for Afghanistan", rather than a [caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph) claiming leadership of all [Muslims worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummah).[^cite_note-NYU-20][^cite_note-MEI-21]

Following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) and the [United States invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Afghanistan) in 2001, Omar was deposed and went into hiding in [Zabul Province](362239.md), and the [presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Afghanistan) was restored as Afghanistan's head of state. The Taliban reorganised for [an insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgency) in 2002, based out of Pakistan. They continued to claim Omar as their supreme leader, though he had little involvement in the insurgency, having turned over operational control to his deputies.[^cite_note-Bette_Dam-22] Though the Taliban continued to maintain the office of the supreme leader in exile, it had no diplomatic recognition.

Following its [offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive) in 2021, the Taliban [recaptured Kabul](68481047.md) on 15 August and restored the supreme leader as Afghanistan's head of state.[^cite_note-guardian15-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-head_of_state-13]

## Powers and duties

Under Omar, the leader held absolute power, and the Taliban's interpretation of [Sharia](28840.md) was entirely his decision.

Under the 1998 draft constitution of the first Islamic Emirate, the Leader of the Faithful would appoint justices of the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Afghanistan).[^cite_note-27]

Under the current government, however, the Emir has final authority on political appointments, as well as political, religious, and military affairs. The Emir carries out much of his work through the Rabbari Shura, or the [Leadership Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_Council_of_Afghanistan) (which he chairs[^cite_note-28]), based in [Kandahar](17260.md), which oversees the work of the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Afghanistan), and appointment of individuals to key posts within the cabinet.[^cite_note-29]

However, in a report from [Al Jazeera](30204052.md), the Cabinet has no authority, with all decisions being made confidentially by Akhundzada and the Leadership Council.[^cite_note-30] The supreme leader receives the highest government salary in the reinstated Islamic Emirate, at 228,750 [Afghan afghanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_afghani) monthly.[^cite_note-31]

The **deputy leader of Afghanistan**, officially the **deputy leader of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): د افغانستان د اسلامي امارت مرستیال, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Pashto): *da ufghanstan da islami amarat marstial*;[^cite_note-48] [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): معاون امارت اسلامی افغانستان, romanized: *moavan emart eslami afghanstan*[^cite_note-49]), is the deputy [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) of the Taliban, tasked with assisting the supreme leader with his duties.[^cite_note-VOA_deputy_emir-50][^cite_note-VOA_organization-43] All three supreme leaders of the Taliban have had deputies, with the number of deputies fluctuating between one and three.[^cite_note-51] Akhundzada has three deputies: [Sirajuddin Haqqani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirajuddin_Haqqani), [Mullah Yaqoob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah_Yaqoob), and [Abdul Ghani Baradar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Ghani_Baradar). Haqqani was first appointed as a deputy leader by [Akhtar Mansour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhtar_Mansour) in 2015, and was retained by Akhundzada. Upon assuming office in 2016, Akhundzada appointed Yaqoob, a son of Mullah Omar, as a second deputy. Akhundzada appointed Baradar as a third deputy in 2019.[^cite_note-52]

Since the 2021 return of power to the Taliban, Akhundzada has grown more isolated and he has primarily communicated through his three deputies rather than holding meetings with other Taliban leaders. The deputies' exclusive access to Akhundzada has grown their power.[^cite_note-UNSC_May_2022-15][^cite_note-Amanpour_after_interview-53]
