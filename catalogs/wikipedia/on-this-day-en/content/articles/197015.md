# Drug cartel

A **drug cartel** is a [criminal organization](22625.md) composed of independent [drug lords](23002805.md) who [collude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collude) with each other in order to improve their profits and dominate the [illegal drug trade](218449.md).[^cite_note-1] Drug cartels form with the purpose of controlling the [supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_\(economics\)) of the illegal drug trade and maintaining prices at a high level. The formations of drug cartels are common in [Latin American](18524.md) countries. [Rivalries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivalry) between multiple drug cartels cause them to wage [turf wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turf_war_\(conflict\)) against each other. The term "narcotics cartel" is sometimes used to describe an organization that sells and trafficks narcotics (defined as substances that are illegal, such as cocaine in most places), but this term is not always used accurately as cartels can traffick eg. marijuana in jurisdictions where it is not illegal.

## Structure

### Falcons

Considered as the "eyes and ears" of the streets, the "falcons" are the lowest rank in any drug cartel. They are scouts, who are responsible for conducting [reconnaissance](146911.md), such as reporting the activities of the [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police), the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) and rival groups.[^cite_note-2]

### Hitmen

The armed group within the drug cartel, responsible for carrying out assassinations, kidnappings, thefts and extortions, operating protection rackets, as well as [defending their *plaza* (turf)](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/turf%20war) from rival groups and the military.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

### Lieutenants

The second highest position in the drug cartel organization, responsible for supervising the hitmen and falcons within their own territory. They are allowed to carry out low-profile murders without permission from their bosses.[^cite_note-5]

### Drug lords

[Drug lords](23002805.md) are the highest position in any drug cartel, responsible for supervising the entire drug industry, appointing territorial leaders, making alliances, in addition to planning high-profile murders.[^cite_note-6]

### Other roles

There are other operating groups within the drug cartels. For example, the drug producers and suppliers,[^cite_note-7] although not considered in the basic structure, are critical operators of any drug cartel, along with the [smugglers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mule_\(smuggling\)), [distributors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_\(marketing\)), [sales representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales), [accountants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accountant) and [money launderers](19390.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Furthermore, the arms suppliers operate in a completely different circle;[^cite_note-11] they are technically not considered part of the cartel's logistics.

[Mexican cartel](82533550.md) headquarters

Mexican cartels (also known in [Mexico](3966054.md) as: *la Mafia* (the [mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia) or the mob), *La Maña* (the skill / the bad manners),[^cite_note-22] *narcotraficantes* (narco-traffickers), or simply as *narcos* usually refers to several, rival, criminal organizations that are combated by the Mexican government in the [Mexican drug war](8442323.md) (List sorted by branches and heritage):[^cite_note-23]

Mexican academic Oswaldo Zavala, in his book *[Drug Cartels Do Not Exist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_Cartels_Do_Not_Exist)*, argues that academics, officials, journalists and writers are mistaken to label the criminal gangs as cartels, noting that they do not meet the definition due to the competitive nature of the drugs trade, and the lack of hierarchal structure. He states that the Mexican state perpetuates the label to justify their militarised response.[^cite_note-\:0-24]

According to some observers in 2010, [Los Zetas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Zetas) instituted social media demonstrations of torture and sadism in their reprisals which changed the rules of the game among the Mexican cartels.[^cite_note-25] The level of violence and social instability greatly increased during the presidency of [Felipe Calderon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Calderon).[^cite_note-26] The Calderon and Foxe administrations worked closer with American law enforcement and utilized the military forces which led to some confusion among local law enforcement in public security and a variety of human rights abuses and corruption.[^cite_note-27] The former head of Mexican law enforcement, [Genaro García Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genaro_Garc%C3%ADa_Luna), was sentenced in the Eastern District of New York on October 16, 2024 for his role in [Sinaloa Cartel](16881931.md) associated drug trafficking in the United States.[^cite_note-28]

Note: As of 2020 the DEA considered the cartels of Sinaloa, Jalisco and Golfo-Noreste-Zetas to be the most influential cartels in Mexico.[^cite_note-29]

##### La Cosa Nostra

Italian immigrants to the United States in the early 19th century formed various small-time gangs which gradually evolved into sophisticated crime syndicates which dominated organized crime in America for several decades. Although government crackdowns and a less-tightly knit Italian-American community have largely reduced their power, they remain an active force in the underworld.

#### Venezuela

Historically Venezuela has been a path to the [United States](3434750.md) for [illegal drugs originating in Colombia](13005346.md), through Central America and [Mexico](3966054.md) and Caribbean countries such as [Haiti](13373.md), the [Dominican Republic](8060.md), and [Puerto Rico](23041.md).

According to the [United Nations](31769.md), there has been an increase of cocaine trafficking through Venezuela since 2002.[^cite_note-93] In 2005, Venezuela severed ties with the United States [Drug Enforcement Administration](146720.md) (DEA), accusing its representatives of spying.[^cite_note-NYT2012-94] Following the departure of the DEA from Venezuela and the expansion of DEA's partnership with Colombia in 2005, Venezuela became more attractive to drug traffickers.[^cite_note-VICEdrugs-95] Between 2008 and 2012, Venezuela's cocaine seizure ranking among other countries declined, going from being ranked fourth in the world for cocaine seizures in 2008[^cite_note-unodc.org-96] to sixth in the world in 2012.[^cite_note-97] The cartel groups involved include:

- The [Cuntrera-Caruana Mafia clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuntrera-Caruana_Mafia_clan) moved to Venezuela,

  [^cite_note-roth-98]

  &#x20;which became an important hideout as the clan bought hotels and founded various businesses in Caracas and Valencia, as well as an extended ranch in [Barinas](556254.md), near the Colombian border. *"Venezuela has its own Cosa Nostra family as if it is Sicilian territory,"* according to the Italian police. *"The structure and hierarchy of the Mafia has been entirely reproduced in Venezuela."* The Cuntrera-Caruana clan had direct links with the ruling Commission of the Sicilian Mafia, and are acknowledged by the American Cosa Nostra.

  [^cite_note-roth-98]

Pasquale, Paolo and Gaspare Cuntrera were expelled from Venezuela in 1992, "almost secretly smuggled out of the country, as if it concerned one of their own drug transports. It was imperative they could not contact people on the outside who could have used their political connections to stop the expulsion." Their expulsion was ordered by a commission of the Venezuelan Senate headed by Senator [Cristobal Fernandez Dalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristobal_Fernandez_Dalo?action=edit\&redlink=1) and his money laundering investigator, [Thor Halvorssen Hellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_Halvorssen_Hellum). They were arrested in September 1992 at [Fiumicino airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci-Fiumicino_Airport) (Rome),[^cite_note-Fonzi-99][^cite_note-100] and in 1996 were sentenced to 13–20 years.[^cite_note-roth-98]

- Norte del Valle Cartel : In 2008 the leader of the Colombian [Norte del Valle Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_del_Valle_Cartel), [Wilber Varela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilber_Varela), was found murdered in a hotel in [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida_\(state\)) in Venezuela.

  [^cite_note-101]

- The [Cartel of the Suns](45272133.md) According to [Jackson Diehl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Diehl). Deputy Editorial Page Editor of *[The Washington Post](102226.md)*, the Bolivarian government of Venezuela shelters "one of the world's biggest drug cartels". There have also been allegations that former president [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez) and [Diosdado Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Cabello) being involved with drug trafficking.

  [^cite_note-JDmay2015-102]

In May 2015, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported from United States officials that drug trafficking in Venezuela increased significantly with Colombian drug traffickers moving from Colombia to Venezuela due to pressure from law enforcement.[^cite_note-SUNwsj-103] One [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) official described the higher ranks of the Venezuelan government and military as "a criminal organization", with high ranking Venezuelan officials, such as National Assembly President [Diosdado Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Cabello), being accused of drug trafficking.[^cite_note-SUNwsj-103] Those involved with investigations stated that Venezuelan government defectors and former traffickers had given information to investigators and that details of those involved in government drug trafficking were increasing.[^cite_note-SUNwsj-103]

The [yakuza](44774.md) of Japan are similar to the Italian mafias in that they originated centuries ago and follow a rigid set of traditions, but have several aspects that make them unique, such as their [full-body tattoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_suit_\(tattoo\)) and their fairly open place in Japanese society. Many yakuza groups are umbrella organizations, smaller gangs reporting to a larger crime syndicate.
