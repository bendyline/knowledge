**Lyman** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Лиман, IPA: [\[lɪˈmɑn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a0/LL-Q8798\_%28ukr%29-Gzhegozh-%D0%9B%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BD.wav/LL-Q8798\_%28ukr%29-Gzhegozh-%D0%9B%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BD.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a0/LL-Q8798_%28ukr%29-Gzhegozh-%D0%9B%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BD.wav/LL-Q8798_%28ukr%29-Gzhegozh-%D0%9B%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BD.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Лиман, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Liman*), formerly known as **Krasnyi Lyman** (Ukrainian: Красний Лиман; Russian: Красный Лиман, romanized: *Krasnyy Liman*; lit. 'Red [Liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liman_\(landform\))') from 1925 to 2016,[^cite_note-rename-2] is a city in [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md), [Ukraine](31750.md).

Until 2016 it also served as the administrative center of [Lyman Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyman_Raion,_Donetsk_Oblast), though administratively it was not part of the raion and incorporated as a [city of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_regional_significance_\(Ukraine\)). It still serves as the administrative center of [Lyman urban hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyman_urban_hromada)[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] and is part of [Kramatorsk Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kramatorsk_Raion). The population was 20,066 (2022 estimate),[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-5] down from 28,172 in 2001.

The city is located in the historic region of [Sloboda Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_Ukraine).[^cite_note-mai-6] It had its origins in a military settlement established by Ukrainian Cossacks in c. 1667.[^cite_note-mai-6] It grew to become a city following the construction of a railway station, and became a key railway hub in the region.

During the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), the city has seen several battles. The first was the [battle of Krasnyi Lyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Krasnyi_Lyman) in 2014, between Ukrainian government forces and Russian proxies loyal to the breakaway [Donetsk People's Republic](42425747.md), which was won by Ukrainian forces. It remained under government control throughout the remainder of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas), and was renamed to its historic name Lyman in 2016 as part of [decommunization in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization_in_Ukraine).

During the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022, Lyman has been a scene of heavy fighting. It was [captured by the Russian military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_battle_of_Lyman) in May 2022, before [being recaptured by Ukrainian forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Lyman_\(2022\)) in October 2022. The city has been nearly destroyed by the 2022 fighting, and most of the population has fled.

## History

### Pre-founding

Archaeologists have discovered [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) stone sculptures in the Lyman district and [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians) remains from the fourth and third centuries BCE.[^cite_note-ssr-7][^cite_note-esu-8]

### Founding and early history



Orthodox Church of St. Peter and St. Paul, completed in 1848

In 1646, the "Mayaka" fort was built 18 kilometres (11 mi) away from the location of modern Lyman, as part of the larger effort to defend the southern border of [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia) from the attacks of the [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars), especially along the [Donets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets) river. Lyman itself was founded by the Izium Cossack Regiment[^cite_note-mai-6] in 1667/1678 as a fortified town near the Mayaka fortress.[^cite_note-ssr-7][^cite_note-esu-8] In the course of the [administrative reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1708_Russian_administrative_reform) carried out in 1708 by [Moscow](19004.md) tsar [Peter I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great), Lyman was explicitly mentioned as one of the towns included to [Azov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Governorate).[^cite_note-1708List-9][^cite_note-ATSBook-10]

### 20th century

From 1904 to 1910, a railway was constructed that passed through Lyman. This railway stimulated growth of civilian settlement in the area, where earlier settlers had been entirely military.[^cite_note-esu-8]



Monument to the victims of [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor)

Lyman changed hands numerous times during [World War I](4764461.md) and the [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence).[^cite_note-esu-8] In April 1918, Lyman was taken over by the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers),[^cite_note-ssr-7] and passed to the [Ukrainian State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_State). In December 1918 it was captured by the Soviets, then in April 1919 it was captured by [White Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Russia_\(1919%E2%80%931920\)) forces, and in December 1919 it was recaptured by the Soviets.[^cite_note-ssr-7] Afterwards it was administratively part of the [Donets Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Governorate) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The town was given the prefix *Krasnyi* ("red") on 9 January 1925 by the Soviet government.[^cite_note-ssr-7][^cite_note-esu-8] According to the [1926 Soviet census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Soviet_census), Krasnyi Lyman had a population of 4,800 people.[^cite_note-esu-8]

Krasnyi Lyman received city status in 1938, and its population continued to grow: by 1939, it had a population of 25,600. During [World War II](32927.md), Krasnyi Lyman was [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine) by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) between 7 July 1942 and 3 February 1943.[^cite_note-esu-8]

The city's population continued to grow after the end of the war. In 1988, Krasnyi Lyman became a [city of regional significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_regional_significance_\(Ukraine\)).[^cite_note-esu-8]

### Russo-Ukrainian War

Further information: [Battle of Krasnyi Lyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Krasnyi_Lyman), [Battle of Lyman (September–October 2022)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lyman_\(September%E2%80%93October_2022\)), and [2026 Ukrainian counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ukrainian_counteroffensive)



Destroyed Russian equipment after the [battle of Lyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lyman_\(September%E2%80%93October_2022\)), October 2022

In June 2014, the city was the scene of the [battle of Krasnyi Lyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Krasnyi_Lyman) during the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)).[^cite_note-bbc1-14] On 5 June the town returned to Ukrainian control.[^cite_note-15] Following the 2015 [law on decommunization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization_in_Ukraine) the city returned to its original name *Lyman*, removing the prefix *Krasnyi*.[^cite_note-renameKL-16] The change was approved by the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md) (the Ukrainian parliament) on 4 February 2016.[^cite_note-renameKL-16]

Lyman is an important railway junction. During the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Lyman was seized by Russian troops on 27 May 2022.[^cite_note-bbc27May-17][^cite_note-guardian-18] The Russian occupiers changed the city's name back to the Soviet name *Krasnyi Liman* shortly thereafter.[^cite_note-19] The control of Lyman, while it is not a large city, gave Russia and its proxies control of a key road that leads to [Sloviansk](2533448.md) and [Kramatorsk](2864798.md).[^cite_note-bbc27May-17][^cite_note-guardian-18] From 10 September, Ukrainian troops advanced to Lyman as part of a [counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ukrainian_Kharkiv_counteroffensive) launched in [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md), and engaged Russian troops in a [battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Lyman).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] On 1 October, a [Russian Defense Ministry](7875884.md) spokesman announced that Russian forces were withdrawing from the city, hours after Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) had declared [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) to have been [annexed by Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Southern_and_Eastern_Ukraine).[^cite_note-\:0-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

The largely destroyed city has still been regularly shelled by Russia after the battles.[^cite_note-fears-26] On 8 July 2023, the Russian Armed Forces [shelled the residential area in the center of Lyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2023_Lyman_cluster_bombing) with [9M55K-series Smerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-30_Smerch) [cluster munition](79745.md). Lyman was at the time approximately 15 kilometers west of [Russian-occupied territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-occupied_territories_of_Ukraine). The bombing killed 9 civilians, while 13 were wounded.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

In November 2025, Russian forces entered southern part of Lyman, and the city became contested.[^cite_note-30] On 21 December 2025, a Ukrainian military observer Kostyantyn Mashovets reported and confirmed that the Russian forces currently occupy positions in southeastern Lyman.[^cite_note-31]

Since May 2026 there were unofficial reports about a number of minor Ukrainian counterattacks in the area. Codenamed [Operation Vivaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vivaldi), the Ukrainian counterattack in the area was kept in secrecy by the Ukrainian military and there were no official reports. However by September 2026 it has become known that the Ukrainian forces made locally noticeable advances, increasing their contol by 150-250 square kilometers (95-135 square miles) and pushed the Russian forces beyond the [Zherebets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zherebets_\(river\)) river, with it also being the first recorded operation involving the airdropping of [Unmanned ground vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_ground_vehicle) behind enemy lines. Russian bloggers criticized Russian military for false reports they dubbed "pretty reports" ("красивые доклады"). In particular, it appears that Putin's September 1 announcement about Russian control of [Sviatohirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatohirsk), a city in the area, was unsupported.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

## Economy



Centre of Culture and Leisure

The city is known by locals as "The Gates of [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas)", and is a regional export hub for goods like [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [salt](1605200.md), and [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand).[^cite_note-36]

Lyman is a key railway hub,[^cite_note-fears-26] carrying up to 30% of cargo on the Donetsk railway system. 35% of residents are employed in rail transport, and 18% in industry. Railway transport enterprises include the local office of the Donetsk Railway Administration, the PMS-10 track engine station, the ТЧ-1 locomotive depot, the РПЧ-3 motor car depot, and numerous maintenance sites and sections of the railway. Other industries include food processing, a feed mill, quarry management, the Leman-Beton concrete manufacturer, and others.

More than 80 agricultural enterprises operate in the region. The forestry and animal husbandry business is among the most important in Ukraine. More than 40,000 mink skins are produced in Lyman annually. There is also a branch of the energy company 000 Donbasnefteprodukt. Other industries have included a silicate brick factory, an asphalt and concrete factory, and a food canning factory.
