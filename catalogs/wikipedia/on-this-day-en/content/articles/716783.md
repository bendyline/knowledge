**Batam**, officially the **City of Batam** or **Batam City** ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Kota Batam*, not to be confused with *Batam Kota*, a [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kecamatan) within this city), is the largest [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regencies_and_cities_of_Indonesia) in the [Indonesian province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_province) of the [Riau Islands](453745.md).

The city of Batam includes Batam Island at 410 square kilometres (160 mi<sup>2</sup>), as well as other major islands such as [Rempang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rempang_Island) at 165 square kilometres (64 mi<sup>2</sup>) and [Galang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galang_Island) at 80 square kilometres (31 mi<sup>2</sup>). Together, the three-[interconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barelang_Bridge) islands of the [Riau Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Archipelago) are known as *[Barelang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barelang)* (an abbreviation of Batam–Rempang–Galang). The remaining hundreds of tiny islands are largely conservation areas, small fishing outposts, or uninhabited mangroves. Collectively, the land area of all these islands make up around 1,020.28 square kilometres (393.93 mi<sup>2</sup>). Batam Island is the core [urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) and [industrial zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoning), while both Rempang Island and Galang Island maintain their rural character and low-density population; they are connected to Batam Island by short bridges. [Bulang Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulan_Island) and the islands to its north lie to the west of Batam Island; while they are part of the Belakang Padang District, they are also administratively within the city.&#x20;

Batam City is an industrial [boomtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomtown), an emerging transport hub and part of a [free trade zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_zone) in the [Indonesia–Malaysia–Singapore Growth Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Malaysia%E2%80%93Singapore_Growth_Triangle), located 20 km (12 mi) off [Singapore](27318.md)'s south coast.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] According to [Statistics Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Indonesia)'s 2020 census, Batam had a municipal population of 1,196,396,[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2021-5] making it the third largest city in the region of [Sumatra](28679.md), after [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan) and [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang).[^cite_note-BATAM2019-6] Islands that form part of Batam are also the closest parts of [Indonesia](14579.md) to [Singapore](27318.md), at a minimum distance of 6 km (3.7 miles) across the [Singapore Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Strait). In clear weather and good visibility, both Batam and Singapore are visible from each other with the naked eye. Batam was the fastest-growing municipality in [Indonesia](14579.md) during the decade prior to 2010, with a population growth rate of 11% per year;[^cite_note-7] however, there were setbacks during the subsequent decade. In 2017, the island suffered severe job losses; some 300,000 workers were laid off.[^cite_note-8] The official estimated population as of mid 2025 was 1,296,960.[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1]

## History



Harbor view with oil tanks from [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell) at Sambu Island, 1936

The first recorded inhabitants at Batam Island are the [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)) known as the [Orang Laut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Laut), from the year 231 CE.[^cite_note-9] Then the Orang Darat who are the remnants of the pre-Malay population on the island of Batam and [Rempang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rempang).[^cite_note-BRIN2023-10] The island group once served as the field of struggle of Admiral [Hang Nadim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Nadim), against Portuguese invaders in the 16th century, and was utilised by the Indonesian government in the 1960s, who maintained a petroleum logistics base on [Sambu Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambu_Island), one of the minor islands in the Batam group of islands.

In the 1970s, according to Presidential Decree No. 41/1973, [Batam Island](716783.md) was designated as a working environment of an industrial area supported by *Batam Island Industrial Development Authority* or better known as *Batam Authority Board* (*BOB*, now *Batam Development Board* ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Badan Pengusahan Batam* or *BP Batam*) as the driving force for the development of Batam, with the initial aim of making Batam the "Indonesian version of Singapore". With the rapid development of the island, based on Government Regulation No. 34/1983, the Batam District (which is part of Riau Islands Regency) was upgraded to municipality status which has the duties to run government administration and society and support the development of *BP Batam*.

In the [Indonesian Reformation era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformasi_\(Indonesia\)) in the late 1990s, with Act No. 53/1999, the Batam administrative municipality changed its status to an autonomous region, namely Batam City [Government](12229.md) to carry out governmental and developmental functions by involving *BP Batam*.

## Geography

Batam is a roughly oval island with many bays, islets, and peninsulas, located west of [Bintan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island), south of Singapore, north of Rempang Island and Galang Island, and east of Bulang Island. The [Singapore Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Strait) separates [Singapore](27318.md) and Batam, while the [Riau Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau_Strait) separates Batam and [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island) island. Batam Municipality covers 3,869 km<sup>2</sup>, of which 1,020.28 km<sup>2</sup> is land, a figure which includes some [land reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation). However, *[Barelang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barelang_Bridge) Island* (not actually one but three, see above) covers roughly 715 km<sup>2</sup> of that 1,020.28 km<sup>2</sup>, and Batam island itself covers only about 410 km<sup>2</sup> out of the total. The bulk of the municipal population resides on Batam Island.

## Governance

### Mayor

The Batam city government takes care of all population administration and civil registration as well as human resources. The current mayor is [Amsakar Achmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsakar_Achmad?action=edit\&redlink=1) who has served since 2025, accompanied by Li Claudia Chandra as deputy mayor.[^cite_note-12]

### Batam Development Board (BP)

The Agency for [Free Trade Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Trade_Zone) and [Free Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Port) of Batam, often abbreviated as BP Batam, is a government nonstructural body under the [Chairmanship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairmanship) of the Board of Regions (DK) Batam Central Government, which is chaired by a [Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinating_Ministry_for_Economic_Affairs#Minister). BP Batam plays a role in land governance and investment in the [Special Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Economic_Zone) (SEZ) of Batam.

Today, Batam is inhabited by a heterogeneous mixture of people due to labor migration and desire to be close to [Singapore](27318.md); it is very diverse, two-thirds of the population are migrants.[^cite_note-sijgoog-26] The predominant ethnicities are [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesian), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_people), [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people), and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians). The indigenous people of Batam who are related to the Malays are the [Orang Laut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Laut) who are nomads who depend on the sea for their livelihood and the Orang Darat who live nomadically in the interior forests of [Rempang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rempang) whose population is threatened with extinction.[^cite_note-BRIN2023-10] As a municipality it exemplifies the national motto of "[Bhinneka Tunggal Ika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhinneka_Tunggal_Ika)" (Unity in Diversity). The location of Batam, which is close to [Singapore](27318.md), is conducive to the development of [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic), [sociopolitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociopolitical), and [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural) aspects of the [local community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_community).

### Religion



Grand Mosque of Batam



Hock An Kiong Temple

[Islam](6037917.md) is the majority religion in Batam, followed by [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Indonesia), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Indonesia) and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Indonesia). Most of those who follow [Islam](6037917.md) are [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesians) and [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people). The Great Mosque of Batam, located in the city center adjacent to the main square, the mayor's office and the provincial Parliament, is an important symbol of Islamic religious life in Batam. Christianity is also widely embraced by the people of Batam, especially those from the [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) and [Nias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias_people) people of North Sumatra (Protestantism) and migrants from [Flores](11426.md) island in [Nusa Tenggara Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusa_Tenggara_Timur) province, [East Indonesia](59023273.md) (Roman Catholicism) as well as Javanese Catholics from [Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Region_of_Yogyakarta) and [Central Java](453584.md) who emigrated to this province along with a minority of Chinese Indonesians and internal migrant [Indian Indonesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Indonesians) from Medan, [North Sumatra](453595.md). The majority of the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Chinese) population practises [Buddhism](3267529.md). A number of [Viharas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vihara) are located in Batam, of which Vihara Duta Maitreya is one of the most frequented, and is the largest [Vihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vihara) in [Southeast Asia](28741.md) with an area of 4.5 hectares. In addition, Hinduism in practiced by a quarter of its small ethnic Indian Indonesian minority as well as by its [Balinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_people) emigrant minority ethnic populace who migrated from the tourist island of [Bali](4147.md) and are resident for business purposes in this city.

### Language

[Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) is the common lingua franca of Batam in daily use. Other languages such as local [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) (similar to the [Malaysian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_language) standard as spoken in neighbouring [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Singapore](27318.md)) since both varieties coexist with each other as the former is the official and national language widely spoken and used like in the rest of Indonesia whilst the latter is a trade language and widely spoken by [Malaysian Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malays) expatriates from [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor), [Malaysia](3607937.md) as well as [Malay Singaporeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Singaporeans) from Singapore who reside for business purposes and also for [historical purposes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Sultanate). Other ethnic minority languages such as [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_language), [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_languages), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language) and [Chinese ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Chinese) dialects like [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Nan), [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese), [Teochew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_dialect) and [Hainanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainanese) are also widely used owing to the city's ethnolinguistic diversity in addition to the dominant ethnic Malay populace. The usage of [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporean_Mandarin) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) are trending due to [Singaporean](38208458.md) influence, especially by expatriates living in Batam.

## Economy



Industrial area in Batam

Based on Presidential Decree No. 41/1973, the Batam Industrial Development Authority (BP Batam) was established to manage 415 square kilometers of industrial complex in Batam City for heavy industry. Previously only [Pertamina](4042974.md), the [Indonesian](4885606.md) state oil company, settled there with only 6,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-BatamFTZ-29] Shipbuilding and electronics manufacturing are important industries on the island. Being located close to the ports of Singapore, the speed of goods shipping and product distribution is increased, benefiting the island's [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy). With lower labor costs and special government incentives, it is the site of many factories operated by foreign companies.[^cite_note-xinhua20090119-30] For example, an [Apple](856.md) [AirTag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirTag) factory in Batam produces around 70 percent of AirTags globally, and employs around 2,000 workers.[^cite_note-mokhtar2026-31]

Under a framework signed in June 2006, Batam, along with parts of neighbouring [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island) and [Karimun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimun_Regency), are a part of a [Special Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Economic_Zone) with [Singapore](27318.md); this zone eliminates [tariffs](55551.md) and [value-added taxes](52177473.md) for goods shipped between Batam and [Singapore](27318.md).[^cite_note-32] In 2007, Law No. 44 was enacted to supplement Law No. 36/2000 in establishing Batam as a free trade zone for 70 years. BP Batam still manages the industrial complex. And in 2016, the responsibility of industrial complex changed from the governor of [Riau Islands](453745.md) [Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province) to the Office of the Coordinating Economic Minister as per Presidential Decree No. 8/2016.[^cite_note-BatamFTZ-29]

### Tourism



Turi Beach Resort at Nongsa, Batam

Over 1.5 million tourists visited the city in 2015.[^cite_note-33] Batam is the third-busiest entry port to [Indonesia](14579.md) next to [Bali](4147.md) and [Jakarta](16275.md).[^cite_note-34] In 2014, around 58.8% of foreign tourists came from [Singapore](27318.md), 12.8% from [Malaysia](3607937.md) and 4.2% from [South Korea](27019.md).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] Popular tourism spots in Batam include the Maha Vihara Duta Maitreya Temple, [shopping malls](235723.md) (including Grand Batam Mall, Mega Mall Batam Centre, Nagoya Hill Shopping Centre, etc), Jabal Arafah Mosque and the [Barelang Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barelang_Bridge). The former Harbour Bay Mall stopped operating in early March 2020.[^cite_note-37]

### Foreign investment

In the first semester of 2023, the value of foreign investment reached US\$1.2 billion, while domestic investment was recorded at US\$168 million. Most of the foreign investment came from [Singapore](27318.md).[^cite_note-38] Foreigners are allowed to buy apartments with limited leases but not landed homes in [Indonesia](14579.md).[^cite_note-39] In 2021, a group of [Singaporeans](38208458.md) were forcibly evicted after their 30-year property leases expired in Sekupang.[^cite_note-40]

Batam has benefitted from the [China–United States trade war](57055272.md), especially in the [second presidency of Donald Trump](78298108.md) with Chinese manufacturers relocating to Batam to avoid US tariffs.[^cite_note-mokhtar2026-31] In June 2026, Australian [artificial intelligence](1164.md) infrastructure company Firmus Technologies announced plans to develop a 360 MW [Nvidia](39120.md) DSX AI Factory campus in Batam with Singapore-based DayOne. The data centre project, Firmus’s first in Indonesia, was reported to be under construction and scheduled to begin operations in the first quarter of 2027.[^cite_note-41] In July 2026, the 50 km (31 mi) Nongsa–Changi Cable was completed between Nongsa Digital Park in Batam and Changi in Singapore. The 24-fibre-pair subsea cable, developed by BW Digital and Telekomunikasi Indonesia International, was scheduled to begin full operations in September 2026 and provide more than 1.6 petabits per second of capacity with latency below two milliseconds.[^cite_note-42]

## Transport

### Ferry



Sekupang International Ferry Terminal

[Ferries](50771.md) connect Batam to [Singapore](27318.md), [Bintan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bintan_Island), and [Johor Bahru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Bahru) ([Malaysia](3607937.md)). Five ferry terminals are on the [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island): [Batam Harbour Bay Ferry Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batam_Harbour_Bay_Ferry_Terminal), Nongsapura Ferry Terminal, Sekupang, Waterfront City and [Batam Center Ferry Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batam_Center_Ferry_Terminal). Connections to [Singapore](27318.md) are by way of [HarbourFront Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HarbourFront_Centre) and [Tanah Merah Ferry Terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanah_Merah_Ferry_Terminal) run by [Singapore Cruise Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Cruise_Centre) (SCC).

The most recent incident happened on 29 November 2015 when a ferry, 'Sea Prince', hit floating object(s) while en route to [Singapore](27318.md) from Batam and began leaking. A total of 97 passengers were rescued on life rafts.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

Signs showing a picture of a raised finger over a pair of lips have been placed in August 2014 at the Batam Centre International Ferry Terminal to request silence while queuing for [immigration](4599312.md) to hear names being called and clearly hear instructions given by the immigration officer. Some visitors have been sent back immediately on the first ferry available for flouting this rule. The signs are also applicable at other terminals, but are not strictly enforced.[^cite_note-45]

### Trans Batam



Trans Batam interior

Trans Batam is the most reliable and the cheapest public transportation in Batam. It began to operate in 2005. Trans Batam is the second BRT system in [Indonesia](14579.md), after [Jakarta](16275.md)'s [TransJakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransJakarta). The price for one ride is Rp2,500 for students and Rp5,000 for the public.[^cite_note-46] Trans Batam operates from 05\:30 to 19\:00.

### Taxi

Taxis in Batam are available almost anywhere at ferry terminals, the [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport), [shopping malls](235723.md), [hotels](14276.md), etc. More than 3000 taxis are in Batam.&#x20;

### Airport



Hang Nadim International Airport

[Hang Nadim International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Nadim_Airport) is Batam island's main [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport), and has the longest [runway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway) of all [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) in [Indonesia](14579.md). The [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) was the largest [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) in the [Sumatra region](28679.md) from 1995 to 2012 with a capacity of six million passengers annually, and is now the second-largest in the [Sumatra region](28679.md) after [Kuala Namu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Namu_International_Airport) in [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan), which has a capacity of eight million passengers. The [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) has 4 paired [jetbridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_bridge) and 2 single [jetbridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_bridge). [Hang Nadim International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Nadim_International_Airport) is a hub for [Lion Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Air), [Batik Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batik_Air) and [Citilink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citilink). BP Batam, which operates [Hang Nadim International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_Nadim_International_Airport), will build a new terminal with a capacity of eight million passengers per year for each terminal (16 million passengers per year in total for two terminals) in late 2016. BP Batam intends to build eight paired [jetbridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetbridges) in the new terminal.[^cite_note-48] Domestic destinations include [Pekanbaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Syarif_Kasim_II_Airport), [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Mahmud_Badaruddin_II_International_Airport), [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Namu_International_Airport), [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soekarno-Hatta_International_Airport), [Padang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_International_Airport), [Surabaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juanda_International_Airport), [Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husein_Sastranegara_International_Airport), [Bandar Lampung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radin_Inten_II_Airport), [Balikpapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Aji_Muhammad_Sulaiman_Airport), [Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adisucipto_International_Airport), [Makassar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Hasanuddin_International_Airport), and many more. [International flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_flight) currently include [Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subang_Airport) (operated by [Batik Air Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batik_Air_Malaysia)).

## Environment

Due to piling works for touristic activity, sand mining, and logging for charcoal business, only 4.2% of Batam Island is covered in [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) forests, a great decrease from 24% in 1970.[^cite_note-49] Originally, Batam Island had 41,500 hectares of [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) forests.[^cite_note-50]
