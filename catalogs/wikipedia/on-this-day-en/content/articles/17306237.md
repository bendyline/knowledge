**Melbourne** ([/ˈmɛlbərn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*MEL-bərn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key),[^cite_note-4] locally [\[ˈmæɫbən\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/10/En-au-Melbourne.oga/En-au-Melbourne.oga.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/10/En-au-Melbourne.oga/En-au-Melbourne.oga.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Australian_capital_cities) and [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Australia_by_population) of the [Australian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_state) of [Victoria](4689460.md) and the second-most populous city in [Australia](4689264.md).[^cite_note-24pop-2] The city's name generally refers to a 9,993-square-kilometre (3,858 sq mi) area,[^cite_note-GCCSAgeo-1] comprising an urban agglomeration of [30 local government areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Areas_of_Victoria#Municipalities_of_Melbourne).[^cite_note-5] The name is also used specifically to refer to the local government area, also named the [City of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Melbourne), whose administrative area is centred on the [Melbourne central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_central_business_district) and some immediate surrounds.

The city occupies much of the northern and eastern coastlines of [Port Phillip Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Phillip). As of June 2025, the population of the city was 5.435 million, or 19% of the [population of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_of_Australia).\[^cite_note-[www.abs.gov.au-2026-6](http://www.abs.gov.au-2026-6/)] It is the southernmost city in the world with a population exceeding one million.[^cite_note-7] Its inhabitants are known as "Melburnians".

The area of Melbourne has been home to [Aboriginal Victorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Victorians) for over 40,000 years.[^cite_note-settlement8-9-8][^cite_note-merrimerri-9] Of the five peoples of the [Kulin nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulin_nation), the traditional custodians of the land encompassing Melbourne are the [Boonwurrung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boonwurrung), [Woiwurrung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woiwurrung) and the [Wurundjeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurundjeri) peoples. In 1803, a short-lived British [penal settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_settlement) was established at Port Phillip, then part of the [Colony of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_South_Wales). Melbourne was founded in 1835 with the arrival of free settlers from [Van Diemen's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Diemen's_Land) (modern-day [Tasmania](29944.md)).[^cite_note-settlement8-9-8] It was incorporated as a [Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown) settlement in 1837 and named after the then [Prime Minister of the United Kingdom](24150.md), [William Lamb, 2nd Viscount Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lamb,_2nd_Viscount_Melbourne).[^cite_note-settlement8-9-8] Declared a city by [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) in 1847, it became the capital of the newly separated [Colony of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Victoria) in 1851.[^cite_note-MilesLewis25-10] During the 1850s [Victorian gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_gold_rush), the city entered a lengthy boom period that, by the late 1880s, had transformed it into one of the largest and wealthiest metropolises in both Australia and the world.[^cite_note-RobertCervero320-11][^cite_note-12] After the [federation of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) in 1901, Melbourne served as the interim seat of government of the new nation until [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra) became the permanent capital in 1927.[^cite_note-13]

Today, Melbourne is culturally diverse. It is a leading financial centre in the Asia-Pacific region, ranking 28th globally in the 2024 [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index).[^cite_note-GFCI2-14] The city's [eclectic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Melbourne) blends Victorian-era structures, such as the [World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage)–listed [Royal Exhibition Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Exhibition_Building), with [a large skyline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Melbourne). Landmarks include the [Melbourne Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Ground) and the [National Gallery of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Victoria). Noted for [its cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Melbourne), the city gave rise to [Australian rules football](2403.md), [Australian impressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_School) and [Australian cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_cinema), and is known for [its street art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_art_in_Melbourne), [live music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Melbourne) and theatre scenes. It hosts major annual sporting events, such as the [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Grand_Prix) and the [Australian Open](97851.md) Grand Slam, and also hosted the [1956 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Summer_Olympics). Melbourne ranked as the [world's most liveable city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_quality_of_life_indices) on *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)*'s measure for much of the 2010s.[^cite_note-ABC_20170816-15] It was also voted the world's best city by *[Time Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Out_\(magazine\))* in 2026.[^cite_note-16]

[Melbourne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Airport) is the second-busiest airport in Australia and the [Port of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Melbourne) is the nation's busiest seaport.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Its main [metropolitan rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_in_Melbourne) terminus is [Flinders Street station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flinders_Street_station) and its main [regional rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Victoria) and road coach terminus is [Southern Cross station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cross_station). It also has Australia's most [extensive freeway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Melbourne) and the [largest urban tram network in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Melbourne).[^cite_note-DOT_tram-19]

## Name

The Port Phillip area of Victoria was known in the [Boonwurrung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boonwurrung_language)/[Woiwurrung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woiwurrung_language) languages as *Narrm* or *Naarm*.[^cite_note-naarm-20][^cite_note-Nicholson-21]

Different parts of Melbourne had different traditional Indigenous names, though these do not match up exactly with modern council districts. The Melbourne city area near St Paul's (or possibly St Patrick's) Cathedral was called *Geeburr*, the Fitzroy area was referred to as *Ngár-go*, and Collingwood was known as *Yálla-birr-ang* in the [Woiwurrung language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woiwurrung_language).[^cite_note-22] The wider Port Phillip Bay area is referred to as *Naarm* in the Woiwurrung language[^cite_note-ABCDeepTime-23]; in the Boonwurrung language, *Naarm* means "the Bay".[^cite_note-ABCDeepTime-23]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Melbourne history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Melbourne_history).

### Indigenous people

Further information: [Aboriginal Victorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Victorians) and [Aboriginal Australians](2912594.md)

[Aboriginal Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Victorians) have lived in the Melbourne area for at least 40,000 years.[^cite_note-24] When [British colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Victoria) arrived in the 19th century, up to 20,000 [Kulin people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulin_people) from three distinct language groups – the [Wurundjeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurundjeri), [Bunurong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunurong) and [Wathaurong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wathaurong) – resided in the area.[^cite_note-rbg.vic.gov.au-25][^cite_note-26] It was an important meeting place for the clans of the [Kulin nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulin_nation) alliance and a vital source of food and water.[^cite_note-settlement-27][^cite_note-merrimerri-9] In June 2021, the boundaries between the land of two of the [traditional owner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_owner) groups, the Wurundjeri and Bunurong, were agreed after being drawn up by the [Victorian Aboriginal Heritage Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Aboriginal_Heritage_Council). The borderline runs across the city from west to east, with [the CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_central_business_district), [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Victoria) and [Hawthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorn,_Victoria) included in Wurundjeri land, and [Albert Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Park,_Victoria), [St Kilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Kilda,_Victoria) and [Caulfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caulfield,_Victoria) on Bunurong land.[^cite_note-28] However, this change in boundaries remains contested by individuals on both sides of the debate, including N'arweet [Carolyn Briggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolyn_Briggs).[^cite_note-29] Some sources indicate that the name *Narrm* refers to the area where Melbourne city is built;[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-naarm-20] other sources indicate that *Naarm* refers to the wider Port Phillip Bay area in the Woiwurrung language,[^cite_note-ABCDeepTime-23] and means "the Bay" in the Boonwurrung language.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-ABCDeepTime-23] *Narrm* means "scrub" in Eastern Kulin languages, which reflects the Creation Story of how the Bay was filled by the creation of the Birrarung (Yarra River). Before this, the dry Melbourne region extended out into the Bay, which was filled with tea-tree scrub where *boorrimul* (emu) and *marram* (kangaroo) were hunted.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

### British colonisation

Further information: [Foundation of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_of_Melbourne)

The first [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) settlement in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)), then part of the [penal colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_colony) of [New South Wales](21654.md), was established by Colonel [David Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Collins_\(lieutenant_governor\)) in October 1803, at [Sullivan Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sullivan_Bay), near present-day [Sorrento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorrento,_Victoria). The following year, due to a perceived lack of resources, these settlers relocated to [Van Diemen's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Diemen's_Land) (present-day [Tasmania](29944.md)) and founded the city of [Hobart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart). It would be 30 years before another settlement was attempted.[^cite_note-34]



A late 19th-century artist's depiction of [John Batman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Batman)'s [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman's_Treaty) with a group of [Wurundjeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurundjeri) elders.

In May and June 1835, [John Batman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Batman), a leading member of the [Port Phillip Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Phillip_Association) in Van Diemen's Land, explored the Melbourne area, and later claimed to have [negotiated a purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman's_Treaty) of 2,400 km<sup>2</sup> (600,000 acres) with eight Wurundjeri elders. However, the nature of the treaty has been heavily disputed, as none of the parties spoke the same language, and the elders likely perceived it as part of the gift exchanges which had taken place over the previous few days amounting to a [tanderrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanderrum) ceremony which allows temporary access to and use of the land.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] Batman selected a site on the northern bank of the [Yarra River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarra_River), declaring that "this will be the place for a village" before returning to Van Diemen's Land.[^cite_note-37] In August 1835, another group of Vandemonian settlers arrived in the area and established a settlement at the site of the current [Melbourne Immigration Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Museum,_Melbourne). Batman and his group arrived the following month, and the two groups ultimately agreed to share the settlement, initially known by the Indigenous name of Dootigala.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

Batman's Treaty with the Aboriginal elders was annulled by [Richard Bourke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bourke), the [Governor of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_South_Wales) (who at the time governed all of eastern mainland Australia), with compensation paid to members of the association.[^cite_note-settlement-27] In 1836, Bourke declared the city the administrative capital of the [Port Phillip District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Phillip_District) of [New South Wales](21654.md), and commissioned the first plan for its urban layout, the [Hoddle Grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoddle_Grid), in 1837.[^cite_note-40] Previously known under various informal names,[^cite_note-41] the settlement was named Melbourne on 10 April 1837 by Bourke[^cite_note-42] after the [British Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Prime_Minister), [William Lamb, 2nd Viscount Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lamb,_2nd_Viscount_Melbourne), whose [seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_seat) was [Melbourne Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Hall) in the [market town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town) of [Melbourne, Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne,_Derbyshire).[^cite_note-43] That year, the settlement's [general post office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Post_Office,_Melbourne) officially opened with that name.[^cite_note-44]



Melbourne in 1840.

Between 1836 and 1842, Victorian Aboriginal groups were largely dispossessed of their land by British colonists.[^cite_note-45] In 1840, the Superintendent of the [Port Phillip District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Phillip_District), [Charles La Trobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_La_Trobe), issued a directive to banish Aboriginal people from the immediate vicinity of Melbourne.[^cite_note-46] This was enforced later that same year by the mass arrest and imprisonment of hundreds of Indigenous people during the [Lettsom raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lettsom_raid).[^cite_note-47] However, Aboriginal people continued to live near the settlement, and by January 1844, there were said to be 675 residing in squalid camps around Melbourne.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoyce2011186-48] The British Colonial Office had appointed five [Aboriginal Protectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protector_of_Aborigines) for the Aboriginal people of Victoria, in 1839, but their work was nullified by a land policy that favoured [squatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatting_\(pastoral\)) who took possession of Aboriginal lands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoyce2011199-49] By 1845, fewer than 240 wealthy Europeans held all the pastoral licences then issued in Victoria; this group would remain a powerful political and economic force in the colony for generations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoyce2011163-50] [Letters patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_patent) of [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria), issued on 25 June 1847, declared Melbourne a city.[^cite_note-MilesLewis25-10] On 1 July 1851, the Port Phillip District separated from [New South Wales](21654.md) to become the Colony of Victoria, with Melbourne as its capital.[^cite_note-51]

### Victorian gold rush



South Melbourne's "Canvas Town" provided temporary accommodation for the thousands of migrants who arrived each week during the 1850s gold rush.



A large crowd outside the Victorian Supreme Court, celebrating the release of the [Eureka rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_Rebellion) in 1855.

Further information: [Victorian gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_gold_rush)

The discovery of gold in Victoria in mid-1851 sparked [a gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_gold_rush), and Melbourne, the colony's major port, experienced rapid growth. Within months, the city's population had nearly doubled from 25,000 to 40,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-52] Exponential growth ensued, and by 1865, Melbourne had overtaken [Sydney](27862.md) as Australia's most populous city.[^cite_note-53]

An influx of intercolonial and international migrants, particularly from Europe and China, saw the establishment of slums, including [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Melbourne) and a temporary "tent city" on the southern banks of the Yarra. In the aftermath of the 1854 [Eureka Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_Rebellion), mass public support for the plight of the miners resulted in major political changes to the colony, including improvements in working conditions across mining, agriculture, manufacturing and other local industries. At least twenty nationalities took part in the rebellion, giving some indication of immigration flows at the time.[^cite_note-54]

With the wealth brought in from the gold rush and the subsequent need for public buildings, a program of grand civic construction soon began. The 1850s and 1860s saw the commencement of [Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Melbourne), the [Treasury Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Treasury_Building,_Melbourne), the [Old Melbourne Gaol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Melbourne_Gaol), [Victoria Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Barracks,_Melbourne), the [State Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Library_of_Victoria), the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), the [General Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_GPO), [Customs House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Museum,_Melbourne), the [Melbourne Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Town_Hall), and [St Patrick's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick's_Cathedral,_Melbourne), though many remained incomplete for decades.

The layout of the inner suburbs on a largely one-mile grid pattern, cut through by wide radial boulevards and parklands surrounding the central city, was largely established in the 1850s and 1860s. These areas rapidly filled with the ubiquitous terrace houses, as well as with detached houses and grand mansions, while some of the major roads developed as shopping streets. Melbourne quickly became a major finance centre, home to several banks, the [Royal Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Mint), and (in 1861) Australia's first [stock exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Securities_Exchange).[^cite_note-55] In 1855, the [Melbourne Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Club) secured possession of its now famous ground, the [MCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCG). Members of the [Melbourne Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Football_Club) codified [Australian football](2403.md) in 1859,[^cite_note-56] and in 1861, the first [Melbourne Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cup) race was held. Melbourne acquired its first public monument, the [Burke and Wills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burke_and_Wills) statue, in 1864.[^cite_note-57]

With the gold rush largely over by 1860, Melbourne continued to grow through ongoing gold mining, its role as the major port for exporting Victoria's agricultural products—especially wool—and a developing manufacturing sector protected by high tariffs. An extensive radial railway network spread into the countryside from the late 1850s. Construction started on further major public buildings in the 1860s and 1870s, such as the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Victoria), [Government House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_House,_Melbourne), and the [Queen Victoria Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria_Market). The central city filled up with shops and offices, workshops, and warehouses. Large banks and hotels faced the main streets, with fine townhouses in the east end of Collins Street, contrasting with tiny cottages down laneways within the blocks. The Aboriginal population continued to decline, with an estimated 80% total decrease by 1863, due primarily to introduced diseases (particularly [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox)[^cite_note-rbg.vic.gov.au-25]), frontier violence and dispossession of their lands.[^cite_note-58]

### Land boom and bust



The [Federal Coffee Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Coffee_Palace), demolished in 1973, was one of many notable buildings built after the discovery of gold in Victoria.

The 1880s saw extraordinary growth: consumer confidence, easy access to credit, and steep increases in land prices led to an enormous amount of construction. During this "land boom", Melbourne reputedly became the richest city in the world,[^cite_note-RobertCervero320-11] and the second-largest (after London) in the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire).[^cite_note-59]

The decade began with the Melbourne International Exhibition in 1880, held in the large purpose-built [Exhibition Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Exhibition_Building). A telephone exchange was established that year, and the foundations of [St Paul's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Paul's_Cathedral,_Melbourne) were laid. In 1881, electric light was installed in the [Eastern Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Market,_Melbourne), and a generating station capable of supplying 2,000 incandescent lamps was in operation by 1882.[^cite_note-60] The [Melbourne cable tramway system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_cable_tramway_system) opened in 1885 and became one of the world's most extensive systems by 1890.[^cite_note-61]

In 1885, visiting English journalist [George Augustus Henry Sala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Augustus_Henry_Sala) and Joe Harold coined the phrase "Marvellous Melbourne", which stuck long into the twentieth century and has come to refer to the opulence and energy of the 1880s,[^cite_note-62] during which time large commercial buildings, grand hotels, banks, [coffee palaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_palace), [terrace housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrace_housing) and palatial mansions proliferated in the city.[^cite_note-University1-63] The establishment of the Melbourne Hydraulic Power Company in 1886 led to the availability of high-pressure piped water, allowing for the installation of hydraulically powered [elevators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevator), which led to the construction of the first high-rise buildings in the city.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] The period also saw the huge expansion of a significant radial rail-based transport network throughout the city and suburbs.[^cite_note-66]

Melbourne's land boom peaked in 1888,[^cite_note-University1-63] the year it hosted the Centennial Exhibition. The brash [boosterism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boosterism) that had typified Melbourne during that time ended in the early 1890s. The bubble supporting the local finance and property industries burst, resulting in a severe economic depression.[^cite_note-University1-63][^cite_note-67] Sixteen small [land banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_banking) and building societies collapsed, and 133 limited companies went into liquidation. The Melbourne financial crisis was a contributing factor to the [Australian economic depression of the 1890s](2505080.md) and the [Australian banking crisis of 1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_banking_crisis_of_1893). The effects of the depression on the city were profound, with virtually no significant construction until the late 1890s.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

### Temporary capital of Australia and World War II

Further information: [Federation of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia)



*[The Big Picture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Picture_\(painting\))*, the opening of the first [Parliament of Australia](302298.md) on 9 May 1901, painted by [Tom Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Roberts).

At the time of Australia's [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) on 1 January 1901, Melbourne became the seat of government of the federated [Commonwealth of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Australia). The first federal parliament convened on 9 May 1901 in the [Royal Exhibition Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Exhibition_Building), subsequently moving to the Victorian Parliament House, where it sat until it moved to [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra) in 1927. The [Governor-General of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia) resided at [Government House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_House,_Melbourne) in Melbourne until 1930, and many major national institutions remained in Melbourne well into the twentieth century.[^cite_note-70] During [World War II](32927.md), the city hosted American military forces who were fighting the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), and the government requisitioned the [Melbourne Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Ground) for military use.[^cite_note-71]

### Post-war period

In the immediate years after [World War II](32927.md), Melbourne expanded rapidly, its growth boosted by [post-war immigration to Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-war_immigration_to_Australia), primarily from Southern [Europe](9239.md) and the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_region).[^cite_note-72] While the "Paris End" of Collins Street began Melbourne's boutique shopping and open-air [café](370815.md) culture,[^cite_note-73] the city centre was seen by many as stale—the dreary domain of office workers—something expressed by [John Brack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brack) in his famous painting *[Collins St., 5 pm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins_St.,_5_pm)* (1955).[^cite_note-74] Up until the 21st century, Melbourne was considered Australia's "industrial heartland".[^cite_note-Wade2014-75]



[Orica House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orica_House) (formerly ICI House), a symbol of modernity in post-war Melbourne.

Height limits in the CBD were lifted in 1958, after the construction of [ICI House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICI_House), transforming the city's skyline with the introduction of skyscrapers. Suburban expansion then intensified, served by new indoor malls beginning with [Chadstone Shopping Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadstone_Shopping_Centre).[^cite_note-76] The post-war period also saw a major renewal of the CBD and [St Kilda Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Kilda_Road), which significantly modernised the city.[^cite_note-77] New fire regulations and redevelopment saw most of the taller pre-war CBD buildings either demolished or partially retained through a policy of [facadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facadism#Melbourne). Many of the larger suburban mansions from the boom era were also either demolished or subdivided.

To counter the trend towards low-density suburban residential growth, the state government initiated a series of controversial inner-city public housing projects through the [Housing Commission of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_Commission_of_Victoria), resulting in the demolition of many neighbourhoods and a proliferation of high-rise towers.[^cite_note-78] In later years, with the rapid rise of motor vehicle ownership, the investment in freeway and highway developments greatly accelerated outward suburban sprawl and the decline of the inner-city population. The [Bolte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Bolte) government sought to rapidly accelerate the modernisation of Melbourne. Major road projects including the remodelling of [St Kilda Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Kilda_Junction), the widening of [Hoddle Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoddle_Highway) and then the extensive [1969 Melbourne Transportation Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Melbourne_Transportation_Plan) changed the face of the city into a car-dominated environment.[^cite_note-79]

Australia's financial and mining booms during 1969 and 1970 resulted in the establishment of the headquarters of many major companies ([BHP](200825.md) and [Rio Tinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Tinto_\(corporation\)), among others) in the city. [Nauru](21302.md)'s then booming economy resulted in several ambitious investments in Melbourne, such as [Nauru House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauru_House).[^cite_note-80] Melbourne remained Australia's main business and financial centre until the late 1970s, when it began to lose this primacy to Sydney.[^cite_note-81]

Melbourne experienced an economic downturn between 1989 and 1992, following the collapse of several local financial institutions. In 1992, the newly elected [Kennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Kennett) government began a campaign to revive the economy with an aggressive development campaign of public works coupled with the promotion of the city as a tourist destination with a focus on major events and sports tourism.[^cite_note-82] During this period the [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Grand_Prix) moved to Melbourne from [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide_Street_Circuit). Major projects included the construction of a new facility for the [Melbourne Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Museum), [Federation Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_Square), the [Melbourne Convention & Exhibition Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Convention_&_Exhibition_Centre), [Crown Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Melbourne) and the [CityLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityLink) [tollway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tollway). Other strategies included the privatisation of some of Melbourne's services, including power and public transport, and a reduction in funding to public services such as health, education and public transport infrastructure.[^cite_note-83]

### Contemporary Melbourne



The skyline of Melbourne from [Port Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Melbourne), 2023.

Since the mid-1990s, Melbourne has maintained significant population and employment growth. There has been substantial international investment in the city's industries and property market. Major inner-city urban renewal has occurred in areas such as [Southbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southbank,_Victoria), [Port Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Melbourne), [Melbourne Docklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docklands,_Victoria) and [South Wharf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Wharf). Melbourne sustained the highest population increase and economic growth rate of any Australian capital city from 2001 to 2004.[^cite_note-84]

From 2006, the growth of the city extended into "green wedges" and beyond the city's [urban growth boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_growth_boundary). Predictions of the city's population reaching 5 million people pushed the state government to review the growth boundary in 2008 as part of its Melbourne @ Five Million strategy.[^cite_note-85] In 2009, Melbourne was less affected by the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession) in comparison to other Australian cities. At this time, more new jobs were created in Melbourne than in any other Australian city—almost as many as in the next two fastest-growing cities, Brisbane and Perth, combined;[^cite_note-86] meanwhile, Melbourne's property market remained expensive,[^cite_note-87] resulting in historically high property values and widespread rent increases.[^cite_note-88]

Beginning in the 2010s, the State Government of Victoria initiated a number of major infrastructure projects designed to reduce congestion in Melbourne and encourage economic growth, including the [Metro Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Tunnel), the [West Gate Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Gate_Tunnel), the [Level Crossing Removal Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_Crossing_Removal_Project) and the [Suburban Rail Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburban_Rail_Loop).[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] New urban renewal zones were initiated in inner-city areas like [Fisherman's Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisherman's_Bend) and [Arden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arden_railway_station,_Melbourne), while suburban growth continued on the urban periphery in Melbourne's outer west and east in suburbs like [Wyndham Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyndham_Vale) and [Cranbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranbourne,_Victoria).[^cite_note-91] Middle suburbs like [Box Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_Hill,_Victoria) became denser as a greater proportion of Melburnians began living in apartments.[^cite_note-92] A construction boom resulted in 34 new [skyscrapers](29485.md) being built in the central business district between 2010 and 2020.[^cite_note-93] In 2020, Melbourne was classified as an Alpha city by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network).[^cite_note-94]

Of all major Australian cities, Melbourne was the worst affected by the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and spent a long time under lockdown restrictions.[^cite_note-95] Over the course of its six lockdowns, Melbourne experienced the longest total lockdown period of any city in the world, totalling 262 days.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] While this contributed to a net outflow of migration that caused a slight reduction in Melbourne's population between 2020 and 2022, Melbourne's population is projected to reach 6.4 million people by 2033–34.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Melbourne)

See also: [Lakes and reservoirs of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakes_and_reservoirs_of_Melbourne)



Melbourne and [Geelong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong) urban areas.

Melbourne is in the southeastern part of mainland Australia, within the state of [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)).[^cite_note-101] Geologically, it is built on the confluence of [Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary) lava flows to the west, [Silurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silurian) [mudstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudstone) to the east, and [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) sand accumulation to the southeast along [Port Phillip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Phillip). The southeastern suburbs are situated on the [Selwyn fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selwyn_fault), which transects [Mount Martha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Martha) and [Cranbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranbourne,_Victoria).[^cite_note-102] The western portion of the metropolitan area lies within the [Victorian Volcanic Plain grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Volcanic_Plain_grasslands) vegetation community,[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] and the southeast falls in the [Gippsland Plains Grassy Woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gippsland_Plains_Grassy_Woodland) zone.[^cite_note-105]

Melbourne extends northward through the undulating bushland valleys of the [Yarra Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarra_Valley)'s tributaries—[Moonee Ponds Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonee_Ponds_Creek) (toward [Melbourne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Airport)),[^cite_note-106] [Merri Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merri_Creek), [Darebin Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darebin_Creek) and [Plenty River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenty_River).[^cite_note-107] The city reaches southeast through [Dandenong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dandenong) to the growth corridor of [Pakenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakenham,_Victoria) towards [West Gippsland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Gippsland).[^cite_note-108] In the west, it extends along the [Maribyrnong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maribyrnong_River) and its tributaries north towards [Sunbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunbury,_Victoria).[^cite_note-109]

Melbourne's major bayside beaches are in the various suburbs along the shores of Port Phillip Bay, in areas like [Port Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Melbourne), [Albert Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Park,_Victoria), [St Kilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Kilda,_Victoria), [Elwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwood,_Victoria), [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton,_Victoria), [Sandringham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandringham,_Victoria), [Mentone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentone,_Victoria), [Frankston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankston,_Victoria), [Altona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altona,_Victoria), [Williamstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamstown,_Victoria) and Werribee South. The nearest [surf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_surface_wave) beaches are 85 kilometres (53 mi) south of the Melbourne CBD in the back-beaches of [Rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye,_Victoria), [Sorrento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorrento,_Victoria) and [Portsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsea,_Victoria).[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Melbourne)

Further information: [Environmental issues in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_Melbourne) and [Extreme weather events in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather_events_in_Melbourne)



Storm passing over the CBD in August. Melbourne is said to have "four seasons in one day" due to its changeable weather.

Melbourne has, depending on the classification system used, a temperate [oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*) or [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Cf*) climate, with warm summers and cool winters.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] Melbourne is well known for its [changeable weather conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southerly_buster), mainly due to it being located on the boundary of hot inland areas and the cool southern ocean. This temperature differential is most pronounced in the spring and summer months and can cause strong [cold fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front) to form. These cold fronts can be responsible for varied forms of severe weather from [gales](320909.md) to [thunderstorms](70807.md) and [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail), large temperature drops and heavy rain. Winters, while exceptionally dry by southern Victorian standards, are often cloudy. The lack of winter rainfall is because of Melbourne's [rain-shadowed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) location between the [Otway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otway_Ranges) and [Macedon Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedon_Ranges), which block much of the rainfall arriving from the north and west.[^cite_note-115]

[Port Phillip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Phillip) is often warmer than the surrounding oceans or the land mass, particularly in spring and autumn; this can set up a "[bay effect rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake-effect_rain)", where showers are intensified leeward of the bay. Relatively narrow streams of heavy showers can often affect the same places (usually the eastern suburbs) for an extended period, while the rest of Melbourne and its surrounds remain dry. Overall, the area around Melbourne is, owing to its rain shadow, nonetheless significantly drier than average for southern Victoria.[^cite_note-116] Within the city and surrounds, rainfall varies widely, from about 425 millimetres (16.7 in) at [Little River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_River,_Victoria) to 1,250 millimetres (49 in) on the eastern fringe at [Gembrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gembrook). Melbourne receives 48.6 clear days annually. Dewpoint temperatures in the summer range from 9.5 to 11.7 °C (49.1 to 53.1 °F).[^cite_note-Bureau-117]

Melbourne rarely receives widespread or continuous rainfall (as experienced in other Australian cities, such as [Sydney](27862.md) or [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane)) and instead experiences precipitation through fleeting showers that can vary in intensity. Melbourne is particularly prone to these isolated convective showers forming when a [cold pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_pool) crosses the state, especially if there is considerable daytime heating. These showers are often heavy and can include hail, squalls, and significant drops in temperature, but they often pass through quickly, followed by rapid clearing, sunny and relatively calm weather, and a return to the preceding temperature. This can occur in the space of minutes and can be repeated many times a day, giving Melbourne a reputation for having "four seasons in one day",[^cite_note-Bureau-117] a phrase that is part of local [popular culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_culture).[^cite_note-118] The lowest temperature on record is −2.8 °C (27.0 °F), recorded on 21 July 1869.[^cite_note-Monthly_climate_statistics-119] The highest temperature recorded in Melbourne city was 46.4 °C (115.5 °F), recorded on [7 February 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Saturday_bushfires).[^cite_note-Monthly_climate_statistics-119] While [snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_in_Australia) is occasionally seen at higher elevations in the outskirts of the city, and dustings were observed in 2020, it has not been recorded in the central business district since 1986.[^cite_note-120]

The sea temperature in Melbourne is warmer than the surrounding ocean during the summer months, and colder during the winter months. This is predominantly due to Port Phillip Bay being an enclosed and shallow bay that is largely protected from the ocean,[^cite_note-121] resulting in greater temperature variation across seasons.

## Urban structure

See also: [Melbourne city centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_city_centre), [List of heritage listed buildings in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heritage_listed_buildings_in_Melbourne), [Lanes and arcades of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanes_and_arcades_of_Melbourne), [Parks and gardens of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_gardens_of_Melbourne), and [List of tallest buildings in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Melbourne)



Melbourne population density by mesh blocks (MB), according to the 2016 census.



The CBD as viewed from above [Kings Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Domain).



Spring at the [Melbourne Botanic Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Botanic_Gardens). Melbourne is often referred to as Australia's garden city.

Melbourne's [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) is approximately 2,704 square kilometres (1,044 sq mi), the largest in Australia and the 33rd-largest in the world.[^cite_note-128] The [Hoddle Grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoddle_Grid), a grid of streets measuring approximately 1.61 by 0.80 km (1 by 1⁄2 mi), forms the nucleus of Melbourne's [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) (CBD). The grid's southern edge fronts onto the [Yarra River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarra_River). More recent office, commercial and public developments in the adjoining districts of [Southbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southbank,_Victoria) and [Docklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Docklands) have made these areas into extensions of the CBD in all but name. A byproduct of the CBD's layout is its network of lanes and [arcades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_\(architecture\)), such as [Block Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Arcade) and [Royal Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Arcade,_Melbourne).[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

Melbourne's CBD has become Australia's most densely populated area, with approximately 19,500 residents per square kilometre,[^cite_note-131] and is home to [more skyscrapers than any other Australian city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Australia#Cities_with_the_most_skyscrapers), the tallest being [Australia 108](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_108), situated in Southbank.[^cite_note-132]

The CBD and surrounds also contain many significant historic buildings such as the [Royal Exhibition Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Exhibition_Building), the [Melbourne Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Town_Hall) and [Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Melbourne).[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

Although the area is described as the *centre*, it is not the demographic centre of Melbourne due to urban sprawl to the south-east; the demographic centre is instead located at [Camberwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camberwell,_Victoria).[^cite_note-135]

Melbourne is typical of Australian capital cities in that after the turn of the 20th century, it expanded with the underlying notion of a 'quarter-acre home and garden' for every family, often referred to locally as the [Australian Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Dream).[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] This, coupled with the popularity of the private motor vehicle after 1945, led to the car-centric urban structure present today in the middle and outer suburbs. Much of [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) Melbourne is accordingly characterised by low-density sprawl, while its inner-city areas feature predominantly medium-density, transit-oriented urban forms. The city centre, Docklands, St Kilda Road, and Southbank areas feature high-density development.

Melbourne is often referred to as Australia's garden city, and the state of Victoria is known as *the garden state*.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] There is an abundance of [parks and gardens in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_parks_and_gardens),[^cite_note-140] many close to [the CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Central_Business_District), featuring a variety of common and rare plant species amid landscaped vistas, pedestrian pathways, and tree-lined avenues. Melbourne's public parks are often considered the finest among those of Australia's major cities.[^cite_note-141] There are also many parks in the surrounding suburbs of Melbourne, such as in the municipalities of [Stonnington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Stonnington), [Boroondara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroondara) and [Port Phillip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Port_Phillip), southeast of the central business district. Several [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park) have been designated around the urban area of Melbourne, including the [Mornington Peninsula National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mornington_Peninsula_National_Park), [Port Phillip Heads Marine National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Phillip_Heads_Marine_National_Park) and [Point Nepean National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Nepean_National_Park) in the southeast, [Organ Pipes National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_Pipes_National_Park) to the north and [Dandenong Ranges National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dandenong_Ranges_National_Park) to the east. There are also a number of significant state parks just outside Melbourne.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] The extensive area covered by urban Melbourne is formally divided into hundreds of suburbs (for addressing and postal purposes), and administered as local government areas,[^cite_note-144] 31 of which are located within the metropolitan area.[^cite_note-145]

### Housing

Main article: [Housing in Victoria, Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_in_Victoria,_Australia)



19th-century terrace houses are common in the inner suburbs.

Melbourne has minimal public housing and high demand for rental housing, which is becoming unaffordable for many.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] Public housing is managed and provided by the [Victorian Government's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_State_Government) [Department of Families, Fairness and Housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Families,_Fairness_and_Housing), and operates within the framework of the Commonwealth-State Housing Agreement, by which both federal and state governments provide funding for housing.

Melbourne is experiencing high population growth, generating high demand for housing. This housing boom has increased house prices and rents, as well as the availability of all types of housing. [Subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivision_\(land\)) regularly occurs in the outer areas of Melbourne, with numerous developers offering house and land packages. However, since the release of [Melbourne 2030](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_2030) in 2002, planning policies have encouraged [medium-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-density_housing) and [high-density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_density_housing) development in existing areas with good access to [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) and other services. Consequently, Melbourne's middle- and outer-ring suburbs have seen significant [brownfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownfield_land) redevelopment.[^cite_note-149]

## Architecture

Further information: [Architecture of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Melbourne) and [List of tallest buildings in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Melbourne)



Victorian-era buildings on [Collins Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins_Street,_Melbourne), preserved by setting skyscrapers back from the street.

On the back of the 1850s gold rush and 1880s land boom, Melbourne became renowned as one of the world's great Victorian-era cities, a reputation that persists due to its diverse range of [Victorian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_architecture).[^cite_note-150] High concentrations of well-preserved Victorian-era buildings can be found in the inner suburbs, such as [Carlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton,_Victoria), [East Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Melbourne) and [South Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Melbourne).[^cite_note-151] Outstanding examples of Melbourne's built Victorian heritage include the [World Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage)–listed [Royal Exhibition Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Exhibition_Building) (1880), the [General Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Post_Office,_Melbourne) (1867), the [Hotel Windsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Windsor_\(Melbourne\)) (1884), and the [Block Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Arcade) (1891).[^cite_note-152] Comparatively little remains of Melbourne's pre-gold rush architecture; [St James Old Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James_Old_Cathedral) (1839) and [St Francis' Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Francis'_Church,_Melbourne) (1845) are among the few examples left in the CBD. Many of the CBD's Victorian boom-time landmarks were also demolished in the decades after World War II, including the [Federal Coffee Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Coffee_Palace) (1888) and the [APA Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APA_Building,_Melbourne) (1889), one of the tallest [early skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_skyscraper) upon completion.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] [Heritage listings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heritage-listed_buildings_in_Melbourne) and [heritage overlays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_overlay) have since been introduced in an effort to prevent further losses of the city's historic fabric.



Melbourne is home to [79 skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Melbourne), the tallest being [Australia 108](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_108) (centre-right)—the Southern Hemisphere's only [100-plus-storey building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_buildings_with_100_floors_or_more)—and [Eureka Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_Tower) (right),

[^cite_note-155]

&#x20;pictured in February 2021.

In line with the city's expansion during the early 20th century, suburbs such as [Hawthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorn,_Victoria) and [Camberwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camberwell,_Victoria) are defined largely by [Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_architecture) and [Edwardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwardian_architecture) architectural styles. The [City Baths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Baths,_Melbourne), built in 1903, are a prominent example of the latter style in the CBD. The 1926 [Nicholas Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Building) is the city's grandest example of the [Chicago School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_school_\(architecture\)) style, while the influence of [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) is apparent in the [Manchester Unity Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Unity_Building), completed in 1932. The city also features the [Shrine of Remembrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine_of_Remembrance), which was built as a memorial to the men and women of Victoria who served in [World War I](4764461.md) and is now a memorial to all Australians who have served in war.

Residential architecture is not defined by a single architectural style, but rather by an eclectic mix of large [McMansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMansion)-style houses (particularly in areas of urban sprawl), apartment buildings and townhouses, all of which generally characterise the medium-density inner-city neighbourhoods. Freestanding dwellings with relatively large gardens are perhaps the most common type of housing outside inner city Melbourne. Victorian terrace housing, townhouses and historic [Italianate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italianate), [Tudor Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Revival) and [Neo-Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_architecture) mansions are all common in inner-city neighbourhoods such as Carlton, Fitzroy and further into suburban enclaves like [Toorak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toorak).[^cite_note-156]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Melbourne)



La Trobe Reading Room, [State Library Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Library_Victoria)

Often referred to as Australia's cultural capital, Melbourne is known for its music, theatre and arts scenes, as well as its diverse range of cultural events and festivals, including the [Melbourne International Arts Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_International_Arts_Festival), [Melbourne Fringe Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Fringe_Festival) and [Moomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moomba_Festival), Australia's largest free community festival.[^cite_note-157] For much of the 2010s, Melbourne topped *[The Economist Intelligence Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist_Intelligence_Unit)*'s list of the [world's most liveable cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_most_liveable_cities#World's_Most_Liveable_Cities), partly due to its cultural attributes.[^cite_note-ABC_20170816-15]

[State Library Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Library_Victoria), founded in 1854, is one of the world's oldest free [public libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_libraries) and, as of 2018, the fourth-most-visited library globally.[^cite_note-temple-158] During the 19th-century boom period, Melbourne-based authors and poets [Marcus Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Clarke), [Adam Lindsay Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Lindsay_Gordon) and [Rolf Boldrewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Boldrewood) produced classic visions of colonial life,[^cite_note-159] and many visiting writers recorded literary responses to the city: for [Henry Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kendall_\(poet\)), it was a "wild bleak Bohemia",[^cite_note-160] while [Henry Kingsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kingsley) stated that, in its rapid growth, Melbourne "surpasses all human experience".[^cite_note-161] [Fergus Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fergus_Hume)'s *[The Mystery of a Hansom Cab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mystery_of_a_Hansom_Cab_\(novel\))* (1886), the fastest-selling crime novel of the era, is set in Melbourne, as is Australia's best-selling book of poetry, *[The Songs of a Sentimental Bloke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Songs_of_a_Sentimental_Bloke)* (1915) by [C. J. Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._J._Dennis).[^cite_note-162] Contemporary Melbourne authors who have set novels in the city include [Peter Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Carey_\(novelist\)), [Helen Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Garner) and [Gerald Murnane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Murnane).[^cite_note-163] Melbourne has Australia's widest range of bookstores, as well as the nation's largest publishing sector.[^cite_note-164] The city also hosts the [Melbourne Writers Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Writers_Festival) and the [Victorian Premier's Literary Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Premier's_Literary_Awards). In 2008, it became the second UNESCO [City of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Literature).[^cite_note-165]



Founded in 1854, the [Princess Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Theatre_\(Melbourne\)) is the oldest theatre in the [East End Theatre District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_Theatre_District).

Melbourne is home to [many theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_theatres_in_Melbourne), eight of which are concentrated in the [East End Theatre District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End_Theatre_District), including the Victorian-era [Athenaeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenaeum,_Melbourne), [Her Majesty's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Majesty's_Theatre,_Melbourne) and [Princess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Theatre,_Melbourne) theatres, as well as the [Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_Theatre) and the [Regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent_Theatre,_Melbourne). Other heritage-listed theatres include the avant-garde picture palace [The Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Capitol,_Melbourne) and St Kilda's [Palais Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_Theatre), Australia's largest seated theatre with a capacity of 3,000 people.[^cite_note-166] The [Arts Precinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Arts_Precinct) in Southbank is home to [Arts Centre Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_Centre_Melbourne) (which includes the [State Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Theatre_\(Melbourne\)) and [Hamer Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamer_Hall,_Melbourne)), as well as the [Melbourne Recital Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Recital_Centre), [Malthouse Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malthouse_Theatre) and [Southbank Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southbank_Theatre), home of the [Melbourne Theatre Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Theatre_Company), Australia's oldest professional theatre company.[^cite_note-167] The [Australian Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Ballet), [Opera Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Australia) and [Melbourne Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Symphony_Orchestra) are also based in the precinct. Many of Melbourne's theatres join the [Melbourne Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Town_Hall) in hosting the annual [Melbourne International Comedy Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_International_Comedy_Festival), one of the world's three largest comedy festivals.[^cite_note-168]



St Kilda's [Crystal Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Ballroom_\(Melbourne\)), famed for hosting local and international [post-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-punk) and [new wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_music) bands

Melbourne has been called "the live music capital of the world";[^cite_note-Donoughue-2018-169] one study found it has more music venues per capita than any other world city sampled, with 17.5 million patron visits to 553 venues in 2016.[^cite_note-Donoughue-2018-169][^cite_note-170] Australia's first global music star, opera singer [Nellie Melba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nellie_Melba), took her stage name from her hometown. Composer [Percy Grainger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Grainger) followed her in becoming the most famous Melburnian of the Edwardian era. The [Sidney Myer Music Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Myer_Music_Bowl) in [Kings Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Domain) hosted the largest crowd ever for a music concert in Australia when an estimated 200,000 attendees saw Melbourne band [The Seekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seekers) in 1967.[^cite_note-171] Airing between 1974 and 1987, Melbourne's *[Countdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countdown_\(music_show\))* helped launch the careers of local acts as diverse as [AC/DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC/DC)[^cite_note-172] and [Kylie Minogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kylie_Minogue). Several distinct [post-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-punk) scenes flourished in Melbourne during the late 1970s and early 1980s, including the [Little Band scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Band_scene) and [St Kilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Kilda,_Victoria#Music)'s [Crystal Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Ballroom_\(Melbourne\)) scene, which gave rise to [Dead Can Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Can_Dance) and [Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Cave_and_the_Bad_Seeds).[^cite_note-173] More recent independent acts from Melbourne to achieve global recognition include [The Avalanches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Avalanches), [Gotye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotye) and [King Gizzard and the Lizard Wizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Gizzard_and_the_Lizard_Wizard). Melbourne is also regarded as a centre of [EDM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music), and lends its name to the [Melbourne Bounce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Bounce) genre and the [Melbourne Shuffle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Shuffle) dance style, both of which emerged from the city's underground [rave](43488.md) scene.[^cite_note-174]

NGV International, home of the National Gallery of Victoria's international collection

NGV International in Southbank, home of the [National Gallery of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Victoria)'s international collection

Established in 1861, the [National Gallery of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Victoria) is Australia's oldest and largest art museum, and houses its collection across two sites: [NGV International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Victoria#St_Kilda_Road:_NGV_International) in Southbank and [NGV Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGV_Australia) at Federation Square. Several art movements originated in Melbourne, most famously the [Heidelberg School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_School) of impressionists, named after [a suburb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg,_Victoria) where they camped to paint *[en plein air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_plein_air)* in the 1880s.[^cite_note-175] The [Australian tonalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Tonalism) followed in the 1910s,[^cite_note-176] some of whom founded [Montsalvat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montsalvat) in [Eltham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eltham,_Victoria), Australia's oldest surviving art colony. Mid-century Melbourne became a stronghold of figurative modernism through the paintings of the [Antipodeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipodeans) and [Angry Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angry_Penguins); the latter group often met at a pastoral estate in [Bulleen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulleen), now the [Heide Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heide_Museum_of_Modern_Art).[^cite_note-177] The city is also home to the [Australian Centre for Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Centre_for_Contemporary_Art), as well as numerous independent galleries and artist-run spaces. In the 2000s, [street art proliferated in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_art_in_Melbourne), with [Banksy](396241.md) declaring that its graffiti scene "leads the world",[^cite_note-178] and "laneway galleries" becoming major tourist sites; [Hosier Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosier_Lane), for example, attracts more [Instagram hashtags](31591547.md#Hashtags) than some of the city's traditional destinations, such as the [Melbourne Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Zoo).[^cite_note-Scopelianos2020-179][^cite_note-180] Melbourne's [many public artworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_art_in_Melbourne) range from the [Burke and Wills monument](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File\:Burke%20and%20Wills%20Monument.jpg) (1865) to the abstract sculpture *[Vault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_\(sculpture\))* (1978), the latter a popular reference point amongst Melbourne designers.[^cite_note-181]



[The Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Capitol,_Melbourne), built in 1924, was Melbourne's first major [picture palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picture_palace).

The oldest film in Australia's [National Film and Sound Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_and_Sound_Archive) is of the [1896 Melbourne Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896_Melbourne_Cup).[^cite_note-film1-182] Melbourne filmmakers spurred Australia's first cinematic boom with *[The Story of the Kelly Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Story_of_the_Kelly_Gang)* (1906), shot a quarter century after [bushranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushranger) [Ned Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned_Kelly)'s execution at [Old Melbourne Gaol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Melbourne_Gaol), and since recognised as the world's first feature-length narrative film.[^cite_note-Bushranger1-183] Melbourne remained a world leader in film production until the mid-1910s, when several factors, including a [ban on bushranger films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushranger_ban), contributed to a decades-long decline of the industry.[^cite_note-Bushranger1-183] A notable film shot and set in Melbourne during this lull was *[On the Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Beach_\(1959_film\))* (1959).[^cite_note-184] In the wake of the 1970s [Australian Film Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_New_Wave), many [films have been shot and set in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_shot_in_Melbourne), including *[Mad Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_Max_\(film\))* (1979),[^cite_note-film2-185] *[Romper Stomper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romper_Stomper)* (1992),[^cite_note-film1-182] *[Chopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopper_\(film\))* (2000), and *[Animal Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Kingdom_\(film\))* (2010).[^cite_note-film2-185] The [Melbourne International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_International_Film_Festival) began in 1952 and is one of the world's oldest film festivals.[^cite_note-186] The [AACTA Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AACTA_Awards), Australia's top screen awards, were inaugurated at the [Melbourne International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_International_Film_Festival) in 1958. [Docklands Studios Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docklands_Studios_Melbourne) is the city's largest film and television studio complex and has attracted major international productions.[^cite_note-187] Melbourne is also home to the [ACMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACMI_\(museum\)), the Australian Centre for the Moving Image.[^cite_note-188]

## Sport

Further information: [Sport in Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Victoria)



Statue at the MCG of [Australian rules football](2403.md) founder [Tom Wills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Wills) umpiring an 1858 football match. The first games of Australian rules were played in adjacent parklands.

Rod Laver Arena in Melbourne during the Australian Open, 2023

Melbourne hosts the [Australian Open](97851.md), the first of four annual [Grand Slam](197638.md) tennis tournaments.

Melbourne has long been regarded as Australia's sporting capital—and is sometimes dubbed the sporting capital of the world[^cite_note-189]—due to its role in the development of [Australian sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_sport), the range and quality of its events and venues, and its high rates of spectatorship and participation.[^cite_note-190] The city is home to [27 professional sports teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Victoria#Melbourne_2) competing at the national level, more than in any other Australian city. Melbourne's sporting reputation was recognised in 2016 when, after being ranked as the world's top sports city three times biennially, the Ultimate Sports City Awards in [Switzerland](26748.md) named it 'Sports City of the Decade'.[^cite_note-191]

The city has hosted a number of major international sporting events, most notably the [1956 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Summer_Olympics), the first Olympic Games held outside Europe and the United States.[^cite_note-192] Melbourne also hosted the [2006 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Commonwealth_Games), and is home to several major annual international events, including the [Australian Open](97851.md), the first of the four [Grand Slam](197638.md) tennis tournaments. First held in 1861 and declared a public holiday for all Melburnians in 1873, the [Melbourne Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cup) is the world's richest handicap horse race, and is known as "the race that stops a nation".[^cite_note-193] The [Formula One](10854.md) [Australian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Grand_Prix) has been held at the [Albert Park Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Park_Circuit) since 1996.[^cite_note-194]

[Cricket](25675557.md) was one of the first sports to become organised in Melbourne with the [Melbourne Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Club) forming within three years of settlement. The club manages one of the world's largest stadiums, the 100,000-capacity [Melbourne Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Ground) (MCG).[^cite_note-195] Established in 1853, the MCG is notable for hosting the first [Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket) match and the first [One Day International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International), played between [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_cricket_team) and [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_cricket_team) in 1877 and 1971, respectively. It is also the home of the [National Sports Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sports_Museum),[^cite_note-196] and serves as the home ground of the [Victoria cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_cricket_team). At [Twenty20](770690.md) level, the [Melbourne Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Stars) and [Melbourne Renegades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Renegades) compete in the [Big Bash League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bash_League).

[Australian rules football](2403.md), Australia's most popular spectator sport, traces [its origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origins_of_Australian_rules_football) to matches played in [parklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarra_Park) next to the MCG in 1858. Its first laws were codified the following year by the [Melbourne Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Football_Club),[^cite_note-197] also a founding member of the [Australian Football League](4732785.md) in 1896 (AFL), the sport's elite professional competition. Headquartered at [Docklands Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docklands_Stadium), the AFL fields a further eight Melbourne-based clubs: [Carlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_Football_Club), [Collingwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collingwood_Football_Club), [Essendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essendon_Football_Club), [Hawthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorn_Football_Club), [North Melbourne](22106.md), [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Football_Club), [St Kilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Kilda_Football_Club), and the [Western Bulldogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bulldogs).[^cite_note-198] The city hosts up to five AFL matches per round during the home-and-away season, attracting an average of 40,000 spectators per game.[^cite_note-199] The [AFL Grand Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Grand_Final), traditionally held at the MCG, is the [best-attended club championship event in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sports_attendance_figures).

In [soccer](10568.md), Melbourne is represented in the [A-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-League) by [Melbourne Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Victory), [Melbourne City FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_City_FC) and [Western United FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_FC), and in [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) it is home to the [National Rugby League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rugby_League) team, [Melbourne Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Storm). North American sports have also gained popularity in Melbourne: basketball sides [South East Melbourne Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_Melbourne_Phoenix) and [Melbourne United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_United) play in the [NBL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_League_\(Australia\)); the [Melbourne Ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Ice) and the [Melbourne Mustangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Mustangs) play in the [Australian Ice Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Ice_Hockey_League); and the [Melbourne Aces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Aces) play in the [Australian Baseball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Baseball_League). [Rowing](25736.md) also forms part of Melbourne's sporting identity, with a number of clubs located on the Yarra River, at which many Australian Olympians trained.

[Netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball), the biggest team sport in Australia, is another popular sport throughout the city.[^cite_note-200] Melbourne is represented by the [Melbourne Vixens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Vixens) and the [Melbourne Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Mavericks) in the [Suncorp Super Netball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suncorp_Super_Netball_League).

## Economy

See also: [Category\:Companies based in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Companies_based_in_Melbourne) and [Tourism in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Melbourne)



The 19th-century [Coop's Shot Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coop's_Shot_Tower) enclosed in [Melbourne Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Central_Shopping_Centre), one of the city's major retail hubs

Melbourne has a highly diversified economy with particular strengths in finance, manufacturing, research, IT, education, logistics, transportation and tourism. Melbourne houses the headquarters of many of Australia's largest corporations, including five of the ten largest in the country based on revenue, and five of the seven largest based on [market capitalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalisation)[^cite_note-201]: [ANZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_&_New_Zealand_Banking_Group), [BHP](200825.md), the [National Australia Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Australia_Bank), [CSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSL_Limited), and [Telstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telstra), as well as representative bodies and think tanks such as the [Business Council of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Council_of_Australia) and the [Australian Council of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Council_of_Trade_Unions). Melbourne's suburbs also have the head offices of [Coles Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coles_Group) (owner of [Coles Supermarkets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coles_Supermarkets)) and [Wesfarmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesfarmers) companies [Bunnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunnings), [Target](2307774.md), [K-Mart](1601117.md) and [Officeworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officeworks), as well as the head office for [Australia Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_Post). The city is home to Australia's second-busiest [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Melbourne), after [Port Botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Botany_\(seaport\)) in [Sydney](27862.md).[^cite_note-202] [Melbourne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Airport) provides an entry point for national and international visitors, and is Australia's second-busiest airport.[^cite_note-203]

Melbourne is also an important financial centre. In the 2024 [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index), Melbourne was ranked as having the 28th-most competitive financial centre in the world.[^cite_note-GFCI2-14] Two of the [big four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_\(banks\)) banks, the ANZ and National Australia Bank, are headquartered in Melbourne. The city has carved out a niche as Australia's leading centre for [superannuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superannuation_in_Australia) (pension) funds, with 40% of the total, and 65% of [industry super-funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_superannuation) including the AU\$109 billion Federal Government [Future Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Fund). The city was rated 41st within the top 50 financial cities as surveyed by the MasterCard Worldwide Centers of Commerce Index (2008),[^cite_note-204] second only to Sydney (12th) in Australia. Melbourne is Australia's second-largest industrial centre.[^cite_note-Wade2014-75]



The [Crown Casino and Entertainment Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Melbourne) generates AU\$2.7 billion in net revenue annually.

[^cite_note-205]

&#x20;Melbourne is the Australian base for several significant manufacturers, including [Boeing Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Australia), truck-makers [Kenworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenworth) and [Iveco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iveco), [Cadbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadbury), [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom), and [Jayco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayco). It is also home to a wide variety of other manufacturers, ranging from petrochemicals and pharmaceuticals to fashion garments, paper manufacturing and food processing.[^cite_note-206] The southeastern suburb of [Scoresby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoresby,_Victoria) is home to [Nintendo's Australian headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Australia). The city also features a research and development hub for [Ford Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Australia), as well as a global design studio and a technical centre for [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) and [Toyota Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Australia), respectively.

[CSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSL_Limited), one of the world's top five biotech companies, and [Sigma Pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Pharmaceuticals) have their headquarters in Melbourne. The two are the largest listed Australian pharmaceutical companies.[^cite_note-207] Melbourne's [ICT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communication_technology) industry is home to more than half of Australia's top 20 technology companies and employs over 91,000 people (one third of Australia's ICT workforce), with a turnover of AU\$34 billion and export revenues of AU\$2.5 billion in 2018.[^cite_note-208] Tourism plays an important role in Melbourne's economy, attracting 13.5 million domestic overnight tourists and 2.7 million international overnight tourists in the year ending March 2026.[^cite_note-Budget_Direct-209] Melbourne has been attracting an increasing share of domestic and international conference markets. Construction of an AU\$1 billion, 5,000-seat international convention centre, [Hilton Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_Hotel) and commercial precinct began in February 2006 adjacent to the [Melbourne Convention & Exhibition Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Convention_&_Exhibition_Centre) to link development along the [Yarra River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarra_River) with the [Southbank precinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southbank,_Victoria) and multibillion-dollar [Docklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Docklands) redevelopment.[^cite_note-210]

### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Melbourne)

See also: [Parks and gardens of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_gardens_of_Melbourne)



Known for its bars, [street art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_art_in_Melbourne) and coffee culture, the [inner city's network of laneways and arcades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanes_and_arcades_of_Melbourne) is a popular cultural attraction.

Melbourne is the second-most-visited city in Australia. In the 12-month to March 2026, 13.5 million domestic overnight tourists and 2.7 million international overnight tourists visited Melbourne.[^cite_note-Budget_Direct-209] The most visited attractions are [Federation Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_Square), [Queen Victoria Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria_Market), [Crown Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Melbourne), [Southbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southbank,_Victoria), [Melbourne Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Zoo), [Melbourne Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Aquarium), [Docklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docklands,_Victoria), [National Gallery of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Victoria), [Melbourne Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Museum), [Melbourne Observation Deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_Tower#Observation_deck_\(Melbourne_Skydeck\)), [Arts Centre Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_Centre_Melbourne), and the [Melbourne Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Ground).[^cite_note-211] In 2025, the [State Library of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Library_of_Victoria) was named the third-most popular and the seventh-most beautiful library in the world.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-temple-158] [Luna Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_Park,_Melbourne), a theme park modelled on New York's [Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island) and Seattle's [Luna Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_Park,_Seattle),[^cite_note-213] is also a popular destination for visitors.[^cite_note-214] In its annual survey of readers, the [Condé Nast Traveler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cond%C3%A9_Nast_Traveler) magazine found that both Melbourne and [Auckland](18660332.md) were considered the world's friendliest cities in 2014.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216] Melbourne's laneways and arcades are of particular importance for the city's tourism; [Hosier Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosier_Lane), for example, attracted one million visitors annually prior to the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-Scopelianos2020-179] The laneways of Melbourne have been gentrified and now feature prominent displays of [street art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_art) that attract global visitors. Melbourne is considered one of the world's safest cities for travellers.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218]



[Queen Victoria Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria_Market) is the Southern Hemisphere's largest open-air market.

&#x20;Melbourne has a renowned culinary scene that attracts international tourists.[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] [Lygon Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lygon_Street), which runs through the inner-northern suburbs of Melbourne, is a popular dining destination featuring an abundance of Italian and Greek restaurants that date back to earlier waves of European immigration to the city. Food festivals are particularly popular in Melbourne, many of which are held during early autumn, earning this period the nickname "mad March".[^cite_note-222] The most well-known of these events, the [Melbourne Food and Wine Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Food_and_Wine_Festival), takes place over the course of ten days and began in 1993.[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224]&#x20;



Established during the gold rush, [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Melbourne) is the longest continuous Chinese settlement outside Asia.

Melbourne is also home to many annual events and festivals. The [Melbourne International Comedy Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_International_Comedy_Festival) is held annually from March to April. Established in 1987, it is one of the three largest international comedy festivals in the world. Other notable festivals and events include the [Melbourne Flower and Garden Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_International_Flower_and_Garden_Show), the [Melbourne International Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_International_Jazz_Festival), the [Melbourne Royal Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Royal_Show) and the [Midsumma Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsumma_Festival).

[St Patrick's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick's_Cathedral,_Melbourne)

Melbourne has a wide range of religious faiths, the most widely held of which is [Christianity](5211.md). This is signified by the city's two large cathedrals—[St Patrick's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick's_Cathedral,_Melbourne) (Roman Catholic) and [St Paul's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Paul's_Cathedral,_Melbourne) (Anglican). Both were built in the [Victorian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era) and are of considerable heritage significance as major landmarks of the city.[^cite_note-236] In recent years, Greater Melbourne's irreligious community has grown to be one of the largest in Australia.[^cite_note-237]

According to the 2021 census, persons stating that they had [no religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) constituted 36.9% of the population.[^cite_note-Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics-2-234] [Christianity](5211.md) was the most popular religious affiliation at 40.1%.[^cite_note-Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics-2-234] The largest Christian denominations were [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Australia) (20.8%) and [Anglicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism_in_Australia) (5.5%).[^cite_note-Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics-2-234] The most popular non-Christian religious affiliations were [Islam](6037917.md) (5.3%), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) (4.1%), [Buddhism](3267529.md) (3.9%), [Sikhism](27964.md) (1.7%) and [Judaism](15624.md) (0.9%).[^cite_note-Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics-2-234]

Over 258,000 Muslims live in Melbourne.[^cite_note-id-235] Muslim religious life in Melbourne is centred on about 25 mosques and a number of prayer rooms on university campuses, workplaces and other venues.[^cite_note-238] As of 2000, Melbourne had the largest population of [Polish Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Jews) and Holocaust survivors in Australia, and the largest number of Jewish institutions.[^cite_note-239]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Melbourne)



[Ormond College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormond_College), part of the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne)

Of the top twenty high schools in Australia according to the My Choice Schools Ranking, five are in Melbourne.[^cite_note-240] There has also been a rapid increase in the number of international students studying in the city, with Melbourne considered the fifth-best student city in the world for studying abroad in the 2026 Best Student Cities ranking by [QS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QS_World_University_Rankings).[^cite_note-241] Eight public universities operate in Melbourne: the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), [Monash University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_University), [Swinburne University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swinburne_University_of_Technology), [Deakin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deakin_University), [Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology (RMIT University)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMIT_University), [La Trobe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trobe_University), [Australian Catholic University (ACU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Catholic_University) and [Victoria University (VU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Technology).

Melbourne universities have campuses throughout Australia and overseas. Swinburne University and Monash University have campuses in [Malaysia](3607937.md), RMIT in [Vietnam](202354.md), with Monash also having a campus in [Indonesia](14579.md) and research centres in [Prato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prato), Italy, and a joint partnership research academy with [IIT Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIT_Bombay) in [Mumbai](19189.md), India. The University of Melbourne, the second-oldest university in Australia,[^cite_note-242] is the highest-ranked university in the nation across the three major global rankings as of July 2025—QS (19th),[^cite_note-Top_Universities-243] [THES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Higher_Education) (39th),[^cite_note-244] and the Academic Ranking of World Universities (37th),[^cite_note-245] with Monash University also ranking within the top 50 in QS (36th).[^cite_note-Top_Universities-243] Both are members of the [Group of Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Eight_\(Australian_universities\)), a coalition of leading Australian tertiary institutions offering comprehensive and leading education.[^cite_note-246]

As of 2025, [RMIT University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMIT_University) is ranked 21st in the world in architecture.[^cite_note-247] [Swinburne University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swinburne_University_of_Technology), based in the inner-city Melbourne suburb of Hawthorn, was ranked 76th–100th in the world for physics by the Academic Ranking of World Universities as of 2014.[^cite_note-248] [Deakin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deakin_University) maintains two major campuses in Melbourne and Geelong, and is the third-largest university in Victoria. In recent years, the number of [international students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_student) at Melbourne's universities has risen rapidly, a result of an increasing number of places being made available for them.[^cite_note-249] Education in Melbourne is overseen by the Victorian [Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Education_and_Training_\(Victoria\)) (DET), whose role is to 'provide policy and planning advice for the delivery of education'.[^cite_note-250]

## Governance



[Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Melbourne)

The governance of Melbourne is split between the [government of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Victoria) and the [27 cities and four shires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Areas_of_Victoria#Municipalities_of_Greater_Melbourne) that make up the metropolitan area. There is no ceremonial or political head of Melbourne, but the [Lord Mayor of the City of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mayor_of_Melbourne) often fulfils such a role as a first among equals.[^cite_note-254]

The local governments are responsible for providing the functions set out in the *Local Government Act* 1989[^cite_note-255] such as [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) and [waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management). Most other government services are provided or regulated by the [Victorian state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_state_government), which governs from [Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Melbourne) in [Spring Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Street,_Melbourne). These encompass services typically associated with local government in other countries, such as public transport, main roads, traffic control, policing, education above preschool level, healthcare, and the planning of major infrastructure projects.

## Transport

### Roads

Main article: [Transport in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Melbourne)



The [Bolte Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolte_Bridge) is part of the [CityLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityLink) tollway system.

Like many Australian cities, Melbourne has a high [dependency on motor vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_dependency) for transport,[^cite_note-newman-256] particularly in outer suburban areas, where the largest number of cars are registered.[^cite_note-257] There are 3.6 million private vehicles using 22,320 km (13,870 mi) of road in Melbourne, which has one of the greatest lengths of road per capita in the world.[^cite_note-newman-256] The early 20th century saw an increase in the popularity of private motor vehicles, resulting in large-scale suburban expansion and a tendency towards the development of [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl)—as in all Australian capital cities, inhabitants typically lived in the suburbs and commuted to the city centre for work.[^cite_note-258] By the mid-1950s, there were just under 200 passenger vehicles per 1,000 people; by 2013, this figure had risen to 600 per 1,000 people.[^cite_note-259]

The [road network in Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_in_Victoria) is managed by the [Department of Transport and Planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Transport_and_Planning) (DTP), which oversees planning and integration. Road maintenance is undertaken by various bodies depending on road classification: local roads are maintained by local councils, secondary and main roads are the responsibility of the DTP, and major national freeways and corridors integral to trade are overseen by the federal government.[^cite_note-260]

Today, Melbourne has an extensive network of freeways and arterial roadways. These are used by private vehicles, including road freight vehicles, as well as road-based public transport modes like buses and taxis. Major highways feeding into the city include the [Eastern Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Freeway,_Melbourne), [Monash Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_Freeway) and [West Gate Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Gate_Freeway) (which spans the large [West Gate Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Gate_Bridge)). Other freeways include the [Calder Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Freeway), the [Tullamarine Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullamarine_Freeway) (the primary airport link), and the [Hume Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hume_Freeway), which connects Melbourne to Canberra and Sydney. Melbourne's middle suburbs are connected via an orbital freeway, the [M80 Ring Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M80_Ring_Road), which will be connected to the Eastern Freeway when the [North East Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_Link) opens.[^cite_note-261]

Of Melbourne's twenty declared freeways open or under construction, six are electronic toll roads. These include the [M1 and M2 CityLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityLink) (incorporating the [Bolte Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolte_Bridge)), [EastLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EastLink,_Melbourne), the [North East Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_Link), and the [West Gate Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Gate_Tunnel). Apart from [EastLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EastLink_\(Melbourne\)), which is owned and operated by [ConnectEast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConnectEast), toll roads in Melbourne are operated by the private company [Transurban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transurban). Unlike untolled roads that use green signage, Melbourne's tollways feature blue and yellow signage.

### Public transport

Main article: [Transport in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Melbourne)

Melbourne has an integrated public transport system based around extensive train, tram, bus and taxi systems. [Flinders Street station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flinders_Street_station) was the world's busiest passenger station in 1927 and Melbourne's tram network overtook Sydney's to become the world's largest in the 1940s. From the 1940s, public transport use in Melbourne declined due to a rapid expansion of the road and freeway network, with the largest declines in tram and bus usage.[^cite_note-Mees-2014-262] This decline quickened in the early 1990s due to large public transport service cuts.[^cite_note-Mees-2014-262] Melbourne's public transport operations were privatised in 1999 through a franchising model, with operational responsibilities for the train, tram and bus networks licensed to private companies.[^cite_note-263] After 1996, rapid employment growth in central Melbourne drove an increase in public transport patronage, with public transport accounting for 14.8% of work trips and 8.4% of all trips.[^cite_note-264][^cite_note-Mees-2014-262] A target of 20% public transport mode share for Melbourne by 2020 was set by the state government in 2006.[^cite_note-TransportFringe-265] Since 2006, patronage has grown by over 20%, and several major infrastructure projects have commenced to further expand public transport capacity and usage.[^cite_note-TransportFringe-265]

#### Train

Main article: [Railways in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_in_Melbourne)

See also: [Rail transport in Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Victoria)



Situated on the City Loop, [Southern Cross station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cross_station) is Victoria's main hub for regional and interstate trains.

The [Melbourne metropolitan rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_rail_network) dates back to the 1850s [gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_rush) era, and today consists of [227 suburban stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Melbourne_railway_stations) on sixteen lines which radiate from the [City Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Loop), a mostly underground subway system around the CBD. [Flinders Street station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flinders_Street_station), one of Australia's busiest [rail hubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_hub), serves all lines except the Pakenham, Cranbourne and Sunbury lines, which depart from nearby [Town Hall station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Hall_railway_station,_Melbourne). Flinders Street station remains a prominent Melbourne landmark and meeting place.[^cite_note-266] The city has rail connections with regional Victorian cities run by [V/Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V/Line), as well as direct interstate rail services which depart from Melbourne's other major rail terminus, [Southern Cross station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cross_station), in Docklands. *[The Overland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Overland)* to [Adelaide](1148.md) departs twice a week, while the [XPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales_XPT) to Sydney departs twice daily. In the 2017–2018 financial year, the Melbourne metropolitan rail network recorded 240.9 million passenger trips, the highest ridership in its history.[^cite_note-Public_Transport_Victoria-2018-267] Many rail lines, along with dedicated lines and [rail yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_yards), are also used for freight.

Several new railway lines are under construction or have recently been completed in Melbourne. A new AU\$15 billion [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit)[^cite_note-268] heavy rail corridor through the inner city—the [Metro Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Tunnel)—opened in late 2025 and became fully operational in February 2026. It comprises five new stations and twin nine-kilometre tunnels under the CBD, connecting the Sunbury line to the Cranbourne/Pakenham line.[^cite_note-269] The ongoing [Level Crossing Removal Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_Crossing_Removal_Project) is grade-separating much of the network and rebuilding many older stations. In June 2022, early works commenced on the [Suburban Rail Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburban_Rail_Loop), a 90-kilometre underground automated [orbital line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_line) through Melbourne's middle suburbs approximately 12–18 km (7.5–11.2 mi) from the [CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_CBD).[^cite_note-270] Additionally, early works for [an airport rail link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Airport_rail_link) have commenced in Keilor East.[^cite_note-271]

#### Tram

Main article: [Trams in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Melbourne)



A [D-class tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-class_Melbourne_tram) on [St Kilda Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Kilda_Road). [The city's tram network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Melbourne) consists of 493 trams and is the largest in the world.

[Melbourne's tram network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Melbourne) dates from the 1880s land boom and, as of 2021, consists of 250 km (155.3 mi) of double track, 475 trams, [25 routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Melbourne_tram_routes), and 1,763 [tram stops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram_stop), making it the largest in the world.[^cite_note-Yarra_Trams-272][^cite_note-DOT_tram-19][^cite_note-273] In 2017–2018, 206.3 million passenger trips were made by tram.[^cite_note-Public_Transport_Victoria-2018-267] About 75% of Melbourne's tram network shares road space with other vehicles, while the remainder of the network is grade-separated or operates on [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) tracks.[^cite_note-Yarra_Trams-272] Melbourne's trams are recognised as iconic cultural assets and a tourist attraction. [Heritage trams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W-class_Melbourne_tram) operate on the free [City Circle route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Circle_tram) around the CBD.[^cite_note-Metlink1-274] Trams are free within the central city Free Tram Zone and selected routes operate [24-hour services on weekends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Network_\(Melbourne\)).[^cite_note-275]

#### Bus

Main article: [Buses in Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses_in_Melbourne)

Melbourne's bus network consists of more than [400 routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Melbourne_bus_routes) which mainly service the outer suburbs and fill the gaps in the network between rail and tram services.[^cite_note-276][^cite_note-Metlink1-274][^cite_note-277] In the 2023–24 financial year, 114.9 million passenger trips were recorded on Melbourne's buses, an increase of 15.2% over the previous period.[^cite_note-278]

#### Airports

Main article: [List of airports in the Melbourne area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_the_Melbourne_area)

Melbourne is served by four airports. [Melbourne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Airport), located in [Tullamarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullamarine), is the city's primary international and domestic gateway and the second-busiest airport in Australia, recording passenger traffic of over 37 million in 2018–19.[^cite_note-279] The airport, which comprises four terminals,[^cite_note-280] is the home base for passenger airline [Jetstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetstar) and cargo airlines [Australian airExpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_airExpress) and [Team Global Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Global_Express), and is a major hub for [Qantas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qantas) and [Virgin Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Australia). [Avalon Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalon_Airport), located between Melbourne and [Geelong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geelong), is a secondary hub of Jetstar. It is also used as a freight and maintenance facility. Buses and taxis are the only forms of public transport to and from the city's main airports. A [rail link to Tullamarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Airport_rail_link) is planned to open in the 2030s.[^cite_note-281] Air ambulance facilities are available for the domestic and international medical transport of patients.[^cite_note-282] Melbourne also features a significant [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) airport, [Moorabbin Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorabbin_Airport), in the city's south-east, which also accommodates a limited number of passenger flights. [Essendon Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essendon_Airport), once the city's principal airport, handles regional passenger flights, general aviation, and air cargo services.[^cite_note-283]

#### Water transport

Maritime shipping is an essential component of Melbourne's freight and transport system. The [Port of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Melbourne) is Australia's largest container and general cargo port and also its busiest, handling more than one-third of the nation's container trade. The port processed 3.39 million twenty-foot equivalent units (TEUs) in 2024, making it one of the top five ports in the Southern Hemisphere.[^cite_note-284] [Station Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_Pier) on Port Phillip Bay serves as the city's primary passenger terminal, accommodating visiting [cruise ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_ships). Ferries and water taxis run from [berths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berth_\(moorings\)) along the Yarra River as far upstream as South Yarra and across Port Phillip Bay.

## Infrastructure

### Health



[Royal Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Children's_Hospital)

Among Australian capital cities, Melbourne ties with Canberra in first place for the highest male life expectancy (80.0 years) and ranks second behind Perth in female life expectancy (84.1 years).[^cite_note-285] The Victorian Government's [Department of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health_\(Victoria\)) oversees about 30 public hospitals in the Melbourne metropolitan region and 13 health services organisations.[^cite_note-286]

Major medical, [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience), and [biotechnology](4502.md) research institutions located in Melbourne include [St Vincent's Institute of Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Vincent's_Institute_of_Medical_Research?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Australian Stem Cell Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Stem_Cell_Centre), the [Burnet Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnet_Institute), the [Peter Doherty Institute for Infection and Immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doherty_Institute), [Australian Regenerative Medicine Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Regenerative_Medicine_Institute), Victorian Institute of Chemical Sciences, Brain Research Institute, [Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_MacCallum_Cancer_Centre), the [Walter and Eliza Hall Institute of Medical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_and_Eliza_Hall_Institute_of_Medical_Research), and the Melbourne Neuropsychiatry Centre.

The headquarters of Australian pharmaceutical company [CSL Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSL_Limited) is located in the Melbourne Biomedical Precinct in [Parkville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkville,_Victoria), which contains over 40 biomedical and research institutions.[^cite_note-287] It was announced in 2021 that a new Australian Institute for Infectious Disease would also be built in Parkville.[^cite_note-288] Other institutions include the [Howard Florey Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Florey_Institute), the [Murdoch Children's Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murdoch_Children's_Research_Institute), [Baker Heart and Diabetes Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Heart_and_Diabetes_Institute), and the [Australian Synchrotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Synchrotron).[^cite_note-289] Many of these institutions are associated with and located near universities. Melbourne is also home to the [Royal Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Children's_Hospital) and the [Monash Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_Children's_Hospital).

### Utilities

Main article: [Energy in Victoria (Australia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Victoria_\(Australia\))



Sugarloaf Reservoir at [Christmas Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Hills,_Victoria) in the metropolitan area is one of Melbourne's closest water supplies.

Water storage and supply for Melbourne is managed by [Melbourne Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Water), which is owned by the Victorian Government. The organisation is also responsible for the management of Melbourne's sewerage network and major regional water catchments, as well as the [Wonthaggi desalination plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonthaggi_desalination_plant) and the [North–South Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Pipeline). Water is stored in a series of reservoirs located within and outside the Greater Melbourne area. The largest dam, the [Thomson River Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_River_Dam), located in the Victorian Alps, is capable of holding about 60% of Melbourne's water capacity,[^cite_note-290] while smaller dams such as the [Upper Yarra Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Yarra_Dam), [Yan Yean Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan_Yean_Reservoir), and the [Cardinia Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinia_Reservoir) carry secondary supplies.

Gas is provided by three distribution companies:

- [AusNet Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AusNet_Services), which provides gas from Melbourne's inner western suburbs to southwestern Victoria.

  [^cite_note-smh05172013-291]

- [Multinet Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinet_Gas), which provides gas from Melbourne's inner eastern suburbs to eastern Victoria (owned by SP AusNet after acquisition, but continuing to trade under the brand name Multinet Gas).

  [^cite_note-age040917-292]

- [Australian Gas Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Gas_Networks), which provides gas from Melbourne's inner northern suburbs to northern Victoria, as well as the majority of southeastern Victoria.

  [^cite_note-age040917-292]

  [^cite_note-ABCDeepTime-23]

Electricity is provided by five distribution companies:

- [Citipower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citipower), which provides power to Melbourne's CBD and some inner suburbs.

  [^cite_note-spark-293]

- [Powercor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powercor), which provides power to the outer western suburbs as well as all of western [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)) (Citipower and Powercor are owned by the same entity).

  [^cite_note-spark-293]

- [Jemena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemena), which provides power to the northern and inner western suburbs.

  [^cite_note-alinta-294]

- [United Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alinta), which provides power to the inner eastern and southeastern suburbs and the [Mornington Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mornington_Peninsula).

  [^cite_note-alinta-294]

- [AusNet Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AusNet_Services), which provides power to the outer eastern suburbs and all of the north and east of Victoria.

  [^cite_note-smh05172013-291]
