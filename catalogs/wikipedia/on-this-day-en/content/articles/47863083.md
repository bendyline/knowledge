The **Election Commission of India** (**ECI**) is a [constitutional body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_body) that is empowered to conduct free and fair elections in India. Established by the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India), it is headed by a [chief election commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Election_Commissioner_of_India) and consists of two other [election commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commissioner_of_India) as constituent members. The commission is headquartered in [New Delhi](51585.md).

The election commissioners are appointed by the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) on the recommendation of a selection committee headed by the [prime minister](24452.md). The term of the chief election commissioner (CEC) can be a maximum of six years provided they do not attain the age of sixty-five years before the expiry of the term. CEC can only be removed by impeachment in the [parliament](410238.md), while election commissioners can be removed by the president on the recommendation of the CEC.

The election commission decides the dates for the filing of nominations, voting, counting and announcement of results for all the elections. At the states and union territories, the elections are supervised by a chief electoral officer, under the direction of the Election Commission. At the district and constituency levels, election related work is carried out by the district election officers, electoral registration officers and returning officers. The commission carries out the registration and recognition of political parties in the country. It prepares electoral rolls, and issues identification for eligible voters. It is also responsible for scrutinizing candidates, allotting ballot symbols, issuing a model code of conduct to be followed by the political parties and candidates, and monitoring election spends.

## Structure

See also: [Chief Election Commissioner of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Election_Commissioner_of_India) and [Election Commissioner of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commissioner_of_India)

In 1950, the Election Commission of India was established as a single member body. As per *The Election Commissioner Amendment Act, 1989*, the commission was made a multi-member body headed by a [chief election commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Election_Commissioner_of_India) and two other [election commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commissioner_of_India), who were appointed to the commission for the first time on 16 October 1989. On 1 January 1990, it reverted back to a single member body after the post of election commissioner was abolished, before being restored to the three member structure on 1 October 1993.[^cite_note-CEC-7][^cite_note-ECI-3] The commission is headed by the chief election commissioner and consists of two other election commissioners.[^cite_note-ECI-3][^cite_note-8] The chief election commissioner does not have overruling powers and any decision is taken by the opinion of the majority among the three.[^cite_note-ECI-3][^cite_note-9] The commission is assisted by directors generals, principal secretaries, and other officers. The commission is headquartered at Nirvachan Sadan in [New Delhi](51585.md).[^cite_note-ECI-3]

At the [states and union territories](375986.md), the elections are supervised by a chief electoral officer appointed by the Election Commission. At the district and constituency levels, election related work is carried out by the district election officers, electoral registration officers and returning officers.[^cite_note-ECI-3]

## Appointment and term of office

The appointment and term of the election commissioner is prescribed in the [Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Election_Commissioner_and_Other_Election_Commissioners_\(Appointment,_Conditions_of_Service_and_Term_of_Office\)_Act,_2023). As per the Section 7 of the act, an election commissioner is appointed by the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) on the recommendation of a selection committee headed by the [prime minister of India](24452.md) and consisting of the [leader of the opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(India\)) in [Lok Sabha](275456.md) and a member of the [Union Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers) to be nominated by the Prime Minister.[^cite_note-10] They were earlier appointed by the president on the recommendation of the prime minister. In March 2023, the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) ruled that the appointments shall be made by a committee consisting of the prime minister, leader of opposition and the [chief justice of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India) and the process would be in place until a new law is enacted with regard to the same.[^cite_note-11] The new law enacted in 2023, replaced the chief justice with a member appointed by the prime minister in the selection committee,[^cite_note-12] thus giving the ruling government a dominant role in the appointment of election commissioners.[^cite_note-13]

The term of the CEC can be a maximum of six years from the date on which they assume their office. However, the CEC retires from office if they attain the age of sixty-five years before the expiry of the term.[^cite_note-CEC-7] While the CEC can only be removed by office through the process of impeachment requiring two-thirds majority of the Lok Sabha and the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md) to be present and voting for the same, election commissioners can be removed by the President on the recommendation of the CEC.[^cite_note-CEC-7]

## Powers and functions

See also: [Election Commission of India's Model Code of Conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_India's_Model_Code_of_Conduct)

The Election Commission of India is a body constitutionally empowered to conduct free and fair elections to the [national](410238.md), the [State Legislative Assemblies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_assemblies_of_India), [State Legislative Councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_councils_of_India) and the offices of the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) and [vice-president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-President_of_India).[^cite_note-ECI-3][^cite_note-14] The Election Commission operates under the powers granted by [Article 324](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_XV_of_the_Constitution_of_India) of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) and subsequently enacted [Representation of the People Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_the_People_Act_\(India\)).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The [State Election Commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_election_commission_\(India\)) are independent constitutional bodies vested with the responsibility of conducting elections to the [local bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_India) in their respective at the [states and union territories](375986.md).[^cite_note-CEC-7] The election commission decides the dates for the filing of nominations, voting, counting and announcement of results.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

It issues a [Model Code of Conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commission_of_India's_Model_Code_of_Conduct) for political parties and candidates to ensure that the elections are conducted in a free and fair manner.[^cite_note-19] The Code of Conduct was issued for the first time in 1971 for the [5th Lok Sabha elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_general_election,_1971) and has revised it from time to time. It lays down guidelines for the conduct of political parties and candidates during an election period.[^cite_note-20] Instances of violation of the code by various political parties and misuse of official machinery by the candidates are dealt according to the law.[^cite_note-21]

A law for the registration process for political parties was enacted in 1989.[^cite_note-22] The registration ensures that the political parties are recognized as national, state and regional parties. The election commission has the right to allot symbols to the political parties depending on the status. The same symbol cannot be allocated to two political parties even if they do not contest in the region.[^cite_note-23]

The commission prepares electoral rolls and updates the voter list. To prevent electoral fraud, Electors Photo Identity Cards (EPIC) were introduced in 1993. However certain legal documents such as [ration cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ration_card_\(India\)) have been allowed for voting in certain situations.[^cite_note-24]

The commission is empowered to prohibit the dissemination or publication of voting trends that seek to influence voters by [opinion polls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polls) or [exit polls](665947.md).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

The Election Commission is responsible for scrutinizing and accepting the applications of the candidates willing to contest in the elections. A person can be disqualified from contesting the elections if incorrect or incomplete information is provided in the affidavits and if they have been convicted by any court in India in which a jail term of two or more years has been awarded.[^cite_note-28] In 2017, the Election Commission supported the case for a lifetime ban on convicted felons from contesting elections in an affidavit filed in the [Supreme Court](395852.md).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

The Election Commission sets limits on poll related expenditure by the candidates during election campaigns. The commission appoints officers of [Indian Revenue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Revenue_Service) from the Income Tax Department as election observers.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The commission takes details of the candidate's assignment in an affidavit at the time of submitting the nomination paper, and they are also required to give details of their expenditure within 30 days of the declaration of results.[^cite_note-33]

The election commission operates various electronic media including [websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Website) and [mobile applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_applications) for enabling various functions such as addressing grievances, checking electoral rolls, disseminating information on candidates, announcement of results and monitoring of assigned tasks.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

## Voting

Further information: [Electronic voting in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voting_in_India)



[Electronic voting machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voting_machines) (EVMs) with [Voter-verified paper audit trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter-verified_paper_audit_trail) (VVPAT)

Voting in India is done using [electronic voting machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voting_machines) (EVMs) and there are provisions for [postal voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_voting#India) and special arrangements for disabled people.[^cite_note-ECI-3]

Electronic voting machines (EVM) were introduced by the Election Commission to reduce malpractices and improve efficiency. The EVMs were first trialed in 1982 in the by-election to [Paravur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paravur_\(State_Assembly_constituency\)) assembly constituency in [Kerala](4349459.md) in a limited number of polling stations.[^cite_note-Intro-36] After successful testing and legal inquiries, the commission decided to introduce these voting machines on a large scale.[^cite_note-ECI-3]

EVMs are manufactured by two [public sector undertakings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector_undertakings): [Bharat Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Electronics) and [Electronics Corporation of India Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_Corporation_of_India_Limited).[^cite_note-37]

[Voter-verified paper audit trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter-verified_paper_audit_trail) (VVPAT) was introduced on a trail basis in a by-poll in September 2013 in [Noksen (Assembly Constituency)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noksen_\(Assembly_Constituency\)) in [Nagaland](408677.md).[^cite_note-38] It was later used in various [legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Assembly_elections_in_India) and in eight Lok Sabha constituencies in [2014 Indian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Indian_general_election).[^cite_note-39]



NOTA Voting Symbol in India

In 2014, [none of the above](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/None_of_the_above) (NOTA) was also added as an option on the voting machines which is now a mandatory option to be provided in any election.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] The specific symbol for NOTA, a ballot paper with a black cross across it, was introduced on 18 September 2015.[^cite_note-42] Photo electoral rolls with photographs of the candidates on the EVMs were first introduced in the [2015 Bihar Legislative Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Bihar_Legislative_Assembly_election).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

Election Commission organised an open hackathon on 3 June 2017 encouraging people to attempt hacking of EVMs used by the commission in various Indian elections.[^cite_note-45] While none of them participated, functioning of the EVM and VVPAT machines were demonstrated in the event.[^cite_note-46]

[Postal voting in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_voting#India) is done only through electronically transmitted postal ballot papers (ETPB). Ballot papers are distributed to the registered eligible voters who return the votes by post. Postal votes are counted first before the counting of votes from the EVM. Only certain categories of people are eligible to register as postal voters. Employees working in the union armed forces and state police as well as their spouses, and those working for the [Government of India](553883.md) who are officially posted abroad can register for the postal vote. People in [preventive detention](2896261.md) can use postal vote while prisoners are not allowed to vote.[^cite_note-47] The Election Commission of India has granted permission for individuals aged 80 and above and those with physical challenges to cast their votes from their homes.[^cite_note-48]

The Election Commission of India did not have data with regard to disabilities of voters as ascertained by a [RTI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_Information_Act,_2005) application filed in 2014.[^cite_note-49] The commission offered sign language support to assist voters with speech and hearing impairment.[^cite_note-50]
