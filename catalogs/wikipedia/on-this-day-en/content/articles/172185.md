The **World Economic Forum** (**WEF**) is an [international advocacy non-governmental organization](4142591.md) and [think tank](37101.md), based in [Cologny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologny), Canton of Geneva, Switzerland. It was founded on 24 January 1971 by German engineer [Klaus Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Schwab). The foundation's stated mission is "improving the state of the world by engaging business, political, academic, and other leaders of society to shape global, regional, and industry agendas".[^cite_note-1]

The WEF hosts an annual meeting at the end of January in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos), a mountain resort in the Swiss canton of [Graubünden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graub%C3%BCnden), in the eastern [Alps](981.md) region. The meeting brings together some 3,000 paying members and selected participants – among whom are investors, business leaders, political leaders, [economists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist), celebrities and journalists – for up to five days to discuss [global issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_global_issues) across 500 sessions.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The foundation is mostly funded by its 1,000-member [multi-national companies](214491.md).[^cite_note-4]

Aside from Davos, the organization convenes regional conferences, it produces a series of reports, engages its members in sector-specific initiatives[^cite_note-pigman1-5] and provides a platform for leaders from selected stakeholder groups to collaborate on projects and initiatives.[^cite_note-6]

The World Economic Forum and its annual meeting in Davos have received criticism over the years, including allegations of the organization's corporate capture of global and democratic institutions, institutional [whitewashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewashing_\(communications\)) initiatives, the public cost of security, the organization's [tax-exempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax-exempt) status, unclear decision processes and membership criteria, a lack of financial transparency, and the [environmental footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_footprint) of its annual meetings.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

The WEF was founded in 1971 by [Klaus Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Schwab), a business professor at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva).[^cite_note-pigman2-9] First named the **European Management Forum**, it changed its name to the World Economic Forum in 1987 and sought to broaden its vision to include providing a platform for resolving international conflicts.[^cite_note-wef40-10]

In February 1971, Schwab invited 450 executives from [Western European](33800.md) firms to the first European Management Symposium held in the [Davos Congress Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos_Congress_Centre) under the patronage of the [European Commission](9974.md) and European industrial associations, where Schwab sought to introduce European firms to American management practices.[^cite_note-11] He then founded the WEF as a nonprofit organization based in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) and drew European business leaders to [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos) for the annual meetings each January.[^cite_note-12]

The second European Management Forum in 1972 was the first meeting where a [head of government](37570.md) featured as a speaker, Prime Minister [Pierre Werner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Werner) of [Luxembourg](17515.md).[^cite_note-13]

Events in 1973, including the collapse of the [Bretton Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretton_Woods_system) fixed-exchange rate mechanism and the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War), saw the annual meeting expand its focus from management to economic and social issues, and, for the first time, political leaders were invited to the annual meeting in January 1974.[^cite_note-14]

Through the forum's first decade, it maintained a playful atmosphere, with many members skiing and participating in evening events. Appraising the 1981 event, one attendee noted that "the forum offers a delightful vacation on the expense account".[^cite_note-15]

Political leaders soon began to use the annual meeting as venue for promoting their interests. The *Davos Declaration* was signed in 1988 by [Greece](12108.md) and [Turkey](11125639.md), helping them turn back from the brink of war. In 1992, South African president [F. W. de Klerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._W._de_Klerk) met with [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela) and [Chief Mangosuthu Buthelezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangosuthu_Buthelezi) at the annual meeting, their first joint appearance outside South Africa. At the 1994 annual meeting, Israeli foreign minister [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres) and [PLO chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Palestine_Liberation_Organization) [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat) reached a draft agreement on [Gaza](55387.md) and [Jericho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho).[^cite_note-16] The "Davos Pact" of 1996 saw the forum's elite help [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin) retain [power as president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Russian_presidential_election) of the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation) over the then-presumptive favorite [Gennady Zyuganov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennady_Zyuganov), leader of the [Communist Party of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Russian_Federation).[^cite_note-bs1-17]

After 9/11, the WEF was held in the US, in New York City, for the first time.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-\:0-19] And in January 2003, US secretary of state Powell went to the forum to drum up sympathy for the [global war on terrorism](13425800.md) and the US's impending invasion of Iraq.[^cite_note-\:0-19][^cite_note-20]

In October 2004, the World Economic Forum gained attention through the resignation of its CEO[^cite_note-21] and executive director [José María Figueres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Figueres) over the undeclared receipt of more than US\$900,000 in consultancy fees from the French telecommunications firm [Alcatel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcatel-Lucent).[^cite_note-22] [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International) highlighted this incident in their [Global Corruption Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Corruption_Report) two years later in 2006.[^cite_note-23]

In January 2006, the WEF published an article in its *Global Agenda* magazine titled "[Boycott Israel](43473050.md)", which was distributed to all 2,340 participants of the annual meeting.[^cite_note-24] Following the publication, Klaus Schwab described the publication as "an unacceptable failure in the editorial process".[^cite_note-25]

In late 2015, the invitation was extended to include a [North Korean](21255.md) delegation for the 2016 WEF, "in view of positive signs coming out of the country", the WEF organizers noted. North Korea has not been attending the WEF since 1998. The invitation was accepted.[^cite_note-26] However, WEF revoked the invitation on 13 January 2016, after the [6 January 2016 North Korean nuclear test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2016_North_Korean_nuclear_test), and the country's attendance was made subject to "existing and possible forthcoming sanctions".[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Despite protests by [North Korea](21255.md) calling the decision by the WEF managing board a "sudden and irresponsible" move, the WEF committee maintained the exclusion because "under these circumstances there would be no opportunity for international dialogue".[^cite_note-29]

In 2017, the WEF in Davos attracted considerable attention when, for the first time, a head of state from the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) was present at the alpine resort. With the backdrop of [Brexit](41688778.md), an incoming [protectionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectionism) US administration and significant pressures on [free-trade zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-trade_zone) and [trade agreements](217089.md), [Paramount leader](262766.md) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) defended the global economic scheme, and portrayed China as a responsible nation and a leader for environmental causes. He sharply rebuked the current populist movements that would introduce tariffs and hinder global commerce, warning that such protectionism could foster isolation and reduced economic opportunity.[^cite_note-30]

In 2018, [Indian](14533.md) prime minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) gave the keynote speech, becoming the first head of government from India to deliver the inaugural keynote for the annual plenary at Davos. Modi highlighted global warming ([climate change](5042951.md)), terrorism and protectionism as the three major global challenges, and expressed confidence that they can be tackled with collective effort.[^cite_note-31]

In 2019, [Brazilian](3383.md) president [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md) gave the keynote address at the plenary session of the conference. On his first international trip to Davos, he emphasized liberal economic policies despite his populist agenda, and attempted to reassure the world that [Brazil](3383.md) is a protector of the [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest) while utilizing its resources for food production and export. He stated that "his government will seek to better integrate Brazil into the world by mainstreaming international best practices, such as those adopted and promoted by the [OECD](33853117.md)".[^cite_note-32] Environmental concerns like extreme weather events, and the failure of [climate change mitigation](2119179.md) and [adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation) were among the top-ranking global risks expressed by WEF attendees.[^cite_note-33] On 13 June 2019, the WEF and the [United Nations](31769.md) signed a "Strategic Partnership Framework" in order to "jointly accelerate the implementation of the [2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2030_Agenda_for_Sustainable_Development)".[^cite_note-34]

The 2021 World Economic Forum was due to be held from 17 to 20 August in [Singapore](27318.md).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] However, on 17 May, the forum was cancelled; with a new meeting to take place in the first half of 2022 instead with a final location and date to be determined later in 2021.[^cite_note-38]

In late December 2021, the World Economic Forum said in a release that pandemic conditions had made it extremely difficult to stage a global in-person meeting the following month; transmissibility of the [SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2_Omicron_variant) and its impact on travel and mobility had made deferral necessary, with the meeting in Davos eventually rescheduled for 22 to 26 May 2022.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]



German chancellor [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) and Azerbaijani president [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) in January 2025

Topics in the 2022 annual meeting included the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), climate change, energy insecurity and [inflation](38286.md). Ukraine's president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) gave a special address at the meeting,[^cite_note-41] thanking the global community for its efforts but also calling for more support.[^cite_note-42] The 2022 forum was marked by the absence of a Russian delegation for the first time since 1991, which *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* described as signalling the "unraveling of globalization".[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The former Russia House was used to present Russia's war crimes.[^cite_note-45]

The 2023 annual meeting of the World Economic Forum took place in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos), Switzerland, from 16 to 20 January under the theme "Cooperation in a fragmented world".[^cite_note-DavosMeeting2023-46]

On 21 April 2025 Klaus Schwab, Chairman of the Board of the World Economic Forum, informed the Board: "Following my recent announcement and as I enter my 88th year, I have decided to step down from my position as Chair and as a member of the Board of Trustees, with immediate effect."[^cite_note-47]

In August 2025, the Forum faced renewed scrutiny after whistleblowers alleged financial irregularities and a toxic work environment. Interim chair [Peter Brabeck-Letmathe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Brabeck-Letmathe), former CEO of [Nestlé](160227.md), resigned citing his personal observations of such conditions.[^cite_note-48] Although an internal investigation by the Zurich-based law firm Homburger and US firm Covington & Burling found no evidence of "material wrongdoing" by Schwab or his wife Hilde, the Board pledged to strengthen governance structures.[^cite_note-49] At the same time, the Forum appointed [Larry Fink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Fink), CEO of [BlackRock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackRock), and [André Hoffmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Hoffmann_\(businessman\)), vice-chair of [Roche Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roche_Holding), as interim co-chairs of its board.[^cite_note-50]

In February 2026, Børge Brende, head of the World Economic Forum, resigned over his relationship with convicted sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

## Organization

Headquartered in Cologny, the WEF also has offices in New York, Beijing, Tokyo and Seoul. In January 2015, it was designated an NGO with "other international body" status by the Swiss Federal Government under the Swiss Host-State Act.[^cite_note-53]

On 10 October 2016, the WEF announced the opening of its new Center for the Fourth Industrial Revolution in San Francisco. According to the WEF, the center will "serve as a platform for interaction, insight and impact on the scientific and technological changes that are changing the way we live, work and relate to one another".[^cite_note-54] WEF has 19 such centers spread across [Africa](5334607.md), [Asia](689.md), [Europe](9239.md), [North America](21139.md) and [South America](26769.md).[^cite_note-55]

The World Economic Forum declares that it is impartial and that it is not tied to any political, [partisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisan_\(political\)), or national interests.[^cite_note-56] Until 2012, it had [observer status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observer_status) with the [United Nations Economic and Social Council](31958.md); it is under the supervision of the [Swiss Federal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Council). The foundation's highest governance body is the foundation board.[^cite_note-57]

The managing board is chaired by the WEF's president and CEO and acts as the executive body of the World Economic Forum. Managing board members are WEF's president and CEO, Julien Gattoni, Jeremy Jurgens, [Adrian Monck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Monck), Sarita Nayyar, Olivier M. Schwab, [Saadia Zahidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadia_Zahidi), and Alois Zwinggi.[^cite_note-58]

### Membership

The foundation is funded by its 1,000 member companies, typically global enterprises with more than five billion dollars in turnover (varying by industry and region). These enterprises rank among the top companies within their industry and/or country and play a leading role in shaping the future of their industry and/or region. Membership is stratified by the level of engagement with forum activities, with the level of membership fees increasing as participation in meetings, projects, and initiatives rises.[^cite_note-62] In 2011, an annual membership cost \$52,000 for an individual member, \$263,000 for "Industry Partner" and \$527,000 for "Strategic Partner". An admission fee costs \$19,000 per person.[^cite_note-63] In 2014, WEF raised annual fees by 20 percent, bringing the cost for "Strategic Partner" from CHF 500,000 (\$523,000) to CHF 600,000 (\$628,000).[^cite_note-64]

## Activities

### Annual meeting in Davos



A sports shop turned into a temporary informal reception location "Caspian Week", WEF 2018.

The flagship event of the World Economic Forum is the invitation-only annual meeting held at the end of January in Davos, Switzerland, bringing together chief executive officers from its 1,000 member companies, as well as selected politicians, representatives from academia, [NGOs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGOs), religious leaders, and the media in an [alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Alps) environment. The winter discussions ostensibly focus around key issues of global concern (such as the [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization), capital markets, wealth management, international conflicts, environmental problems and their possible solutions).[^cite_note-pigman1-5][^cite_note-65] The participants also take part in role playing events, such as the Investment Heat Map.[^cite_note-66] Informal winter meetings may have led to as many ideas and solutions as the official sessions.[^cite_note-67] In addition to the official programme inside the Congress Hall, numerous independent (by invitation only) events are hosted by governments, corporations, and civil-society organisations across Davos. These include the FT/CNBC Nightcap[^cite_note-68], Open Forum[^cite_note-69], the Swedish Lunch[^cite_note-70], Goals House[^cite_note-71], and SAP House[^cite_note-72] and [Avicii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avicii) Tribute Concert for Mental Health Awareness.[^cite_note-73]

At the annual meeting, usually 3,000 participants from nearly 110 countries participate in over 400 sessions. Participation included more than 340 public figures, including more than 70 heads of state and government and 45 heads of international organizations; 230 media representatives and almost 40 cultural leaders were represented.[^cite_note-74]



Inside the Media Centre, 2007

As many as 500 journalists from online, print, radio, and television take part, with access to all sessions in the official program, some of which are also [webcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webcast).[^cite_note-75] Not all the journalists are given access to all areas, however. This is reserved for white badge holders. "Davos runs an almost caste-like system of badges", according to BBC journalist Anthony Reuben. "A white badge means you're one of the delegates – you might be the chief executive of a company or the leader of a country (although that would also get you a little holographic sticker to add to your badge), or a senior journalist. An orange badge means you're just a run-of-the-mill working journalist."[^cite_note-76] Since 2024 the WEF launched a new badge system where Accredited Badges are issued. These badges have access to the Ice Village, which is a smaller version of the official Congress Hall.[^cite_note-77] All [plenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenary_session) debates from the annual meeting also are available on YouTube[^cite_note-78] while photographs are available on [Flickr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flickr).[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

#### Secure hotels and secure zone

The town of Davos is designated as a high-security zone during the annual meeting, with extensive protective measures led by the Swiss authorities. Security operations include airspace monitoring, military deployments, and the protection of key sites and official delegations. The Swiss Armed Forces support the Canton of Graubünden with troops, infrastructure, and specialised units to safeguard participants and residents. Access to parts of the town is restricted, with secure hotels and controlled zones requiring specific accreditation to enter.[^cite_note-90]

A number of hotels in Davos are designated as "secure hotels", providing controlled access and heightened protection for heads of state, ministers and senior officials. The specific hotels receiving this status vary from year to year, but have included the Belvedere Hotel, the Alpengold Hotel (formerly the InterContinental), the Seehof Hotel, and the Hilton Garden Inn. These properties fall within the Secure Zone (Promenade 95 – 101) and require special accreditation for entry (Secure Hotel Badge), functioning as protected accommodation and meeting zones for official delegations during the World Economic Forum.[^cite_note-91] The Belvedere also hosts the Main Stage, a dedicated venue for high-level discussions and gatherings during the Annual Meeting. Located within the secure hotel environment, the space brings together world leaders, government and other participants for engagements outside the Congress Centre.[^cite_note-92]

#### 2025 meeting

The World Economic Forum 2025 took place in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos), Switzerland, from 20 to 24 January, under the theme **Collaboration for the Intelligent Age**.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] The event brought together approximately 3,000 global leaders from over 125 countries, including 350 heads of state and government, business executives, policymakers, and representatives from international organizations.[^cite_note-95] Discussions focused on [geopolitical stability](163225.md), economic resilience, [climate change](5042951.md),[^cite_note-96] [artificial intelligence](1164.md) governance, and inclusive economic growth. Sessions covered topics such as the future of [global trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_trade), [energy transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition), and the impact of artificial intelligence and [automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation) on the labor market. Several initiatives were introduced, including policy frameworks for AI regulation, [climate financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_finance) mechanisms, and economic strategies for sustainable development.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

Among the initiatives discussed was the Global India Dialogues, launched by the Motwani Jadeja Foundation, which focused on [India](14533.md)’s role in global geopolitics, technology, and innovation.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] Discussions on [gender equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_equality)[^cite_note-101] and economic inclusion were also highlighted through initiatives such as the **Global Good Alliance for Gender Equity and Equality**, which explored the economic impact of investments in [women's health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_health).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] The event featured key figures such as [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md), [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken),[^cite_note-104] [Christian Lindner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Lindner),[^cite_note-105] and [Sam Altman](22476815.md),[^cite_note-106] alongside representatives from the [United Nations](31769.md),[^cite_note-107] [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), and [World Bank](36752.md).[^cite_note-108] The forum underscored the importance of international cooperation in addressing global economic and technological challenges.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

#### Corporate participants

Next to individual participants, the World Economic Forum maintains a dense network of corporate partners that can apply for different partnership ranks within the forum.[^cite_note-114] For 2019, [Bloomberg](432006.md) has identified a total of 436 listed corporates that participated in the annual meeting while measuring a stock underperformance by the Davos participants of around −10% versus the S\&P 500 during the same year. Drivers are among others an overrepresentation of financial companies and an underrepresentation of fast-growing health care and information technology businesses at the conference.[^cite_note-115] *The Economist* had found similar results in an earlier study, showing an underperformance of Davos participants against both the MSCI World Index and the S\&P 500 between 2009 and 2014.[^cite_note-Econ-Magic-113]

### Summer annual meeting

In 2007, the foundation established the Annual Meeting of the New Champions (also called Summer Davos), held annually in China, alternating between [Dalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalian) and [Tianjin](50821.md), bringing together 1,500 participants from what the foundation calls Global Growth Companies, primarily from rapidly growing emerging countries such as China, Russia, [Mexico](3966054.md), and [Brazil](3383.md), but also including quickly growing companies from [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_countries). The meeting also engages with the next generation of global leaders from fast-growing regions and competitive cities, as well as technology pioneers from around the globe.[^cite_note-116] The [premier of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) has delivered a plenary address at each annual meeting.

### Regional meetings



[Prithviraj Chavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prithviraj_Chavan), chief minister of Maharashtra, India; Sudha Pilay, member-secretary, Planning Commission, India; and [Ben Verwaayen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Verwaayen), chief executive officer, Alcatel-Lucent, France, were the co-chairs of the India Economic Summit 2011 in [Mumbai](19189.md).

Every year regional meetings take place, enabling close contact among corporate business leaders, local government leaders, and NGOs. Meetings are held in Africa, East Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East. The mix of hosting countries varies from year to year, but consistently China and India have hosted throughout the decade since 2000.[^cite_note-117]

### Social entrepreneurs

Since 2000, the WEF has been promoting models developed by those in close collaboration with the [Schwab Foundation for Social Entrepreneurship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwab_Foundation_for_Social_Entrepreneurship),[^cite_note-120] highlighting [social entrepreneurship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_entrepreneurship) as a key element to advance societies and address social problems.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] Selected social entrepreneurs are invited to participate in the foundation's regional meetings and the annual meetings where they may meet chief executives and senior government officials. At the annual meeting 2003, for example, [Jeroo Billimoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeroo_Billimoria) met with Roberto Blois, deputy secretary-general of the [International Telecommunication Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Telecommunication_Union), an encounter that produced a key partnership for her organization [Child Helpline International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_Helpline_International).[^cite_note-123]

### Research reports



Two-time [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award) winner and Pakistani journalist [Sharmeen Obaid-Chinoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharmeen_Obaid-Chinoy) at WEF in 2013

The foundation also acts as a [think tank](37101.md), publishing a wide range of reports. In particular, "Strategic Insight Teams" focus on producing reports of relevance in the fields of competitiveness, global risks, and [scenario thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scenario_planning).

The "Competitiveness Team"[^cite_note-124] produces a range of annual economic reports (first published in brackets): the *[Global Competitiveness Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Competitiveness_Report)* (1979) measured competitiveness of countries and economies; the *[Global Information Technology Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Information_Technology_Report)* (2001) assessed their competitiveness based on their IT readiness; the *[Global Gender Gap Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Gender_Gap_Report)* examined critical areas of inequality between men and women; the *[Global Risks Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Risks_Report)* (2006) assessed key global risks; the *[Global Travel and Tourism Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_and_Tourism_Competitiveness_Report)* (2007) measured travel and tourism competitiveness; the Financial Development Report (2008)[^cite_note-125] aimed to provide a comprehensive means for countries to establish benchmarks for various aspects of their financial systems and establish priorities for improvement; and the [Global Enabling Trade Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Enabling_Trade_Report) (2008) presented a cross-country analysis of the large number of measures facilitating trade among nations.[^cite_note-pigman3-126]

The "Risk Response Network"[^cite_note-127] produces a yearly report assessing risks which are deemed to be within the scope of these teams, have cross-industry relevance, are uncertain, have the potential to cause upwards of US\$10 billion in economic damage, have the potential to cause major human suffering, and which require a multi-stakeholder approach for mitigation.[^cite_note-riskreport-128]

In 2020, the forum published a report entitled *Nature Risk Rising: Why the Crisis Engulfing Nature Matters for Business and the Economy*.[^cite_note-weforum.org-129][^cite_note-130] In this report the forum estimated that approximately half of global GDP is highly or moderately dependent on nature (the same as [IPBES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPBES)'s 2019 assessment report[^cite_note-131]). The report also found that 1 dollar spent on nature restoration yields 9 dollars in economic benefits.[^cite_note-132]

### Initiatives

#### Health

On 19 January 2017 the [Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations](51522180.md) (CEPI), a global initiative to fight epidemics, was launched at WEF in Davos. The internationally funded initiative aims at securing vaccine supplies for global emergencies and pandemics, and to research new vaccines for tropical diseases, that are now more menacing. The project is funded by private and governmental donors, with an initial investment of US\$460m from the governments of Germany, Japan and Norway, plus the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation and the Wellcome Trust.[^cite_note-133]

Between 21 and 24 January 2020, at the early stages of the [COVID-19 outbreak](62750956.md), [CEPI](51522180.md) met with leaders from [Moderna](39743243.md) to establish plans for a [COVID-19 vaccine](63319438.md) at the [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos,_Switzerland) gathering,[^cite_note-134] with a total global case number of 274 and total loss of life the virus at 16.[^cite_note-135] The [WHO](33583.md) declared a global health emergency 6 days later.[^cite_note-136]

#### Society

The Global Water Initiative brings together diverse stakeholders such as [Alcan Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcan_Inc.), the [Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Agency_for_Development_and_Cooperation), [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAID) India, [UNDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNDP) India, [Confederation of Indian Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_Indian_Industry) (CII), Government of [Rajasthan](26291.md), and the [NEPAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEPAD) Business Foundation to develop public-private partnerships on water management in South Africa and India.

In an effort to combat corruption, the Partnering Against Corruption Initiative (PACI) was launched by CEOs from the engineering and construction, energy and [metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metals), and mining industries at the annual meeting in Davos during January 2004. PACI is a platform for peer exchange on practical experience and dilemma situations. Approximately 140 companies have joined the initiative.[^cite_note-pigman4-137]

##### Coronavirus and green recovery

In April 2020, the forum published an article that postulates that the COVID-19 pandemic is linked to the destruction of nature. The number of emerging diseases is rising and this rise is linked to [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) and [species loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_loss). In the article, there are multiple examples of the degradation of ecological systems caused by humans. It is also says that half of the global [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) is moderately or largely dependent on nature. The article concludes that the recovery from the pandemic should be linked to nature recovery.[^cite_note-weforum.org-129]

The forum proposed a plan for a [green recovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_recovery). The plan includes advancing [circular economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_economy). Among the mentioned methods, there is [green building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_building), [sustainable transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_transport), [organic farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_farming), [urban open space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_open_space), [renewable energy](25784.md) and [electric vehicles](279350.md).[^cite_note-157]

### Global Shapers Community

The Global Shapers Community (GSC), an initiative of World Economic Forum, selects young leaders below 30 years old to be change agents in the world.[^cite_note-158] Global Shapers develop and lead their city-based hubs to implement social justice projects that advance the mission of World Economic Forum. The GSC has over 10,000 members in 500+ hubs in 154 countries. Some critics see the WEF's increasing focus on activist areas such as [environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection)[^cite_note-World_Economic_Forum-138] and [social entrepreneurship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_entrepreneurship)[^cite_note-159] as a strategy to disguise the true [plutocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutocracy) goals of the organisation.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

### The Great Reset

Main article: [The Great Reset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Reset)

In May 2020, the WEF and the then-[Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles,_Prince_of_Wales)'s Sustainable Markets Initiative launched "The Great Reset" project, a five-point plan to enhance sustainable economic growth following the global recession caused by the [COVID-19 pandemic lockdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_lockdowns).[^cite_note-163] "The Great Reset" was to be the theme of WEF's annual meeting in August 2021.[^cite_note-164]

According to forum founder Schwab, the intention of the project is to reconsider the meaning of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) and capital. While not abandoning capitalism, he proposes to change and possibly move on from some aspects of it, including [neoliberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism) and [free-market fundamentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-market_fundamentalism). The role of corporations, taxation and more should be reconsidered. International cooperation and trade should be defended and the [Fourth Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Industrial_Revolution) also.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

The forum defines the system that it wants to create as "Stakeholder Capitalism". The forum supports [trade unions](17626.md).[^cite_note-167]

The 'Great Reset' has also been the target of [several "debunked" conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Reset#Conspiracy_theories), which heavily overlap with related conspiracy theories concerning the ['New World Order'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_Order_\(conspiracy_theory\)), [Qanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qanon), and [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_misinformation).

## Criticism

### Physical protests



Protest march against the WEF in [Basel](4911.md), 2006

During the late 1990s, the WEF, as well as the [G7](1196634.md), [World Bank](36752.md), [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization), and [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), came under heavy criticism by [anti-globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-globalization) activists who asserted that capitalism and globalization were increasing poverty and destroying the environment. In 2000, about 10,000 demonstrators [disrupted a regional WEF meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S11_\(protest\)) in [Melbourne](17306237.md), by obstructing the path of 200 delegates.[^cite_note-168] Small demonstrations are held in Davos on most but not all years, organised by the local Green Party (see [Anti-WEF protests in Switzerland, January 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-WEF_protests_in_Switzerland,_January_2003)) to protest against what have been called the meetings of "fat cats in the snow", a [tongue-in-cheek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongue-in-cheek) term used by rock singer [Bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bono).[^cite_note-169]

After 2014, the physical protest movement against the World Economic Forum largely died down, and [Swiss police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_police) noted a significant decline in attending protesters, 20 at most during the meeting in 2016. While protesters are still more numerous in large Swiss cities, the protest movement itself has undergone significant change.[^cite_note-170] Around 150 [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) and [Uighurs](31783.md) protested in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) and 400 Tibetans in [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern) against the visit of China's [paramount leader](262766.md) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) for the 2017 meeting, with subsequent confrontations and arrests.[^cite_note-171]

### Growing gaps in wealth

A number of NGOs have used the World Economic Forum to highlight growing inequalities and [wealth gaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_gap), which they consider to have been neglected, or even to be exacerbated, through institutions like the WEF. [Winnie Byanyima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnie_Byanyima), the former executive director of the anti-poverty confederation [Oxfam International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxfam_International) co-chaired the 2015 meeting, where she presented a critical report of global wealth distribution based on statistical research by the [Credit Suisse Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_Suisse). In this study, the richest 1% of people in the world own 48% of the world's wealth.[^cite_note-172] At the 2019 meeting, she presented another report in which she stated that the gap between rich and poor has widened. The report "Public Good or Private Wealth" stated that 2,200 billionaires worldwide saw their wealth grow by 12% while the poorest half saw its wealth fall by 11%. Oxfam calls for a global tax overhaul to increase and harmonise global tax rates for corporations and wealthy individuals.[^cite_note-173]

"[You'll own nothing and be happy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You'll_own_nothing_and_be_happy)" is a phrase adapted from an essay written by [Ida Auken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Auken) in 2016 for the WEF, pondering a future in which urban residents would rely on shared services for many expensive items such as appliances and vehicles. Shortly after its publication, a commentator for [European Digital Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Digital_Rights) criticized Auken's vision of centralized property ownership as a "[benevolent dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benevolent_dictatorship)".[^cite_note-edri-2017-174] During the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the phrase went viral, eliciting strongly negative reactions from mostly conservative but also some left-wing and unaffiliated commentators.[^cite_note-bronitsky-175][^cite_note-nationalreview-2023-176] Responding to viral social media posts based on the phrase, the WEF denied that it had a goal related to limiting ownership of private property.[^cite_note-reuters-factcheck-177]

[Rutger Bregman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutger_Bregman), a Dutch historian invited to a 2018 WEF panel on inequality, went viral when he suggested that the best way for the attendees to attack inequality was to stop avoiding taxes.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179] Bregman described his motivation, saying "it feels like I’m at a firefighters’ conference and no one’s allowed to speak about water".

### Formation of a detached elite

Main article: [Global elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_elite)

&#x20;The formation of a detached elite, sometimes labeled with the [neologism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism) "Davos Man", refers to a global group whose members view themselves as completely "international". The term refers to people who "have little need for national loyalty, view national boundaries as obstacles, and see national governments as residues from the past whose only useful function is to facilitate the elite's global operations" according to political scientist [Samuel P. Huntington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_P._Huntington), who is credited with inventing the neologism.[^cite_note-180] In his 2004 article "Dead Souls: The Denationalization of the American Elite", Huntington argues that this international perspective is a minority elitist position not shared by the nationalist majority of the people.[^cite_note-181]

The [Transnational Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnational_Institute) describes the World Economic Forum's main purpose as being "to function as a socializing institution for the emerging global elite, globalization's "Mafiocracy" of bankers, industrialists, oligarchs, technocrats and politicians. They promote common ideas, and serve common interests: their own".[^cite_note-182]

In 2019, the *[Manager Magazin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manager_Magazin)* journalist Henrik Müller argued that the "Davos Man" had already decayed into different groups and camps. He saw three central drivers for this development:[^cite_note-183]

- Ideologically: the liberal western model was no longer considered a universal role model that other countries strive for (with China's digital totalitarianism or the traditional absolutism in the Persian Gulf as counter-proposals, all of which are represented by government members in Davos).

- Socially: societies increasingly disintegrated into different groups, each of which evoked its own identity (e.g. embodied through the Brexit vote or congressional blockades in the USA).

- Economically: the measured economic reality largely contradicted the established ideas of how the economy should actually work (despite economic upswings, wages and prices e.g. barely rose).

In 2022 the term was again used in a book by *The New York Times* journalist [Peter S. Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_S._Goodman). Titled *Davos Man: How the Billionaires Devoured the World*, it was described as a passionate book against global financial inequality.[^cite_note-184]

### Public cost of security



The Graubünden Civil Defense Police during the 2013 annual meeting in Davos

Critics argue that the WEF, despite having reserves of several hundred million Swiss francs and paying its executives salaries of around 1 million Swiss francs per year, would not pay any federal tax and moreover allocate a part of its costs to the public.[^cite_note-185] Following massive criticism from politicians and Swiss civil society, the Swiss federal government decided in February 2021 to reduce its annual contributions to the WEF.[^cite_note-186]

As of 2018, the police and military expenditures carried by the federal government stood at 39 million Swiss francs.[^cite_note-187] The *[Aargauer Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aargauer_Zeitung)* argued in January 2020 that the additional cost borne by the Kanton Graubünden stands at CHF 9 million per year.[^cite_note-188]

The Swiss Green Party summarised their criticism within the Swiss National Council that the holding of the World Economic Forum has cost Swiss taxpayers hundreds of millions of Swiss francs over the past decades. In their view, it was however questionable to what extent the Swiss population or global community benefit from these expenditures.[^cite_note-Geschäft_Ansehen-189]

### Gender debate

Further information: [Gender bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_bias)

Women have been broadly underrepresented at the WEF, according to some critics. The female participation rate at the WEF increased from 9% to 15% between 2001 and 2005. In 2016, 18% of the WEF attendees were female; this number increased to 21% in 2017, and 24% in 2020.[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191]

Several women have since shared their personal impressions of the Davos meetings in media articles, highlighting that issues were more profound than "a quota at Davos for female leaders or a session on diversity and inclusion".[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194] The World Economic Forum has in this context filed legal complaints against at least three investigative articles by reporters Katie Gibbons and Billy Kenber that were published by the British newspaper *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* in March 2020, with the articles still online as of January 2024.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198]

### Workplace discrimination

According to *The Wall Street Journal*, the WEF has had numerous accusations of workplace discrimination against women and [Black people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people).[^cite_note-199]

### Undemocratic decision making

According to the [European Parliament's think tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliamentary_Research_Service), critics see the WEF as an instrument for political and business leaders to "take decisions without having to account to their electorate or shareholders".[^cite_note-200]

Since 2009, the WEF has been working on a project called the Global Redesign Initiative (GRI), which proposes a transition away from intergovernmental decision-making towards a system of [multi-stakeholder governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-stakeholder_governance). According to the [Transnational Institute (TNI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnational_Institute), the Forum is hence planning to replace a recognised democratic model with a model where a self-selected group of "stakeholders" make decisions on behalf of the people.[^cite_note-Davos_and_its_danger_to_Democracy-201]

Some critics have seen the WEF's attention to goals like [environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection) and [social entrepreneurship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_entrepreneurship) as mere window dressing to disguise its true [plutocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutocracy) nature and goals.[^cite_note-Meyer-202] In a *[Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* opinion piece, Cas Mudde said that such plutocrats should not be the group to have control over the political agendas and decide which issues to focus on and how to support them.[^cite_note--Mudde-203] A writer in the German magazine *[Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero_\(magazine\))* saw the situation as academic, cultural, media and economic elites grasping for social power while disregarding political decision processes. A materially well-endowed milieu would in this context try to "cement its dominance of opinion and sedate ordinary people with maternalistic-paternalistic social benefits, so that they are not disturbed by the common people when they steer".[^cite_note-Meyer-202] The French *[Les Echos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Echos_\(France\))* furthermore concludes that Davos "represents the exact values people rejected at the ballot box".[^cite_note-204]

### Lack of financial transparency

Further information: [Financial transparency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_transparency)

In 2017, the former *[Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung](513356.md)* journalist Jürgen Dunsch criticized that financial reports of the WEF were not very transparent since neither income nor expenditures were broken down. In addition, he outlined that the foundation capital was not quantified while the apparently not insignificant profits would be reinvested.[^cite_note-SZ-money_machine-205]

Recent annual reports published by the WEF include a more detailed breakdown of its financials and indicate revenues of CHF 349 million for the year 2019 with reserves of CHF 310 million and a foundation capital of CHF 34 million. There are no further details provided to what asset classes or individual names the WEF allocates its financial assets of CHF 261 million.[^cite_note-206] From July 2019 to June 2020, the World Economic Forum has spent €250,000 on lobbying the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-207]

The German newspaper *[Süddeutsche Zeitung](30873120.md)* criticised in this context that the WEF had turned into a "money printing machine", which is run like a family business and forms a comfortable way to make a living for its key personnel. The foundation's founder Klaus Schwab draws a salary of around one million Swiss francs per year.[^cite_note-SZ-money_machine-205]

### Unclear selection criteria

In a request to the [Swiss National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)), the [Swiss Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Green_Party) criticised that invitations to the annual meeting and programmes of the World Economic Forum are issued according to unclear criteria. They highlight that "despots" such as the son of the former Libyan dictator [Saif al-Islam al-Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saif_al-Islam_al-Gaddafi) had been invited to the WEF and even awarded membership in the club of "Young Global Leaders".[^cite_note-Geschäft_Ansehen-189] Even after the beginning of the [Arab spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_spring) in December 2010 and related violent uprisings against despot regimes, the WEF continued to invite Gaddafi to its annual meeting.[^cite_note-208]

### Environmental footprint of annual meetings

Critics emphasise that the annual meeting of the World Economic Forum is counterproductive when combating pressing problems of humanity such as the climate crisis. Even in 2020, participants travelled to the WEF annual meeting in Davos on around 1,300 private jets while the total emissions burden from transport and accommodation were enormous in their view.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-Geschäft_Ansehen-189]

### Corporate capture of global and democratic institutions

The World Economic Forum's "Global Redesign" report suggests to create "public-private" [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) in which selected agencies operate and steer global agendas under shared governance systems.[^cite_note-Martens-210]

In September 2019, more than 400 civil society organizations and 40 international networks heavily criticised a partnership agreement between WEF and the United Nations and called on the [UN secretary-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Secretary-General) to end it.[^cite_note-211] They see such an agreement as a "disturbing corporate capture of the UN, which moved the world dangerously towards a privatised global governance".[^cite_note-212] The Dutch Transnational Institute think tank summarises that we are increasingly entering a world where gatherings such as Davos are "a silent global [coup d'état](25914154.md)" to capture governance.[^cite_note-Davos_and_its_danger_to_Democracy-201]

### Non-accreditation of critical media outlets

In 2019, the Swiss newspaper *[WOZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WOZ_Die_Wochenzeitung)* received a refusal of its accreditation request for the annual meeting with the editors and subsequently accused the World Economic Forum of favoring specific media outlets. The newspaper highlighted that the WEF stated in its refusal message that it \[the forum] prefers media outlets it works with throughout the year. *WOZ* deputy head Yves Wegelin called this a strange idea of journalism because in "journalism you don't necessarily have to work with large corporations, but rather critique them".[^cite_note-213]

### Institutional initiatives

In addition to economic policy, the WEF's agenda is in recent years increasingly focusing on positively connoted activist topics such as [environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection)[^cite_note-World_Economic_Forum-138] and [social entrepreneurship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_entrepreneurship),[^cite_note-214] which critics see as a strategy to disguise the organisation's true [plutocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutocracy) goals.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217]

In a December 2020 article by *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)*, author [Naomi Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Klein) described that the WEF's initiatives like the "Great Reset" were simply a "coronavirus-themed rebranding" of things that the WEF was already doing and that it was an attempt by the rich to make themselves look good. In her opinion, "the Great Reset is merely the latest edition of this gilded tradition, barely distinguishable from earlier Davos Big Ideas.[^cite_note-intercept120820-218]

Similarly, in his review of *COVID-19: The Great Reset*, ethicist Steven Umbrello makes parallel critiques of the agenda. He says that the WEF "whitewash\[es] a seemingly optimistic future post-Great Reset with buzz words like equity and sustainability" while it functionally jeopardizes those goals.[^cite_note-219]

A study published in the *[Journal of Consumer Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Consumer_Research)* investigated the sociological impact of the WEF. It concluded that the WEF does not solve issues such as, poverty, global warming, and chronic illnesses, or debt. The forum has, according to the study, simply shifted the burden for the solution of these problems from governments and business to "responsible consumers subjects: the green consumer, the health-conscious consumer, and the financially literate consumer".[^cite_note-220]

### Appropriation of global crises

In December 2021, the Catholic Cardinal and former [prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect) of the [Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith) (CDF) [Gerhard Ludwig Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Ludwig_M%C3%BCller) criticised in a controversial interview that people like WEF founder Schwab were sitting "on the throne of their wealth" and were not touched by the everyday difficulties and sufferings people face e.g. due to the COVID-19 pandemic. On the contrary, such elites would see crises as an opportunity to push through their agendas. He particularly criticised the control such people would exercise on people and their embracement of areas such as [transhumanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transhumanism).[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222] The [Central Council of Jews in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Council_of_Jews_in_Germany) condemned this criticism, which is also linked to Jewish financial investors, as antisemitic.[^cite_note-223]

On the other hand, the WEF has been criticized as "hypocritical" towards Palestinian human rights, when it rejected a petition from its own constituents to condemn Israel's aggression against Palestinians.[^cite_note-224] WEF cited the need to remain "impartial" on the issue. However, [Khaled Al Sabawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Al_Sabawi), writing in [MondoWeiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MondoWeiss) called it hypocritical after it voluntarily condemned Russia's aggression against Ukraine months later.[^cite_note-225]

### Tampering data

In July 2025, the founder of World Economic Forum, Klaus Schwab, was accused of abuse of power after an internal WEF investigation found that when data for the 2017/18 WEF's Annual Competitiveness Report showed the UK had moved up the ranking from seventh to fourth place he intervened by writing to staff that the UK "must not see any improvement" as this would otherwise be "exploited by the Brexit camp". The final report published showed the UK had instead dropped by one place to eighth.[^cite_note-226] In the same report India should have dropped 20 places in the ranking. However Schwab told his staff that "we must protect our relationship with India before Davos 2019", consequently the published report showed India had dropped by only one place to fortieth.[^cite_note-227]

## Controversies

### Whistleblower allegations and controversy with Klaus Schwab

In May 2025, Klaus Schwab launched a defamation and coercion complaint against anonymous whistleblowers whose allegations prompted his resignation as chair of the WEF board of trustees in 2025. The accusations, including claims of financial impropriety, research manipulation, and mishandling of sexual harassment cases, were described by Schwab as "stupid and constructed". Despite stepping down from the WEF, Schwab maintains his innocence and has stated that his lawyers filed a criminal complaint with the Geneva public prosecutor, marking a turbulent period for the World Economic Forum as an organisation. The controversy also highlighted management issues at the WEF identified in earlier investigations and reignited scrutiny over its workplace culture.[^cite_note-228]

### Davos municipality

In June 2021, WEF founder Klaus Schwab sharply criticised what he characterized as the "profiteering", "complacency" and "lack of commitment" by the municipality of Davos in relation to the annual meeting. He mentioned that the preparation of the COVID-related meeting in Singapore in 2021/2022[^cite_note-229] had created an alternative to its Swiss host and sees the chance that the annual meeting will stay in Davos between 40 and 70 percent.[^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231]

### Usage of "Davos"

As there are many other international conferences nicknamed with "Davos" such as the "Davos of the Desert" event organised by [Saudi Arabia](349303.md)'s [Future Investment Initiative Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Investment_Initiative_Institute),[^cite_note-Quartz-18-10-22-232] the World Economic Forum objected to the use of "Davos" in such contexts for any event not organised by them.[^cite_note-WEF-PR_18-10-22-233][^cite_note-WSJ-18-10-22-234][^cite_note-Bloomberg-18-10-22-235][^cite_note-Quartz-18-10-22-232] This particular statement was issued on 22 October 2018, a day before the opening of 2018 [Future Investment Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Investment_Initiative) (nicknamed "Davos in the desert") organised by the [Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Investment_Fund_of_Saudi_Arabia).[^cite_note-WSJ-18-10-22-234][^cite_note-Bloomberg-18-10-22-235][^cite_note-Quartz-18-10-22-232]

## Alternatives

### Open Forum Davos

Since the annual meeting in January 2003 in Davos, an *Open Forum Davos*,[^cite_note-236] which was co-organized by the [Federation of Swiss Protestant Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Reformed_Church), is held concurrently with the Davos forum, opening up the debate about globalization to the general public. The Open Forum has been held in the local high school every year, featuring top politicians and business leaders. It is open to all members of the public free of charge.[^cite_note-pigman5-237][^cite_note-238]

### Public Eye Awards

The [Public Eye Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Eye_Awards) were held every year from 2000 to 2015. It is a counter-event to the annual meeting of the World Economic Forum in Davos. Public Eye Awards is a "public competition of the worst corporations in the world." In 2011, more than 50,000 people voted for companies that acted irresponsibly. At a ceremony at a Davos hotel, the "winners" in 2011 were named as Indonesian [palm oil](57561.md) diesel maker, [Neste Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neste_Oil) in Finland, and mining company [AngloGold Ashanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AngloGold_Ashanti) in South Africa.[^cite_note-239] According to [Schweiz aktuell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schweiz_aktuell) broadcast on 16 January 2015, a public presence during the WEF 2015, may not be guaranteed because the massively increased security in Davos. The Public Eye Award will be awarded for the last time in Davos: "Public Eyes says Goodbye to Davos", confirmed by Rolf Marugg (now [Landrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landrat) politician), by not directly engaged politicians, and by the police responsible.[^cite_note-schweizaktuell-20150116-240]
