**Las Palmas** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/ˌlæs ˈpælməs, - ˈpɑːl-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/ˌlɑːs ˈpɑːlməs, -mɑːs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Spanish: [\[las ˈpalmas\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)), officially **Las Palmas de Gran Canaria**,[^cite_note-6] is the capital and largest city of the autonomous community of the [Canary Islands](5717.md) in [Spain](26667.md). With a population of 383,516 as of 2024, it is the [ninth-largest city in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_lists_of_Spanish_municipalities#By_population).[^cite_note-population-1] It is also the fifth-most populous urban area in Spain and, depending on sources, [ninth or tenth most populous metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_Spain) in Spain.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Las Palmas is located in the northeastern part of [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria), about 150 km (93 mi) west of the African coast[^cite_note-SitClim-12] in the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md). Las Palmas experiences a [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate),[^cite_note-koppen-13] offset by the local cooler [Canary Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Current), with warm temperatures throughout the year. It has an average annual temperature of 21.2 °C (70.2 °F).[^cite_note-14]

The city was founded in 1478, and considered the *[de facto](43594.md)* (without legal and real recognition)[^cite_note-junta-15] capital of the Canary Islands until the seventeenth century.[^cite_note-junta-15] It is the home of the Canarian Ministry of Presidency (shared in a four-year term with [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife)), as well as half of the ministries and boards of the Canarian government, and the High Court of Justice of the Canary Islands.

## History

See also: [Timeline of Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Las_Palmas)

Rotunda lighthouse in La Luz port

The city was founded by [Juan Rejón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rej%C3%B3n) on 24 June 1478, with the name "Real de Las Palmas".[^cite_note-16] Rejón was head of the invading [Castilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile) army, which then engaged in war with the locals.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndrews_&_Quintero200727–28-17][^cite_note-Clavijo17732-18]

The war began at the mouth of the Guiniguada ravine, where he settled together with his 30 soldiers El Real de Las Palmas, which today is the district of [Vegueta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegueta?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Clavijo17732-18]

The struggle lasted for a period of five years, costing a great number of lives, especially on the aboriginal side, which lacked sufficient means to defend itself against the armies sent by the Catholic monarchs. Even so, resistance was fierce. The end of the conquest came in 1483, with the incorporation of the island into the Crown of Castile by [Pedro de Vera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_Vera), who managed to subjugate the natives of Gáldar in the northwest of the island.[^cite_note-Clavijo1773-19]

On November 20, 1485, the diocese was transferred from El Rubicón (Lanzarote) to Real de Las Palmas.[^cite_note-\:0-20] The importance of the city grew gradually, with the establishment of the Bishopric of the Canary Islands, the first Court of the Holy Inquisition, the Royal Court of the Canary Islands and the residence of the Captains General of the Canary Islands. Although the capital, as understood from the 19th century onwards, did not exist as such in the archipelago, given that the Captain General's Residence was in Las Palmas, it can be considered that this was the capital of the Canary Islands during part of the 16th and 17th centuries; afterwards, although without legal or real significance, it continued to be considered the honorary capital of the Canary archipelago.[^cite_note-\:0-20]

In 1492, [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) (Spanish: Cristóbal Colón) anchored in the port of Las Palmas for a repair of the rudder of his ship *Pinta*[^cite_note-Bradford2014-21] and spent some time on the neighbour island on his first trip to the Americas.[^cite_note-Bradford2014-21][^cite_note-Morison1991-22] He also stopped there on the way back to Spain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndrews_&_Quintero200729-23] The [Colón House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col%C3%B3n_House?action=edit\&redlink=1) – a museum in the [Vegueta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegueta?action=edit\&redlink=1) district of the city – is named after him.[^cite_note-24]

In 1595, [Francis Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Drake) tried to plunder the town, leading to the [Battle of Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Las_Palmas).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndrews_&_Quintero200729-23] A Dutch raid under vice-admiral [Pieter van der Does](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_van_der_Does) in 1599 was only slightly more successful; some of the town was destroyed, but the raiders were repelled.[^cite_note-Armas1999-25][^cite_note-Torres1977-26]

Las Palmas' seaport, *Puerto de la Luz* (known internationally as *La Luz port*), the construction of which began in 1883 played significant role in modernizing the city and sped up its advancement.[^cite_note-27] The port benefited greatly from the closure of the [Suez Canal](29323.md) from 1967 to 1975 during the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis). Many foreign workers migrated to the city at this time.

In 1927, the [Province of Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canary_Islands) was split into two provinces: of [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife) and Las Palmas. Las Palmas de Gran Canaria became the capital of the latter, and integrated the islands of Gran Canaria, Lanzarote and Fuerteventura.

## Geography

The city has four main beaches: [Las Canteras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_de_Las_Canteras), Las Alcaravaneras, La Laja, and El Confital.

- *[Playa de Las Canteras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_de_Las_Canteras)* (**Las Canteras Beach**) is the largest beach in the city, and is frequented throughout the year by city dwellers as well as by large numbers of foreign visitors. The beach lies on the west side of the isthmus of Guanarteme, which links the peninsula of La Isleta, located to the northeast, with the rest of the island of Gran Canaria. The 3,100 m beach is oriented toward the northwest in what is known as Confital bow or bay, and stretches from the foothills of La Isleta until shortly before the mouth of the ravine Tamaraceite. Along much of this length, the beach is sheltered from most of the waves and currents of the Atlantic by a natural barrier of coral sandstone popularly known as "the bar", which is in easy swimming distance from shore. A system for environmental management has been introduced, and the beach has received [ISO 14001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_14001) certification – one of only three beaches in Spain to do so, namely La Concha in [San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n) and La Victoria in [Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1diz). Inside the beach runs the Paseo de Las Canteras, a wide pedestrian boardwalk, or sidewalk, which runs parallel to the beach from near the Auditorio Alfredo Kraus to the area known as "Puntilla" until reaching Playa del Confital. La Playa de Las Canteras covers three areas that correspond to the arches and inflections that it conducts on the coast. Each presents certain morphological characteristics.

- *Playa de Las Alcaravaneras* (**Las Alcaravaneras beach**) extends from the rising side of the Isthmus of Guanarteme, an old spit of sand dunes and mountains linking the peninsula of La Isleta, located to the northeast, with the rest of the island of Gran Canaria. It extends from the Real Club Náutico de Gran Canaria to the new marina breakwater of the city, for just over 800 metres (1⁄2 mi) of fine golden sand.

  [^cite_note-30]

  &#x20;The whole beach is serviced by the promenade, which starts in Las Alcaravaneras, connects with the Playa de San Cristobal, and ends in Playa de La Laja, 16 kilometres (10 mi) to the south. The promenade is one of the recreational areas of the city and is popular with people who take the opportunity to walk, run, play sports, or cycle. The tranquility of the bay, and yacht clubs close to the existing beach, make Playa de Las Alcaravaneras a great place to practice sports such as sailing and canoeing. The beach also offers facilities for sports such as beach volleyball, beach soccer or futvóley (which has organized tournaments in the summer) and court sports such as basketball, indoor soccer, and volleyball.

- *Playa de la Laja* (**La Laja Beach**), with fine gray sand, is approximately 1200 m long and has an average width of 40 m.

  [^cite_note-Playa_de_la_Laja-31]

  &#x20;Its moderate waves and currents are no longer dangerous since the construction of a dam in the south in the 1990s. At the time, the Ministry of Environment also trawled the seabed to bring sand onto the beach, and the construction of a boardwalk has significantly improved pedestrian access. Due to the intensity of its streams and incoming waves, La Laja has been hailed as a favorite surfers' beach.

  [^cite_note-Playa_de_la_Laja-31]

  &#x20;It is the starting point for boat races that occur every weekend between April and October.

  [^cite_note-32]

- *Playa del Confital* (**Confital Beach**), southwest of the peninsula of La Isleta, is the northern part of the large bay which contains [Playa de Las Canteras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_de_Las_Canteras) and Playa del Confital. While Playa de Las Canteras is a long and wide, sandy beach, Playa del Confital is a narrower and mostly hard, volcanic beach equipped with comfortable foot paths and large, slanted slabs of stone suitable for relaxing, exercising and sunbathing. Advanced off the beach surfing replaces swimming as the major water activity on Playa del Confital.

  [^cite_note-33]

  &#x20;Until some years ago, the beach was home to a small shanty town, which has since been eradicated and the land of the Playa del Confital returned to general, public use. Ensuing improvements, however, proved controversial as some environmental organizations and residents questioned the legality of the proceedings. The waves arriving at the beach are highly thought of by amateur and professional surfers alike, some of whom consider the Confital as having one of the best right hand breaks in Europe.

  [^cite_note-34]

  &#x20;Here, the ocean currents form a tube that is used by more experienced surfers for its speed and strong contrasts. Each year, qualifying events for the professional world surfing championship take place on this beach.

  [^cite_note-35]

### Climate

Las Palmas has a [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BWh*; [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *BWal*) with [tropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate) influences as the average temperature on the coldest month does not drop below 18 °C (64 °F).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Summers are dry and warm, while winters are also warm. Its average annual temperature is 21.2 °C (70.2 °F) – 28 °C (82 °F) during the day and 18 °C (64 °F) at night. In January, the coldest month, the temperature typically ranges from 19 to 23 °C (66 to 73 °F) (and sometimes higher) during the day, and around 15 to 16 °C (59 to 61 °F) at night, with an average sea temperature at 20 °C (68 °F). In the warmest months – August and September – the temperature typically ranges from 27 to 30 °C (81 to 86 °F) during the day, above 21 °C (70 °F) at night, with the average sea temperature at 23 °C (73 °F). Large fluctuations in temperature are rare.

August 1990 was the warmest month on record, with the average maximum temperature of the month during the day being 30.6 °C (87.1 °F).[^cite_note-38] The highest temperature ever recorded was 44.2 °C (111.6 °F), and the coldest temperature ever recorded was 9.4 °C (48.9 °F). The highest wind speed ever recorded was on 28 November 2005, measuring 113 km/h (70.21 mph). Las Palmas city has never recorded any snow or sleet.

Annual average [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) is 66%, ranging from 64% in March to 69% in October. The amount of annual sunshine hours is above 2,800 per year, from around 190 in winter (average of six hours a day) to around 300 in summer (average of 10 hours a day).[^cite_note-aemet-39] It rains on average only 22 days a year, with total precipitation per year of only 151 mm (5.9 in).[^cite_note-40]

As of 2008, nearly half (45.9%) of Gran Canaria's inhabitants live in Las Palmas, as well as 18.35% of the Canary Islands' total population. According to a study by the National Statistics Institute of Spain Las Palmas de Gran Canaria has a life expectancy of 80.9 years.

As of 2024, the foreign-born population of the city is 60,185, equal to 15.7% of the total population.[^cite_note-population-1] Throughout history, Las Palmas received waves of immigrants from mainland Spain and countries from every continent. The majority of the population is Spanish, although large North- and sub-Saharan African and Latin American communities exist (especially the [Venezuelan community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_people), which is growing fast), as well as important historical minorities such as Indians ([Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_people)), [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_Spain), and a growing [Chinese](372983.md) population.

One street near the city's port has a number of Korean businesses, and has been called the city's Koreatown. It caters to Korean sailors who arrive at the island, who affectionately call the city the "Second [Busan](68115.md)", after the port city in South Korea.[^cite_note-50]

Ethnically, most indigenous Canarians are descendants of a mixture of aboriginal people (*[guanches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanches)*) of the Canary Islands (now extinct), the Spanish *conquistadores* and later European (mainly Spanish, Portuguese, [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people), French, Italian, German, and British) colonizers.

## Culture



Windows of Santa Ana cathedral, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria

Las Palmas offers a variety of theater, cinema, opera, concerts, visual arts and dance performances. The city hosts the Canary Islands Music Festival, the Theatre and Dance and the International Film Festival. The main City Festival, celebrating the foundation of the "City Fiestas de San Juan"[^cite_note-59] is held in June. The Carnival of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria[^cite_note-60] is one of the main attractions for tourists. The city center of Las Palmas, specifically the Vegueta and Triana neighbourhoods, are included in the tentative List of [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_World_Heritage_Sites).[^cite_note-61]

### Museums, theatres and exhibition halls

- The [Museo Canario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Canario) is located in the historic district of [Vegueta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegueta_\(Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). Founded in 1879, it is an international partner of the [Council for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_Scientific_Research?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CSIC). It has a valuable collection of Canary archaeological objects, which are exhibited in 16 halls. It is also equipped with a library of over 60,000 volumes, many of them dealing with the Canary Islands topics. Its archive covers the period from 1785 until today.



Casa de Colón (*Columbus House*) y Pilar Nuevo

- The [Casa Museo de Colón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Museo_de_Col%C3%B3n_\(Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) is in the Plaza de San Antonio Abad, behind the [cathedral of Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Santa_Ana_\(Canary_Islands\)), focusing on the history of the Canary Islands and its relations with America. It has 13 permanent exhibition halls, a library and a dedicated study center, and diverse spaces for temporary activities. The complex consists of several houses, one of which was accessed by [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) during his first trip to America in 1492; it was the residence of former Governor (now better known as the home of Columbus). It is organized into five subject areas: America before the Discovery, Columbus and his journeys, Canary enclave strategic base for experimenting with the New World, The history and genesis of the city of Palmas, and painting of the 16th century to start of the 20th century.

- The [Casa Museo Pérez Galdós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Museo_P%C3%A9rez_Gald%C3%B3s?action=edit\&redlink=1) is located in the [Triana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triana_\(Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) neighborhood of the city. It is the birthplace of [Benito Pérez Galdós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_P%C3%A9rez_Gald%C3%B3s). ## Architecture

- [Bandama Caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandama_Caldera) (*Bandama Natural Monument*) in [Santa Brígida, Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Br%C3%ADgida,_Las_Palmas) is part of the *Tafira Protected Landscape*. It is considered a point of geological interest because of the Caldera de Bandama: this volcanic [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera) reaches 569 m (1,867 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level) at the highest point on its rim, Pico de Bandama, and is about 1,000 m (3,300 ft) wide and 200 m (660 ft) deep. The steep walk to the bottom of the caldera takes about half an hour. Volcanic ash of different hues is in great abundance, and there are some interesting botanic species of Canary Islands origin. There are facilities for food and refreshments as well.

## Transportation

### Roads and highways



Road in the city

Urban road infrastructure is overburdened on workdays and in certain areas; the city street plan is not at all rectilinear, and may be confusing even to experienced drivers. However, there are no toll roads; entrances, exits, main streets and important zones are all well-signposted.

Las Palmas, being the centre of the Las Palmas metropolitan area, is the hub for the island's motorway network. The city is linked with three highways: the [GC-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_GC-1) to the south, the [GC-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_GC-2) to the west and [GC-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_GC-3) acting as a city bypass connecting the other two.

The [GC-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_GC-1) links the capital with [Puerto de Mogán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_de_Mog%C3%A1n) in the south. It is the fastest route from the top of the island to the bottom and vice versa with a speed limit of 120 km/h (75 mph). It is approximately 75 km (47 mi) in length and runs along the eastern and the southern coasts, and is also the second longest superhighway in the Canary Islands. The road provides easy access from the [Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria_Airport) to the major cities and resorts, which include [Maspalomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspalomas) and [Playa del Inglés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_del_Ingl%C3%A9s). The increase in tourism over the years has necessitated the route's upgrading and widening to cope with traffic growth. The GC-1 begins south of the downtown area of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, the highway runs within the beach of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria and 2 km (1 mi) south intersects with the GC-2 and later runs with a few clover leaf interchanges and later forms a junction with GC-5 and south, the GC-31.

The [GC-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_GC-2) North Highway connects Las Palmas with the small northern port and village of [Agaete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agaete). The highway begins by the beach area of the island, and runs through the downtown area, linking with the GC31 at a roundabout interchange. The freeway runs within the beaches and the coastline of the Atlantic Ocean for the half part but at around the 20th km, it becomes a highway after the unidirectional parclo interchanges and runs within the coastline, it later has several interchanges and several towns as it passes to the northwest and finally, it ends in Agaete.

### Airport



[Gran Canaria Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria_Airport)

Las Palmas is served by [Gran Canaria Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria_Airport), also called *Las Palmas Airport* ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **LPA**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **GCLP**).

The airport is located in the eastern part of the island, about 18 km (11 mi) from Las Palmas city centre. In 2008, it handled 10,212,106 passengers and 33,695,248 kg (74,285,306 lb) of cargo, and is the fourth busiest in Spain.[^cite_note-aena2009-70] It is also the only airport on the islands with two runways, thus can accommodate up to 53 landings and take-offs per hour. The lengthy runways made the airport an alternative landing site for the NASA [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle).[^cite_note-71] This airport is also a base for [Binter Canarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binter_Canarias) and [Canaryfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaryfly), airlines which operate regional inter-island flights within the Canary Islands.

An airbase of the [Spanish Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Air_Force) is located to the east of the runways. Beyond several hangars opposite the passenger terminal, the Gando Air Base (*Base Aérea de Gando*) contains ten shelters situated on the southern end of the eastern runway.

### Seaport



The [port of Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_de_Las_Palmas)

[Puerto de Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_de_Las_Palmas) (Las Palmas Port), also known as *Puerto de la Luz*, is a main port for fishing, commercial, passenger, and sports boats in the northwest of the city. It has been the traditional base for scale and supplying ships on their way through the Middle Atlantic for five centuries. The Port of Las Palmas is not only the first port of the Canary Islands, it is one of the main ports of Spain and the first of the geographical area of West Africa. As the leading port in the mid-Atlantic, it serves as the crossroads between Europe, Africa and America.

Despite experiencing some decline in recent years, it retains its dominance in the fishing industry over other ports in the Canary Islands. At the foot of pier, special refrigerated containers and preparation rooms for frozen products can carry out the entire chain of post-processing and storage of fish, from refrigeration and distribution, to manufacture and supply of industrial ice. The port's EU-approved border inspection post is responsible for inspecting all types of imports and exports between the European Economic Union and its trading partners.

Muelle Deportivo is the main yachting marina on the island opposite the commercial port with a capacity of 1250 boats. Close to the centre of the city it is popular with yachtsmen largely as a base for preparing their trans Atlantic passage. It is the start point for the ARC and ARC+ Atlantic Rally for Cruisers in which up to 300 yachts of different sizes leave in November for the Caribbean.

### Bus



Guaguas Municipales

Las Palmas boasts a bus system, provided by the company *Guaguas Municipales*.[^cite_note-72] Municipal Bus Lines offers 40 urban transport routes. The main lines are the 1 (Teatro – Puerto), 2 (Alameda de Colón – Puerto), 17 (Teatro – El Rincón), 25 (Campus Universitario – El Rincón), 12 (Puerto – Hoya de la Plata) and 30 (Alameda de Colón – Santa Catalina, via Rehoyas). In addition, two circular lines (A: Santa Catalina – Santa Catalina, via Alcaraveneras) and B (Santa Catalina – Santa Catalina, via Ciudad Alta).

The most important bus lines have frequencies of between 3 and 15 minutes during the day and between 10 and 40 minutes at night; some lines have service throughout the night. The bright yellow buses are known simply as 'guaguas'.

The 10-ride ticket ('bono de diez') was once a disposable paper card ticket with magnetic stripe at one time widely available in city shops. This is now replaced by a reusable plastic card issued by the company which may be re-charged in multiples of 10 at bus stations and at machines situated at various sites such as public libraries. The Tarjeta Insular (Island Card) which offered a 20% discount on both municipal buses and Global buses was discontinued on 1 January 2011.

A separate bus company *Global* with distinctive blue color, inter-hire company, has 119 lines, many to or from the capital. This company was formed 17 March 2000, resulting from the merger of the previous Salcai and interurban lines Utinsa.[^cite_note-73]

There is also the *Guagua Turística*, which covers the most interesting sites of the city with a guide in several languages.

### Rail

There is currently no rail transport system on Gran Canaria. Between 1893 and 1944 steam tram ran between Las Palmas and [Puerto de La Luz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Las_Palmas). The line was electrified in 1910, although the line reverted to steam traction in 1944, when trams were hauled by a steam locomotive known as *La Pepa*. A reproduction of this locomotive is now on display in the [Elder Museum of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_Museum_of_Science_and_Technology) in Las Palmas.[^cite_note-74] In the early 1970s an experimental [elevated railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevated_railway_line) operated through Las Palmas. Called the *Tren Vertebrado* ("vertebrate train"), it was designed by Basque engineer [Alejandro Goicoechea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Goicoechea) and consisted of an unusual low-profile train running on elevated concrete tracks through the city. The project was unsuccessful and was dismantled in 1974.[^cite_note-75]

In the early 21st century, plans were put forward by the [Gran Canaria Cabildo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabildo_insular) to develop a [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) railway line on Gran Canaria. If built, the *[Tren de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tren_de_Gran_Canaria)* (TGC) line would run along the eastern coast and connect Las Palmas with the airport and [Maspalomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspalomas) in the south.[^cite_note-canarias7-0408-76] In 2004 the Spanish Ministry of Development put a contract out to competitive tender for a feasibility study on a 50 km railway line from Las Palmas to Maspalomas.[^cite_note-railwaygazette-77] This railway project is currently suspended due to funding difficulties.

## Sports



[Estadio Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Gran_Canaria)

Las Palmas is home to three major professional sports teams. These are:

- [UD Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UD_Las_Palmas) – association football club playing in Spain's [Segunda División](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n). The team play their home games at [Estadio de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_de_Gran_Canaria), with a capacity of 32,665. The team used to play at [Estadio Insular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Insular) (which is now closed), with a capacity of 22,000. Honours: [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga): Runner-up 1968–69, [Spanish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_del_Rey): Runner-up 1977–78, Semifinal: 1974, 1984, 1997.

- [CB Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CB_Gran_Canaria) – basketball club playing in [Liga ACB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_ACB) at the [Palacio de Deportes de Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_de_Deportes_de_Las_Palmas), with a capacity of 9,870. Honours: [Spanish SuperCup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_SuperCup?action=edit\&redlink=1): 2016 Winner.

- [La Caja de Canarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Caja_de_Canarias) (*Club Voleibol J.A.V. Olímpico*) – women's volleyball club playing in [Superliga Femenina de Voleibol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superliga_Femenina_de_Voleibol).

Las Palmas was one of the arenas of [2014 FIBA World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIBA_World_Championship) for [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIBA_Basketball_World_Cup_Group_D), consisting [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania_men's_national_basketball_team), [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_men's_national_basketball_team), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_men's_national_basketball_team), [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_men's_national_basketball_team), [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_men's_national_basketball_team) and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_men's_national_basketball_team). Matches were played in the new arena – [Gran Canaria Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria_Arena) with a capacity of about 10,000.

Many (mainly) outdoor sports are practised in city and neighbourhood, for example: surfing, windsurfing, kitesurfing, swimming, diving, skydiving, paragliding, running, cycling, rowing, tennis and golf (mainly in *Las Palmeras Golf*, *Real Club De Golf De Las Palmas*, *El Cortijo Club de Campo* and *Oasis Golf*). Real Club De Golf De Las Palmas, inaugurated on 17 December 1891, is the oldest golf club in Spain.[^cite_note-78]

## Health system

The two general hospitals of Gran Canaria are in Las Palmas. While [Hospital Universitario de Gran Canaria Doctor Negrín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_Universitario_de_Gran_Canaria_Doctor_Negr%C3%ADn?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Doctor Negrín University Hospital of Gran Canaria) is geared to health care in the north and west of the island, [Hospital Universitario Insular de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_Universitario_Insular_de_Gran_Canaria) (Insular University Hospital of Gran Canaria) is geared to health care in the south and east of the island. There are also smaller private hospitals and clinics.
