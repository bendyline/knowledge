The **cubic metre** (in [Commonwealth English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_and_British_English_spelling_differences#-re,_-er) and international spelling as used by the [International Bureau of Weights and Measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bureau_of_Weights_and_Measures)) or **cubic meter** (in [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_and_British_English_spelling_differences#-re,_-er)) is the unit of [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume) in the [International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) (SI).[^cite_note-bipm-1] Its symbol is **m<sup>3</sup>**.[^cite_note-bipm-1] It is the volume of a [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_\(geometry\)) with edges one [metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) in length. An alternative name, which allowed a different usage with [metric prefixes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_prefix), was the [stère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A8re), still sometimes used for [dry measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_measure) (for instance, in reference to [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood)). Another alternative name, no longer widely used, was the **kilolitre**. The acronym **CBM** is in common use to describe a cubic metre.[^cite_note-2]

A cubic metre of pure water at the temperature of maximum density (3.983 °C) and standard [atmospheric pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_pressure) (101.325 kPa) has a [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) of 1000 [kg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilogram), or one [tonne](31185.md), which makes its [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum) [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) at 101.325 kPa 1 Mg/m<sup>3</sup>. At 0 °C, the freezing point of water, a cubic metre of water has slightly less mass, 999.85 kilograms.[^cite_note-5]

The "cubic metre" symbol is encoded by [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) at code point U+33A5 ㎥ SQUARE M CUBED.[^cite_note-Unicode-U3300-6]
