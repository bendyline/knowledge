# Narrative verdict

&#x20;A **narrative verdict** is a verdict available to [coroners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coroner) in [England and Wales](85441.md) and in [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_the_Republic_of_Ireland) following an [inquest](13363086.md).[^cite_note-1] In such a verdict the circumstances of a death are recorded,[^cite_note-bbcservicemandeath-2][^cite_note-3] being a brief free-form, factual statement (either instead of, or in addition to, one of the standard, and familiar, Short-Form Conclusions), which does not attribute the cause to an individual.[^cite_note-4] Narrative verdicts were introduced to England and Wales in 2004.[^cite_note-5]

## Historical examples

The inquest into the [death of Charlotte Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Charlotte_Shaw) concluded with a narrative verdict in October 2010. Charlotte Shaw drowned while crossing a swollen stream on [Dartmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmoor) during training for [Ten Tors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Tors) in 2007.[^cite_note-6]

A coroner delivered a narrative verdict into the death of [Secret Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Intelligence_Service) officer [Gareth Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Gareth_Williams) whose decaying corpse was found padlocked into a red sports bag in the bath at his home in August 2010.[^cite_note-7]

A further narrative verdict was delivered following the death of former footballer [Gary Speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Speed), who was found hanged in his garage on 27 November 2011.[^cite_note-inquest-8]
