A presidential election is scheduled to be held in the [Philippines](23440.md) on [May 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_8), [2028](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028), as part of a general election that will also determine the control of the [Congress of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines) and numerous local positions in the country. This will be the 18th direct presidential election in the country since [1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Philippine_presidential_election), and will be the seventh [sextennial](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/sextennial) presidential election since [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Philippine_presidential_election).

Incumbent president [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) is term-limited under the [Constitution of the Philippines](19554336.md) and is ineligible for re-election. This election will determine the 18th president of the Philippines. The president and vice president are elected separately, thus, the two winning candidates may come from different political parties.

## Background

See also: [2022 Philippine presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Philippine_presidential_election) and [2025 Philippine Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Philippine_Senate_election)



[Bongbong Marcos](572460.md), the incumbent president whose term will expire at noon on June 30, 2028.

In the [2022 Philippine presidential and vice presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Philippine_presidential_election), the [UniTeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UniTeam) ticket of former Senator [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) of the [Partido Federal ng Pilipinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partido_Federal_ng_Pilipinas) (PFP) and [Davao City mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Davao_City) [Sara Duterte](42504861.md) of [Lakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakas%E2%80%93CMD) won, running on a platform that promised broad continuity of incumbent president [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md)'s programs and policies.[^cite_note-1] The two became the first presidential ticket to win since [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Philippine_presidential_election) and the first president and vice president to be elected by a majority since the establishment of the Fifth Republic in 1987, defeating the [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Philippines\))-led opposition ticket of incumbent vice president [Leni Robredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leni_Robredo) and senator [Kiko Pangilinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiko_Pangilinan) alongside several other candidates.[^cite_note-2] Being the only candidate of the opposition [Team Robredo–Pangilinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Robredo%E2%80%93Pangilinan) alliance to be re-elected in the [2022 Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Philippine_Senate_election),[^cite_note-3] senator [Risa Hontiveros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risa_Hontiveros) of [Akbayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbayan) emerged as the *de facto* leader of the opposition against Marcos and Duterte.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

During the Marcos presidency, relations between Marcos and Duterte worsened. Duterte left Lakas in May 2023 and later resigned from his cabinet in June 2024.[^cite_note-6] On February 5, 2025, the vice president was [impeached](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_impeachment_of_Sara_Duterte) in the [House of Representatives](293605.md), following Duterte's controversial use of confidential and intelligence funds and her assassination threat against Marcos and his family.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] She became the first sitting vice president, and the fourth official in Philippine history, to be impeached.[^cite_note-9] If Duterte had been convicted, she would have been removed from office and be barred from holding any government position, including the presidency.[^cite_note-10]

For the [2025 midterm Senate election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Philippine_Senate_election), Marcos formed the [Alyansa para sa Bagong Pilipinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alyansa_para_sa_Bagong_Pilipinas) (Alyansa) electoral alliance,[^cite_note-11] while former president Duterte formed a separate slate of candidates under the [DuterTen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuterTen) in support of the vice president.[^cite_note-12] The traditional opposition led by Hontiveros fielded two candidates under the [KiBam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KiBam) ticket.[^cite_note-13] During the campaign period, former president Duterte was [arrested](79430656.md) on charges related to the [Philippine drug war](51032591.md). In response, the president's sister, [Imee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imee_Marcos), withdrew from Alyansa.[^cite_note-14] Six Alyansa candidates won seats, while three DuterTen candidates were elected.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Two winning candidates, Imee and [Camille Villar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Villar) (an Alyansa candidate), were guest candidates on the DuterTen slate.[^cite_note-17] Meanwhile, both candidates from the KiBam ticket secured victories, marking a significant gain for the opposition.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Duterte ally and incumbent senator [Bong Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bong_Go) emerged as the top-ranking candidate in the Senate race.[^cite_note-20]

Following the election, the Senate minority sought to formally convene the Senate as an impeachment court on June 9; the court was officially convened the following day, June 10. Senators allied with Duterte attempted to dismiss the impeachment complaint, but the Senate ultimately voted to return the articles of impeachment to the House.[^cite_note-21] On July 25, Duterte's impeachment was ruled unconstitutional by the [Supreme Court](1335534.md) on the basis that the fourth impeachment case violated the one-year ban on the filing of multiple impeachment cases but maintained that it was not absolving the vice president.[^cite_note-22] While the House filed a motion for reconsideration, on August 6, the Senate voted to archive the articles of impeachment, with only senators [Bam Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bam_Aquino), Hontiveros, Pangilinan, and [Tito Sotto](3873709.md) voting against.[^cite_note-23]

A [flood control projects scandal](80804811.md) emerged shortly after the election, with allegations of corruption around [ghost projects](81103468.md), substandard construction, and irregularities in the bidding process of projects around the country.[^cite_note-24] In response, a series of [anti-corruption protests](81111441.md) began in September 2025.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

On May 11, 2026, Duterte was [impeached again](82338655.md), becoming the first official in Philippine history to be impeached twice,[^cite_note-27] and triggering a period of severe instability within the Senate. Her allies in the upper chamber ousted Sotto and [elected](83157100.md) [Alan Peter Cayetano](10220741.md), a move incited by the return of [Ronald dela Rosa](50966529.md) following a prolonged absence due to an arrest warrant on charges related to the Philippine drug war.[^cite_note-28] Tensions escalated after a Senate lockdown due to a shooting incident and [attempts to arrest](83172418.md) dela Rosa failed after he left the Senate's premises.[^cite_note-\:0-29][^cite_note-\:3-30] On May 27, the minority bloc staged a [walkout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkout_\(political\)) in opposition to a proposed rule change by the majority bloc for remote voting, which they claim was designed to accommodate dela Rosa and senators facing arrest warrants.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] [Jinggoy Estrada](2395334.md) was later detained on plunder and graft charges, further reducing the majority bloc to ten seats.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] On June 3, the minority bloc formed a [quorum](237014.md) to declare all leadership positions vacant and installed [Sherwin Gatchalian](22207546.md) as [Senate President pro tempore](8750570.md) and acting Senate President, starting a [leadership dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Philippine_Senate_leadership_dispute). This was ultimately resolved on June 17, when Gatchalian was formally [elected](83481651.md) as Senate President, officially ousting Cayetano.[^cite_note-\:4-36][^cite_note-\:5-37] The impeachment trial is scheduled to begin on July 6.[^cite_note-38]

## Electoral system

Main article: [Presidential elections in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_elections_in_the_Philippines)

Presidential elections in the Philippines are held every six years, after 1992, on the second Monday of May. Elections to the presidency use the [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md) to determine the winner, with the candidate with the highest number of votes, whether or not one has a majority, winning the contested position.[^cite_note-Hartmann-39] The elections are held in parallel and voters may split their ticket. If two or more candidates are tied for either position, [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines) shall vote from among them which shall be president, as the case may be.

The winner will serve a six-year term commencing at noon on June 30, 2028, and ending on the same day, six years later.[^cite_note-Hartmann-39]

### Eligibility

The [Constitution of the Philippines](19554336.md) limits the occupancy of the presidency to natural-born citizens aged 40 on the day of the election who are registered to vote, who have been a resident of the Philippines for at least ten years immediately preceding such election, and are able to read and write.[^cite_note-Constitution-40] Incumbent presidents who have served a complete a six-year term are term-limited while their vice president may seek reelection for a second consecutive term.[^cite_note-Constitution-40]

##### Sara Duterte

Main articles: [First impeachment of Sara Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_impeachment_of_Sara_Duterte) and [Second impeachment of Sara Duterte](82338655.md)

See also: [Sara Duterte 2028 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Duterte_2028_presidential_campaign) and [Sara Duterte confidential funds controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Duterte_confidential_funds_controversy)

[Sara Duterte](42504861.md) is the eldest daughter of former president [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) and has served as the 15th [vice president of the Philippines](2271639.md) since her landslide victory in the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Philippine_presidential_election) under the [UniTeam Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UniTeam_Alliance), she won alongside with Bongbong Marcos. ##### Joy Belmonte

[Joy Belmonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Belmonte) has served as the 12th [mayor of Quezon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Quezon_City) since 2019. She was described by former vice president and [Naga, Camarines Sur, mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga,_Camarines_Sur) [Leni Robredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leni_Robredo) as a good candidate for the presidency.[^cite_note-\:1-94]

##### Mar Roxas

[Mar Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_Roxas) served as the 37th [secretary of the interior and local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Interior_and_Local_Government) and senator from 2004 to 2010, Roxas previously ran for president in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Philippine_presidential_election), vice president in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Philippine_presidential_election), and senator in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Philippine_Senate_election). He lost all elections, causing him to retire in 2019.[^cite_note-95] Representative [Edgar Erice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Erice) said that Roxas is being considered as a possible standard bearer of the opposition after Leni Robredo ruled out a national run, saying that Roxas has the integrity and credentials to be president and that his allies are encouraging him to reconsider a presidential bid.[^cite_note-\:1-94] Erice also said that Roxas could "reset" the Philippines to a progressive path.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

##### Gilberto Teodoro

[Gibo Teodoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibo_Teodoro) currently serves as the [Secretary of National Defense](36166520.md), a post he previously served from 2007-2009 during the term of President [Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal-Arroyo). He formerly served as a Representative of Tarlac's First Congressional District from 1998-2007. A prominent relative of the Cojuangco's, he was Former President [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino)'s first cousin. He previously ran as President during the 2010 Philippine Presidential Elections but lost to his Second Cousin, Former President [Noynoy Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noynoy_Aquino)[^cite_note-98]. In 2022, he unsuccessfully bid for a Senate seat, placing 15th overall.[^cite_note-99]

His name was recently floated again by Senator [Ping Lacson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping_Lacson) when the latter floated the idea of a "Gilberto Teodoro Presidency" while commending on the Defense Chief's speech in Seoul Forum denouncing China’s latest assertion and aggression in South China Sea.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]
