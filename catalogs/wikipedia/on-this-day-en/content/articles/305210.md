The **secretary of the Air Force**, sometimes referred to as the **secretary of the Department of the Air Force**,[^cite_note-1] (**SecAF**, or **SAF/OS**) is the head of the [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force) and the service secretary for the [United States Air Force](32090.md) and [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force). The secretary of the Air Force is a civilian appointed by the [president](24113.md), by and with the [advice and consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent) of the [Senate](24909346.md).[^cite_note-9013a-2] The secretary reports to the [secretary of defense](43998.md) and/or the [deputy secretary of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Defense), and is by statute responsible for and has the authority to conduct all the affairs of the Department of the Air Force.[^cite_note-9013a-2][^cite_note-9013b-3]

The secretary works closely with their civilian deputy, the [under secretary of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Under_Secretary_of_the_Air_Force); and their military deputies, the [chief of staff of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force) and the [chief of space operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Space_Operations).

The first secretary of the Air Force, [Stuart Symington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Symington), was sworn in on September 18, 1947, upon the split and re-organization of the [Department of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War) and [Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) into an air military department and a military service of its own, with the enactment of the [National Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947).

## Responsibilities



[Stuart Symington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Symington) is sworn-in as the first Secretary of the Air Force by [Chief Justice](31739.md) [Fred M. Vinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_M._Vinson) on September 18, 1947.

The secretary is the head of the Department of the Air Force. The Department of the Air Force is defined as a *Military Department*.[^cite_note-101a8-4] It is not limited to the Washington headquarters staffs, rather it is an entity which includes all the components of the United States Air Force and United States Space Force, including their reserve components:

> The term 'department', when used with respect to a military department, means the executive part of the department and all field headquarters, forces, reserve components, installations, activities, and functions under the control or supervision of the Secretary of the department.[^cite_note-101a6-5]

The exclusive responsibilities of the secretary of the Air Force are enumerated in [10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 9013(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/9013#b) of the [United States Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Code). They include, but are not limited to:

> (1) Recruiting.\
>
>
> (2) Organizing.\
> &#x20;(3) Supplying.\
> &#x20;(4) Equipping (including research and development).\
> &#x20;(5) Training.\
> &#x20;(6) Servicing.\
> &#x20;(7) Mobilizing.\
> &#x20;(8) Demobilizing.\
> &#x20;(9) Administering (including the morale and welfare of personnel).\
> &#x20;(10) Maintaining.\
> &#x20;(11) The construction, outfitting, and repair of military equipment.\
>
>
> (12) The construction, maintenance, and repair of buildings, structures, and utilities and the acquisition of real property and interests in real property necessary to carry out the responsibilities specified in this section.[^cite_note-9013b-3]

By direction of the secretary of defense, the secretary of the Air Force assigns military units of the Air Force and Space Force, other than those who carry out the functions listed in [10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 9013(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/9013#b), to the [Unified and Specified Combatant Commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combatant_command) to perform missions assigned to those commands. Air Force and Space Force units while assigned to Combatant Commands may only be reassigned by authority of the secretary of defense.[^cite_note-6]

However, the [chain of command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_hierarchy) for Air Force and Space Force units for other purposes than the operational direction goes from the *president* to the *secretary of defense* to the *secretary of the Air Force* to the *commanders of Air Force and Space Force Commands*.[^cite_note-7] Air Force and Space Force officers have to report on any matter to the secretary, or the secretary's designate, when requested. The secretary has the authority to detail, prescribe the duties, and to assign Air Force and Space Force service members and civilian employees, and may also change the title of any activity not statutorily designated.[^cite_note-8] The secretary has several responsibilities under the [Uniform Code of Military Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Code_of_Military_Justice) (UCMJ) with respect to Air Force and Space Force service members, including the authority to convene general courts martial and to commute sentences.

The secretary of the Air Force may also be assigned additional responsibilities by the president or the secretary of defense,[^cite_note-9] e.g. the secretary is designated as the "*DoD Executive Agent for Space*", and as such:

> ... shall develop, coordinate, and integrate plans and programs for space systems and the acquisition of DoD Space Major Defense Acquisition Programs to provide operational space force capabilities to ensure the United States has the space power to achieve its national security objectives.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The secretary of the Air Force's principal staff element, the **Office of the Secretary of the Air Force**, has responsibility for acquisition and auditing, comptroller issues (including financial management), inspector general matters, legislative affairs, and public affairs within the Department of the Air Force. The Office of the Secretary of the Air Force is one of the Department of the Air Force's three headquarter staffs at the seat of government, with the others being the [Air Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Staff_\(United_States\)) and the [Office of the Chief of Space Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Space_Operations).

## History

The Air Corps Act of 1926 (44 *Stat.* 780), passed on July 2 of that year,[^cite_note-13] created a second assistant secretary position in the [War Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War) variously called "[Assistant Secretary of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Assistant_Secretary_of_War) for Aviation," "Assistant Secretary of War for Air," or "Assistant Secretary of War for Aeronautics." Those holding the office, with an eight-year vacancy between 1933 and 1941, were [F. Trubee Davison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Trubee_Davison) (1926–1933),[^cite_note-14] [Robert A. Lovett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Lovett) (1941–1945),[^cite_note-15] and [Stuart Symington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Symington) (1946–1947), who became the first Secretary of the Air Force.[^cite_note-16]
