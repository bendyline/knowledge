**Times Square** is one of the world's busiest [pedestrian intersections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian_intersections),[^cite_note-TimesSquareWorldsBusiestPedestrianIntersections-2] [tourist destinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_destinations), and entertainment hubs. It is formed by the junction of [Broadway](4626.md), [Seventh Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)), and [42nd Street](61068.md) in [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), New York City. Together with adjacent [Duffy Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duffy_Square), Times Square is a [bowtie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowtie)-shaped plaza five blocks long between 42nd and [47th Streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47th_Street_\(Manhattan\)).[^cite_note-TransformingTimesSquare-3] As an epicenter of the world's [entertainment industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_industry) and as the physical hub of the [Theater District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_District,_Manhattan),[^cite_note-4] where most [Broadway theaters](725252.md) are located, Times Square is constantly illuminated by numerous [digital billboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_billboard) and advertisements as well as businesses offering [24/7 service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24/7_service).[^cite_note-5]

Times Square is one of the world's most visited tourist attractions, drawing an estimated 50 million visitors annually.[^cite_note-6] Approximately 330,000 people pass through Times Square daily,[^cite_note-7] many of them tourists,[^cite_note-hellman-8] while over 460,000 pedestrians walk through Times Square on its busiest days.[^cite_note-TransformingTimesSquare-3] The [Times Square–42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square%E2%80%9342nd_Street_station) and [42nd Street–Port Authority Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street%E2%80%93Port_Authority_Bus_Terminal_station) stations have consistently ranked as the busiest in the [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway) system, transporting more than 200,000 passengers daily.[^cite_note-9] The Times Square [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_in_New_York_City) takes its name after the intersection.

Formerly known as **Longacre Square**, Times Square was renamed in 1904 after *[The New York Times](30680.md)* moved its headquarters to the then newly erected Times Building, now [One Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Times_Square).[^cite_note-10] It is the site of the annual New Year's Eve [ball drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Ball), which began on December 31, 1907, and continues to attract over a million visitors to Times Square every year,[^cite_note-11] in addition to a worldwide audience of one billion or more on various [digital media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_media) platforms.[^cite_note-12]

Times Square, specifically the intersection of Broadway and 42nd Street, is the eastern terminus of the [Lincoln Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Highway), the first road across the United States for motorized vehicles.[^cite_note-13] Times Square is sometimes referred to as "the Crossroads of the World",[^cite_note-14] "the Center of the Universe",[^cite_note-TimesSquareCenterOfTheUniverse-15] and "the heart of [the Great White Way](4626.md#Great_White_Way)".[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Geography

Times Square functions as a [town square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_square), but is not geometrically a [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square). It is closer in shape to a bowtie, with two triangles emanating roughly north and south from 45th Street,[^cite_note-19] where [Seventh Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) intersects [Broadway](4626.md). Broadway similarly intersects other north–south avenues at [Union Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Square_\(Manhattan\)), [Madison Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_and_Madison_Square_Park), [Herald Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_Square), and [Verdi Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdi_Square), none of which are squares.[^cite_note-nyt-2012-01-03-20] The area is bounded by West [42nd Street](61068.md), West [47th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47th_Street_\(Manhattan\)), 7th Avenue, and Broadway. Broadway runs diagonally, crossing through the horizontal and vertical [street grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_grid) of Manhattan laid down by the [Commissioners' Plan of 1811](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioners'_Plan_of_1811), and that intersection creates the "bowtie" shape of Times Square.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

Times Square is the official name of the southern triangle, below 45th Street.[^cite_note-23] The northern triangle is officially known as [Duffy Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duffy_Square)[^cite_note-24] and was dedicated in June 1939 to honor World War I chaplain Father [Francis P. Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_P._Duffy) of the [69th New York Infantry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_New_York_Infantry_Regiment).[^cite_note-25] A statue by [Charles Keck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Keck) was dedicated in May 1937 as a memorial to Duffy.[^cite_note-26] There is also [a statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_George_M._Cohan) of composer and entertainer [George M. Cohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_M._Cohan),[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] and the [TKTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TKTS) discount ticket booth for same-day Broadway and off-Broadway theaters that has been at the site since June 1973.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## History

### Early history

When [Manhattan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Island) was first settled by the [Dutch colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonization_of_the_Americas), three small streams united near what is now the intersection of [10th Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) and [40th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_Street_\(Manhattan\)). These three streams formed the "Great Kill" (Dutch: *Grote Kil*). From there the Great Kill wound through the low-lying Reed Valley, known for fish and waterfowl,[^cite_note-31] and emptied into a deep bay in the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) at the present 42nd Street.[^cite_note-32] The name was retained in a tiny hamlet, Great Kill, that became a center for carriage-making, as the upland to the south and east became known as Longacre.[^cite_note-33]

Before and after the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), the area belonged to [John Morin Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Morin_Scott), a general of the [New York militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_militia), in which he served under [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington). Scott's [manor house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manor_house) was at what is currently 43rd Street, surrounded by countryside used for farming and breeding horses. In the first half of the 19th century, it became one of the prized possessions of [John Jacob Astor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jacob_Astor), who made a second fortune selling off lots to hotels and other real estate concerns as the city rapidly spread [uptown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Manhattan).[^cite_note-34]

By 1872, the area had become the center of New York's horse carriage industry. The locality had not previously been given a name, and city authorities called it Longacre Square after [Long Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Acre) in London, where the horse and carriage trade was centered in that city.[^cite_note-35] [William Henry Vanderbilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Vanderbilt) owned and ran the American Horse Exchange there. In 1910, it became the Winter Garden Theatre.[^cite_note-36]

As more profitable commerce and industrialization of [Lower Manhattan](455646.md) pushed homes, theaters, and [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution) northward from the [Tenderloin District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenderloin,_Manhattan), Longacre Square became nicknamed the *Thieves Lair* for its increasingly prominent reputation as a low entertainment district. The first theater on the square, the [Olympia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia_Theatre_\(New_York_City\)), was built by cigar manufacturer and [impresario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impresario) [Oscar Hammerstein I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Hammerstein_I).[^cite_note-37] According to *Gotham: A History of New York City to 1898*, "By the early 1890s this once sparsely settled stretch of Broadway was ablaze with electric light and thronged by crowds of middle- and upper-class theatre, restaurant and cafe patrons."[^cite_note-38]

### 1900s–1930s

In 1904, *[New York Times](30680.md)* publisher [Adolph S. Ochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolph_Ochs) moved the newspaper's operations to a new skyscraper on 42nd Street at Longacre Square, on the site of the former [Pabst Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pabst_Hotel), which had existed on the site for less than a decade since it opened in November 1899.[^cite_note-39] Ochs persuaded Mayor [George B. McClellan Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._McClellan_Jr.) to construct a [subway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_%E2%80%93_42nd_Street_\(New_York_City_Subway\)) there, and the area was renamed "Times Square" on April 8, 1904.[^cite_note-NYT20040408-40] Just three weeks later, the first electrified advertisement appeared on the side of a bank at the corner of 46th Street and Broadway.[^cite_note-41] The north end later became Duffy Square, and the former Horse Exchange became the [Winter Garden Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Garden_Theatre), constructed in 1911.[^cite_note-42]

*The New York Times* moved to more spacious offices one block west of the square in 1913 and sold the building in 1961.[^cite_note-NYT20040408-40] The old Times Building was later named the [Allied Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Corp.) Building in 1963.[^cite_note-43] Now known simply as [One Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Times_Square), it is famed for the [Times Square Ball drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Ball) on its roof every New Year's Eve.

In 1913, the [Lincoln Highway Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Highway_Association), headed by entrepreneur [Carl G. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_G._Fisher), chose the intersection of 42nd Street and Broadway (at the southeast corner of Times Square) to be the Eastern Terminus of the [Lincoln Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Highway). This was the first road across the United States, which originally ran 3,389 mi (5,454 km) coast to coast through 13 states to its western terminus in [Lincoln Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Park_\(San_Francisco\)) in San Francisco.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

Times Square grew dramatically after [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-leach339-46] It became a cultural hub full of theaters, [music halls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_hall), and upscale hotels.[^cite_note-leach339-46]

> Times Square quickly became New York's [agora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agora), a place to gather to await great tidings and to celebrate them, whether a World Series or a presidential election.

— [James Traub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Traub), *The Devil's Playground: A Century of Pleasure and Profit in Times Square*

[Advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising) also grew significantly in the 1920s, growing from \$25 million to \$85 million over the decade.[^cite_note-47] For example, the [Wrigley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrigley_Company) Spearmint Gum sign, possibly the biggest electric sign "in the world," cost \$9,000 per month to rent.[^cite_note-48] Some contemporary critics, such as [Thorstein Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorstein_Veblen)[^cite_note-leach345-49] and [G. K. Chesterton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._K._Chesterton),[^cite_note-leach346-50] disliked the advertising at Times Square. [Fritz Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Lang), after seeing Times Square in 1923, used it as inspiration for his dark industrial film *[Metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_\(1927_film\))*.[^cite_note-leach345-49]

Entertainment icons such as [Irving Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Berlin), [Charlie Chaplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Chaplin), and [Fred Astaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Astaire) were closely associated with Times Square in the 1910s, 1920s, and 1930s. However, it was also during this period that the area began to be besieged by crime and corruption, in the form of gambling and prostitution; one case that garnered huge attention was the arrest and subsequent execution of police officer [Charles Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Becker).[^cite_note-51]

### 1930s–1950s



Crowds celebrating in Times Square on [V-J Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-J_Day) (August 15, 1945)

The general atmosphere of Times Square changed with the onset of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) in the early 1930s. City residents moved uptown to cheaper neighborhoods, and many popular theaters closed, replaced by saloons, brothels, "burlesque halls, vaudeville stages, and dime houses".[^cite_note-52] The area acquired a reputation as a dangerous and seedy neighborhood in the following decades.[^cite_note-53]

Nevertheless, Times Square continued to be the site of the annual [ball drop on New Year's Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Ball). The ball drop was placed on hiatus for New Year's Eve in 1942 and 1943 due to lighting restrictions during [World War II](32927.md), replaced by a moment of silence that was observed at midnight in Times Square, accompanied by the sound of chimes played from sound trucks.[^cite_note-54]

On May 8, 1945, a massive crowd celebrated [Victory in Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day) in Times Square;[^cite_note-55] and on August 15, 1945, the largest crowd in the history of Times Square gathered to celebrate [Victory over Japan Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_over_Japan_Day), reaching an estimated two million.[^cite_note-life1945082721-56] The victory itself was announced by a headline on the "zipper" [news ticker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_ticker) at [One Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Times_Square), which read "OFFICIAL \*\*\*TRUMAN ANNOUNCES JAPANESE SURRENDER \*\*\*".[^cite_note-57]

### 1960s–1990s

#### Decline



Camel Cigarettes sign, 1965. Below and near the letters "Cam" is smoke from a disintegrating smoke ring.



Times Square, 1965; the *My Fair Lady* marquee is at center.

From the 1960s to the early 1990s, the seediness of the area, especially due to its [go-go bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-go_bar), [sex shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_shop), [peep shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peep_show), and porn theaters, became an infamous symbol of the city's decline.[^cite_note-58] As early as 1960, 42nd Street between Seventh and Eighth avenues was described by *[The New York Times](30680.md)* as "the 'worst' \[block] in town".[^cite_note-bracker-59] Later that decade, Times Square was depicted in *[Midnight Cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Cowboy)* as gritty, depraved, and desperate.[^cite_note-60] Conditions only worsened in the 1970s and 1980s, as did the [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_New_York_City) in the rest of the city, with a 1981 article in *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* magazine calling 42nd Street in Times Square the "sleaziest block in America".[^cite_note-61] In the mid-1980s, the area bounded by 40th and 50th Streets and Seventh and Ninth Avenues saw over 15,000 crime complaints per year.[^cite_note-62] The block of 42nd Street between Seventh and Eighth avenues had 2,300 crimes per year in 1984, of which 20% were felonies.[^cite_note-Farley_2018-63][^cite_note-Stern_2015-64]

Times Square was known in the 1970s-80s as the most notorious area for prostitution. In this era, formerly elegant movie theaters began to show [x-rated films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_rating), and [peep shows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peep_show) hustlers were common.[^cite_note-65] In 1984, the area was so derelict and dilapidated, that the entire Times Square area paid the city only \$6 million in property taxes (equivalent to \$18.6 million in 2025).[^cite_note-66]

#### 1980s building boom



Times Square in 1982

In the 1980s, a commercial building boom began in the western parts of Midtown as part of a long-term [development plan](4565772.md) developed under mayors [Ed Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Koch) and [David Dinkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Dinkins). These included office buildings such as [1540 Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1540_Broadway), [1585 Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1585_Broadway), and [750 Seventh Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/750_Seventh_Avenue), as well as hotels such as the [Macklowe Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Times_Square_New_York), [Marriott Marquis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Marriott_Marquis), [Crowne Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowne_Plaza_Times_Square_Manhattan), and DoubleTree Suites.[^cite_note-nyt-1986-11-01-67] By 1986, [New York City Planning Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Planning_Commission) (CPC) was considering enacting regulations that would have forced new buildings along Times Square to include bright signage as well as deep [setbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setback_\(architecture\)).[^cite_note-68] The CPC adopted a planning regulation in 1987, which required large new developments in Times Square to set aside about 5 percent of their space for "entertainment uses".[^cite_note-nyt-1987-09-03-69][^cite_note-70] The regulation also required new buildings on Times Square to include large, bright signs.[^cite_note-nyt-1987-09-03-69] The buildings at 1540 Broadway, 1585 Broadway, and 750 Seventh Avenue were completed at the beginning of the [early 1990s recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_1990s_recession), when 14.5 percent of Manhattan office space was vacant.[^cite_note-p236695270-71] Furthermore, some 9×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> ft<sup>2</sup> (840,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of office space in the western section of Midtown had been developed in the 1980s, of which only half had been leased.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Consequently, 1540 Broadway was completely empty, while 1585 Broadway and 750 Seventh Avenue had one tenant each, despite the buildings having over 2×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> ft<sup>2</sup> (190,000 m<sup>2</sup>) of office space between them.[^cite_note-p236695270-71][^cite_note-74] Entertainment conglomerate [Bertelsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertelsmann) bought 1540 Broadway in 1992,[^cite_note-nyt-1992-03-04-75][^cite_note-n94674085-76] spurring a revival of Times Square in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] This was hastened when financial firm [Morgan Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Stanley) bought 1585 Broadway in 1993,[^cite_note-nyt-1993-08-12-79] followed by 750 Seventh Avenue in 1994.[^cite_note-p219127068-80]

#### 42nd Street Redevelopment, further revitalization



The [pace](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/New%20York%20minute), extensive [transit connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square%E2%80%9342nd_Street_station), and the [theatrical tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_\(musical\)) of [42nd Street](61068.md), between [Seventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) and [Eighth Avenues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)), have made this one of the best known streets in the Times Square neighborhood and the [Broadway Theater](725252.md) [District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_District,_Manhattan).

The [Empire State Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_State_Development_Corporation) (ESDC), an agency of the New York state government, had proposed redeveloping the area around a portion of West 42nd Street in 1981.[^cite_note-81] Four towers designed by [Philip Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Johnson) and [John Burgee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Burgee) were to be built around 42nd Street's intersections with [Broadway](4626.md) and Seventh Avenue.[^cite_note-Stephens_p._92-82][^cite_note-nyt19920803-83] These towers would have been redeveloped by George Klein of Park Tower Realty, though the [Prudential Insurance Company of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudential_Insurance_Company_of_America) joined the project in 1986.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] Furthermore, as part of the West Midtown special zoning district created in 1982, the New York City government had allowed new buildings in Times Square to be developed with an increased floor area ratio. To ensure the area would not be darkened at nightfall, the city passed zoning regulations that encouraged developers to add large, bright signs on their buildings.[^cite_note-Stephens_p._92-82][^cite_note-87]

In 1990, the [State of New York](8210131.md) took possession of six of the nine historic theaters on 42nd Street, and the [New 42nd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_42nd_Street) [non-profit organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-profit_organization) was appointed to oversee their restoration and maintenance. The theaters underwent renovation for Broadway shows, conversion for commercial purposes, or demolition.[^cite_note-nyt20150107-88] Opposition to the towers on Times Square, along with Prudential and Park Tower's inability to secure tenants for the proposed buildings,[^cite_note-89] led government officials to allow Prudential and Park Tower to postpone the project in 1992.[^cite_note-nyt19920803-83][^cite_note-90] By then, Prudential had spent \$300 million on condemning the sites through [eminent domain](144089.md).[^cite_note-nyt19951121-91] The partners retained the right to develop the sites in the future,[^cite_note-92] and the ESDC's zoning guidelines remained in effect.[^cite_note-Stephens_p._92-82] In exchange for being permitted to delay construction of the sites until 2002, Prudential and Park Tower were compelled to add stores and install large signage on the existing buildings.[^cite_note-93]

In 1992, the Times Square Alliance (formerly the Times Square [Business Improvement District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Improvement_District), or "BID" for short), a coalition of city government and local businesses dedicated to improving the quality of commerce and cleanliness in the district, started operations in the area.[^cite_note-94] In 1998, the alliance opened a tourist information center in the former [Embassy Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_Theatre_\(New_York_City\)) at 1560 Broadway;[^cite_note-95] the information center operated until 2014.[^cite_note-96]



[Madame Tussauds Wax Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Tussauds_New_York) and [Ripley's Believe It or Not!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripley's_Believe_It_or_Not!) Odditorium are two of the newer attractions on the redeveloped [42nd Street](61068.md).

In the mid-1990s, Mayor [Rudolph Giuliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolph_Giuliani) led an effort to clean up the area, an effort that is described by Steve Macek in *Urban Nightmares: The Media, the Right, and the Moral Panic Over the City*: Security was increased, pornographic theaters were closed, and "undesirable" low-rent residents were pressured to relocate, and then more tourist-friendly attractions and upscale establishments were opened. Advocates of the [remodeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remodeling) claim that the neighborhood is safer and cleaner. Detractors have countered that the changes have homogenized or ["Disneyfied"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyfication) the character of Times Square and have unfairly targeted lower-income New Yorkers from nearby neighborhoods such as [Hell's Kitchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell's_Kitchen,_Manhattan).[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] The changes were shaped in large part by the actions of [The Walt Disney Company](37398.md), which bought and restored the [New Amsterdam Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam_Theatre) after several attempts at redevelopment had failed. As part of a contract with Disney, officials from the city and state evicted the pornographic theaters and contracted with [Madame Tussauds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Tussauds) and [AMC Theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_Theatres) to move onto 42nd Street. This spurred the construction of new office towers, hotels, and tourist attractions in the area.[^cite_note-99]

&#x20;Prudential and Klein dissolved their partnership for the four office-building sites at Times Square's southern end in 1996.[^cite_note-Stern_(2006)_p._719-100][^cite_note-101] The same year, [Douglas Durst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Durst) acquired the site at the northeast corner of Broadway and 42nd Street,[^cite_note-102] and he developed [4 Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Times_Square) there.[^cite_note-103] The northwest corner of Seventh Avenue and 42nd Street was taken by [Reuters](18998750.md), which enlisted [Rudin Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudin_Management) as its development partner and built [3 Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_Times_Square) on that corner;[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] that building opened in 2001.[^cite_note-n86065892-106] In 1998, a joint venture of Klein, [The Blackstone Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blackstone_Group), and [Boston Properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Properties) won the right to acquire the sites at the southwest and southeast corners of 42nd Street and Seventh Avenue for \$330 million.[^cite_note-p398611537-107][^cite_note-n86125629-108] [5 Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_Times_Square) was completed on the southwest-corner site in 2002,[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] and [Times Square Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Tower) opened on the southeast-corner site in 2004.[^cite_note-nyt20040701-111]

#### Effects

Times Square now features attractions such as [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)'s [Times Square Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Studios), where *[Good Morning America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Morning_America)* is broadcast live; competing [Hershey's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hershey_Company) and [M\&M's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M\&M's) stores across the street from each other, and multiple multiplex movie theaters. Additionally, the area contains restaurants such as the [Bubba Gump Shrimp Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubba_Gump_Shrimp_Company), a [seafood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafood) establishment; [Planet Hollywood Restaurant and Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_Hollywood), a [theme restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_restaurant); and Carmine's, serving [Italian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_cuisine). It has also attracted several large financial, publishing, and media firms to set up headquarters in the area. A larger presence of police has improved the safety of the area.[^cite_note-nyt20150107-88]

The theaters of Broadway and the huge number of animated [neon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_sign) and [LED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LED) signs have been one of New York's iconic images as well as a symbol of the intensely urban aspects of Manhattan. Since 1987 such signage has been mandated by [zoning ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoning_ordinance) that require building owners to display illuminated signs, the only district in New York City with this requirement.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] The neighborhood has a minimum limit for lighting instead of the standard maximum limit.[^cite_note-114] The density of illuminated signs in Times Square rivals that in [Las Vegas](47737.md). Officially, signs in Times Square are called "spectaculars", and the largest of them are called "[jumbotrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumbotron)". This signage ordinance was implemented in accordance with guidelines set in a revitalization program that New York Governor [Mario Cuomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Cuomo) implemented in 1993.[^cite_note-nyt20150107-88]



The "[Naked Cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naked_Cowboy)" has been a fixture on Times Square for decades.

Notable signage includes the [Toshiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiba) billboard directly under the NYE ball drop, the curved seven-story [NASDAQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASDAQ) sign at the [NASDAQ MarketSite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASDAQ_MarketSite) at [4 Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Times_Square) on 43rd Street, and the curved [Coca-Cola sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola_sign) located underneath another large LED display owned and operated by [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung). Both the Coca-Cola sign and Samsung LED displays were built by LED display manufacturer [Daktronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daktronics). Times Square's first environmentally friendly billboard powered by wind and solar energy was first lit on December 4, 2008.[^cite_note-115] The new [20 Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_Times_Square) development hosts the largest LED signage in Times Square at 18,000 square feet.[^cite_note-116] The display is 1,000 square feet larger than the Times Square [Walgreens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walgreens) display and one of the largest [video-capable screens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_video_screens) in the world.[^cite_note-117]

### 2000s–present

In 2002, New York City mayor [Rudy Giuliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Giuliani) administered the oath of office to the city's next mayor, [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg), at Times Square after midnight on January 1 as part of the 2001–02 New Year's celebration. Approximately 500,000 revelers attended. Security was high following the [September 11 terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_terrorist_attacks) in 2001, with more than 7,000 [New York City police officers](255413.md) on duty in the Square, twice the number for an ordinary year.[^cite_note-118]



Looking southeast at [TKTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TKTS) ticket booth in December 2008 at \
Times Square

Times Square started hosting other major annual events in the 2000s. Since 2002, the summer solstice has been marked by "Mind over Madness", a mass yoga event involving up to 15,000 people. Tim Tompkins, a co-founder of the event, said part of its appeal was "finding stillness and calm amid the city rush on the longest day of the year".[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] Architect [Mark Foster Gage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Foster_Gage) proposed and designed the original Times Square Valentine's Day heart in 2009. Since then, designing the heart has become an annual competition.[^cite_note-LeeSquare-121][^cite_note-JohnsonValentines-122]

In February 2011, Times Square became smoke-free as New York extended the outdoors [smoking ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_smoking_bans_in_the_United_States) to the area. The measure imposed a \$50 fine for any person caught smoking within the area.[^cite_note-123] From January 29 to February 1, 2014, a "Super Bowl Boulevard" was held on Broadway, especially in Times Square, between 34th and 47th Streets, as part of [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md). The boulevard contained activities such as autographs, a 60 ft (18 m)-high [toboggan run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toboggan_run), and photographs with the [Vince Lombardi Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Lombardi_Trophy).[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] The area was under increased security and witnessed over 400,000 people during the period.[^cite_note-127]

In October 2022, casino operator [Caesars Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesars_Entertainment) and commercial property developer [SL Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SL_Green) submitted a joint proposal to open a [casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino) at [1515 Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1515_Broadway), along Times Square.[^cite_note-Brachfeld_2022-128][^cite_note-nyt-2022-10-19-129] The community advisory committee rejected the proposal in September of 2025, therefore ending the prospects of a [casino hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino_hotel) in Times Square.[^cite_note-130]

#### Pedestrian plaza

Pedestrian plaza



Pilot program (2009)



Temporary conversion (2012)



Permanent reconstruction (2017)

On February 26, 2009, Mayor [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg) announced that traffic lanes along [Broadway](4626.md) from [42nd Street](61068.md) to 47th Street would be de-mapped starting Memorial Day 2009 and transformed into pedestrian plazas as a trial until at least the end of the year. The same was done in [Herald Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_Square) from 33rd to 35th Street. The results were to be closely monitored to determine if the project was successful and should be extended.[^cite_note-131] Bloomberg also stated that he believed the street shutdown would make New York more livable by reducing pollution, cutting down on pedestrian-vehicle accidents, and helping traffic flow more smoothly through the Midtown street grid.[^cite_note-132]

The pedestrian plaza project was originally opposed by local businesses, who thought that closing the street to cars would hurt business.[^cite_note-133] The original seats put out for pedestrians were inexpensive multicolored plastic lawn chairs, a source of amusement to many New Yorkers; they lasted from the onset of the plaza transformation until August 14, 2009, when they were ceremoniously bundled together in an installation christened *Now You See It, Now You Don't* by the artist Jason Peters, and shortly afterward were replaced by sturdier metal furniture.[^cite_note-134] Although the plaza had mixed results on traffic in the area, injuries to motorists and pedestrians decreased, fewer pedestrians were walking in the road, and the number of pedestrians in Times Square increased.[^cite_note-135] On February 11, 2010, Bloomberg announced that the pedestrian plazas would become permanent.[^cite_note-136]

The city started rebuilding the plaza in 2010, hiring the design and landscaping firm [Snøhetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sn%C3%B8hetta_\(company\)) to permanently replace Broadway's roadway with custom-made granite pavers and benches.[^cite_note-137] By December 2013, the first phase of the Times Square pedestrian plaza had been completed at the southern end of the square in time for the Times Square Ball drop on New Year's Eve.[^cite_note-ArchDaily-2014-138] The project was originally intended to be completed by the end of 2015.[^cite_note-ArchDaily-2014-138] The entire project was finally completed just before New Year's Eve 2016.[^cite_note-139] Some safety [bollards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollard_\(traffic\)) were also installed as part of the renovation to prevent [vehicular attacks](44318569.md) or [collisions](19740545.md) on the sidewalk.[^cite_note-140] After [a 2017 vehicle-ramming attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Times_Square_car_attack), there were calls to install more bollards along Times Square.[^cite_note-141]

Times Square's pedestrian plaza is frequented by [topless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toplessness) women (with painted breasts) called "[desnudas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desnuda)", as well as [costumed characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costumed_characters), who typically [panhandle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begging) for tips.[^cite_note-142] The pedestrian plaza became a source of controversy in the summer of 2015 because of a large number of complaints about the topless women and panhandling characters.[^cite_note-143] Although neither of these activities are illegal, opponents believed that the panhandlers' presence was detrimental to the [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life) in the area.[^cite_note-Barkan_2015-144] There were calls from Police Commissioner Bratton and Mayor [Bill de Blasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_de_Blasio) to remove the plaza, although Manhattan Borough President [Gale Brewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gale_Brewer) opposed the proposal.[^cite_note-Barkan_2015-144] In June 2016, the city added "pedestrian flow zones" where no one was allowed to loiter, as well as "activity zones" where costumed characters were allowed to perform.[^cite_note-145]

## Number of visitors

Times Square is the most visited place globally with 360,000 pedestrian visitors a day, amounting to over 131 million a year.[^cite_note-TS_ped_counts-151] As of 2013, it had a greater attendance than each of the [Disney theme parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_theme_parks) worldwide, with 128,794,000 visitors between March 2012 and February 2013, versus 126,479,000 for the [Walt Disney World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_World) theme parks in [Bay Lake, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Lake,_Florida), in 2012.[^cite_note-TS_ped_counts-151][^cite_note-152] Even excluding residents from the visitor count, Times Square is the world's second most visited tourist attraction, behind the [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip).[^cite_note-153] The high level of pedestrian traffic has resulted in \$4.8 billion in annual retail, entertainment, and hotel sales,[^cite_note-TS_Economics-154] with 22 cents out of every dollar spent by visitors in New York City being spent within Times Square.[^cite_note-TS_Economics-154][^cite_note-155]

### New Year's Eve celebrations

See also: [Times Square Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_Ball)



The Times Square Ball in 2007

Times Square is the site of the annual New Year's Eve ball drop. About one million revelers crowd Times Square for the New Year's Eve celebrations, more than twice the usual number of visitors the area usually receives daily.[^cite_note-156] However, for the millennium celebration on December 31, 1999, published reports stated approximately two million people overflowed Times Square, flowing from Sixth Avenue to Eighth Avenue and back on Broadway and Seventh Avenue to 59th Street, making it the largest gathering in Times Square since August 1945 during [celebrations marking the end of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_over_Japan_Day).[^cite_note-157]

On December 31, 1907, a ball signifying New Year's Day was first dropped at Times Square,[^cite_note-TSLNY-158] and the Square has held the main New Year's celebration in New York City ever since. On that night, hundreds of thousands of people congregate to watch the [Waterford Crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterford_Crystal) ball being lowered on a pole atop the building, marking the start of the new year. It replaced a lavish fireworks display from the top of the building that was held from 1904 to 1906 but stopped by city officials because of the danger of fire. Beginning in 1908, and for more than eighty years thereafter, Times Square sign maker [Artkraft Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artkraft_Strauss) was responsible for the ball-lowering. During World War II, a minute of silence, followed by a recording of church bells pealing, replaced the ball drop because of wartime blackout restrictions. Today, Countdown Entertainment and One Times Square handle the New Year's Eve event in conjunction with the Times Square Alliance.[^cite_note-TSLNY-158] A new energy-efficient LED ball debuted for the arrival of 2008, which was the centennial of the Times Square ball drop. The 2008–09 ball is larger and has become a permanent installation as a year-round attraction, being used for celebrations on days such as [Valentine's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine's_Day) and [Halloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween).[^cite_note-TSLNY-158]

The New Year's Eve celebrations are usually overseen by thousands of police officers. Aluminum barriers are erected to accommodate spectators; for the 2020 celebration, attended by a million people, barriers were erected from 38th to 59th Street and from Sixth to Eighth Avenue.[^cite_note-nyt20201230-159] Typically, the celebrations create large amounts of waste. The waste includes the 3,000 pounds of biodegradable [confetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confetti) dropped at midnight, a tradition of which started in 1992.[^cite_note-160] The [New York City Department of Sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Sanitation) estimated that by 8 a.m. on New Year's Day 2014, it had cleared over 50 short tons (45 long tons; 45 t) of trash from the New Year's celebration, using 190 workers from their crews and the Times Square Alliance.[^cite_note-161]

### Impact of COVID-19



An empty Times Square in May 2020

The onset of the [COVID-19 pandemic in New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_New_York_City) during 2020 reduced the number of people traveling to Times Square. About 108,000 pedestrians visited Times Square each day in late 2020 compared to the 380,000 before the pandemic. From March to October 2020, 26 of the area's 46 hotels closed, as well as 39 of 151 stores and 84 of 162 restaurants.[^cite_note-162] Times Square was closed to the public for New Year's Day 2021 and observers were dispersed into enclosures measuring 8 by 8 ft (2.4 by 2.4 m).[^cite_note-nyt20201230-159][^cite_note-163]

## In popular culture



Times Square looking north from 44th Street

Times Square has been featured countless times in literature, films, video games, music videos, and on television.

The seediness of the area was featured prominently in such films as *[Midnight Cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Cowboy)* (1969),[^cite_note-172] *[Born to Win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born_to_Win)* (1971),[^cite_note-173] and *[Taxi Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_Driver)* (1976).[^cite_note-174] The area was shown in the 1980 film *[Times Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Square_\(1980_film\))*, which featured a [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock)/[new wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_music) soundtrack.[^cite_note-175] It was also depicted in the 2011 movie *[New Year's Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year's_Eve_\(2011_film\))*.[^cite_note-176] The area also appeared on *[The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race_\(American_TV_series\))* as the starting location in a race around the world in the first episode of the show's [25th season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race_25),[^cite_note-177] as well as on the [sixth season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion_6) of the [Israeli edition of *The Amazing Race*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMerotz_LaMillion) with teams finishing their second leg in Times Square.[^cite_note-178]

Times Square has been fictionally attacked and destroyed in several movies, including *[Knowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowing_\(film\))*, when a [solar flare](54648.md) destroys New York City;[^cite_note-PopPlaces-179] *[Deep Impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Impact_\(film\))*, when a [tsunami](31161.md) created from a [meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor) impact destroys New York City; the 1998 film *[Godzilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godzilla_\(1998_film\))*, where Godzilla is chased through Times Square; the *[Ghostbusters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghostbusters)* movies; Stephen King's *[The Stand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stand_\(1994_miniseries\))*, where the intersection is overcome by total anarchy; *[Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformers:_Revenge_of_the_Fallen)*, *[Cloudy with a Chance of Meatballs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudy_with_a_Chance_of_Meatballs_\(film\))*, and *[2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_\(film\))*. It was also seen in the festival battle scene in the 2002 film *[Spider-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man_\(2002_film\))*, and a stand-off in the later film *[The Amazing Spider-Man 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Spider-Man_2)*.[^cite_note-180]

Films and TV shows have also employed the opposite tactic, depicting the typically bustling area as eerily still, such as in *[Vanilla Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla_Sky),*[^cite_note-181] as well as the post-apocalyptic *[I Am Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_Legend_\(film\))*, in which [Will Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Smith) and his dog go hunting for deer in the deserted urban canyon.[^cite_note-PopPlaces-179] In the pilot episode of the TV series *[Blindspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blindspot_\(TV_series\))*, Times Square is completely emptied due to an abandoned bag being suspected to be a bomb.[^cite_note-182]

Times Square also has featured prominently in video games. For instance, in *[Grand Theft Auto IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_IV)*, a recreation of the Times Square area referred to in-game as "Star Junction", is included in the game's fictional "[Liberty City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_City_\(Grand_Theft_Auto\))" setting.[^cite_note-PopPlaces-179] Times Square is also shown in *[Battlefield 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlefield_3)*, where the final fight with the main antagonist takes place, where the player must stop him from detonating a nuclear bomb in the square; and *[Crysis 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crysis_2)*, in which player must fight off attacking alien forces to assist U.S. Marines in evacuating the area.[^cite_note-183] *[Gran Turismo 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Turismo_4)* also features Times Square both as a photo spot and as a part of the New York city circuit which also includes [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park).
