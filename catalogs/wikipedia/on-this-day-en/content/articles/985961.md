**Leinster Rugby** ([Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language): *Rugbaí Laighean*) is one of the four professional provincial club [rugby union](25405.md) teams from the island of [Ireland](147575.md). They compete in the [United Rugby Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship) and the [European Rugby Champions Cup](40682953.md). The team represents the **Leinster Branch**, one of the four provincial unions that together make up the [Irish Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Rugby_Football_Union) (IRFU), but is not restricted to players from that province.

Leinster play their home games primarily at the 20,600-capacity [RDS Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDS_Arena), 'the RDS' in [Dublin](8504.md). Larger fixtures are played in the 51,700-capacity [Aviva Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviva_Stadium) when the capacity of the RDS is insufficient.[^cite_note-1] Occasional one-off games have been moved to [Croke Park](322713.md), home of the [Gaelic Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_Athletic_Association) and by some distance the [largest stadium in Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stadiums_in_Ireland_by_capacity).[^cite_note-2] Before moving to the RDS in 2005, Leinster's traditional home ground was [Donnybrook Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnybrook_Stadium), in [Dublin 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_4). The province plays primarily in blue with white or yellow trim and the team [crest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crest_\(sports\)) features a harp within a rugby ball, the harp being an ancient symbol of the province found in and taken from the [flag of Leinster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Leinster), although the traditional colours of Leinster Rugby mean the design more resembles the [flag of the president of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Standard_\(Ireland\)) or the [coat of arms of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Ireland).

Leinster turned professional along with its fellow [Irish provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_provinces) in 1995 and has competed in the [United Rugby Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship) (formerly known as the Celtic League, Magners League and the Pro12 / Pro14) since it was founded in 2001. Before 1995, the Leinster Branch had previously competed in the annual [Irish interprovincial championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Interprovincial_Rugby_Championship).[^cite_note-3]

During their professional history, Leinster have been the most successful team in Ireland or from the Celtic League and its successors. As of 2026, Leinster have won their domestic title on ten occasions, while topping the regular season table a further five times, and have won as of 2026 all four [URC Irish Shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship). In [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Professional_Club_Rugby), Leinster are second only to [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Toulousain) with four [European Champions Cups](40682953.md), one [EPRC Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPCR_Challenge_Cup) and five runners-up placings in the Champions Cup.

Leinster enjoy historical rivalries with all three provincial unions, most notably with [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_Rugby), where some games between the teams have been watched by over 80,000 fans in Croke Park. In addition, Leinster have notable rivalries with two French club teams; erstwhile rivals Toulouse with whom they have jockeyed for the title of Europe's most decorated team, and [La Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Rochelais), a frequent opponent in European competition who defeated Leinster in back to back European Champions Cup finals in 2022 and 2023 under the direction of long-time Munster player [Ronan O'Gara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronan_O'Gara). In more recent years a rivalry has also developed with the [South African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_South_Africa) [Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulls_\(rugby_union\)) franchise, with the sides meeting twice in three seasons in the semi-final of the United Rugby Championship, before meeting twice in the finals of 2025 and 2026 United Rugby Championships.

## History

### Founding (1879–1899)

The Leinster Branch was inaugurated at a meeting on 31 October 1879. The meeting was held at Lawrence's premises at 63 [Grafton Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grafton_Street) and was largely attended. Although this was the formal founding of Leinster as we know it today, with the amalgamation of the Irish Football Union and the Northern Union, the Leinster provincial team had been active since 1879 – when the first interprovincial derby was played against [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_rugby). The Leinster and Ulster teams also made up the representative Irish team that competed against England in Ireland's first-ever international in 1875. Upon the founding of the union, [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_rugby) were also added to the fray in 1879, when their first provincial team was selected and first Munster players represented Ireland.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

F. Kennedy (Wanderers) was elected first Hon. Secretary of the Branch and C.B. Crocker (Lansdowne) first Hon. Treasurer.

The function of the Branch was to organise the game of [rugby football](25405.md) in the province. Every year five representatives would be selected to join the [IRFU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Rugby_Football_Union) Committee. They would be known as the "Leinster Five" and would pick the Leinster representative teams.

The first Interprovincial matches between Leinster, [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_rugby) and [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_rugby) were held in 1875. At this time the matches were played with 20 players a side. Leinster lost to Ulster by a converted try and beat Munster by one goal to nil. Since then there has been a match between these teams annually, with [Connacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connacht_Rugby) joining the fold in 1885.

Leinster Schools Interprovincial matches have been taking place since 1888. Leinster Schools beat the Ulster Schools in [Belfast](5046.md) on Saturday 7 April by a dropped goal to a try. Their first match against Munster Schools took place on 18 March 1899, when Leinster won by two tries to one.

### Amateur period (1900–1990s)

The early 1920s led to the creation of the Provincial Towns Cup and the Metropolitan Cup, which are still hard-fought competitions in the Leinster Rugby calendar. Much has changed in rugby over the years, but the original idea of Leinster Club Rugby acting as a feeder for the Leinster Interprovincial side, though now professional, still stands true.

All Interprovincial matches were abandoned during the years of the [Great War](4764461.md) (1914–1918) and the [War period](32927.md) (1939–1945), though unofficial matches were played.[^cite_note-Branch-6]

The first major touring side to play Leinster was a team drawn from the [New Zealand Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Army) – the Kiwis, in 1946. Although it was not an official touring side organised by the [New Zealand Rugby Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Rugby_Union), the quality of the match, which was drawn 10 points each, is still remembered to this day.[^cite_note-Branch-6]

The first official overseas touring side that came to play Leinster was an [Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_rugby_union_team) touring side in 1957.[^cite_note-Branch-6] Since then, Leinster has played against every major touring side from [Fiji](1074616.md) to [France](955656.md).[^cite_note-Branch-6]

Before the days of professional rugby union, there was further emphasis on [Irish club rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIB_League) as opposed to the provincial game. During these times the provincial sides were purely representative sides and games were far less frequent than now. Between 1946 and 2002 the sides would meet annually to contest the [Irish Interprovincial Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Interprovincial_Rugby_Championship) and on rare occasions would be tested against touring international sides. When rugby union was declared 'open' in 1995, these four teams became the four professional teams run by the [Irish Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Rugby_Football_Union) and therefore much of the history of the side has been made in the modern era.

### Leinster Lions (1990s–2005)



Leo the Leinster Lion, team mascot

Leinster became a professional outfit in the mid-1990s. The "Leinster Lions" name came into existence during the [2001–02 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Celtic_League) as the result of a joint marketing initiative between Leinster Rugby and its kit sponsors, the [Canterbury Clothing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_of_New_Zealand). Before the start of the [2004–05 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_Celtic_League), the 'Lions' was dropped from the name. It is still used for marketing and branding, in particular, the Cubs Club for Junior members of Leinster Rugby.[^cite_note-7] The Leinster mascot is "Leo the Lion". It was also during this time that the song “Molly Malone” became a match fixture to be sung by the fans.[^cite_note-8]

Leinster's first season in the newly formed [Celtic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship) ended in success as the Lions were crowned the inaugural champions, beating rivals [Munster Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_Rugby) in the [2001–02 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Celtic_League).[^cite_note-9] In [2002–03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Heineken_Cup), they became only the third team in the history of the [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Cup) to win all their games in pool play. They also went one step further in the playoffs than the previous season by reaching the semi-finals (for the first time since 1995–96), but lost at home against French side [Perpignan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Perpignan), which was accompanied by an [unsuccessful season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Celtic_League) in the Celtic League. The [2003–04 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_Celtic_League) also ended in disappointment as Leinster slumped to their worst ever league performance and failed to qualify from [their European Cup group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_Heineken_Cup).

### Title misses (2004–2007)

Leinster improved during the [2004–05 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_Celtic_League), finishing 3rd, just three points behind the eventual winners, the [Ospreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ospreys_\(rugby_union\)).[^cite_note-10] Leinster also won all of their pool games in that year's European Cup, and were again among the favourites for the title, however they went out at the quarter final stage to [Leicester Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_Tigers).[^cite_note-11]

The next two seasons of the Celtic League were to end in near misses for Leinster, as they lost out on the [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Celtic_League) and [2006–07](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_Celtic_League) league titles on the final day of the season. These seasons also saw progress in the [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Cup). In [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Heineken_Cup), Leinster progressed to the semi-final but were eliminated by Irish rivals [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_Rugby) at [Lansdowne Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansdowne_Road) and they reached the quarter-final the following year where they were beaten by eventual winners [London Wasps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasps_RFC).

### European and domestic dominance (2008–2014)

Increasing attendances at Leinster games led to a move across [Dublin 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_4) from [Donnybrook Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnybrook_Stadium) to the redeveloped [RDS Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDS_Arena).

In [2007–08](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Heineken_Cup), Leinster failed to qualify from their European Cup pool, but did end the season as [Celtic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship) champions, sealing the title with a 41–8 victory over the [Newport Gwent Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragons_\(rugby_union\)) in front of their home fans at the RDS.[^cite_note-12]

In the [2008–09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Heineken_Cup) season, Leinster topped their European Cup pool despite away losses to French side Castres and English side Wasps.[^cite_note-13] Victory over [Harlequins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlequin_F.C.) in the quarter-finals followed, despite the [Bloodgate Scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodgate). Leinster overcame Munster 25–6 in a semi-final in Dublin's [Croke Park](322713.md) that broke the world record attendance for a club rugby union game with a crowd of over 82,200.[^cite_note-14] Leinster won the [2009 European Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Heineken_Cup_Final) in [Murrayfield Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrayfield_Stadium) in Edinburgh, beating [Leicester Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_Tigers) 19–16 to claim their first European crown.[^cite_note-15]



The [RDS Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDS_Arena) before the [2010 Celtic League Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Celtic_League)

In [2009–10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Heineken_Cup) Leinster was eliminated from the European Cup at the semi-final stage by eventual winners [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Toulousain). Also despite having topped the [Pro12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship) league during the regular season, Leinster lost the first-ever [Play-off Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Celtic_League) 17–12 on their home ground to the [Ospreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ospreys_\(rugby_union\)).[^cite_note-16]

In the [2010–11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Heineken_Cup) European Cup, Leinster defeated the top English teams ([Leicester Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_Tigers), [Saracens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saracens_F.C.) & [Northampton Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton_Saints)), as well as top French sides, [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Toulousain) (who were the defending European champions), [Racing Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Metro) & [Clermont Auvergne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont_Auvergne), (the [French Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_14)).[^cite_note-17] to go on to regain their title as champions of Europe in the [2011 European Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Heineken_Cup_Final) at the [Millennium Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Stadium) in Cardiff. Trailing at half time, Leinster scored 27 unanswered points in the second half to beat Northampton 33–22 and claim their second European crown with the biggest comeback in European Cup final history.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Leinster were also chasing a [Pro12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship) & [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Cup) double, but lost 19–9 to Irish rivals Munster in the [Pro12 Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Celtic_League_Grand_Final).[^cite_note-20]

In [2011–12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Leinster_Rugby_season) Leinster became only the second side ever to retain the title of European Champions. Leinster emerged unbeaten in group play to top their group[^cite_note-21] and went on to defeat the [Cardiff Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_Blues) 34–3 in the quarterfinals,[^cite_note-22] followed by a 19–15 semifinal victory over [ASM Clermont Auvergne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASM_Clermont_Auvergne).[^cite_note-23] and defeated [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_rugby) in the first all-Irish final 42–14, recording the most points scored and the most tries scored in a European Cup final as well as becoming the first unbeaten side to win the European Cup.[^cite_note-24] Once again, Leinster targeted the double, and faced a repeat of the 2010 Pro12 final against the [Ospreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ospreys_\(rugby_union\)). Leinster's domestic title challenge fell at the final hurdle, conceding a final minute try to slump to a one-point defeat, and unable to complete the double despite topping the table in the regular season.[^cite_note-25]

The [2012–13 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Pro12) proved to be another successful season for Leinster Rugby. The club finished in second place during the regular season of the Pro12 and defeated [Glasgow Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Warriors) by a score of 17–15 in their semi-final play-off match on 11 May 2013.[^cite_note-26] On 17 May, Leinster were crowned champions of the [European Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_European_Challenge_Cup) after defeating [Stade Français](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Fran%C3%A7ais) 34–13 in the final at their home ground, the RDS Arena.[^cite_note-27] Leinster successfully completed the double on 25 May, defeating Ulster 24–18 in the Pro12 final to claim their third league championship.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

Leinster continued their success in the [2013–14 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Pro12) by becoming the first team ever to defend the [Pro12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship) title, topping the league in the regular season and defeating [Glasgow Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Warriors) 34–12 in their fifth consecutive Pro12 [play-off final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Pro12) and also secured their seventh major title in as many years.[^cite_note-30]

### Blooding a new generation (2015–2017)

Following a remarkable run of seven major trophies in seven years, Leinster's title run came to an end following the 2013–14 season. The [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Pro12) saw a dip in form, with Leinster finishing in fifth place in the league and failing to make the play-offs. Fortunes in the newly formed [Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_European_Rugby_Champions_Cup) were better, with the team reaching the semi-final where they were defeated in extra-time by eventual winners, Toulon. At the end of the season, head coach [Matt O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_O'Connor_\(rugby_union\)) left the club by mutual consent with former club captain, Leo Cullen, being named as his replacement. Cullen then brought in ex-England coach [Stuart Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Lancaster_\(rugby_union\)) as senior coach at the start of the 2016–17 season, which saw a huge improvement from Leinster as well a big group of young players coming through. Despite playing brilliant rugby all season, Leinster failed to win any silverware, falling short in the Champions Cup semi-final to old rivals Clermont and shocked by the Scarlets in the Pro12 Semi-Final at the RDS. However, there was huge optimism amongst the players and supporters as they believed this was only the start of a new generation and perhaps another era of success.

### Present Day (2018–2026)



[Croke Park](322713.md) before the [2023–24 European Rugby Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_European_Rugby_Champions_Cup) Semi-Final victory against [Northampton Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton_Saints).

Starting with the 2017–18 season, Leinster won four straight Pro14 championships.[^cite_note-4_in_a_row_Pro14-31] In Europe, they won the [2017-18 Heineken Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_European_Rugby_Champions_Cup), defeating [Racing 92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_92) by a score of 15–12 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_European_Rugby_Champions_Cup_Final) in [Bilbao](68029.md).[^cite_note-2017-18_H_Cup-32] They were runners-ups four times in [2018-19 Heineken Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_European_Rugby_Champions_Cup), [2021-22 Heineken Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_European_Rugby_Champions_Cup), [2022-23 Heineken Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_European_Rugby_Champions_Cup) and the [2023-24 Heineken Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_European_Rugby_Champions_Cup).[^cite_note-33] Leinster were knocked out of the Heineken cup competition in the quarter-finals in 2019–20, and again at the semi-finals stage in 2020–21.

Four South African teams joined the Guinness Pro 14 and the tournament was renamed the United Rugby Championship (URC).[^cite_note-34]

Leinster lost to South African side the Bulls in the inaugural semi-final. In the subsequent two years, Leinster again lost at the URC semi-final stage to Munster, following a late drop goal by Jack Crowley,[^cite_note-35] and the Bulls.[^cite_note-36] They subsequently won the URC Grand Finals in 2025 and 2026.

In the Champions Cup during this period, Leinster were less successful making the final in three successive seasons, before losing in a semi-final to Northampton Saints in 2024–25. They reached the final again in 2025-26 but lost heavily to Bordeaux-Begles.

Their mentality has been routinely questioned during this period given their failure to win European trophies despite a relative abundance of resources.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

## Colours and crest



Flag of the province of Leinster

The current crest was introduced in 2005 as Leinster Rugby held no copyright on the previous crest. The new, stylised crest, is made specific to Leinster Rugby as it incorporates the harp with a rugby ball.[^cite_note-54] The Leinster Rugby crest is on all official club merchandise including replica jerseys.

The province's current kit (2018/19) is blue with a pattern of spearheads on the jersey which takes inspiration from the people of Laighean (the ancient Irish name for [Leinster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leinster)), while the alternative kit is green with gold features, the colours seen in the [Flag of Leinster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Leinster), with a pattern of geographical cutouts for its 12 counties. The European kit is 'night navy' with the name of each of the 12 counties visible on the jersey written in the ancient Irish alphabet [ogham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogham).

The Leinster jersey also features four stars above the crest, to represent the four [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Cup) titles won to date.

## Stadia

### RDS Arena

Main article: [RDS Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDS_Arena)



The RDS Arena

Leinster's current home ground is the [RDS Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDS_Arena).[^cite_note-55] Games were first played at the RDS during the [2006–07 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_Leinster_Rugby_season), initially just for [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Cup) games. By the following season, however, all games had been moved to the RDS. The RDS has undergone large scale redevelopment since Leinster moved in. The arena now has a mostly seated capacity of 18,500. As the RDS remains a showjumping venue, the North and South stands are removable. A roof has been constructed to cover the grandstand opposite the pre-existing Anglesea stand.[^cite_note-56] The RDS will be Leinster's home until 2027, as a 20-year lease was signed in 2007.[^cite_note-57]

In July 2014, it was announced by the RDS and Leinster rugby that a design competition was being held to develop the arena into a 25,000 capacity world-class stadium, with work expected to commence on the redevelopment in April 2016.[^cite_note-58] The selling of naming rights to the arena will be a key component in funding the project, with an initial budget of €20,000,000 being proposed.[^cite_note-59]



Inside the RDS Arena prior to a Leinster Game

### Aviva Stadium

Main article: [Aviva Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviva_Stadium)



The Aviva Stadium prior to Leinster game

For bigger games where the RDS does not have sufficient capacity, Leinster play their games at the Aviva Stadium, which has an all-seater capacity of 51,700. These are often key home games in the [European Cup](40682953.md) or [United Rugby Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship) games against domestic rivals. In 2010 they first played a home league game against [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_Rugby), the first time the stadium sold out,[^cite_note-60] and then against [ASM Clermont Auvergne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASM_Clermont_Auvergne).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] Leinster defeated [Leicester Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_Tigers) at the venue in the [2010–11 European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Heineken_Cup) quarter-finals and went on to beat [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Toulousain) in the semi-finals, also held at the Aviva stadium on 30 April 2011, en route to winning their second European Cup.[^cite_note-Leinster_32_Toulouse_23-64] The following season Leinster hosted Munster, Bath and Cardiff at the Aviva Stadium and remained unbeaten at the ground until December 2012 when they lost 21–28 to [ASM Clermont Auvergne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASM_Clermont_Auvergne).

### Donnybrook Stadium

Main article: [Donnybrook Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnybrook_Stadium)



Donnybrook Stadium

Leinster's traditional home over the years has been [Donnybrook Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnybrook_Stadium) in [Donnybrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnybrook,_Dublin), [Dublin 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_4). Donnybrook consists of a single covered stand and three sides of open terracing. A move across [Dublin 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_4) to the [RDS Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDS_Arena) for Leinster was needed to accommodate growing crowds, as the 6,000 capacity stadium had become too small.[^cite_note-65] For this reason, Leinster have signed a long-term lease with the [Royal Dublin Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dublin_Society) to play home games at the [RDS Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDS_Arena). Donnybrook has since, been improved as a venue with the reconstruction of the grandstand in 2008[^cite_note-66] and remains an important venue for [rugby union](25405.md) in Dublin.[^cite_note-Branch-6] Due to limited space, it is unlikely that Donnybrook will undergo further redevelopment. Leinster A play their [British and Irish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_and_Irish_Cup) games in the stadium and the senior team have continued to hold certain pre-season friendlies in the stadium as well as most Leinster schools cup matches being held at the venue.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

## Supporters

Before the advent of professionalism in the Irish game, provincial rugby games were generally poorly attended. During most of the 1990s, Leinster matches regularly attracted crowds of about 500 to 2,000.[^cite_note-Leinster_Rugby_Comes_of_Age-69] The decision to structure the game professionally via the provincial network through centralised player contracts and the subsequent on-field success achieved by Leinster and the other provinces resulted in a significant increase in support within a decade.[^cite_note-Leinster_Rugby_Comes_of_Age-69] Leinster had 3,700 season ticket holders in 2006, double the number of the previous season.[^cite_note-Leinster_Rugby_Comes_of_Age-69] The Official Leinster Supporters Club was formally established as a club in 2007.[^cite_note-70] The last match at the old [Lansdowne Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansdowne_Road) stadium was against [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Rugby) on 31 December 2006 before it was demolished to make way for the new [Aviva Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviva_Stadium), earning the match the moniker of "The Last Stand". Leinster won the match 20–12, with an attendance of 48,000 – a record at the time.[^cite_note-71] A previous attendance record in the Pro12 was also set at Lansdowne Road, for a game between Leinster and [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_Rugby) which drew a crowd of 30,000.[^cite_note-72] Leinster's supporters were named as 'Player of the Month' for April 2009 following their support in the [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Cup) Quarter Final against [Harlequins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlequin_F.C.) at [The Stoop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stoop).[^cite_note-73]



The Leinster Jet

On several occasions Leinster have set the record for the largest Celtic league and Pro12 attendances. On 2 October 2010, Leinster beat [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_Rugby) 13–9 in the [5th round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Celtic_League) of the league at the [Aviva Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviva_Stadium). This set a new crowd attendance record for a Pro12 game at 50,645.[^cite_note-74] They subsequently set a new record on 29 March 2014 during a sellout match against Munster in which 51,700 fans were in attendance. This Pro12 record was subsequently surpassed by attendances at the [Judgement Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judgement_Day_\(rugby_union\)) fixtures. During the [2014–15 Pro12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Pro12) Leinster had the best support of any club in the PRO12 league with an average attendance of 17,717.[^cite_note-75]

Leinster's [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Heineken_Cup) clash against Munster at [Croke Park](322713.md) on 2 May 2009 set a world record attendance at the time for a club rugby union game with a crowd of 82,208.[^cite_note-76]

## Leinster A

&#x20;Leinster A is the team that represents Leinster in the [British & Irish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_and_Irish_Cup),[^cite_note-78] having won the competition a record two times to date, in the [2012–13 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_British_and_Irish_Cup) as well as the [2013–14 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_British_and_Irish_Cup), also becoming the first and only side to ever successfully defend the trophy. Leinster A also compete in the [All Ireland Inter-provincial Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Interprovincial_Rugby_Championship). Pre-professionalism and a formal Celtic league structure, the main Leinster team competed in the AIIPC. Since the advent of professionalism, the provinces have fielded lesser teams to concentrate on the [Celtic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship). The team is composed of Senior Leinster squad players requiring game time, Development contract & Academy players and, occasionally, AIL players called up from their clubs.

For the 2019–20 season, the Leinster A team was coached by Noel McNamara.[^cite_note-79]

## Sponsorship

### Kit sponsor

From the 1999–2000 season until the 2017–18 season Leinster's kits were supplied by [Canterbury of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_of_New_Zealand) having previously been supplied by [O'Neills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Neills).[^cite_note-81] For the next five seasons starting with the 2018–19 season Leinster's kits were supplied by [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas). From the 2023–24 season, the kits are now supplied by [Castore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castore).[^cite_note-82]

### Main sponsor

Leinster's main shirt sponsor was [ACC Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACC_Loan_Management) from 1992 until the year 2000.[^cite_note-83] [Esat Digifone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esat_Digifone) also featured on the back of the jerseys from 1997.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

From 2001 to 2007 the main sponsor was [Bank of Scotland (Ireland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Scotland_\(Ireland\)).[^cite_note-86]

Since 2007 [Bank of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Ireland), the country's oldest banking institution are Leinster's primary sponsors appearing in the front of their shirt, their sleeves, the top back of their shirt and the front right of their shorts. The Bank of Ireland symbol appeared on Leinster's front right and front left collars. On occasion, the team will wear a shirt adorned with the logo of another sponsor due to a promotion run annually by the bank offering up the sponsorship space to an Irish business by way of a competition to win the right to become a sponsor for a day.[^cite_note-87] During the 2013–14 season the contest was won by Dublin-based meat wholesaler Gahan Meats[^cite_note-88] and for 2014–15 the shirt sponsorship winners were accounting software provider Big Red Cloud.[^cite_note-89] The sponsorship prize package is valued at €50,000 and attracts hundreds of companies keen to be shortlisted each year.[^cite_note-90] The left of Leinster's back shorts had Bank of Ireland between 2009 and 2013 where it was replaced by Bank of Ireland's Twitter address right up until 2015 where it was replaced by [Laya Healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laya_Healthcare).[^cite_note-91] For about a decade, the team's "official airline"' was Ireland's [CityJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityJet).[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

Since the inception of the Celtic league, Leinster have dominated their Irish provincial rivals [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_rugby), with a 44–12 win–loss record. Similarly, Leinster enjoy a 40–9 win–loss ratio against western province [Connacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connacht_Rugby). Leinster also hold a 37–19 head-to-head advantage against arch-rivals [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_rugby), in one of the most intense derbies in world rugby. Of the United Rugby Championship sides, [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_rugby) have the most competitive record against Leinster; all of the league's other sides, excluding South African clubs, have substantial losing records against Leinster. The Welsh side, [Celtic Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Warriors) competed in the Celtic league during the first couple of seasons and have a winning record against Leinster of two wins and zero defeats.

Among European teams, of those who have played at least four matches against Leinster, only [RC Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_Toulon) enjoys a winning record. They have a commanding 4–1 head-to-head lead.
