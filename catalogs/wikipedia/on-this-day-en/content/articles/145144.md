**Ukrainians** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): українці, [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *ukraintsi*, pronounced [\[ʊkrɐˈjin⁽ʲ⁾ts⁽ʲ⁾i\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian))[^cite_note-47] are an [East Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavs) [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) native to [Ukraine](31750.md). Their native tongue is [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language), and the majority adhere to [Eastern Orthodoxy](10186.md). At around 46 million worldwide, Ukrainians are the second largest [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) ethnic group after [Russians](59510.md).[^cite_note-УКРАЇНЦІ-1]

Ukrainians have been [given various names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym_and_exonym) by foreign rulers,[^cite_note-rulers-48] which have included [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth), the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy), the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire), and then [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary). The East Slavic population inhabiting the territories of modern-day Ukraine were known as [Ruthenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenians), referring to the territory of [Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenia); the Ukrainians living under the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) were known as Little Russians, named after the territory of [Little Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Russia).[^cite_note-49]

The ethnonym Ukrainian, which was associated with the [Cossack Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate), was adopted following the [Ukrainian national revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_national_revival) of the late 18th century.[^cite_note-50] The [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks#Ukrainian_Cossacks) are frequently emphasised in modern Ukrainian identity and symbolism, such as in the [Ukrainian national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthem_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-51] [Citizens of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_of_Ukraine) are also called Ukrainians regardless of ethnicity,[^cite_note-multiethnic-52] and many identify themselves as a [civic nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nation).[^cite_note-53]

## Ethnonym

Further information: [Name of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Ukraine)

The modern name *Ukraintsi* (Ukrainians) is derived from *Ukraina* (Ukraine), a name first documented in the *[Kievan Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Chronicle)* under the year 1187. The terms *Ukrainiany* (first recorded in the *[Galician–Volhynian Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician%E2%80%93Volhynian_Chronicle)* under the year 1268[^cite_note-56]), *Ukrainnyky*, and even *narod ukrainskyi* (the Ukrainian people) were used sporadically before *Ukraintsi* attained currency under the influence of the writings of Ukrainian activists in Russian-ruled Ukraine in the 19th century.[^cite_note-57] From the 14th to the 16th centuries, the western portions of the European part of what is now known as Russia, plus the territories of northern Ukraine and Belarus ([Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenia)), were largely known as *Rus*, continuing the tradition of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'). People of these territories were usually called *[Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenia)* or *[Rusyns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenians)* (known as Ruthenians in [Western](33800.md) and Central Europe).[^cite_note-58]

The [Ukrainian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) is, like modern Russian and Belarusian, a descendent of Old East Slavic.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] In Western and Central Europe it was known by the exonym "Ruthenian". In the 16th and 17th centuries, with the establishment of the [Zaporozhian Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Sich), names of Ukraine and Ukrainian began to be used in Sloboda Ukraine.[^cite_note-61] After the decline of the Zaporozhian Sich and the establishment of [Imperial Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) hegemony in Left Bank Ukraine, Ukrainians became more widely known by Russians as "*Little Russians*", with the majority of Ukrainian elites espousing [Little Russian identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Russian_identity) and adopting the Russian language, as Ukrainian was outlawed in almost all contexts.[^cite_note-luchenko.com-62]\[^cite_note-[http://litopys.org.ua-63](http://litopys.org.ua-63/)][^cite_note-ReferenceA-64]

This [exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym_and_exonym)—regarded now as a humiliating imperialist imposition—did not spread widely among the peasantry, which constituted the majority of the population.[^cite_note-Plokhy2008-65] Ukrainian peasants still referred to their country as "Ukraine" (a name associated with the Zaporozhian Sich, with the [Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate) and with their struggle against Poles, Russians, Turks and Crimean Tatars) and to themselves and their language as [Ruthenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyns)/[Ruthenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenian_language).\[^cite_note-[http://litopys.org.ua-63](http://litopys.org.ua-63/)][^cite_note-ReferenceA-64]

With the publication of [Ivan Kotliarevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Kotliarevsky)'s *Eneyida* (Aeneid) in 1798, which established the modern [Ukrainian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language), and with the subsequent Romantic revival of national traditions and culture, the [ethnonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnonym) *Ukrainians* and the notion of a Ukrainian language came into more prominence at the beginning of the 19th century and gradually replaced the words "Rusyns" and "Ruthenian(s)". In areas outside the control of the Russian/Soviet state until the mid-20th century ([Western Ukraine](3258511.md)), Ukrainians were known by their pre-existing names for much longer.[^cite_note-luchenko.com-62]\[^cite_note-[http://litopys.org.ua-63](http://litopys.org.ua-63/)][^cite_note-ReferenceA-64][^cite_note-Wolczuk2001-66] The appellation *Ukrainians* initially came into common usage in [Central Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Ukraine)[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] and did not take hold in [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)) and [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina) until the latter part of the 19th century, in [Transcarpathia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Ruthenia) not until the 1930s, and in the [Prešov Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre%C5%A1ov_Region) not until the late 1940s.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

The modern name *Ukraintsi* (Ukrainians) derives from *Ukraina* (Ukraine), a name first documented in 1187.[^cite_note-72] Several scientific theories attempt to explain the [etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) of the term. According to the traditional theory, it derives from the Proto-Slavic root *\*kraj-*, which has two meanings—one being the homeland as in "*nash rodnoi kraj*" (our homeland), and the other being "edge, border"—and originally had the sense of "periphery", "borderland" or "frontier region".[^cite_note-litopys.org.ua-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-gaida-75] According to another theory, the term *ukraina* should be distinguished from the term *okraina*: whereas the latter term means "borderland", the former has the meaning of "cut-off piece of land", thus acquiring the connotation of "our land", "land allotted to us".[^cite_note-litopys.org.ua-73][^cite_note-76]

In the last three centuries, the population of Ukraine experienced periods of [polonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonization) and [russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification), but preserved a common culture and a sense of common identity.[^cite_note-encyclopediaofukraine.com-77][^cite_note-78]

## Geographic distribution

Most ethnic Ukrainians live in Ukraine, where they make up over three-quarters of the population. The largest population of Ukrainians outside of Ukraine lives in Russia where about 1.9 million Russian citizens identify as Ukrainian, while millions of others (primarily in [southern Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Federal_District) and [Siberia](29639.md)) have some Ukrainian ancestry.[^cite_note-rus2010-79] The inhabitants of the [Kuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban), for example, have vacillated among three identities: Ukrainian, Russian (an identity supported by the [Soviet regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Soviet_Union)) and "[Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack)".[^cite_note-Ukrainians_IEU-80] Approximately 800,000 people of Ukrainian ancestry live in the [Russian Far East](173135.md) in an area known historically as "[Green Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Ukraine)".[^cite_note-81]

In a 2011 national poll of Ukraine, 49% of Ukrainians said they had relatives living in Russia.[^cite_note-al-jazeera-82]

According to some previous assumptions, an estimated number of almost 2.4 million people of Ukrainian origin live in North America (1,359,655 in Canada and 1,028,492 in the United States). Large numbers of Ukrainians also live in Brazil (600,000);[^cite_note-83] Kazakhstan (338,022); Moldova (325,235); Argentina (305,000); (Germany) (272,000); Italy (234,354); Belarus (225,734); Uzbekistan (124,602); the Czech Republic (110,245); Spain (90,530–100,000) and Romania (51,703–200,000). There are also large Ukrainian communities in such countries as Latvia, Portugal, France, Australia, Paraguay, the UK, Israel, Slovakia, Kyrgyzstan, Austria, Uruguay and the former [Yugoslavia](34244.md). Generally, the Ukrainian diaspora is present in more than 120 countries of the world.

The number of Ukrainians in Poland amounted to some 51,000 people in 2011 (according to the [Polish census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_census_of_2011)).[^cite_note-84] Since 2014, the country has experienced a large increase in immigration from Ukraine.[^cite_note-85] More recent data put the number of Ukrainian migrant workers at 1.2[^cite_note-interia-86]–1.3 million in 2016.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

In the last decades of the 19th century, many Ukrainians were forced by the [Tsarist autocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsarist_autocracy) to move to the Asian regions of Russia, while many of their counterpart Slavs under [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) rule emigrated to the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World) in search of work and better economic opportunities.[^cite_note-89] According to some sources in the first decade of the 2000s, around 20 million people outside Ukraine identify as having Ukrainian ethnicity;[^cite_note-ucc.ca-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] however, the official data of the respective countries calculated together does not show more than 10 million. In any event, Ukrainians have one of the largest [diasporas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora) in the world.

## Origin

Further information: [Early Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Slavs), [East Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavs), [Ruthenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenians), and [Prehistoric Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Ukraine)

The [East Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavs) emerged from the undifferentiated [early Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Slavs) in the [Slavic migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_migrations) of the 6th and 7th centuries CE. The state of [Kievan Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus) united the East Slavs during the 9th to 13th centuries. East Slavic tribes cited as "proto-Ukrainian" include the [Volhynians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynians), [Derevlianians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drevlyans), [Polianians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polans_\(eastern\)), and [Siverianians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severians) and the less significant [Ulychians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulichs), [Tivertsians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tivertsi), and [White Croats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Croats).[^cite_note-93] The [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths) historian [Jordanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanes) and 6th-century [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) authors named two groups that lived in the south-east of Europe: *Sclavins* (western Slavs) and [Antes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antes_\(people\)). Polianians are identified as the founders of the city of [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev) and as playing the key role in the formation of the Kievan Rus' state.[^cite_note-94]

At the beginning of the 9th century, [Varangians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangians) used the waterways of Eastern Europe for military raids and trade, particularly the [trade route from the Varangians to the Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks). Until the 11th century these Varangians also served as key mercenary troops for a number of princes in medieval Kiev, as well as for some of the [Byzantine emperors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_emperor), while others occupied key administrative positions in Kievan Rus' society, and eventually became slavicized.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] Besides other cultural traces, several Ukrainian names show traces of [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsemen) origins as a result of influences from that period.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

Differentiation between separate East Slavic groups began to emerge in the later medieval period; and an East Slavic [dialect continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect_continuum) developed within the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth), with the [Ruthenian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenian_language) emerging as a written standard. The active development of a concept of a Ukrainian nation and the Ukrainian language began with the [Ukrainian National Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_National_Revival) in the early 19th century in times when Ruthenians (Русини) changed their name because of the regional name. In the [Soviet era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (1917–1991), [official historiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography_in_the_Soviet_Union) emphasised "the cultural unity of 'proto-Ukrainians' and 'proto-Russians' in the fifth and sixth centuries".[^cite_note-Yekelchyk2004-99]

A poll conducted in April 2022 by the polling organisation [Rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rating_\(sociological_group\)) found that the vast majority (91%) of Ukrainians (excluding the [Russian-occupied territories of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-occupied_territories_of_Ukraine)) do not support the thesis that "Russians and Ukrainians are [one people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Russian_nation)".[^cite_note-100]

### Genetics and genomics

See also: [Genetic history of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_Europe)



Neolithic migrations c. 5000–4000 BC. The people of the Proto-Indo-European [Sredny Stog culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sredny_Stog_culture) were the result of a genetic admixture between the [Eastern European hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Hunter-Gatherer) and [Caucasus hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_hunter-gatherer).

Ukrainians, like most Europeans, largely descend from three distinct lineages:[^cite_note-Indo-European-101] Mesolithic [hunter-gatherers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer), descended from populations associated with the Paleolithic [Epigravettian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigravettian) culture;[^cite_note-102] Neolithic [Early European Farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_Farmers), who migrated from Anatolia during the [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution) 9,000 years ago;[^cite_note-103] and [Yamnaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamnaya_culture) [Steppe pastoralists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Steppe_Herders), who expanded into Europe from the [Pontic–Caspian steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic%E2%80%93Caspian_steppe) of Ukraine and southern Russia in the context of [Indo-European migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_migrations) 5,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Indo-European-101]

&#x20;In a survey of 97 genomes for diversity in full genome sequences among self-identified Ukrainians from Ukraine, a study identified more than 13 million genetic variants, representing about a quarter of the total genetic diversity discovered in Europe.[^cite_note-104] Among these, nearly 500,000 were previously undocumented and likely to be unique for this population, medically relevant mutations whose prevalence in the Ukrainian genomes differed significantly compared to other European genome sequences, particularly from Western Europe and Russia.[^cite_note-105] Ukrainian genomes form a single cluster positioned between the Northern European populations on one side, and Western European populations on the other.<https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7804371/>



Principal Component Analysis of European populations from the Genome Ukraine Project

&#x20;There was a significant overlap with Central European populations as well as with people from the [Balkans](4829.md).&#x20;

The Ukrainian gene-pool includes the following Y-[haplogroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup), in order from the most prevalent:[^cite_note-106]

- [R1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R1a) (43%)

- [I2a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_I-M438) (23%)

- [R1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R1b) (8%)

- [E1b1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E1b1b) (7%)

- [I1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_I-M253) (5%)

- [N1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_N-M231) (5%)

- [J2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_J-M172) (4%)

- [G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_G-M201) (3%)

- [T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_T-M184) (1%)

Roughly all R1a Ukrainians carry [R1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R1a)-Z282; it has been found significantly only in Eastern Europe.[^cite_note-Luca_Giacomo_Benincasa_Popa_2006_pp._132–139-107] [Chernivtsi Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernivtsi_Oblast) is the only region in Ukraine where Haplogroup I2a occurs more frequently than R1a, much less frequent even in [Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk_Oblast).[^cite_note-108] In comparison to their northern and eastern neighbours, Ukrainians have a similar percentage of [Haplogroup R1a-Z280](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1a) (43%) in their population—compare [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians), [Russians](59510.md) and [Lithuanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians) (55%, 46%, and 42% respectively). Populations in Eastern Europe that have never been Slavic do as well. Ukrainians in Chernivtsi Oblast (near the Romanian border) have a higher percentage of [I2a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_I_\(Y-DNA\)) as opposed to R1a, which is typical of the Balkan region, but a smaller percentage than Russians of the N1c1 lineage found among Finno-Ugric, Baltic and Siberian populations, and also less [R1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R1b) than [West Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavs).[^cite_note-Semino2000-109][^cite_note-Varzari2006-110][^cite_note-Pericic2005-111] In terms of haplogroup distribution, the genetic pattern of Ukrainians most closely resembles that of Belarusians.&#x20;

The presence of the N1c lineage is explained by a contribution of the assimilated [Finno-Ugric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finno-Ugric_peoples) tribes.[^cite_note-112]

Within Ukraine and adjacent areas, there are several other distinct ethnic sub-groups, especially in western Ukraine: places like [Zakarpattia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Ruthenia) and [Halychyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)). ## History

Further information: [History of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ukraine)

Ukraine has had a very turbulent history, a fact explained by its geographical position.&#x20;

### Kievan Rus'

In the 9th century, the [Varangians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangians) from [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) conquered the proto-Slavic tribes on the territory of today's Ukraine, Belarus and western Russia and laid the groundwork for the [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') state. The ancestors of the Ukrainian nation, such as [Polianians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polans_\(eastern\)), had an important role in the development and culturalisation of the Kievan Rus' state. The internecine wars between the princes of Rus', which began after the death of [Yaroslav the Wise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslav_the_Wise),[^cite_note-115] led to the political fragmentation of the state into a number of principalities. Because the princes' quarrelling left Kievan Rus' vulnerable to foreign attacks, the invasions of the Mongols in 1236 and 1240 finally destroyed the state. Another important state in Ukrainians history is the [Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia) (1199–1349).[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

### Cossack Hetmanate

The third important state in Ukrainian history is the [Cossack Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate). Since the late 15th century, the Cossacks of [Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_\(region\)) controlled the lower bends of the Dnieper River between Russia, Poland, and the [Tatars of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate), with the fortified capital, [Zaporozhian Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Sich). Hetman [Bohdan Khmelnytsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohdan_Khmelnytsky) is one of the most celebrated and at the same time most controversial political figures in Ukraine's early-modern history. A brilliant military leader, his greatest achievement in the process of national revolution was the formation of the Cossack Hetmanate state of the Zaporozhian Host (1648–1782).&#x20;

The period of the [Ruin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ruin_\(Ukrainian_history\)) in the late 17th century is characterised by the disintegration of Ukrainian statehood and general decline. During the Ruin, Ukraine became divided along the Dnieper River into [Left-Bank Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-Bank_Ukraine) and [Right-Bank Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-Bank_Ukraine), and the two halves became hostile to each other. The Ukrainian leaders during this period are considered largely opportunists and men of little vision who could not muster broad popular support for their policies.[^cite_note-118] At the end of the century, there were roughly 4 million Ukrainians.[^cite_note-119]

### Short-lived independence



Mykhailo Hrushevsky

In the final stages of the First World War, a powerful struggle for an independent Ukrainian state developed in the central Ukrainian territories, which, until 1917, were part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). The newly established Ukrainian government, the [Central Rada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Rada), headed by [Mykhailo Hrushevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykhailo_Hrushevsky), issued four universals, the Fourth of which, dated 22 January 1918, declared the independence and sovereignty of the [Ukrainian National Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_National_Republic) (UNR) on 25 January 1918. The session of the Central Rada on 29 April 1918 ratified the Constitution of the UNR and elected Hrushevsky president.[^cite_note-encyclopediaofukraine.com-77]

### Soviet period

See also: [Executed Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executed_Renaissance) and [Ukrainization § Early 1930s (reversal of Ukrainization policies)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainization#Early_1930s_\(reversal_of_Ukrainization_policies\))



A girl in [Kharkiv](2788558.md) during the [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor)

During the 1920s, under the Ukrainisation policy pursued by the national Communist leadership of [Mykola Skrypnyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykola_Skrypnyk), Soviet leadership encouraged a national renaissance in the Ukrainian culture and language. Ukrainisation was part of the Soviet-wide policy of [Korenisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korenisation) (literally "indigenisation").

In 1932–1933, millions of Ukrainians were starved to death by the Soviet regime, which led to a [famine](51329.md) known as the [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor).[^cite_note-120] The Soviet regime remained silent about the Holodomor and provided no aid to the victims or the survivors. But news and information about what was going on reached the West and evoked public responses in Polish-ruled Western Ukraine and in the [Ukrainian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_diaspora). Since the 1990s, the independent Ukrainian state, particularly under President [Viktor Yushchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yushchenko), the Ukrainian mass media and academic institutions, many foreign governments, most Ukrainian scholars, and many foreign scholars have viewed and written about the Holodomor as genocide and issued official declarations and publications to that effect. Modern scholarly estimates of the direct loss of human life due to the [famine](51329.md) range between 2.6 million,[^cite_note-Vallin-121][^cite_note-Vallin2-122] (3–3.5 million),[^cite_note-HowMany-123] and 12 million,[^cite_note-124] although much higher numbers are usually published in the media and cited in political debates.[^cite_note-finn-125] As of March 2008, the [parliament of Ukraine](844993.md) and the governments of several countries, including the United States, have recognised the Holodomor as an act of [genocide](12441.md).[^cite_note-126]

Following the [Invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) in September 1939, German and Soviet troops divided the territory of Poland. Thus, [Eastern Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Galicia) and [Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia) with their Ukrainian population became part of Soviet Ukraine. When the German armies invaded the Soviet Union on 22 June 1941, those regions temporarily became part of the Nazi-controlled [Reichskommissariat Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine) (Ukrainian Realm Commissariat). In total, the number of ethnic Ukrainians who fought in the ranks of the Soviet Army is estimated from 4.5 million to 7 million. The pro-Soviet partisan guerrilla resistance in Ukraine is estimated to have numbered 47,800 from the start of occupation to 500,000 at its peak in 1944, with about 50% being ethnic Ukrainian. Of the estimated 8.6 million Soviet troop losses, 1.4 million were ethnic Ukrainians.

In 1943, under the command of Roman Shukhevych, the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (*[Ukrayins'ka Povstans'ka Armiia](1045217.md)*, or UPA) began ethnic cleansing. Shukhevych was one of the perpetrators of the Galicia-Volhynia massacres of tens of thousands of Polish civilians. It is unclear to what extent Shuchevych was responsible for the massacres of Poles in Volhynia, but he certainly condoned them after some time and also directed the massacres of Poles in Eastern Galicia. Historian Per Anders Rudling has accused the Ukrainian diaspora and Ukrainian academics of "ignoring, glossing over, or outright denying" Shuchevych's role in this and other war crimes.

## Ethnic/national identity



[Cossack Mamay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Mamay), one of several [national personifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_personification) of Ukrainians

The watershed period in the development of modern Ukrainian national consciousness was the struggle for independence during the creation of the [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic) from 1917 to 1921.[^cite_note-127] A concerted effort to reverse the growth of Ukrainian national consciousness was begun by the regime of [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) in the late 1920s, continuing with minor interruptions until the most recent times. The man-made [famine of 1932–33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor), the deportations of the so-called [kulaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulak), the physical annihilation of the nationally conscious intelligentsia, and terror in general were used to destroy and subdue the Ukrainian nation.[^cite_note-128] Even after Joseph Stalin's death, the concept of a Russified though multiethnic Soviet people was officially promoted, according to which non-Russian nationals were relegated to second-class status. Despite this, many Ukrainians played prominent roles in the Soviet Union, including such public figures as [Semen Tymoshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Timoshenko).

The creation of a sovereign and independent Ukraine in 1991, however, pointed to the failure of the policy of the "merging of nations" and to the enduring strength of the Ukrainian national consciousness.&#x20;

[Biculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biculturalism) is especially present in southeastern Ukraine, where there is a significant Russian minority. Historical colonisation of Ukraine is one reason that creates confusion about national identity to this day.[^cite_note-129] Many citizens of Ukraine have adopted Ukrainian national identity in the past 20 years. According to the concept of nationality dominant in Eastern Europe, the Ukrainians are people whose native language is Ukrainian (an objective criterion) whether or not they are nationally conscious, and all those who identify themselves as Ukrainian (a subjective criterion) whether or not they speak Ukrainian.[^cite_note-130]

Attempts to introduce a territorial-political concept of Ukrainian nationality on the Western European model (presented by political philosopher [Vyacheslav Lypynsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Lypynsky)) were unsuccessful until the 1990s. Territorial loyalty has also been manifested by the historical national minorities living in Ukraine. The official declaration of Ukrainian sovereignty of 16 July 1990 stated that "citizens of the Republic of all nationalities constitute the people of Ukraine."[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Ukraine)

Due to Ukraine's geographical location, its culture primarily exhibits Eastern European influence, as well as Central European to an extent (primarily in the western region). Over the years, it has been influenced by movements such as those brought about during the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance). Today, the country is somewhat culturally divided with the western regions bearing a stronger Central European influence and the eastern regions showing a significant Russian influence. A strong [Christian culture](5211.md) was predominant for many centuries, although Ukraine was also the centre of conflict between the Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Islamic spheres of influence.

### Language

Main article: [Ukrainian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language)

See also: [Ivan Kotliarevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Kotliarevsky), [Russification of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification_of_Ukraine), and [Surzhyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surzhyk)



Population of those whose mother tongue is Ukrainian in Ukraine (2001); the Russian linguistic influence in the south and east is noticeable

Ukrainian (украї́нська мо́ва, *ukraі́nska móva*) is the sole [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) in Ukraine.[^cite_note-rulers-48] It belongs to the [East Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavic_languages) branch of the [Slavic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages). Written Ukrainian uses the [Ukrainian alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_alphabet), one of many alphabets based on the [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_alphabets).[^cite_note-133] The language is a lineal descendant of the colloquial [Old East Slavic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_East_Slavic_language) of the medieval state of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'), which first split into [Ruthenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenian_language) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language).[^cite_note-Ukrainian-134]<sup>: 2–3 </sup> The Ruthenian languages then evolved into modern-day Ukrainian, [Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language) and [Rusyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyn_language).[^cite_note-Ukrainian-134]<sup>: 53–60 </sup> In modern-day Ukraine, most of its population are also fluent in Russian and many use it as their native tongue.[^cite_note-multiethnic-52]

Comparisons are often made between Ukrainian and Russian, yet there is more [mutual intelligibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_intelligibility) with Belarusian[^cite_note-135] and a very close [lexical distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexical_similarity) between the two.[^cite_note-136]<sup>: 13 </sup> Historically, state-enforced [Russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification) saw the Ukrainian language banned as a subject from schools and as a language of instruction in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).[^cite_note-137] The linguistic oppression continued in various ways while Ukraine was a part of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union);[^cite_note-138] however, the language continued to be used throughout the country, especially in [western Ukraine](3258511.md).[^cite_note-139]

### Religions

Main article: [Religion in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Ukraine)



Historic [Saint Sophia's Cathedral, Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sophia_Cathedral,_Kyiv)

Ukraine was inhabited by [pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism) tribes until [Byzantine rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Rite) Christianity was introduced by the turn of the first millennium. It was imagined by later writers who sought to put Kievan Rus' Christianity on the same level of primacy as Byzantine Christianity that [Apostle Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_the_Apostle) himself visited the site where the city of [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev) would be later built.

However, it was only by the 10th century that the emerging state, the Kievan Rus', became influenced by the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). The first known conversion was by the Princess [Saint Olga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_of_Kiev), who came to [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) in 945 or 957. Several years later, her grandson, Prince [Vladimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_the_Great) baptised his people in the [Dnieper River](380138.md). This began a long history of the dominance of the [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) in Ruthenia (Ukraine).

Ukrainians are majority [Eastern Orthodox](10186.md) Christians, forming the [second largest ethno-linguistic group among Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_contemporary_ethnic_groups) in the world.[^cite_note-Pew20152016-140][^cite_note-141] The autocephalous [Orthodox Church of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Church_of_Ukraine), headed by [Metropolitan Epiphanius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Kyiv), is the most common church; whereas in the small areas of the country, the [Ukrainian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_\(Moscow_Patriarchate\)), who were under the jurisdiction of the [Moscow Patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_\(Moscow_Patriarchate\)), is more common. The [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) had an impact on the religious identity of some Ukrainians.



[St. George's Cathedral, Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George's_Cathedral,_Lviv)

In the Western region known as [Halychyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)), the [Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church), one of the [Eastern Rite Catholic Churches](214903.md), has a strong membership. Since the fall of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), there has also been a growth of [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) churches ([Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists), [Evangelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism), [Pentecostalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism)).[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143] Some Ukrainians are members of the [Seventh-day Adventist Church](28632.md) and [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses). In addition, there are ethnic minorities practising other religions: [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars) ([Islam](6037917.md)), and [Jews](25955086.md) and [Karaites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaite_Judaism) ([Judaism](15624.md)).

A 2020 survey conducted by the [Razumkov Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razumkov_Centre) found that majority of Ukrainian populations was adhering to Christianity (81.9%). Of these Christians, 75.4% are Eastern Orthodox (34% of the Orthodox Church of Ukraine and 13.8% of the Moscow Patriarchate, and 27.6% are simply Orthodox); 8.2% are Greek Catholics; 7.1% are simply Christians; and a further 1.9% are Protestants and 0.4% are [Latin Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church_in_Ukraine).[^cite_note-144] As of 2016, 16.3% of the population claims no religious affiliation, and 1.7% adheres to other religions.[^cite_note-Razumkov2016-145] According to the same survey, 70% of the people of Ukraine declare themselves believers but do not belong to any church; 8.8% identifies with none of the denominations; and another 5.6% identify themselves as non-believers.[^cite_note-Razumkov2016-145]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Ukrainian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_cuisine)



[Borscht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borscht) with [smetana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smetana_\(dairy_product\)) ([sour cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sour_cream))

Ukrainian cuisine has been formed by the nation's tumultuous history, geography, culture and social customs. [Chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken) is the most consumed type of [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein), accounting for about half of the meat intake, followed by [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork) and [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef).[^cite_note-146]<sup>: 12 </sup> Vegetables such as [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato), [cabbages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage), [mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushroom) and [beetroots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beetroot) are widely consumed.[^cite_note-147] [Pickled vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickling) are considered a delicacy.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] [Salo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salo_\(food\)), which is [cured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curing_\(food_preservation\)) pork fat, is considered the national delicacy.[^cite_note-150] Widely used [herbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb) include [dill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dill), [parsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsley), [basil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil), [coriander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriander) and [chives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chives).[^cite_note-hercules-151]

Ukraine is often called the "[Breadbasket of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadbasket#Europe)", and its plentiful [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain) and [cereal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal) resources such as [rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye) and [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) play an important part in its cuisine; essential in making various kinds of bread.[^cite_note-bbccuisine-152][^cite_note-153] [Chernozem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernozem), the country's black-coloured highly fertile soil, produces some of the world's most flavourful crops.[^cite_note-154]

Popular traditional dishes *_[varenyky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varenyky)_* ([dumpling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumpling#European#Central_and_Eastern_Europe)), *[nalysnyky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalysnyky)* ([crêpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%AApe)), *[kapusnyak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage_soup)* (cabbage [soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup)), *nudli* (dumpling [stew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stew)), [borscht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borscht) ([sour soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sour_soups)) and *[holubtsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go%C5%82%C4%85bki)* ([cabbage roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage_roll)).[^cite_note-bbccuisine-152] Among traditional baked goods are decorated [korovai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korovai) and [paska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paska_\(bread\)) ([Easter bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_bread)).[^cite_note-155] Ukrainian specialties also include [chicken Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_Kiev)[^cite_note-hercules-151] and [Kyiv cake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_cake). Popular drinks include *uzvar* ([kompot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kompot)),[^cite_note-hercules-151][^cite_note-hill-156] *[ryazhanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazhenka)*[^cite_note-157] and *_[horilka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horilka)_*.[^cite_note-hercules-151][^cite_note-hill-156] [Liquor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquor) (spirits) is the most consumed type of [alcoholic beverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage).[^cite_note-pmid-158] Alcohol consumption has seen a stark decrease, although per capita it remains [among the highest the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_alcohol_consumption_per_capita).[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-pmid-158]

### Music



[Odesa Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_Opera_and_Ballet_Theatre)

Main article: [Music of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Ukraine)

Ukrainian music incorporates a diversity of external cultural influences. It also has a very strong indigenous [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_peoples) and Christian uniqueness, the elements of which elements were used among many neighbouring nations.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

Ukrainian folk oral literature, poetry and songs (such as the *dumas*) are among the most distinctive ethnocultural features of Ukrainians as a people. Religious music existed in Ukraine before the official adoption of Christianity, in the form of plainsong *obychnyi spiv* or *musica practica*. Traditional Ukrainian music is easily recognised by its somewhat melancholy tone. It first became known outside of Ukraine during the 15th century as musicians from Ukraine would perform before the royal courts in Poland (later in Russia).

A large number of famous musicians around the world was educated or born in Ukraine, among them famous names like [Dmitry Bortniansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Bortniansky), [Sergei Prokofiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Prokofiev) and [Myroslav Skoryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myroslav_Skoryk). Ukraine is also the rarely acknowledged musical heartland of the former [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), home to its first professional music academy, which opened in the mid-18th century and produced numerous early musicians and composers.[^cite_note-162]

### Dance

Main article: [Ukrainian dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_dance)



*[Hopak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopak)*, a Ukrainian dance&#x20;

[Ukrainian dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_dance) refers to the traditional [folk dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_dance) of the peoples of Ukraine. Today, Ukrainian dance is primarily represented by what [ethnographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography), [folklorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore) and dance historians refer to as Ukrainian folk-stage dances: stylised representations of traditional dances and their characteristic movements that have been choreographed for [concert dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_dance) performances. This stylised art form has so permeated the [culture of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Ukraine) that very few purely traditional forms of Ukrainian dance remain today.

Ukrainian dance is often described as energetic, fast-paced and entertaining, and along with traditional Easter eggs (*[pysanky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pysanky)*), it is a characteristic example of Ukrainian culture recognised and appreciated throughout the world.

### Symbols

Main articles: [Flag of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ukraine) and [Coat of arms of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Ukraine)

- Coat of arms of Ukraine

  [Coat of arms of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Ukraine)

- Flag of Ukraine

  [Flag of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ukraine)

Ukraine's national symbols include [its flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ukraine) and [its coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Ukraine).

The national flag of Ukraine is a blue and yellow bicolour rectangle, with the colour fields of the same form and equal size and the colours representing a blue sky above yellow fields of wheat.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] The flag was designed for the convention of the [Supreme Ruthenian Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Ruthenian_Council), meeting in [Lviv](49968.md) in October 1848. Its colours were based on the coat-of-arms of the [Kingdom of Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia).[^cite_note-166]

The [coat of arms of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Ukraine) features the same colours found on the [Ukrainian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ukraine): a blue [shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shield) with yellow [trident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trident)—the symbol of ancient [East Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Slavic_people) tribes that once lived in Ukraine and later adopted by [Ruthenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenians) and [Kievan Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus) rulers.
