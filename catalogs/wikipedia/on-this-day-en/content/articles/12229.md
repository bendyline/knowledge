A **government** is the system or group of people which lead a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)).[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:1-2][^cite_note-EncBrit-3] As such, it is responsible for directing the only entity whose use of force is socially acceptable.[^cite_note-\:2-4] Governments have come in many forms, whether dominated by one person ([autocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracy)) a small ruling class ([oligarchy](22315.md)), or the masses ([democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy)).[^cite_note-EncBrit-3]

The main types of modern [political systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_system) recognized are [democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy), [totalitarian regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian_regime), and, sitting between these two, [authoritarian regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) with a variety of [hybrid regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_regime).[^cite_note-Dobratz_2015_p._47-5][^cite_note-LinzLinz2000-6] Modern classification systems also include [monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy) as a standalone entity or as a hybrid system of the main three.[^cite_note-Garcia-AlexanderWooCarlson2017-7][^cite_note-8]

## Definitions and etymology

A government is the [system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System) to [govern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance) a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)) or community. The *[Cambridge Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Advanced_Learner's_Dictionary)* defines government as, "the system used for controlling a country, city, or group of people", or "an organization that officially manages and controls a country or region, creating laws, collecting taxes, providing public services".[^cite_note-9] It is the major or dominant organizing power in modern societies.[^cite_note-10] While all types of organizations have governance, the word *government* is often used more specifically to refer to the approximately 200 [independent national governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states) on Earth, as well as their subsidiary organizations, such as [state and provincial governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_government) as well as [local governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmelserBaltes2001\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_July_2022\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (July_2022)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-11]

The word *government* derives from the Greek verb κυβερνάω \[*kubernáo*] meaning *to steer* with a [gubernaculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gubernaculum_\(classical\)) (rudder), the metaphorical sense being attested in the literature of [classical antiquity](252905.md), including [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato)'s [Ship of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_State).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrock201353–62-12] In [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English), "government" sometimes refers to what is also known as a "[ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(collective_executive\))" or an "[administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administration_\(government\))", i.e., the policies and government officials of a particular executive or governing [coalition](6036.md). Finally, *government* is also sometimes used in English as a [synonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synonym) for rule or governance.[^cite_note-13]

## History

Main articles: [Political history of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_history_of_the_world) and [Political philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_philosophy)

### Earliest governments

The moment and place that the phenomenon of human government developed is lost in time; however, history does record the formations of early governments. About 5,000 years ago, the first small city-states appeared.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChristian2004245-14] By the third to second millenniums BC, some of these had developed into larger governed areas: [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer), [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), the [Indus Valley civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilization), and the [Yellow River civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Neolithic_cultures_of_China).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChristian2004294-15]

One reason that explains the emergence of governments includes agriculture. Since the [Neolithic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Revolution), agriculture has been an efficient method to create food surplus. This enabled people to specialize in non-agricultural activities. Some of them included being able to rule over others as an external authority. Others included social experimentation with diverse governance models. Both these activities formed the basis of governments.[^cite_note-Eagly99-16] These governments gradually became more complex as agriculture supported larger and denser populations, creating new [interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture) and [social pressures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_issue) that the government needed to control. [David Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Christian_\(historian\)) explains

> As farming populations gathered in larger and denser communities, interactions between different groups increased and the social pressure rose until, in a striking parallel with star formation, new structures suddenly appeared, together with a new level of complexity. Like stars, cities and states reorganize and energize the smaller objects within their gravitational field.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChristian2004245-14]

Another explanation includes the need to properly manage infrastructure projects such as water infrastructure. Historically, this required centralized administration and complex social organisation, as seen in regions like Mesopotamia.[^cite_note-Fukuyama-2012-17] However, there is archaeological evidence that shows similar successes with more egalitarian and decentralized complex societies.[^cite_note-18]

### Classification

In political science, it has long been a goal to create a typology or taxonomy of [polities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polities), as typologies of political systems are not obvious.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewellen2003\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_July_2022\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (July_2022)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-21] Given the government's role in managing the state,[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:1-2][^cite_note-EncBrit-3] any taxonomy of polities would need to include all forms of [monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly_on_violence) on [legitimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_legitimacy) force to be complete.[^cite_note-\:2-4]

Superficially, all governments have an official *[de jure](43594.md)* or ideal form. The United States is a federal constitutional republic, while the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) was a federal [socialist republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_state). However, self-identification is not objective, and as Kopstein and Lichbach argue, defining [regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regime) can be tricky, especially *[de facto](43594.md)*, when both its government and its economy deviate in practice.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKopsteinLichbach20054-22] For example, [Voltaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltaire) argued that "the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) is neither Holy, nor Roman, nor an Empire".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERenna2015-23] In practice, the Soviet Union was a centralized autocratic one-party state under [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin).

Identifying a form of government can be challenging because many [political systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_system) originate from socio-economic movements, and the parties that carry those movements into power often name themselves after those ideologies. These parties may have competing political ideologies and strong ties to particular forms of government. As a result, the movements themselves can sometimes be mistakenly considered as forms of government, rather than the ideologies that influence the governing system.[^cite_note-24]

Other complications include general non-consensus or deliberate "[distortion or bias](142690.md)" of reasonable technical definitions of political ideologies and associated forms of governing, due to the nature of politics in the modern era. For example: The meaning of "conservatism" in the United States has little in common with the way the word's definition is used elsewhere. As Ribuffo notes, "what Americans now call conservatism much of the world calls liberalism or [neoliberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism)"; a "conservative" in Finland would be labelled a "[socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist)" in the United States.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERibuffo20112–6quote_on_p._6-25] Since the 1950s, conservatism in the United States has been chiefly associated with [right-wing politics](56522.md) and the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Republican_Party_\(United_States\)). However, during the era of [segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States) many [Southern Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Democrats) were conservatives, and they played a key role in the [conservative coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_coalition) that controlled Congress from 1937 to 1963.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrederickson200012-26][^cite_note-27]

### Social-political ambiguity

Opinions vary by individuals concerning the types and properties of governments that exist. "Shades of gray" are commonplace in any government and its corresponding classification. Even the most liberal democracies limit rival political activity to one extent or another while the most tyrannical dictatorships must organize a broad base of support thereby creating difficulties for "[pigeonholing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeonholing)" governments into narrow categories. Examples include the claims of the [United States as being a plutocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutocracy#United_States) rather than a democracy since some American voters believe elections are being manipulated by wealthy [Super PACs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_PACs).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFreeland2012-28] Some consider that government is to be reconceptualised where in times of climatic change the needs and desires of the individual are reshaped to generate sufficiency for all.[^cite_note-29]

## Measurement of governing

The quality of a government can be measured by [Government effectiveness index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_effectiveness_index), which relates to [political efficacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_efficacy) and [state capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capacity).[^cite_note-Guisan-30] If a government no longer directs a monopoly on force, or if that monopoly loses legitimacy, it is no longer leading the state.[^cite_note-\:2-4] The [political bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_bias) of a government can be measured relative to the [median voter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_voter_theorem).[^cite_note-m117-31] The [fiscal sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_sustainability) of a government measures the [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability) of [fiscal policies](132645.md), [government budget balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_budget_balance) and the [fiscal gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_gap) in the long-term.[^cite_note-Bohn_Sustainability_of_Fiscal_Policy-32] For some countries the current government budget balances and [unfunded mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfunded_mandate) were found unsustainable,[^cite_note-33] with higher burdens for younger generations.[^cite_note-u688-34]

[Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato) in his book *[The Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_\(Plato\))* (375 BC) divided governments into five basic types (four being existing forms and one being Plato's ideal form, which exists "only in speech"):[^cite_note-Abjorensen2019-35]

- [Aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy) (rule by [law and order](25166191.md), like ideal traditional "benevolent" kingdoms that are not tyrannical)

- [Timocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timocracy) (rule by honor and duty, like a "benevolent" military; Sparta as an example)

- [Oligarchy](22315.md) (rule by wealth and market-based-ethics, like a [laissez-faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire) capitalist state)

- [Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) (rule by pure [liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty) and [equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_equality), like a [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom) citizen)

- [Tyranny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrant) ([rule by fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_by_fear), like a [despot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despotism))

These five regimes progressively degenerate starting with aristocracy at the top and tyranny at the bottom.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrill2016-36]

In his *[Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_\(Aristotle\))*, Aristotle elaborates on Plato's five regimes discussing them in relation to the government of one, of the few, and of the many.[^cite_note-Jordović2019-37] From this follows the classification of forms of government according to which people have the authority to rule: either one person (an [autocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracy), such as monarchy), a select group of people (an aristocracy), or the people as a whole (a democracy, such as a republic).

[Thomas Hobbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hobbes) stated on their classification:

> The difference of [Commonwealths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth) consisteth in the difference of the [sovereign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign), or the person representative of all and every one of the multitude. And because the sovereignty is either in one man, or in an assembly of more than one; and into that assembly either every man hath right to enter, or not everyone, but certain men distinguished from the rest; it is manifest there can be but three kinds of Commonwealth. For the representative must need to be one man or more; and if more, then it is the assembly of all, or but of a part. When the representative is one man, then is the Commonwealth a monarchy; when an assembly of all that will come together, then it is a democracy or popular Commonwealth; when an assembly of a part only, then it is called an aristocracy. In other kinds of Commonwealth there can be none: for either one, or more, or all, must have the sovereign power (which I have shown to be indivisible) entire.[^cite_note-Leviathan-38]

### Modern basic political systems

According to [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale) professor [Juan José Linz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Jos%C3%A9_Linz), there a three main types of [political systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_system) today: [democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy), [totalitarian regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarian_regime) and, sitting between these two, [authoritarian regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) with [hybrid regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_regime).[^cite_note-LinzLinz2000-6][^cite_note-Michie2014-39] Another modern classification system includes [monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchies) as a standalone entity or as a hybrid system of the main three.[^cite_note-Garcia-AlexanderWooCarlson2017-7] Scholars generally refer to a [dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship) as either a form of authoritarianism or totalitarianism.[^cite_note-ToddWaller2015-40][^cite_note-LinzLinz2000-6][^cite_note-Sondrol-41]

### Autocracy

Main article: [Autocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracy)

An autocracy is a system of government in which supreme [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_\(social_and_political\)) is concentrated in the hands of one person, whose decisions are subject to neither external legal restraints nor regularized mechanisms of popular control (except perhaps for the implicit threat of a [coup d'état](25914154.md) or mass [insurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurrection)).[^cite_note-42] [Absolute monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy) is a historically prevalent form of autocracy, wherein a [monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch) governs as a singular [sovereign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign) with no limitation on [royal prerogative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_prerogative). Most absolute monarchies are [hereditary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_monarchy), however some, notably the [Holy See](13393.md), are [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elective_monarchy) by an [electoral college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_college) (such as the [college of cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_cardinals), or [prince-electors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-elector)). Other forms of autocracy include [tyranny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyranny), [despotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despotism), and [dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship).

### Aristocracy

Main article: [Aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy)

Aristocracy[^cite_note-43] is a form of government that places power in the hands of a small, [elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite) [ruling class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruling_class),[^cite_note-OED-44] such as a hereditary [nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobility) or [privileged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_privilege) [caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste). This class exercises [minority rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_rule), often as a [landed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_tenure) [timocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timocracy), wealthy [plutocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutocracy), or [oligarchy](22315.md).

Many monarchies were aristocracies, although in modern constitutional monarchies, the monarch may have little effective power. The term *aristocracy* could also refer to the non-[peasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasant), non-servant, and non-[city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgher_\(social_class\)) classes in [feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism).[^cite_note-45]

### Democracy

Main articles: [Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) and [Types of democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_democracy)



- &#x20; National governments which self-identify as democracies

- &#x20; National governments which do not self-identify as democracies



Governments recognised as "electoral democracies" as of 2022 by the *[Freedom in the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_in_the_World)* survey

[^cite_note-46]

Some governments combine both direct and indirect democratic governance, wherein the citizenry selects representatives to administer day-to-day governance, while also reserving the right to govern directly through [popular initiatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative), [referendums](51582.md) (plebiscites), and the [right of recall](129203.md). In a [constitutional democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_democracy) the powers of the majority are exercised within the framework of representative democracy, but the constitution limits [majority rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_rule), usually through the provision by all of certain [universal rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_right), such as [freedom of speech](21401843.md) or [freedom of association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_association).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-britannica-48]

### Republics

Main article: [Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic)

A republic is a form of government in which the country is considered a "public matter" ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *res publica*), not the private concern or property of the rulers, and where offices of states are subsequently directly or indirectly elected or appointed rather than inherited. The people, or some significant portion of them, have supreme control over the government and where offices of state are elected or chosen by elected people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontesquieu1748book_2,_chapters_1-49][^cite_note-Britannica-50]

A common simplified definition of a republic is a government where the head of state is not a monarch.[^cite_note-WordNet-51][^cite_note-M-W-52] [Montesquieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montesquieu) included both [democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy), where all the people have a share in rule, and [aristocracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy) or [oligarchies](22315.md), where only some of the people rule, as republican forms of government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMontesquieu1748book_2,_chapters_2–3-53]

### Federalism

Main article: [Federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism)

Federalism is a political concept in which a *group* of members are bound together by [covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_\(law\)) with a governing [representative head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_headship). The term "federalism" is also used to describe a system of government in which [sovereignty](28791.md) is constitutionally divided between a central governing authority and constituent political units, variously called states, provinces or otherwise. Federalism is a system based upon democratic principles and institutions in which the power to govern is shared between national and provincial/state governments, creating what is often called a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation).[^cite_note-54] Proponents are often called [federalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalist).

## Institutions

The organization, exercise, and constraints of power in a country are shaped by political institutions.[^cite_note-55] Such institutions can increase the stability of a government.[^cite_note-56]

### Branches



Separation of powers in the [US government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_government), demonstrating the *trias politica* model

Further information: [Separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) and [Fusion of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_of_powers)

Governments are often organised into three branches with separate powers: a legislature, an executive, and a judiciary; this is sometimes called the *trias politica* model. However, in [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) and [semi-presidential systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system), branches of government often intersect, having shared membership and overlapping functions. Many governments have fewer or additional branches, such as an independent [electoral commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_commission) or [auditory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_audit_institution) branch.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENeedler1991\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidFcssj8yigS4CpgPA116_116\]–118-57]

A [majority government](465640.md) is a government by one or more [governing parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governing_parties) together holding an absolute majority of seats in the parliament, in contrast to a [minority government](271434.md) in which they have only a plurality of seats and often depend on a [confidence-and-supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence-and-supply) arrangement with other parties. A [coalition government](6036.md) is one in which multiple parties cooperate to form a government as part of a [coalition agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_agreement). In a single-party government, a single party forms a government without the support of a coalition, as is typically the case with majority governments,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGallagherLaverMair2006-58][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKettle2015-59] but even a minority government may consist of just one party unable to find a willing coalition partner at the moment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuxbury2021-60]

## Public policy

Main article: [Public policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_policy)



Government spending composition by country (2023)

[^cite_note-v504-65]

Public policy can be considered the sum of a government's direct and indirect activities.[^cite_note-66] It includes various aspects of life such as education, health care, employment, finance, economics, transportation, and all over elements of society.[^cite_note-67] Public policy making can be characterized as a dynamic, complex, and interactive system through which public problems are identified and resolved through the creation of new policy or reform of existing policy.[^cite_note-Analysing_Public_Policy-68] [Government spending](1203070.md) includes [social protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_protection), [publicly funded health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicly_funded_health_care), [general public services](1291782.md), education, economic affairs, [public security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_security), defense, recreation and culture.[^cite_note-v504-65] The [government budget balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_budget_balance) affects the [government bond](93768.md) yields.[^cite_note-l377-69]
