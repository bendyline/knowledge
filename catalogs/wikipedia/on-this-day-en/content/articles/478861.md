**Belgorod** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Белгород, pronounced [\[ˈbʲelɡərət\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian)) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia) that serves as the administrative center of [Belgorod Oblast](478857.md), Russia, located on the [Seversky Donets River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets), approximately 40 kilometers (25 mi) north of the [border with Ukraine](36430484.md). It has a population of 339,978 ([2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census)).[^cite_note-2021Census-13]

It was founded in 1596 as a defensive fort on the southern border of Russia. Contested by various factions during [World War I](4764461.md) and the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), the city served as the temporary Soviet Ukrainian capital at the turn of 1918 and 1919.[^cite_note-ist-14]

## Etymology

The name *Belgorod* (Белгород) in [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) literally means "white city", a compound of "белый" (*bely*, "white, light") and "город" (*gorod*, "town, city"). The name is a reference to the region's historical abundance of [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone).[^cite_note-15]

## Geography

### Urban layout

Like many Russian cities, Belgorod began as a fortified settlement. The oldest Belgorod fortress was built at the end of the 16th century on a [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk) mountain. According to scientific excavations and surviving archival data, the first fortress outpost was erected in 1596.[^cite_note-20] The site of the construction of the defensive facility was the top of the Belaya Gora ("White Mountain"). This is the highest point of the right bank of the Seversky Donets channel. On 17 September 1650, voivode Vasily Petrovich Golovin laid the foundation for the third Belgorod Fortress on the left bank of the Vezenitsa River, which flows into the Seversky Donets.[^cite_note-21] In the fall of 1650, a wooden fort with 11 towers was attached to the rampart of the Belgorod line, which runs from the fortress town Bolkhovets to the mouth of the Vezelka River in the area of the former brewery. The two parts of the city were connected by the Nikolskaya Passage Tower located in the eastern wall of the Kremlin. The position of the eastern wall of the Kremlin corresponded to the modern street of the 50th anniversary of the Belgorod Oblast. With the expansion of the borders of the [Russian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia), the military significance of the Belgorod fortress gradually decreased and by the middle of the 18th century, only the Kremlin remained from the formidable fortress.[^cite_note-22]



Men's Monastery in 1911

In the fall of 1766, the new governor, Andrei Fliverk pushed for a new city plan. A regular street plan was developed and signed on 18 April 1767. The architect's signature is not legible, but it may have been signed by [Andrey Kvasov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kvasov). The central part of the plan was occupied by an octahedral "marketplace" with 64 stone shops and 20 warehouse barns. Moskovskaya, Kievskaya, Voronezhskaya and Kharkovskaya streets ran from the trading area in four directions. According to the plan, it was supposed to divide the entire city into 16 quarters, 4 of which should be built up with stone houses, and the rest with wooden and huts. The plan was executed formally without taking into account the buildings that survived the fire, the Kremlin fortress and the terrain.[^cite_note-23] On 28 April 1768, a new plan was developed[^cite_note-24] under the leadership of [Andrey Kvasov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kvasov) who was the author of a number of city plans. The plans overlaid the old city center layout and the projected one. It provided for a trading area, which in the west adjoined the fortress Kremlin, and in the east ended with stone benches of the [Gostiny dvor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gostiny_dvor) in the form of two arcs. The central planning axis was also chosen relative to which the directions of mutually [perpendicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpendicular) streets were formed.

Travel notes which were published in 1781 showed the location of a sketch of the ramparts of the lost ancient settlement. Only in the middle of the 1950s, the archaeologist [Arkady Nikitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkady_Nikitin?action=edit\&redlink=1) carried out excavations at the site of the first fortress, where the remains of ancient ramparts and ditches were still clearly visible.[^cite_note-25] though the fortress itself was destroyed already in the 1860s during the construction of the railway the eastern part of the chalk mountain, on which the Kremlin was located, was collapsed. The location of the first fortress approximately corresponded to the location of the modern car market on Byelaya Gora.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

In the 1780s, during the general survey of the Russian lands, several plans of Belgorod were fulfilled. When drawing up plans, an overlay of the old and new layouts of Kvasov was used. The plans described above give a distorted position of church estates, which were fixed when the city was laid and, as a rule, did not change. The plan, signed by the titular adviser Salkov, is the most accurate plan of Belgorod in the second half of the 18th century.[^cite_note-28]

### Climate

Belgorod's climate is [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfb* slightly cooler than *Dfa*) featuring moderate precipitation. Winters are rather cold and changeable with frequent warmings followed by rains. Temperatures may occasionally fall below −15 °C (5 °F) for about one week or more. Summer is warm and either humid and rainy or hot and droughty. Autumn is mild and rainy. The Belgorod reservoirs get covered with ice by the end of November or the beginning of December, and the ice layer typically lasts until March or April.

- average year [temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature): + 7.7 °C

- average [humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity): 76%

- average wind speed: 5–7 m/s

- average precipitation 380–620 mm (14.96–24.41 in), mostly in summer.

### Before the 20th century



Monument to Grand Prince [Vladimir the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_the_Great) in Belgorod

There was a settlement of the [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) tribe of [Severians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severians) in the area, which was probably destroyed at the beginning of the 10th century by the nomadic [Pechenegs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechenegs). In 965, the lands in the upper reaches of Seversky Donets were annexed to the [Principality of Pereyaslavl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Pereyaslavl).

Records first mention the settlement in 1237, when the Mongol-led army of [Batu Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu_Khan) ravaged it during the [Mongol invasion of Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Kievan_Rus'). It is unclear whether this Belgorod stood on the same site as the current city. In 1596 Tsar [Feodor Ioannovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feodor_I_of_Russia) of Russia ordered its re-establishment as one of numerous forts set up to [defend Russian southern borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muravsky_Trail) from the [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Crimea).[^cite_note-gr-2] Belgorod was part of a chain of [fortification lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Zasechnaya_cherta), created by [Grand Duchy of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow) and later the [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia) to protect it from the [Crimean–Nogai slave raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean%E2%80%93Nogai_slave_raids_in_Eastern_Europe) that ravaged the southern provinces of the country during the [Russo-Crimean Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Crimean_Wars).[^cite_note-32] The tsar appointed two princes-governors to supervise the construction of Belgorod: Mikhail Vasilyevich Nozdrovaty and Andrei Romanovich Volkonsky. The first Belgorod fortress was built on the high right bank of the Seversky Donets, in the area of the current car market; the Belgorod Kremlin was close to the present-day Belaya Gora restaurant. The legendary White Mountain has not survived, as it was completely torn down for chalk mining in the 1950s.



Belgorod fortress in the 17th century

The first Belgorod fortress stood for sixteen years, withstanding several major attacks, both from the Tatars and from the [Polish–Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) troops who participated in the [wars with the Russian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Muscovite_Wars_\(1605%E2%80%931618\)) during the [Time of Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_of_Troubles). In 1612 the Belgorod fortress was taken and burned by a detachment of Lithuanians. The governor, Nikita Likharev, by order of the tsar, was already building the second Belgorod fortress on the opposite bank of the Seversky Donets the following year, 1613. Over the next decades, Belgorodians repulsed a large number of attacks on their lands. By the middle of the 17th century, the question arose about the construction of a new Belgorod fortress three kilometers south of the existing one.

In the 17th century Belgorod suffered repeatedly from [Tatar incursions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_slave_trade), against which Russia built (from 1633 to 1740) an earthen wall, with twelve forts, extending upwards of 200 miles (320 kilometres) from the [Vorskla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorskla_River) in the west to the [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)) in the east, and called the [Belgorod line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgorod_line?action=edit\&redlink=1). In 1666 the [Moscow Patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) established an [archiepiscopal see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Belgorod_and_Stary_Oskol) in the town.[^cite_note-33]

Tsar [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) visited Belgorod on the eve of the [Battle of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poltava) in 1709.

After the Russian border moved south following successful wars against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth in the second half of the 17th century, the strategic importance of the city gradually decreased, and on 13 May 1785, by decree of [Catherine II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great), Belgorod was excluded from the number of fortresses of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). From that moment on, the city plunged into the measured provincial life of the central black earth zone of Russia. Military life was replaced by agricultural life, the number of spiritual, educational, industrial and commercial institutions were growing, and in the historical chronicles of the Russian Empire, the city seems to have fallen asleep for a century. The Belgorod province disappeared from the geographical maps, and the city was for a long time a part of the first [Kursk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_Governorate), then the Kursk province, and, finally, the Kursk region. A [dragoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragoon) regiment had its base in the town until 1917.

### 20th century



[Glorification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonization) of [Joasaph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joasaph_of_Belgorod), Belgorod, 4 September 1911

[Joasaph of Belgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joasaph_of_Belgorod), an 18th-century bishop of Belgorod and Oboyanska, became widely venerated as a miracle worker and was [glorified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church#Canonization) as a saint of the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) in 1911.



View of Belgorod in 1912

During [World War I](4764461.md), in 1917, a Polish rifle regiment was stationed in the city.[^cite_note-34] Soviet power was established in the city on 26 October (8 November) 1917. On 10 April 1918, troops of the [Imperial German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Army) occupied Belgorod. After the conclusion of the [Brest-Litovsk peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk_\(Ukraine%E2%80%93Central_Powers\)) of 9 February 1918 the demarcation line passed to the north of the city. Belgorod became part of the newly proclaimed [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic) (February to May 1918) and [Ukrainian State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_State) headed by [Hetman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetman) [Pavlo Skoropadskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Skoropadskyi).

On 20 December 1918, after the overthrow of German-backed Skoropadskyi, the Soviet [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) regained control over the city. From 24 December 1918 to 7 January 1919, the [Provisional Workers' and Peasants' Government of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Workers'_and_Peasants'_Government_of_Ukraine), then led by General [Georgy Pyatakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Pyatakov), was based in Belgorod, before its relocation to [Kharkiv](2788558.md). The city was then claimed by both the Ukrainian and Russian Soviet authorities.[^cite_note-ist-14] On 7 February 1919, it was recognized as part of the [Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic); however, Soviet Ukraine still tried to regain the city.[^cite_note-ist-14] Russian Communists justified the incorporation into Russia by the area's agricultural importance to Russia and the possibility of the collapse of Soviet power in Ukraine, as a result of which Russia would lose its claim to the city.[^cite_note-ist-14] From 23 June to 7 December 1919, the [Volunteer Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_Army) occupied the town as part of [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement)-controlled [South Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Russia_\(1919%E2%80%931920\)).

From September 1925, the territorial 163rd Infantry Regiment of the 55th Infantry Division of Kursk was stationed in Belgorod. In September 1939, it was deployed to the 185th Infantry Division.

On 2 March 1935, the [Presidium of the Central Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidium_of_the_Central_Executive_Committee) of the Soviet Union decided to allocate the city of Belgorod, Kursk region, into an independent administrative unit directly subordinate to the [Kursk Regional](480916.md) Executive Committee.



War destruction in Belgorod in 1943

The German [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) occupied Belgorod from 25 October 1941 to 9 February 1943. The Germans operated a [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) battalion for Jews in the city.[^cite_note-35] The Germans re-captured it on 18 March 1943 in the final move of the [Third Battle of Kharkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Kharkov). On 12 July 1943, during the [Battle of Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk), the largest [tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank) battle in world history took place near [Prokhorovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokhorovka,_Belgorod_Oblast), and the Red Army definitively retook the city on 5/6 August 1943. The [Belgorod Diorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgorod_Diorama?action=edit\&redlink=1) is one of the World War II monuments commemorating the event.

In 1954, the city became the administrative center of [Belgorod Oblast](478857.md) and rapidly developed as a regional industrial and cultural center.[^cite_note-36]

### 21st century

On 22 April 2013, [a mass shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Belgorod_shooting) occurred at approximately 2\:20 PM [Moscow](19004.md) time on a street in Belgorod. The shooter, identified as 31-year-old Sergey Pomazun ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Сергей Помазун), opened fire with a semi-automatic rifle on several people at a gun store and on a sidewalk, killing all six people whom he hit: three people at the store and three passers-by, including two teenage girls. Pomazun was apprehended after an extensive day-long manhunt; during his arrest, he wounded a policeman with a knife. He was sentenced to life in prison on 23 August 2013.



View of Belgorod in 2018

There were several [attacks](70539619.md) on Belgorod during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). Russian officials claimed that Belgorod was repeatedly targeted by indiscriminate Ukrainian attacks.[^cite_note-37] On 1 April 2022, two Ukrainian [Mi-24](384591.md) performed a night raid and set fire to a fuel depot in Belgorod, in a low-altitude airstrike.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] On 20 April 2023, a Russian [Su-34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-34) fighter jet accidentally [dropped a bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Belgorod_accidental_bombing) on the city, leaving a crater 20 metres (66 ft) across and injuring two people.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] On 22 April, more than 3,000 people were evacuated from their homes after an undetonated explosive was found; it was not known if the second bomb had come from the same aircraft.[^cite_note-42] On 30 December, an [alleged Ukrainian airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_December_2023_Belgorod_shelling) killed 25 people and wounded over 100.[^cite_note-43] In March 2024, authorities began evacuating 9,000 children from the city and wider region due to shelling and drone attacks.[^cite_note-44] On 6 May, at least six people were killed following a Ukrainian drone strike.[^cite_note-45] On 12 May 16 people were killed when a section of an apartment block collapsed. [Russian MOD](7875884.md) claimed it as a Ukrainian missile strike.[^cite_note-46] [CIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_Intelligence_Team) investigators said the building was most likely hit by a Russian bomb, as the explosion occurred on the North-Eastern side of the building, opposite to the border with Ukraine.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] On 28 September 2025, Ukraine's [HIMARS](147011.md) struck the Belgorod thermal power plant, causing a "complete power outage" in Belgorod and surrounding areas.[^cite_note-49]

## Administrative and municipal status



Bogdan Khmelnitsky Avenue in Belgorod

## Transportation



LiAZ-5293 CNG low-entry bus



AKSM-420 Vitovt trolleybus

There has been a railway connection between Belgorod and Moscow since 1869.[^cite_note-50] The city is served by [Belgorod International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgorod_International_Airport) (EGO) in the north of the city.

There are two bus stations: Bus Belgorod, Belgorod- 2 Bus Terminal (located on the forecourt), and the bus stop complex Energomash. The Energomash bus station is mainly for commuter buses. Buses run from the Belgorod-2 station mainly to nearby regional centers, and depart in accordance with the arrival of trains.

Trolleybus services were discontinued on 30 June 2022 and replaced by diesel buses, despite public support for retention of the trolleybus system. The officially cited reason for the closure was inadequate condition of the overhead contact lines and insufficient funds for its modernization. The length of trolley lines was over 120 km (75 mi). Trolleybus city park consisted of 150 pieces of equipment, mainly Russian-made trolley ZiU-682V, 2 units ZiU-683, operated from 1990, and 3 units ZiU-6205, 30 units "Optima", and one trolley Skoda-VSW -14Tr, which started operation in 1996. The city administration purchased 15 new ZiU-682G trolleybuses in 2002, another 20 new ZiU-682Gs in 2005, 30 Trolza-5275.05 "Optima" trolleybuses in 2011, and 20 new ACSM-420 trolleybuses in 2013.
