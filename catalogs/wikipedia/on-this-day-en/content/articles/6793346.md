**Kumamoto** (熊本市, *Kumamoto-shi*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[kɯ.ma.mo.to, -toꜜ.ɕi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-3]) is the capital [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Japan) of [Kumamoto Prefecture](220713.md) on the island of [Kyushu](16914.md), Japan. As of June 1, 2019, the city had an estimated population of 738,907 and a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 1,893 people per km<sup>2</sup>. The total area is 390.32 km<sup>2</sup>.

Greater Kumamoto (熊本都市圏) had a population of 1,461,000, as of the 2000 census. As of 2010, Kumamoto [Metropolitan Employment Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Employment_Area) has a GDP of US\$39.8 billion.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] It is not considered part of the [Fukuoka–Kitakyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka%E2%80%93Kitakyushu) metropolitan area, despite their shared border. The city was designated on April 1, 2012, by [government ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_designated_by_government_ordinance).

## History

See also: [History of Kumamoto Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Kumamoto_Prefecture)

### Early modern period

#### Shokuhō period

[Katō Kiyomasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kat%C5%8D_Kiyomasa), a contemporary of [Toyotomi Hideyoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyotomi_Hideyoshi), was made *[daimyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimy%C5%8D)* of half of the (old) administrative region of [Higo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higo_Province) in 1588. Afterwards, Kiyomasa built [Kumamoto Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumamoto_Castle). Due to its many innovative defensive designs, Kumamoto Castle was considered impenetrable, and Kiyomasa enjoyed a reputation as one of the finest castle-builders in Japanese history.

#### Edo period

After Kiyomasa died in 1611, his son, Tadahiro, succeeded him. In 1632, Tadahiro was removed by [Tokugawa Iemitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_Iemitsu) and replaced with the [Hosokawa clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosokawa_clan). [Hosokawa Tadatoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosokawa_Tadatoshi), the third lord of Kumamoto, was the patron of the artist[^cite_note-6] and [swordsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swordsman) [Miyamoto Musashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyamoto_Musashi).[^cite_note-7]

### Late modern period

#### Meiji period

The current administrative body of the City of Kumamoto was founded on April 1, 1889.

#### Showa period

On July 1, 1945, near the end of World War II, Kumamoto was bombed in an [Allied air raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_raids_on_Japan) that destroyed one square mile, which was 20% of the city's area.[^cite_note-9]

### Contemporary history

#### After World War II

After the war, the Japanese Buddhist monk [Nichidatsu Fujii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nichidatsu_Fujii) decided to construct a [Peace Pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Pagoda) atop Mount Hanaoka in the city to commemorate all those lost in war and to promote peace.[^cite_note-Kisala_1999_52–53-10] Inaugurated in 1954, it was the first of over 80 Peace Pagodas built by Fujii and his followers all over the world.[^cite_note-11]

#### Heisei period

On February 1, 1991, the towns of [Akita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akita,_Kumamoto), [Kawachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawachi,_Kumamoto), [Tenmei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenmei,_Kumamoto), and [Hokubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokubu,_Kumamoto) (all from [Hōtaku District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C5%8Dtaku_District,_Kumamoto)) were merged into Kumamoto. On October 6, 2008, the town of [Tomiai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomiai,_Kumamoto) (from [Shimomashiki District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimomashiki_District,_Kumamoto)) was merged into Kumamoto. On March 23, 2010, the town of [Jōnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dnan,_Kumamoto) (also from Shimomashiki District) and the town of [Ueki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ueki,_Kumamoto) (from [Kamoto District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamoto_District,_Kumamoto)) were merged into Kumamoto.[^cite_note-Kumamotomerges-12]

A [series of earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Kumamoto_earthquakes) struck the area beginning April 14, 2016, including a tremor with [moment magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude) 7.1 early in the morning of April 16, 2016.[^cite_note-13]

### Climate

Kumamoto has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*) with hot, humid summers and cool winters. There is significant precipitation throughout the year, especially during June and July. The average annual temperature in Kumamoto is 17.2 °C (63.0 °F). The average annual rainfall is 2,007.0 mm (79.02 in) with June as the wettest month. The temperatures are highest on average in August, at around 28.4 °C (83.1 °F), and lowest in January, at around 6.0 °C (42.8 °F). The highest temperature ever recorded in Kumamoto was 40.3 °C (104.5 °F) on 3 August 2026; the coldest temperature ever recorded was −9.2 °C (15.4 °F) on 26 January 1904 and 11 February 1929.

## Government

Kazufumi Ōnishi has been the city's mayor since December 2014.[^cite_note-16]

### Working mother incident

In November 2017, Kumamoto politician [Yuka Ogata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuka_Ogata) was forced to leave the Kumamoto municipal assembly because she had brought her baby.[^cite_note-17] The incident was reported by international media as an example of the challenges facing [women in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Japan).[^cite_note-18]

## Landmarks

### Kumamoto Castle



Kumamoto Castle

The city's most famous landmark is [Kumamoto Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumamoto_Castle), a large and once extremely well fortified Japanese castle. The *[donjon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donjon)* (castle central keep) is a concrete reconstruction built in the 1970s, but several ancillary wooden buildings from the original castle remain. The castle was assaulted during the [Satsuma Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Rebellion) and sacked and burned after a 53-day [siege](26905.md). It was during this time that the tradition of eating *[basashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_meat)* (raw horse meat) originated. *Basashi* remains popular in Kumamoto and, to a lesser extent, elsewhere in Japan, although these days it is usually considered a delicacy.

Within the outer walls of Kumamoto Castle is the Hosokawa Gyobu-tei, the former residence of the Higo *[daimyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimy%C5%8D)*. This traditional wooden mansion has a fine [Japanese garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_garden) located on its grounds.

### Religious sites

The first of many [peace pagodas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_pagoda) around the world was erected by Japanese Buddhist monk [Nichidatsu Fujii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nichidatsu_Fujii) atop [Mount Hanaoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hanaoka?action=edit\&redlink=1) beginning 1947.[^cite_note-Kisala_1999_52–53-10] Inaugurated in 1954, it was the first of over 80 built by Fujii and his followers all over the world.[^cite_note-19]

Kumamoto is also the location of [Takahashi Inari Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takahashi_Inari_Shrine) and [Fujisaki Hachimangū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujisaki_Hachimang%C5%AB).

### Suizenji area



Suizenji jojuen garden

Kumamoto is home to [Suizen-ji Jōju-en](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suizen-ji_J%C5%8Dju-en), a formal garden neighboring Suizenji Temple approximately 3 kilometers southeast of Kumamoto Castle. Suizenji Park is also home to the Suizenji Municipal Stadium, where the city's [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_\(soccer\)) team, [Roasso Kumamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roasso_Kumamoto), used to play regularly. The team now uses the larger [KKWing Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KKWing_Stadium) in Higashi Ward.

### Other notable sites

[Miyamoto Musashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyamoto_Musashi) lived the last part of his life in Kumamoto. His tomb and the cave where he resided during his final years (known as [Reigandō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reigand%C5%8D), or "spirit rock cave") are situated close by. He penned the famous *Go Rin no Sho* (*[The Book of Five Rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Five_Rings)*) whilst living here.

The downtown area has a commercial district centred on two shopping arcades, the Shimotori and Kamitori, which extend for several city blocks. The main department stores are located here along with a large number of smaller retailers, restaurants, and bars. Many local festivals are held in or near the arcades.

Cultural venues include the [Kumamoto Prefectural Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumamoto_Prefectural_Museum_of_Art) and [Kumamoto Prefectural Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumamoto_Prefectural_Theater).
