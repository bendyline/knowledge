**Kaman Corporation** [/kəˈmɑːn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) is an American [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) company, with headquarters in [Bloomfield, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomfield,_Connecticut). It was founded in 1945 by [Charles Kaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Kaman). During the first ten years the company operated exclusively as a designer and manufacturer of several [helicopters](8286923.md) that set world records and achieved many aviation firsts.

In 1956, Kaman began to diversify[^cite_note-2] as an aerospace subcontractor of [McDonnell Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas), [Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman), and others. In the mid-1960s Kaman diversified outside of the aerospace industry, using the expertise Kaman had gained in [composite materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_material) and the end of the need for skilled woodworkers to craft wooden rotor blades. Charles Kaman, a [guitarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar) as well as an aerospace pioneer, worked with his engineers and other musicians to create the round-backed, composite-body [Ovation guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovation_Guitar_Company), which led to the eventual creation of [Kaman Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KMCMusicorp), a major distributor and manufacturer of musical instruments and accessories.

## History

Charles Kaman founded the company in December 1945 with \$2,000 of capital and his invention of the servo-flap controlled rotor.[^cite_note-Web.mit.edu/invent/iow/kaman.html_2003-03-18-3]

January 15, 1947

[K-125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_K-125) - Kaman's first helicopter; Kaman selected an [intermeshed contrarotating twin rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermeshing_rotors) design.

July 1949

[K-225](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_K-225) - An improved version of the K-125; the U.S. Navy bought two and the U.S. Coast Guard one for \$25,000 each. Later, they received the H-22 designation.

December 1951

A modified K-225 equipped with a [Boeing 502 (YT50)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_T50) turboshaft engine becomes the world's first [gas turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_turbine) powered helicopter.

[^cite_note-archive.vn_2012-07-20-4]

&#x20;This aircraft is now at the Smithsonian Institution's [National Air and Space Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Air_and_Space_Museum).

1953

Kaman produced the first electrically-powered [drone](58900.md).

April 1953

Ordered for the U.S. Marine Corps, the [HOK-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_HH-43_Huskie) first flies; Air Force version was the [H-43A Huskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HH-43_Huskie).

1954

K-16 A [V/STOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V/STOL) designed around a rotoprop.

March 1954

A modified Kaman HTK-1 becomes the world's first twin-turbine powered helicopter.

September, 1956

HH-43 Huskie — A variant of the OH-43, equipped with a [Lycoming T-53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_T53) gas turbine engine.

July 1957

QH-43 - A HTK-1 modified as a [UAV](58900.md).

[^cite_note-Helis.com_2020-08-20-5]

In the late 1950s, Kaman built the [Kaman K-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_K-17), an experimental [tip jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tip_jet) powered helicopter.

[^cite_note-6]

&#x20;using a [Blackburn Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackburn_Aircraft)-built [Turbomeca Turmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbomeca_Turmo) turbine powering a compressor delivering cold air to the rotor tips.

[^cite_note-7]

July 2, 1959

The HU2K-1, selected by the U.S. Navy as a general purpose naval helicopter, makes its first flight. It enters service as the [UH-2A Seasprite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_SH-2_Seasprite) in 1962.

March 1960

Kaman develops and flies the first all-composite main rotor blade.

In October 1961 the H-43 Huskie set an altitude record of 10,000 m (33,000 ft) and rate of climb records. [Anton Flettner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Flettner) who emigrated to the United States and became the chief designer for Kaman Aircraft, creating the Kaman HH-43 Huskie.

During the Korean and Vietnam Wars, the Huskie flew more rescue missions than all other aircraft combined, with the best safety record of any U.S. military aircraft.

1962

UH-2A / B production begins.

January 1964

First flight of Kaman's experimental [Convertiplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convertiplane) equipped with a [GE J-85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_J85) turbojet engine and wings from a [Beechcraft Queen Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_Queen_Air). The aircraft achieves speeds of over 320 km/h.

1965

Tomahawk — A Seasprite modified with stub wings and a pair of twin guns side-by-side under the nose. Kaman's proposal for the U.S. Army 's interim gunship helicopter between the AAFSS (AH-56) and AAH (AH-64) competitions. Lost to the Bell 209 ([AH-1 HueyCobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_AH-1_Cobra)).

Due to the limited power of its single engine, the Seasprites are modified for the U.S. Navy into twin-turbine helicopters.

1969

U.S. Navy begins [Light Airborne Multi-Purpose System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_Airborne_Multi-Purpose_System) (LAMPS) development to obtain an onboard helicopter for escort ships.

1971

SAVER — The Stowable Aircrew Vehicle Escape Rotoseat is the first jet-powered autogyro with telescoping rotor blades.

May 1973

[SH-2F Seasprite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_SH-2_Seasprite) — The LAMPS Mk I enters U.S. Navy service.

July 1976

Kaman designs and begins manufacturing the K-747 blade, the world's first production all-composite rotor blade for the [Bell AH-1 Cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_AH-1_Cobra) helicopter. Total production exceeds 4,000 blades.

January 1991

Magic Lantern, a new laser-based mine countermeasures system, is deployed in the Persian Gulf during Operation Desert Storm. It is highly successful in locating mines.

February 1993

[SH-2G Super Seasprite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_SH-2G_Super_Seasprite) — The new version of the Seasprite, with new avionics, mission electronics, and GE T-700 engines, enters [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) service.

August 1994

[K-1200 K-MAX](8916729.md) — a purpose-built helicopter with [intermeshing rotors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermeshing_rotors) specializing in external load operations.

November 1998

The Egyptian Air Force accepts delivery of its 10th SH-2G Super Seasprite for use in anti-submarine warfare missions, completing the order and becoming the first international customer to operate the aircraft.

August 1999

New Zealand approves purchase of SH-2G(NZ).

January 2000

Australian SH-2G(A) begins initial flight testing.

January 2001

U.S. federal government purchases 5 K-MAX for Peru.

July 24, 2002

Kaman Acquires RWG Frankenjura-Industrie Flugwerklager GmbH, a manufacturer of spherical and ball bearings, rod ends, bushings and specialty components primarily for the aerospace industry.

[^cite_note-8]

October 2002

The 5 Peruvian K-MAX are transferred to the Colombian Army where they are still in service as of 2008.

June 2008

All Royal Australian Navy SH-2G Seasprite helicopters are withdrawn from service and returned to supplier

[^cite_note-9]

&#x20;as budget blew-out and contract specifications unable to be met.

Kaman acquires Brookhouse Holdings Limited, a world leading composite development company based in the United Kingdom, and renames it Kaman Composites UK Ltd.

[^cite_note-10]

January 31, 2011

[Charles Kaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Kaman), founder of Kaman Aircraft, died in [Bloomfield, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomfield,_Connecticut).

[^cite_note-Avstop.com_2020-08-20-11]

November 2015

Kaman announces that it was acquiring GRW Bearing GmbH for \$142.9 million. GRW designs and manufactures precision ball bearings, and has production facilities in Europe.

[^cite_note-12]

August 26, 2019

Kaman announced that it has completed the sale of its "KAMAN Distribution Group" segment to affiliates of Littlejohn & Co. for total cash consideration of \$700 million&#x20;

[^cite_note-13]

January 20, 2020

Kaman completes acquisition of Bal Seal Engineering Inc., which designs, develops, and manufactures highly engineered products including precision springs, seals, and contacts.

[^cite_note-14]

[^cite_note-15]

May 2022

Kaman announced it had acquired [Parker-Hannifin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Hannifin)'s aircraft wheel and brake division for \$440 million US.

[^cite_note-16]

January 18, 2023

Kaman Corporation announced it will end the K-MAX production line, citing “low demand and variation in annual deliveries, coupled with low profitability and large working capital inventory requirements”, according to an 18 January statement.

[^cite_note-17]

[^cite_note-18]

January 19, 2024

Kaman agreed to be acquired by private equity firm Arcline Investment Management for \$1.29 billion.

[^cite_note-19]

April 19, 2024

It was announced that Arcline Investment Management, L.P. had completed its purchase of the company for approximately \$1.8 billion.
