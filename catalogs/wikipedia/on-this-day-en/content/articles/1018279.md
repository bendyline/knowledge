# Memorandum of understanding

"MoU" redirects here. For other uses, see [MOU (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOU_\(disambiguation\)).

This article is about intentions of the parties involved. For pre-contractual undertakings, see [Letter of understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_of_understanding).



20 January 2026 – [House of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Switzerland) at the [World Economic Forum](172185.md) (WEF) Annual Meeting in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos): Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi, Secretary General of the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) (GCC), and [Federal Councillor](218299.md) [Ignazio Cassis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignazio_Cassis) sign a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to establish a strategic dialogue between the GCC and the [Swiss Confederation](26748.md).

A **memorandum of understanding** (**MoU**) is a type of agreement between two ([bilateral](429154.md)) or more ([multilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateralism)) parties. It expresses a convergence of will between the parties, indicating an intended common line of action.[^cite_note-2] It is often used either in cases where parties do not imply a legal commitment or in situations where the parties cannot create a legally enforceable agreement. It is a more formal alternative to a [gentlemen's agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentlemen's_agreement).[^cite_note-what_is_a_mou_2018_04_07_investopedia-3][^cite_note-contract_or_mou_2017_03_02_score_org-4]

Whether a document constitutes a binding contract depends only on the presence or absence of well-defined legal elements in the text proper of the document (the so-called "[four corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_corners_\(law\))"). The required elements are [offer and acceptance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offer_and_acceptance), [consideration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consideration), and the [intention to be legally bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intention_to_be_legally_bound) (*animus contrahendi*).[^cite_note-5] In the US, the specifics can differ slightly depending on whether the contract is for goods (so falling under the [Uniform Commercial Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Commercial_Code)) or services (falling under the common law of the state).

Many companies and government agencies use MoUs to define a relationship between departments, agencies or closely held companies.[^cite_note-6]

## In private enterprise

In [business](39206.md), an MoU is typically a legally non-binding agreement between two (or more) parties, outlining terms and details of a mutual understanding or agreement, noting each party's requirements and responsibilities—but without establishing a formal, legally enforceable contract (though an MoU is often a first step towards the development of a formal contract).[^cite_note-what_is_a_mou_2018_04_07_investopedia-3][^cite_note-contract_or_mou_2017_03_02_score_org-4]

## In government and public affairs

In the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and other commonwealth countries, such as [New Zealand](4913064.md), the term MoU is commonly used to refer to an agreement between parts of [The Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown). The term is often used in the context of [devolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution#United_Kingdom), for example the 1999 concordat between the central [Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Environment,_Food_and_Rural_Affairs) and the [Scottish Environment Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Executive_Environment_and_Rural_Affairs_Department).&#x20;

MoUs can also be used between a government agency and a non-commercial, non-governmental organization.

## In public international law

In international relations, MoUs fall under the broad category of [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) and should be registered in the [United Nations](31769.md) treaty collection.[^cite_note-7] In practice and in spite of the [United Nations Office of Legal Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_of_Legal_Affairs)' insistence that registration be done to avoid 'secret diplomacy', MoUs are sometimes kept confidential. As a matter of law, the title of MoU does not necessarily mean the document is binding or not binding under [international law](8195726.md). To determine whether a particular MoU is meant to be a legally binding document (i.e., a treaty), one needs to examine the parties' intent as well as the signatories' position (e.g., Minister of Foreign Affairs vs. Minister of Environment). A careful analysis of the wording will also clarify the exact nature of the document. The [International Court of Justice](14918.md) has provided some insight into the determination of the legal status of a document in the landmark case of *[Qatar v. Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_v._Bahrain)*, 1 July 1994.[^cite_note-8]

### Advantages

One advantage of MoUs over more formal instruments is that, because obligations under international law may be avoided, they can often be put into effect without requiring legislative approval. Hence, MoUs are often used to modify and adapt existing treaties, in which case these MoUs have factual treaty status.[^cite_note-9] The decision concerning ratification, however, is determined by the parties' internal law and depends to a large degree on the subject agreed upon. MoUs that are kept confidential (i.e., not registered with the UN) cannot be enforced before any UN organ, and it may be concluded that no obligations under international law have been created.

Although MoUs in the [multilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateralism) field are seldom seen, the transnational [aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation) agreements are actually MoUs.

### Examples

Examples include:

- The *Memorandum of Understanding Relating to the Treaty between the United States of America and the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics on the Limitation of Anti-Ballistic Missile Systems on May 26, 1972* signed by the [United States](3434750.md) ([Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon)) and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) ([Leonid Brezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev)) updating the [Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Ballistic_Missile_Treaty).

  [^cite_note-10]

- The *Memorandum of Understanding on Hijacking of Aircraft and Vessels and Other Offenses* between the US and [Cuba](5042481.md), meant to criminalize hijacking in both countries (February 3, 1973).

- The agreement between the [Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Islands) and [Cuba](5042481.md), under which Cayman immigration officers must give Cuban refugees two choices: disembark and be repatriated back to Cuba, or continue on their way without any assistance.

  [^cite_note-11]

- The [Agreed Framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreed_Framework) between the U.S. and North Korea over nuclear weaponry on October 21, 1994.

- The [Oil-for-Food programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil-for-Food_programme), for which [Iraq](7515928.md) signed an MoU in 1996.

- The agreement between the government of [Indonesia](14579.md) and the [GAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Aceh_Movement) in the [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh) peace process, 15 August 2005.

- The agreement between the [UK](31717.md) and [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_\(country\)), [Libya](17633.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md) regarding potential extradition of suspects (commonly [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) suspects), who, if they are to be tried, must be tried fairly and in a manner similar to the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights); for example, withholding from using evidence obtained through the use of [torture](47702.md) (Article 3). Such an understanding has been criticised for its inability to be legally enforced. This has been highlighted in the current deportation process of the suspected terrorist [Abu Qatada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Qatada_al-Filistini), who is wanted by Jordan in connection with a terrorist attack. However, at present, the Court of Appeal has rejected the UK Government's appeal based on the Court's concern of Jordan obtaining evidence potentially incriminating Qatada through the use of torture.

- The *Memoranda of Understanding on Labour Cooperation* between the [People's Republic of China](5405.md), [Singapore](27318.md) and [New Zealand](4913064.md) on 2008, in parallel with their respective free trade agreements.

- The [Under2 Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under2_Coalition) is a 2015 memorandum of understanding which sets [climate change mitigation](2119179.md) goals for jurisdictions with over 1 billion residents.

- On December 2, 2019, the [Michigan Department of Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Department_of_Natural_Resources) and the [Saginaw Chippewa Tribal Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saginaw_Chippewa_Tribal_Nation) signed an MoU to co-manage the [Sanilac Petroglyphs Historic State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanilac_Petroglyphs_Historic_State_Park). The agreement marks the first state-tribal co-management of a Michigan [state park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_park).

  [^cite_note-12]

- The [Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding between the United States of America and the Islamic Republic of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Memorandum_of_Understanding_between_the_United_States_of_America_and_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran) drawn up in 2026 and intended to end the [2026 Iran War](82533582.md).
