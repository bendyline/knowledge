# Spain at the FIFA World Cup

For the women's team, see [Spain at the FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_at_the_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup).



Spain celebrating their second title at the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md)

[Spain](609825.md) is [one of the most experienced nations in FIFA World Cup history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_team_appearances_in_the_FIFA_World_Cup), having made 17 appearances in the tournament's 23 editions. Spain has reached the final twice, winning the title on both occasions, in [2010](168079.md) and [2026](36581929.md).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

Prior to their two wins, Spain's best World Cup result was a fourth-place finish in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-3]

## 2010 FIFA World Cup



Spain celebrating their first victory in the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) in South Africa.

The Spanish team went to the 2010 World Cup in South Africa as [European champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008) and enjoyed a record unbeaten streak of 35 matches from 2007 to 2009.[^cite_note-5] Although they lost their first match 1–0 against [Switzerland](681862.md), they went on to win the group after victories over [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team) and [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team), while the Swiss failed to win another match and were eliminated.

The Spanish team won all three following knockout matches by a score of 1–0, always scoring in the second half of regular time. The opponents were [Portugal](576328.md), [Paraguay](804025.md) and [Germany](250204.md).

### 2010 World Cup final v [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team)

Main article: [2010 FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_final)

This was Spain's first and the Netherlands' third appearance in a World Cup final, but neither had ever won the trophy. Spain was the dominating side with 57% ball possession, but the Dutch opponents had several chances as well. In a rough match, referee [Howard Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Webb) showed fourteen yellow cards and sent off Dutch defender [John Heitinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Heitinga) in the second half of extra time, after regular time ended goalless. However, even with the five yellow cards received in the final, the Spanish team was awarded the [FIFA Fair Play Trophy](3482503.md#FIFA_Fair_Play_Trophy) after collecting only eight yellow cards in seven matches.

The winning goal was scored by [Andrés Iniesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta), a right foot strike from inside the box after an assist by [Cesc Fàbregas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesc_F%C3%A0bregas).[^cite_note-6]

## 2026 FIFA World Cup

Just like in 2010, Spain entered the tournament as the current [European champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024), and were thus considered among the top favourites to win the cup. They began their campaign with a 0–0 draw against World Cup debutants [Cape Verde](1205575.md). The result was considered a major World Cup upset and put doubts in the minds of many if Spain would be true contenders for the tournament. It also mirrored Spain's surprise 0–1 opening defeat against [Switzerland](681862.md) in 2010. In their following match against [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), Spain were able to find their rhythm in a dominant 4–0 win. For their last group fixture, they faced former winners [Uruguay](679782.md); in a match that was marked by many fouls from the South Americans, Spain was able to come out with a 1–0 victory, ensuring they entered the knockout stage as group winners and eliminating Uruguay in the process.

In the newly-established round of 32, Spain would face off against [Austria](695226.md), where they cruised to a 3–0 victory. In the round of 16, Spain faced their [Iberian rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal%E2%80%93Spain_football_rivalry) [Portugal](576328.md), in what was considered their toughest opponent yet. After a close first half, Spain managed to find the net in the dying moments of the game and win 1–0, advancing to the quarter-finals for the first time since 2010. Spain would then defeat [Belgium](679716.md) 2–1 in the quarter-finals following another late winner, conceding a goal at the tournament for the first time. Spain's 2–0 win over [second-ranked](719095.md) side [France](250197.md) in the semi-finals saw them equal the historic record held by [Italy](362466.md) of 37 consecutive matches unbeaten, and ensured Spain's progression to the World Cup final for only the second time in their history.

### 2026 World Cup final v [Argentina](454699.md)

Main article: [2026 FIFA World Cup final](75845879.md)

Spain faced defending champions [Argentina](454699.md) in the final, the first competitive meeting between the two sides since the [1966 World Cup](61629.md). Following a contest largely dominated by Spain and a red card shown to Argentina's [Enzo Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Fern%C3%A1ndez), Spain finally broke the deadlock in the second half of extra time thanks to a goal from [Ferran Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferran_Torres). Just as in 2010, Spain would win the final 1–0 after extra time, becoming the first national team to hold the men's and [women's World Cup](244862.md) titles simultaneously, as the [Spanish women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_women's_national_football_team) had won the [2023 title](45271353.md). They conceded only one goal across the eight matches they played at the tournament, and also overtook Italy as the sole holders of the longest undefeated streak in men's international football.[^cite_note-10]

## Top goalscorers

On 27 May 1934, [José Iraragorri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Iraragorri) made history by scoring Spain's first-ever FIFA World Cup goal, coming in their opening match against [Brazil](149286.md) in [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Luigi_Ferraris).



[David Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Villa) is Spain's record World Cup scorer and a 2010 tournament winner.

No Spanish player has ever won the [Golden Boot](3482503.md#Golden_Boot), but [Emilio Butragueño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Butrague%C3%B1o) and Spain's record World Cup scorer [David Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Villa) have each won the Silver Boot after scoring five goals in 1986 and 2010, respectively.
