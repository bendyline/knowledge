The **Football Federation Islamic Republic of Iran** (**FFIRI**; [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): فدراسیون فوتبال جمهوری اسلامی ایران, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *Fedrasion-e Football-e Jomhuri-e Eslami-e Iran*) is the governing body for [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_\(soccer\)) in [Iran](14653.md). It was founded in 1920.[^cite_note-foundation-4] The federation has been a member of [FIFA](11049.md) since 1948[^cite_note-5] and a member of the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) since 1958.

## History

The federation was founded in 1920,[^cite_note-foundation-4] has been a member of [FIFA](11049.md) since 1948, and a member of the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) since 1954.

In 2001, Iran was a founding member of the [West Asian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asian_Football_Federation), along with [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Football_Association), [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Football_Association), [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Football_Association), [Palestine](5531531.md), and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Football_Association).

In 2014, Iran was a founding member of the [Central Asian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asian_Football_Association) alongside [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_Football_Federation), [Kyrgyzstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyz_Football_Union), [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan_Football_Federation), [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Turkmenistan), and [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan_Football_Association).[^cite_note-6]

## Controversies

On 23 November 2006, the federation was suspended by [FIFA](11049.md),[^cite_note-7] due to government interference in football matters. FIFA rules decree that a national football federation should remain autonomous from the national government. However, on 17 December 2006, the ban was lifted and a new Transitory Board was composed.

During the [2009 Iranian election protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Iranian_election_protests), seven members of the [Iran national football team](272865.md) were initially reported to have been banned for life by the federation for wearing green armbands in support of the [Iranian Green Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Green_Movement).[^cite_note-8]

[Iran women's national football team](9896170.md) was briefly suspended by FIFA from international competition in 2011 for being required by the federation to wear headscarves.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In 2015, the federation banned players from the men's national team due to [conscription](5735.md) problems.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

In August 2017, the federation banned [Masoud Shojaei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoud_Shojaei) and [Ehsan Hajsafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehsan_Hajsafi) from the national team after they played against the Israeli club [Maccabi Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccabi_Tel_Aviv) in the [UEFA Europa League](232175.md).[^cite_note-13]

On 9 November 2018 [Fatma Samoura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatma_Samoura), Secretary General of International Federation of Football Association FIFA said she would ask the Iranian government to end the ban on women's entry to sports stadiums.[^cite_note-14] On 7 August 2019, [Mohammad Jafar Montazeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Jafar_Montazeri), prosecutor general of Iran, supporting the ban, said that it should not concern FIFA if women in Iran can enter sports stadiums or not.[^cite_note-15] However, after continuous pressure from FIFA, [IRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRNA) deputy Sports Minister Jamshid Taghizadeh said women would be allowed to enter [Azadi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Stadium) to watch the men's [World Cup 2022](17742072.md) qualifier match between Iran and [Cambodia](334751.md) in October.[^cite_note-16] However, in March 2022, Iranian women were again banned from entering the stadium for a World Cup qualifier.[^cite_note-17]

During the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) as well as the [2023 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup), the Iranian government and federation reportedly sent paid members of the [Basij](2892082.md) and [IRGC](1285472.md) to [cheer for the team in the stands and to counter protesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2022_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Iranian_protests).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

In April 2024, goalkeeper [Hossein Hosseini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Hosseini_\(footballer,_born_1992\)) was summoned, suspended, and fined by the Iranian federation for hugging a female fan.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In October 2024, [Zahra Ghanbari](64417617.md) was suspended and forced to apologize by the Iranian federation for "improper hijab" during a goal celebration at the [2024–25 AFC Women's Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_AFC_Women's_Champions_League).[^cite_note-23] In December 2024, defender [Ramin Rezaeian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramin_Rezaeian) was summoned by the federation as well after hugging a female fan.[^cite_note-24]

### 2026

Prior to the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) draw, a group stage match scheduled to be played in Seattle was designated as a "[Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_\(LGBTQ_culture\)) Match" by the City of [Seattle](11388236.md) to coincide with the city's [annual Pride celebrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Pride) and to promote [LGBTQ+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ+) inclusion.[^cite_note-25] Following the draw, the fixture was confirmed to be [the match between Iran and Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_vs_Iran_at_the_2026_FIFA_World_Cup). Both Iran and Egypt are countries where homosexuality is criminalized under domestic law, with [Iran in particular having imposed capital punishment in certain cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_history_in_Iran).[^cite_note-26] The Iranian Football Federation and the [Egyptian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Football_Association) objected to the match's designation; however, [FIFA](11049.md) and local organizers confirmed that associated LGBTQ+ events would proceed, and that [rainbow flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_flag_\(LGBTQ\)) would be permitted inside the stadium.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

In January 2026, multiple Iranian footballers and athletes were killed during the [2026 Iran massacres](82036668.md) amid the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md), including former [Tractor Sazi F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tractor_Sazi_F.C.) midfielder [Mojtaba Tarshiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojtaba_Tarshiz), prompting former Iran national team captain [Masoud Shojaei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoud_Shojaei) to criticise FIFA for its silence over the killing of Iranian athletes during protests, while then-captain [Mehdi Taremi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Taremi) expressed solidarity with the Iranian people.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] On 14 January, the federation, along with the [Islamic Republic of Iran Wrestling Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Wrestling_Federation), were exempt from posting a picture to their [Instagram](31591547.md) accounts opposing [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s threats to attack Iran for its response to the protests, and also claiming that athletes were killed during the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War).[^cite_note-32] After the massacres, the [AFC](715249.md) ruled that Iranian clubs cannot host [AFC Champions League Elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_AFC_Champions_League_Elite) and [AFC Champions League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_AFC_Champions_League_Two) matches at home, and would therefore have to move them to neutral venues.[^cite_note-sport-thousands-killed-33] Following the massacres, activists called on FIFA to ban the Iranian national team from the 2026 FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] [Ali Karimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Karimi), a former footballer for the Iran national team, along with a coalition of prominent Iranians, wrote an open letter to FIFA and all its member associations, calling on FIFA president [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md) to speak up on the protest deaths.[^cite_note-36] Following the protests and ensuing crackdown, the [Spanish Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation) reportedly pulled out of a planned friendly match with Iran.[^cite_note-37]

After a number of players resigned from the [Iran women's national football team](9896170.md) and refereeing organization following [the massacres](82036668.md), the Iranian football federation reportedly threatened them with multi-year bans from professional football activities, judicial action, and long prison sentences.[^cite_note-38] In March 2026, after the [Iran women's national football team players refused to sing the national anthem of the Islamic Republic as a form of silent protest](82634971.md) ahead of their [2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup](67991141.md) opener against [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_football_team), concerns grew for their safety following threats from Iranian state media.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Ahead of the following match against [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_football_team), the national team players were reportedly forced to sing the national anthem of the Islamic Republic of Iran, with threats to the players' family members if they did not.[^cite_note-41] After the team's exit from the tournament on 8 March, members of the team gave what appeared to be [SOS](145605.md) hand signals from the bus as they were leaving, leading to protests and growing calls for Australia to offer the team refuge after the players were accused of being wartime traitors by Iranian state media for not singing the national anthem of the Islamic Republic in their opening game amid the [Iran War](82533582.md).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

Amid the [Iran War](82533582.md), [Sardar Azmoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Azmoun) was reportedly expelled from the national team after posting a picture on social media, per [IRGC](1285472.md)-affiliated media.[^cite_note-46]

On April 30, 2026, [protests](82164645.md) were held outside the 2026 [FIFA Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Congress) in [Vancouver](32706.md), calling on FIFA to ban the Iranian team and stating that it represents the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) instead of the people of Iran.[^cite_note-47] On May 19, it was reported that FIFA would again [ban](83181783.md#Protests_and_massacres_in_Iran) the pre-revolutionary [Lion and Sun flag](82020140.md) from World Cup stadiums, [similar to previous World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2022_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Iranian_protests).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]
