# Concessions and leases in international relations

In [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations), a **concession** is a "[synallagmatic](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/synallagmatic) act by which a State transfers the exercise of rights or functions proper to itself to a foreign private person which, in turn, participates in the performance of public functions and thus gains a privileged position vis-a-vis other private law subjects within the jurisdiction of the State concerned."[^cite_note-Grantconcession-1] International concessions are not defined in [international law](8195726.md) and do not generally fall under it. Rather, they are governed by the [municipal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_law) of the [conceding](3261664.md) state. There may, however, be a law of succession for such concessions, whereby the concession is continued even when the conceding state ceases to exist.[^cite_note-Grantconcession-1]

In international law, a **lease** is "an arrangement whereby territory is [leased](635681.md) or [pledged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pledge_\(law\)) by the owner-State to another State. In such cases, sovereignty is, for the term of the lease, transferred to the lessee State."[^cite_note-Grantlease-2] The term "international lease" is sometimes also used to describe any leasing of property by one state to another or to a foreign national, but the normal leasing of property, as in [diplomatic premises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_law), is governed by municipal, not international, law. Sometimes the term "quasi-international lease" is used for leases between states when less than full sovereignty over a territory is involved. A true international lease, or "political" lease, involves the transfer of sovereignty for a specified period of time. Although they may have the same character as [cessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cession), the terminability of such leases is now fully accepted.[^cite_note-Grantlease-2]

## Canadian concessions

Following the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) the [French Republic](5843419.md) granted [Canada](5042916.md) perpetual use of a portion of land on [Vimy Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimy_Ridge) under the understanding that the Canadians were to use the land to establish a battlefield park and memorial. The park, known as the [Canadian National Vimy Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_National_Vimy_Memorial), contains a monument to the fallen, a museum and extensive re-creations of the wartime trench system, preserved tunnels and cemeteries.

## Chinese concessions

Between 1882 and 1884, the [Qing Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_Dynasty) obtained concessions in [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea) at [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon), [Busan](68115.md) and [Wonsan](527626.md). The [Chinese concession of Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_concession_of_Incheon) and those in Busan and Wonsan were occupied by Japan in 1894 after the outbreak of the [First Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sino-Japanese_War). After China's defeat in that war, Korea (now with Japanese support) declared the unequal treaties with Qing China to be void, and unilaterally withdrew the extraterritoriality and other powers granted to China in respect of the concessions. The concessions were formally abolished in 1898.

## Dutch concessions

In Japan, since 1609, the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) had run a trading post on the island of Hirado. Also, after a [rebellion by mostly Catholic converts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimabara_Rebellion), all Portuguese were expelled from [Dejima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejima) in 1639. So, in 1641, The Dutch were forced, by government officials of [Tokugawa shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate), to move from Hirado to Dejima in Nagasaki.[^cite_note-9] The [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company)'s trading post at Dejima was abolished when Japan concluded the [Treaty of Kanagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kanagawa) with the United States in 1858.

## Italian concessions

The [Italian concession of Tianjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_concession_of_Tianjin) (Tientsin) was conceded to the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(1861-1946\)) by [Qing China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_China) on 7 September 1901. It was administered by Italy's [Consul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consul_\(representative\)) and had a population of 6,261 in 1935, including 536 foreigners. Several ships of the Italian Royal Navy (*[Regia Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regia_Marina)*) were based at Tianjin. During [World War II](32927.md), the primary Italian vessels based at Tianjin were the [minelayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minelayer) [*Lepanto*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_minelayer_Lepanto) and the [gunboat](274471.md) [*Carlotto*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_gunboat_Ermanno_Carlotto). On 10 September 1943, the Italian concession at Tianjin was occupied by [Japan](15573.md). In 1943, Italian Fascist dictator [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini)'s (however virtually powerless) [Italian Social Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic) relinquished the concession to the Japanese-sponsored ['Chinese National Government'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Jingwei_Government), a Japanese [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) led by [Wang Jingwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Jingwei); it was never recognized by the Kingdom of Italy, the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), or most world governments. On 10 February 1947, by [peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Peace_with_Italy,_1947), the zone was formally returned to [Nationalist China](5405.md) by the [Italian Republic](14532.md).

## Portuguese concession

- [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Macau): around 1552–1553, the Portuguese obtained permission to establish a settlement as a reward for defeating pirates and to mediate in trade between China and Japan and between both nations and Europe; it was leased from the empire of China from 1670. The concession turned into a Portuguese colony in the mid-19th century. The [Chinese government assumed sovereignty over Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_sovereignty_over_Macau) on 20 December 1999, ending 329 years of Portuguese colonial rule.

In 1502, the Portuguese built a trade post in [Pulicat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulicat) because its location at the mouth of a lagoon made it a great natural harbor.

  &#x20;Then, [Timoji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timoji) (vassal of [Vijayanagara Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire) and ally of [Portuguese empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire)) prompted the Portuguese to conquer [Goa](40010153.md) (controlled by Muslims), the main port for the horse trade, so Portugal invested in the [capture of Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_conquest_of_Goa) to the [Sultanate of Bijapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bijapur) with the support of Timoji and recognized by the Raja of [Cochin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cochin), [Cannanore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolathunadu) and emperor [Krishnadevaraya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishnadevaraya).

- Nagasaki and [Dejima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejima): In 1580, [Ōmura Sumitada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cmura_Sumitada) cedes jurisdiction over Nagasaki and Mogi to the Portuguese [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus), but in 1588 [Toyotomi Hideyoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyotomi_Hideyoshi) exerts direct control over Nagasaki, Mogi, and Urakami from the Jesuits (after Hideyoshi banished [Christian](5211.md) [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) from Kyūshū, to exert greater control over the *[Kirishitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishitan)* *daimyō*).

## Jointly held concessions

- 21 September 1863 (after the 1862 Proposal to make Shanghai an independent "free city" was rejected) an [International Settlement in Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_International_Settlement) was created by union of the American and British concessions (consummated December 1863); in 1896 the concession was expanded. On 7 July 1927, a Chinese city government of Greater Shanghai was formally established. Its internationality can be seen in the flag of the Ministry of Industry and Trade-Shanghai: Flags of the [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), [Denmark](76972.md), [Third Republic of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic), [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy), [Netherlands](21148.md), [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), [United Kingdom of Sweden-Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_between_Sweden_and_Norway), [Kingdom of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), [Kingdom of Spain](26667.md), [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [United States of America](3434750.md). [Belgium](3343.md), [Peru](170691.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), [Japan](15573.md) and [Switzerland](26748.md) were also part of the international concession. The ROC government refused to grant treaty rights to new countries after World War I, such as Germany, Austria and Hungary (formerly the Austro-Hungarian and German Empire, whose privileges were abolished after 1918), [Poland](22936.md), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), [Yugoslavia](34244.md), the [Baltic States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states), and [Finland](10577.md). Russia waived its rights for Soviet political expediency. China declared that Belgium had lost its rights in 1927.

  [^cite_note-21]

  &#x20;In January/February 1931, the Japanese occupied the [Hongkou District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongkou_District) (Hongkew), and on 9 November 1937 the Chinese city of Shanghai, but only on 8 December 1941 would Japanese troops occupy the International Settlement (but not the French concession); it was dissolved by Japan in 1942. In February 1943 the settlement is officially abolished by the U.S. and Britain; in September 1945, the last territory is restored to China.

- On January 10, 1902, the consuls of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), the [United States](3434750.md), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), [France](5843419.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Spain](26667.md), [Denmark](76972.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md), [Sweden-Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_between_Sweden_and_Norway), [Japan](15573.md) and other eight countries signed the "Gulangyu delimitation charter" in the Gulangyu Japanese Consulate. Subsequently, in January 1903, the [Gulangyu International Settlement Municipal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulangsu_International_Settlement) was established.

  [^cite_note-22]

- [Beijing Legation Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Legation_Quarter): a de facto concession.

- [Tangier International Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier_International_Zone): Under the Paris Convention, Tangier was made a neutral zone under joint administration by the participating countries (Spain, France and UK).

  [^cite_note-23]

  &#x20;The Paris Convention was proposed for ratification to the other powers that were party to the [Algeciras Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras_Conference) - except [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), [Austria](26964606.md) and [Hungary](13275.md), disempowered by the peace treaties (respectively of [Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles), [Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain-en-Laye_\(1919\)) and [Trianon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Trianon)), and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), then estranged from the international system.

  [^cite_note-24]

  &#x20;Italy's demand to join the international framework on a par with the signatories of the Paris Convention was supported by Spain from 1926, then by the UK, and a new conference eventually started in Paris in March 1928.

  [^cite_note-25]

  &#x20;Then, [Portugal](23033.md), [Belgium](3343.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md), and [Italy](14532.md) joined the city government. Then, after [World War II](32927.md) and the [Spanish occupation of Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_occupation_of_Tangier_\(1940%E2%80%931945\)), a quadripartite conference ([France](5843419.md), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), [UK](31717.md) and [United States](3434750.md)) met in Paris in August 1945, concluding a temporary Anglo-French Agreement of 31 August 1945, in which the two powers made arrangements for the re-establishment of the Zone's international institutional framework, inviting the United States and the Soviet Union to join it, passing both of them to be present in the city government.

  [^cite_note-26]

- [Dobruja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobruja): The [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers) occupied all of Dobruja and, according to [Treaty of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Bucharest_\(1918\)), the territory from south of [Cernavodă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cernavod%C4%83)-[Constanța](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba) railroad up to the Danube and the [Sfântu Gheorghe branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sf%C3%A2ntu_Gheorghe_branch) in a joint condominium recognized by Romania and Bulgarian states (then Bulgarian annexed it).
