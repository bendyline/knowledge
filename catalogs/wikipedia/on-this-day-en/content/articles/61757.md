**Kanagawa Prefecture** (神奈川県, *Kanagawa-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[ka.naꜜ.ɡa.wa, -ŋa.wa, -na.ɡa.waꜜ.keɴ, -ŋa.waꜜ-\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-2]) is a [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located in the [Kantō region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region) of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu).[^cite_note-3] Kanagawa Prefecture is the [second-most populous prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Japanese_prefectures_by_population) of Japan at 9,221,129 (1 April 2022) and third-densest at 3,800 inhabitants per square kilometre (9,800/sq mi). Its geographic area of 2,415 km<sup>2</sup> (932 sq mi) makes it fifth-smallest. Kanagawa Prefecture borders [Tokyo](30057.md) to the north, [Yamanashi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamanashi_Prefecture) to the northwest and [Shizuoka Prefecture](180999.md) to the west.

[Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama) is the capital and largest city of Kanagawa Prefecture and the [second-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Japan) in Japan, with other major cities including [Kawasaki](6793101.md), [Sagamihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagamihara), and [Fujisawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujisawa,_Kanagawa).[^cite_note-4] Kanagawa Prefecture is located on Japan's eastern [Pacific coast](751843.md) on [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay) and [Sagami Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagami_Bay), separated by the [Miura Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miura_Peninsula), across from [Chiba Prefecture](179796.md) on the [Bōsō Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C5%8Ds%C5%8D_Peninsula). Kanagawa Prefecture is part of the [Greater Tokyo Area](354951.md), the most populous [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in the world,[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] with Yokohama and a number of its cities being major commercial hubs and southern [suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb) of Tokyo. Kanagawa Prefecture was the political and economic center of Japan during the [Kamakura period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura_period) when [Kamakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura) was the [de facto](43594.md) capital and largest city of Japan as the seat of the [Kamakura shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura_shogunate) from 1185 to 1333. Kanagawa Prefecture is a popular tourist area in the Tokyo region, with Kamakura and [Hakone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakone,_Kanagawa) being two popular side trip destinations.

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Kanagawa Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Kanagawa_Prefecture)



*[The Great Wave off Kanagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Wave_off_Kanagawa)* original print

The prefecture has some archaeological sites going back to the [Jōmon period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_period) (around 400 BCE). About 3,000 years ago, [Mount Hakone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hakone) produced a volcanic explosion which resulted in [Lake Ashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ashi) on the western area of the prefecture.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

It is believed that the [imperial dynasty](3177105.md) ruled this area from the 5th century onwards. In the [ancient era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_era), its plains were sparsely inhabited.

In medieval Japan, Kanagawa was part of the provinces of [Sagami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagami_Province) and [Musashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musashi_Province).[^cite_note-9] [Kamakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura) in central Sagami was the capital of Japan during the [Kamakura period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura_period) (1185–1333).

During the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period), the western part of Sagami Province was governed by the *[daimyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimy%C5%8D)* of [Odawara Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odawara_Castle), while the eastern part was directly governed by the [Tokugawa shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate) in [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_\(Tokyo\)) (modern-day Tokyo).

[Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(USN\)) [Matthew Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_C._Perry) landed in Kanagawa in 1853 and 1854 and signed the [Convention of Kanagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_Kanagawa) to force open Japanese ports to the United States. [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama), the largest deep-water port in [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay), was opened to foreign traders in 1859 after several more years of foreign pressure, and eventually developed into the largest trading port in Japan. Nearby [Yokosuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokosuka), closer to the mouth of Tokyo Bay, developed as a naval port and now serves as headquarters for the [U.S. 7th Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._7th_Fleet) and the fleet operations of the [Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force](2370744.md). After the [Meiji period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_period), a number of foreigners lived in Yokohama City, and visited [Hakone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakone). The [Meiji government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Meiji_Japan) developed the first railways in Japan, from [Shinbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinbashi) (in Tokyo) to Yokohama in 1872.

The epicenter of the [1923 Great Kantō earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_Great_Kant%C5%8D_earthquake) was deep beneath [Izu Ōshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izu_%C5%8Cshima) (island) in Sagami Bay. It devastated Tokyo, the port city of [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama), surrounding prefectures of [Chiba](179796.md), [Kanagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanagawa), and [Shizuoka](180999.md), and caused widespread damage throughout the [Kantō region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region).[^cite_note-10] The sea receded as much as 400 metres from the shore at [Manazuru Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manazuru,_Kanagawa), and then rushed back towards the shore in a great wall of water which swamped Mitsuishi-shima.[^cite_note-11] At [Kamakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura), the total death toll from earthquake, tsunami, and fire exceeded 2,000 victims.[^cite_note-12] At [Odawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odawara), ninety percent of the buildings collapsed immediately, and subsequent fires burned the rubble along with anything left standing.[^cite_note-13]

Yokohama, Kawasaki, and other major cities were heavily damaged by the U.S. bombing in 1945. Total casualties amounted to more than several thousand. After the war, General [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur), the [Supreme Commander of the Allied Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Commander_of_the_Allied_Powers) for the [Occupation of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan), landed in Kanagawa, before moving to other areas. U.S. military bases still remain in Kanagawa, including [Camp Zama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Zama) ([Army](32087.md)), [Yokosuka Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fleet_Activities_Yokosuka), [Naval Air Facility Atsugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Facility_Atsugi) ([Navy](20518076.md)).[^cite_note-14]

## Geography

[Minato Mirai 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minato_Mirai_21) commercial area is located between [Nishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi-ku,_Yokohama) and [Naka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naka_District,_Kanagawa) districts, [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama) city, Kanagawa prefecture at sunset. Kanagawa is a relatively small prefecture located at the southeastern corner of the [Kantō Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_Plain)[^cite_note-jiban.co.jp-15] wedged between Tokyo on the north, the foothills of [Mount Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fuji) on the northwest, and the [Sagami Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagami_Bay)[^cite_note-jiban.co.jp-15] and [Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Bay) on the south and east. The eastern side of the prefecture is relatively flat and heavily urbanized, including the large port cities of [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama) and Kawasaki.

The southeastern area nearby the [Miura Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miura_Peninsula) is less urbanized, with the ancient city of [Kamakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura) drawing tourists to temples and shrines. The western part, bordered by [Yamanashi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamanashi_Prefecture) and [Shizuoka Prefecture](180999.md) on the west,[^cite_note-pref.kanagawa.jp-16] is more mountainous and includes resort areas like [Odawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odawara) and [Hakone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakone). The area, stretching 80 kilometres (50 mi) from west to east and 60 kilometres (37 mi) from north to south, contains 2,400 square kilometres (930 sq mi) of land, accounting for 0.64% of the total land area of Japan.[^cite_note-pref.kanagawa.jp-16]

As of 1 April 2012, 23% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely the [Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji-Hakone-Izu_National_Park); Tanzawa-Ōyama Quasi-National Park; and [Jinba Sagamiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinba_Sagamiko_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Manazuru Hantō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manazuru_Hant%C5%8D_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Okuyugawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okuyugawara_Prefectural_Natural_Park), and [Tanzawa-Ōyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzawa-%C5%8Cyama_Prefectural_Natural_Park) Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-17]

### Topography

Topographically, the prefecture consists of three distinct areas. The mountainous western region features the [Tanzawa Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzawa_Mountains) and the volcano [Mount Hakone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hakone). The hilly eastern region is characterized by the [Tama Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_Hills) and [Miura Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miura_Peninsula). The central region, which surrounds the Tama Hills and Miura Peninsula, consists of flat stream terraces and low lands around major rivers including the [Sagami River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagami_River), [Sakai River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakai_River_\(Tokyo,_Kanagawa\)), [Tsurumi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsurumi_River), and [Tama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_River).[^cite_note-pref.kanagawa.jp-16]

The [Tama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_River) forms much of the boundary between Kanagawa and Tokyo. The [Sagami River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagami_River) flows through the middle of the prefecture. In the western region, the Sakawa runs through a small lowland, the Sakawa Lowland, between Mount Hakone to the west and the Ōiso Hills to the east, and flows into Sagami Bay.[^cite_note-jiban.co.jp-15]

The Tanzawa Mountain Range, part of the Kantō Mountain Range, contains [Mount Hiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hiru) (1,673 m or 5,489 ft), the highest peak in the prefecture. Other mountains measure similar mid-range heights: Mount Hinokiboramaru (1,601 m or 5,253 ft), [Mount Tanzawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tanzawa), (1,567 m or 5,141 ft), [Mount Ōmuro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_%C5%8Cmuro_\(Tanzawa\)) (1,588 m or 5,210 ft), Mount Himetsugi (1,433 m or 4,701 ft), and [Mount Usu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Usu) (1,460 m or 4,790 ft). The mountain range is lower in height southward leading to Hadano Basin to the Ōiso Hills. At the eastern foothills of the mountain range lies the Isehara Plateau and across the Sagami River the [Sagamino plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagamino_plateau).[^cite_note-jiban.co.jp-15]

In 1945, Kanagawa was the 15th most populous prefecture in Japan, with a population of about 1.9 million. In the years after the war, the prefecture underwent rapid urbanization as a part of the Greater Tokyo Area. The population as of September 1, 2014 is estimated to be 9.1 million.[^cite_note-19] Kanagawa became the second most populous prefecture in 2006.&#x20;

## Transportation

Kanagawa's transport network is heavily intertwined with that of Tokyo (*see: [Transportation in Greater Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Greater_Tokyo)*). [Shin-Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin-Yokohama_Station) and [Odawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odawara_Station) stations on the [Tōkaidō Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkaid%C5%8D_Shinkansen) are located in the prefecture, providing high-speed rail service to Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka, and other major cities.
