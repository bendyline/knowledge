**Kampala** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/kæmˈpɑːlə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English),[^cite_note-Collins-2][^cite_note-\:2-3] [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/kɑːmˈ-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e8/Kampala.ogg/Kampala.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e8/Kampala.ogg/Kampala.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>)[^cite_note-Collins-2][^cite_note-4] is the [capital](181337.md) and largest city of [Uganda](31816.md). The city proper has a population of 1,797,722 (2024 census)[^cite_note-5] and is divided into the five political divisions of [Kampala Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala_Central_Division), [Kawempe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawempe_Division), [Makindye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makindye_Division), [Nakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakawa_Division), and [Rubaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubaga_Division).[^cite_note-6]

Kampala's metropolitan area consists of the city proper and the neighboring [Wakiso District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakiso_District), [Mukono District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukono_District), [Mpigi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpigi_District), [Buikwe District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buikwe_District) and [Luweero District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luweero_District). It has a rapidly growing population that is estimated at 6,709,900 people in 2019, in an area of 8,451.9 km<sup>2</sup> (3,263.3 square miles).[^cite_note-7] Other estimates put the size of the metropolitan area at around four million people.[^cite_note-8]

In 2015, this metropolitan area generated an estimated nominal GDP of \$13.8 billion (constant US dollars of 2011),[^cite_note-9] which was more than half of Uganda's GDP for that year, indicating the importance of Kampala to Uganda's economy.

In 2018, Kampala was among the fastest-growing cities in [Africa](5334607.md), with an annual population growth rate of 4.03 percent.[^cite_note-10] American consulting firm [Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_\(consulting_firm\)) has regularly ranked Kampala as East Africa's best city to live in,[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] ahead of [Nairobi](21482.md) and [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali).

## Etymology

Kampala originally referred to only the present-day [Old Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kampala) hill, on whose summit [Fort Lugard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lugard) was located, and the initial headquarters of the British colonial authorities in the soon to be [Uganda Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Protectorate).[^cite_note-13]

Before the British construction of [Fort Lugard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lugard), the hill was a hunting reserve of the [Kabaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaka_of_Buganda) (King) of [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda_Kingdom) and had several species of antelope, especially the [impala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impala). As a result, when the British colonial officials were allocated this hill by the then [Kabaka of Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaka_of_Buganda), they referred to it as "The Hill of the Impala".[^cite_note-14]

The natives, in whose territory this British settlement was located, then translated "Hill of the Impala" as *Akasozi k’empala*. This was then shortened to *k'empala* and finally, the name ‘*Kampala*’ was brought to life. *Kasozi* means "hill", *ke* "of", and *empala* the plural of "impala". Hence the name "Kampala" came to refer to this initial British colonial settlement that would later on spread out from the occupied [Old Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kampala) hill near the pre-existing [Kibuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibuga?action=edit\&redlink=1) (capital) of the [Buganda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda).[^cite_note-15]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Kampala).



Kampala, the city from Cathedral Hill in 1936

This area of numerous [hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill) and [swamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp) that later become known as Kampala was part of the core of the highly centralised [Buganda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda_Kingdom). It was also the site of the shifting *Kibuga* (capital) of the different *Bassekabaka* (kings) of the [Buganda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda), with each *[Kabaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaka_of_Buganda)* (king) upon coronation, or subsequently during their reign, setting up their *Kibuga* (capital) on a new or different hill as they wished or desired.

### 19th century

The first written description of this *Kibuga* (capital) was by the explorer [Sir Richard Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Richard_Burton) in his book, *The Lake Region of [East Africa](260987.md)*, published in 1860. In the book, Burton, relying on the information collected by Snay Bin Amir, an Arab trader, described the *Kibuga* as:

> …the settlement is not less than a [day's journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day's_journey) in length, the buildings are of cane and rattan. The sultan's (Kabaka) palace is at least a mile long and the circular huts neatly arranged in a line are surrounded by a strong fence which has only four gates.

In 1862, when explorer [John Speke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Speke) arrived in [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda), the *Kibuga* (capital) was at Bandabarogo, present-day [Banda Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda,_Uganda), and the reigning *[Kabaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaka_of_Buganda)* (King) was [Mutesa I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutesa_I_of_Buganda).[^cite_note-16]

In 1875, explorer [Henry Morton Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morton_Stanley) reported the capital as being at present-day [Lubaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubaga) Hill, where he met the same *Kabaka*, Mutesa I.

During this visit, Stanley wrote a letter that was published in *[The Daily Telegraph](23797577.md)*, inviting [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) to come to [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda). He also described the *Kibuga* in his 1870s dispatches to *[The New York Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Herald)*, thus:

> As we approached the capital, the highway from Usavara \[Busabala] increased in width from 20 ft \[6 meters] to 150 ft \[45 meters]...Arrived at the capital I found the vast collection of huts crowning the eminence were the Royal Quarters, around which ran several palisades and circular courts, between which and the city was a circular road, ranging from 100 ft \[30 meters] to 200 ft \[60 meters] in width with gardens and huts...

— Bennet, N.R. (ed.) *Stanley's Dispatches to the New York Herald*, 1871–1872, 1874–1877, Boston, 1970.

In 1877, the first [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionaries) from the [Church Mission Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Mission_Society), who were of the [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) faith, arrived from the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) and were allocated [Namirembe Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namirembe_Hill). Two years later, in 1879, the Catholic [White Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Fathers) also arrived, first settling at the present-day village of Kitebi near Lubaga; subsequently, they would be allocated [Lubaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubaga) Hill. The arrival of these two missionary groups laid the ground for the religious wars of 1888 to 1892 between their new converts and forced the missionaries from [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) to then lobby for the British government to take over [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda)/[Uganda](31816.md) as a protectorate.

In 1890, [Frederick Lugard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Lugard), an agent of the [Imperial British East Africa Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_British_East_Africa_Company), arrived in [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda) during the reign of [Kabaka Mwanga II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwanga_II_of_Buganda), with whom he signed a treaty of protection by the British government over Buganda, and the *Kibuga* (capital) was located at [Mengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengo,_Uganda) Hill. [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(British_Army\)) Lugard would, later on, be allocated the Kampala hill that would soon be known as [Old Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kampala), and on which he built a fort.[^cite_note-17]

In 1895, [Mengo Senior School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengo_Senior_School), the first school offering Western education in Kampala, was opened by the [Church Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Missionary_Society) at [Namirembe hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namirembe_hill), where mostly the children of chiefs and pages of the royal palaces were students.

In 1897, Mwanga launched a rebellion but was defeated and was subsequently captured and [exiled](9855.md), in 1899, to [the Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seychelles) alongside Omukama [Kabalega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabalega), and his 3-year-old son was made *[Kabaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaka)* by the combined forces of the European officers leading Nubian and Baganda [colonial soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_troops).[^cite_note-18] This state of affairs later culminated in the signing of the [Buganda Agreement (1900)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda_Agreement_\(1900\)) that formalised British colonial rule in [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda).

Also in 1897, Kampala's first Western-style health facility, [Mengo Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengo_Hospital), was opened on [Namirembe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namirembe) hill by British doctor and missionary [Sir Albert Ruskin Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ruskin_Cook). In addition, Sir Albert Ruskin Cook founded [Mulago Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulago_Hospital), the current National Referral Hospital, at [Mulago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulago) hill in 1913.[^cite_note-19]

In 1899, the [Missionary Sisters of Our Lady of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary_Sisters_of_Our_Lady_of_Africa) founded [Lubaga Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubaga_Hospital) on [Lubaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubaga) Hill.

### 20th century

In 1900, the regents of the infant *[Kabaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaka)* [Daudi Cwa II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daudi_Cwa_II_of_Buganda) (who were [Apolo Kagwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apolo_Kagwa), the *[Katikiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katikiro)* (Prime Minister) of Buganda, Stanislaus Mugwanya, the *Mulamuzi* (Chief Judge) of Buganda, and Zakaria Kisingiri, the *Muwanika* (Chief Treasurer) of Buganda, with [Bishop](4092.md) [Alfred Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Tucker)), signed the [Buganda Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda_Agreement) on behalf of Buganda with [Sir Harry Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Harry_Johnston), who signed on behalf of the [British government](25318118.md).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] This agreement with Sir Harry Johnston created new land tenures such as freehold, [Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown) land, and [mailo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mailo), and divided up and allocated the land in such a way that would come to define the development of Kampala.

The land in [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda)'s *Kibuga* (capital), including [Mengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengo,_Uganda) Hill and [Makerere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makerere) Hill, was allocated to the young *[Kabaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaka)*, the [Baganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baganda) colonial collaborators, etc., under mailo and freehold. The religious missions were also formally allocated land they were previously occupying. Thus, the [Catholic](606848.md) [White Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Fathers) got [Lubaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubaga) Hill, the Protestant [Church Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Missionary_Society) got [Namirembe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namirembe) Hill, the Muslims under Prince Nuhu Mbogo's leadership received [Kibuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibuli) Hill, the [British Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_England_and_Wales) [Mill Hill Missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph's_Missionary_Society_of_Mill_Hill) received most of [Nsambya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsambya) Hill. The [Uganda Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Protectorate) government obtained land classified as Crown lands in the area such as [Old Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kampala) Hill, [Nakasero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakasero) Hill, etc.

To legalise the above changes, the following laws and ordinances were subsequently passed: The Crown lands Ordinance of 1903, The Land Law of 1908, The Registration of [Land Titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_titles) ordinance of 1922, and the Busulu and Envujo law of 1928.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

In 1906, the boundary of Kampala was set as a three-mile radius from the “present Nakasero Fort” at Old Kampala, and the Kampala Local Sanitary Board was designated as the authority for urban administration in the Kampala Township Area. By the time of Kampala Township's first planning scheme in 1912, the area covered by the plan was about 1,400 acres (567 ha).[^cite_note-24]

In 1912, Kampala Township received its first land-use plan and had a European and Asian population of 2,850.[^cite_note-25]

In 1922, Kampala's oldest university, [Makerere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makerere_University), was founded as the Uganda Technical College at the present Makerere Hill and initially offered [carpentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpentry), [building construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction), [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics), [arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_arts), [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), and [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine).

In 1930, the first sewerage plan was prepared to target a population of 20,000 people in the [Nakasero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakasero) and [Old Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kampala) areas of the Kampala township. This plan guided sewerage development from 1936 to 1940 in planned urban areas of the Kampala Township and excluded the *Kibuga* area occupied by the [Baganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baganda) and other natives.[^cite_note-26]



Kampala. The Imperial Hotel in 1936.

In 1931, the [Uganda Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Railway) line reached Kampala, connecting Kampala to [Mombasa Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Port), thirty-five years after the commencement of its construction.[^cite_note-27]

In 1938, The East African Power & Lighting Company was granted a licence for thermal electric power generation and distribution for the towns of Kampala and [Entebbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe), and in the same year [Sir Philip Mitchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Mitchell_\(colonial_administrator\)), the [Governor of Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Uganda), switched on Kampala and [Uganda](31816.md)'s first electric [street lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_lights).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

In 1945, [Ernst May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_May), a German architect, was commissioned by the [Uganda Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Protectorate) Government to design a new physical plan for Kampala. Ernst May's plan of 1947 was intended to extend Kampala eastwards covering [Kololo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kololo) Hill and [Naguru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naguru,_Uganda) Hill, and with the commercial centre on the southern slopes of [Nakasero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakasero) Hill, an industrial zone in the southeast of Kampala, and, for the first time, a planned residential zone for the [Ugandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan) natives.[^cite_note-auto1-30] The plan was never fully implemented, and in 1951 the third physical plan by [Henry Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kendall_\(urban_planner\)) was instead adopted, though it incorporated some elements of Ernst May's 1947 plan.[^cite_note-Folkers,_Antoni_S._2019_p._61-31]



Kampala in the 1950s

Henry Kendall's 1951 plan expanded Kampala from the 5.67 km<sup>2</sup> (2.19 square miles) area of the 1930 plan to an area of 28 km<sup>2</sup> (11 square miles) incorporating areas like [Kololo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kololo) Hill, and the Industrial Area. However, like the first two planning schemes, the 1951 plan failed to achieve many of its stated objectives.[^cite_note-Folkers,_Antoni_S._2019_p._61-31]

On 9 October 1962, [Uganda](31816.md) gained independence; subsequently the capital city was transferred from [Entebbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe) to Kampala and in the same year, Kampala was granted [city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status).

In 1968, six years after [Uganda](31816.md) attained independence, the boundaries of Kampala were expanded incorporating the *Kibuga* (then known as Mengo Municipality), [Kawempe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawempe) and [Nakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakawa) Townships, and areas including Muyenga and Ggaba.[^cite_note-auto2-32] This increased the administrative area of Kampala from 28 km<sup>2</sup> (11 square miles) to the current 189 km<sup>2</sup> (73 square miles).

In 1972, the fourth physical plan for Kampala was made covering the newly incorporated areas of Kampala's boundary extensions of 1968, but the subsequent political and economic turmoil of the 1970s and 1980s meant the plan was never implemented.[^cite_note-auto2-32][^cite_note-33]

The [Battle of Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kampala) during the [Ugandan Bush War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_Bush_War) occurred in January 1986. It resulted in the capture of the city by the [National Resistance Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Resistance_Movement), led by [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md) and the subsequent surrender of the [Ugandan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_government).

Similarly, the fifth physical plan for Kampala, made in 1994, like the 1972 plan, was also never implemented.[^cite_note-34]

### 21st century

In 2010, the [Kampala Capital City Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala_Capital_City_Authority) Act was enacted, giving the [Ugandan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_government) more control of the administration of Kampala. The act also created the Kampala Metropolitan Physical Planning Authority with the stated aims of improving the [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure) of the City of Kampala and the surrounding districts of [Wakiso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakiso_District), [Mukono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukono_District), [Buikwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buikwe_District), [Mpigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpigi_District) and [Luwero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luweero_District).[^cite_note-35]

On 11 July 2010, suicide bombers affiliated with [al-Shabaab](8726463.md), a Sunni [Islamist](15012.md) group based in [Somalia](27358.md), carried out [two nearly simultaneous bombings in Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Kampala_bombings), killing 74 people.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-\:3-37] After eleven years of relative calm, on 16 November 2021, the [Allied Democratic Forces](1258240.md) (ADF), an Islamist group based in [eastern Congo](19599929.md) with ties to the [Islamic State](9087364.md), carried out [two suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Uganda_bombings) near the central police station and parliament, killing three people and injuring 36.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-\:3-37]

## Geography

### Topography



An apartment block in Kololo

The City of Kampala covers a total area of 189 km<sup>2</sup> (73 square miles), comprising 176 km<sup>2</sup> (68 square miles) of land and 13 km<sup>2</sup> (5.0 square miles) of water.

Kampala is a hilly place with its [valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley) filled with sluggish [rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River), lakes /swamps. The highest point in the city proper is the summit of [Kololo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kololo) hill at 1,311 metres (4,301 ft), located in the centre of the city and the lowest point at the shores of [Lake Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Victoria) south of the city centre at altitude of 1,135 metres (3,724 ft).

#### Hills



The Kasubi Tombs

Kampala was originally [built on seven hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_claimed_to_be_built_on_seven_hills), but it has expanded to cover more than the original seven hills.

The original seven hills are:

- [Old Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kampala) Hill on which [Fort Lugard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lugard) was located, the first seat of the British colonial authorities in colonial [Uganda](31816.md).

- The second is [Mengo Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengo,_Uganda) which was the then kibuga (capital) of [Buganda kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda_kingdom) at the start of British colonial rule.

- The third is [Kibuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibuli) Hill, that is home to the [Kibuli Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibuli_Mosque).

- The fourth is [Namirembe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namirembe) Hill, that was home to the Anglican [(Wangeleza)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wangeleza?action=edit\&redlink=1) faction of the Buganda religious wars of 1888 to 1892 and site of Namirembe [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral).

- The fifth is [Lubaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubaga) Hill, that was home to the White Fathers Catholic (Wafaransa) faction of the above-mentioned [Buganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda) religious wars and also site of the [Rubaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubaga_Cathedral) Catholic Cathedral.

- The sixth is [Nsambya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsambya) Hill, site of the former Cathedral of St Peter's Nsambya and allocated to the [British Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_England_and_Wales) Mill Hill Mission during the signing of the [Uganda Agreement (1900)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buganda_Agreement_\(1900\)).

- The seventh is [Nakasero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakasero) Hill on whose summit was Fort Nakasero, a British military installation built after relocating from [Fort Lugard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lugard) in Old Kampala. The hill was also the site of the European Hospital (the current government analytical laboratory opposite Ministry of Public Service headquarters).

### Vegetation

Kampala, due to the diversity of habitats that include [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland) and hills, was previously covered with short grasses on the tops of the hills, elephant grass (*Pennisetum purpureum* Schumach.), *[Cyperus papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyperus_papyrus)*, African water lily etc. in the [swamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp) and evergreen forests with trees such as [African olive (mpafu)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarium_schweinfurthii) and [Natal fig (mutuba)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficus_natalensis).[^cite_note-43]

### Geology

Kampala is located on the [East African Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Plateau) between the two arms of the [East African Rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Rift) and on the northern limits of [Tanzania Craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_craton).

### Climate

Kampala has a [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) (*Af*) under the [Köppen-Geiger climate classification system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification).

Kampala has two annual [wetter seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season). While the city does not have a true [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season) month, it has heavier precipitation from August to December and from February to June. Between February and June Kampala has substantially heavier rainfall per month. April has the heaviest amount of precipitation, at an average of around 169 millimetres (6.7 in) of rain.

## Demographics



A street map of Kampala



A view of Kampala from space, 2005

See also: [Demographics of Uganda](56923.md)

The population of Kampala city proper has grown from 62,264 in 1948, to 1,189,142 in 2002, then 1,507,080 in 2014. In 2019, the population was estimated to be 1,650,800.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

In 2024, Kampala had 1,797,722 residents, consisting of 1,004,884 males and 792,838 females. The average household size was 2.9 persons.[^cite_note-\:4-54]

Kampala, being the capital city and economic engine of [Uganda](31816.md), has a diverse ethnic population drawn from all parts of the country and also from neighbouring countries such as [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), [Rwanda](25645.md), [South Sudan](32350676.md), [Eritrea](17238590.md), [Somalia](27358.md), and even from countries as far away as India and China.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

Cross-cultural intimate relations in Kampala and even Uganda as a whole is still unusual. Although many of Kampala's residents live and work in close contact, they still define themselves by their ethnic origins. This is more evident in the native languages (alongside [Luganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luganda) and English) that are used at home, workplaces, and public spaces. In addition to the [Baganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baganda) and Banyankole, other large ethnic groups include the [Basoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basoga), [Bafumbira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisoro), [Batoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toro_Kingdom), [Bakiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakiga), [Alur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alur_people), [Bagisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagisu) (better known as Bamasaba), [Banyoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyoro), [Iteso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iteso), [Langi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lango_people), and [Acholi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acholi_people).[^cite_note-59]

## Economy



The Church House, downtown Kampala

Efforts are underway to relocate heavy industry to the [Kampala Business and Industrial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala_Business_and_Industrial_Park), located in [Namanve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namanve), [Mukono District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukono_District), approximately 14 kilometres (8.7 mi) east of the city's central business district,[^cite_note-64] thereby cutting down on city traffic congestion. Some of the businesses that maintain their headquarters in the city centre include all of the 25 [commercial banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_bank) licensed in Uganda; the [New Vision Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Vision_Group), the leading news media conglomerate and majority owned by the government; and the *[Daily Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Monitor)* publication, a member of the Kenya-based [Nation Media Group](19847787.md). [Air Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Uganda) maintained its headquarters in an office complex on [Kololo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kololo) Hill in Kampala.[^cite_note-65]

Crown Beverages Limited, the sole [Pepsi-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi-Cola) franchise bottler in Uganda, is situated in [Nakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakawa), a division of Kampala, about 5 kilometres (3 mi) east of the city centre.[^cite_note-66]

The informal sector is a large contributor to Kampala's [GDP](12594.md). Citizens who work in the formal sector also participate in informal activities to earn more income for their families. A public servant in Kampala, for example, may engage in agriculture in addition to working in the formal sector. Other informal fields include owning taxis and urban agriculture. The use of Kampala's wetlands for urban farming has increased over the past few decades. It connects the informal rural settlements with the more industrialized parts of the city. The produce grown in the wetlands is sold in markets in the urban areas.[^cite_note-67]

In December 2015, Google launched its first [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi) network in Kampala.[^cite_note-68]

While more than 30 percent of Kampala's inhabitants practice [urban agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agriculture), the city of Kampala donated 13 hectares (32 acres) to promote urban agriculture in the northeastern parish of Kyanja, in [Nakawa Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakawa_Division).[^cite_note-69]

## Transport

Kampala is served by [Entebbe International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe_International_Airport), which is the largest airport in Uganda.



Taxi station in Kampala



Bodaboda in Kampala

[Boda-bodas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boda-boda) (local motorbike transport) are a popular mode of transport that gives access to many areas within and outside the city. Standard fees for these range from USh\:1,000 to 2,000 or more. Boda-bodas are useful for passing through rush-hour traffic, although many are poorly maintained and dangerous.[^cite_note-70]

In early 2007, it was announced that Kampala would remove commuter taxis from its streets and replace them with a comprehensive city bus service. In Kampala, the term "taxi" refers to a 15-seater minibus used as public transport. The bus service was expected to cover the greater Kampala metropolitan area including [Mukono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukono), [Mpigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpigi), Bombo, [Entebbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe), [Wakiso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakiso) and [Gayaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gayaza). As of December 2011 the service had not yet started.[^cite_note-71]

Having successfully completed the Northern Bypass, the government, in collaboration with its stakeholders, now plans to introduce the [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) (BRT) system in Kampala by 2014. In March 2012, Pioneer Easy Bus Company, a private transport company, started public bus service in Kampala with an estimated 100 buses each with a 60-passenger capacity, 30 seated and 30 standing, acquired from [China](5405.md). Another 422 buses were expected in the country in 2012. The buses operate 24 hours daily.

The company has a concession to provide public transport in the city for the next five years.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] The buses were impounded for back taxes in December 2013. The company expected to resume operation in February 2015.[^cite_note-74]

In 2014, Uganda's President [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md) and [China Civil Engineering Construction Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Civil_Engineering_Construction_Corporation) signed a Memorandum of Understanding, to embark on building a light rail system, the [Greater Kampala Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Kampala_Light_Rail),[^cite_note-75] similar to the one in [Addis Ababa](52593.md), Ethiopia.

In April 2011, the pressure group Activists for Change (A4C) held its first [Walk to Work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk_to_work) protest near Kampala, in response to a comment by President [Museveni](293409.md) on the increased cost of fuel, which had risen by 50 percent between January and April 2011. He said: "What I call on the public to do is to use fuel sparingly. Don't drive to bars."[^cite_note-Time-76][^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-77] The protest, which called on workers to walk to work to highlight the increased cost of transport in Uganda,[^cite_note-Time-76] was disrupted by police, who fired [tear gas](1202646.md) and arrested three-time presidential candidate [Kizza Besigye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kizza_Besigye) and [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Uganda\)) leader [Norbert Mao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Mao).[^cite_note-78] During the protest, Besigye was shot in the right arm by a rubber bullet. The government blamed the violence on protesters.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-77]

In 2016, the [Rift Valley Railways Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rift_Valley_Railways_Consortium) (RVR) and [Kampala Capital City Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala_Capital_City_Authority) established passenger rail service between [Namanve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namanve) and Kampala and between Kampala and [Kyengera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyengera). Those services were temporarily discontinued after RVR lost its concession in Uganda in October 2017.[^cite_note-Temp-79]

In February 2018, the service was restored when [Uganda Railways Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Railways_Corporation) took over the operations of the [metre gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_gauge) railway system in Uganda.[^cite_note-80] A new Kampala to [Port Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Bell) route is being planned to be added in the 2018/2019 financial year.[^cite_note-Temp-79]

## Health care

Kampala is Uganda's main hub for health care services. It has a mix of public hospitals, private clinics, and specialized medical centres that serve locals and international visitors. The quality of care varies depending on the type of facility, but in general, Kampala provides the best medical options in the country.[^cite_note-81]

Public health care in Kampala is overseen by the Ministry of Health. The city hosts key government hospitals such as Mulago National Referral Hospital, Uganda's largest and most advanced public hospital. Mulago serves as a teaching hospital for [Makerere University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makerere_University)'s College of Health Sciences and provides a wide range of services, including surgery, maternal health, paediatrics, and specialized care. While public hospitals are often affordable or free, they face challenges like overcrowding, long waiting times, limited medical supplies, and under-staffing.[^cite_note-82]

Kampala has a growing number of private hospitals and clinics offering higher standards of care, often preferred by the middle class, expats, and medical tourists. Some notable private institutions include: Nakasero Hospital – a modern private hospital known for advanced diagnostics and surgery. [International Hospital Kampala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hospital_Kampala) (IHK) – a leading facility offering general and specialized care. Kampala Hospital – known for quality services and patient-focused care.

Case Hospital provides inpatient and outpatient services among many others. Private facilities tend to be better equipped and offer faster service but come at a higher cost. Many accept international health insurance, though it's important to confirm in advance.

Kampala is also known for its vibrant shopping scene, offering everything from traditional [crafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft) to modern fashion. **Owino** **market** (*also known as St Balikuddembe market*) is one of the largest and busiest markets in [Uganda](31816.md), where one can find an array of goods including clothes, shoes, and fresh produce.

The city is also filled with a number of shopping malls, such as Acacia Mall in [kamwookya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamwookya), Arena Mall in [Nsambya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsambya), and Village Mall in [bugolobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugolobi).[^cite_note-\:5-84]

[Wandegeya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandegeya) Market is located near [Makerere University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makerere_University).[^cite_note-85] There is also [Nakasero Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakasero_Market), one of [Africa](5334607.md)'s largest and liveliest.[^cite_note-86]

Kampala's nightlife is another aspect of the city's leisure that cannot be overlooked. The city is alive with music and dance, with numerous bars, clubs, and lounges offering entertainment late into the night. [Kabalagala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabalagala) and [Kololo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kololo) are popular areas for nightlife.

### Cuisine

&#x20;Foods in Kampala

Kampala's cuisine

For those who prefer dining in a more formal setting, Kampala has numerous restaurants that offer a variety of cuisines.&#x20;

Cafe javas is a popular chain that offers a blend of local and international dishes for both a casual meal and a business meeting.&#x20;

One dish is called TV Chicken, named because the chicken is roasted in a rotisserie oven with a glass window that customers can watch the chicken through.[^cite_note-87]

Vendors selling **[nsenene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsenene)** (*Ruspolia differens*, a type of katydid formerly called "long-horned grasshoppers" or "bush crickets") set up shop in May and November, during their season.[^cite_note-88]

Gaba Fish Market, located on [Lake Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Victoria), is the biggest fish market in the city.[^cite_note-89]

### Ndere Cultural Centre

A prominent [cultural centre](6065329.md) in the Kampala area of Kisasi that aims to promote [Ugandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan) and [African cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Africa) expressions through [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music), dance, and drama. The name *Ndere* is derived from the noun '***endere***', which means *flute*. As an instrument found in all cultures, it is chosen as a peaceful symbol of the universality of [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture) expressions. The Ndere centre is famous for its *Ndere troupe*, a music and dance troupe that perform several nights every week at the centre showcasing music and dance from all over [Uganda](31816.md) as well as [Rwanda](25645.md) and [Burundi](21490998.md).[^cite_note-90]

## Sport

Kampala is home to the [City Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Oilers), one of [East Africa](260987.md)'s top [basketball](3921.md) club teams. It is the only East African team that competes in the [FIBA Africa Clubs Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Africa_Clubs_Champions_Cup). The Oilers play their home games in the [MTN Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTN_Arena?action=edit\&redlink=1), which is based in Kampala's [Lugogo Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugogo_Area).[^cite_note-91]

The city hosted the [IAAF World Cross Country Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF_World_Cross_Country_Championships) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_IAAF_World_Cross_Country_Championships). The city also hosted the [Rugby Africa Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Africa_Cup) in 2024 at the [Mandela National Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandela_National_Stadium). The [2027 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) will be hosted in the city alongside neighbouring countries of [Tanzania](30118.md) and [Kenya](188171.md). The city will also host the [2031 African Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2031_African_Games).

The [Mandela National Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandela_National_Stadium), commonly known as Namboole Stadium, is the largest and most iconic sports facility in Uganda. Located just outside Kampala in [Bweyogerere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bweyogerere), this multi-purpose stadium has a seating capacity of over 45,000 and is the primary venue for major sporting events, including football matches, athletics, and concerts. The stadium is home to the [Uganda](31816.md) national football team, the Cranes, and hosts various local and international competitions.

Lugogo Sports Complex is a [sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport) hub in Kampala, offering facilities for a range of sports including [tennis](29773.md), [cricket](25675557.md), and [boxing](4243.md). The MTN [Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena) within the complex is a modern indoor arena with a capacity of around 3,000, used for [basketball](3921.md), [netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball), [volleyball](32558.md), and other indoor sports. The [Lugogo Cricket Oval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugogo_Stadium) is another highlight, hosting national and international cricket matches, making it the centre of cricket in [Uganda](31816.md).

The Kampala [Golf](19568112.md) Club, located in the heart of the city, is [Uganda](31816.md)'s oldest golf course and a premier destination for golfers. The 18-hole course offers a challenging yet scenic layout, with well-maintained greens and fairways. The club also has a driving range and a clubhouse, making it a popular spot for both serious golfers and those looking to relax in a green environment.

&#x20;Sports in Kampala

Hamz stadium in Nakivubo

The renewed [Hamz Nakivubo Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakivubo_Stadium), formerly known as Nakivubo War Memorial Stadium, is in the central business district of Kampala.

The city is home to various football clubs playing in the [Uganda Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Premier_League) such as [Kampala Capital City Authority FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampala_Capital_City_Authority_FC), [SC Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Villa), [Police FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_FC_\(Uganda\)), and [Express FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_FC) There are also a number of teams in Kampala playing in the [FUFA Women Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FUFA_Women_Super_League) like [Kawempe Muslim LFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawempe_Muslim_LFC), [Lady Doves FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Doves_FC) and many others
