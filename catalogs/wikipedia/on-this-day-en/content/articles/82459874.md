***Spinosaurus mirabilis*** is a large [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct) species of the [spinosaurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinosaurid) theropod dinosaur [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) *[Spinosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinosaurus)*, known from the [Late Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cretaceous) [Farak Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farak_Formation) of Niger. The species was named and described in 2026, based on various cranial and postcranial bones collected in two localities. It is characterized by a long, low snout, a [scimitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scimitar)-shaped midline crest on the top of the skull, and a large sail over the back.

Fossils of *Spinosaurus mirabilis* were collected from outcrops of the [Farak Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farak_Formation) of Niger during a series of excavations in 2000, 2019, and 2022, led by paleontologist [Paul Sereno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Sereno). Material was collected from two localities, Iguidi and Jenguebi. Most of the material comes from the latter, including the [holotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holotype) specimen, [accessioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_number_\(cultural_property\)) as MNBH JEN1, which comprises a fragmentary skull (right [premaxilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#premaxilla), both [maxillae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#maxillae), [nasal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#nasal) crest, part of the right [dentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#dentary), and five maxillary teeth). A second Jenguebi specimen, MNBH JEN2, includes part of a left maxilla, a nasal crest, fragments of the [cervical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#cervicals) (neck) and [dorsal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#dorsals) (back) vertebrae, part of the left [ischium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#ischium), and part of the left [femur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#femur). Other specimens from this locality, MNBH JEN3–9, include a left maxilla, isolated teeth, another partial nasal crest, a right dentary, partial dorsal and [caudal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#caudals) (tail) vertebrae, a partial [chevron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#chevron), a left [tibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#tibia), and pedal [phalanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#phalanges) (toe bones). Material from Iguidi, MNBH IGU11, 25, and 38–40, includes many isolated tooth crowns, fragments of the dorsal vertebrae, part of a caudal vertebra, and a single toe bone. As of its 2026 description, the Iguidi material is temporarily on loan to Sereno's Fossil Lab at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), while all other specimens, including the holotype, are held at [Abdou Moumouni University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdou_Moumouni_University) in Niger.[^cite_note-Sereno2026-1]

MNBH IGU11, an anterior dorsal [centrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#centrum), was initially identified as a mid-cervical centrum and referred to the coeval [carcharodontosaurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcharodontosaurid) *[Carcharodontosaurus iguidensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcharodontosaurus_iguidensis)*[^cite_note-B\&S2007-2] due to its similarities to cervical vertebrae erroneously referred to *C. saharicus*, which actually come from a spinosaurid. This dorsal centrum was later thought to belong to *[Sigilmassasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigilmassasaurus)*[^cite_note-Evers2015-3][^cite_note-C\&C2016-4] or an indeterminate species of *Spinosaurus*[^cite_note-Sereno2022-5] until the description of *S. mirabilis* referred it to the species.[^cite_note-Sereno2026-1] In 2022, Sereno and colleagues identified MNBH JEN1 and MNBH JEN2 (then under the specimen numbers MNBH EGA1 and MNBH EGA2, respectively) as belonging to an indeterminate species of *Spinosaurus*.[^cite_note-Sereno2022-5]

In 2026, Sereno and colleagues [described](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_description) *Spinosaurus mirabilis* as a new species of the genus *Spinosaurus* based on these fossil remains. The [specific name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_name_\(zoology\)), *mirabilis*, is a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word meaning 'astonishing', alluding to the remarkably enlarged nasal-prefrontal crest that characterizes the species.[^cite_note-Sereno2026-1] Sereno and colleagues gave the species the nickname "hell heron".[^cite_note-6]

## Description



MNBH IGU11, a dorsal vertebral centrum referred to *S. mirabilis*

As expected, the skeletal anatomy of *Spinosaurus mirabilis* is similar to *S. aegyptiacus*, the [type species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_species) of the genus. It is only known from immature specimens, so the full size of this species when mature is uncertain. The holotype specimen, which belongs to a subadult individual, was estimated to have a full body length of around 8 metres (26 ft). This is about 15% smaller than FSAC-KK-11888, the proposed [neotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neotype) of *S. aegyptiacus*, and about 61% the size of MSMN V4047 (a partial snout), one of the largest *S. aegyptiacus* specimens.[^cite_note-Sereno2026-1]

A [diastema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diastema) (gap between the teeth) is present in the upper jaw to accommodate three of the large lower jaw teeth. The end of the snout in *S. mirabilis* differs from *S. aegyptiacus* in its slightly more arched [premaxilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premaxilla) that rises above the rest of the snout, and subquadrate-shaped expansion of the anterior dentary, compared to the rounder morphology in *S. aegyptiacus*. Specimen MNBH JEN4, which preserves a dentary and tibia, suggests the hindlimbs of *S. mirabilis* may be slightly proportionally longer than *S. aegyptiacus*. The morphology of the preserved dorsal vertebrae implies the presence of a trunk sail formed by greatly elongated [neural spines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#neural_spines), although the shape of this sail is unknown. [Autapomorphies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autapomorphies) (unique derived traits) identified in *S. mirabilis* include the proportionately low snout compared to other spinosaurids, with the [dorsal and ventral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomical_terms_of_location#Dorsal_and_ventral) margins parallel when seen from the side. The spacing of the rear maxillary teeth is also greater than in *S. aegyptiacus*.[^cite_note-Sereno2026-1]

One of the most unique traits of *S. mirabilis* is its distinctive pointed crest with a superficial [scimitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scimitar)-shape, formed by the [nasal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#nasal) and [prefrontal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_dinosaur_anatomy#prefrontal) bones. This crest curves posterodorsally (upward and toward the rear). A network of vessels, in addition to longitudinal and crisscrossing striations and grooves, covers this crest, comparable to the condition seen in modern birds with bony crests, such as [cassowaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassowaries), the [helmeted guinea fowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmeted_guinea_fowl), and the [maleo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maleo). In these animals, the bone is encased in a [keratinous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keratinous) sheath that significantly expands its size. In addition to the prominent dorsal (back) and caudal (tail) sails, the cranial crest likely functioned primarily for visual communication and display.[^cite_note-Sereno2026-1]

## Paleoecology

The remains of *S. mirabilis* were unearthed from the Farak Formation, characterized by [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) and sandy [shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale), representing a Cenomanian-aged fluvial system.[^cite_note-ff-9] *S. mirabilis* coexisted with other dinosaurs and crocodylomorphs in the Farak Formation including the carcharodontosaurid theropod *[Carcharodontosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcharodontosaurus)*,[^cite_note-Sereno2026-1] indeterminate theropod remains previously assigned to *[Bahariasaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahariasaurus)*,[^cite_note-10] the [sauropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropoda) *[Rebbachisaurus tamesnensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebbachisaurus_tamesnensis)* and *[Aegyptosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegyptosaurus)*, and the possible [itasuchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itasuchidae) [crocodylomorph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodylomorpha) *[Fortignathus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortignathus)*.[^cite_note-\:0-11]
