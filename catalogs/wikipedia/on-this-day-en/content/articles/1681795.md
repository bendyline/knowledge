In [journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalism), **local news** covers events that would not be of interest to another locality, or otherwise be of national or international scope. It, in contrast to national or international news, caters to the interests of the local community and focuses on local events and issues.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Some key features of local newsrooms include regional politics, weather, business, and human interest stories.[^cite_note-\:1-2]

Local news readership has been declining in recent years, according to a recent study.[^cite_note-\:2-3] As more and more television consumers tap into streamed programming, local news viewership is declining.[^cite_note-4] Nikki Usher, an associate professor at the College of Media at the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign), argued in "The Complicated Future of Local News" that "critical and comprehensive local news is a recent invention, not a core element of the history of American democracy."[^cite_note-5]

Conversely, citing [Alexis de Tocqueville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_de_Tocqueville) in the second volume of *[Democracy in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_America)* (1840),[^cite_note-6] political scientist [Robert D. Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_D._Putnam) noted in the first edition of *[Bowling Alone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Alone)* (2000) that differences between the [Greatest Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Generation) and the [Silent Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Generation) in the United States versus [Baby boomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_boomers) and [Generation X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_X) in local civic association membership rates and various forms of civic and political engagement was significantly associated with generational differences in news consumption broadly and of newspaper subscriptions especially,[^cite_note-7] such that Americans born before 1946 reported in a [Yankelovich Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Yankelovich) survey at the end of the 20th century that they derived [belongingness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belongingness) from local newspapers at more than twice the rate of Americans born after 1964.[^cite_note-8]

## Television

Opt-outs of local [television news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_news) are frequent before, during, or after national evening news [television programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_program). Television networks can also commission or make provisions for local stations to produce longer standalone local news programs. Some local television markets/viewing areas within a country may even have a dedicated 24-hour local news channel. Local news stations have also started covering less and less local politics in favor of stories that they believe will garner more clicks or attention.[^cite_note-\:0-1] A study has also shown that there has been less investigative journalism within local news stations in recent years.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

### Differences

Local news largely covers the following:

- local sports

- local crime and justice

- local weather

- local business and economy

- local events

- local education

- local politics

- local traffic updates (especially during morning and evening newscasts)

National and international news, however, tend to cover a wider range of content, including news concerning specialized institutions of wide-ranging international power or influence, such as:

- (inter)national political and intergovernmental events

- stock markets

- (inter)national sports competitions

- regional environmental events

- media/entertainment events

- science/technology events

- air traffic/aviator transport events

- defense/security events

### Practices in each country

In the [United States](3434750.md), local news is provided on local [commercial broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_broadcasting) channels (some of which are [television network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_network) [affiliates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_affiliate)). They can either be standalone newscasts that run for at least a half-hour or short segments that air attached to national morning newscasts approximately 25 and 55 minutes past the hour. As not all stations are owned and operated by a television network, the graphics, branding, and studio designs of a station's newscasts often differ from the network they are affiliated with although in recent years, affiliates have made some form of on-air reference to their corresponding networks in the branding of their newscasts. In addition, the local news departments of stations also superimpose their on-screen digital clocks, thermometers, and (occasionally) local news tickers on graphics provided by networks during morning network newscasts. Some cable channels are dedicated to local news coverage. Examples of this include [NY1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NY1) in New York and [WJLA 24/7 News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WJLA_24/7_News) (formerly NewsChannel 8) in the Washington, D.C. market.

In Canada, the commercial broadcasters such as [CTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTV_television_network) have regional morning newscasts. There is also a bloc in the late afternoon dedicated to regional news.

In the [United Kingdom](31717.md), most local news is provided on a local network station with similar branding and studio design to that of the national network news. Examples of this include the nationally networked [BBC News](1139893.md) and its regional news services such as [BBC North West Tonight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_North_West_Tonight) (on [BBC North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_North_West)) and [BBC Newsline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Newsline) (on [BBC Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Northern_Ireland)); the nationally networked [ITV News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_News) and its regional stations including [ITV Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Granada) and [UTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTV_\(TV_channel\)). The long version of BBC and ITV's local news shows often air during the 18\:00 hour on weekday evenings. STV, which simulcasts most of the ITV network's programs in Scotland, is not owned and operated by ITV but has its own branded newscast that broadcasts Scottish-centred news at the same time as ITV's regional news services.

In Australia, local news for regional areas outside of major metropolitan cities is usually produced by local affiliate stations of the major television networks. Local affiliates of television networks [Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Network) and [Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Network) produce a total of twenty-six news bulletins across Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria, Tasmania, the Australian Capital Territory, and Western Australia. Each bulletin is half an hour long, and stories for the bulletin are usually produced from a local newsroom in a region, before being sent to a central broadcast studio for transmission, with one bulletin usually airing live. Seven uses studios in Maroochydore, Surfers Paradise, Canberra, and Bunbury for bulletins in Queensland, the Gold Coast, New South Wales/Victoria, and Western Australia respectively, whereas Nine's local affiliate [WIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WIN_Television) uses two broadcast centers in Wollongong and Maroochydore for its news bulletins. Each market also receives either a shortened edition of the nearest metropolitan bulletin or an in-house produced national news bulletin.

In France, most local news is aired on France Television's [France 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_3). Additionally BFM TV also has a local news channel for the Ile-de-France region called BFM Paris.

In Germany, each regional public broadcaster shows a half-hour's worth of regional news at 19\:30 Central European Time on channel 3.

Norway's public broadcaster, [NRK1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRK1), airs a local news program called NRK Distriktsnyheter (name of the viewing area) every weekday evening at 18\:45 Central European Time, just before the main national newscast Dagsrevyen. A replay of all local newscasts across the country is shown on NRK2 the following morning (Tuesday to Saturday).

Finland's public broadcaster (YLE) airs an eight-minute-long regional news bulletin at 18\:21 Eastern European Time every weeknight on YLE1. The bulletins from all regions are replayed across the country the following day starting from 10\:50 to 12\:10 Eastern European Time on YLE1.

In Sweden, SVT's regional news is simply called SVT Nyheter (name of the viewing area) which shares the same branding as the network's main newscast, Rapport. Unlike SVT's counterparts in the US and UK, during morning programs, the local news opt-out airs in the middle of each half-hour. Since June 2017, the morning regional cut-ins no longer feature an in-studio presenter but instead show compiled short reports from various correspondents across the region. When SVT World was still airing, the simulcast of the morning program featured a different regional opt-out during each half-hour. A 13-minute standalone local newscast airs at 18\:30 Central European Time each weekday evening after Sportnytt (SVT's sports news) on SVT1. Additionally, a short local news segment is incorporated into SVT's other evening newscast Aktuellt which airs on SVT2. In that case, this local news segment shares the same branding and graphics as Aktuellt. There are no local SVT newscasts on Saturdays.

## Newspapers

A lot of newspapers specialize in covering the cities they are based in. Although paper copies of local newspapers are usually sold and circulated exclusively in the local areas they operate (with entities such as libraries or relatives wanting a copy of the paper where a family member is mentioned being mailed copies of the outside of circulation areas), companies may make digital copies of their newspapers available to interested readers directly on their website or through services such as PressReader, often with a paid subscription.

In the United States, although newspapers such as *[The New York Times](30680.md)* and *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* have a 'national' focus on their front pages, they still have dedicated sections for news in the areas they are based in. Weather sections also highlight conditions in the local area and the sports sections feature local teams alongside national sports stories. Their local editions also feature local classified ads.

## Local news in the digital age

In the age of digital media, local news readership has started to fall.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:2-3] This can be attributed to the younger generation's disinterest in traditional news sources and the inability of news stations to fully integrate their business models and practices into the age of digital journalism.[^cite_note-\:1-2][^cite_note-\:2-3] While national and international news industries began using [Twitter](9988187.md) as a way to break news and interact with their audiences, local news media have fallen behind, trending towards using Twitter as a secondary source for spreading information rather than a primary tool for audience engagement.[^cite_note-\:1-2]

Since Internet sites reach a larger audience, more local news agencies have started their websites to compete in the digital age.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Websites are a great way for local news stations to produce more interactive content, which engages the audiences and increases readership.[^cite_note-\:1-2]

Aside from the Internet, Twitter specifically has become a great way to engage the younger generation in news, gain more readership, and spread information.[^cite_note-\:1-2] National news sources have started using Twitter to quickly notify the public of breaking news and to interact with their readers, but local news has failed to integrate Twitter and other forms of social media into their journalism practices successfully.[^cite_note-\:1-2] While local TV news stations have been a bit more effective with using social media, local newspapers have overall fallen behind.[^cite_note-\:1-2] By engaging the audience and spreading important information, social media has been considered a solid method for strengthening the core standards of journalism.[^cite_note-\:1-2] As a result, social media like Twitter has become a vital tool for news agencies to incorporate into their everyday practices.[^cite_note-\:1-2]

As a result of the transition to digital content, local news agencies have had to change their business models. Although they previously gained revenue from subscriptions, more agencies have started making money from online advertising, but this only accounts for a small portion of revenue.[^cite_note-\:2-3] This loss in revenue has been linked to a decline in local journalistic integrity because, with less profit, the need to make money through clickbait articles has become a necessity.[^cite_note-\:2-3]

staff reduction in full-time statehouse reporters from 2003 to 2014 at 220 newspapers surveyed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPew20144,_13–14-17] A subsequent report issued by the [Government Accountability Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office) (GAO) in 2023 found that increases in staffing at digital-native news websites from 2008 to 2020 were not offsetting cuts in [newsroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsroom) staffing among newspapers (which numbered in the tens of thousands of jobs),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGAO202310–11-18] while a 2023 [Congressional Research Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Research_Service) (CRS) report found that newspapers and television still employed the majority of payrolled newsroom staff in the United States in 2022 while online-only news websites employed less than 10%.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS20238–9-19]

The 2023 GAO and CRS reports noted further that the reduction in [subscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscription_business_model) and [advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising) revenue for the newspaper industry from 2000 to 2020 that constituted the overwhelming majority of its [inflation-adjusted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_and_nominal_value) total revenue was not being offset by [digital circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_newspaper) or [online advertising](1555671.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGAO20235–9-20][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS20233–4-21] Also, the 2011 FCC report noted that even though the share of total U.S. advertising spending received by newspapers had been falling since the 1950s, the share received by television and cable did not exceed the share received by newspapers until the 1990s,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC201175-22] and that [paid circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Print_circulation) by newspapers did not begin to consistently decline until the 1980s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC201135-23] However, despite the shift in advertising revenue to television and cable, the 2011 FCC report noted that local television stations were also seeing declining newsroom staffing alongside newspapers, with some stations outsourcing, reducing, or ending their local news programming and more frequently in smaller [media markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_market).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC201134,_87–91,_96–102-24] The 2011 FCC report also noted that the agency's must-carry rules did not favor television stations that provided local news programming.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC2011298–299-25] The 2023 GAO and CRS reports both noted [survey research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survey_\(human_research\)) showing an increasing preference by the American public to receive news through the internet rather than through television, radio, or in print and a declining [willingness to pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willingness_to_pay) subscriptions for news,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGAO202312–15-26][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS20231–3,_4–6-27] while the 2011 FCC report referenced survey research that found declining news consumption across technological mediums and a preference for entertainment programming on television rather than news.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC2011226–227-28]

Additionally, the 2011 FCC report and the 2023 GAO report both noted the [unbundling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbundling) of [soft news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_media) and [hard news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News) content on the internet that formerly provided [cross subsidization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_bundling#Advantages_and_disadvantages) for newspaper beats,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC2011123–125-29][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGAO202312-30] and along with the 2023 CRS report, both noted the unbundling of news content from advertising due to the rise of [classified advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classified_advertising) websites and [search engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine_marketing) and [social media marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media_marketing) that offer greater [targeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeted_advertising) than news websites.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC2011126–132-31][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGAO202317–19-32][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS20236–8-33] In light of such changes in [consumer preferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_behaviour) and market dynamics, the 2023 GAO report suggested along with the 2011 FCC report that local public interest journalism is at risk of [market failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_failure) due to having the [non-excludable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excludability) and [non-rivalrous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivalry_\(economics\)) features of a [public good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_good) that generates [positive externalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externality) and is vulnerable to [free riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-rider_problem).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGAO202341–44-34][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC2011125–126-35] The 2023 GAO report also noted that the FCC has no regulations or guidelines for broadcasters that define what constitutes public interest programming and allows broadcasters wide discretion in determining how to fulfill their [public interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_interest) obligations under the [Communications Act of 1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_Act_of_1934).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGAO202371–75-36] Like the [Federal Radio Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Radio_Commission) under the [Radio Act of 1927](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Act_of_1927), the FCC promulgated various public interest programming guidelines and regulations (such as the [fairness doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairness_doctrine)) and a process for ascertaining local programming of interest to the [communities that the stations were licensed to serve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_license) through the 1970s,[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC2011280–283-38][^cite_note-Conversation-39] but these were rescinded or relaxed under [FCC Chairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_chairs_of_the_Federal_Communications_Commission) [Charles Ferris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_D._Ferris) and [Mark Fowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_S._Fowler) in the 1980s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC2011277–278,_283–285-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-Conversation-39]

In 2021, the FCC's Office of Economics and Analytics released a [working paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_paper) that found that the market size of [Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research) designated market areas (DMA) as measured by the total number of television households was the key factor that effected the number of commercial television stations with local news operations in the DMA and that increased market size led to [diminishing returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminishing_returns).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC20214-42] The 38 to 51 largest DMAs, which had at least 615,000 to 800,000 television households and comprised 61% to 69% of U.S. television households in total, could sustain four or more stations while a DMA needed at least 35,000 to 70,000 television households to be able to sustain two stations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC20214,_20–21-43] However, the working paper also found that in some markets that there was a [trade-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade-off) between local news programming and the total number of stations due to high [fixed costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_cost) for production and low [marginal costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_cost) for distribution,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC20211–4,_21-44] and that despite rising total revenue from 2010 to 2018 due to rising [retransmission consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retransmission_consent) fees, the number of local television stations that originated news fell by 5% while inflation-adjusted advertising revenue fell nationally.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFCC20215–6-45] In the same year, the Pew Research Center released a survey that found that the percentage of American adults that reported [having a cable or satellite television subscription fell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cord-cutting) from 76% in 2015 to 56% in 2021,[^cite_note-Pew_2021-46] while a 2025 Pew survey found that only 36% of American adults reported having a cable or satellite television subscription.[^cite_note-Pew_2025-47]

Also, along with the 2023 GAO and CRS reports,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGAO202351–56-48][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS20239–13-49] a 2022 [United States Copyright Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Copyright_Office) (USCO) report noted that while the news industry and [Big Tech](57174306.md) companies do not dispute that [news aggregators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_aggregator) and the [web feeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_feed) of [social media](5897742.md) websites drive significant [traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_traffic) to news sites, the news industry disputes the extent of the traffic and its [economic value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_\(economics\)) since aggregators and feeds direct [users](16301990.md) to articles rather than [home pages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_page) and because of a [substitution effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_good) from aggregators and feeds featuring [headlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headline) and portions of [article lead paragraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_style#Lead).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUSCO20227–16-50] The 2023 CRS report noted that the Big Tech companies that receive the majority of online advertising revenue and compete with the news industry for such revenue are also the largest providers of advertising technology to the news industry,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS20236–8,_14–20-51] and that the Big Tech companies had been [alleged to have engaged in anti-competitive conduct](57174306.md#Antitrust_efforts) in the provision of advertising technology, news aggregators, [search engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine), [social networking services](2041117.md), and [app stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/App_store).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS202320–27-52] The CRS noted further that almost two-thirds of total U.S. advertising spending by 2020 was online and that more than half of online advertising spending was received by [Google](1092923.md) and [Facebook](7529378.md) alone, while inflation-adjusted total U.S. advertising spending fell overall during the preceding two decades.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS20236–8-33]

Ultimately, the 2022 USCO report found that the effectiveness of copyright protections for the news industry were undermined by an [inequality of bargaining power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inequality_of_bargaining_power) between the news industry and the Big Tech companies in concluding that the online advertising revenue dispute between the industries was more of an issue for [competition policy](92025.md) rather than copyright law.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUSCO20223–4,_48–54,_58–59-53][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS202313–14-54] The 2021 Pew survey also noted that previous industry research released in 2020 found that 55% of American households subscribed to multiple [streaming services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media),[^cite_note-Pew_2021-46][^cite_note-55] while the 2025 Pew survey found that 83% of American adults watched streaming services and that 55% watched streaming services and did not subscribe to cable or satellite television.[^cite_note-Pew_2025-47] While [streaming television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_television) services often include local broadcast television channels,[^cite_note-56] reports issued by the CRS in 2016 and 2020 noted that the FCC had not expanded its definition of [multichannel video programming distributors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multichannel_television_in_the_United_States) to include [online video distributors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-top_media_service), and that the regulatory framework for broadcast, cable, and satellite television does not generally apply to streaming television (including must-carry requirements).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS202012–14-58] The 2020 CRS report noted further that [vertical integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_integration) was a common practice in the streaming television industry,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECRS20203–12-59] and while subsequent [market analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_analysis) using the [Herfindahl–Hirschman index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herfindahl%E2%80%93Hirschman_index) suggested a trend towards greater [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_\(economics\)), the analysis still found [market concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_concentration) to the degree of [oligopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligopoly).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

Relatedly, the Pew Research Center issued a report in June 2023 that analyzed the content of 451 [podcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast) listed on the [daily top 200 most popular charts](1795886.md) of [Apple Podcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Podcasts) and [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify) from April through September 2022.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPew20235–9-62] The report found that only 15% of the podcasts surveyed had a news or current events focus, and that 46% of the news-focused podcasts were affiliated with a traditional news organization.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPew202322–23-63] Additionally, 51% of the news-focused podcasts covered [sports](558219.md) and topics other than [politics and government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_journalism),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPew202322-64] and while the episodes of the news-focused podcasts tended to be longer and released more frequently,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPew202325-65] 78% of the news-focused podcasts followed a [commentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_journalism), [interview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_\(journalism\)), news summary, or recap format rather than an in-depth [reporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Report) format.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPew202324-66] Also, while only 8% of the 451 podcasts surveyed were available [exclusively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_right) through one site (since 90% were also available through [Google Podcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Podcasts), 82% through [Stitcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stitcher), and virtually all of the 51% with a [video component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia) through [YouTube](3524766.md)),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPew202312–13-67] only 31% were [operated independently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barriers_to_entry) while 69% were affiliated with a larger organization, and 47% sought financial support from listeners by paid subscriptions, merchandise sales, or donations with 60% of independently-operated podcasts doing so in comparison to 41% of organization-affiliated podcasts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPew202314–16-68]

### Influence of Facebook on news stories

In 2018, Tech Crunch journalist Josh Constine reported that [Facebook](7529378.md) "stole the news business" by using its sponsorship of new outlets to make many news publishers its "ghostwriters."[^cite_note-constine-69] Constine further noted that Facebook has been targeting local news sources for many years.[^cite_note-constine-69] In January 2019, Facebook founder [Mark Zuckerberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Zuckerberg) announced that he would spend \$300 million investing in local news over three years.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]
