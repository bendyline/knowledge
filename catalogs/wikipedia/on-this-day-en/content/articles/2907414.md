Municipal presidents' government headquarters are known as municipal palaces (*palacios municipales*)

A **municipal president** ([Spanish](26825.md): *presidente municipal*) is the chief of government of [municipalities in Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Mexico). This title was also used in the Philippines during the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_the_Philippines) and [American colonial periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\));[^cite_note-1] it is comparable to a mayor of the town or city. The position is comparable to the [county executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_executive) of a [county in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(United_States\)) or to the [mayor](101146.md) of a city in the United States, although the jurisdiction of a *presidente municipal* includes not only a city but the municipality surrounding it. Nationally, this position is also equivalent to that of [Head of Government of the Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_Government_of_the_Federal_District) and that is why these positions are sometimes referred to as "mayors" in English-language publications.
