The **Starmer ministry** began on 5 July 2024 when [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) was invited by King [Charles III](125248.md) to form a [government](12229.md), following the resignation of [Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak) after the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election). The Starmer ministry concluded on 20 July 2026 when [Andy Burnham](414818.md) became Prime Minister.

## Background

Starmer formed his government throughout 5–7 July, after his party won 411 seats in the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election), with the new [Cabinet](162790.md) meeting for the first time on 6 July, and the new Parliament being called to meet on 9 July.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] It has been noted for its female political representation, appointing women to a record half of the Cabinet (including [Rachel Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Reeves) as the first female [Chancellor of the Exchequer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer) in British history) and three of the five top positions in the British government, including [Angela Rayner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Rayner) as [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom) and [Secretary of State for Housing, Communities and Local Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Housing,_Communities_and_Local_Government).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Starmer also appointed experts: scientist [Patrick Vallance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Vallance) as [Minister of State for Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_for_Science), rehabilitation campaigner [James Timpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Timpson) as [Minister of State for Prisons, Parole and Probation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_for_Prisons,_Parole_and_Probation), and international law expert [Richard Hermer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hermer) as [Attorney General for England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_for_England_and_Wales).[^cite_note-6] The government includes a few ministers from the [New Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Labour) governments of [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) and [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown), including [Hilary Benn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilary_Benn), [Yvette Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvette_Cooper), [David Lammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lammy), [Ed Miliband](1290018.md) and [Douglas Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Alexander) in the Cabinet, and [Jacqui Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqui_Smith), [David Hanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hanson,_Baron_Hanson_of_Flint) and [Stephen Timms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Timms) as junior ministers.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

#### Changes

*Changes from Starmer's [final Shadow Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Cabinet_of_Keir_Starmer#September_2023_%E2%80%93_July_2024) to Cabinet.*[^cite_note-LabourList_28Jul24-11]

- [Thangam Debbonaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thangam_Debbonaire) ([Shadow Culture Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Culture_Secretary)) lost her seat and [Lisa Nandy](27266258.md) was appointed to Cabinet in her place.

- Nandy's previous role ([Shadow International Development Cabinet Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Cabinet_Minister_for_International_Development)) was a full member of Shadow Cabinet despite the department having merged into the Foreign Office under the previous government (not reformed by Starmer). [Anneliese Dodds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anneliese_Dodds) was appointed to attend Cabinet as [Minister of State (Development)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_for_Development).
