The **Parliament of Uganda** is the [unicameral](204304.md) legislature of the [Republic of Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Uganda). One of its primary functions is to pass laws that support effective governance in the country. Government ministers are required to answer to the people's representatives on the floor of the house and may be appointed from amongst its members.

Through the various parliamentary committees, parliament scrutinises government programmes, particularly as outlined in the *State of the Nation* address[^cite_note-3] by the president. Fiscal matters, such as taxation and loans, require parliamentary approval after appropriate debate.[^cite_note-4] Parliament also holds the authority to confirm certain presidential nominations and, through a [motion of censure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_of_censure), may compel a Minister to resign.

## Composition

See also: [List of constituencies in Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_constituencies_in_Uganda)

The 11th Parliament (2021–2026) has a total of 557 seats, including 353 representatives elected using [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md) in single winner constituencies. Using the same method, 146 seats reserved for women are filled, with one seat per [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Uganda). Finally, 30 seats are indirectly filled via special electoral colleges: 10 by the army, 5 by youths, 5 by elders, 5 by unions, 5 by people with disabilities and 28 [Ex Officio Members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_officio_member). In each of these groups, at least one woman must be elected (at least two for the army group).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

In 2016, it was composed of 288 constituency representatives, 121 district woman representatives, ten [Uganda People's Defence Force](56928.md) representatives, five representatives of the youth, five representatives of persons with disabilities, five representatives of workers, and seventeen *ex officio* members.[^cite_note-9]

The Ugandan parliament was established in 1962, soon after the country's independence.[^cite_note-10]

### First Parliament of Uganda (1956–1962)

Uganda Legislative Council (LEGCO) is considered to be the first Parliament of Uganda.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

[The Legislative Council (LEGCO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Legislative_Council) of Uganda was created in 1920, replacing governance by the Commissioner’s decrees. At its inception, the Council was composed entirely of European members under the leadership of Governor [Sir Robert Coryndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Coryndon). An Asian representative was first admitted in 1926, and on 4 December 1945, the first African members, Michael Ernest Kawalya Kaggwa (the Katikkiro of Buganda), Petero Nyangabyaki (the Katikkiro of Bunyoro), and Yekonia Zirabamuzaale, were sworn in to the LEGCO to represent Buganda, Western and Eastern Provinces.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

In the 1950s, the structure and procedures of LEGCO underwent notable reforms. A ministerial system was introduced in 1956, and Sir [John Bowes Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bowes_Griffin) was appointed as the first Speaker in January 1958 for LEGCO.[^cite_note-\:0-11] Uganda’s first direct general elections, held in March 1961, resulted in an African majority within the Council. LEGCO remained in place until 1 March 1962, when self government was established and it was replaced by the National Assembly.[^cite_note-\:0-11]



The tower that separates the east from the north wing buildings at the Uganda Parliament. this tower together with the parliament was built by the British colonial government in the late 1950s as a gift to Uganda just before it attained its independence.

### Second Parliament (1962–1979)

During this period, Prime Minister [Milton Obote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Obote) abrogated the constitution and declared himself [President of Uganda](450146.md) in 1966. This parliament also witnessed the abolition of Uganda's traditional kingdoms and the declaration of Uganda as a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic). This decision was impacted by the legal counsel of Member of Parliament [A. G. Mehta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._G._Mehta) following the [Ugandan Constitutional Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_Constitutional_Conference) in which Uganda became an independent nation.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The speaker during the Second Parliament was [Narendra M. Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_M._Patel), a Ugandan of [Indian](14533.md) descent. This parliament ended when [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin) overthrew Milton Obote's government in January 1971.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

### Third Parliament (1979–1980)

Following the overthrow of Idi Amin in April 1979, a new legislative body known as the National Consultative Council (NRC) was established and it served as the interim parliament. With an initial membership of 30that was elected in [Moshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshi,_Tanzania), Tanzania, the membership was later increased to 120.[^cite_note-\:0-11] This was the Third Parliament and was chaired by [Edward Rugumayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Rugumayo). This legislative body continued to function until the general elections of December 1980.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

### Fourth Parliament (1980–1985)

This period marked the return to power of [Milton Obote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Obote) and the [Uganda People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_People's_Congress) (UPC), following the disputed national elections of 1980. The speaker of the Fourth Parliament was [Francis Butagira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Butagira), a [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_School)-trained lawyer. the Fourth Parliament ended when General [Basilio Olara Okello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilio_Olara_Okello) overthrew Obote and the UPC government in 1985.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

### Fifth Parliament (1986–1996)

Known as the National Resistance Council (NRC), the Fifth Parliament was established following the end of the [Ugandan Bush War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_Bush_War) (1980–1986). Starting with 38 historical members of the [National Resistance Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Resistance_Movement) and [National Resistance Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Resistance_Army), the legislative body was gradually expanded to include representatives from around the country. The speaker during the Fifth Parliament was [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md), who also concurrently served as the President of Uganda.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

### Sixth Parliament (1996–2001)

The Sixth Parliament was constituted during one-party rule (NRM). [James Wapakhabulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wapakhabulo) served as speaker from 1996 until 1998. From 1998 until 2001, [Francis Ayume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ayume), a member of Parliament from [Koboko District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koboko_District), served as speaker.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

### Seventh Parliament (2001–2006)

The Seventh Parliament was presided over as Speaker by [Edward Ssekandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Ssekandi). The most controversial legislation passed during this period was the amendment of the constitution to remove presidential term limits.[^cite_note-\:0-11]

#### 2017 Parliament fight

On September 27, 2017, a fight ensued during a legislative session of the Ugandan parliament. The legislation in discussion at the time was to remove the presidential age limit of 75 from the Ugandan constitution. Following accusations from the parliamentary speaker against certain lawmakers in the chamber of disorderly conduct, a full-fledged fight broke out in which chairs were thrown, microphone stands used as clubs, and eventual removal of some members by plain clothes security officers.[^cite_note-14]

### Eleventh Parliament (2021–2026)

### Twelfth Parliament (2026–present)

Main article: [List of members of the twelfth Parliament of Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_twelfth_Parliament_of_Uganda)

At the beginning of the 12th Parliament in May 2026, [Jacob Marksons Oboth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Oboth-Oboth) was elected Speaker of Parliament, with [Thomas Tayebwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tayebwa) serving as Deputy Speaker.[^cite_note-\:1-17] Oboth was elected Speaker on 25 May 2026 at the Kololo Ceremonial Grounds, receiving 441 votes against [Paul Mwiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Mwiru), who received 60 votes, and [Norbert Mao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Mao), who received 15 votes.[^cite_note-\:2-18] On the same day, Tayebwa was elected Deputy Speaker with 457 votes, defeating [Asinansi Nyakato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asinansi_Nyakato), who received 45 votes, and [Sarah Aguti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Aguti_Nyangkori), who received 14 votes. Tayebwa had previously served as Deputy Speaker during the 11th Parliament.[^cite_note-\:1-17][^cite_note-\:2-18]

Main article: [List of members of the twelfth Parliament of Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_twelfth_Parliament_of_Uganda)

Many prominent MPs were [unseated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unseating) including [Mathias Mpuuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathias_Mpuuga), [Ibrahim Ssemujju Nganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Ssemujju_Nganda), [Abed Bwanika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abed_Bwanika) and [Asuman Basalirwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asuman_Basalirwa).[^cite_note-19]
