# Flat coast



Schematic diagram of a flat coast.

At a **flat coast** or **flat shoreline**, the land descends gradually into the [sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea). Flat coasts can be formed either as a result of the sea advancing into gently sloping terrain or through the [abrasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrasion_\(geology\)) of loose rock. They may be basically divided into two parallel strips: the shoreface and the [beach](40630.md).

Flat coasts consist of loose material such as [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand) and [gravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel). [Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind) transports finer grains of sand inland over the [dunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune). The sea washes [pebbles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pebble) and sand away from the [coast](5236.md) and dumps it at other locations.

## Flat coast littoral series



Diagram of a flat coast littoral series. Key: B: bar, TNW: average low tide, THW: average high tide, US: shoreface; GS: [foreshore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreshore), SW: [berm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berm), SR: [runnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream), HS: [backshore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backshore), DG: [dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune) belt, SK: dune cliff

The typical sequence of landforms created by the sea is described as a "littoral series".

### Sandbars, runnels and creeks



[Wadden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadden) creek with exposed sandbar during an ebb tide

The littoral series of a flat coast starts in the permanently flooded shallow water region, or shoreface, with a sand or gravel [reef](240844.md) (also called a [bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbar)). The longshore bar is an elongated ridge of sand found parallel to the shore in the [surf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_shoaling) zone on many flat coasts. It consists mainly of sand or gravel, depending on the material available along the coast. The sides of the sandbar fall gently away. The basin between a sandbar and the shore zone is called the [runnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream) or [swale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swale_\(landform\)). The presence of a bar clearly indicates that the movement of waves is transporting and depositing material on the seabed. There may be several bars whose longitudinal axes all run parallel to the beach and which are separated by equally parallel runnels or [creeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream). The drainage troughs in areas of [tidal flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_flats) also run parallel to the coast.&#x20;

### Shoreface and beach



Start of the dune belt by the sand cliff

The shoreface (or underwater platform) on flat coasts encompasses in its narrow sense that area which is subject to the constant action of moving water. This means that the landward boundary between shoreface and beach is the line of the average low-water mark. However this definition is not universal and frequently varies from author to author in the literature. Whilst some define the beach as the landward transition to the shoreface that extends from the low-water mark to the highest high-water mark, i.e. the zone that is only periodically or episodically (after a [storm surge](449744.md)) flooded by water; other authors do not use the term "beach" for the landward element of a flat coast at all. They describe the region between the mean low-water mark and the mean high-water mark of the [tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide) as the [intertidal zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertidal_zone) or [foreshore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreshore) and that area above the average high-water mark as the [supratidal zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supratidal_zone) or [backshore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backshore) that is only directly attacked by water during storms. Because the backshore is often considerably flatter in appearance than the foreshore, which slopes clearly down towards the sea, it is also often referred to as a beach platform, which is why this part of the shore can be considered in practice to be the actual beach. The farthest point inland that is reached by storm surges is bounded by a belt of dunes, where floods can form a dune cliff.

### Berm



The berm: where the gravel is no longer washed back into the sea by the backwash

On the beach (the beach platform) there is very often a bank of sand or a gravel ridge parallel to the shoreline and a few tens of centimetres high, known as the [berm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berm). On its landward side there is often a shallow [runnel](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/runnel). The berm is formed by material transported by the breaking waves that is thrown beyond the average level of the sea. The coarse-grained material that can no longer be washed away by the [backwash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swash) remains behind. The location and size of the berm is subject to seasonal changes. For example, a winter berm that has been thrown up by storm surges in winter is usually much more prominent and higher up the beach than berms formed by summer high tides.

A similar landform is a [beach ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_ridge).

## Beach losses and gains



Beach losses after a [hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane)

Beaches are usually heavily [eroded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion_\(geology\)) during [storm surges](449744.md) and the beach profile steepened, whereas normal wave action on flat coasts tends to raise the beach. Not infrequently a whole series of parallel berms is formed, one behind the other. There is a consequent gradual increase in height with the result that, over time, the shoreline advances seawards. A striking example of land-forming system of berms is [Skagen Odde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenen) on the northern tip of Vendsyssel in the extreme north of [Denmark](76972.md). This headland is still growing today as more berms are added.

[Coastal defences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_defences) against erosion are [groynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groyne), [stone walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_walls), or [tetrapods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapod_\(structure\)) of concrete, which act as [breakwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakwater_\(structure\)). The first plants to colonise the dunes include [sea buckthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_buckthorn) or [beach grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_grass) which prevent wind erosion.
