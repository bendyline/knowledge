The **mayor of New York City**, officially **mayor of the City of New York**, is head of the executive branch of the [government of New York City](531637.md) and the chief executive of [New York City](645042.md). The [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayoralty_in_the_United_States)'s office administers all city services, public property, police and fire protection, and most public agencies, and enforces all city and state laws within New York City.

The budget, overseen by [New York City Mayor's Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Mayor's_Office_of_Management_and_Budget), is the largest municipal budget in the [United States](3434750.md), totaling \$126.8 billion in fiscal year 2026.[^cite_note-3] The city employs 325,000 people, spends about \$38 billion[^cite_note-4] to educate more than 1.1 million students (the largest public school system in the United States), and levies \$27 billion in taxes. It receives \$14 billion from the state and federal governments.

The mayor's office is located in [New York City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Hall); it has jurisdiction over all five [boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_\(New_York_City\)) of New York City: [Manhattan](45470.md), [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [the Bronx](3338.md), [Staten Island](127062.md) and [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens). The mayor appoints numerous officials, including deputy mayors and the commissioners who head city agencies and departments. The mayor's regulations are compiled in title 43 of the *[New York City Rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Rules)*. According to current law, the mayor is [limited to two consecutive four-year terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits) in office but may run again after a four-year break. The limit on consecutive terms was changed from two to three on October 23, 2008, when the [New York City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council) voted 29–22 in favor of passing the term limit extension into law,[^cite_note-5] but in 2010, a referendum reverting the limit to two terms passed overwhelmingly.[^cite_note-6]

The New York City mayoralty has become known as the "second toughest job in America".[^cite_note-7] It has been observed that politicians are rarely elected to any higher office after serving as mayor of New York City; the last elected mayor who later achieved higher office was [John T. Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_T._Hoffman), who became [governor of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_York) in 1869. (The last mayor to win a roughly comparable office was [Ardolph Kline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardolph_L._Kline), elected to the [U.S. Congress](19468510.md) for one term in 1920; Kline had become Acting Mayor for part of 1913 without ever having won a city-wide election.) Former mayor [Ed Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Koch) said that the post was jinxed due to divine intervention, whereas [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg), who later [unsuccessfully ran for president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg_2020_presidential_campaign), called the supposed curse "a statistical fluke".[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-NYTNov24-9]

The current mayor is [Zohran Mamdani](64618347.md), who was [elected](73831797.md) on November 4, 2025, and took office shortly after midnight on January 1, 2026.

## History

See also: [List of mayors of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_New_York_City)

In 1665, Governor [Richard Nicolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nicolls) appointed [Thomas Willett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Willett) as the first mayor of New York. For 156 years, the mayor was appointed and had limited power. Between 1783 and 1821 the mayor was appointed by the [Council of Appointment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Appointment) in which the state's governor had the loudest voice. In 1821 the Common Council, which included elected members, gained the authority to choose the mayor. An amendment to the New York State Constitution in 1834 provided for the direct popular election of the mayor. [Cornelius W. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Van_Wyck_Lawrence), a [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Democratic_Party), was elected that year.

[Gracie Mansion](1206268.md) has been the official residence of the mayor since [Fiorello La Guardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorello_La_Guardia)'s administration in 1942. Its main floor is open to the public and serves as a small museum.

The mayor is entitled to a salary of \$258,750 a year.[^cite_note-lwvnyc1-10] [Michael Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg), mayor of the city from 2002 to 2013 and one of the richest people in the world,[^cite_note-Forbes-11] declined the salary and instead was paid [\$1 yearly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-dollar_salary).

In 2000, direct control of the city's public school system was transferred to the mayor's office. Thereafter, in 2003, the reorganization established the [New York City Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Education).

As a position that is elected, appointed, or hired by the city, the mayor of New York must file their oath of office with the city clerk's office and pay a \$9 fee by credit card or money order.[^cite_note-12]



"New York's new solar system": [Tammany Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammany_Hall) revolves around [Boss Croker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Croker) in this 1899 cartoon in *[Puck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puck_\(magazine\))*.

[Tammany Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammany_Hall), which evolved from an organization of craftsmen into a Democratic political machine, was an American political organization founded in 1786 and incorporated on May 12, 1789. It became the main local political machine of the Democratic Party and played a major role in controlling New York City and New York State politics. The organization gained control of Democratic Party nominations in the state and city in 1861, and played a major role in New York City politics into the 1960s and was a dominant player from the mayoral victory of [Fernando Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Wood) in 1854 through the era of [Robert Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Wagner_Jr.) (1954–1965). Its last political leader was an African American man named [J. Raymond Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Raymond_Jones).

## Deputies

Further information: [List of deputy mayors of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_deputy_mayors_of_New_York_City)

The mayor of New York City may appoint several deputy mayors to help oversee major offices within the executive branch of the city government. The powers and duties, and even the number of deputy mayors, are not defined by the City Charter. The majority of agency commissioners and department heads report to one of the deputy mayors, giving the role a great deal of power within a mayoral administration.

## Removal from office

According to the [New York City Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Charter), the [governor of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_York) has the power to remove the mayor from office in response to allegations of misconduct, but the governor must hear the mayor's defense of the allegations before doing so.[^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-\:1-15] The governor can suspend the mayor for 30 days while considering the allegations. In 2024, it was reported that Governor [Kathy Hochul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Hochul) was considering whether to use that process against [Eric Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Adams) after his indictment on federal corruption charges. Prior to 2024, the last New York governor to consider exercising this power was [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), who in 1932 considered suspending Mayor [Jimmy Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Walker), after the latter was accused of taking bribes from city contractors. Walker resigned before Roosevelt could remove him.[^cite_note-\:1-15]

The charter also provides a separate process for the mayor's removal without the involvement of the governor: a five-member "Inability Committee" is established by the charter composed of the city's corporation counsel (head of the [New York City Law Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Law_Department)), the speaker of the New York City Council, a deputy mayor (designated by the mayor, or the longest serving deputy if none is designated), the [New York City Comptroller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Comptroller), and the longest-serving [borough president](531637.md#Borough_presidents); by a four-fifths vote, the committee can refer allegations of misconduct or incapacity to the [New York City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council), which can then by a two-thirds vote either permanently remove the mayor from office or temporarily suspend them.[^cite_note-16] This process has never been used.[^cite_note-17]

## Line of succession

### Order of succession

In the event that the mayor is suspended, removed from office, deceased, or otherwise unable to fulfill their duties, the following people serve as acting mayor[^cite_note-\:2-18]:

1. [Public Advocate of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Public_Advocate)

2. [Comptroller of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Comptroller)

### Limitations on the acting mayor

An acting mayor has all the power of the office of mayor, but cannot fire any [deputy mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_deputy_mayors_of_New_York_City). Unless serving as acting mayor for more than 9 days, they also cannot sign, approve, or disapprove any local law or resolution.[^cite_note-\:2-18]
