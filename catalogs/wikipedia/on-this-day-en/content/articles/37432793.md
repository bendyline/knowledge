**Organized crime in France** is primarily based in major cities in [France](5843419.md) like [Marseille](40888948.md), [Grenoble](50972.md), [Paris](22989.md), and [Lyon](8638634.md). It is often referred to in France as **_grand banditisme_** [^cite_note-books.google.com-1][^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2] or **milieu**.

From the 1900s to the late 1930s, *le milieu* primarily engaged in [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution), [bookmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookmaker_\(gambling\)), [fencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fence_\(criminal\)), and hijacking. The favored [criminal activity in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_France) turned to [bank robbery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_robbery), [drug trafficking](218449.md), and smuggling from 1940 to the late 1970s. The 1980s saw a resurgence of large-scale bank robberies and [heists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_robbery). From 1990 to 2000, criminal organizations established complex [extortion](162449.md) rings in [Marseille](40888948.md) extending to [Aix-en-Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-en-Provence) and the greater [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera). Since 2002, *Le Milieu* is known for, in addition to its extortion rings, large [counterfeiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterfeit) and [white-collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-collar_crime) crime operations. Due to increased financial regulation, *Le Milieu* has collectively pushed to [integrate their crime profits](19390.md) into the legal economy.

[France's geographical location](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_France) makes it an attractive venue for [trafficking](28645.md) (i.e. smuggling) and counterfeiting. The [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) of Marseille is a hub for *Le Milieu* to move large amounts of product into domestic and European markets.[^cite_note-Oved-2016-3] Low economic development continues to be the largest factor in youth joining French criminal organizations.

The most prominent criminal organization within *Le Milieu* is the [Corsican mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsican_mafia) (*milieu corse*). Although the mafia has encompassed many criminal groups from the 1960s to the 1980s, modern (1990s–present) criminal activity is managed by the Marseille-based *[Unione Corse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unione_Corse)* and Northern Corsica–based *[Gang de la Brise de Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_de_la_Brise_de_Mer)* (i.e. "the sea breeze gang"). In 2007, an internal conflict led to the deaths of 102 people on the island of Corsica fracturing the influence of the two larger groups in the island ([Brise de mer gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_de_la_Brise_de_Mer) and Colonna family).[^cite_note-4] Both mobs remain powerful as of 2024 and often control [nightclubs](19321330.md), bars, restaurants, apartments, and hotels in [Aix-en-Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-en-Provence), [Marseille](40888948.md) and the [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera). In 2016, it was estimated that France's organized crime net [US\$](18717338.md)23 billion in its [underground economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market).[^cite_note-Mob-2015-5]

## Terms used

The term **milieu** literally means *middle* or *environment* and figuratively *[society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_society)* or *[environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_environment)*, used as shorthand for **le milieu criminel** (criminal environment) or **le milieu interlope** (illegal environment). The generic term can designate any type it criminal society (e.g.: *le milieu chinois*, short for *le milieu criminel chinois*, the Chinese criminal underworld), when used without additional info or context (*Le milieu*) it is inferred as native historical French criminal society and it refers to the set of criminal figures operating in the French metropolitan areas who are known to the public for being involved in high level organized crime. Members working within the *milieu* are collectively known as the **French mob**. This category does not include criminal organizations that were formed in another country and operate within France (e.g. the Campanian [Camorra](44945.md), [Serbian mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_mafia), [Albanian mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_mafia), Chinese [Triads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triad_\(underground_society\)), or Kurdish terrorist [PKK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKK)).[^cite_note-AsianTrib-2014-6][^cite_note-AgoraVox-2010-7] French gangs considered part of the *milieu* usually have long-standing ties to the country.[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004b-8] An increase in youth-centered gang activity lead to the separation of the *Milieu*.[^cite_note-Oved-2016-3]

- "**Traditional *Milieu***" (1930 to 1989): encompasses the older, foundational criminal organizations of the French underworld

- "**French [Corsican mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsican_mafia)**" or "**Milieu corso-marseillais**" (1990–present): encompasses the newer, modern criminal groups in France and [Corsica](5714828.md)

## Organization



[Étienne Monier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tienne_Monier), a *beau voyou* of the *[Bande à Bonnot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnot_Gang)*

Due to the early historical connection the [Corsican mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsican_mafia) shares with the [Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia) one, the modern structure of most French mobs typically break down into crime families with a strict hierarchy. Usually the goodfellas that carry out "orders" are known as *une équipe multi-qualifiée* (i.e. a multi-skilled team) composed of *les beaux voyous* (i.e. "the good fellows"). Most of these groups of members maintain and protect "mouvances" (i.e. [territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory)).[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2] Members of the French Mob are highly professional as compared to lower-level crime groups in that they usually split their work by specialty (e.g. some members serve as the "brain", while others the "muscle" and/or "specialist").[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2]

- *Caïd* (French: \[kay-id] "the big boss"; "the boss")

- *Parrain* (French: \[pah-rahn] "godfather")

- *Spécialiste* (French: \[speh-sjah-leest] "specialist")

- *Associés* ("associate")

- *Beaux voyous* (\[bow vwah-yo] "goodfella"; "the good fellows")

Externally, and more generally, the organization of the French mob is as follows:[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2][^cite_note-AgoraVox-2010-7][^cite_note-AsianTrib-2014-6]

- *Le Milieu* (French: \[luh mil-yuh]) "the underworld") / The French Mob: a general term for criminal organizations in France

- Mafia-type criminal organizations (e.g. the [Corsican mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsican_mafia)): a criminal network composed of [crime families](5887241.md)

- Gangs (e.g. [Rédoine Faïd gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9doine_Fa%C3%AFd), *[Gang des postiches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_des_postiches)*): a smaller criminal group with a [soft hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy)

- Crime families (e.g. [Carbone crime family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Carbone)): a subset of a mafia-type organization that executes orders and/or missions

## Activities and strategies

According to a 2004 study on [organized crime](22625.md), the activities of criminals within the French underworld typically fall into three broad categories:[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2]

- **Wholly illegal activity**: criminal activities that are blatantly and duly illegal e.g. racketeering, kidnapping, armed robbery, trafficking, assault, etc.

- **Associative illegal activity**: criminal activities associated with their main wholly illegal activities e.g. [bribery](260979.md), corruption of police officials, tax fraud, [grand theft auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_vehicle_theft), etc.

- **Legal activity**: activities used to cover up illegal activities e.g. operating a business for [money laundering](19390.md) purposes, running gambling machines, restaurants, etc.

## History

### Origins: 1910s to 1920s



[Jules Bonnot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Bonnot) (upper right) founded the [Bonnot Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnot_Gang) in 1911.

The history of organized crime in France can be traced to the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) (1789 to 1799). It is however with the turn of the 20th century that local mobsters became highly organized and created [crime families](5887241.md). During the 1910s and the 1920s, [Pigalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartier_Pigalle) and [Rue Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rue_Saint-Denis_\(Paris\)) (in Paris) served as a hub for [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution).[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2] Due to decentralized political control in [Corsica](5714828.md) during the 1900s, many criminal organizations flourished. Members of these gangs moved from the island of Corsica to the southern coasts of France during the early 20th century. From 1920 to 1930, prostitution was the most important criminal activity and was controlled in [Marseille](40888948.md) by the Corsican Godfather, [Paul Carbone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Carbone) and the Italian-French Godfather, [François Spirito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Spirito).[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2] In Paris, the [Pigalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartier_Pigalle) area were fought over by Corsican godfathers such as Jean-Paul Stefani and Ange Saliceti.

### "French Connection": 1930s to 1970s

Main article: [French Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Connection)

During the 1940s and 1950s, many gangs — most notably the [Tractions Avant gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_des_Tractions_Avant) – committed large scale [bank robberies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_robbery). During the 1970s, the most powerful gang in Paris was the Zemmour crime family, a [Jewish](25955086.md)/[pied noir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pied-noir) clan. The Zemmour brothers controlled prostitution in the French capital and were considered to be Godfathers of Paris.[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2] As prostitution decreased, criminal activities turned to more [white-collar crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-collar_crime). In 1930, the two godfathers ([Carbone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Carbone) and [Spirito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Spirito)) spearheaded the [French Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Connection), a global [heroin](14034.md)-smuggling ring reaching its peak in the 1960s and 1970s. Drugs were smuggled from [Turkey](11125639.md) to France and then to the United States through Canada. The French Connection was guided by various crime families into the late 1970s before it was disbanded by French authorities. The "**Milieu of Marseille**" (i.e. "the underworld of Marseille")[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2][^cite_note-Mob-2015-5] was left without political leadership after the French Connection was destroyed.[^cite_note-Fijnaut-2004a-2] The [Unione Corse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unione_Corse) established widespread infrastructure in the 1930s and helped guide the *Milieu* out of the French Connection era.[^cite_note-9]

Raymond Callemin was a prominent member of the [Bonnot Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnot_Gang); he was executed by [guillotine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillotine).

During the 1980s, many "godfathers" (e.g. Tany Zampa, [Jacky Imbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacky_Imbert), and Francis Vanverberghe) fought for control over racketeering and [drug trafficking](218449.md) in the port area of Marseille. It was during this time that the Zemmour brothers, known as "The Godfathers of Paris," were killed. Claude Genova quickly assumed the gap the brothers left and continued running low-level prostitution rings until his 1994 assassination. The *[Gang des postiches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_des_postiches)* robbed nearly 30 banks during their run from 1981 to 1986. Claude Genova's connections and infrastructure was overtaken by the [Hornec brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornec_gang) in 1994.

### Push to extortion: 1990–2000

&#x20;At the start of the 1990s, cities were split up by families or "godfathers"[^cite_note-Mob-2015-5] (*parrains*). Larger cities, however, were fractured into various "mouvances" ([territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory)). Many of these crime families established complex [extortion](162449.md) rings in [Marseille](40888948.md) extending to [Aix-en-Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-en-Provence) and the greater [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera).



The [Citroën Traction Avant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_Traction_Avant) was the preferred vehicle of the French mob, the *[Gang des Tractions Avant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_des_Tractions_Avant)* (1910s to 1950s).

The [Rédoine Faïd gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9doine_Fa%C3%AFd), during the mid-1990s committed [armed robbery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_robbery), [jewel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewellery) theft, and [extortion](162449.md) in the [Paris area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_aire_urbaine).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-marianne2-11][^cite_note-telegraph-12] The leader of the gang, [Rédoine Faïd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9doine_Fa%C3%AFd), was arrested in 2011. He broke out of prison on 13 April 2013 with a staged bombing and fled, but was captured on 29 May 2013.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-australian-15]

### Revitalization: 2000–present

Minority members of French society are being lured into crime families as a way of "fitting in". Low economic growth in the south of France is also leading youth to join the ranks as [drug mules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mule_\(smuggling\)) to gain an [income alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income).[^cite_note-Oved-2016-3] An increase in youth-centered gang activity lead to the separation of the Milieu. Since the 1990s, the French Corsican mafia (*Milieu corso-marseillais* has encompassed the newer, modern criminals in an effort to distinguish them from the "traditional *Milieu*" (1930s to 1980s).[^cite_note-Oved-2016-3]

In the early 2000s, minorities (notably French [Maghrebis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebis): [Algerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerians), [Tunisians](5639337.md) and [Moroccans](5639315.md)) living in impoverished suburbs participate in the construction of new criminal networks, not as hierarchical and centralized as gangs, but involved in different types of trafficking, particularly drug trafficking.[^cite_note-Pierrat_2006-16] These networks are built into neighborhoods, and their location and the semi-organized nature their crimes make them difficult to identify and dismantle.[^cite_note-Sauvadet_2008-17][^cite_note-Sauvadet_2004-18]

Inter-gang fighting led to a spike in murder rates on Corsica in 2008.[^cite_note-19] In 2010, The "[Venzolasca Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venzolasca_Gang?action=edit\&redlink=1)" penetrated large amounts of territory previously occupied by the [Brise de Mer Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_de_la_Brise_de_Mer).

In 2011, it was reported that various North African gangs had moved to the suburbs of Paris to sell [crack cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine).[^cite_note-20] Members of these gangs introduced heroin and pure cocaine to their business operations in 2015.[^cite_note-lefigaro-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-marianne-23] On 18 October 2011, a large holding of art was stolen from the [Musée des Beaux-Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_Fesch) in [Ajaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajaccio), Corsica, by the Valinco, Brise de Mer, and Venzolasca gangs.[^cite_note-ArtMedia-2018-24]

In 2014, there was also an influx of "Voyageurs" or "Traveller" gangs in Paris, [Marseille](40888948.md), [Grenoble](50972.md), and [Montpellier](73369.md). The most famous of them is the [Hornec crime family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornec_gang);[^cite_note-cites2grenoble-25] they regularly participate in [robberies](54849.md), [extortion](162449.md), drug trafficking, [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution), and illegal slots machines. Their hubs of operation are in the [Camargue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camargue) and the [Étang de Berre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tang_de_Berre) regions.[^cite_note-cites2grenoble-25]

In 2023, a war erupted in Marseille between the [Yoda](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoda_\(gang\)) and [DZ Mafia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/DZ_Mafia) gangs left 49 people dead and more than 120 injured.[^cite_note-26]
