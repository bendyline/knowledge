# The Big One (motorsport)



[Tyler Reddick](41108702.md) (middleground), Brad Keselowski (foreground) and [Chase Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Elliott) (background) all spinning through the grass at [Las Vegas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Motor_Speedway) before Reddick would eventually hook a curb in the infield and flip once before landing on his wheels. Keselowski later came back up onto the far-side of the track and was nearly struck by [Austin Cindric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Cindric) and Ryan Blaney.

**The Big One** is a phrase describing any crash usually involving five or more cars in [NASCAR](21114.md), [ARCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_Racing_Club_of_America), [F1](10854.md), and [IndyCar](30446232.md) racing. It is most commonly used at [Daytona International Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_International_Speedway) and [Talladega Superspeedway](681674.md), although occasionally seen at other tracks as well, such as [Dover Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_Motor_Speedway) and [Watkins Glen International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watkins_Glen_International).

## Coining the phrase



Typical three-wide pack during the [2021 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Daytona_500).



Five-wide pack at Talladega Superspeedway during the [2019 GEICO 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_GEICO_500).

Until the 2000s, massive crashes were referred to as "major"[^cite_note-ESPN_Winston_89-1][^cite_note-CBS_92-2][^cite_note-3] or "terrific"[^cite_note-ESPN_Pepsi_1990-4] crashes.

By the mid-1990s, competitors and media began taking note of the multi-car wrecks at Daytona and Talladega. In 1997, [Dale Earnhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Earnhardt) described a final-lap crash at the 1997 [Pepsi 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi_400) as "the Big Wreck".[^cite_note-Augusta_Pepsi_97-5] News articles began using the term "Big Wreck" to describe such crashes in 1998,[^cite_note-NYT_Fall_98-6] and by 1999, its use was widespread. Drivers began to openly admit they were apprehensive of its possibility.[^cite_note-CBSnews_Spring_99-7]

One of the first times the term "The Big One" was used on-air was during the [Winston 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMP_Energy_500) on [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) October 11, 1998. Commentator [Bob Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Jenkins) said during the crash on lap 134 "This is the big one that we had hoped we would not have."[^cite_note-8] One of the first published instances of the term "The Big One," was an April 18, 2000, article on [ESPN.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN.com) about a crash in the [DieHard 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron's_499). [Benny Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Parsons) also used the term on the air during that particular race when replays of the crash were being shown.[^cite_note-ESPN_April_2000-9] The term was also being used informally by fans on [message boards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_forum).

During the [2001 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Daytona_500), [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_on_Fox) commentator [Darrell Waltrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darrell_Waltrip) used the term on-air to describe an 18-car crash in the backstretch on lap 173, as saying "It's the big one, gang, it's the big one. It's what we've all been fearing in this kind of racing is going to happen."[^cite_note-10]

By 2001, the phrase was widely used by competitors, fans, and in print and broadcast media.[^cite_note-NYT_Pepsi_2001-11] It soon became standard NASCAR vernacular, and it became a [retronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retronym) to describe past such accidents as well.[^cite_note-Oct_2005-12][^cite_note-Need_for_change-13]

*The Big One* has been the subject of [criticism of NASCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_NASCAR).[^cite_note-Need_for_change-13] Some have complained that the sanctioning body, promoters and media have celebrated the crashes.[^cite_note-Need_for_change-13]

By 2009, Talladega Superspeedway marketed itself on the notorious crashes with hot dogs, and later meatballs billed as "The Big One," with activities on Friday night after on-track action concludes billed as "The Big One on Talladega Boulevard".[^cite_note-14]

## Examples

### NASCAR Cup Series

- 2001 Daytona 500:&#x20;

  - On the back straightaway on lap 173, fifth-place [Robby Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robby_Gordon) got into the back of fourth-place [Ward Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_Burton), turning Burton left into third-place [Tony Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Stewart)'s right rear. Stewart's car went straight into the wall, catching air, landing on its side on top of Gordon's car, and tumbling across the track while getting hit by other cars. The car briefly landed on top of [Joe Gibbs Racing](2410190.md) teammate [Bobby Labonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Labonte) before falling onto the track and rolling to a stop in the infield grass. Burton's and Stewart's cars blocked the track, starting a chain reaction crash that collected 18 cars in total, including Burton, Stewart, Labonte, Gordon, [Rusty Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusty_Wallace), [Steve Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Park), [Terry Labonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Labonte), [Mark Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Martin), [Jerry Nadeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Nadeau), [Jason Leffler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Leffler), [Elliott Sadler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Sadler), [John Andretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Andretti), [Jeff Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Burton), [Andy Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Houston), [Jeff Gordon](18600829.md), [Kenny Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Wallace), [Buckshot Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckshot_Jones), and [Dale Jarrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Jarrett). This crash, however, was later overshadowed by Dale Earnhardt's [fatal crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Dale_Earnhardt) on the last lap. Incidentally, Earnhardt had narrowly escaped being caught up in the lap 173 crash, nearly being hit by the spinning Ward Burton.
