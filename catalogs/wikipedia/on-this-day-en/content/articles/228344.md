**Mixed martial arts** (**MMA**)[^cite_note-10] is a full-contact [combat sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_sport) based on [striking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_\(attack\)) and [grappling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grappling), incorporating techniques from various combat sports from around the world.

The precursors of modern MMA existed in various parts of the world since the ancient period to the 20th century in different forms. Prior to 1970s, various **inter-stylistic contests** and/or **no-holds-barred bouts** took place throughout different countries in [East Asia](19605700.md). One example is from mid-20th-century [Hong Kong](13404.md), where rooftop [street fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_fighting) contests between different [martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_arts) styles occurred. As a result, this naturally contributed to the rise of [Bruce Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Lee)'s [hybrid martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_martial_arts) style, [Jeet Kune Do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeet_Kune_Do) in [United States](3434750.md).

The modern MMA started to emerge in different forms during the 20th century as well. In [Brazil](3383.md) there was a phenomenon called [vale tudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_tudo), an early form of modern MMA, which became known for unrestricted fights between various styles such as [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu), [catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling), [Luta Livre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luta_Livre), [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai) and [capoeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capoeira). An early high-profile mixed bout was [Kimura vs Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masahiko_Kimura_vs._H%C3%A9lio_Gracie) in 1951. Another early form of modern MMA was the 1976 [Ali vs. Inoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_vs._Inoki) exhibition bout, fought between boxer [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali) and wrestler [Antonio Inoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Inoki) in Japan, where it later inspired the foundation of [Shooto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooto) in 1985, [Pancrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancrase) in 1993, and the [Pride Fighting Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_Fighting_Championships) in 1997. In the 1990s, the [Gracie family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracie_family) brought their Brazilian jiu-jitsu style, first developed in Brazil from the 1920s, to the United States—which culminated in the founding of the [Ultimate Fighting Championship](169660.md) (UFC) promotion company in 1993. The company held an event with almost no rules, mostly due to the influence of [Art Davie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Davie) and [Rorion Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rorion_Gracie) attempting to replicate mixed contests that existed in Brazil[^cite_note-11] and Japan. They would later implement a different set of rules (example: eliminating kicking a grounded opponent), which differed from other leagues which were more in favour of realistic, "street-like" fights.[^cite_note-MixedMartialArts.com-12] The first documented use of the term *mixed martial arts* was in a review of [UFC 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1) by television critic [Howard Rosenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Rosenberg) in 1993. Originally promoted as a competition to find the most effective martial arts for real unarmed combat, competitors from different fighting styles were pitted against one another in contests with relatively few rules.[^cite_note-slate-13] Later, individual fighters incorporated multiple martial arts into their style. MMA promoters were pressured to adopt [additional rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_martial_arts_rules) to increase competitors' safety, to comply with sport regulations and to broaden mainstream acceptance of the sport.[^cite_note-NY2000009-14] Following these changes, the modern MMA, which was once dismissed as "human cockfighting," became a global phenomenon in the early 2000s while seeing an increased popularity with a [pay-per-view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay-per-view) business that rivals [boxing](4243.md) and [professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling).[^cite_note-PPV-15][^cite_note-Rivera-16]

## From history to present day

### Precursors of modern MMA



A [Chinese martial artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_martial_arts) preparing to throw his opponent during a [lei tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lei_tai) contest in [ancient China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_China)

In [ancient China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_China), combat sport appeared in the form of [Leitai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leitai), a no-holds-barred mixed combat sport that combined [Chinese martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_martial_arts), boxing and wrestling.[^cite_note-Brownell-17]



[*The Pancrastinae*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestlers_\(sculpture\)): a statue portraying the [pancratium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancratium), an event which took place in the Roman [Colosseum](49603.md). Even as late as the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages), statues were put up in Rome and other cities to honor remarkable pankratiasts. This statue, now part of the [Uffizi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uffizi) collection, is a Roman copy of a lost Greek original, circa 3rd century BC.



Ancient Greek pankratiasts fighting. This drawing is an early 20th century copy of a scene from a [Panathenaic amphora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panathenaic_amphora).

[^cite_note-18]

In [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece), there was a sport called [pankration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankration), which featured grappling and striking skills similar to those found in modern MMA. Pankration was formed by combining the already established wrestling and boxing traditions and, in Olympic terms, first featured in the 33rd [Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Olympic_Games) in 648 BC. All strikes and holds were allowed with the exception of biting and gouging, which were banned. The fighters, called pankratiasts, fought until someone could not continue or signaled submission by raising their index finger; there were no rounds.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] According to the historian E. Norman Gardiner, "No branch of athletics was more popular than the pankration."[^cite_note-21] There is also evidence of similar mixed combat sports in [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_martial_arts) and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_martial_arts).[^cite_note-Brownell-17]

The mid-19th century saw the prominence of the new sport [savate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savate) in the combat sports circle. French savate fighters wanted to test their techniques against the traditional combat styles of its time. In 1852, a contest was held in France between French *savateurs* and English [bare-knuckle boxers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bare-knuckle_boxing), in which French fighter Rambaud alias *la Resistance* fought English fighter Dickinson and won using his kicks. However, the English team still won the four other match-ups during the contest.[^cite_note-MAW-22] Contests occurred in the late 19th to mid-20th century between French *savateurs* and other combat styles. Examples include a 1905 fight between French savateur Georges Dubois and [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) practitioner Ernest "Re-nier" Regnier, which resulted in the latter winning by submission with an [armbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armlock), as well as the highly publicized 1957 fight between French savateur and [professional boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_boxer) Jacques Cayron and a young Japanese [karateka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karateka) named Hiroo Mochizuki, which ended when Cayron knocked Hiroo out with a [hook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hook_\(boxing\)).[^cite_note-MAW-22]

[Catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling) appeared in the late 19th century, combining several global styles of [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling), including Indian [pehlwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pehlwani) and English wrestling.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] In turn, catch wrestling went on to greatly influence modern MMA.[^cite_note-25] No-holds-barred fighting reportedly took place in the late 1880s when wrestlers representing the style of catch wrestling and many others met in tournaments and music-hall challenge matches throughout Europe. In the US, the first major encounter between a boxer and a wrestler in modern times took place in 1887 when [John L. Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_L._Sullivan), then heavyweight world boxing champion, entered the ring with his trainer, wrestling champion [William Muldoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Muldoon), and was slammed to the mat in two minutes. The next publicized encounter occurred in the late 1890s when future heavyweight boxing champion [Bob Fitzsimmons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Fitzsimmons) took on European wrestling champion [Ernest Roeber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Roeber). In September 1901, [Frank "Paddy" Slavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_%22Paddy%22_Slavin), who had been a contender for Sullivan's boxing title, knocked out future world wrestling champion [Frank Gotch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Gotch) in [Dawson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson_City), Canada.[^cite_note-26] The judo-practitioner Ren-nierand, who gained fame after defeating George Dubois, would fight again in another similar contest, which he lost to Ukrainian Catch wrestler [Ivan Poddubny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Poddubny).[^cite_note-MAW-22]

Another early example of mixed martial arts was [Bartitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartitsu), which [Edward William Barton-Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_William_Barton-Wright) founded in London in 1899. Combining [catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [boxing](4243.md), [savate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savate), [jujutsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jujutsu) and [canne de combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canne_de_combat) (French stick fighting), Bartitsu was the first martial art known to have combined Asian and European fighting styles,[^cite_note-bart-27] and which saw MMA-style contests throughout England, pitting European catch wrestlers and Japanese judoka champions against representatives of various European wrestling styles.[^cite_note-bart-27]

Among the precursors of modern MMA are mixed style contests throughout Europe, Japan, and the Pacific Rim during the early 1900s.[^cite_note-28] In Japan, these contests were known as *merikan*, from the Japanese slang for "American \[fighting]". *Merikan* contests were fought under a variety of rules, including points decision, best of three throws or knockdowns, and victory via knockout or submission.[^cite_note-29]

In 1909, [Erich Rahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Rahn) opened a [Tsutsumi Hōzan-ryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsutsumi_H%C5%8Dzan-ry%C5%AB) jujutsu school in Germany where he mixed his style with boxing and wrestling.[^cite_note-30] [Sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambo_\(martial_art\)), a martial art and combat sport developed in Russia in the early 1920s, merged various forms of combat styles such as wrestling, judo and striking into one unique martial art.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] In 1925, the founder of judo, [Jigoro Kano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan%C5%8D_Jigor%C5%8D), also created a form of [physical education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_education) that included a combative form based in both judo and karate moves.[^cite_note-33]

The popularity of professional wrestling, which was contested under various [catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling) rules at the time, waned after [World War I](4764461.md), when the sport split into two genres: "[shoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoot_\(professional_wrestling\))", in which the fighters actually competed, and "[show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_\(professional_wrestling\))", which evolved into modern [professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling).[^cite_note-willoughby-34] In 1936, heavyweight boxing contender [Kingfish Levinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingfish_Levinsky) and professional wrestler [Ray Steele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Steele_\(US_wrestler\)) competed in a mixed match, which catch wrestler Steele won in 35 seconds.[^cite_note-willoughby-34] 27 years later, Ray Steele's protégé [Lou Thesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Thesz) fought boxer [Jersey Joe Walcott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_Joe_Walcott) twice in mixed style bouts. The first match was a real contest which Thesz won while the second match was a work, which Thesz also won.

In the 1940s in the Palama Settlement in Hawaii, five martial arts masters, under the leadership of [Adriano Emperado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriano_Directo_Emperado), curious to determine which martial art was best, began testing each other in their respective arts of kenpo, jujitsu, Chinese and American boxing and [tang soo do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_soo_do). From this they developed [kajukenbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajukenbo), the first American mixed martial arts. Also in 1941, 7th dan judoka Haruna Hoshizaki addressed the topic of fights between judokas and boxers, and instructed judokas to learn boxing to better engage them.[^cite_note-35]

On February 12, 1963, three karatekas from *Oyama dojo* ([kyokushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyokushin) later) went to the [Lumpinee Boxing Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumpinee_Boxing_Stadium) in Thailand and fought against three Muay Thai fighters. The three karate fighters were [Tadashi Nakamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadashi_Nakamura_\(martial_artist\)), [Kenji Kurosaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenji_Kurosaki) and Akio Fujihira (also known as Noboru Osawa),[^cite_note-8_Limbs-36] while the Thai fighters were Tan Saren, Haufi Lukkontai and Rawee Dechachai.[^cite_note-37] Japan won 2–1: Nakamura and Fujihira both knocked out their opponents with punches, with Fujihira making usage of [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) techniques to gain advantage over the bigger Lukkontai,[^cite_note-38] while Kurosaki was knocked out by elbows by Dechachai. Kurosaki was an instructor rather than a contender, and that he had stood in as a substitute for an absent chosen fighter. In June of the same year, karateka and future kickboxer [Tadashi Sawamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadashi_Sawamura) faced top Thai fighter Samarn Sor Adisorn: Sawamura was knocked down sixteen times on his way to defeat, but he went on to incorporate what he learned in that fight in kickboxing tournaments.[^cite_note-8_Limbs-36] On December 2, 1963, a catch wrestler and judoka "[Judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo)" [Gene Lebell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Lebell) fought professional boxer Milo Savage in a [no-holds-barred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling) match. Lebell won by [harai goshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harai_goshi) to [rear naked choke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_naked_choke), leaving Savage unconscious. This was the first televised bout of mixed-style fighting in North America. The hometown crowd was so enraged that they began to boo and throw chairs at Lebell.[^cite_note-39]

*Prokeseucheo* ("프로케스처" if written in Korean) became South Korea's first mixed martial arts organization, was founded on March 7, 1964. Created by Korean professional wrestlers, including Cho Gyeong-su and Ahn Myeong-gil, the sport combined the elements of [Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling), [Boxing](4243.md), [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai), [Judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai), [Tang Soo Do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_Soo_Do) and [traditional Karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate). Their rules required use of open-finger gloves and only forbid attacks to the groin. Other Korean professional wrestlers such as Kim Ki-nam, Jeon In-su, Ahn Myeong-gil, and Kim Sang-hyeon competed in this organization. Unfortunately, the organization eventually ceased to exist after failing to obtain official registration approval from the Ministry of Education and disappeared in history. Interestingly, because this organization was forgotten entirely, the later MMA organization that emerged in 2003, [Spirit MC](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%8A%A4%ED%94%BC%EB%A6%BF%20MC), played a role to reintroduce MMA to South Korea's mainstream in 2003, thus [Spirit MC](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%8A%A4%ED%94%BC%EB%A6%BF%20MC) being credited as Korea's pioneering MMA organization today. However, the version of MMA that [Spirit MC](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%8A%A4%ED%94%BC%EB%A6%BF%20MC) reintroduced is in the form of modern MMA, which differs from its 1960s predecessor, *Prokeseucheo*.[^cite_note-40]

In the mid-20th century, mixed martial arts contests emerged in Hong Kong's [street fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_fighting) culture in the form of rooftop fights. During the early 20th century, there was an influx of migrants from mainland China, including [Chinese martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_martial_arts) teachers who opened up martial arts schools in Hong Kong. In the mid-20th century, soaring [crime in Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_Hong_Kong), combined with limited [Hong Kong Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Police) manpower, led to many young Hongkongers learning martial arts for [self-defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defence). Around the 1960s, there were about 400 martial arts schools in Hong Kong, teaching their own distinctive styles of martial arts. In Hong Kong's street fighting culture, there emerged a rooftop fight scene in the 1950s and 1960s, where gangs from rival martial arts schools challenged each other to bare-knuckle fights on Hong Kong's rooftops, in order to avoid crackdowns by colonial [British Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong) authorities. The most famous fighter to emerge from Hong Kong's rooftop fight scene was [Bruce Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Lee), who combined different techniques from different martial arts schools into his own [hybrid martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_martial_arts) system called [Jeet Kune Do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeet_Kune_Do). Lee went on to popularize the concept of mixed martial arts internationally.[^cite_note-41]



[Bruce Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Lee) popularized the concept of mixed martial arts via his [hybrid philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_martial_arts) of [Jeet Kune Do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeet_Kune_Do) during the late 1960s to early 1970s.

During the late 1960s to early 1970s, the concept of [hybrid martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_martial_arts) was popularized in the West by [Bruce Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Lee) via his system of [Jeet Kune Do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeet_Kune_Do).[^cite_note-42] Lee believed that "the best fighter is not a boxer, karate or judo man. The best fighter is someone who can adapt to any style, to be formless, to adopt an individual's own style and not following the system of styles."[^cite_note-43] In 2004, UFC President [Dana White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_White) would call Lee the "father of mixed martial arts" stating: "If you look at the way Bruce Lee trained, the way he fought, and many of the things he wrote, he said the perfect style was no style. You take a little something from everything. You take the good things from every different discipline, use what works, and you throw the rest away".[^cite_note-44]

A contemporary of Bruce Lee, [Wing Chun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_Chun) practitioner [Wong Shun-leung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wong_Shun-leung), gained prominence fighting in 60–100 illegal *[beimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_fighting)* fights against other Chinese martial artists of various styles. Wong also fought and won against Western fighters of other combat styles, such as his match against Russian boxer Giko,[^cite_note-PetersonPeterboro-45] his televised fight against a [fencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fencing),[^cite_note-LamLeBlanc-46] and his fight against Taiwanese kung fu master Wu Ming-jeet.[^cite_note-Longley-47] Wong combined boxing and [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing) into his kung fu, as Bruce Lee did.

A fight between [Golden Gloves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gloves) boxing champion [Joey Hadley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cus_D'Amato#Joey_Hadley) and Arkansas Karate Champion David Valovich happened on June 22, 1976, at Memphis Blues Baseball Park. The bout had mixed rules: the [karateka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karateka) was allowed to use his fists, feet and knees, while the boxer could only use his fists. Hadley won the fight via knockout on the first round.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

In 1988, [Rick Roufus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Roufus) challenged [Changpuek Kiatsongrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changpuek_Kiatsongrit) to a non-title [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai) vs. [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing) super fight. Roufus was at the time an undefeated Kickboxer and held both the [KICK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate_International_Council_of_Kickboxing) Super Middleweight World title and the PKC Middleweight U.S. title. Kiatsongrit was finding it increasingly difficult to get fights in Thailand as his weight (70 kg) was not typical for Thailand, where competitive bouts tended to be at the lower weights. Roufus knocked Changpuek down twice with punches in the first round, breaking Changpuek's jaw, but lost by technical knockout in the fourth round due to the culmination of low kicks to the legs that he was unprepared for. This match was the first popular fight which showcased the power of such low kicks to a predominantly Western audience.[^cite_note-50]

Regulated mixed martial arts competitions were first introduced in the United States by [CV Productions, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CV_Productions,_Inc.) Its first competition, called *[Tough Guy Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tough_Guy_Contest)* was held on March 20, 1980, [New Kensington, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kensington,_Pennsylvania), [Holiday Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_Inn). During that year the company renamed the brand to *Super Fighters* and sanctioned ten regulated tournaments in [Pennsylvania](23332.md). In 1983, Pennsylvania State Senate passed a bill known as the "[Tough Guy Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tough_Guy_Law)" that specifically called for: "Prohibiting Tough Guy contests or Battle of the Brawlers contests", and ended the sport.[^cite_note-NashJohn-51][^cite_note-WernerSam-52][^cite_note-53]

### Modern MMA

Modern mixed martial arts began in two places: the [vale tudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_tudo) events in Brazil in 1920s, and Japan's [Shoot wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoot_wrestling) in 1970s. The modern form of Mixed Martial Arts had mainstream exposure in United States along with an initial international exposure when the [Gracie family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracie_family) created [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC) in the early 90s. In the mid-90s, after the first event of [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC), the sport went through a process of globalization, spreading to different countries. Finally, in the beginning of the 21st century, the modern MMA became a sport that enjoys worldwide recognition.[^cite_note-Rivera-16]

Vale tudo began in the 1920s and became renowned through its association with the "[Gracie challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracie_challenge)", which was issued by [Carlos Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Gracie) and [Hélio Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9lio_Gracie) and upheld later by descendants of the [Gracie family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracie_family). The "Gracie Challenges" were held in the garages and gyms of the Gracie family members. When the popularity grew, these types of mixed bouts were a staple attraction at the carnivals in Brazil.[^cite_note-54]



[Panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panel_\(comics\)) by Alfredo Storni featuring capoeirista [Ciríaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_da_Silva_Cir%C3%ADaco) defeating jujitsu fighter [Sada Miyako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sada_Miyako) with a [rabo de arraia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabo_de_arraia) kick, *[O Malho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Malho)*, 1909.

The fight between [capoeirista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capoeirista) [Francisco da Silva Ciríaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_da_Silva_Cir%C3%ADaco) and jujitsu fighter [Sada Miyako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sada_Miyako) in Brazil stands as one of the earliest recorded interdisciplinary martial arts clashes in the country.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

In 1920, capoerista [Mestre Sinhozinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agenor_Moreira_Sampaio) opened his martial arts school, where he taught *[capoeira carioca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capoeira_carioca)* mixed with judo, wrestling and other styles.[^cite_note-57]



[Masahiko Kimura vs. Hélio Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masahiko_Kimura_vs._H%C3%A9lio_Gracie), a 1951 bout between Japanese [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) fighter [Masahiko Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masahiko_Kimura) and [Brazilian jiu jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu_jitsu) founder [Hélio Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9lio_Gracie) in Brazil, was an early high-profile mixed martial arts bout.

The "[Gracie challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracie_challenge)" at times used rules other than that of MMA. In 1951, a high-profile grappling match was [Masahiko Kimura vs. Hélio Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masahiko_Kimura_vs._H%C3%A9lio_Gracie), which was wrestled between [judoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judoka) [Masahiko Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masahiko_Kimura) and [Brazilian jiu jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu_jitsu) founder [Hélio Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9lio_Gracie) in Brazil. Kimura defeated Gracie using a [gyaku-ude-garami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimura_lock) armlock, which later became known as the "Kimura" in Brazilian jiu jitsu.[^cite_note-58]

In the 1970s, [Antonio Inoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Inoki), a disciple of [Rikidōzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikid%C5%8Dzan), rose to pronominance in Japan by founding [New Japan Pro-Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Japan_Pro-Wrestling) (NJPW) and introducing his own style of [professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling) he dubbed "[Strong-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong-style)", derived from training in [Karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate) and [Catch-As-Catch-Can](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch-As-Catch-Can_Wrestling), an earlier style of legit [Professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling) and [submission grappling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_grappling), taught by [Karl Gotch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Gotch). This style was more realistic, using [full contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_contact_fighting) strikes and a lot of kicks, as well as realistic grappling moves and submissions. Inoki promoted professional wrestling as a legit and real combat sport and the "strongest" fighting style, and to prove it he fought [worked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_\(professional_wrestling\)) (i.e. predetermined) matches against fighters from other martial arts and combat sports, such as [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing), [sumo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumo) and [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate), known as "heterogeneous combat sports bouts" (*Ishu Kakutōgi Sen*; 異種格闘技戦).[^cite_note-59] [Muhammad Ali vs. Antonio Inoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_vs._Antonio_Inoki) took place in Japan in 1976 and was the highest profile of these fights. The classic match-up between professional boxer and professional wrestler turned sour as each fighter refused to engage in the other's style, and after a 15-round stalemate it was declared a draw. [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali) sustained a substantial amount of damage to his legs, as [Antonio Inoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Inoki) slide-kicked him continuously for the duration of the bout, causing him to be hospitalized for the next three days.[^cite_note-60] The fight played an important role in the history of mixed martial arts.[^cite_note-61]

The match against Muhammad Ali, as well the other heterogeneous style bouts inspired a lot of Inoki's students. Who would later leave NJPW founded a new company named [Universal Wrestling Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Wrestling_Federation_\(Japan\)) (UWF), which promoted a realistic style of pro-wrestling that eschewed the most theatrical elements of wrestling and resembled closer to an actual fight, but it was still predetermined. This style would become known as "[shoot wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoot_Wrestling)".[^cite_note-62] Many shoot wrestlers would follow Inoki's example and book matches which pitched "pro wrestling" various other martial arts. Although most of these matches were [worked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_\(professional_wrestling\)) (i.e. scripted and predetermined).[^cite_note-63]

Japan had its own form of mixed martial arts discipline, [Shooto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooto), which was created from shoot wrestling in 1985 by ["Tiger Mask" Satoru Sayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoru_Sayama), who left UWF in 1984 and wanted to create a style that mixed kickboxing with submission grappling. As well as the shoot wrestling derivative [Pancrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancrase), which was founded by shoot-style professional wrestlers [Masakatsu Funaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masakatsu_Funaki) and [Minoru Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoru_Suzuki) as a promotion in 1993 as an attempt to create a shoot wrestling event without predetermined matches. Their first event, [*Pancrase: Yes, We Are Hybrid Wrestlers*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancrase:_Yes,_We_Are_Hybrid_Wrestlers_1) was held in Japan in September 1993, two months before [UFC 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1) was held in the United States in November 1993. Another promotion formed around the same time by [Akira Maeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Maeda) called [Fighting Network RINGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighting_Network_RINGS) initially started as a shoot-style professional wrestling promotion but it also promoted early mixed martial arts contests. From 1995 onwards it began identifying itself as a mixed martial arts promotion and moved away from the original shoot style and predetermined bouts. These promotions inspired [Pride Fighting Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_Fighting_Championships) which started in 1997. Pride was acquired by its rival Ultimate Fighting Championship in 2007.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

In 1993, the sport was reintroduced to the United States by the [Ultimate Fighting Championship](169660.md) (UFC).[^cite_note-Sánchez_Garcia,_R._2010-66] UFC promoters initially pitched the event as a real-life [fighting video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighting_video_game) tournament similar to *[Street Fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter)* and *[Mortal Kombat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortal_Kombat)*.[^cite_note-67] The sport gained international exposure and widespread publicity when jiu-jitsu fighter [Royce Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royce_Gracie) won the [first Ultimate Fighting Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1) tournament, submitting three challengers in a total of just five minutes.[^cite_note-68] sparking a revolution in martial arts.[^cite_note-peligro-69][^cite_note-70]



[Yuki Nakai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Nakai) fights [UFC 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1) finalist [Gerard Gordeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Gordeau) at [Vale Tudo Japan 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_Tudo_Japan).

The first [Vale Tudo Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_Tudo_Japan) tournaments were held in 1994 and 1995 and were both won by [Rickson Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickson_Gracie). Around the same time, International Vale Tudo competition started to develop through (World Vale Tudo Championship (WVC), [VTJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTJ), [IVC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Vale_Tudo_Championship), UVF etc.). In December 1994, Pancrase did the *[King of Pancrase tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Pancrase_tournament)*, a two-night event which featured many of the sport's leading fighters, including future [UFC champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_champions) [Bas Rutten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas_Rutten), [Ken Shamrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Shamrock), [Frank Shamrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Shamrock) and [Maurice Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Smith_\(fighter\)), alongside prominent Japanese fighters such as [Masakatsu Funaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masakatsu_Funaki), [Minoru Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoru_Suzuki) and [Manabu Yamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manabu_Yamada). The tournament drew approximately 11,000 spectators on its first night and 11,500 spectators on its second night respectively.[^cite_note-\:4-71][^cite_note-72] Interest in mixed martial arts as a sport in Japan resulted in the creation of the [Pride Fighting Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_Fighting_Championships) (Pride) in 1997.[^cite_note-whatispride-73]

The sport reached a new peak of popularity in North America in December 2006: a [rematch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_66) between then [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC) light heavyweight champion [Chuck Liddell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Liddell) and former champion [Tito Ortiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tito_Ortiz), rivaled the [PPV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay-per-view) sales of some of the biggest boxing events of all time,[^cite_note-74] and helped the UFC's 2006 PPV gross surpass that of any promotion in PPV history. In 2007, [Zuffa LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuffa), the owners of the UFC MMA promotion, bought Japanese rival MMA brand [Pride FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_FC), merging the contracted fighters under one promotion.[^cite_note-75] Comparisons were drawn to the consolidation that occurred in other sports, such as the [AFL-NFL Merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL-NFL_Merger) in [American football](18951490.md).[^cite_note-76]

#### Origin of the term *MMA*

The first documented use of the name *mixed martial arts* was in a review of [UFC 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1) by television critic, [Howard Rosenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Rosenberg), in 1993.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] The term gained popularity when the website, newfullcontact.com (one of the biggest websites covering the sport at the time), hosted and reprinted the article. The first use of the term by a promotion was in September 1995 by Rick Blume, president and CEO of Battlecade Extreme Fighting, just after UFC 7.[^cite_note-79] UFC official [Jeff Blatnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Blatnick) was responsible for the Ultimate Fighting Championship officially adopting the name mixed martial arts. It was previously marketed as "Ultimate Fighting" and "No Holds Barred (NHB)", until Blatnick and [John McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCarthy_\(mixed_martial_arts\)) proposed the name "MMA" at the [UFC 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_17) rules meeting in response to increased public criticism.[^cite_note-80] The question as to who actually coined the name is still in debate.[^cite_note-KirikJenness-81]

This is a deliberate misnomer to promote the events as a means to determine the efficacy of different martial arts when in reality it was a business venture to promote the Gracie family's brand in the US. A more appropriate name would be Mixed Martial Sports (MMS) as competitors combine martial sports such as Boxing, Wrestling, Thai Kickboxing and sportified versions of Brazilian Jujutsu. The current term of Combat Sports reflects more accurately the nature of the contests.[^cite_note-82]

#### Regulation



A "[soccer kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_kick)", movement where the fighter kicks the head of a downed opponent. While common in [vale tudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_tudo), early MMA and Japanese promotions, the soccer kick has been banned from the [Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Rules_of_Mixed_Martial_Arts).

The first state-regulated MMA event was held in Biloxi, Mississippi on August 23, 1996, with the sanctioning of IFC's Mayhem in Mississippi[^cite_note-83] show by the Mississippi Athletic Commission under William Lyons. The rules used were an adaptation of the kickboxing rules already accepted by most state athletic commissions. These modified kickboxing rules allowed for take downs and ground fighting and did away with rounds, although they did allow for fighters to be stood up by the referee and restarted if there was no action on the ground. These rules were the first in modern MMA to define fouls, fighting surfaces and the use of the cage.

In March 1997, the Iowa Athletic Commission officially sanctioned [Battlecade Extreme Fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlecade_Extreme_Fighting) under a modified form of its existing rules for [Shootfighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shootfighting). These rules created the three 'five-minute round/one-minute break' format, and mandated shootfighting gloves, as well as weight classes for the first time. Illegal blows were listed as groin strikes, head butting, biting, eye gouging, hair pulling, striking an opponent with an elbow while the opponent is on the mat, kidney strikes, and striking the back of the head with closed fist. Holding onto the ring or cage for any reason was defined as a foul.[^cite_note-EXTREME_FIGHTING_4_RULES_MEETING_GOES_ON_AS_PLANNED-84][^cite_note-Iowa_Professional_Shoot_Fighting_Regulations-85] While there are minor differences between these and the final Unified Rules, notably regarding elbow strikes, the Iowa rules allowed mixed martial arts promoters to conduct essentially modern events legally, anywhere in the state. On March 28, 1997, Extreme Fighting 4 was held under these rules, making it the first show conducted under a version of the modern rules.

In April 2000, the [California State Athletic Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Athletic_Commission) voted unanimously in favor of regulations that later became the foundation for the [Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Rules_of_Mixed_Martial_Arts). However, when the legislation was sent to the California capital in Sacramento for review, it was determined that the sport fell outside the jurisdiction of the CSAC, rendering the vote meaningless.[^cite_note-86]

On September 30, 2000, the [New Jersey State Athletic Control Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_State_Athletic_Control_Board) (NJSACB) began allowing mixed martial arts promoters to conduct events in New Jersey. The first event was an IFC event titled Battleground 2000 held in Atlantic City. The intent was to allow the NJSACB to observe actual events and gather information to establish a comprehensive set of rules to regulate the sport effectively.[^cite_note-njacb-87]

On April 3, 2001, the NJSACB held a meeting to discuss the regulation of mixed martial arts events. This meeting attempted to unify the myriad rules and regulations which had been utilized by the different mixed martial arts organizations. At this meeting, the proposed uniform rules were agreed upon by the NJSACB, several other regulatory bodies, numerous promoters of mixed martial arts events and other interested parties in attendance. At the conclusion of the meeting, all parties in attendance were able to agree upon a uniform set of rules to govern the sport of mixed martial arts.[^cite_note-njacb-87]

The rules adopted by the NJSACB have become the de facto standard set of rules for professional mixed martial arts across North America. On July 30, 2009, a motion was made at the annual meeting of the [Association of Boxing Commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Boxing_Commissions) to adopt these rules as the "Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts". The motion passed unanimously.[^cite_note-88]

In November 2005, the [United States Army](32087.md) began to sanction mixed martial arts with the first annual Army Combatives Championships held by the [US Army Combatives School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army_Combatives_School).[^cite_note-89]

[Canada](5042916.md) formally decriminalized mixed martial arts with a vote on Bill S-209 on June 5, 2013. The bill allows for provinces to have the power to create athletic commissions to regulate and sanction professional mixed martial arts bouts.[^cite_note-CANL1-90]

#### Globalization, worldwide recognition, and popularity

The event, [UFC 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1), became the beginning of the international exposure of this sport. Since then, the growth of mixed martial arts (MMA) globally has been remarkable over the past few decades. MMA has evolved from being a niche combat sport to one of the most popular and commercially successful sports worldwide.[^cite_note-91] In the mid-90s following [UFC 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1), [Frederico Lapenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederico_Lapenda), a film producer and fight promoter, helped the modern MMA expand further internationally, thus playing a pivotal role in the process of the sport's globalization.[^cite_note-92] [Dana White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_White) became the CEO of [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC) in 2001 and brought success to the promotion. This caused the modern MMA to become known worldwide from the early 21st century and afterwards.[^cite_note-Rivera-16] From 2000s and on, at the global scale, this caused the revolution in martial arts communities in different countries as a result, similarly to what happened in the martial arts community in the [United States](3434750.md) during the 90s after the [UFC 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1). A good example of this can be seen in [China](5405.md). In 2003, the no-holds-barred fight between [Andy Pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Pi) (who introduced [Brazilian Jiu jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Jiu_jitsu) to [China](5405.md) in 1997) and [Xu Xiaodong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Xiaodong) (who then represented his country's martial arts with [Sanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanda_\(sport\))) gained much attention since it was the early example of a [Chinese martial art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_martial_arts) being tested against the realities of [ground fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_fighting). After his defeat, Xu started to train in modern MMA and became a coach. Ultimately, Xu himself started to challenge [different local styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_martial_arts) practiced by the Chinese martial arts community, starting from 2017, to prove the effectiveness of modern MMA while debunking the myths behind the traditional [Chinese martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_martial_arts).

## MMA organizations

### Promotions

Since the [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC) came to prominence in [mainstream media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainstream_media) in 2006, and with their 2007 merger with [Pride FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_FC) and purchases of [WEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Extreme_Cagefighting) and [Strikeforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikeforce_\(mixed_martial_arts\)), it has been the most significant MMA promotion in the world in terms of popularity, salaries, [talent](#Fighter_ranking), and level of competition.

According to [Fight Matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_Matrix), these are the promotions with the top ranked talent as of May 2026:[^cite_note-93]

1. [Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Fighting_Championship_\(UFC\)). Based in Las Vegas, United States. Broadcasts their fights locally on [Paramount Skydance](79917576.md) (prior to 2026 on [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN)) and on other networks around the world.

2. [Rizin Fighting Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizin_Fighting_Federation) (RIZIN). Based in Tokyo, Japan. ### Fighter development

As a result of an increased number of competitors, organized training camps, information sharing, and modern [kinesiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinesiology), the understanding of the effectiveness of various strategies has been greatly improved. UFC commentator [Joe Rogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Rogan) claimed that martial arts evolved more in the ten years following 1993 (the [first UFC event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1)) than in the preceding 700 years combined.[^cite_note-97]

> During his reign atop the sport in the late 1990s he was the prototype – he could strike with the best strikers; he could grapple with the best grapplers; his endurance was second to none.

— Mike Sloan describing UFC champion [Frank Shamrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Shamrock)'s early dominance

[^cite_note-98]

The high profile of modern MMA promotions such as UFC and Pride has fostered an accelerated development of the sport. The early 1990s saw a wide variety of traditional styles competing in the sport.[^cite_note-99] However, early competition saw varying levels of success among disparate styles. In the early 1990s, practitioners of grappling based styles such as [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu) dominated competition in the United States. Practitioners of striking based arts such as [boxing](4243.md), [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing), and [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate), who were unfamiliar with submission grappling, proved to be unprepared to deal with its [submission techniques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_hold).[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-UFCHISTORYHSW1-104] As competitions became more and more common, those with a base in striking arts became more competitive as they cross-trained in styles based around [takedowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takedown_\(grappling\)) and submission holds.[^cite_note-UFCHISTORYHSW1-104] Likewise, those from the varying grappling styles added striking techniques to their arsenal. This increase of [cross-training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-training) resulted in fighters becoming increasingly multidimensional and well-rounded in their skill-sets.

The new hybridization of fighting styles can be seen in the technique of "ground and pound" developed by wrestling-based UFC pioneers such as [Dan Severn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Severn), [Don Frye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Frye) and [Mark Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Coleman). These wrestlers realized the need for the incorporation of strikes on the ground as well as on the feet, and incorporated ground striking into their grappling-based styles. Mark Coleman stated at [UFC 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_14) that his strategy was to "Ground him and pound him", which may be the first televised use of the term.

Since the late 1990s, both strikers and grapplers have been successful at MMA, although it is rare to see any fighter who is not schooled in both striking and grappling arts reach the highest levels of competition.

## Rules

Main article: [Mixed martial arts rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_martial_arts_rules)



[MMA gloves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMA_gloves). They are fingerless gloves which allow both striking and grappling to occur.

The rules for modern mixed martial arts competitions have changed significantly since the early days of [vale tudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_tudo), Japanese [shoot wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoot_wrestling), and [UFC 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1), and even more from the historic style of [pankration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankration). As the knowledge of fighting techniques spread among fighters and spectators, it became clear that the original minimalist rule systems needed to be amended.[^cite_note-Warriors-111] The main motivations for these rule changes were protection of the health of the fighters, the desire to shed the perception of "barbarism and lawlessness", and to be recognized as a legitimate sport.

The new rules included the introduction of [weight classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMA_weight_classes); as knowledge about submissions spread, differences in weight had become a significant factor. There are nine different weight classes in the [Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Rules_of_Mixed_Martial_Arts). These nine weight classes include [flyweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyweight_\(MMA\)) (up to 125 lb / 56.7 kg), [bantamweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantamweight_\(MMA\)) (up to 135 lb / 61.2 kg), [featherweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Featherweight_\(MMA\)) (up to 145 lb / 65.8 kg), [lightweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightweight_\(MMA\)) (up to 155 lb / 70.3 kg), [welterweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welterweight_\(MMA\)) (up to 170 lb / 77.1 kg), [middleweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middleweight_\(MMA\)) (up to 185 lb / 83.9 kg), [light heavyweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_heavyweight_\(MMA\)) (up to 205 lb / 93.0 kg), [heavyweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavyweight_\(MMA\)) (up to 265 lb / 120.2 kg), and [super heavyweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_heavyweight_\(MMA\)) with no upper weight limit.[^cite_note-njacb-87]

[Small, open-fingered gloves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMA_gloves) were introduced to protect fists, reduce the occurrence of cuts (and stoppages due to cuts) and encourage fighters to use their hands for striking to allow more captivating matches. Gloves were first made mandatory in Japan's [Shooto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooto) promotion and were later adopted by the UFC as it developed into a regulated sport. Most professional fights have the fighters wear 4 oz gloves, whereas some jurisdictions require amateurs to wear a slightly heavier 6 oz glove for more protection for the hands and wrists.

Time limits were established to avoid long fights with little action where competitors conserved their strength. Matches without time limits also complicated the airing of live events. The time limits in most professional fights are three 5 minute rounds, and championship fights are normally five 5-minute rounds. Similar motivations produced the "stand up" rule, where the referee can stand fighters up if it is perceived that both are resting on the ground or not advancing toward a dominant position.[^cite_note-Warriors-111]

In the United States, state athletic and boxing commissions have played a crucial role in the introduction of additional rules because they oversee MMA in a similar fashion to boxing. In Japan and most of Europe, there is no regulating authority over competitions, so these organizations have greater freedom in rule development and event structure.

Previously, Japanese-based organization [Pride Fighting Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_Fighting_Championships) held an opening 10-minute round followed by two five-minute rounds. Stomps, [soccer kicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_kick) and knees to the head of a grounded opponent are legal, but elbow strikes to the head are not.[^cite_note-112] This rule set is more predominant in the Asian-based organizations as opposed to European and American rules. More recently, Singapore-based organization [ONE Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ONE_Championship) allows soccer kicks and knees to the head of a grounded opponent as well as elbow strikes to the head, but does not allow head stomps.[^cite_note-113] In 2016, ONE later banned soccer kicks.[^cite_note-114] However, they still allow knees to the head of a grounded opponent. In 2024, the Association of Boxing Commissions and Combative Sports Mixed Martial Arts Committee made changes to the [Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Rules_of_Mixed_Martial_Arts), loosening the rules surrounding what a grounded opponent is, meaning fighters are now more vulnerable to kicks and knees to the head, and they also eliminated the rules prohibiting "12 to 6" elbows.[^cite_note-115]

### Victory



[UFC 74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_74); [Clay Guida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Guida) vs. [Marcus Aurelio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelio)

Victory in a match is normally gained either by the judges' decision after an allotted amount of time has elapsed, a stoppage by the referee (for example if a competitor cannot defend themselves intelligently) or the fight doctor (due to an injury), a [submission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_\(combat_sports\)), by a competitor's [cornerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornerman) throwing in the towel, or by [knockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knockout).

#### Knockout (KO)

As soon as a fighter is unable to continue due to legal strikes, his opponent is declared the winner. As MMA rules allow submissions and [ground and pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_and_pound), the fight is stopped to prevent further injury to the fighter.

#### Technical knockout (TKO)

**Referee stoppage:** The referee may stop a match in progress if:

- a fighter becomes dominant to the point where the opponent cannot intelligently defend themselves and is taking excessive damage as a result.

- a fighter appears to be losing consciousness as they are being struck.

- a fighter appears to have a significant injury such as a cut or a broken bone.

**Doctor stoppage/cut:** The referee will call for a time out if a fighter's ability to continue is in question as a result of apparent injuries, such as a large cut. The ring doctor will inspect the fighter and stop the match if the fighter is deemed unable to continue safely, rendering the opponent the winner. However, if the match is stopped as a result of an injury from illegal actions by the opponent, either a disqualification or no contest will be issued instead.

**Corner stoppage:** A fighter's corner may announce defeat on the fighter's behalf by throwing in the towel during the match in progress or between rounds. This is normally done when a fighter is being beaten to the point where it is dangerous and unnecessary to continue. In some cases, the fighter may be injured.

**Retirement:** A fighter is so dazed or exhausted that they cannot physically continue fighting. Usually occurs between rounds.

#### Submission

A fighter may admit defeat during a match by:

- A physical tap on the opponent's body or mat/floor.

- Tapping verbally.

**Technical Submission:** the referee stops the match when the fighter is caught in a submission hold and is in danger of being injured. This can occur when a fighter is choked unconscious, or when a bone has been broken in a submission hold (a broken arm due to a [kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armlock#Double_wristlock_\(Gyaku_ude-garami\)), etc.).

#### Decision

If the match goes the distance, then the outcome of the bout is determined by three judges. The judging criteria are organization-specific.

**Technical decision:** in the unified rules of MMA, if a fighter is unable to continue due to an accidental illegal technique late in the fight, a technical decision is rendered by the judges based on who is ahead on the judges' scorecards at that time. In a three-round fight, two rounds must be completed for a technical decision to be awarded and in a five-round fight, three rounds must be completed.

#### Other conditions

**Forfeit:** a fighter or their representative may forfeit a match prior to the beginning of the match, thereby losing the match.

**Disqualification:** a "warning" will be given when a fighter commits a foul or illegal action or does not follow the referee's instruction. Three warnings will result in a disqualification. Moreover, if a fighter is unable to continue due to a deliberate illegal technique from his opponent, the opponent will be disqualified.

**No contest:** in the event that both fighters commit a violation of the rules, or a fighter is unable to continue due to an injury from an accidental illegal technique, the match will be declared a "no contest", except in the case of a technical decision in the unified rules. A result can also be overturned to a no contest if the fighter that was originally victorious fails a post fight drug test for banned substances.

### Clothing

Mixed martial arts promotions typically require that male fighters wear shorts in addition to being [barechested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barechested), thus precluding the use of [gi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keikogi) or fighting kimono to inhibit or assist [submission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_\(combat_sport_term\)) holds. Male fighters are required by most athletic commissions to wear groin protectors underneath their trunks.[^cite_note-njacb-87] Female fighters wear [short shorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_shorts) and [sports bras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_bra) or other similarly snug-fitting tops. Both male and female fighters are required to wear a [mouthguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouthguard).[^cite_note-njacb-87][^cite_note-116]

The need for flexibility in the legs combined with durability prompted the creation of various fighting shorts brands, which then spawned a range of [mixed martial arts clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_martial_arts_clothing) and casual wear available to the public.

## Common disciplines

See also: [Mixed martial artists by style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_by_style)

Most 'traditional' martial arts have a specific focus and these arts may be trained to improve in that area. Popular disciplines of each type include:[^cite_note-119]

- **[Stand-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_fighting)**: [boxing](4243.md), [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing), [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai), [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate), [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo), [capoeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capoeira), [combat sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_sambo), [savate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savate) and [sanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanda_\(sport\)) are trained to improve stand-up striking.

- **[Clinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinch_fighting)**: [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [freestyle wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freestyle_wrestling), [folkstyle wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folkstyle_wrestling), [Greco-Roman wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_wrestling), [catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling), [sanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanda_\(sport\)) and [sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambo_\(martial_art\)) are trained to improve [clinching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_grappling_position), [takedowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takedown_\(grappling\)) and [throws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throw_\(grappling\)), while [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai) is trained to improve the [striking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_\(attack\)) aspect of the clinch.

- **[Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_fighting)**: [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambo_\(martial_art\)), [folkstyle wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folkstyle_wrestling), [freestyle wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freestyle_wrestling), [catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling), [Greco-Roman wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_wrestling), [luta livre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luta_livre), and other styles of [Submission wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_wrestling) are trained to improve ground control and position, as well as to achieve [submission holds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_holds), and defend against them.

Most styles have been adapted from their traditional forms, such as [boxing](4243.md) stances, which lack effective counters to leg kicks, the [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai) stance, which is poor for defending against takedowns due to its static nature and a light front leg, and [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) or [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu) techniques, which must be adapted for no-gi competition and susceptibility to strikes. It is common for a fighter to train with multiple coaches of different styles or an organized fight team to improve various aspects of their game at once. [Cardiovascular conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobic_exercise), speed drills, [strength training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strength_training) and [flexibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexibility_\(anatomy\)) are also important aspects of a fighter's training. Some schools advertise their styles as simply "mixed martial arts", which has become a style in itself, but the training will still often be split into different sections.

While mixed martial arts was initially practiced almost exclusively by competitive fighters, this is no longer the case. As the sport has become more mainstream and more widely taught, it has become accessible to wider range of practitioners of all ages. Proponents of this sort of training argue that it is safe for anyone, of any age, with varying levels of competitiveness and fitness.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

### Brazilian jiu-jitsu

Main article: [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu)

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_Brazilian_jiu-jitsu)

Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ) is a form of [submission grappling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_grappling). It came to international prominence in the martial arts community in the early 1990s, when BJJ expert [Royce Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royce_Gracie) won the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_1), [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_2), and [fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_4) Ultimate Fighting Championships, which at the time were [single-elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-elimination) martial arts tournaments. Royce often fought successfully against larger opponents who practiced other styles, including [boxing](4243.md), [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling), [shoot-fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoot-fighting), [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate), and [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo). It has since become a staple art and key component for many MMA fighters. BJJ is largely credited for bringing widespread attention to the importance of [ground fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_fighting). BJJ is primarily a ground-based fighting style that applies close range [grappling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grappling) techniques and uses [joint locks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_lock) and [chokeholds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chokehold) to submit the adversary. But standup techniques can also be used such as [throws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throw_\(grappling\)), [holds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grappling_hold), and [strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_\(attack\)).

#### Greco-Roman

Main article: [Greco-Roman wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_wrestling)

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing Greco-Roman wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_Greco-Roman_wrestling)

Greco-Roman wrestling is one of two styles of wrestling contested at the Olympic Games, the other being Freestyle. Greco-Roman wrestling only allows for holds above the waist and has a strong emphasis on clinch fighting. Due to the difficulty to achieve takedowns when one is not allowed to attack the legs, Greco-Roman is not utilized in MMA as often as styles that do allow fighters to attack the legs, such as Freestyle and Catch. Despite this, there have been fighters who come from a background in Greco-Roman wrestling. Notable examples are [Randy Couture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Couture), [Dan Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Henderson), [Mark Madsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Madsen_\(fighter\)), [Matt Lindland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Lindland) (all four were Olympic wrestlers or Olympic alternates), [Jon Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Jones), [Dan Severn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Severn), [Ilia Topuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilia_Topuria), [Alexander Volkanovski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Volkanovski), [Magomed Ankalaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magomed_Ankalaev) and [Sergei Pavlovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Pavlovich).

#### Catch-as-catch-can

Main article: [Catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling)

See also: [Category\:Mixed martial artists utilizing catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_catch_wrestling)

[Catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling) is the ancestor of freestyle wrestling and includes submissions which are prohibited in freestyle wrestling.[^cite_note-Catch-126] Widely popular around the world during the 19th and 20th centuries, catch wrestling underwent a decline as its amateur-side became olympic freestyle wrestling, while the professional side became modern [professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling). Catch survived in Japanese *[Puroresu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puroresu)*-style Pro Wrestling, where wrestlers such as [Antonio Inoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Inoki) and [Karl Gotch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Gotch) promoted "strong style pro wrestling", that while [worked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_\(professional_wrestling\)), had realistic and full contact moves, resulting in the creation of the [Universal Wrestling Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Wrestling_Federation_\(Japan\)) and [Shoot wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoot_wrestling) (which in their own turn would inspire the creation of legit proto-MMA [shootfighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shootfighting) organizations such as [Shooto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooto) and [Pancrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancrase)). Many pro wrestlers that trained in shoot-style would later compete in MMA, which led to resurgence of Catch with the advent of mixed martial arts in the 90s. The term *no holds barred* was used originally to describe the wrestling method prevalent in catch wrestling tournaments during the late 19th century wherein no wrestling holds were banned from the competition, regardless of how dangerous they might be. The term was applied to mixed martial arts matches, especially at the advent of the Ultimate Fighting Championship.[^cite_note-127]

A lot of MMA fighters train in catch wrestling as their sole grappling style or as a complement to Brazilian jiu-jitsu, as it teaches techniques and tactics not found in Brazilian jiu-jitsu.[^cite_note-Catch-126] Notable MMA fighters who use catch wrestling as their primary grappling style include: [Josh Barnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Barnett), [Ken Shamrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Shamrock), [Frank Shamrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Shamrock), [Bas Rutten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas_Rutten), [Kazushi Sakuraba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazushi_Sakuraba), [Erik Paulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Paulson), [Bobby Lashley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Lashley), [Minoru Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoru_Suzuki), [Masakatsu Funaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masakatsu_Funaki), [Rumina Sato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumina_Sato), [Masakazu Imanari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masakazu_Imanari), [Muhammad Mokaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Mokaev) and [Paul Sass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Sass).[^cite_note-Catch-126]

#### Muay Thai

Main article: [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai)

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_Muay_Thai)

[Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai) (also known as "Thai boxing") originated in [Thailand](30128.md), and is known as the "art of eight limbs", which refers to the use of the legs, knees, elbows and fists. Muay Thai emphasizes powerful and versatile striking, clinching, balance, footwork, and the use of combinations at both long and close range. Techniques include punches, kicks, knee strikes, elbow strikes, sweeps, and various forms of [clinch fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinch_fighting). Muay Thai became an important component of modern MMA, as the use of striking techniques and clinch work were effective and easily adaptable to the MMA ruleset, while combining it with grappling disciplines.[^cite_note-128] Muay Thai differs from kickboxing by placing greater emphasis on elbows, knees, clinching, and close-range fighting, with the two disciplines also differing in their rulesets, [stances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stance_\(martial_arts\)), [footwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footwork_\(martial_arts\)), and methods of executing techniques.[^cite_note-129] Brazil played a significant role in introducing and developing Muay Thai within MMA. Muay Thai fighters and practitioners were involved in the early [Vale Tudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_Tudo) scene, where they fought against and trained alongside practitioners of other martial arts. [Chute Boxe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chute_Boxe), founded in Curitiba by Rudimar Fedrigo in 1978, became a prominent symbol of this development, as they combined Muay Thai striking with Brazilian jiu-jitsu and other grappling techniques, and later became one of the leading teams in Vale Tudo and MMA.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] Notable fighters who use Muay Thai include [Joanna Jędrzejczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_J%C4%99drzejczyk), [Zhang Weili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Weili), [Edson Barboza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edson_Barboza), [Darren Till](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Till), [Anderson Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Silva), [José Aldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Aldo), [Maurício Rua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maur%C3%ADcio_Rua), [Wanderlei Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanderlei_Silva), [Ciryl Gane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciryl_Gane), [Jiří Procházka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Proch%C3%A1zka), [Marco Ruas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Ruas), [Rafael dos Anjos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_dos_Anjos), [Fabrício Werdum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabr%C3%ADcio_Werdum), [Cris Cyborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cris_Cyborg), [Yair Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Rodr%C3%ADguez), and [Leon Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Edwards).

### Luta Livre

Main article: [Luta Livre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luta_Livre)

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing luta livre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_Luta_Livre)

[Luta Livre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luta_Livre) (also referred to Luta Livre *Brasileira*, *Submission* or *Esportiva*) is a Brazilian [submission wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_wrestling) style, developed in Brazil in the 1920s by [catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling) practitioner [Euclydes "Tatu" Hatem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclydes_Hatem), including techniques from [catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling) and [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu). In 1942, Hatem solidified the art's legitimacy by defeating George Gracie in a historic match.[^cite_note-135] Decades later, in 1968, his student [Euclides Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclides_Pereira) — repeated the feat by defeating [Carlson Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlson_Gracie).[^cite_note-136]

Luta livre is divided in the categories of *esportiva*, which is a form of submission grappling, differentiating from no-gi BJJ with its focus on quick and energetic submissions, and *[vale tudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_tudo)*, which includes strikes both standing up and on the ground in addition to grappling and submissions.[^cite_note-137] Luta livre was important to the development of mixed martial arts, as rivalry between jiu-jitsu and luta livre fueled the [vale tudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_tudo) scene. However, the success of Brazilian jiu-jitsu over luta livre practitioners, especially after the *Desafio: Jiu Jitsu vs Luta Livre* event in 1991 (which was broadcast live by [Rede Globo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rede_Globo)), resulted in the style waning in popularity,[^cite_note-138] although it seems to be making a resurgence, especially as an alternative to both Brazilian jiu-jitsu and catch wrestling.[^cite_note-139] Some notable luta livre practitioners in MMA include: [Marco Ruas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Ruas), [Eugenio Tadeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Tadeu), [José Aldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Aldo), [Renato Sobral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renato_Sobral), [Pedro Rizzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Rizzo), [Alexandre Franca Nogueira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Franca_Nogueira), [Terry Etim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Etim), [Jesus Pinedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Pinedo) and [Darren Till](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Till).

### Judo

Main article: [Judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo)

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_judo)



Fighter performs a [takedown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takedown_\(grappling\)) on his opponent.

Judo is a Japanese grappling martial art which has both *[ne-waza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ne-waza)* (ground grappling) and *[tachi-waza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachi-waza)* (standing grappling), several [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) practitioners have competed in mixed martial arts matches. They use their knowledge in judo for clinching and for doing explosive and fast takedowns which quickly transition into submission holds in the ground. However, judo is traditionally and exclusively trained using the [judogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judogi), as such, many techniques and strategies from judo can not be translated into MMA.[^cite_note-140] Fighters who hold a black belt in judo include [Fedor Emelianenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fedor_Emelianenko), [Marco Ruas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Ruas), [Khabib Nurmagomedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabib_Nurmagomedov), [Islam Makhachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_Makhachev), [Ian Garry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Garry), [Dong Hyun Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Hyun_Kim), [Cub Swanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cub_Swanson), [Don Frye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Frye), [Antônio Rodrigo Nogueira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B4nio_Rodrigo_Nogueira), [Fabricio Werdum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabricio_Werdum), [Vitor Belfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitor_Belfort), [Benoît Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beno%C3%AEt_Saint-Denis), [Merab Dvalishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merab_Dvalishvili), [Reinier de Ridder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinier_de_Ridder) and Olympian judokas [Ronda Rousey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronda_Rousey),[^cite_note-141] [Hector Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hector_Lombard), [Rick Hawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Hawn)[^cite_note-142] and [Hidehiko Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidehiko_Yoshida). Former WEC middleweight champion [Paulo Filho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Filho) has credited judo for his success in an interview.[^cite_note-Paulo_Filho_Youtube_interview-143]

### Sambo

Main article: [Sambo (martial art)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambo_\(martial_art\))

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_sambo)

Sambo is a Russian martial art, combat sport and self-defense system.[^cite_note-144] It is a mixture of [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) and [freestyle wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freestyle_wrestling) using a [keikogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keikogi) known as [kurtka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurtka). Sambo focuses on throwing, takedowns, grappling, and includes submissions from judo and [catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling). Sports sambo is characterized as a grappling style focused in [pinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pin_\(amateur_wrestling\)) and in explosive takedowns which can be quickly transitioned into devastating [leglocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leglock). Sambo also has a modality known as *combat sambo*, which adds punches, kicks, elbows and knees, making it a proto-MMA hybrid fighting style. Sambo is popular in Russia and eastern Europe, where it is taught as a complement to judo and wrestling training, Sambo also provides a good base for MMA with all-around skills for combining grappling and striking. Some notable Sambo fighters that transitioned into MMA include: [Fedor Emelianenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fedor_Emelianenko), [Khabib Nurmagomedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabib_Nurmagomedov), [Islam Makhachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_Makhachev), [Igor Vovchanchyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Vovchanchyn), [Oleg Taktarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Taktarov), [Andrei Arlovski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Arlovski), [Yaroslav Amosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslav_Amosov), and [Shavkat Rakhmonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shavkat_Rakhmonov).

### Karate

Main article: [Karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate)

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_karate)

[Karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate) is a striking-based Japanese with [Okinawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island) origins martial art using punches, kicks, sometimes elbows, knees and even limited grappling. It is divided in [various schools and styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_karate_styles), which distinguishes techniques, training methods, among other things. Some styles, especially [Kyokushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyokushin) and other [full contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_contact_karate) styles, has proven to be effective in MMA as it is one of the core foundations of kickboxing, and specializes in striking techniques.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-karate_UFC-147][^cite_note-148] Karate from all styles has also been a common base, with many getting introduced to martial arts and combat sports by training Karate in their youth. Various styles of karate are practiced by some MMA fighters, notably [Chuck Liddell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Liddell), [Bas Rutten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas_Rutten), [Lyoto Machida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyoto_Machida), [Stephen Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Thompson_\(fighter\)), [John Makdessi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Makdessi), [Uriah Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uriah_Hall), [Erik Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_P%C3%A9rez), [Ryan Jimmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Jimmo), [Georges St-Pierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_St-Pierre), [Kyoji Horiguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoji_Horiguchi), [Giga Chikadze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giga_Chikadze), [Robert Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Whittaker_\(fighter\)), [Henry Cejudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cejudo), and [Louis Gaudinot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Gaudinot). Liddell is known to have an extensive striking background in [Kenpō with Fabio Martella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Kenpo),[^cite_note-149] Lyoto Machida practices [Shotokan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotokan), coming from a lineage of Shotokan practitioners,[^cite_note-150] and St-Pierre practices [Kyokushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyokushin).[^cite_note-knucklepit-151]

### Wushu Sanda

Main article: [Sanshou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanshou)

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing wushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_wushu)

Sanda, or Sanshou, is one of the two disciplines of [sport *wushu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_wushu). It is a modernized and full contact version of *wushu*, created in the late 20th century as a condensation of traditional Chinese [kung fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kung_fu) techniques to be used in a [full contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-contact) competition environment.[^cite_note-Dynasty_Clothing-152][^cite_note-153] It is a kickboxing style which has [punching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_\(combat\)), [kicking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kicking), some use of elbows and [knee strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knee_strikes)—similar to Kickboxing or Muay Thai— but it has the distinction of allowing a range of [takedowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takedown_\(grappling\)), [throws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throw_\(grappling\)) and [sweeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweep_\(martial_arts\)), similar to judo and wrestling.[^cite_note-Dynasty_Clothing-152][^cite_note-Payne-2017-154]

They can be highly effective in competition due to their mixture of striking and takedowns, which can be easily synthesized with the rest of MMA training, such as groundfighting.[^cite_note-Payne-2017-154] It is prominently used by fighters from China, but it has found a following amongst many fighters around the world.[^cite_note-Payne-2017-154] Chief amongst these fighters is [Cung Le](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cung_Le), who is most notable for his TKO and KO victories over former UFC champions Frank Shamrock and Rich Franklin, and UFC strawweight champion [Zhang Weili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Weili), the first Chinese champion in the UFC. Other wushu sanshou based fighters who have entered MMA include [Michael Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Page), [Song Yadong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Yadong), [K. J. Noons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._J._Noons), [Pat Barry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Barry_\(fighter\)), [Zhang Tiequan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Tiequan),[^cite_note-155] [Muslim Salihov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Salihov),[^cite_note-156] and [Zabit Magomedsharipov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabit_Magomedsharipov).[^cite_note-157]

### Taekwondo

Main article: [Taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo)

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_taekwondo)

Taekwondo is a [Korean martial art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_martial_art), emerging in the 1950s as a mixture between Japanese Karate, traditional Korean martial arts and some Chinese kung fu. It is a striking-based style with heavy focus on various styles of kicking, such as head-height kicks, spinning jump kicks, and fast kicking techniques.[^cite_note-158] Several accomplished MMA fighters have an extensive background in taekwondo, and many were introduced to martial arts through it.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160] Some fighters who use taekwondo techniques in MMA are former UFC lightweight champion and WEC lightweight champion [Anthony Pettis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Pettis), who is 3rd dan black belt as well as an instructor,[^cite_note-fightmagazine.com-161] [Benson Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benson_Henderson), [Yair Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Rodriguez), [Marco Ruas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Ruas) and former UFC middleweight champion [Anderson Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Silva), who is a 5th dan black belt.[^cite_note-162]

In his instructional book, Anderson Silva admitted the influence of taekwondo in the formation of his unique style. "In each of my fights, I tried to utilize techniques from all the various styles I had studied. I threw taekwondo kicks. I threw Muay Thai knees and elbows, and I used my knowledge of Brazilian jiu-jitsu on the ground."[^cite_note-SilvaKrauss2008-163] Anthony Pettis has also stated that he is "definitely a traditional martial artist first and a mixed martial artist second",[^cite_note-fightmagazine.com-161] as well as his "style of attacking is different \[because of his] taekwondo background".[^cite_note-164]

Other notable fighters who have a base in Taekwondo or are known for using their Taekwondo skills while fighting include [Edson Barboza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edson_Barboza), [Valentina Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentina_Shevchenko), [Benson Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benson_Henderson), [Yair Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yair_Rodriguez), [Anthony Pettis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Pettis), [Mirko Cro Cop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirko_Cro_Cop), [Cory Sandhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cory_Sandhagen), [Israel Adesanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Adesanya) and [Conor McGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_McGregor).

### Capoeira

Main article: [Capoeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capoeira)

See also: [Mixed martial artists utilizing capoeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_utilizing_capoeira)

Capoeira is an [Afro-Brazilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Brazilian) art form that incorporates elements of martial arts, games, music, and dance. Capoeira is often practiced as a form of dancing and game, but its origins lie as a concealed style of self-defense and combat, and can be used as such. It uses a style of fighting with quick and complex maneuvers, which use power, speed, and leverage across a wide variety of kicks, spins and techniques. Pure Capoeira is difficult to use in MMA due its complexity, but many fighters incorporated individual techniques into their reportoire.[^cite_note-165] Additionally, Capoeira has an importance to MMA history, as many capoeiristas participated in [Vale Tudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_Tudo) challenges in Brazil against practitioners of other martial arts, in particular with a rivalry with Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.[^cite_note-166] In 1995 at the *Desafio Internacional de Vale Tudo* event, Capoeirista Mestre Hulk defeated two-time BJJ world champion [Amaury Bitetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaury_Bitetti) using Capoeira techniques in an underdog victory.[^cite_note-167]

Some fighters which have trained Capoeira and incorporated techniques include [Anderson Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Silva), [Conor McGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_McGregor), [Deiveson Figueiredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deiveson_Figueiredo), [Thiago Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Santos), [Marco Ruas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Ruas) and [Michel Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Pereira).

### Savate

Main article: [Savate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savate)

Although not as common as other disciplines, some fighters have used Savate effectively in MMA. Savate restricts the use of shins and knees, allowing only foot kicks. It focuses on kicking more than punching, and its kicks are characteristically very fast, mobile and flexible. It also possesses a complex and evasive footwork. However, because Savatuers train wearing shoes, adjustments have to be made to how they throw kicks in MMA. Notable Savate fighters include [Karl Amoussou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Amoussou), [Gerard Gordeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Gordeau), [Cheick Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheick_Kongo), and former Bellator Light Heavyweight Champion [Christian M'Pumbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_M'Pumbu).

## Basic strategies

Main page: [Mixed martial artists by strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mixed_martial_artists_by_strategy)

The techniques utilized in mixed martial arts competition generally fall into two categories: [striking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_\(attack\)) techniques (such as [kicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kick), [knees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knee_\(strike\)), [punches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_\(strike\)) and [elbows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbow_\(strike\))) and [grappling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grappling) techniques (such as [clinch holds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinch_hold), [pinning holds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinning_hold), [submission holds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_hold), [sweeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweep_\(grappling\)), [takedowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takedown_\(grappling\)) and [throws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throw_\(grappling\))).

Today, mixed martial artists must [cross-train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-training) in a variety of styles to counter their opponent's strengths and remain effective in all the phases of combat.

### Sprawl-and-Brawl

Main page: [Sprawl-and-brawl fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Sprawl-and-brawl_fighters)

Sprawl-and-Brawl is a [stand-up fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_fighting) tactic that consists of effective stand-up striking while avoiding [ground fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_fighting) typically by using [sprawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprawl_\(grappling\)) to defend against [takedowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takedown_\(grappling\)) or [throws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throw_\(grappling\)).[^cite_note-Kendall_Shields-2010-123]

A Sprawl-and-Brawler is usually a [boxer](4243.md), [kickboxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickbox), or [karateka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate) who has trained in various styles of [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), and/or [sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambo_\(martial_art\)) to avoid takedowns to keep the fight standing. This is a form which is heavily practiced in the amateur leagues.

These fighters will often study [submission grappling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_grappling) to avoid being forced into submission should they find themselves on the ground. This style can be deceptively different from traditional kickboxing styles, since sprawl-and-brawlers must adapt their techniques to incorporate [takedown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takedown_\(grappling\)) and [ground fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_fighting) defense. A few notable examples are [Igor Vovchanchyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Vovchanchyn), [Mirko Filipović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirko_Filipovi%C4%87), [Chuck Liddell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Liddell), [Mark Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Hunt) and more recently [Alex Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Pereira), [Francis Ngannou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ngannou), [Junior dos Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_dos_Santos), [Justin Gaethje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Gaethje), [Andrei Arlovski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Arlovski),[^cite_note-168] and [Joanna Jędrzejczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_J%C4%99drzejczyk).[^cite_note-169]

### Ground-and-pound

Main page: [Ground-and-pound fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Ground-and-pound_fighters)



[Gina Carano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_Carano) applying a ground-and-pound on her opponent

Ground-and-pound is a strategy consisting of taking an opponent to the ground using a [takedown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takedown_\(grappling\)) or throw, obtaining a top, or [dominant grappling position](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grappling_position), and then striking the opponent repeatedly, primarily with fists, [hammerfists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_\(attack\)#Hammerfist), and elbows. Ground-and-pound is also used as a precursor to attempting [submission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_\(combat_sport_term\)) holds.

The style is used by fighters well-versed in submission defense and skilled at takedowns. They take the fight to the ground, maintain a grappling position, and strike until their opponent submits or is [knocked out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocked_out). Although not a traditional style of striking, the effectiveness and reliability of ground-and-pound has made it a popular tactic. It was first demonstrated as an effective technique by [Mark Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Coleman), then popularized by fighters such as [Chael Sonnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chael_Sonnen), [Glover Teixeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glover_Teixeira), [Don Frye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Frye), [Frank Trigg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Trigg), [Jon Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Jones), [Cheick Kongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheick_Kongo), [Mark Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Kerr_\(fighter\)), [Frank Shamrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Shamrock), [Tito Ortiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tito_Ortiz), [Matt Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Hughes_\(fighter\)), [Daniel Cormier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Cormier), [Chris Weidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Weidman), and [Khabib Nurmagomedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabib_Nurmagomedov).[^cite_note-Yahoo-2009-122]

While most fighters use ground-and-pound statically, by way of holding their opponents down and hitting them with short strikes from the top position, a few fighters manage to utilize it dynamically by striking their opponents while changing positions, thus not allowing their opponents to settle once they take them down. [Cain Velasquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cain_Velasquez) is one of the most devastating ground strikers in MMA and is known for continuing to strike his opponents on the ground while transitioning between positions.[^cite_note-170] [Fedor Emelianenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fedor_Emelianenko), considered among the greatest masters of ground-and-pound in MMA history, was the first to demonstrate this dynamic style of striking in transition. He was striking his opponents on the ground while passing [guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_\(grappling\)), or while his opponents were attempting to recover guard.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

In the year 2000, MMA play-by-play commentator [Stephen Quadros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Quadros) coined the popular phrase *lay and pray*. This refers to a situation where a wrestler or grappler keeps another fighter pinned or controlled on the mat, throwing light strikes to avoid a stand up, yet exhibit little urgency to finish the grounded opponent with a knockout or a submission for the majority or entirety of the fight, looking for a decision win through high control time.[^cite_note-bleacherreport.com-173] The implication of "lay and pray" is that after the wrestler/grappler takes the striker down and 'lays' on them to neutralize the opponent's striking weapons, they 'pray' that the referee does not return them to the standing position. This style is considered by many fans as the most boring style of fighting and is highly criticized for intentionally creating non-action, yet it is effective. Some argue that 'lay-and-pray' is justified and that it is the responsibility of the downed fighter to be able to protect themselves from this legitimate technique.[^cite_note-bleacherreport.com-173][^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176] Former [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC) Welterweight champion [Georges St-Pierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_St-Pierre) has been criticized by fans for playing it safe and applying the lay-and-pray tactic in his fights,[^cite_note-177] as has former [Bellator MMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellator_MMA) Welterweight champion [Ben Askren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Askren), who justified the tactic, explaining that championship fights are much harder, as they are five rounds long compared with the usual three.[^cite_note-178]



MMA fighter attempts a [Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_choke)-[Armbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armbar) submission on his opponent.

### Submission-seeking

Submission-seeking is a reference to the strategy of taking an opponent to the ground using a takedown or [throw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throw_\(grappling\)) and then applying a [submission hold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_hold), forcing the opponent to [submit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submission_\(combat_sport_term\)). While grapplers will often work to attain dominant position, some may be more comfortable fighting from other positions (ex. fighters pulling [guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_\(grappling\))). It enable fighters to force opponents into submission through joint locks or chokes. Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ) plays a significant role in MMA submission grappling, with techniques such as armbars, triangles, rear-naked chokes, guillotines, and kimuras being commonly utilized.[^cite_note-179]

If a grappler finds themselves unable to force a takedown, they may resort to *pulling [guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_\(grappling\))*, whereby they voluntarily pull their opponent into a dominant position on the ground.[^cite_note-180] This was one of the first fighting styles that had shown success, popularized by BJJ exponent [Royce Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royce_Gracie) during early UFC events. Submissions are an essential part of many disciplines, most notably [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu), [catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambo_\(martial_art\)), [luta livre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luta_livre) and [shoot wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoot_wrestling). Submission-based styles were popularized in the early UFC events by [Royce Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royce_Gracie) and [Ken Shamrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Shamrock), and were the dominant tactic in the early UFCs. Modern proponents of the submission-seeking style, such as [Demian Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demian_Maia), [Ronaldo Souza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_Souza), [Charles Oliveira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Oliveira), [Ryan Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Hall_\(grappler\)), [Marcin Held](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcin_Held), and [Paul Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Craig) tend to come from a [Brazilian jiu-jitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu) background.[^cite_note-181]

### Clinch-fighting

Clinch-Fighting is a tactic consisting of using a [clinch hold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinch_hold) to prevent the opponent from moving away into more distant striking range, while also attempting takedowns or throws and [striking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_\(attack\)) the opponent using [knees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knee_\(strike\)), [stomps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomp_\(strike\)), [elbows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbow_\(strike\)), and [punches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_\(combat\)). The clinch is often utilized by wrestlers and judokas that have added components of the striking game (typically boxing), and [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai) fighters.

[Ken Shamrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Shamrock) was known for his impressive clinch work when he submitted [Dan Severn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Severn) with a standing guillotine choke at [UFC 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_6).

Wrestlers and judoka may use clinch fighting as a way to neutralize the superior striking skills of a stand-up fighter to prevent takedowns or throws by a superior ground fighter. [Ronda Rousey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronda_Rousey), with her [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) background, is considered a master at initiating throws from the clinch to set up [armbars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armlock).[^cite_note-Marrocco-182]

The [clinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai-clinch) or "plum" of a [Muay Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai) fighter is often used to improve the accuracy of [knees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knee_\(strike\)) and [elbows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbow_\(strike\)) by physically controlling the position of the opponent. [Anderson Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Silva) is well known for his devastating Muay Thai clinch. He defeated UFC middle weight champion [Rich Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Franklin) using the Muay Thai clinch and kneeing Franklin repeatedly to the body and face—breaking Franklin's nose. In their rematch Silva repeated this and won again.[^cite_note-Slack-183]

Other fighters may use the clinch to push their opponent against the cage or ropes, where they can effectively control their opponent's movement and restrict mobility while striking them with punches to the body or stomps also known as *dirty boxing* or "Wall and Maul". [Randy Couture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Couture) used his [Greco-Roman wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_wrestling) background to popularize this style en route to six title reigns in the UFC.[^cite_note-Sllack-184]

### Score-oriented fighting

Score-oriented fighting is a style that is based around trying to win through outscoring their opponent and winning a decision. Usually fighters who adopt this strategy use takedowns only for [scoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Score_\(sport\)), allowing the adversary to stand up and continue the fight. They also want to land clear strikes and control the octagon. In order to win the fight by decision, all score oriented fighters have to have strong defensive techniques and avoid takedowns.[^cite_note-185]

In general, fighters who cannot win fights through lightning offense, or are more suited to win fights in the later rounds or via decision are commonly known as *grinders*. Grinders aim to shut down their opponent's game plan and chip away at them via light strikes, clinching, smothering and ground-and-pound for most of the rounds. Prominent examples of grinders are [Sean Strickland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Strickland), who has a very defensive and stand-up striking focused style, and [Merab Dvalishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merab_Dvalishvili) who has a very aggressive, wrestling focused style.

### Recovery

The ability to actively recover in a fast manner is crucial in MMA training and competitions. Recovery may involve regaining energy, breath and balance. It is integrated into all martial arts strategies. For example, a martial artist needs to try to consciously recover as fast as possible after attempting a punch, a kick or grappling move. If recovery is not achieved to a sufficient extent after these acts of exertion then tiredness will increase and fatigue may set in meaning that their performance suffers. Successful acts of recovery during MMA activities means that higher levels of power, accuracy and coordination can be better sustained.[^cite_note-186]

A martial artist's ability to recover can be aided by training regimes which similarly alternate between intense acts of physical exertion and rapid recovery. For example, in high intensity interval training, 20 seconds of jumping may be alternated with 20 seconds of rest. The increased ability to recover quickly which is gained from such training can be directly beneficial to MMA performance because it transfers effectively to it.[^cite_note-187]

## Women's mixed martial arts

Main article: [Women's mixed martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_mixed_martial_arts)



[Megumi Fujii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megumi_Fujii) (left) facing [Cody Welchlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Welchlin?action=edit\&redlink=1) (right)

While mixed martial arts is primarily a male dominated sport, it does have female athletes. Female competition in Japan includes promotions such as the all-female [Valkyrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkyrie_\(mixed_martial_arts\)), and [Jewels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewels_\(mixed_martial_arts\)) (formerly known as [Smackgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smackgirl)).[^cite_note-188] However historically, there has been only a select few major professional mixed martial arts organizations in the [United States](3434750.md) that invite women to compete. Among those are the [UFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC), [Strikeforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikeforce_\(mixed_martial_arts\)), [Bellator Fighting Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellator_Fighting_Championships), the all female [Invicta Fighting Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invicta_Fighting_Championships), and the now defunct [EliteXC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EliteXC).

There has been a growing awareness of women in mixed martial arts due to popular female fighters and personalities such as [Ronda Rousey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronda_Rousey), [Megumi Fujii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megumi_Fujii), [Miesha Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miesha_Tate), [Cristiane Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristiane_Santos), [Joanna Jędrzejczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_J%C4%99drzejczyk), [Holly Holm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Holm), [Alexa Grasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexa_Grasso) and [Gina Carano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_Carano) among others.

### History

In Japan, female competition has been documented since the mid-1990s. Influenced by female professional wrestling and [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing), the Smackgirl competition was formed in 2001 and became the only major all-female promotion in mixed martial arts. Other early successful Japanese female organizations included [Ladies Legend Pro-Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladies_Legend_Pro-Wrestling), ReMix (a predecessor to Smackgirl), U-Top Tournament, K-Grace, and AX.

Aside from all-female organizations, most major Japanese male dominated promotions have held select female competitions. These have included [DEEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_\(mixed_martial_arts\)), MARS, Gladiator, HEAT, Cage Force, [K-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-1), [Sengoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Victory_Road), [Shooto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooto) (under the name G-Shooto), and [Pancrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancrase) (under the name Pancrase Athena).

In the United States, prior to the success of *[The Ultimate Fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ultimate_Fighter)* reality show that launched mixed martial arts into the mainstream media, there was no major coverage of female competitions. Some early organizations who invited women to compete included, International Fighting Championships, SuperBrawl, [King of the Cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_Cage), Rage in the Cage, Ring of Combat, Bas Rutten Invitational, and HOOKnSHOOT. From the mid-2000s, more coverage came when organizations such as [Strikeforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikeforce_\(mixed_martial_arts\)), [EliteXC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EliteXC), [Bellator Fighting Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellator_Fighting_Championships), and [Shark Fights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_Fights) invited women to compete.

Outside Japan and the United States, female competition is almost exclusively found in minor local promotions. However, in Europe some major organizations have held select female competitions, including [It's Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_Showtime_\(kickboxing\)), Shooto Europe, [Cage Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cage_Warriors), and [M-1 Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_Global).

Following Zuffa's acquisition of Strikeforce in March 2011,[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] the UFC began promoting women's fights, with Ronda Rousey rapidly becoming one of the promotion's biggest draws.[^cite_note-193]

Controversy arose in 2013, when CFA (Championship Fighting Alliance) fighter [Fallon Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallon_Fox) came out as a [transgender](19904525.md) woman. The case became a centerpiece of debates concerning whether it was fair to have a transgender woman compete against [cisgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisgender) women in a contact sport.[^cite_note-194] Neither the UFC nor Invicta FC says they will allow her to fight, and then-UFC Bantamweight champion Ronda Rousey said she would not fight her.[^cite_note-195]

## Amateur mixed martial arts

Amateur Mixed Martial Arts is the [amateur version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_sports) of the Mixed Martial Arts in which participants engage largely or entirely without remuneration.

Amateur MMA is practiced with board shorts and with approved protection gear that includes shin protectors, and amateur [MMA gloves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMA_gloves).

### International Mixed Martial Arts Federation

Main article: [International Mixed Martial Arts Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mixed_Martial_Arts_Federation)

On February 29, 2012, the International Mixed Martial Arts Federation (IMMAF) was set up to bring international structure, development and support to mixed martial arts worldwide.[^cite_note-196] IMMAF launched with support of market leader, the [Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Fighting_Championship_\(UFC\)).[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198] The IMMAF is a non-profit, democratic federation organized according to international federation standards to ensure that MMA as a sport is allowed the same recognition, representation and rights as all other major sports. The IMMAF is registered under Swedish law and is founded on democratic principles, as outlined in their statutes.[^cite_note-199] As of 2025, there were 128 total members.[^cite_note-200]

The IMMAF held its first Amateur World Championships in Las Vegas, US, from June 30 to July 6, 2014.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203]

#### World Mixed Martial Arts Association

World Mixed Martial Arts Association (WMMAA) was founded in 2012 in Monaco by [M-1 Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_Global) commercial promoters and is under the leadership of the President Finkelstein and [Fedor Emelianenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fedor_Emelianenko).[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207] The World MMA Association was an organization that managed and developed mixed martial arts, establishing rules and procedures, hosting MMA competitions.

On October 20, 2013, the first World MMA Championship was held in Saint Petersburg, Russia.[^cite_note-208]

By December 2013, WMMAA had 38 member states representing the sport and registered in accordance with national laws. By 2017, WMMAA had expanded to 83 members: Afghanistan, Albania, Armenia, Austria, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Belgium, Bulgaria, Canada, Croatia, Colombia, Czech Republic, France, Guatemala, Georgia, Greece, India, Iran, Italy, Kazakhstan, Latvia, Lithuania, Macedonia, Moldova, Monaco, Mongolia, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Russia, Senegal, Serbia, Slovakia, South Korea, Spain, Switzerland, Tajikistan, Trinidad and Tobago, Turkey, Ukraine, Uzbekistan, and Venezuela.[^cite_note-209]

The International Mixed Martial Arts Federation and the World Mixed Martial Arts Association announced an amalgamation on April 11, 2018, uniting the two organizations behind one bid for Olympic sport recognition after being instructed by Global Association of International Sport Federations (GAISF). The WMMAA and the IMMAF signed a legally binding affiliation [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) (MOU) in May 2018 and finalized the agreement by November 2018, along with the first unified world championships.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211]

Under the [International Mixed Martial Arts Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mixed_Martial_Arts_Federation) (IMMAF) and World MMA Association (WMMAA), it is practiced within a safe and regulated environment which relies on a fair and objective scoring system and competition procedures similar to those in force in the [professional Mixed Martial Arts rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_martial_arts_rules).[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213]

### Global Association of Mixed Martial Arts (GAMMA)



GAMMA medallists on the podium at the 2023 African Games

The Global Association of Mixed Martial Arts (GAMMA) was founded in Rome through Paolo Biotti's initiative on July 17, 2018, as the international governing body for mixed martial arts.[^cite_note-Levy-2019-214][^cite_note-Taylor-2019-215]

GAMMA is a non-profit Federation headquartered in Lausanne (Switzerland), and chaired by Tommy Paulus Hermawan.[^cite_note-Executive_Board-216] GAMMA has 80 national federations as members across 5 continents[^cite_note-Executive_Board-216] and organises international, nations tournaments around the world under amateur MMA rules.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220]

It aims are to achieve official international sport recognition for mixed martial arts, to develop the sport and its governance structures globally and promote its growth and benefits in communities worldwide. Its vision is Olympic Games inclusion.[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222][^cite_note-insidethegames.biz-2024-223]

From 8 to 12 March 2024, mixed martial arts was included as a demonstration sport in the 2023 African Games in Accra, Ghana, under GAMMA.[^cite_note-insidethegames.biz-2024-223][^cite_note-msn.com-224][^cite_note-Redaction-2024-225][^cite_note-Akakpo-2024-226] From 11 to 13 July 2024, GAMMA member federations participated in the 2nd Asian Mixed Martial Arts Championships organised by the Asian Mixed Martial Arts Association (AMMA) under the Olympic Council of Asia.[^cite_note-227]

### Asian Mixed Martial Arts Association/Federation of International Mixed Martial Arts

The Asian Mixed Martial Arts Association was founded in September 2022 and is recognised by the Olympic Council of Asia as the official governing body of MMA.[^cite_note-228]

On November 2025, seeking the olympic inclusion of MMA, founded the Federation of International Mixed Martial Arts as international governing body.[^cite_note-229]

It achieved the inclusion of mixed martial arts as a medal sport in the [2026 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_martial_arts_at_the_2026_Asian_Games).[^cite_note-\:0-230][^cite_note-\:1-231][^cite_note-\:2-232]

### United World Wrestling (UWW)

[United World Wrestling](6387827.md)[^cite_note-233] promotes amateur MMA with their own set of rules, with matches held on open mats, similar to how the Asian Mixed Martial Arts Association does it.

## Safety



A ring-side doctor attends to a fighter following a loss.

Mixed Martial Arts competitions have changed dramatically since the first Ultimate Fighting Championship in 1993, specifically with the inception of the [Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_martial_arts_rules#Unified_rules). There remains a paucity of data on injuries that occur in MMA, and resulting concerns with regard to MMA's safety remain. A 2014 systematic review concluded that the injury incidence rate in MMA appears to be greater than in most, if not all, other popular and commonly practiced combat sports.[^cite_note-Lystad,_R.P._2014-234]

### Injury rates

In a 2014 meta-analysis of the available injury data in MMA, the injury incidence rate was estimated to be 228.7 injuries per 1000 athlete-exposures (one athlete-exposure is defined as one athlete participating in a single fight).[^cite_note-Lystad,_R.P._2014-234] The estimated injury incidence rate in MMA is greater than in other full-contact combat sports such as [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) (44.0 injuries per 1000 athlete-exposures),[^cite_note-Frey,_A._2004-235] [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo) (79.4 injuries per 1000 athlete-exposures),[^cite_note-Lystad,_R.P._2009-236] [amateur boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_boxing) (77.7 injuries per 1000 athlete-exposures),[^cite_note-Estwanik,_J.J._1984-237] and [professional boxing](4283645.md) (118.0–250.6 injuries per 1000 athlete-exposures).[^cite_note-Bledsloe,_G.H._2005-238][^cite_note-Zazryn,_T.R._2003-239][^cite_note-Zazryn,_T.R._2006-240][^cite_note-Zazryn,_T.R._2009-241]

### Injury pattern

In general, the injury pattern in MMA is very similar to that in professional boxing but unlike that found in other combat sports such as judo and taekwondo.[^cite_note-Lystad,_R.P._2014-234] The most commonly injured body region is the head (66.8% to 78.0% of reported injuries) followed by the wrist/hand (6.0% to 12.0% of reported injuries), while the most frequent types of injury were laceration (36.7% to 59.4% of reported injuries), fracture (7.4% to 43.3% of reported injuries), and concussion (3.8% to 20.4% of reported injuries).[^cite_note-Lystad,_R.P._2014-234] The frequency of impact to the ear and low utilization of ear protection leads to a high frequency of perichondral hematoma that can lead to [cauliflower ear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauliflower_ear).[^cite_note-242]

### Brain injury and CTE

See also: [Chronic traumatic encephalopathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_traumatic_encephalopathy)

The repeated head strikes received by MMA competitors causes frequent concussions and permanent [brain damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_damage).[^cite_note-243] Also, the repeated head trauma in contact sports, especially combat sports, are directly related to a brain disease called [chronic traumatic encephalopathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_traumatic_encephalopathy) (CTE). This disease begins to develop during the life of the athlete, and continues to develop even after sports activity has ceased.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249]

Because the disease is detected post-mortem, and MMA as a sport is relatively young, there are still few cases of CTE recognized in MMA, although there are more and more suspected and confirmed cases of CTE.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251] In preliminary results reported in April 2012 as part of an ongoing study of a 109 professional boxers and MMA fighters being conducted by Charles Bernick and his colleagues at [Cleveland Clinic's Lou Ruvo Center for Brain Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Clinic_Lou_Ruvo_Center_for_Brain_Health), fighters with more than six years of ring experience were observed to have reductions in size in their [hippocampus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocampus) and [thalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalamus), while fighters with more than twelve years of ring experience were observed to have both reductions in size and symptoms such as [memory loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_loss) (the hippocampus and thalamus deal with memory and alertness). Bernick speculates that the cumulative damage over time from lesser blows may eventually prove an even more important topic of study than that of infrequent concussions.[^cite_note-252]

In January 2021, veteran UFC fighter [Spencer Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Fisher) confirmed to MMA Fighting that he suffered from all the symptoms of CTE: "I'm just beat up, and it's just gotten worse, like all my injuries are catching up with me now, aside from the brain thing, which is the biggest one, because it adds, it adds the depression, and putting things, thoughts together and staying on track." Later, [Dana White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_White) ruled on this: "He's not the first and he's definitely not going to be the last. This is a contact sport and anybody who's ever done this younger, myself included, is dealing with brain issues. It's part of the gig."[^cite_note-253] In late 2021, Rose Gracie, daughter of UFC founder [Rorion Gracie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rorion_Gracie), in an interview about CTE on the *Trocação Franca* podcast, called the lack of CTE awareness in MMA 'criminal negligence'.[^cite_note-254]

### Fatalities

Main article: [Fatalities in mixed martial arts contests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatalities_in_mixed_martial_arts_contests)

There have been seven known deaths in MMA to date.[^cite_note-martialhistory-255] There were no documented cases of deaths after a sanctioned MMA event prior to 2007.[^cite_note-Houston_Vasquez-256]

Since 2007, there have been six fatalities in mixed martial arts matches. The first was the death of [Sam Vasquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Vasquez) on November 30, 2007.[^cite_note-Vasquez_death-257] Vasquez collapsed shortly after being knocked out by Vince Libardi in the third round of an October 20, 2007, fight at the [Toyota Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Center) in Houston, Texas.[^cite_note-Houston_Vasquez-256] Vasquez had two separate surgeries to remove blood clots from his brain, and shortly after the second operation suffered a stroke and did not regain consciousness.[^cite_note-Vasquez_death-257]

The second death stemming from a sanctioned mixed martial arts contest occurred in South Carolina on June 28, 2010, when 30-year-old Michael Kirkham was knocked out and never regained consciousness. He was pronounced dead two days after the fight.[^cite_note-258]

The third death on August 11, 2012, involved 30 year old Tyrone Mims, who was making his amateur MMA debut at "Conflict MMA: Fight Night at the Point VI" in South Carolina, making his the second MMA-related death in the state.[^cite_note-259] After being TKO'd in the second round of the fight he became unresponsive and was taken to Medical University Hospital, where he was pronounced dead one hour later. No evidence of brain trauma or concussion was found and the initial autopsy has proved inconclusive. Coroner Rae Wooten explained that his death may probably have been from an irregular heartbeat caused by over exertion; however whether or not his death was a direct result of his fight remains a mystery.[^cite_note-260]

On February 27, 2014, 29-year-old Booto Guylain was transported to Johannesburg General Hospital to be treated for swelling and bleeding on the brain after suffering a KO loss via elbow in the last round of his fight in South African promotion "Extreme Fighting Championship Africa". He was unable to make a recovery, and after one week in the hospital he was pronounced dead.[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262]

On April 9, 2016, 28-year-old [João Carvalho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Carvalho_\(fighter\)), died following a Total Extreme Fighting event at the National Stadium in Dublin (Ireland). The Portuguese fighter was beaten by technical knockout in a fight on 9 April 2016 against [Charlie Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Ward_\(fighter\)), and fell ill twenty minutes after the fight. He was taken immediately to Beaumont hospital, where he underwent emergency brain surgery, but died two days later.[^cite_note-263]

On July 15, 2017, after a TKO loss resulting from unanswered punches in the second round of his fight, 37 year old Donshay White became unresponsive and collapsed in his locker room and was rushed to the KentuckyOne Health Sts. Mary & Elizabeth Hospital in Kentucky.[^cite_note-264] He was soon pronounced dead and was revealed to be caused by hypertensive/atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.[^cite_note-265]

## Legality of professional competitions

Main article: [Legality of professional MMA competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legality_of_professional_MMA_competitions)

Professional MMA competitions enjoy different levels of legality in different countries. This is dependent on the laws of the specific state.

The [International Mixed Martial Arts Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mixed_Martial_Arts_Federation) attempts to provide an internationally recognised set of standards which will be universally adhered to.[^cite_note-266] However, they have no basis in national law and are meant as advisement only. Whereby regulation exists, however, the likelihood of MMA being legally recognised as a legitimate sport is increased. This was the case with France who banned it as a sport in 2016, due to a lack of regulation, then unbanned it in 2020 after a French federation began regulating it.[^cite_note-267]
