The **Humboldt–Toiyabe National Forest** (**HTNF**) is the principal [U.S. national forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_forest_\(United_States\)) in the U.S. state of [Nevada](21216.md), and has a smaller portion further west in [Eastern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_California) in the state of California. With an area of 6,289,821 acres (25,454.00 km<sup>2</sup>), it is the [largest U.S. national forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_forests_of_the_United_States) reservation outside of [Alaska](624.md).

## History

### Native Americans and European settlement

Between 12 and 13,000 years ago, [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) in central Nevada, including the area of Toiyabe Crest in Toiyabe National Forest. Populations of [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Paiute) and [Southern Paiute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Paiute), [Western Shoshone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Shoshone), [Washoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_people), [Chemehuevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemehuevi), and [Colorado River Yuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_Yuman?action=edit\&redlink=1) have been confirmed. Native people depended on [Pleistocene megafauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene_extinctions) and [water fowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatidae), which was found in marshy areas between high [mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range). They would migrate to higher elevations during the [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) in permitting summer conditions; abandoned seasonal settlements have been found at up to 12,000 feet (3,700 m) above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level). Higher elevations of the [Toiyabe Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toiyabe_Range) offered hunting opportunities for [deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer) and [bighorn sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bighorn_sheep). Middle ranges presented similar hunting opportunities and gathering [pinyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyon_pine) seeds.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-pv1-5] Archaeological and cultural evidence suggest Native American populations in Nevada 10,000 years ago. The [Western Shoshone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Shoshone), Northern Paiute, and descendent [Ancient Puebloans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Puebloans) tribes have relied on the land in and around Humboldt National Forest, primarily for [hunting and gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer). The Northern Paiutes lived a [nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad) lifestyle, residing in higher elevations in summer and lower elevations in winter.[^cite_note-pv2-6]

[European settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) reached the [Great Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basin) and [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada) as early as the [17th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_century). In 1833, [fur trapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) [Benjamin Bonneville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Bonneville) established two major trails: one following the [Humboldt River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_River) throughout Nevada and one over [Walker Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker_Pass) in California that traversed the Sierra Nevada. The latter would eventually become the [California Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Trail),[^cite_note-pv1-5] which would bring [gold prospectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_prospector) through the Great Basin. European settlers began permanently settling in the 1840s and, in the ensuing decades, there were several conflicts between settlers and Native Americans.[^cite_note-pv2-6] Native populations subsequently dwindled from the conflict and disease, namely [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox). Once gold mines were depleted, mining attention turned to other parts of the Great Basin. It is estimated there are 80,000 historical or archaeological sights in the forest land.[^cite_note-pv1-5]

### Forest mergers and establishment

The lands now part of Humboldt–Toiyabe have been administratively reorganized many times, and include parts of several defunct national forests. [Ruby Mountains National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_Mountains_National_Forest) was established on May 3, 1906 by [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt). [Independence National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_National_Forest) was established on November 5 that same year. On March 2, 1907, Toiyabe National Forest was created. On July 1, 1908, [Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_National_Forest) and [Toquima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toquima_National_Forest) National Forests were added, followed by [Moapa National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moapa_National_Forest) on July 15, 1915. In 1932, Toiyabe National Forest was merged into [Nevada National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_National_Forest); the name was discontinued. Toiyabe National Forest was reestablished in May 1938 from portions of the Humboldt and Nevada reserves. [Mono National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mono_National_Forest) was divided between Toiyabe and [Inyo National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inyo_National_Forest) in 1945, and Nevada National Forest was split between Humboldt and Toiyabe National Forests on October 1, 1957.[^cite_note-7] The [Mount Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Charleston) Division, which was originally part of Nevada National Forest and was transferred to [Dixie National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_National_Forest) in 1916, was re–designated as part of Toiyabe National Forest.[^cite_note-pv1-5]

Humboldt and Toiyabe National Forests were administratively joined almost four decades later in 1995 during President [Bill Clinton](3356.md)'s administration. Although managed since as a single entity, the two forests remain legally and geographically separated.[^cite_note-8]

## Geography



[Lamoille Canyon Scenic Byway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamoille_Canyon_Road)

HTNF is unlike most other national forests as it contains numerous non–contiguous sections scattered across Nevada and [eastern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_California).[^cite_note-9] Six [park ranger](499846.md) district equivalents are located in Nevada and one lies in California.[^cite_note-10] In Nevada, the reservation spans from [Mountain City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_City,_Nevada) Ranger District is Northern Nevada to the [Spring Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Mountains) in [Southern Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Nevada) and west from the [Lake Tahoe Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tahoe_Basin) east to [Mount Moriah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Moriah_\(Nevada\));[^cite_note-tn-11] in California, it lies on the slopes of the eastern Sierra Nevada.[^cite_note-sngt1-12] The forest is headquartered in [Sparks, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparks,_Nevada).[^cite_note-tn-11]

### Humboldt National Forest section

The smaller and more northeasterly Humboldt National Forest is located in eastern and northern Nevada, in parts of Elko, White Pine, Humboldt, Nye, and [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_County,_Nevada) counties, and contains about 43.5% of the total area. This section of the national forest is named after [Alexander von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt) (1769–1859), the famous 18th- and 19th-century [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) / [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Confederation) multi-talented scientist and philosopher. Born, raised, and educated in Europe, Humboldt traveled extensively in the Americas / [Western Hemisphere](73162.md) in 1799–1804, exploring and conducting scientific research in various fields.[^cite_note-13] Local ranger district offices are located in four towns: [Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ely,_Nevada), [Elko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elko,_Nevada), [Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells,_Nevada), and [Winnemucca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnemucca,_Nevada) of Nevada.

### Toiyabe National Forest section

The larger and more southwesterly Toiyabe National Forest is located in central, western, and southern Nevada and eastern California, in parts of [Nye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nye_County,_Nevada), [Lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lander_County,_Nevada), [Mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_County,_Nevada), [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_County,_Nevada), [Eureka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_County,_Nevada), [Washoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washoe_County,_Nevada), [Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_County,_Nevada), and [Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Nevada) counties, and the [state capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capital) of [Carson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_City,_Nevada) in the "Battle Born State" of [Nevada](21216.md); as well as in the adjacent "Golden State" with [Mono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mono_County,_California), [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_County,_California), [Sierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_County,_California), [Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_County,_California), [Lassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassen_County,_California), and [El Dorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Dorado_County,_California) counties in the state of California. *Toiyabe* is a Native American name.[^cite_note-14] The section contains about 56.5% of the total area. Local ranger district offices are located in the towns / cities of [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Nevada), [Bridgeport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeport,_California), [Carson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_City,_Nevada), [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas,_Nevada), and [Tonopah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonopah,_Nevada). Bridgeport is the only station that is in California.
