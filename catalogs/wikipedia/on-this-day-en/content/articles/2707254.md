The **1500 metres** or **1500-metre run** is the foremost [middle-distance track event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-distance_track_event) in [athletics](43719.md). The distance has been contested at the [Summer Olympics](27566.md) since 1896 and the [World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Championships_in_Athletics) since 1983. It is equivalent to 1.5 kilometres or approximately 15⁄16 miles. The event is closely associated with its slightly longer variant, the [mile run](5227492.md), from which it derives its nickname "the metric mile".[^cite_note-1]

The demands of the race are similar to that of the [800 metre run](585165.md), but with a slightly higher emphasis on aerobic endurance and a slightly lower sprint speed requirement. The 1500-metre run is predominantly [aerobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobic_exercise), but [anaerobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaerobic_exercise) conditioning is also required.[^cite_note-2]



Athletes competing in the 2024 men's Olympic final

Each lap run during the men's world-record race of 3\:26.00, run by [Hicham El Guerrouj](492488.md) of Morocco in 1998, averaged just under 55 seconds per lap. Since El Guerrouj, only three other men in history have broken the 3\:27 barrier; [Bernard Lagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lagat), [Asbel Kiprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asbel_Kiprop), and [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen). El Guerrouj remains the only man to break the 3\:27 barrier more than once, having done so five times.[^cite_note-3]

1500 metres is three and three-quarter laps around a 400-metre track (or seven and a half laps around an indoor 200 m track). During the 1970s and 1980s this race was dominated by British runners, along with an occasional Finn, American, or New Zealander. Through the 1990s, many African runners began to win Olympic medals in this race, especially runners from [Kenya](188171.md), [Ethiopia](187749.md), and East Africa, as well as North African runners from [Morocco](19291.md) and [Algeria](358.md). In the mid-2010s and 2020s, European and American runners began to emerge again in the men's event. American [Matthew Centrowitz Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Centrowitz_Jr.) won at the [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres). In the [2020 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen), the youngest of a dynasty of Norwegian middle-distance runners, became Olympic champion, while Scottish and British runner [Jake Wightman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Wightman) became world champions the following year at the head of an all-European podium. Wightman's compatriot [Josh Kerr](54894149.md) won at the world championships the year after. In the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_1500_metres), Americans and Europeans continued to dominate the podium, with [Cole Hocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cole_Hocker), Kerr, and [Yared Nuguse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yared_Nuguse) earning gold, silver, and bronze respectively. [Faith Kipyegon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Kipyegon) of Kenya maintained Africa's grip on the global titles in the female event in the same time period, although here again, Europeans [Sifan Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sifan_Hassan) and [Laura Muir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Muir), and Americans such as [Jenny Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Simpson) also contended for the podium, along with Australian [Jessica Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Hull).



[Olavi Salsola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olavi_Salsola), [Olavi Salonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olavi_Salonen) and [Olavi Vuorisalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olavi_Vuorisalo) (*The three Olavis*) break the 1500 m [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_records_in_athletics) in 1957 in [Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku), Finland.

In the [Modern Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Olympic_Games), the men's 1500-metre run has been contested since the [1896 Games](48644.md). The first winner, in 1896, was [Edwin Flack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Flack) of Australia, who also became Olympic champion in the 800-metre race. The women's 1500-metre race was first added to the [Summer Olympics](27566.md) in 1972, and the first champion was [Lyudmila Bragina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyudmila_Bragina) of the Soviet Union. During the Olympic Games of 1972 through 2008, the women's 1500-metre race has been won by three [Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) plus one Russian, one Italian, one Romanian, one Briton, one [Kenyan](188171.md), and two [Algerians](358.md). The 2012 Olympic results are still undecided as a result of multiple [doping](860860.md) cases. The best women's times for the race were controversially[^cite_note-4] set by Chinese runners, all set in the same race on just two dates four years apart at the [Chinese National Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Games_of_China). At least one of those top Chinese athletes has admitted to being part of a doping program.[^cite_note-5] This women's record was finally broken by [Genzebe Dibaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genzebe_Dibaba) of Ethiopia in 2015.

In American [high schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_schools), the [1600-metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1600_metres) run, also colloquially referred to as "[metric mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_mile)", is the designated official distance by the [National Governing Body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_governing_body) the [NFHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Federation_of_State_High_School_Associations). Because of the legacy, since [US customary units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_customary_units) are better-known in America, the [mile run](5227492.md) (which is 1609.344 metres in length) is more frequently run than the 1500-metre run. For convenience, national rankings are standardized by converting all 1500-metre run times to their mile run equivalents.[^cite_note-6]

## Strategy

Many 1500 metres events, particularly at the championship level, turn into slow, strategic races, with the pace quickening and competitors jockeying for position in the final lap to settle the race in a final sprint. Such is the difficulty of maintaining the pace throughout the duration of the event, most records are set in planned races led by [pacemakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacemaker_\(running\)) or "rabbits" who sacrifice their opportunity to win by leading the early laps at a fast pace before dropping out.

> The person who wins the race is behind watching.

— [Filbert Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filbert_Bayi), former world record holder[^cite_note-7]

## Other sports

In swimming, the 1500 m (1640 yd) race is commonly referred to as "the swimmer's mile", and is often the longest distance swum by competitors in a pool. The standard distance triathlon also employs the swimmer's mile, except that it is in open water instead of in a pool. 1500 metres is also an event in [speed skating](28028.md) and [wheelchair racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelchair_racing).

The world records for the distance in swimming for men are 14\:31.02 (swum in a 50-metre pool) by [Sun Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yang), 14\:08.06 (swum in a 25-metre pool) by [Gregorio Paltrinieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorio_Paltrinieri); and by women 15\:20.48 (swum in a 50-metre pool)[^cite_note-65] by [Katie Ledecky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Ledecky), and 15\:19.71 (swum in a 25-metre pool) by [Mireia Belmonte García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mireia_Belmonte_Garc%C3%ADa).

The [world records](3157211.md) for the distance in [speed skating](28028.md) are 1\:40.17 by [Kjeld Nuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kjeld_Nuis) and 1\:49.83 by [Miho Takagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miho_Takagi_\(speed_skater\)).

The [records for wheelchair racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_IPC_world_records_in_athletics) vary by disability classification:

- [T51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T51_\(classification\)): 4\:53.50 by [Hélder Mestre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9lder_Mestre?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- [T52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T52_\(classification\)): 3\:29.79 by [Raymond Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Martin_\(wheelchair_athlete\))

- [T53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T53_\(classification\)) and [T54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T54_\(classification\)): 2\:51.84 by [Brent Lakatos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brent_Lakatos)
