**Timiș** (Romanian pronunciation: [\[ˈtimiʃ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Romanian)) is a county (*[județ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude%C8%9B)*) of western [Romania](25445.md) on the border with [Hungary](13275.md) and [Serbia](29265.md), in the [historical region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_regions_of_Romania) of [Banat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat), with the county seat at [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara). It is the westernmost and the largest [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Romania) in Romania in terms of land area. The county is also part of the [Danube–Criș–Mureș–Tisa Euroregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube%E2%80%93Cri%C8%99%E2%80%93Mure%C8%99%E2%80%93Tisa_Euroregion).

## Name

The name of the county comes from the [Timiș River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99_\(river\)), known in Roman antiquity as *Tibisis* or *Tibiscus*. According to Lajos Kiss' [etymological dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymological_dictionary), the name of the river probably comes from the [Dacian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacian_language): *thibh-isjo* ("marshy").[^cite_note-5] In [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language), Timiș County is known as *Temes megye*, in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) as *Kreis Temesch*, in [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language) as Тамишки округ/*Tamiški okrug*, in [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language) as *Tamiška županija*, in [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) as Тімішський повіт, and in [Banat Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat_Bulgarian_dialect) as *okrug Timiš*.

Timiș is the largest county in Romania, occupying 8,696.7 km<sup>2</sup>, i.e. 3.65% of the country's area.[^cite_note-bnr-6]<sup>: 3 </sup> It is crossed by the [46th parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/46th_parallel_north), the [21st meridian east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_meridian_east) and the [22nd meridian east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_meridian_east). On its territory is the westernmost point of Romania, i.e. [Beba Veche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beba_Veche), at 20° 15′ 44″, in the *[Triplex Confinium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_extreme_points_of_Romania)* point.[^cite_note-7]

Timiș County is part of the [West Development Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vest_\(development_region\)) along with the counties of [Arad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arad_County), [Caraș-Severin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara%C8%99-Severin_County) and [Hunedoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunedoara_County), being located in the center of the historical province of [Banat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat). Due to its geographical position, almost a third of the county's borders are at the same time state borders. Thus, in the northwestern part, it borders [Csongrád-Csanád County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csongr%C3%A1d-Csan%C3%A1d_County) ([Hungary](13275.md)), 18 km of this border being on the [Mureș River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mure%C8%99_River). To the southwest, between [Beba Veche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beba_Veche) and [Lățunaș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamu_Mare), Timiș County borders [Serbia](29265.md). The land connection with the counties of the neighboring countries is ensured by the border crossing points from [Cenad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenad), [Moravița](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravi%C8%9Ba) and [Jimbolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimbolia). The neighboring Romanian counties with Timiș County are [Arad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arad_County) to the north, [Hunedoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunedoara_County) to the east and [Caraș-Severin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara%C8%99-Severin_County) to the southeast.[^cite_note-strategie-1-8]<sup>: 20 </sup>

### Relief



Physical map of Timiș County

The relief is characterized by the predominance of [plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain), which cover the western part (low plain) and the central part (high plain) of the county. Timiș County features all [landforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landform), with altitudes between 75 m in Banat Plain and 1,374 m in [Poiana Ruscă Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poiana_Rusc%C4%83_Mountains). Proportionally, the plain covers about 6,700 km<sup>2</sup>, representing 77.2% of the county's area; the hills cover about 1,650 km<sup>2</sup>, i.e. 19.01%; and the mountains cover a relatively small area of 300 km<sup>2</sup>, i.e. 3.45%.[^cite_note-bnr-6]<sup>: 3 </sup>

The plain penetrates in a gulf-like manner the hilly areas, on the valleys of [Timiș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99_\(river\)) (towards [Lugoj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugoj)) and [Bega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bega_\(Tisza\)) (towards [Făget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C4%83get)).[^cite_note-9] In the east of the county lie the pre-mountain hills of Buziaș and the southern sector of the Lipova Plateau, whose peaks rise to altitudes between 200 and 400 m. The hills of Lăpugiu, Făget, Lugoj, Lipova, Silagiu and Sacoș are rich in pastures, orchards of fruit trees, vineyards, but also in cereal crops. The relief in the eastern part of the county is dominated by the Poiana Ruscă Mountains, with deep valleys and steep slopes, with heights that rarely exceed 1300 m (Padeș peak – 1374 m, Rusca peak – 1355 m).[^cite_note-barbu-10]<sup>: 8–9 </sup>

### Natural resources

Located in the southeastern part of the [Pannonian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Plain), the subsoil of Timiș County there are deposits of [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite) ([Sinersig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boldur)), [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) ([Lucareț](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brestov%C4%83%C8%9B)–[Șanovița](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghizela)), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese) ([Pietroasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietroasa,_Timi%C8%99)), [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) ([Biled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biled), [Cărpiniș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83rpini%C8%99), [Jimbolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimbolia), [Lugoj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugoj) and [Sânnicolau Mare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A2nnicolau_Mare)), [crude oil](23195.md) and [gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas) (in the western part of the county), [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand) ([Șag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C8%98ag)) and glass sand ([Groși](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margina)–[Făget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C4%83get), [Tomești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tome%C8%99ti,_Timi%C8%99) and [Gladna Montană](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A2rdea)).[^cite_note-bnr-6]<sup>: 4 </sup> Mineral waters are also exploited in [Buziaș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzia%C8%99), [Călacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Or%C8%9Bi%C8%99oara), [Ivanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulv%C4%83z), [Bogda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogda) and [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara). The soil of Timiș County offers extremely favorable conditions for the cultivation of agricultural plants, especially for the cultivation of cereals, but also for technical and fodder plants, as well as for fruit growing and [viticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viticulture). The forests are relatively few. In the eastern part there are forests of [fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fir), [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce) and [beech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beech). In the other parts of the county there are small forests of [oak](39696.md), [Austrian oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_cerris), [Hungarian oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_frainetto), and in the meadows – [poplars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populus) and [willows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow).[^cite_note-11]

### Hydrography



[Bega River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bega_\(Tisza\)) in [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara)

The hydrographic network of Timiș County, spread over 3,104 km, is composed of two hydrographic basins: Bega–Timiș–Caraș and Mureș. The most important rivers in size and hydropower potential in the county, which cross the territory through the center, dividing it into two halves, south and north, are [Timiș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99_\(river\)) and [Bega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bega_\(Tisza\)). Timiș is the largest inland river in Banat, which has its sources on the eastern slopes of the Semenic Mountains, in [Caraș-Severin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara%C8%99-Severin_County). The river is formed at the confluence of three branches: Semenic, Grădiște and Brebu. It crosses the whole of Timiș County, then passes into Serbia where it flows into the [Danube](21209639.md), to [Pančevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan%C4%8Devo). Bega River springs from the Poiana Ruscă Mountains and flows into the [Tisza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisza) after a route of 244 km, being its southernmost tributary. Downstream, towards the western edge, up to the border, the course of the Bega River is completely canalized. The [Bega Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bega_Canal) is navigable both in Romania and in Serbia, having a length of 44 km only in Romania.



[Lake Surduc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Surduc)

The natural lakes, with small areas and depths, are represented by the lake complex of [Satchinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satchinez) (40 ha; 1.5 m deep), the two lakes of [Becicherecu Mic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Becicherecu_Mic) (one of 33 ha and another of 13 ha), the Petra pond of [Jebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel,_Timi%C8%99), etc. Between 1969 and 1975, pond-like lakes for [flood control](20913655.md), [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) and [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing) were arranged on the courses of both Timiș and Bega. The largest of these, [Lake Surduc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Surduc), was arranged on [Gladna stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladna), a tributary of the Bega, and has an area of 460 ha and a volume of 51 million m<sup>3</sup>.[^cite_note-12] In the county there are also two lakes with hot (20 °C) and mineral water at [Românești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tome%C8%99ti,_Timi%C8%99), with an area of 50 m<sup>2</sup> and the muddy volcano from [Ohaba-Forgaci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boldur), in the Magheruș valley, of 50 m<sup>2</sup>.

The high density of the hydrographic network, the low slopes of the riverbeds in the plain sector, the reduced depth of the groundwater, the presence of [ultisols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultisol), all favor the floods and marshes. To reduce the amplitude of these phenomena, since the first half of the 18teenth century, large engineering works (damming, canalization, draining etc.) were made in the region, most of which are still functional today. Among the major [flash floods](244391.md) and [floods](50482.md) in the Banat Plain, the most important were those of May 1912, June 1966 and [April 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_European_floods).[^cite_note-13]

### Flora

The natural vegetation, strongly influenced by human activities, is characterized by the small-scale presence of [forest-steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest-steppe) plants and by a higher frequency of hydro- and hygrophilous vegetation in low plains and meadows with excess moisture. The extensive agricultural crops in the plain alternate with small areas of secondary meadows dominated by fescue associations (*[Festuca valesiaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festuca_valesiaca)*, *[Festuca rupicola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festuca_rupicola)* and *[Festuca pseudovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festuca)*) mixed with wormwood (*[Artemisia austriaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemisia_austriaca)*) and others. The deciduous forests, made up of pedunculate oak (*[Quercus robur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_robur)*), Austrian oak (*[Quercus cerris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_cerris)*) and Hungarian oak (*[Quercus frainetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_frainetto)*), occupy insularly or on compact areas the Lipova Plateau, the Lugoj and Pogăniș Hills and partly the Gătaia Plain. In the eastern extremity of Timiș County there are sessile oak forests (*[Quercus petraea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_petraea)*) mixed with Austrian oak (*[Quercus cerris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_cerris)*), beech forests (*[Fagus sylvatica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fagus_sylvatica)*) mixed with hornbeam (*[Carpinus betulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpinus_betulus)*), and the upper slopes of the Poiana Ruscă Mountains are covered with [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce) forests mixed with [fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fir) and [beech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beech).

In Timiș County there are 45 [nature reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve) that total 134,766.49 ha or about 13% of the county's area.[^cite_note-14]

### Fauna

The fauna, adapted to the relief and vegetation conditions, includes steppe species ([hares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_hare), [ground squirrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermophilus), [polecats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_polecat), [field mice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apodemus), [moles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_mole), [rollers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_roller), [quails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_quail), [partridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_partridge), [larks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lark), [starlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_starling), [lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizard), various insects), forest species ([roe deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_deer), [squirrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_squirrel), [bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_brown_bear), [wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_wolf), [foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_fox), [wild boars](52255.md), [wildcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_wildcat), [dormice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormouse), [jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_jay), [salamanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salamander), etc.) and water species ([herons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heron), [mallards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallard), [geese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose), [storks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stork), [frogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frog) and a wide variety of fish, such as [pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_pike), [barbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_barbel), [Prussian carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_carp), [wels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wels_catfish), [perch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perch), [carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_carp), etc.).

### Climate

The climate has a moderate temperate [continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) character, with hot summers and mild winters due to both the influences of the oceanic (from the west) and Mediterranean (from the south and southwest) air masses and the fact that over 85% of the territory belongs to the plain climate (the remaining 15% belongs to the climate of hilly and mountainous regions).[^cite_note-cadru-natural-15]<sup>: 49 </sup> The western area of the Banat Plain is classified according to [Köppen's guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) as [Cfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate), and the mountainous area in the east of the county as [Dfb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate).[^cite_note-cadru-natural-15]<sup>: 50 </sup> Due to the high uniformity of the relief, the average annual temperatures are poorly differentiated on the territory of Timiș County. The annual averages are close to 11 °C in the region with plain climate and decrease by 1–2 °C on the hills in the east of the county.[^cite_note-barbu-10]<sup>: 9 </sup> The averages of the warmest month (July) exceed 21 °C in the plain and fall below 18 °C in the east. The averages of the coldest month (January) are higher than –2 °C in the plain and rise to values higher than –1 °C in the east of the county. The absolute maximum temperature (42 °C) was recorded in [Banloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banloc) (24 July 2007), and the absolute minimum temperature (–35.3 °C) in Timișoara (29 January 1963).[^cite_note-cadru-natural-15]<sup>: 55 </sup>

The average multiannual precipitation amounts to increasing quantities from west to east, i.e. 569 mm in Jimbolia, 620.6 mm in Timișoara, 734 mm in Făget and over 1,200 mm on the ridges of the Poiana Ruscă Mountains. The maximum rainfall occurs in June, as a result of the intensification of cyclonic activity in the [North Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic) or in the Mediterranean basin. On the other hand, the lowest average monthly amounts fall in February–March. The snow layer is unstable and has insignificant thicknesses.

Predominant winds blow from the north (16.9%), east (15%), northwest (9.1%) and south (8.4%), with average annual speeds between 1.2 and 3.8 m/s. Local winds include *austru* (dry wind from the south), *coșavă* (high-speed cold wind), *rușavăț* (in the Banat Plain) and mountain breezes (in the east).[^cite_note-cadru-natural-15]<sup>: 65 </sup>

According to the 2011 census, [Hungarians](17106358.md) are the largest ethnic minority in Timiș County, numbering 35,295 people or 5.2% of the county's population. Significant concentrations of the Hungarian population follow the areas where the policy of colonization of the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) manifested itself in the 19th century and the beginning of the 20th century. At the 2011 census, there were 16 localities – all rural – that had at least 20% Hungarian population. However, their total population represents only 12.6% of the Hungarian population in the county. This is explained by the migration of Hungarians from rural to urban areas after 1990. By weight, the largest Hungarian communities are found in [Otelec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otelec) (*Ótelek*; 75.1%), [Cherestur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beba_Veche) (*Pusztakeresztúr*; 69.7%), [Bodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balin%C8%9B) (*Nagybodófalva*; 64%), [Tormac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tormac) (*Végvár*; 60.6%) and [Otvești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saco%C8%99u_Turcesc) (*Ötvösd*; 52.6%).[^cite_note-23]

[Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) are the second largest minority, with 14,525 people (or 2.1% of the county's population) at the 2011 census, although unofficial estimates put the figure at 45,000.[^cite_note-24] [Măguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugoj), a component locality of [Lugoj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugoj) municipality, is the only village in the county where the Roma have an absolute majority (69.4% in 2011).[^cite_note-25]

The largest communities of [Serbs](59512.md), [Germans](152735.md) and [Bulgarians](151876.md) in Romania also live in Timiș County. The number of Serbs living in Timiș exceeds 10,000. Most of them are located in Timișoara and in the localities near the border with [Serbia](29265.md): [Cralovăț](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topolov%C4%83%C8%9Bu_Mare) (Краљевац/*Kraljevac*; 64.5%), [Petrovaselo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reca%C8%99) (Петрово Село/*Petrovo Selo*; 48.4%), [Sânmartinu Sârbesc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peciu_Nou) (Српски Семартон/*Srpski Semarton*; 35.6%), [Cenei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenei) (Ченеј/*Čenej*; 24.2%), [Diniaș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peciu_Nou) (Дињаш/*Dinjaš*; 21.8%), etc.[^cite_note-26] Up to the 20th century, the Germans (specifically [Banat Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat_Swabians)) were a very large community. They were colonized here at the end of the 17th century and during the 18th century, in three successive waves, during the reigns of [Charles VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), [Maria Theresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa) and [Joseph II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). They came from different areas of Germany, especially [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland), the [Palatinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatinate_\(region\)) and [Swabia](616237.md), but also [Luxembourg](17515.md), [Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorraine), [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace), [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia), [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse), [East Franconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Franconia) and [Bavaria](3764.md). Deported to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) for forced labor after 1945 and subjected to [nationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) and [collectivization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivization) during the communist period, most Germans in Timiș County left for [Germany](11867.md) and [Austria](26964606.md), with one last wave after 1990.[^cite_note-27] At present, they account for merely 1⁄24 of the number at the 1900 census, when the highest number of Germans was recorded. [Banat Bulgarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat_Bulgarians), a Bulgarian Catholic minority, began settling in Banat in the late 17th century from the north and northwest of today's [Bulgaria](3415.md). The largest communities of Banat Bulgarians in Timiș County live in [Breștea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denta) (*Brešća*; 80.3%), [Dudeștii Vechi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dude%C8%99tii_Vechi) (*Stár Bišnov*; 66.5%) and [Denta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denta) (*Dénta*; 5.2%).[^cite_note-28]

### Religion

In Timiș County there are over 300 places of worship.[^cite_note-29] Timisoara is the seat of the Archbishopric of Timișoara, the see of the [Metropolis of Banat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_of_Banat). The archbishopric has jurisdiction over the territory of Timiș County, which is divided into 273 [parishes](23623.md) organized in six [deaneries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deanery) (Timișoara I, Timișoara II, [Lugoj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugoj), [Făget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C4%83get), [Deta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deta,_Romania) and [Sânnicolau Mare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A2nnicolau_Mare)).[^cite_note-30] Timișoara is also the seat of the [Roman Catholic Archbishopric of Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Timi%C8%99oara) and the [Serbian Orthodox Diocese of Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eparchy_of_Temi%C5%A1var) and one of the nine deaneries of the [Reformed Eparchy of Piatra Craiului](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Diocese_of_Kir%C3%A1lyh%C3%A1g%C3%B3mell%C3%A9k). Lugoj is the seat of the [Greek Catholic Archbishopric of Lugoj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Catholic_Eparchy_of_Lugoj) and one of the two deaneries of the [Ukrainian Orthodox Vicarage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Vicariate_Sighetu_Marma%C8%9Biei).[^cite_note-31]

### Official symbols

See also: [Lion and Sun](8702401.md)

The flag of Timiș County has a rectangular shape, with its width equal to two-thirds of its length and consists of the county coat of arms superimposed on a white background. White chosen for the flag signifies purity and peace. Above the coat of arms is inscribed in capital letters in [vermilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermilion) the word "Romania", and below it is inscribed with the same characters in [cobalt blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt_blue) the words "Timiș County". The county flag was approved in 2021.[^cite_note-34]

The coat of arms was approved in 2002 and consists of a cut [shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escutcheon_\(heraldry\)); at the top, on the red [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_\(heraldry\)), a golden lion coming out of the battlements of a wall of built gold; the lion holds a bent sword, with a wide point, made of silver; in the upper right-hand corner is a golden sun, and in the upper left, a rising silver moon. At the bottom, on the blue field, is a wavy silver band. The lion with armed arm, characteristic of [Banat of Temeswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat_of_Temeswar), symbolizes the defenders of the land and of Christianity against the Ottoman armies; the lion carries the victorious sword of [Pál Kinizsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1l_Kinizsi), Count of [Temes County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temes_County). The gold wall symbolizes the [fortress of Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara_Fortress). The golden sun symbolizes, through its position, the lofty goals for which the heroes of this land fought. The silver moon represents the pair of the sun. The wavy silver band on the blue field symbolizes the [Timiș River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99_\(river\)), which gives the county its name.[^cite_note-35]

## Economy

Timiș County has one of the most dynamic economies in Romania. The county's economy gradually transformed from a predominantly industrial one, sometimes with significant losses due to the non-use of labor and capital endowments, into a [tertiary one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy) (based mainly on [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_\(economics\))).[^cite_note-economie-36]<sup>: 141 </sup> The specialization of Timiș County stands out in the computer, automotive and electrical equipment industry, these registering high values of the [location quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_quotient) (as of 2019).[^cite_note-ghid-37]<sup>: 18 </sup>

In 2021, Timiș County had a GDP per capita of 31,519 euros, 40% above the national average, and an annual growth rate of 5–10%.[^cite_note-cnp-38]<sup>: 4 </sup> At national level, it ranks third in terms of [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP), reaching 62.5 billion lei in 2021, with a contribution of 6.5% of Romania's total GDP,[^cite_note-cnp-38]<sup>: 2 </sup> having a developed economy and among the highest entrepreneurial rates in the country (36 [SMEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium-sized_enterprises) per 1,000 inhabitants in 2019).[^cite_note-economie-36]<sup>: 145 </sup> The most important sector represented locally is the [automotive industry](163778.md), supported by large companies such as [Continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_AG), [Delphi Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi_Technologies), [Hella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hella_\(company\)), [Mahle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahle_GmbH), [Dräxlmaier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr%C3%A4xlmaier_Group), [Flex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flex_\(company\)), etc. In recent years, certain [high tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech) [know-how-intensive services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_intensive_services), such as [IT\&C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology), have seen strong growth. Timiș has one of the lowest [unemployment rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rate) in the country – 0.8% in 2019.[^cite_note-insse-19]<sup>: 39 </sup> Most employees are in manufacturing (27%), trade (15.2%) and agriculture (15.1%).[^cite_note-economie-36]<sup>: 152 </sup>

Timiș stands out as the second business pole at national level, after [Bucharest–Ilfov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest-Ilfov),[^cite_note-39] with a turnover of companies of 72 billion lei, which constitutes about 5% of the turnover of 1,400 billion lei of all companies in Romania.[^cite_note-prezentare-40] The county's exports stood at over 39.05 billion lei, Timiș being among the few counties in the country with a positive trade balance. Timiș County's exports consist mainly of the following groups of goods: [electrical machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_machines), [appliances](185982.md) and [equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_device) (37%), [plastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastics) and [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber) (14%), means of transport (11%), [footwear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footwear) (9%), [optical instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_instrument) and appliances (7%), [furniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furniture) (5%). Timiș County is also the largest importer of consumer goods, after Bucharest.[^cite_note-prezentare-40]

### Industry

The industry of Timiș County is strong and diversified, due to the high rate of [greenfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenfield_project) and brownfield investments, the western location of the county, the high qualification of the labor force, as well as the entrepreneurial tradition. The most significant share in the total production of the county is held by the [manufacturing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_industry), having as sub-branches: [electronics industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_industry), [food industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_industry), [chemical industry](58721.md), [textile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry), [metal](266443.md) and [wood processing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_industry) and [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction). Although with only 8% of the number of companies in Timiș, the manufacturing industry registers a turnover of 40% of the county's total.[^cite_note-prezentare-40]

### Agriculture

Timiș County ranks first in the country in terms of both agricultural and arable land. Timiș County has an agricultural area of 691,299 ha, of which 531,472 ha is [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land), 118,671 [pastures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasture), 28,632 [hayfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay), 4,121 [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyard) and vine nurseries and 8,403 [orchards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchard) and fruit nurseries.[^cite_note-insse-19]<sup>: 74 </sup>

One of the oldest and most important agricultural activities in the county, with favorable climatic conditions, is the cultivation of [cereals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereals) and technical plants, and [viticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viticulture) is practiced in most of the communes in the plain and hill area of the county. Places like [Recaș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reca%C8%99), [Buziaș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzia%C8%99) and [Giarmata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giarmata) are renowned for their [wine production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_production). The production of vegetables in individual micro-farms is also a traditional economic activity, especially in the rural localities in the vicinity of urban centers. By cultivated area, the main crops are [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) (165,643 ha), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) and [rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye) (133,657 ha), [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower) (78,242 ha), perennial [fodder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fodder) (38,060 ha) and [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans) (21,200 ha).[^cite_note-insse-19]<sup>: 77 </sup> Livestock farming is also an important, traditional branch of Timiș agriculture, and in recent years there has been a significant increase in the number of animals in the private sector. As of 2019, livestock in Timiș County consisted of 1,524,625 [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird), 612,638 [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep), 605,619 [swine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swine), 45,332 [bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee) families, 37,305 [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), 21,890 [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat) and 5,659 [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse).[^cite_note-insse-19]<sup>: 80 </sup>

Unlike industry, trade and construction, which also generates a large part of the [gross value added](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_value_added) (GVA) of the county economy, agriculture stands out with a very high share of employment correlated with a low share in GDP (5%), although that it places the county on the first place at national level from the perspective of the GVA obtained in agriculture.[^cite_note-economie-36]<sup>: 152 </sup>

### Banking

With several units of national and international banks operating here ([BCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banca_Comercial%C4%83_Rom%C3%A2n%C4%83), [Raiffeisen Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raiffeisen_Bank_\(Romania\)), [BT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banca_Transilvania), [BRD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRD_%E2%80%93_Groupe_Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_G%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale), [CEC Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEC_Bank), [OTP Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OTP_Bank), [UniCredit Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UniCredit_Bank_Romania), [Alpha Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Bank), [Citibank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank), [Credit Europe Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_Europe_Bank), [Garanti BBVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garanti_BBVA), [ING Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ING_Group), [Intesa Sanpaolo Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intesa_Sanpaolo), etc.), Timiș County is a national leader in the field of financial intermediation.[^cite_note-ghid-37]<sup>: 23 </sup> As of 2021, the turnover of companies with financial and insurance activities was 69 million euros in Timișoara alone.[^cite_note-ghid-37]<sup>: 23 </sup>

### Tourism

The geographical position of the county in the immediate vicinity of the border with Hungary and Serbia leads to a high transit tourism, but with short and medium length of stay. The main form of tourism practiced in the county is [business tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_tourism), followed by recreational, shopping and gastronomic tourism.[^cite_note-turism-41] The tourist infrastructure of Timiș County consists of 56 [hotels](14276.md), 14 [hostels](233996.md), two [motels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motel), a [campsite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campsite), four tourist villas, 49 tourist pensions, 27 agritourism pensions and two camps for students and preschoolers with an accommodation capacity of 7,746 places.[^cite_note-insse-19]<sup>: 94 </sup> In 2019, Timiș County had 396,644 tourists, of which 30% were foreigners.[^cite_note-insse-19]<sup>: 95 </sup>

Cultural-historical tourism includes many objectives in historical and archeological sites, architectural monuments and museums. [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara) has the largest architectural ensemble of historic buildings in Romania (about 14,500), consisting of the urban heritage of the [Cetate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetate,_Timi%C8%99oara), [Iosefin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iosefin), [Fabric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabric,_Timi%C8%99oara) and [Elisabetin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabetin) districts. In addition, there are 31 castles and mansions in Timiș County.[^cite_note-42] Notable are the Swabian-specific localities in the western Banat Plain ([Jimbolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimbolia), [Sânnicolau Mare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A2nnicolau_Mare), [Șandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C8%98andra), [Lovrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovrin), [Lenauheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenauheim), etc.), in the past important settlements of colonists from the German lands of the Habsburg Empire. Religious tourism includes many places of worship, churches and historic monasteries. Among the monastic ensembles are the historic wooden churches in Țara Făgetului, all built after the liberation of Banat from Ottoman occupation (1716),[^cite_note-43] the 11th-century Morisena Monastery (the first monastery in Romania), the 14th-century [Partoș Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parto%C8%99_Monastery) with the relics of St. Joseph of Partoș, the 15th-century Săraca Monastery, the 15th-century Serbian Orthodox [St. George Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sveti_%C4%90ura%C4%91_monastery) of [Birda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birda), the 18th-century Cebza Monastery and, more recently, the Izvorul Miron Monastery (1912; nicknamed the "[Voroneț](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorone%C8%9B) of Banat") and the Timișeni Monastery (1944).

[Ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) is practiced mainly in the mountainous area in the east of the county, where Lake Surduc is located (the largest lake in western Romania), but also a series of waterfalls and caves. Areas with a rich hunting fund, as well as those with a diversified fishing fund are suitable for sport [hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting) and [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing). On the territory of Timiș County there are 86 hunting funds and 30 fishing funds.[^cite_note-turism-41] [Canoeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoeing) and [kayaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayaking) can be practiced on the [Bega Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bega_Canal), and [rafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafting) on the [Timiș River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99_\(river\)). Spa tourism is practiced in [Buziaș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzia%C8%99) (declared a [spa resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa_resort) in 1911), [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara), [Deta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deta,_Romania), as well as [Călacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Or%C8%9Bi%C8%99oara), [Teremia Mare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teremia_Mare) and [Lovrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovrin). Wine tourism is represented by the [Recaș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reca%C8%99)–[Buziaș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzia%C8%99) area, renowned both in the country and abroad for its wines.

## Culture

### Music

Timiș County has a rich musical history. Notable in this respect are the choirs of Banat *Bauer*. Banat choirs have their origins in [church music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_music), learned in schools, where the teacher was both organist, choir conductor, and not infrequently, composer. The choirs, which were to become a real movement in Banat, were an important means of national manifestation of the people of Banat, through which the Romanian language and culture were promoted. The oldest Romanian choir in Banat was the plowmen's vocal choir, founded in 1857 in [Chizătău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belin%C8%9B), which was born from the old church choir. It was also the most famous and appreciated choral formation in the area, which had resounding success at various competitions and festivals, along with other Banat choirs.[^cite_note-44] Banat choirs had a rich repertoire, consisting of national, folk songs or choral works composed by [Ion Vidu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Vidu), [Filaret Barbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filaret_Barbu) or Iosif Velceanu.[^cite_note-45]

Timișoara was a reputed city of music in the 19th century. Many famous musicians performed here, including [Franz Liszt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Liszt) (1846), [Johann Strauss II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Strauss_II) (1847), [David Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Popper) (1867), [Leopold Auer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Auer) (1867), [Pablo de Sarasate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_de_Sarasate) (1877), [Henryk Wieniawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Wieniawski) (1877), [Johannes Brahms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Brahms) (1879) and [Joseph Joachim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Joachim) (1879).[^cite_note-bratu-46] The only [philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philharmonic) in the county, the [Banatul Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banatul_Philharmonic_of_Timi%C8%99oara), was founded in 1947 and organizes, in addition to the music season, events in unconventional spaces and successful festivals, such as *Timișoara muzicală*, the longest-lived cultural festival in Timișoara, which debuted in 1968. The first [music school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_school) in the county was founded by the Hungarian administration in 1907 in Timișoara.[^cite_note-bratu-46] Until 1907, music education was exclusively private.[^cite_note-47]

### Cuisine

Banat cuisine has been strongly influenced by the [Austro-Hungarian one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_cuisine), but to a large extent by [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cuisine), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_cuisine) and [French cuisines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_cuisine) too.[^cite_note-48] The dishes prepared in Banat are mostly made of [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork), [chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken) or [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef), with vegetables fried in [lard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lard) or [oil](4207510.md), with flour sauces, to make them more consistent, seasoned with [pepper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piperaceae), [thyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyme), [paprika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paprika) or [caraway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraway).[^cite_note-bucatarie-49] Broadly speaking, Banat cuisine is characterized by fatty and wholesome food; it can be individualized by the unusual association of salty and sweet taste, by the prevalence of [soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup) to the detriment of [stew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stew) and by showy desserts with multiple influences. Among the traditional dishes in this area are [noodle soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noodle_soup), [goulash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goulash), [papricache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papricache?action=edit\&redlink=1), [sauerkraut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerkraut), [sarme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarma_\(food\)), [pljeskavica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pljeskavica), [rasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasol_\(Romanian_dish\)), [cotoroage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspic) and fresh pork preparations and semi-preparations ([caltaboș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltabo%C8%99?action=edit\&redlink=1), [sângerete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_sausage), [maioș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverwurst), [sausages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sausage), [slănină](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sl%C4%83nin%C4%83), [șonc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ham), [tobă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tob%C4%83), etc.). Desserts include [krofne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krofne), [scovergi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scovard%C4%83), [bejgli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppy_seed_roll) with walnuts or poppy seeds, [Cremeschnitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremeschnitte), [Apfelküchle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apfelk%C3%BCchle), [knedles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knedle), [dobostorta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobos_torte), [ischler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischler?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [vargabéles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vargab%C3%A9les?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-bucatarie-49][^cite_note-50]

## Healthcare

At the county level, there are 22 [hospitals](18994221.md) (15 public and seven private), five [medical dispensaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispensary), three mental health centers, 522 [medical offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor's_office) (school, student and [family medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_medicine)), 846 dental offices, 115 [medical laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_laboratory), 87 dental laboratories and a [transfusion center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_transfusion). Regarding the pharmaceutical network in Timiș County, in 2019 there were 295 [pharmacies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacy) (16 public and 279 private), seven pharmaceutical points and 34 pharmaceutical warehouses.[^cite_note-insse-19]<sup>: 53 </sup>

Timișoara is nationally recognized as a center of excellence in medicine. Timișoara has made several breakthroughs in Romanian medicine, including the first [in vitro fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilization), the first [laser heart surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_surgery) and the first [stem cell transplant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell_transplant).[^cite_note-56] The Pius Brînzeu County Emergency Clinical Hospital and the Institute of Cardiovascular Diseases from Timișoara were included by the [Ministry of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Romania) in the first class of competence (out of five possible).[^cite_note-57] Première Hospital, run by Regina Maria private health network, is the largest [private hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_hospital) in the west of the country.[^cite_note-58]

## Transport

### Road transport





[A1 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_motorway_\(Romania\)) (up) near Timișoara. It is part of the [Pan-European Corridor IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_IV) (down).

Timiș County is connected to the European transport network by the Central and Global [TEN-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_Transport_Network) networks, so the main corridor that serves it is the [Rhine–Danube Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine%E2%80%93Danube_Corridor), which provides the connection with [Vienna](55866.md), with the central-southern part of [Germany](11867.md) ([Munich](19058.md), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) and [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg) and with [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg). The [Orient/East–Med Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient/East%E2%80%93Med_Corridor) also crosses the county's territory and connects with [Budapest](36787.md).[^cite_note-59]

Inter- and intra-county connections are provided by the main and secondary national roads from which a dense network of local roads emerges. The public road network of Timiș County has a length of about 2,930 km, with a density of 33.7 km/100 km<sup>2</sup>. More detailed, there are about 563 km of national roads, 1,145 km of county roads and 1,222 km of communal roads.[^cite_note-untrr-60]<sup>: 5 </sup> Also, Timiș County is served by almost 108 km of [motorways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorway), represented by the [A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_motorway_\(Romania\)) and [A6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A6_motorway_\(Romania\)) motorways.[^cite_note-61] In 2019, Timiș County had a motorization index of 361.64 vehicles/1,000 inhabitants, being among the highest in the country.[^cite_note-strategie-2-62]<sup>: 74 </sup>

### Rail transport

The railway network totals 795 km, of which 113 are electrified railways.[^cite_note-untrr-60]<sup>: 7 </sup> With 91.4 km of railway per 1,000 km<sup>2</sup>, Timiș County has the densest railway network in the country.[^cite_note-untrr-60]<sup>: 8 </sup> On the territory of Timiș County, the railway network consists of a main line ([900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83ile_Ferate_Rom%C3%A2ne_Line_900)) and 18 secondary lines belonging to the CFR lines 900 ([Bucharest](36877.md)–[Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara)), 200 ([Brașov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bra%C8%99ov)–[Curtici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtici)) and 300 ([Bucharest](36877.md)–[Episcopia Bihor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopia_Bihor)). The [railway line 900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83ile_Ferate_Rom%C3%A2ne_Line_900), whose route is [Bucharest](36877.md) (north)–[Roșiori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro%C8%99iorii_de_Vede) (north)–[Craiova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craiova)–[Filiași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filia%C8%99i)–[Caransebeș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caransebe%C8%99)–[Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara) (north), crosses the county on the [Lugoj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugoj)–[Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara) relation. It is part of the [Pan-European Corridor IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_IV).

In Timiș County there are 133 [stations](26161590.md) operated by state and private railway operators such as [Căile Ferate Române](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83ile_Ferate_Rom%C3%A2ne) (CFR), [Astra Trans Carpatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astra_Arad) and [Regio Călători](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regio_C%C4%83l%C4%83tori). The busiest of them is [Timișoara North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara_North_railway_station), with over 1.7 million passengers in 2011.[^cite_note-untrr-60]<sup>: 25 </sup>

### Air transport

Timiș County is served by [Traian Vuia International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara_Traian_Vuia_International_Airport), located northeast of [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara), about 10 km from the city center. With 1.5 million passengers in 2019, it is the [third busiest Romanian airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Romania), after [Bucharest](36877.md)'s [Henri Coandă International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Coand%C4%83_International_Airport) and [Cluj-Napoca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca)'s [Avram Iancu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj_International_Airport). Traian Vuia Airport concentrates 17.2% of the total number of passengers boarded at Romanian airports, 35.5% of the total tons of goods loaded and 13.7% of the total number of flights.[^cite_note-63]

[Cioca Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cioca_Airfield), used as an airport for a short period of time, between 1941 and 1942,[^cite_note-64] serves today as [airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodrome) for recreational aircraft, [air taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_taxi) and [utility aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_aircraft) and venue for various festivals. It is located in the northwest of Timișoara, about 5 km from it.

### Naval transport

Timiș County is crossed by one of the few artificially arranged waterways in Romania, the [Bega Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bega_Canal). The Bega Canal is part of the [Rhine–Main–Danube system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine%E2%80%93Main%E2%80%93Danube_Canal), making possible the connection between the [North Sea](21179.md) and the [Black Sea](3386.md). The length of the canal on the Romanian territory is about 44.5 km and 74 km on the Serbian territory. The navigable part of the canal starts from Timișoara from where it continues to the southwest until it flows into the [Tisza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisza), located on Serbian territory. The Bega Canal is the first navigable canal built in Romania. Originally built for the sanitation of floodplains around Timișoara, it was later arranged for navigation.[^cite_note-untrr-60]<sup>: 13–14 </sup>

Timișoara is the only Romanian city with [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) by water, made with [vaporetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaporetto)-like boats along the nine stations on the Bega Canal.[^cite_note-65]
