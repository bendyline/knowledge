**Plateau** ([pronunciation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/8b/Ha-Plateau_State.ogg/Ha-Plateau_State.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [Nigerian state](226734.md) located in the [North Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_\(Nigeria\)) [geopolitical zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopolitical_zones_of_Nigeria).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Its capital city is [Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos). Officially nicknamed the "Home of Peace and Tourism",[^cite_note-8] the state has a population of around 4.7 million people.[^cite_note-9]

## Geography

### Boundaries

Located between latitude 8°24' N and 10°30' N and longitude 8°32' E and 10°38' E,[^cite_note-10] Plateau State encompasses a range of hills and rock formations of the eponymous [Jos Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos_Plateau),[^cite_note-11] where bare rocks scatter across grasslands.[^cite_note-pictda-12] The state's altitude ranges from around 1,200 metres (3,900 ft) to a peak of 1,829 metres (6,001 ft) above sea level in the [Shere Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shere_Hills) near Jos.

#### Adjacent states

- [Bauchi State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauchi_State) – to the north east for 360 km (224 miles),

- [Kaduna State](2227637.md) – to the north west

- [Nasarawa State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasarawa_State) – to the south west for 219 km (136 miles)

- [Taraba State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraba_State) – to the south east for 202 km (126 miles)

### Climate

Although situated in the [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics) zone, the higher altitude gives Plateau State a near-temperate climate, with an average temperature between 13 and 22 °C. [Harmattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmattan) winds cause the coldest weather between December and February, with the warmest temperatures usually in the dry season months of March and April. The mean annual rainfall varies between 1,317 mm (52 in) in the southern part to 1,460 mm (57 in) on the plateau, with the highest rainfall during the [wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) in July and August. The cooler climate has led to a reduced incidence of some [tropical diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_disease) such as [malaria](20423.md). The Jos Plateau is the source of many rivers in northern Nigeria, including the [Kaduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaduna_River), [Gongola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongola_River), [Hadeja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadejia_River) and [Damaturu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damaturu) rivers.[^cite_note-britannica.com-13]

### Geology

Both Plateau State in general and the Jos Plateau in particular are thought to be formed on areas of younger [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) intruding through an area of older granite rock. These younger granites, thought to be about 160 million years old, create the Jos Plateau's unusual scenery. There are numerous [hillocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillock) with gentle slopes emerging from the ground like mushrooms scattered with huge boulders. Volcanic activity 50 million years ago created numerous volcanoes and vast basaltic plateaus from lava flows, as well as regions of mainly narrow and deep valleys and pediments (surfaces made smooth by erosion) from rounded hills with sheer rock faces.[^cite_note-14] Years of [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) and [columbite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbite) mining have left the state strewn with deep gorges and lakes.[^cite_note-Hodder1959-15][^cite_note-16]

## History



[Topaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topaz) from the [Jos Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos_Plateau) in Plateau State

Plateau state has been adjusted to its present borders many times. Colonial manipulation was from a desire to protect the railway construction and guarantee safe passage of mined tin to the coast. There was also an attempt initially to create a province of largely non-Muslims under one Resident. Later alterations came from strong local desires for [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance).

The [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) began to exert colonial control of Nigeria in the early 20th century. At that time, much of Plateau State was part of [Bauchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauchi_\(city\)) Province. In 1926, Plateau Province, made up of [Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos) and [Pankshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankshin) Divisions, was carved out of Bauchi.[^cite_note-32] The border changed several times in subsequent years as the government sought not to split ethnic groups. In May 1967, Benue and Plateau Provinces were merged to form the large [Benue-Plateau State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benue-Plateau_State). At this time Nigeria had twelve states.[^cite_note-britannica.com-13]

Following the civil war, Benue-Plateau State was one of several large states which were further split up following pressure on the federal government. Under the military administration of General [Yakubu Gowon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakubu_Gowon), the country was further divided into nineteen states in 1976 and Plateau State was created from Benue-Plateau covering the area of the original Plateau Province. In 1996, Plateau State was further subdivided to create [Nasarawa State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasarawa_State) which was carved out of the western half of Plateau State by [Sani Abacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sani_Abacha)'s military regime.

[Tin mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_mining) activities began in 1902 under the British and continue to the present day.

### 21st century conflicts

Main articles: [Boko Haram insurgency](27868253.md), [Herder–farmer conflicts in Nigeria](54964174.md), and [Nigerian bandit conflict](68366617.md)

During the 21st century, Plateau State has experienced significant violence, including riots, bombings, and clashes between different ethnic and religious groups. The region has seen conflicts involving various actors, including jihadist insurgents like [Boko Haram](23769768.md), as well as tensions between [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_people)-[Fulani](102383.md) herders and predominantly [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian) farmers, similar to other states in Nigeria's [Middle Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Belt).[^cite_note-33]

While some attacks have been attributed to Fulani herders, there have also been reports of violence by Christian militias and other local armed groups. In June 2018, ethnic violence in Jos resulted in the deaths of 86 people.[^cite_note-34] According to [Christian Solidarity Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Solidarity_Worldwide), 238 people were killed in a single weekend in June 2018, and 1,061 people lost their lives in the first three months of that year due to attacks linked to Fulani herders.[^cite_note-35]

However, violence in the region is not one-sided. In recent years, Fulani communities have also suffered attacks, including incidents where armed groups have targeted Muslim herders and travelers on roads. Reports indicate that Christian-majority vigilantes and local militias have engaged in reprisal killings, further escalating tensions. For example, in January 2024, a senior military officer and several soldiers were killed in Plateau State in an attack linked to ongoing communal unrest.[^cite_note-36]

While many portray the conflict through an ethnic and religious lens, analysts suggest that factors such as [climate change](5042951.md), competition over land, and political instability also play significant roles.[^cite_note-37] In 2022, a bandit gang [attacked several villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Plateau_State_massacres), leading to significant casualties. More recently, in December 2023, at least 200 people died in a series of [new attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Plateau_State_massacres).[^cite_note-38]

The ongoing violence in Plateau State underscores the complexity of the crisis, where multiple groups engage in attacks and retaliations, worsening instability in the region.

## Government

The Plateau State administrative structure consists of the state cabinet, the [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau_State_House_of_Assembly) and [local government areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_area).[^cite_note-pictda-12]

The state government is run by the [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Plateau_State) (chief executive), deputy governor, secretary to the state government, commissioners (cabinet members), special advisers, permanent secretaries, board chairmen and general managers. The current governor is [Caleb Manasseh Mutfwang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Bako_Lalong).[^cite_note-39] He was sworn in on 29 May 2023 under the party PDP.[^cite_note-40]

The House of Assembly consists of 25 members.[^cite_note-41] The current Speaker of the house is Rt. Hon. [Naanlong Daniel](https://www.channelstv.com/2023/06/13/first-time-lawmaker-moses-sule-emerges-as-plateau-speaker/) who was elected on 2 July 2025 by the members of the 10th assembly after former speaker Gabriel Dewan resigned.[^cite_note-42]

The local government is headed by a chairman, who is the chief executive, while his cabinet consists of elected councilors who make up the legislative arm.[^cite_note-43]

Plateau State is also divided into 17 local government areas, each encompassing ethnic groups who share common affinities or distant bloodlines.[^cite_note-44] Leaders of these local government areas are elected by the people from amongst several contestants who may not be related to any past chiefdom leaders.[^cite_note-45]

### Religion

Plateau state residents are predominantly Christians.

The Roman Catholic Church includes 830,714 faithful in the [Archdiocese of Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Jos) (1934) with 60 parishes under Archbishop [Matthew Ishaya Audu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Ishaya_Audu) (2020), and two suffragan dioceses of Pankshin (2014) with 32 parishes under Bishop Michael Global Gokum (2014), and Shendam (2007) with 31 parishes under Bishop [Philip Davou Dung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Dung) (2016).

[The Anglican Diocese of Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Jos) (1980) within [the Province of Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Jos), is led by [Bishop Benjamin Kwashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Kwashi) (2008).

The [Church of Christ in Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Christ_in_Nations) (COCIN) has its headquarters in Jos North.

- south from [A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_highway_\(Nigeria\)) at Mararaba Jama'a via Ban, Heipang, and Kassa Hausawa, to Barakin Gangare.

- the Baarakin Ladi-Pankshin Rd east from Barakin Gangare via Doorowa, Soko Ca Futa, Mongu, Mangu, Duwop, and Vodni to Angwan Sarki.

- the Jos Rd continues southeast from Angwan Sarki via Duk, Gwolong, Kabwir, Amper and Rek to [Langtang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langtang,_Nigeria)

- the Langtang-Zamko-Yelwa Rd south from Langtang via Zamerum and Zamko to Inshar.

- south from Inshar via Ajikemai, Yamini and Mato to [Taraba State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraba_State) at Ikputu.

- the Amper-Bachumbi Rd west from Taraba State at Zurak via Zok, and Gaji to Bashar

- the Langtang-Wase-Bashar Rd south via Angwan Nasarawa and Yola to Wase,

- the Mavo Wase Rd east from Wase via Mavo Wase to Gwiwan Kpgi,

- the Lafia-Shandam Rd west from Inshar via Lonvel, Shendam, Pershiep, Shepwan, Der, Kurgwi, Kwande, Layi, Taram and Namu to [Nasarawa State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasarawa_State) at Ungwan Mai,

- west from [A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_highway_\(Nigeria\)) at Makera via Kwakwi to Garuwa,

- the Jos Kafanchan Rd from [Kaduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaduna_\(city\)) to Garuwa,

- the Kagoro-Dutsen Kato Rd north and east from Garuwa continuing the Jos-Kafanchan Rd as the Vom Rd to [A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_highway_\(Nigeria\)) at Bukuru.
