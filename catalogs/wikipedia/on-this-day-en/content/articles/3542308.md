The **Hanish Islands** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جزر حنيش, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *juzur ḥanīš*, [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language): ደሴታት ሓኒሽ, romanized: *däsetat ḥaniš*) form an [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) in the [Red Sea](26191.md) consisting of a trio of major islands at the centre of an array of smaller [islets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islet) and [rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_\(geology\)).[^cite_note-Britannica-1] The three major islands are the northern [Zuqar Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuqar_Island), the southern Great Anish (*Al-anīsh al-Kabīr*), and the significantly smaller Little Anish (*Al-anīsh al-Ṣaghīr*) in between.&#x20;

The archipelago is largely under the control of [Yemen](350939.md), with only several small south-western rocks and islets granted to [Eritrea](17238590.md) by the [Permanent Court of Arbitration](38058.md) in 1998 following the [Hanish Islands conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanish_Islands_conflict) in 1994–1995. The name may derive from a local word *hanash* meaning "snake" or "serpent".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELoftie1901123-2][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEal-Shara'i2024-3]

On 14 September 2026, the islands were captured by the [Houthis](17240332.md) (also known as Ansar Allah) during their [ongoing conflict](46215853.md) with the United Nations-recognized [Presidential Leadership Council](70488867.md) (PLC) of [Yemen](350939.md).

## History



French-built lighthouse on the Hanish Islands, seen here in 2007.

The [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) exercised claim over the Hanish archipelago until its dissolution following [World War I](4764461.md), after which the sovereignty and political status of the islands were left indeterminate by the 1923 [Treaty of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne).[^cite_note-Britannica-1] [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) exercised loose control over the fishermen frequenting the archipelago through its geographical proximity to [Italian Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea), until the country's occupation by the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) in 1915 to "forestall the Italians".[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-Britannica-1]

In 1938, Italy and the United Kingdom signed the [Anglo-Italian Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Accords) regarding certain areas in the Middle East, which left the issue unresolved with both parties agreeing not to exercise sovereignty over the islands outside of the stationing of agreed officials.[^cite_note-5]

In 1962, the United Kingdom signed an international treaty affirming its continued maintenance of lighthouses on the archipelago, first established in the 1938 treaty, although this treaty was not signed by [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire) or [North Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yemen). Responsibility for the lighthouses was turned over to Yemen in 1989.[^cite_note-Lefebvre1998-6]

Eritrean independence groups used the archipelago, particularly [Zuqar Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuqar_Island), as a base for attacks on Ethiopian military interests, leading to the Ethiopian desire for control over the archipelago.[^cite_note-7]

In 1991, Eritrea succeeded in [gaining its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence), and began attempts to negotiate and exercise sovereignty over the archipelago, particularly Great Anish. In 1995, the breakdown of peaceful negotiations with Yemen resulted in the [Hanish Islands conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanish_Islands_conflict), a territorial war that lasted two years. In 1998, both countries agreed to accept arbitration, after which the [Permanent Court of Arbitration](38058.md) determined that the archipelago belonged to Yemen, granting several small islands and islets to Eritrean sovereignty. The conflict ultimately claimed the lives of 4 to 15 Yemenis and 12 Eritreans.[^cite_note-Britannica-1]

The archipelago became the scene of intense fighting during the [Yemeni civil war](46215853.md), when forces loyal to former [president](444090.md) [Ali Abdullah Saleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Abdullah_Saleh) and [Houthi](17240332.md) insurgents fought against forces loyal to acting president [Abd Rabbuh Mansur Hadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_Rabbuh_Mansur_Hadi), backed by [Gulf Arab coalition forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen).[^cite_note-8] Having previously been under the control of the pro-[PLC](70488867.md) [Yemeni National Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_National_Resistance), the islands were [captured](84167596.md) by the Houthis on 11 September 2026.[^cite_note-9]
