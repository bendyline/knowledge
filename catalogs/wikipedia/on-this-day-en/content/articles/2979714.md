**Rukirabasaija Omukama Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV** (Tooro: [\[ɾukiɾaβaséíd͡ʒa omukáma óːjo ɲíːmba kaβáːmba igúɾu ɾukídi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA); 16 April 1992 – 27 August 2026), commonly known as **King Oyo of Tooro** or **King Rukidi IV**, was the 13th [Omukama](2022535.md) of the [Kingdom of Tooro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Tooro) in [Uganda](31816.md) from 1995 until his death in 2026. At the time of his accession, Oyo was three years old, making him the world's youngest reigning monarch.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-\:0-3] He was the son of [Omukama Patrick David Matthew Kaboyo Rwamuhokya Olimi III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olimi_III_of_Tooro) and [Omugo Best Kemigisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Kemigisa).[^cite_note-4]

## Biography

After the death of his father [King Olimi III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olimi_III_of_Tooro) in 1995, Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Rukidi IV assumed the role of king during his toddler years. At 2 a.m. on 12 September 1995, a week after Olimi III's burial, the rituals to proclaim Rukidi IV as king began making him the youngest modern monarch at the age of three.[^cite_note-\:0-3] The rituals included a mock battle at the palace entrance fought between enemy forces of a "rebel" prince and the royal army, and a test of Rukidi IV's divine right to the throne, in which the *Omusuuga* (head of the royal clan) called on the gods to strike the prince dead if he was not of royal blood. On passing the test, Rukidi IV was permitted to sound the Nyalebe, a sacred [Chwezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Kitara) drum, as his forefathers had done.[^cite_note-auto-5][^cite_note-6]

At 4 a.m., Rukidi IV was crowned amidst a jubilant crowd and entered the *Karuziika palace* as the new ruler of the Kingdom of Tooro. He was served his first meal as king, which consisted of [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet) dough.[^cite_note-7] The cultural rituals (*Emirwa y'Obuhangwa*) were followed by a religious ceremony presided over by Eustace Kamanyire of [Fort Portal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Portal) based Ruwenzori. President [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md) attended the coronation celebrations and paid tribute to Rukidi IV.[^cite_note-8] Rukidi IV was the *[Omukama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omukama)*, which means "King," and *Rukirabasaija*, which means "the greatest of men." Although he was considered the sovereign leader of the Batooro, Rukidi IV's power was limited to cultural duties. His full title and name was Rukirabasaija Omukama Oyo Nyimba Kabamba-Iguru Rukidi IV.[^cite_note-9]

In October 2013, Rukidi IV graduated from the [University of Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Winchester) with a bachelor's degree.[^cite_note-10]

&#x20;Three [regents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regents) were charged with overseeing Rukidi IV's growth into the role of king and with handling the cultural affairs of the kingdom during the king's childhood and youth. At the time of his coronation, the regents included Prince James Mugenyi, [Princess Elizabeth Bagaaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Elizabeth_of_Tooro) and lastly [Queen Mother Best Kemigisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Kemigisa).[^cite_note-11] [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) was a patron of the Tooro Kingdom with close ties to the royal family. In 2001, the nine-year-old Rukidi IV named Gaddafi the "defender" of the kingdom and invited him to attend the sixth coronation anniversary celebrations in 2001. Gaddafi had made donations to the kingdom, helping pay for refurbishments to the palace in Fort Portal.[^cite_note-12]

## Death

On the evening of 27 August 2026, reports began circulating widely on social media in Uganda claiming that Rukidi IV had died in the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-13] The reports followed earlier rumours about his health, which had been dismissed by [Tooro Kingdom](1506178.md) officials. The kingdom's prime minister confirmed that the king was in critical condition and called on Ugandans and his subjects to pray for him.[^cite_note-14] At around 10\:00 p.m. that night ([EAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa_Time)), Rukidi was pronounced dead while receiving treatment for cancer in the United States. He was 34.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-\:1-17] Tooro's prime minister Calvin Armstrong Rwomiire Akiiki called the death an "immeasurable loss" to the royal family and the people of Tooro.[^cite_note-bbcnews-18] Government minister [Margaret Annet Muhanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Annet_Muhanga_Mugisa) later announced that one million trees would be planted during the mourning period in his honour.[^cite_note-bbcnews-18]

Following his death, his mother, Best Kemigisa, and his sister Ruth Komuntale citing a purported 2022 will in which Rukidi IV reportedly named a biological son as his heir, disputed the selection of [Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma](84244857.md) as his successor.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

## Rukidi IV's Legacy

### Environmental legacy

[Rukidi IV](2979714.md) campaigned on environmental conservation in the later years of his reign, including a "Save River Mpanga Now" campaign addressing pollution and siltation of the Mpanga River, which supplies Fort Portal's water and flows through [Kibale National Park](2435189.md) to [Lake George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_George_\(Uganda\)).[^cite_note-\:2-23] In 2019, amid local concern over pollution of the Mpanga River, Rukidi IV joined community efforts to desilt the river and remove solid waste, taking part in the clean-up himself.[^cite_note-\:2-23]

In 2019, Rukidi IV was named one of the Global Top 100 Most Influential People of African Descent.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Education and youth empowerment

[Rukidi IV](2979714.md) supported [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education) through the [Tooro Kingdom](1506178.md) scholarship programme, which provided [scholarships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship) to students from disadvantaged backgrounds. His wider youth agenda also included skills development, ICT and [entrepreneurship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrepreneurship).[^cite_note-\:4-26][^cite_note-\:5-27][^cite_note-\:6-28]

### Health and HIV/AIDS advocacy

Health was a major area of his public work. He supported medical camps during Empango celebrations and initiatives focused on children's health[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30].[^cite_note-31] He was also designated a UNAIDS champion for HIV and AIDS among young people in Eastern and Southern Africa in 2016[^cite_note-32], using his public position to promote HIV prevention, treatment and awareness.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-\:6-28][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### Champion of Agriculture

Rukidi IV promoted agriculture as a means of improving livelihoods and engaging young people. He established a model farm in [Kyenjojo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyenjojo_District) where people could learn about enterprises including dairy, coffee, bananas, fish farming, poultry and goat keeping.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-\:4-26][^cite_note-\:5-27][^cite_note-\:6-28]

### Tourism Champion

Rukidi IV promoted Tooro's tourism attractions, including the Rwenzori Mountains, Kibale National Park and Sempaya Hot Springs. His 2022 Rwenzori expedition and 2025 gorilla trekking experience were also connected to tourism and conservation promotion.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-\:6-28]

### Culture and heritage

As Omukama (King) of Tooro, cultural preservation was central to his role. He promoted Tooro traditions, ceremonies, dances and cultural identity, while also seeking to make the kingdom's traditional institution relevant to younger generations.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-\:6-28]

### Ict and digital skills

An ICT centre was established at the kingdom headquarters in Muchwa to provide young people with computer and digital-skills training. This formed part of his broader youth empowerment agenda.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-\:6-28][^cite_note-46]

### Sports

Rukidi IV was actively involved in sports both as a participant and as a supporter of sporting activities in Tooro. He played football in his free time and was a supporter of the Uganda Cranes. He also participated in golf, cycling, athletics and motorsport, and had played tennis and swimming during his school years.[^cite_note-\:3-47]

Football was a significant part of his public involvement in sport. He supported the Tooro Kingdom Amasaza Cup, which brought together teams representing the kingdom's counties, and supported the FUFA Drum from its establishment in 2017. In 2018, he met [FUFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Uganda_Football_Associations) president [Moses Magogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Magogo_Hassim) to discuss football development, talent identification and the role of football in bringing communities together. Oyo also pledged his support for football development programmes and activities in [Tooro.](1506178.md)[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

Oyo also supported other sporting disciplines, including rugby, golf, cycling and athletics. He participated in the [Fort Portal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Portal) Tourism City Marathon and used sporting activities to encourage young people to participate in physical activity and health initiatives. He also competed as a driver in the United Motor Club Fort Portal Tourism City Rally in 2024.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

In 2026, Oyo donated 50 acres of his private royal land for a proposed sports stadium in Fort Portal.[^cite_note-52] The planned facility was intended to provide additional opportunities for sporting activities and youth talent development in the Tooro region.[^cite_note-\:3-47][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

### Tooro Kingdom Vision 2045

In 2021, Oyo launched a 25-year Tooro Kingdom Vision 2045, covering areas including agriculture, education, health, tourism, culture, employment and poverty reduction.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]
