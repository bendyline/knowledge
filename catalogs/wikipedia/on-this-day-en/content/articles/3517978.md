The **12th IAAF World Championships in Athletics** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Leichtathletik-Weltmeisterschaften 2009*) were held in Berlin, Germany from 15 to 23 August 2009. The majority of events took place in the [Olympiastadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiastadion_\(Berlin\)), while the [marathon](49132.md) and [racewalking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racewalking) events started and finished at the [Brandenburg Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_Gate).

## Organization

### Bidding process

Berlin was announced the winning bidder by the [IAAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF) on 6 December 2004 beating out bids from [Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_\(city\)) ([Croatia](5573.md)), [Valencia](63861.md) (Spain), [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) (Australia), [Brussels](3708.md) (Belgium), [Delhi](37756.md) (India), [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca) ([Morocco](19291.md)) and [Daegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegu) ([South Korea](27019.md)).[^cite_note-1] The city of Berlin and the [Deutscher Leichtathletik-Verband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutscher_Leichtathletik-Verband) (German Athletics Association) are responsible for the organisation of the event. The Berlin Organising Committee 2009 [GmbH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GmbH), a corporation established by the DLV in 2005, will supervise the operative organisation of the competition.[^cite_note-FAQ-2]

### Costs

Building upon Germany's history of successful athletics events, including the [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2006 FIFA World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) the [1993 World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_World_Championships_in_Athletics), the [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) and [1972 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics), IAAF president [Lamine Diack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamine_Diack) was confident of a well organised competition.[^cite_note-3] The organizers announced a [budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget) of €49.8 million to stage the event, which includes the travel and accommodation costs for all participating athletes.[^cite_note-Host1-4] Revenues include €17 million from ticketing and €7 million from marketing. The city of Berlin will cover a deficit up to €20 million. The organising committee secured 9000 rooms in the city to account for accommodation, with the hope that the booking of the Hotel Estrel (950 rooms) and Hotel Berlin (650 rooms) for athletes would create an atmosphere similar to an [Olympic Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Village).[^cite_note-Host2-5]

Overall, the event was an economic success for the capital. A total of 417,156 tickets were sold over the nine-day period, and estimates placed the total visitor spend in the city at around €120 million. As a result, Berlin's mayor, [Klaus Wowereit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Wowereit), stated that the city would consider applying to host another athletics event in the future, such as the [2016 European Athletics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_European_Athletics_Championships).[^cite_note-6]

### Media and marketing



Mascot "Berlino"

A limited edition [€](1797747.md)10 coin was produced for the event by the German government, which was only the third occasion that they had done so for a sporting event.[^cite_note-7] The organising committee held a contest to decide the name of its mascot, a running [anthropomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropomorphic) [bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear), and the name "[Berlino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlino)" was chosen.[^cite_note-8] The colour scheme of the event, including the official logo, advertising, and the [Olympiastadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiastadion_\(Berlin\))'s track and field, was blue and green. The committee stated that blue represented reliability while green represented the event's environmental ambitions.[^cite_note-Host2-5] The event featured a number of environmentally friendly initiatives, including: [free public transport](11163017.md) with every ticket sold, efforts to reduce energy usage, considerations for waste and recycling management, and environmentally conscious construction and building management. Furthermore, as part of the [United Nations Environment Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme), forty-seven trees (one for each athletics event) were planted to create an "Avenue of Champions" in Berlin.[^cite_note-9] The official song for the event was "[Foot of the Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_of_the_Mountain)" by [Norwegian](21241.md) group [A-ha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-ha).[^cite_note-boctv-10]

The broadcasting rights for the Championships were sold to 213 countries, a new high for the event.[^cite_note-boctv-10] [ARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_\(broadcaster\)) and [ZDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZDF) were the host broadcasting TV networks and producers of the TV signal, and they founded a company named BERTA which provided the signal in high-resolution HDTV for TV stations around the world.[^cite_note-11] The average viewing figures in Germany were 5 million with peaks of 9.9 and 8.6 million for the [men's 100 metres final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_100_metres) and the [women's high jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Women's_high_jump), respectively. The average audience figures in France were 3.5–4 million, 2.5–3.5 million in the United Kingdom and 4–5 million in Japan.[^cite_note-Debrief-12] The IAAF website received a record number of [page hits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Page_hit) and unique users: having around 1 million unique users accessing the website on days five and six,[^cite_note-Debrief-12][^cite_note-13] and a total of over 90 million page views over the course of the nine days of the competition.[^cite_note-14] Around 3500 media representatives were estimated to have attended the event.[^cite_note-FAQ-2]

To provide the public with an opportunity to participate in the event, the local organizers also conducted a **Champions Run 10K** on 22 August between the scheduled time for the men's and women's marathons, using a portion of the official marathon course which passes various Berlin landmarks with a finish at the [Brandenburg Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_Gate). The field was limited to 10,000 runners.[^cite_note-15]

The Championships were staged in the 74,845-seat Olympiastadion, which underwent a [€](9472.md)242 million renovation ahead of the [2006 Football World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-Host1-4] The [marathon](49132.md) races, as well as the [racewalking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racewalking) events, had their start and finish at the [Brandenburg Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_Gate),[^cite_note-16] with the race walks routed along the [Unter den Linden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unter_den_Linden) boulevard and the marathon passing through [Pariser Platz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pariser_Platz) and going past Berlin's other points of interest.[^cite_note-17] An estimated 400,000 tickets were sold by the event organisers for the event.[^cite_note-Debrief-12] In memory of their historic Olympic achievements at the Olympiastadion in 1936, a meeting took place between the families of [Luz Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luz_Long) and [Jesse Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Owens). Long's [long jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_jump) advice to rival Owens remains a prominent example of sportsmanship and friendship in athletics.[^cite_note-18] A reward of US\$100,000 was given to any athlete who broke a [world record](3443107.md) at the competition.[^cite_note-19]

### Anti-doping program

The event featured one of the most comprehensive anti-doping initiatives ever undertaken by the IAAF. A total of 1000 samples were collected from athletes and tested at labs accredited by the [World Anti-Doping Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Anti-Doping_Agency), and additional educational anti-doping activities were available. Diack stressed that samples are retained for future analysis, thus currently undetectable drugs could be tested for in the future, preventing athletes from flouting the anti-doping rules.[^cite_note-20]

Two athletes failed anti-doping tests during the championships: Moroccan steeplechaser [Jamel Chatbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamel_Chatbi) tested positive for the stimulant [clenbuterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clenbuterol) and Nigerian hurdler [Amaka Ogoegbunam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaka_Ogoegbunam) was found to have [Metenolone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metenolone), an anabolic steroid, in her sample.[^cite_note-21] Another Nigerian hurdler, [Olutoyin Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olutoyin_Augustus), was banned from the championships for having abnormal levels of [testosterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testosterone_\(medication\)).[^cite_note-22]

## Highlights

### Records

At the competition, three [world records](3443107.md), nine [Championship records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Championships_in_Athletics_records), eight [area records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_records_in_athletics?action=edit\&redlink=1) and 57 [national records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_records_in_athletics) were broken.[^cite_note-Debrief-12]

### Day 1 (15th)

[Valeriy Borchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriy_Borchin) of Russia won gold in the men's 20 km race walk in a time of 1\:18\:41, [Hao Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Hao_\(racewalker\)) of China won silver and [Eder Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eder_Sanchez) of Mexico won bronze.[^cite_note-30] [Linet Chepkwemoi Masai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linet_Chepkwemoi_Masai) of Kenya won gold in the women's 10,000 m in 30\:51.24, [Meselech Melkamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meselech_Melkamu) of Ethiopia won silver and the bronze medal went to [Wude Ayalew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wude_Ayalew) of Ethiopia.[^cite_note-31] In the men's shot put, [Christian Cantwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Cantwell) of the United States won gold with a mark of 22.03m. [Tomasz Majewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomasz_Majewski) of Poland took silver and [Ralf Bartels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralf_Bartels) of Germany took bronze.[^cite_note-32]

### Day 2 (16th)

In the women's 20 km race walk, the Olympic champion from last years games, [Olga Kaniskina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Kaniskina), took an expectant win by almost a full minute.[^cite_note-33] In the women's shot put, the Olympic gold medallist from last years games and defending world champion, [Valerie Vili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerie_Vili), won with a throw of 20.44.[^cite_note-34] In the men's 100 metres dash, [Usain Bolt](4764846.md) broke his own [100 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_metres) sprint world record with a time of 9.58. The defending world champion, [Tyson Gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyson_Gay) finished second with a time of 9.71, a US national record.[^cite_note-35] Britain's [Jessica Ennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Ennis-Hill) won the [heptathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heptathlon) title with a world-leading points score of 6731.[^cite_note-36]

### Day 3 (17th)

In the men's [hammer throw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammer_throw), the Olympic champion [Primož Kozmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primo%C5%BE_Kozmus) of Slovenia, pulled off the win with a throw of 80.84 m, which is a seasonal best. [Szymon Ziółkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szymon_Zi%C3%B3%C5%82kowski) of Poland achieved a result of 79.30 m earning him a silver medal and the Russian athlete [Aleksey Zagornyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksey_Zagornyi) earned third place with a throw of 78.09m.

In the men's [10,000 m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,000_m) final, [Kenenisa Bekele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenenisa_Bekele) won with a time of 26\:43\:31, which is a Championship record. [Zersenay Tadese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zersenay_Tadese) of [Eritrea](17238590.md) earned the silver medal with a time of 26\:50\:12 and [Moses Ndiema Masai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Ndiema_Masai) of [Kenya](188171.md) took the bronze with a time of 26\:57\:39.

In women's [100 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_metres), [Shelly-Ann Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelly-Ann_Fraser) of [Jamaica](15660.md) triumphed with the time of 10.73 s. [Kerron Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerron_Stewart) finished second with a time of 10.75s and American [Carmelita Jeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmelita_Jeter) took the bronze medal with a time of 10.90 s.

In women's [pole vault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_vault) final, the biggest surprise of the day was the Olympic champion and current world record holder, [Yelena Isinbayeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelena_Isinbayeva), failing to clear any height. [Anna Rogowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Rogowska) of Poland earned the gold with the result of 4.75 m. [Monika Pyrek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monika_Pyrek) and [Chelsea Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Johnson) shared second place with the result of 4.65 m. As a result, for the first time in history of World Championships in Athletics, two Polish athletes took gold and silver medal in the same event. Poland is 16th nation to win gold and silver in the same event in the history of World Championships in Athletics. The previous 15 nations were: Canada, China, [Cuba](5042481.md), [Ethiopia](187749.md), Finland, Germany, Great Britain, [Jamaica](15660.md), [Kenya](188171.md), [Romania](25445.md), Russia, Spain, United States and also Soviet Union and [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany).

In women's [triple jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_jump) final, [Yargelis Savigne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yargelis_Savigne) won the gold and [Mabel Gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabel_Gay) took second place. Both of the Cuban athletes did not cross the line of 15m.

In the women's [3000 m steeplechase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3000_m_steeplechase), [Marta Dominguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Dominguez) of Spain won the gold with a time of 9\:07\:32. [Yuliya Zarudneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuliya_Zarudneva) won the silver and [Milcah Chemos Cheywa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milcah_Chemos_Cheywa) earned the bronze medal.

### Day 4 (18th)

In men's [triple jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_jump), [Phillips Idowu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Idowu) of Great Britain, produced a world leading distance of 17.73 m earning him a gold medal. [Nelson Évora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_%C3%89vora) of Portugal achieved a result of 17.55 m earning him a silver medal and the Cuban athlete [Alexis Copello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Copello) earned third place with a jump of 17.36 m.

### Day 5 (19th)

In the discus final, [Robert Harting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Harting) of Germany won gold in front of a home crowd, trowing 69.43 metres. [Piotr Małachowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Ma%C5%82achowski) of Poland and [Gerd Kanter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerd_Kanter) of Estonia winning silver and bronze, respectively. Jamaican [Brigitte Foster-Hylton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigitte_Foster-Hylton) ran a season's best of 12.51 in the Women's 100 m hurdles to take gold. [Priscilla Lopes-Schliep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priscilla_Lopes-Schliep) of Canada (12.54) took silver and Jamaica's [Delloreen Ennis-London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delloreen_Ennis-London) won bronze.

### Day 6 (20th)

In the men's [200 metres](1833981.md), [Usain Bolt](4764846.md) broke his own world record with a time of 19.19 seconds. [Alonso Edward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonso_Edward) of Panama won silver with a national record of 19.81. [Wallace Spearmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Spearmon) of the USA won bronze, in 19.85. In the women's 400 m hurdles, Melaine Walker of Jamaica won in 52.42 sec, eight hundredths of a second outside [Yulia Pechonkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Pechonkina)'s world record (52.34). [Trey Hardee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_Hardee) of the USA had won the decathlon, but [Leonel Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonel_Su%C3%A1rez) of Cuba reversed positions on [Aleksandr Pogorelov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Pogorelov_\(athlete\)) in the final event.

### Day 7 (21st)

In the 200 m, [Allyson Felix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allyson_Felix) of the USA crossed the line first in 22.02 seconds with double Olympic champion [Veronica Campbell Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_Campbell_Brown) from Jamaican coming second with 22.35. In the 400 m men final, [LaShawn Merritt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaShawn_Merritt) and [Jeremy Wariner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Wariner) battled it out with Merritt securing gold with 44.06. Wariner ran a season's best of 44.60, winning the silver medal.

### Day 8 (22nd)

In the women's [hammer throw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammer_throw), [Anita Włodarczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anita_W%C5%82odarczyk) of Poland won gold medal with a distance of 77.96 m, which is a new world record. [Dwight Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_Phillips), USA, won the men's world long jump title for the third time with a jump of 8.54 metres. Phillips received his gold medal from [Jesse Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Owens)' granddaughter Marlene Dortch. [Godfrey Khotso Mokoena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfrey_Khotso_Mokoena) of South Africa won silver (8.47 m). Jamaica's 4 × 100 m relay teams highlighted the day by capturing the gold medal in both disciplines.

### Day 9 (23rd)

[Bai Xue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_Xue) of China wins gold in the women's marathon, [Kenenisa Bekele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenenisa_Bekele) of Ethiopia took the 5000 metres world title, and Olympic champion [Andreas Thorkildsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Thorkildsen) of Norway won the men's javelin with a throw of 89.59 metres. [Brittney Reese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittney_Reese) won the women's long jump with 7.10 metres, beating defending champion Tatyana Lebedeva of Russia. In the last two events of the Championships, the United States won both 4 × 400 m relays.

## Participating nations

The entry list released on the IAAF Website before the championships contained 2098 athletes from 202 countries and territories.[^cite_note-provisional-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Out of these athletes, a total of 1984 competed (1086 male, and 898 female) at the championships, with 201 of the 213 IAAF National Member Federations represented.[^cite_note-Debrief-12][^cite_note-40] The number of athletes competing at the event broke the previous championship record of 1,821 athletes set at the [1999 World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_World_Championships_in_Athletics) in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville). The 100 metres race attracted 100 entries, while the Marathon race listed 101 athletes for competition.[^cite_note-41]

The event was expected to be the largest sports gathering in 2009, continuing in the vein of the [World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Championships_in_Athletics) being the third largest sports event after the [Olympic Games](22576.md) and the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md).[^cite_note-42]
