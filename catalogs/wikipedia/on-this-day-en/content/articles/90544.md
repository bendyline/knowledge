**Côte-d'Or** (French pronunciation: [\[kot dɔʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e1/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-C%C3%B4te-d%27Or.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-C%C3%B4te-d%27Or.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e1/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-C%C3%B4te-d%27Or.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-C%C3%B4te-d%27Or.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [department](38523.md) in the [Bourgogne-Franche-Comté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgogne-Franche-Comt%C3%A9) [region](58846.md) in France. In 2023, it had a population of 540,100.[^cite_note-pophist-3] Its [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France) is [Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijon), which is also the regional prefecture; its [subprefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprefectures_in_France) are [Beaune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaune) and [Montbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montbard).

## History

Côte-d'Or is one of the original 83 departments created during the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) on 4 March 1790. Formed from part of the former [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_France) of [Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundy_\(historical_region\)), it is arguably unique among the departments in having a name that is poetic rather than geographic. The name was first proposed by the representative of Dijon ([fr\:Charles-André-Rémy Arnoult](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Andr%C3%A9-R%C3%A9my%20Arnoult)), and is said to refer to the autumn gold of the flanks of the hills in wine-growing areas. The literal meaning of *côte d'or* in French is "golden hill".[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-5]

## Geography

The department is part of the current [region](58846.md) of [Bourgogne-Franche-Comté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgogne-Franche-Comt%C3%A9). It is surrounded by the departments of [Yonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonne), [Nièvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C3%A8vre), [Saône-et-Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C3%B4ne-et-Loire), [Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_\(department\)), [Aube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aube), [Haute-Saône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Sa%C3%B4ne), and [Haute-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Marne).

A chain of hills called the Plateau de Langres runs from north-east to south-west through the department to the north of Dijon and continues south-westwards as the [Côte d'Or escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_d'Or_\(escarpment\)), from which the department takes its name. It is the south-east facing slope of this [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) which is the site of the celebrated [Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundy_wine) [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyard). To the west of the Plateau de Langres, towards [Champagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champagne_\(province\)), lies the densely wooded district of Châtillonais. To the south-east of the plateau and escarpment, the department lies in the broad, flat-bottomed valley of the middle course of the [Saône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Sa%C3%B4ne).[^cite_note-EB1911-6]

Rivers include:&#x20;

- The [Saône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C3%B4ne)

- The [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine) rises in the southern end of the Plateau de Langres.

- The [Ouche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouche) rises on the dip slope of the escarpment and flows to the Saône via Dijon.

- The [Armançon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arman%C3%A7on) rises on the dip slope of the escarpment and flows north-westward.

- The [Arroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arroux) rises on the dip slope of the escarpment at the southern end of the department.

### Climate

The climate of the department is continental, with abundant rain on the west side of the central range.

## Economy

Aerial view of neatly arranged vineyard rows covering green hills in Burgundy, France.

Vineyards on rolling hills in the Burgundy region of eastern France

This is a [wine-growing region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine-growing_region) of France. It produces the most expensive [Pinot noir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinot_noir) and [Chardonnay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chardonnay) wines from some of the most classified vineyards in the world. Wine from the Côte-d'Or was a favorite of the emperor Charlemagne. Other crops include cereal grains and potatoes. Sheep and cattle are also raised in the department. The region is famous for [Dijon mustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijon_mustard).

There are coal mines and [heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry), including steel, machinery, and earthenware. The industries most developed in Côte-d'Or are

- agriculture and food (14% of employees)

- metallurgy and metal manufacture (12% of employees)

- chemicals, rubber and plastics (12% of employees)

- pharmacy

- electrical and electronic components and equipment

- wood and paper industries.

The big works are generally in the conurbation of Dijon although the biggest (CEA Valduc) is at [Salives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salives) in the Plateau de Langres. There is also the SEB metal works at Selongey below the plateau on the margin of the Saône plain and the Valourec metalworking group at [Montbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montbard) in the west of the department on the River Brenne near its confluence with the Armançon. The [Pharmaceutical industry](560876.md) has shown the greatest growth in recent years. However, since the Dijon employment statistics zone includes the urban and administrative centre of the [Burgundy region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgogne), the [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector) is proportionately bigger there in relation to the industrial, than in the other three zones of Côte-d'Or.

- Reference [Industry in Bourgogne website](https://web.archive.org/web/20020605135912/http://www.industrie-bourgogne.org/Site/Template/IndexGeo.php?NumSecteur=0\&NumeroPage=0)

## Transport

### Air

Côte-d'Or is served by [Dole–Jura Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dole%E2%80%93Jura_Airport) which is located 51 km (32 mi) south east of Dijon. However, the airport only provides flights to limited European destinations. Residents in Côte-d'Or would normally use [Lyon-Saint Exupéry Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon-Saint_Exup%C3%A9ry_Airport), [Geneva Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Airport) and [EuroAirport Basel Mulhouse Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroAirport_Basel_Mulhouse_Freiburg) which provide more domestic and international destinations.

## Tourism

Some of the major tourist attractions are the Gothic abbey church of [Saint-Seine-l'Abbaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Seine-l'Abbaye) and the 11th-century Romanesque abbey church at [Saulieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saulieu), as well the 12th-century Château de Bussy Rabutin at [Bussy-le-Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bussy-le-Grand).[^cite_note-EB1911-6] The [Abbey of Cîteaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_C%C3%AEteaux), headquarters of the [Cistercian Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistercian_Order), lies to the east of [Nuits-Saint-Georges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuits-Saint-Georges) in the south of the department.
