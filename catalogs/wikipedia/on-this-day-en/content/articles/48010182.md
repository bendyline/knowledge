**Shimon Sakaguchi** (坂口 志文, *Sakaguchi Shimon*; born 19 January 1951) is a Japanese [immunologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunologist). He is a [distinguished professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Professor) at [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University) and a professor emeritus at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University).[^cite_note-IFReC_{{pipe}}_Osaka_University_Immunology_Frontier_Research_Center-1]&#x20;

His work includes the discovery of [regulatory T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_T_cells) and describing their role in the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system). In 2025, he was jointly awarded the [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine](52502.md) with [Mary E. Brunkow](81273907.md) and [Fred Ramsdell](60424610.md).[^cite_note-Nobel-2]&#x20;

## Early life and education

Shimon Sakaguchi was born on 19 January 1951 in [Nagahama, Shiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagahama,_Shiga).[^cite_note-3] He received a medical degree in 1976 from the [Faculty of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_School_of_Medicine_and_Faculty_of_Medicine,_Kyoto_University) at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University). In 1982, he also received a PhD degree from Kyoto University.[^cite_note-4]&#x20;

## Career

Sakaguchi undertook postdoctoral research in the United States at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) from 1983 to 1987 as a Lucille P. Markey Scholar.[^cite_note-jhuhub-5] Later, he worked as an assistant professor in the Department of Immunology at the [Scripps Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Research).[^cite_note-jhuhub-5]&#x20;

After returning to Japan in 1991,[^cite_note-jhuhub-5] he worked at [Riken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riken) as an investigator of the [Japan Science and Technology Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Science_and_Technology_Agency).[^cite_note-gairdnerprofile-6] Later, he became the head of the Department of Immunopathology at the Tokyo Metropolitan Institute of Gerontology.[^cite_note-\:1-7] Between 1998 and 2011, he worked as a professor and chairman of the Department of Experimental Pathology at the Institute for Frontier Medical Sciences of [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University). From 2007 to 2011, he also served as the institute's director.[^cite_note-osakanobelbio-8] His lab was moved to [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Osaka) in 2011.[^cite_note-\:1-7]&#x20;

## Research



Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2025: Pivotal role of FoxP3

In a 1995 study, Sakaguchi and his colleagues showed the existence of [regulatory T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_T_cell), a previously unknown subset of [T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T_cell) expressing [CD4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD4) and [CD25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD25) that modulate the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system) and help maintain [immune tolerance](5024592.md).[^cite_note-naturenobel-9] They injected [BALB/c](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BALB/c) athymic mice with a suspension of [CD4<sup>+</sup> cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD4%2B_cell) previously depleted of CD25<sup>+</sup> cells and found that the mice subsequently developed [autoimmune diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmune_disease) (e.g. [thyroiditis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroiditis) and [gastritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastritis)). However, reconstitution of CD4<sup>+</sup>CD25<sup>+</sup> cells shortly after the initial injection of CD4<sup>+</sup>CD25<sup>−</sup> cells prevented the development of autoimmunity.[^cite_note-nobelbackground-10] In 2003, Sakaguchi's group demonstrated the importance of [FOXP3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FOXP3) in the development and function of regulatory T cells.[^cite_note-naturenobel-9][^cite_note-nobelbackground-10]&#x20;
