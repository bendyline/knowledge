**Palm Beach County** is located in the southeastern part of [Florida](18933066.md), in the [Miami metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_metropolitan_area). It is Florida's third-most populous county after [Miami-Dade County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Dade_County,_Florida) and [Broward County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broward_County,_Florida) and the [24th-most populous in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_most_populous_counties_in_the_United_States), with 1,492,191 residents as of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-2020CensusPopulationByRace-2] Its [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) and largest city is [West Palm Beach](109634.md), which had a population of 117,415 as of 2020.[^cite_note-GR6-5] Named after one of its oldest settlements, [Palm Beach](109617.md), the county was established in 1909, after being split from Miami-Dade County. The county's modern-day boundaries were established in 1963.

Palm Beach County is one of the three counties that make up the [Miami metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_metropolitan_area), which was home to 6.14 million people in 2020. The area has been increasing in population since the late 19th century, with the incorporation of West Palm Beach in 1894 and after [Henry Flagler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Flagler) extended the [Florida East Coast Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_East_Coast_Railway) and built the [Royal Poinciana Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Poinciana_Hotel), [The Breakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Breakers_\(hotel\)), and [Whitehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagler_Museum). In 1928, the [Okeechobee hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Okeechobee_hurricane) [struck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landfall_\(meteorology\)) Palm Beach County and caused thousands of deaths. More recently, the county acquired national attention during the [2000 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_presidential_election), when a [controversial recount occurred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_presidential_election_recount_in_Florida).

In 2004, Palm Beach County was Florida's wealthiest county, with a [per capita personal income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_personal_income_in_the_United_States) of \$44,518.[^cite_note-6] It leads the state in agricultural productivity; agriculture is Palm Beach County's second-largest industry, after real estate development.[^cite_note-7] In undeveloped (central and western) Palm Beach County there is significant tropical agricultural production, especially [nurseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_nurseries), truck crops (vegetables), and [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane).[^cite_note-8] Palm Beach County has been called the "Winter Vegetable Capital" of the nation.[^cite_note-9]

## History

Main article: [History of Palm Beach County, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Palm_Beach_County,_Florida)



[Jupiter Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_Inlet_Light) situated in the Jupiter Inlet Lighthouse Outstanding Natural Area



A montage of images of impact by the [1928 Okeechobee hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Okeechobee_hurricane)

Around 12,000 years ago, Native Americans began migrating into Florida. An estimated 20,000 Native Americans lived in South Florida when the Spanish arrived. Their population diminished significantly by the 18th century, due to warfare, enslavement, and diseases from Europe.[^cite_note-10] In 1513, [Juan Ponce de León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Ponce_de_Le%C3%B3n) became the first European in modern-day Palm Beach County when he landed at the [Jupiter Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_Inlet).[^cite_note-11] Among the first non-Native American residents were [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American), many of whom were former slaves or immediate descendants of former slaves, arriving in what was then [Spanish Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Florida) in the late 17th century. Finding refuge among the [Seminoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminole), the former slaves or descendants of former slaves fought alongside them against [white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_American) settlers and bounty hunters during the [Seminole Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminole_Wars).[^cite_note-blacksites-12][^cite_note-13] Portions of the [Second Seminole War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Seminole_War) occurred in Palm Beach County, including the [Battles of the Loxahatchee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_the_Loxahatchee) in 1838.[^cite_note-14]

The county's oldest surviving structure, the [Jupiter Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_Inlet_Light), was built in 1860, after receiving authorization to the land from President [Franklin Pierce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Pierce) in 1854.[^cite_note-jlh-15] During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), Florida was a member of the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America). Two Confederate adherents removed the lighting mechanism from the lighthouse.[^cite_note-pbchojil-16] One of the men who removed the light, Augustus O. Lang, was also the first white settler in Palm Beach County. He built a palmetto shack along the eastern shore of [Lake Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Worth_Lagoon) in 1863 after abandoning the cause of the Confederacy.[^cite_note-17] After the Civil War ended, the Jupiter Lighthouse was relit in 1866.[^cite_note-pbchojil-16]

In October 1873, a [hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Five_\(1873\)) caused a shipwreck between [Biscayne Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscayne_Bay) and the [New River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_River_\(Broward_County,_Florida\)). The crew survived the wreck but nearly died due to starvation because of the desolation of the area. In response, five [Houses of Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houses_of_Refuge_in_Florida) were built along the east coast of Florida from the [Fort Pierce Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pierce_Inlet_State_Park) southward to Biscayne Bay. Orange Grove House of Refuge No. 3 was built near [Delray Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delray_Beach,_Florida) in 1876.[^cite_note-18]



The [Royal Poinciana Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Poinciana_Hotel) in 1900

Very few people lived in modern-day Palm Beach County prior to the arrival of [Henry Flagler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Flagler), who first visited in the early 1890s. A [Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil) tycoon, Flagler was instrumental in the county's development around the turn of the century. First, he purchased land on both sides of [Lake Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Worth_Lagoon). Other investors followed suit, causing a small boom and bringing in existing businesses and resulting in the establishment of many new businesses. The [Royal Poinciana Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Poinciana_Hotel), constructed by Flagler and his constructed crews to accommodate wealthy tourists, opened for business in February 1894. About a month later, the [Florida East Coast Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_East_Coast_Railway), owned by Flagler, reached West Palm Beach. On November 5, 1894, Palm Beach County's oldest city, West Palm Beach, was incorporated. In 1896, another hotel built by Flagler was opened, the Palm Beach Inn, later renamed [The Breakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Breakers_\(hotel\)).[^cite_note-19] He also constructed [his own winter home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagler_Museum), which he and his wife moved into in 1902.[^cite_note-20] The arrivals of Major [Nathan Boynton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Boynton), Congressman [William S. Linton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Linton), and railroad surveyor Thomas Rickards in the 1890s also proved important because they developed communities that later became [Boynton Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boynton_Beach,_Florida), Delray Beach, and [Boca Raton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Raton,_Florida), respectively.[^cite_note-21]

The [Florida Legislature](429346.md) voted to establish Palm Beach County in 1909, carving it out of what was then the northern portion of [Dade County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Dade_County,_Florida) and initially including all of [Lake Okeechobee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Okeechobee). The southernmost part of Palm Beach County was separated to create the northern portion of [Broward County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broward_County,_Florida) in 1915, the northwestern portion became part of [Okeechobee County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okeechobee_County,_Florida) in 1917, and southern [Martin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_County,_Florida) was created from northernmost Palm Beach County in 1925.[^cite_note-courthouse-22] The boundaries remained the same until 1963, when the Florida Legislature reduced Palm Beach County's share of Lake Okeechobee from about 80 percent to less than 40 percent and divided the lake more equitably among [Glades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glades_County,_Florida), [Hendry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendry_County,_Florida), Martin, and Okeechobee counties.[^cite_note-23] A final change to the county's boundaries occurred in 2009, when a small portion of land was given to Broward County.[^cite_note-24]

The 1910s and much of the 1920s brought prosperity and rapid population growth to South Florida, coinciding with the [Florida land boom of the 1920s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_land_boom_of_the_1920s). Many local historic districts and landmarks [listed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Palm_Beach_County,_Florida) in the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in Palm Beach County were designed and constructed during the 1920s, with the main contributors being architects [Maurice Fatio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Fatio), [Addison Mizner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addison_Mizner), [Marion Sims Wyeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Sims_Wyeth), and the firm [Harvey and Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_and_Clarke), which included [Gustav Maass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Maass_\(architect\)).[^cite_note-25] Total property value in West Palm Beach skyrocketed from \$13.6 million in 1920 to \$61 million in 1925, before briefly reaching a pre-[Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) peak of \$89 million in 1929. The city's population quadrupled between 1920 and 1927.[^cite_note-piland-26]

Early on September 17, 1928, [the Okeechobee hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Okeechobee_hurricane) made landfall near West Palm Beach as a category-4 storm and crossed Lake Okeechobee shortly thereafter.[^cite_note-HURDAT-27] While the hurricane caused catastrophic impact in eastern portions of the county, the Lake Okeechobee region suffered a much heavier loss of life. Wind-driven storm surge in the lake inundated hundreds of square miles, including the nearby communities of [Belle Glade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_Glade,_Florida), [Pahokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahokee,_Florida), and [South Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bay,_Florida). At least 2,500 deaths occurred, many of whom were black migrant farmers.[^cite_note-nwsokeechobee-28] An assessment of impact throughout the county reported 552 businesses destroyed, 1,447 businesses damaged, 3,584 homes destroyed, and 11,409 homes damaged, leaving 4,008 families homeless. However, several cities were excluded, such as Boca Raton, [Greenacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenacres,_Florida), [Lantana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantana,_Florida), and [South Palm Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Palm_Beach,_Florida).[^cite_note-92_views-29]<sup>: 3 </sup> Damage in South Florida totaled roughly \$25 million. In response to the storm, the [Herbert Hoover Dike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover_Dike) was constructed to prevent a similar disaster.[^cite_note-nwsokeechobee-28]

As a result of [1926 Miami hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Miami_hurricane) and the 1928 storm, Palm Beach County, along with the rest of South Florida, began suffering economic turmoil and pushed the region into the Great Depression, even before the [Wall Street Crash of 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_Crash_of_1929). Housing prices dropped dramatically in the county, with the total real estate value in West Palm Beach falling to \$41.6 million in 1930 and then to \$18.2 million in 1935.[^cite_note-30] However, the decade also brought an airport to the county. Morrison Field, later renamed the [Palm Beach International Airport](287471.md), opened in 1936.

After the U.S. entered [World War II](32927.md), it was converted to an [Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_Air_Force_Base) in 1942.[^cite_note-PBIA-31] During the war, thousands of servicemen arrived in Palm Beach County for training and supporting the war effort.[^cite_note-World_War_II-32] Following the conclusion of World War II, a number of veterans returned to the area for work, vacation, or retirement.[^cite_note-World_War_II-32] The base was closed and became a commercial airport again in 1962.[^cite_note-PBIA-31] Migration to the county by workers, tourists, and retirees continued into the 21st century.[^cite_note-33]

On August 28, 1949, a category-4 [hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Florida_hurricane) struck Lake Worth Beach with [maximum sustained winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_sustained_wind) of 130 mph (215 km/h),[^cite_note-HURDAT-27] causing considerable damage. Throughout Palm Beach County, the hurricane destroyed 65 homes and damaged 13,283 others.[^cite_note-34]

The area's first television station, [WIRK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WIRK)-TV Channel 21, began broadcasting on September 13, 1953. It went off the air less than three years later. However, [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) affiliate [WPTV-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WPTV-TV) and [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) affiliate [WPEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WPEC) first aired in 1954 and 1955, respectively – both of which are still in existence today.[^cite_note-35]

[Richard Paul Pavlick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Paul_Pavlick) nearly attempted to assassinate then President-elect [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) while the family vacationed in Palm Beach in December 1960. On December 11, Pavlick forwent his attempt because Kennedy was with his wife, [Jacqueline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Kennedy_Onassis), and their two children.[^cite_note-postman-36] Four days later, Pavlick's car, which had sticks of dynamite inside, was surrounded by police and he was arrested.[^cite_note-hate-37] Charges against Pavlick were dropped on December 2, 1963, 10 days after [Kennedy was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F._Kennedy) in [Dallas, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas,_Texas).[^cite_note-postman-36] Additionally, a secret [blast shelter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detachment_Hotel) was built on [Peanut Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut_Island) during his presidency because escalating [Cold War](325329.md) tensions.[^cite_note-38]

[Hurricane David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_David) struck near West Palm Beach late on September 3, 1979, with sustained winds of 100 mph (155 km/h). The storm's winds shattered windows in stores near the coast and caused property damage. A few roofs were torn off, and numerous buildings were flooded from over 6 in (150 mm) of rainfall. Damage in the county reached \$30 million, most of which was incurred to agriculture.



The "butterfly ballot" used during the 2000 election in Palm Beach County

The county became the center of controversy during the [2000 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_presidential_election). Allegedly, the ["butterfly ballot"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_election_in_Florida,_2000), designed by Palm Beach County Supervisor of Elections [Theresa LePore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_LePore), led to an unexpectedly large number of votes for [Reform Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Party_of_the_United_States_of_America) candidate [Pat Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Buchanan), rather than for [Democrat](5043544.md) [Al Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore). Due to the aforementioned "butterfly ballot" and the closeness of the statewide results between Gore and [Texas Governor](214883.md) [George W. Bush](3414021.md), the [Florida Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Supreme_Court) mandated manual recounts in all counties with disputed results. However, the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) overturned the decision in *[Bush v. Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bush_v._Gore)* on December 12, allowing [Florida Secretary of the State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_of_Florida) [Katherine Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Harris) to award the 25 electoral votes to Bush, as Harris's tally prior to the state-ordered recounts placed him ahead of Gore by 537 popular votes. In turn, this gave Bush victory in the national election.

Following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in 2001, a [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) investigation revealed that 12 of the 19 hijackers trained or resided in Palm Beach County during the months prior to the attacks.[^cite_note-nationalspot-39][^cite_note-nyt-40] Later that month, during the [anthrax attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_anthrax_attacks), a letter containing [spores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthrax) of this substance was mailed to the [American Media, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Media_\(publisher\)) building in Boca Raton. Three people were exposed to the anthrax, including [Robert Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Stevens_\(photo_editor\)), a photo editor who later died after an infection induced by exposure.[^cite_note-nationalspot-39]



[Hurricane Wilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Wilma) over South Florida on October 24, 2005

Three hurricanes severely impacted Palm Beach County in 2004 and 2005: [Frances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Frances), [Jeanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Jeanne), and [Wilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Wilma). On September 5, 2004, Frances made landfall in Martin County as a category-2 hurricane. With wind gusts in Palm Beach County peaking at 91 mph (146 km/h), the storm inflicted structural damage on about 15,000 houses and 2,400 businesses. Six deaths occurred in the county. Jeanne struck near the same location as a category-3 hurricane on September 26, 2004. The storm also brought strong winds, with an official wind gust of 94 mph (151 km/h). About 4,160 homes were damaged and 60 were destroyed. Jeanne left about \$260 million in damage in the county. On October 24, 2005, Hurricane Wilma struck [Collier County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collier_County,_Florida) as a category-3 hurricane. The storm moved northeastward, directly crossing Palm Beach County. Several locations reported hurricane-force winds, including a wind gust of 117 mph (188 km/h) in Belle Glade.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Over 90% of [Florida Power & Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Power_&_Light) customers lost electricity. Two deaths occurred in Palm Beach County.[^cite_note-43] The storm inflicted some degree of impact to more than 55,000 homes and 3,600 businesses. Palm Beach County suffered about \$2.9 billion in damages.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

In August 2012, the outer bands of [Hurricane Isaac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Isaac_\(2012\)) dropped at least 15.86 in (403 mm) of rain near [Lion Country Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Country_Safari). The consequent flooding left neighborhoods in [The Acreage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Acreage,_Florida), [Loxahatchee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loxahatchee,_Florida), [Loxahatchee Groves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loxahatchee_Groves,_Florida), [Royal Palm Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palm_Beach,_Florida), and Wellington stranded for up to several days.[^cite_note-48] As [Hurricane Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irma) approached in September 2017, mandatory or voluntary evacuations were ordered for more than 290,000 residents of Palm Beach County.[^cite_note-49] Although the storm passed well west of the county, much of the area experienced hurricane-force wind gusts, with a peak gust of 91 mph (146 km/h) in West Palm Beach.[^cite_note-nwsirma-50] Impact was generally limited to widespread power outages and damaged trees and vegetation, though isolated property damage was reported. The storm left about \$300 million in damage in the county,[^cite_note-nwsirma-50] as well as five fatalities.[^cite_note-51]

### Toponymy



The ship *Providencia*, wrecked off the coast of Florida, in 1878

The [coconut palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_palm), *Cocos nucifera*, is not native to Florida (nor anywhere else in the United States). Its presence in what is today Palm Beach County is due to the shipwreck of the Spanish ship *Providencia* in 1878, near today's [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md). It was traveling from [Havana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana,_Cuba) to [Cádiz, Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1diz,_Spain), with a cargo of coconuts.[^cite_note-52] The shipwreck was within walking distance of the shore—the Florida State Archives conserves a picture of a painting[^cite_note-53]—and a deliberate grounding so as to obtain an insurance payout has been proposed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOyer200126-54] The coconuts were salvaged, too many to be eaten, and thousands were planted.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOyer200127-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] A lush grove of palm trees soon grew on what was later named Palm Beach.[^cite_note-58]

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau), the county has a total area of 2,383 square miles (6,170 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 1,970 square miles (5,100 km<sup>2</sup>) are land and 413 square miles (1,070 km<sup>2</sup>) (17.3%) are covered by water.[^cite_note-GR1-60] It is the second-largest county in Florida by land area and third-largest by total area. Much of the water is the Atlantic Ocean and [Lake Okeechobee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Okeechobee). The county has an estimated 526,000 acres (213,000 ha) of farmland.

The eastern third of Palm Beach County is highly urbanized, while the central and western portions of the county are suburban or rural.[^cite_note-interesting-61] Palm Beach County is one of three counties in the [Miami metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_metropolitan_area).[^cite_note-62] However, the county's western communities along Lake Okeechobee, such as [Belle Glade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_Glade,_Florida), [South Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bay,_Florida), and [Pahokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahokee,_Florida), have also been considered more part of the rural [Florida Heartland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Heartland).[^cite_note-63]

The Atlantic coastline of Palm Beach County is about 47 mi (76 km) in length.[^cite_note-interesting-61] It consists mainly of barrier islands and peninsulas, including [Jupiter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_Island), [Singer Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singer_Island), and [Palm Beach Island](109617.md). These islands are separated from the mainland by the [Intracoastal Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracoastal_Waterway), with much of the waterway locally known as the [Lake Worth Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Worth_Lagoon). The main barrier landmasses are split by four inlets: the [Jupiter Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_Inlet_Light), the [Lake Worth Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Worth_Inlet), the [South Lake Worth Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lake_Worth_Inlet), and the Boca Raton Inlet. Two of the four inlets are natural, but significantly altered – the Jupiter and Boca Raton inlets – while the Lake Worth and South Lake Worth inlets are man-made, with the former dug in the 1890s and the latter created between 1926 and 1927.[^cite_note-64] Several other islands exist within the Intracoastal Waterway, including Hypoluxo Island, [Munyon Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munyon_Island), and [Peanut Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut_Island).[^cite_note-lwl-65]

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the county had a population of 1,492,191. The median age was 45.3 years. 19.0% of residents were under the age of 18 and 24.3% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 92.3 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 89.7 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-73]

The racial makeup of the county was 57.3% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans), 17.6% [Black or African American](2154.md), 0.6% [American Indian and Alaska Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 3.0% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), <0.1% [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) and [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander), 7.5% from some other race, and 14.0% from [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans). [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) residents of any race comprised 23.5% of the population.[^cite_note-Census2020PL-74]

99.2% of residents lived in urban areas, while 0.8% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-75]

There were 607,880 households in the county, of which 25.9% had children under the age of 18 living in them. Of all households, 45.0% were married-couple households, 18.0% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 30.3% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 29.9% of all households were made up of individuals and 15.7% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-73]

There were 705,988 housing units, of which 13.9% were vacant. Among occupied housing units, 67.4% were owner-occupied and 32.6% were renter-occupied. The homeowner vacancy rate was 2.0% and the rental vacancy rate was 8.3%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-73]

## Economy

See also: [List of companies based in Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companies_based_in_Miami)

Companies headquartered in Palm Beach County include [Office Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_Depot), [The ADT Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_ADT_Corporation), [TBC Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBC_Corporation), [G4S Secure Solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G4S_Secure_Solutions), [NextEra Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NextEra_Energy), [The GEO Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_GEO_Group), [American Sugar Refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Sugar_Refining), [Carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Global), [Globalsat Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalsat_Group), and [Bluegreen Vacations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegreen_Corporation).

There are a significant number of aerospace facilities in the county, operated by [United Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Technologies), [Pratt & Whitney Rocketdyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Rocketdyne), [Sikorsky Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_Aircraft), [General Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics), [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin), and [B/E Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B/E_Aerospace). Many of these companies rank among the top 100 employers for the county.[^cite_note-109] The largest employer in Palm Beach County is the [School District of Palm Beach County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_District_of_Palm_Beach_County), with 27,168 employees, including more than 12,800 teachers.[^cite_note-110]

Previously [W. R. Grace and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._R._Grace_and_Company) had its headquarters in [unincorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area) Palm Beach County, near [Boca Raton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Raton,_Florida), employing about 130 staff.[^cite_note-BRClosing-111] On January 27, 2011, it announced it was closing the Boca headquarters and moving its administrative staff out of state along with some employees.[^cite_note-BRClosing-111][^cite_note-112] Likewise, [A360 Media, LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Media_\(publisher\)), publisher of the *[National Enquirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Enquirer)*, was headquartered in Boca Raton, but moved New York in 2014.[^cite_note-113]

For 2010, the median income for a household in the county was \$53,242, and for a family was \$64,445. Males had a median income of \$44,324 versus \$37,337 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the county was \$33,610. About 8.6% of families and 12.2% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 18.5% of those under age 18 and 7.7% of those aged 65 or over.[^cite_note-114]

## Culture

### Sports

The [St. Louis Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Cardinals) and the [Miami Marlins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Marlins) of the [Major League Baseball](38776.md) conduct their [spring training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_training) at [Roger Dean Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Dean_Stadium) in [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter,_Florida). Two teams in the Class A-Advanced [Florida State League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_League) also play their home games at Roger Dean Stadium: the [Jupiter Hammerheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_Hammerheads), an affiliate of the Miami Marlins, and the [Palm Beach Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_Cardinals), an affiliate of the St. Louis Cardinals.

Prior to the construction of Roger Dean Stadium, the [Montreal Expos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Expos) and [Atlanta Braves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Braves) held their spring training at [Municipal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Palm_Beach_Municipal_Stadium) in West Palm Beach. The [West Palm Beach Expos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Palm_Beach_Expos), a Single-A affiliate of the Montreal Expos, also played their games there.

[The Ballpark of the Palm Beaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ballpark_of_the_Palm_Beaches), opened in February 2017 in West Palm Beach, accommodates both the [Washington Nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Nationals) and the [Houston Astros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Astros) for spring training.

Also popular are the [Florida Atlantic Owls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_University_Owls), an NCAA Division I school that participates in [American Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Athletic_Conference). The [FAU football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_Owls_football) team plays at [FAU Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAU_Stadium), and averaged 17,941 fans during the 2017 season.[^cite_note-115] The [FAU basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_Owls_men's_basketball) team plays at [FAU Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAU_Arena), and averaged 1,346 fans during the 2013–14 season.[^cite_note-116]

[USL Palm Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Palm_Beach) is an upcoming American professional [soccer](10568.md) team based in Palm Beach County. Founded in 2023, the team plans to make its debut in the [USL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship).

The [Palm Beach Imperials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_Imperials) are an [American Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_Association_\(21st_century\)) 2006 expansion franchise.

## Government

The [Florida Department of Corrections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Department_of_Corrections) operates the [Glades Correctional Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glades_Correctional_Institution) in an [unincorporated area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area) in Palm Beach County near Belle Glade.[^cite_note-122]

Palm Beach County's revenue from property taxes, sales taxes and tourist development taxes reached record levels in Fiscal Year (FY) 2018, according to the [Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller, Palm Beach County](https://www.mypalmbeachclerk.com/)'s annual financial report, *[Checks & Balances: Your Guide to County Finances.](https://www.mypalmbeachclerk.com/home/showdocument?id=1526)&#x20;*

The County collected \$1.1 billion in property tax revenue in FY 2018, an increase of 6 percent over the previous year. Sales tax collections rose to \$175.8 million, marking the eighth consecutive year of growth. Revenue from Tourist Development Tax receipts was \$53.8 million, up from \$48.5 million in FY 2017. Meanwhile, Local Option Gas Taxes paid by motorists for gasoline decreased for the first time in five years, partially due to higher gasoline prices, which reduced the number of miles driven, according to the Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller's *[Checks & Balances](https://www.mypalmbeachclerk.com/home/showdocument?id=1526)* report.[^cite_note-123]

### County government

The county is governed by a [board of commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_commission), consisting of seven commissioners, who are all elected from single-member districts. One of the commissioners is elected County Mayor and one of them is elected Vice Mayor.[^cite_note-124] Commissioners serve staggered terms, and commissioners from Districts 1, 3, 5, and 7 are elected during presidential election years, while the commissioners from Districts 2, 4, and 6 are elected in gubernatorial election years.

Elected county officers include a clerk of courts and comptroller, [sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_sheriff_\(Florida\)), property appraiser, [tax collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_collector), and [supervisor of elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervisor_of_Elections_\(Florida\)). State officers serving the Florida judicial district include the [state attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_attorney) and [public defender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_defender). All positions are 4-year terms, requiring direct election by voters in presidential election years.

Five former county commissioners have been accused or found guilty of corruption from 2006 to 2009. A grand jury recommended a strong [inspector general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_general). This position was approved by county voters in 2010. A county judge found that the mandate covered municipal government in 2015.[^cite_note-125]

### Legal and Judicial System

Palm Beach County is part of Florida’s **15th Judicial Circuit**, one of the 20 judicial circuits in the state.[^cite_note-126] The circuit court has jurisdiction over civil disputes involving more than \$30,000, felony criminal cases, probate and guardianship matters, juvenile dependency and delinquency, and appeals from county courts.[^cite_note-127] It serves a population of over 1.5 million residents and operates out of multiple courthouse facilities, including the main Palm Beach County Courthouse located in downtown West Palm Beach.&#x20;

The county court, also under the 15th Circuit, handles misdemeanor criminal cases, traffic infractions, civil disputes under \$30,000, and landlord-tenant matters.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] As of 2026, the Chief Judge of the 15th Judicial Circuit is Glenn D. Kelley, who oversees administrative functions and judicial assignments.[^cite_note-130]

The **Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office** (**PBSO**) provides police services to the county's unincorporated areas and the 13 municipalities covered by PBSO.[^cite_note-PBSO-132] PBSO also oversees the county jail system, provides security at all four of the county courthouses, and is the primary law enforcement agency covering [Palm Beach International Airport](287471.md).[^cite_note-133] The Sheriff's Office is composed of roughly 4,200 employees, including approximately 1,600 sworn law enforcement personnel, 700 sworn corrections personnel, 1,900 civilian personnel and 1,800 volunteers.[^cite_note-134] The annual operating budget for the PBSO is more than \$800 million, as of 2023.[^cite_note-135] Ric Bradshaw has been sheriff for Palm Beach County since 2005.[^cite_note-136]

There are two jail facilities operated by the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office. One is in Belle Glade, called the West Detention Center, which houses all custody levels from minimum to maximum security. At any one time it can contain up to 991 inmates (total bed capacity), with a staff of 188. The facility has a video visitation program which allows families to communicate with inmates remotely.[^cite_note-137] The largest jail is the Main Detention Center.[^cite_note-PBSO-132] Three towers - South, East, and West, make up the center.[^cite_note-pbso.org-138] The largest is the South Tower, constructed in 1993 from concrete and steel at a cost of \$52 million.[^cite_note-pbso.org-138] The East and West Towers were built in 1983; they now flank the South Tower, completing the Main Detention Center.[^cite_note-pbso.org-138] The Main Detention Center's primary function is to hold high risk inmates, federal inmates, and those inmates who are in need of special medical attention or are otherwise unable to operate at other facilities.[^cite_note-pbso.org-138] The total number of beds at the facility is 2,166. The South Tower can hold 1,285 inmates, the East Tower has 418 beds, and the West Tower has 404 beds.[^cite_note-pbso.org-138]

Since the founding of Palm Beach County in 1909, 16 people have served as county sheriff.[^cite_note-139]

As of 2025, 24 Officers and 1 K9 of the PBSO have been killed in the line of duty.[^cite_note-140]

## Transportation

### Roadways

An [advocacy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Growth_America) has criticized Palm Beach County's roadways for being dangerous for non-motorized users.[^cite_note-148] Local municipalities are working to increase safety, but county and state authorities have been hesitant to modify designs.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

#### Expressways

-  [Interstate 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95_in_Florida)

-  [Florida's Turnpike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida's_Turnpike)

I-95 and Florida's Turnpike are controlled-access expressways that serve Palm Beach county. [Southern Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Road_80) (signed [SR 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Road_80)/[US 98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_98_in_Florida)), which runs east–west through central Palm Beach County, is a partial freeway from Interstate 95 in [West Palm Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Palm_Beach) to [US 441](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_441_in_Florida)/[SR 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Road_7) in [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington,_Florida) and [Royal Palm Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palm_Beach). In the late 1980s, there were plans to construct two additional expressways in Palm Beach County. One was to be an 11.5 miles (18.5 km) toll freeway from Royal Palm Beach to downtown West Palm Beach. It would have run between Belvedere Road and [Okeechobee Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Road_704); necessitating the destruction of several homes and churches along its path. The other proposed route was a northern extension of the [Sawgrass Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_Road_869) which was to be called "University Parkway". The University Parkway would have snaked around suburban developments west of [Boca Raton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Raton,_Florida), [Delray Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delray_Beach,_Florida), and [Boynton Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boynton_Beach,_Florida); its path bordering the [Loxahatchee National Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loxahatchee_National_Wildlife_Refuge).[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] Ultimately, both expressways were canceled due to opposition from county residents.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

## Education

### Primary and secondary schools

All of Palm Beach County is served by the [School District of Palm Beach County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_District_of_Palm_Beach_County).[^cite_note-157] As of 2026, the School District of Palm Beach County remains one of the largest in the state and country, serving over 180,000 students across more than 180 schools.[^cite_note-158] [Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek) listed three Palm Beach County [magnet schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet_school)–[Atlantic Community High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Community_High_School), [Suncoast High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suncoast_High_School), and the [Alexander Dreyfoos School of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dreyfoos_School_of_the_Arts)–among the top 50 in its "1200 Top U.S. Schools" ranking.[^cite_note-159] Private schools in the county include [American Heritage School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Heritage_School_\(Delray_Beach,_Florida\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Cardinal Newman High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Newman_High_School_\(West_Palm_Beach,_Florida\)), [Jupiter Christian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_Christian_School), [The King's Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King's_Academy_\(West_Palm_Beach,_Florida\)), [The Benjamin School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Benjamin_School), [Oxbridge Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbridge_Academy), [Palm Beach Day Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_Day_Academy), [Pope John Paul II High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II_High_School_\(Florida\)), [St. Andrew's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Andrew's_School_\(Boca_Raton,_Florida\)), and [Weinbaum Yeshiva High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weinbaum_Yeshiva_High_School).

### Public libraries

Palm Beach County is served by the [Palm Beach County Library System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Beach_County_Library_System), established in 1967 through a Special Act of the [Florida Legislature](429346.md), and operates as a department of county government. It is currently made up of 17 library branches, as well as a bookmobile which travels to more than 40 stops each month. As Palm Beach County continues to see population growth, the library system will also need to plan for continued expansion.[^cite_note-162] Presently, the county is building a new 33,000-square-foot branch in the Canyon Town Center, located in western [Boynton Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boynton_Beach,_Florida).[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] This new branch is projected to be completed in 2024. The system's Main Library is located on Summit Boulevard in an unincorporated section of [West Palm Beach](109634.md). It is the largest provider of library services in the county, serving an area that is comparable to the size of the state of Delaware, with holdings of over 1.88 million items.[^cite_note-165]

Unlike many county library systems, including neighboring [Broward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broward_County,_Florida) and [Miami-Dade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Dade_County,_Florida) counties, several municipalities continue to operate their own libraries. The county library systems works together in a cooperative system model which allows interoperation between the county system and the 13 city libraries in [Boca Raton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Raton_Public_Library), Boynton Beach, [Delray Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delray_Beach_Public_Library), Highland Beach, Lake Park, Lake Worth Beach, Lantana, Manalapan (J. Turner Moore Public Library), North Palm Beach, Palm Springs, Palm Beach ([Society of the Four Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_the_Four_Arts)), Riviera Beach, and West Palm Beach ([Mandel Public Library of West Palm Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandel_Public_Library_of_West_Palm_Beach)).[^cite_note-166]

The county's first library began as a Free Reading Room in 1895 in West Palm Beach's first church, the Union Congregational Church, when Reverend Asbury Caldwell began collecting books for a reading club he hoped would keep construction workers out of the city's many drinking establishments located along First Street, or "Thirst Street" as it was known.[^cite_note-overdue-167]<sup>: 53 </sup> The reading club floundered when Caldwell left West Palm Beach, but in 1899, the West Palm Beach Public Library got its official start, housed in a two-story former Palm Beach Yacht Club building donated by Commodore Charles John Clarke, a Palm Beach yachtsman, with the collection of books from the Reading Room and a \$100 donation from [Henry Flagler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Flagler). A permanent building was constructed in 1924 in Flagler Park along the [Intracoastal Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracoastal_Waterway). Two additional buildings have also housed the library – one at 100 Clematis Street, a state-of-art building complete with a 250-seat auditorium that opened to much fanfare in 1964. The second, located at 411 Clematis Street, is a four-story building in the West Palm Beach City Center complex, which houses both city hall and the library, is two and one-half times the size of the previous building. In 2012, the West Palm Beach Public Library Foundation formally changed its name to the Mandel Public Library of West Palm Beach after receiving a \$5 million grant from the Mandel Foundation.[^cite_note-overdue-167]<sup>: 62 </sup>
