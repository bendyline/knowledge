# Paleontology in Thailand

&#x20; [Paleontology](23084.md) began as a subject of academic interest in [Thailand](30128.md) in the early twentieth century, mainly conducted by foreign researchers working with the Royal Department of Mines and Geology, the precursor of the [Department of Mineral Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mineral_Resources_\(Thailand\)) (DMR). Most early paleontological research was the by-product of mineral exploration for the country's developing mining industry.

The first scientifically described [fossil](10958.md) from Thailand was that of the bivalve *Posidonomya becheri siamensis* from Ban Khuan Dinso in [Phatthalung Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phatthalung_Province), made by [F. R. Cowper Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._R._Cowper_Reed) in 1920. Official cataloging of fossils began two years later, and received contributions by Western paleontologists and geologists. Systematic surveys and studies were later begun by Japanese researchers led by [Teiichi Kobayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teiichi_Kobayashi?action=edit\&redlink=1), who published their findings on [trilobites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilobite) in [Tarutao Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarutao_Island) (the oldest fossils found in the country) in 1957. Over the following decades, Japanese and German researchers conducted paleontological research as part of geological surveys throughout the country.[^cite_note-Directory-1]

[Dinosaur](8311.md) fossils were first discovered in the country in 1973, during uranium surveys in [Phu Wiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phu_Wiang) (now in [Wiang Kao District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiang_Kao_District)) in [Khon Kaen Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khon_Kaen_Province), initiating a wave of dinosaur research by Thai and French academics and a rise in public interest in the field. Several new dinosaur species have since been described from several sites in the country's [Northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Thailand), and paleontology museums have been established. Continued research is undertaken mainly by the DMR and some universities, as well as other public and private institutions.[^cite_note-Directory-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Thailand's first dinosaur discovery



Distal part of a left femur of a sauropod dinosaur regarded as the first dinosaur discovery of Thailand

Beginning in 1970, the [US Geological Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Geological_Survey) conducted a mineral exploration in the Phu Wiang area of Khon Kaen province and discovered a type of uranium ore, [coffinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffinite), in association with copper ores, [azurite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azurite) and [malachite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malachite). The [International Atomic Energy Agency](14984.md) (IAEA) later participated. Between 1975 and 1980, the Department of Mineral Resources conducted a detailed drilling program and in 1976 [Sudham Yaemniyom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudham_Yaemniyom?action=edit\&redlink=1), a geologist, discovered a piece of bone on a streambed, Huai Pratu Tima, which was later identified as a [distal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distal) part of the left [femur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femur) of a [sauropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropod) [dinosaur](8311.md),[^cite_note-4] regarded as the first dinosaur discovery of Thailand.

## Expedition and research

Since 1976, the Department of Mineral Resources, together with the Thai-French Paleontological Project, investigated the dinosaurs in the [Phu Wiang mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phu_Wiang_mountains). The project found many vertebrae, teeth, and dinosaur footprints mainly from sandstone of the Early [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) [Sao Khua Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sao_Khua_Formation) (about 130 million years old), including sauropod and [theropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theropod), with a wide range of sizes, from a hen up to a large [dinosaur](8311.md) with about 15 meters long. These finds led Thai people to visit the dinosaur sites, and Princess [Maha Chakri Sirindhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Chakri_Sirindhorn) visited Site 2 on 3 November 1989. She also revisited the site, viewing Site 3 and the [Phu Wiang Dinosaur Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phu_Wiang_Dinosaur_Museum), with the committees of the [Prince Mahidol International Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Mahidol_International_Award?action=edit\&redlink=1), of the [Prince Mahidol Award Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Mahidol_Award_Foundation), on 25 October 2008.
