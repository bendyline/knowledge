**Marcelo Nuno Duarte Rebelo de Sousa**[^cite_note-3] [GColTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Order_of_the_Tower_and_Sword) [GCIH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Prince_Henry) [ComSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Order_of_Saint_James_of_the_Sword) (born 12 December 1948) is a Portuguese politician and academic who served as the [president of Portugal](516093.md) from 2016 to 2026.[^cite_note-4] He is a member of the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)), though he suspended his party membership for the duration of his presidency.[^cite_note-5] Rebelo de Sousa has previously served as a [government minister](739237.md), parliamentarian in the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md), [legal scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_scholar), journalist, [political analyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_analyst), [law professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_professor), and [pundit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pundit).

## Early life

Born in [Lisbon](18091.md), Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa is the eldest son of [Baltazar Rebelo de Sousa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltazar_Rebelo_de_Sousa) (1921–2001) and his wife Maria das Neves Fernandes Duarte (1921–2003). He has claimed that his mother had Jewish ancestry.[^cite_note-6] He is named after [Marcelo Caetano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Caetano), the last prime minister of the [Estado Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\)) regime and a friend of his father.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa became a professor and [publicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicist) specialized in [constitutional law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_law) and [administrative law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_law), earning his doctorate at the [University of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lisbon), where he taught law.[^cite_note-9]

## Party politics and academic career

Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa started his career during the [Estado Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\)) as a lawyer, and later as a journalist.[^cite_note-\:2-10] He joined the [Popular Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)), becoming a [deputy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_\(legislator\)) to the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md).[^cite_note-\:3-11] During that time, he helped draft Portugal's [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Portugal) in 1976.[^cite_note-12] Later he rose to [Minister](739237.md) of Parliamentary Affairs under [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister) [Francisco Pinto Balsemão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Pinto_Balsem%C3%A3o).[^cite_note-13] Together with him he was a co-[founder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrepreneur), [director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_directors) and [administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_administration) of the *[Expresso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expresso_\(Portuguese_newspaper\))* [newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper),[^cite_note-\:2-10] owned by Pinto Balsemão. He was also a founder of [Sedes.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEDES)[^cite_note-14] He worked with another newspaper, *Semanário*, between 1983 and 1987.[^cite_note-\:2-10] He started as a [political analyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_analyst) and [pundit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pundit_\(expert\)) on the radio broadcaster [TSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSF_\(radio_station\)) with his *Exams*,[^cite_note-\:2-10] in which he gave marks (0 to 20) to the main political players.

In 1989 he ran for [President of the Municipal Chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Municipal_Chamber) of [Lisbon](18091.md) ([Mayor of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Lisbon)) but lost to [Jorge Sampaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Sampaio), though he did win a seat as [City Councilor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Council) (*[Vereador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vereador)*).[^cite_note-15] In that campaign he took a plunge into the waters of the [Tagus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus_River) to prove they were not polluted despite claims to the contrary.[^cite_note-16] In other local elections, he also became the president of the [Municipal Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembleia_Municipal) of [Cascais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascais) (1979–1982)[^cite_note-17] and the president of the Municipal Assembly of [Celorico de Basto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celorico_de_Basto) (1997–2009).[^cite_note-18]

### Leader of the PSD, 1996–1999

Rebelo de Sousa was the leader of the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)) from 31 de March 1996 to 1 May 1999.[^cite_note-\:3-11] He created a center-right coalition, the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(Portugal,_1979\)), with the [People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_\(Portugal\)) in 1998. He became, however, the [vice-president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-President) of the [European People's Party–European Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party%E2%80%93European_Democrats). The coalition did not please large parts of its own party, due to the role the People's Party leader, [Paulo Portas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Portas), had in undermining [Aníbal Cavaco Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C3%ADbal_Cavaco_Silva)'s government while director of the weekly *[O Independente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Independente)*.

### Post-leadership

He had a weekly program of political analysis every Sunday on [public TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_TV) station [RTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A1dio_e_Televis%C3%A3o_de_Portugal) after previously having a similar program on the private TV station [TVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televis%C3%A3o_Independente). President [Jorge Sampaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Sampaio) dissolved the Assembly of the Republic, a move that also meant dismissing the Government at a time when it had a stable coalition majority, and calling for anticipated elections, which led to the defeat of Santana Lopes and the election of the Socialists under [José Sócrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_S%C3%B3crates).

In 2010, he left RTP and returned to TVI to do the same program that he had before.

He was made a member of the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_\(Portugal\)), by President [Aníbal Cavaco Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C3%ADbal_Cavaco_Silva), and was sworn in on 6 April 2006.[^cite_note-19]

He was a leading figure on the [anti-abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-abortion) side of the [2007 abortion referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Portuguese_abortion_referendum). He even founded a website titled "Assim Não" (Not like this), which was divulged with a famous introductory video.[^cite_note-20] It became so well known that it was parodied in *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*-fashion by famous humour group [Gato Fedorento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gato_Fedorento).[^cite_note-21]

President-elect Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa delivering his victory speech on election night, 24 January 2016

On 24 January 2016, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Portuguese_presidential_election) as [President of Portugal](516093.md) in the first round of voting. He stood as an independent, appealing for moderation and cross-party consensus.[^cite_note-22] During his election campaign, he promised to repair political divisions and the hardship of Portugal's 2011–14 bailout. Unlike his predecessor, [Aníbal Cavaco Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C3%ADbal_Cavaco_Silva), he had never previously held a top state position.[^cite_note-23]

In March 2020, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa asked parliament to authorize a [state of emergency](298608.md) to contain the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md); this marked the first time the country declared a state of emergency nationwide in 46 years of democratic history.[^cite_note-24]

In December 2020, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa announced his intention to run for office again in the [2021 Portuguese presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Portuguese_presidential_election).[^cite_note-25] Marcelo was re-elected president in January 2021, with 60.7% of the votes, the third highest vote margin ever in presidential elections in Portugal since the [Carnation Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution). He was also the first candidate ever to win the vote in all [municipalities](317199.md), ranging from 51.3% in the [Beja District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_District) to 72.16% in [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira).[^cite_note-26]

During his presidency, Rebelo de Sousa has publicly supported making restitution and acknowledging abuses made during Portugal's colonialist history and the country's role in the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md).[^cite_note-27]

### Health

In December 2017, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa underwent emergency surgery at [Curry Cabral Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_Curry_Cabral) in order to treat an incarcerated [umbilical hernia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbilical_hernia).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] The procedure was performed by [Eduardo Barroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Barroso?action=edit\&redlink=1), a friend of the president.[^cite_note-34] He was discharged from the hospital and lauded the Portuguese National Health Service, considering it an important achievement of Portuguese democracy.[^cite_note-35]

In June 2018, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa was briefly hospitalized after he collapsed after a visit to [Bom Jesus do Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bom_Jesus_do_Monte) sanctuary in [Braga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braga); the incident was caused by a sudden drop in [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure) alongside acute [gastroenteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroenteritis).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

In October 2019, he underwent planned [cardiac catheterization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_catheterization) at Santa Cruz Hospital, Carnaxide, in the outskirts of Lisbon, after accumulated calcium was detected in one of his coronary arteries.[^cite_note-38]

On 8 March 2020, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa suspended all his public agenda and returned to his private home in [Cascais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascais), entering a voluntary quarantine period for 14 days after being revealed that a group of students from [Felgueiras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felgueiras), who had visited [Belém Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel%C3%A9m_Palace) some days before, had also been quarantined after a positive case of [COVID-19](63030231.md) was detected in their school.[^cite_note-39] Marcelo subsequently tested negative for the virus and worked remotely for a period during the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]



Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa wearing a [protective mask](63631542.md) in 2021, during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md)

On 11 January 2021, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa tested positive for COVID-19, after a contact with a positive case in his staff. He was reportedly asymptomatic, and canceled his appointments, opting to remain in [self-isolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolation_\(health_care\)#Self-isolation).[^cite_note-45] Three further COVID-19 tests yielded negative results.[^cite_note-Expresso12Jan-46] Some physicians said that a [false positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_positive) PCR-RT test, although possible, was unlikely,[^cite_note-Expresso12Jan-46] and tentatively attributed the subsequent negative tests to a low [viral load](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_load).[^cite_note-47] On 13 January, however, the [Lisbon and Tagus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisboa_e_Vale_do_Tejo) regional public health authority confirmed that the president was considered to have had a low-risk exposure, and was therefore simply under [passive surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health_surveillance) for two weeks: the president was instructed that he could resume his agenda save for any events in crowded public places.[^cite_note-48]

In December 2021, he underwent planned surgery to remove two inguinal hernias at the Military Hospital in Lisbon.[^cite_note-49]

On 5 July 2023, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa collapsed during a visit to the [NOVA University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOVA_University_Lisbon) [School of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOVA_School_of_Science_and_Technology), in [Almada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almada). He was taken to Santa Cruz Hospital "as a precaution". His chief of staff, Fernando Frutuoso de Melo, assured the situation was probably due to the heat and to the president's "heavy schedule". After being submitted to several medical exams, the president was discharged four hours later, with a [Holter monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holter_monitor), and addressed journalists on his way out from the hospital, saying he had had an episode of [low blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotension) since he had skipped lunch — as he usually does, replacing it with Fortimel, a medical nutrition supplement — and had been offered a glass of warm [moscatel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set%C3%BAbal_DOC) shortly before he fainted that "must have upset, probably, \[his] digestion". The physicians recommended rest, though the president's personal physician, Daniel de Matos, remarked their recommendation would in all likelihood fall on deaf ears. His scheduled presence at several events was nonetheless cancelled until 9 July, but the president recorded video messages to be displayed at those events. Only indoor audiences at Belém Palace remained scheduled.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

On 1 December 2025, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa was admitted to [University Hospital of São João](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Hospital_of_S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o), in [Porto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto), for urgent surgery due to an incarcerated [hernia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernia), after feeling unwell.[^cite_note-54]

## Personal life

On 22 July 1972, Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa married Ana Cristina da Gama Caeiro da Mota Veiga in the parish of São Miguel de Machede in [Évora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89vora).[^cite_note-55] His wife, born on 4 June 1950 in the [Santos-o-Velho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos-o-Velho) parish of Lisbon, is the daughter of [António da Mota Veiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_da_Mota_Veiga) and Maria Emília da Gama Caeiro. In the following years, Sousa and Mota Veiga had two children:

- Nuno da Mota Veiga Rebelo de Sousa (b. [São Sebastião da Pedreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Sebasti%C3%A3o_da_Pedreira), [Lisbon](18091.md), 8 August 1973) and

- Sofia da Mota Veiga Rebelo de Sousa (b. São Sebastião da Pedreira, Lisbon, 27 September 1976).

The couple separated in 1980 and divorced in 1983,[^cite_note-flash.pt-2] although Marcelo, citing his [Roman Catholic](606848.md) faith, believes marriage lasts until death.[^cite_note-56] He started dating his former student Rita Amaral Cabral in 1981, who at the time was his fellow lecturer at the Faculty of Law of the [University of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lisbon).[^cite_note-57] They continue to entertain a casual relationship, but live separately.[^cite_note-\:1-58]

## Controversies

In 2023, President of Portugal Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa became embroiled in a controversy following a [TVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televis%C3%A3o_Independente) program that suggested he had intervened to expedite treatment for Brazilian twins with [Zolgensma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zolgensma), a rare and highly expensive drug administered in a single dose costing two million euros. Allegations of corruption and influence peddling have emerged surrounding this event, also implicating the president's son, who is reportedly a friend of well-connected Brazilians linked to the family of the Brazilian children. These claims have been raised by various sectors of Portuguese politics and the media.[^cite_note-59] Between July 2019 and July 2021, Infarmed, the Portuguese medicines agency, approved 17 applications for authorisation of the medicine given to the Brazilian twins. In most cases, authorisation took no more than a day.[^cite_note-60]

On 23 April 2024, he compared the current and previous prime ministers, saying that "[António Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Costa) was slow, because he is oriental" and [Luís Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Montenegro), a rural personality in Sousa's opinion, "is not oriental but he is slow", at a dinner with foreign journalists.[^cite_note-61]

On 27 August 2025, while addressing university students at a [PSD Summer University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)) event in [Castelo de Vide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelo_de_Vide), Rebelo de Sousa called the [US president](24113.md), [Donald Trump](4848272.md), a ["Russian asset"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Links_between_Trump_associates_and_Russian_officials), after the [2025 Russia–United States Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Russia%E2%80%93United_States_Summit).[^cite_note-62] He further clarified that American leadership has recently favored the Russian Federation.[^cite_note-63]
