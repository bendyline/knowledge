The **N1** is a [national route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_route_\(South_Africa\)) in [South Africa](17416221.md) that runs from [Cape Town](6653.md) through [Bloemfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein), [Johannesburg](58893.md), [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) and [Polokwane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polokwane) to [Beit Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beitbridge,_Limpopo) on the border with [Zimbabwe](34399.md).[^cite_note-\:1-1] It forms the first section of the famed [Cape to Cairo Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_to_Cairo_Road).

Prior to 1970, the N1 designation was applied to the route from Beit Bridge to [Colesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colesberg) and then along the current [N9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N9_road_\(South_Africa\)) to George. The section from Cape Town to [Colesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colesberg,_Northern_Cape) was designated the N9.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

#### Within Cape Town

The N1 begins in central [Cape Town](6653.md) at the northern end of Buitengracht Street ([M62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M62_\(Cape_Town\))), outside the entrance to the [Victoria & Alfred Waterfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_&_Alfred_Waterfront). The first section of the N1 is [shared with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrency_\(road\)) the beginning of the [N2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N2_\(South_Africa\));[^cite_note-\:1-1] it is a four-lane [elevated freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevated_freeway) that runs along a strip of land between the [city centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town_CBD) and the [Port of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Cape_Town). On the eastern edge of the city centre the two roads split, and the N1 turns east as Table Bay Boulevard, passing the [Ysterplaat Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFB_Ysterplaat) and [Century City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_City_\(Cape_Town\)) before the [N7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N7_\(South_Africa\)) intersects it on its own way out of the city towards [Namibia](21292.md).[^cite_note-\:1-1]

Major improvements have been made to the Koeberg Interchange, where the N1 meets the [M5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M5_road_\(Cape_Town\)), one of the main arterial routes linking [Milnerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnerton) with the [Southern Suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Suburbs,_Cape_Town).[^cite_note-3] The N1 then heads through the suburbs of [Goodwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodwood,_Cape_Town) and [Bellville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellville,_Cape_Town), where the [R300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R300_\(South_Africa\)) terminates at it, before heading towards [Paarl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paarl,_Western_Cape).[^cite_note-\:1-1]

Within the [City of Cape Town](4043753.md) the volume of two-way traffic ranges between 95,000 and 120,000 vehicles a day.[^cite_note-\:2-4] During week days during peak traffic times (inbound towards the [Cape Town city bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Bowl) in the morning and outbound in the afternoon) traffic jams extending up to 12 kilometers in length are common.[^cite_note-\:2-4]

#### Rest of the province

At Paarl, the freeway ends, and the N1 is tolled as it passes through the [Huguenot Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot_Tunnel) running underneath the [Du Toitskloof Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Toits_Peak);[^cite_note-\:1-1] the tunnel was opened in the late 1980s to replace the old [Du Toitskloof Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Toitskloof_Pass) (now designated as part of the [R101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R101_\(South_Africa\))) running over the mountain. Traffic volumes through the tunnel range from an average of 12,000 vehicles daily[^cite_note-5] with up to 22,500 vehicles using it daily in peak periods on holidays.[^cite_note-6]

After emerging from the tunnel, the N1 winds through the Molenaar River Valley (which is a short dual carriageway section) before emerging from the valley and heading towards [Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_Western_Cape), bypassing [Rawsonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawsonville). From Worcester, the route heads through the [Hex River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hex_River_Valley), passing [De Doorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Doorns) and then enters the [Karoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoo) by ascending the [Hex River Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hex_River_Pass) en route to [Touws River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touws_River_\(town\)).[^cite_note-\:1-1]

Currently only the section of the N1 passing through the Huguenot Tunnel is tolled, although there were formerly plans to toll the N1 from the junction with the R300, roughly to [De Doorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Doorns).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-\:5-8] This would have allowed for upgrading of the N1, most especially the opening and construction of the Northern Bore of the Huguenot Tunnel so that two lanes of traffic could pass in each direction through the tunnel, and the building of grade separated junctions along the N1 through Worcester. Although the town centre is bypassed, there are a number of traffic lights on the N1 through Worcester.[^cite_note-\:5-8]

From the top of the pass, the N1 passes [Touws River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touws_River_\(town\)) and [Matjiesfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matjiesfontein) before passing through [Laingsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laingsburg,_Western_Cape), then heads towards [Beaufort West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_West), passing the towns of [Prince Albert Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Albert_Road) and [Leeu-Gamka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeu-Gamka).[^cite_note-\:1-1] The 200 km section between Laingsburg and Beaufort West is notorious for claiming many lives in fatigue-related accidents;[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] also, the N1 begins to turn towards the north-east along this stretch of road. Just before Beaufort West, the [N12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N12_\(South_Africa\)) from [George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George,_Western_Cape) meets the N1; the N12 and the N1 routes are [co-signed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrency_\(road\)) through Beaufort West and for the next 75 km north-east before splitting at [Three Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Sisters,_Northern_Cape).[^cite_note-\:1-1] The N12 later meets the N1 again in [Johannesburg](58893.md), making the N12 an alternative route to the N1, passing through [Kimberley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberley,_Northern_Cape) as opposed to [Bloemfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein). The N1 from Bloemfontein onwards is tolled while the N12 is toll-free. Whereas the N12 passes through most of the towns en route to Johannesburg, the N1 bypasses every town between Beaufort West and Johannesburg (avoiding town centres).

The N1 briefly crosses into the [Northern Cape](447008.md) at Three Sisters for a few kilometres before crossing back into the [Western Cape](313646.md), and remains in the Western Cape until just after its intersection with the [R63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R63_\(South_Africa\)), where it re-enters the Northern Cape.[^cite_note-\:1-1]

### Northern Cape

The N1 has a short section in the [Northern Cape](447008.md). After re-entering the Northern Cape, it passes north-east past the town of [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Northern_Cape) before intersecting with the [N10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N10_\(South_Africa\)) at [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover,_South_Africa). The N1 then continues towards [Colesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colesberg), where it meets the northern terminus of the [N9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N9_\(South_Africa\)) just south-west of the town. Approximately 35 km after Colesberg, the N1 crosses the [Orange River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_River) and enters the [Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_\(South_African_province\)).[^cite_note-\:1-1]

### Free State

&#x20;After the Orange River crossing, the N1 makes a direct line for [Bloemfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein), passing the towns of [Springfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfontein), [Trompsburg](17219947.md) and [Edenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edenburg), heading in a more northerly direction.[^cite_note-\:1-1] Upon entering Bloemfontein, the N1 meets the northern terminus of the [N6](15092828.md) from [East London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_London,_Eastern_Cape). This intersection marks the beginning of the [Bloemfontein Western Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein_Western_Bypass), which is the first freeway section on the route since Paarl. The [N8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N8_\(South_Africa\)) from [Kimberley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberley,_Northern_Cape) in the west intersects with the N1 bypass, joining it for 3 km, before heading east through the [Bloemfontein CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein_Central) and then to [Maseru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maseru) in [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho).[^cite_note-\:1-1]&#x20;



A road sign on the N1 in [Bloemfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein) between the two N8 interchanges

&#x20;The N1 is designated as a [toll road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road) from the N6 interchange until its end at Beit Bridge. A few kilometres north of Bloemfontein, the N1 meets the [R30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R30_\(South_Africa\)) to [Brandfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandfort); the N1 continues as a single carriageway but with two lanes in each direction until 5 km before the toll plaza at [Verkeerdevlei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkeerdevlei), halfway between Bloemfontein and Winburg. Initial plans were for the N1 from Bloemfontein to Winburg to be a dual-carriageway freeway. At [Winburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winburg), the N1 bypasses the town to the west and meets the western terminus of the [N5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N5_\(South_Africa\)), which connects with [Senekal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senekal), [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_South_Africa) and [Harrismith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrismith).[^cite_note-\:1-1]



The N1 northbound as it enters [Ventersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventersburg) (2015)

The N1 continues north and bypasses [Ventersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventersburg) before reaching [Kroonstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kroonstad). There, the [R34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R34_\(South_Africa\)) from [Welkom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welkom) joins the N1 freeway for 9 kilometres, bypassing Kroonstad Central to the east, before splitting from the N1 and making its own way towards [Heilbron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbron).[^cite_note-\:1-1]

After Kroonstad, the N1 heads towards the [Vaal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_River) and [Gauteng](309661.md) as the Kroonvaal Toll Route. Just before passing into Gauteng at the Vaal River, the N1 features another toll plaza (the Vaal Toll Plaza) just south of its interchange with the [R59 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R59_\(South_Africa\)), which provides access to the [Vaal Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_Triangle) ([Vereeniging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vereeniging) and [Sasolburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasolburg)) in the east and [Parys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parys) in the west.[^cite_note-\:1-1]

### Gauteng

After crossing the Vaal River, the N1 continues towards [Johannesburg](58893.md), bypassing [Vanderbijlpark](1413961.md) and featuring another toll plaza at [Grasmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennerdale,_South_Africa). At the Misgund Interchange in the southern outskirts of Johannesburg, the [N12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N12_\(South_Africa\)) once again meets the N1, and they are [co-signed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrency_\(road\)) northwards as one highway for 4 kilometers (bypassing [Soweto](28573.md)) up to the Diepkloof Interchange, where the N12 splits off eastwards to become the [Southern Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N12_Southern_Bypass_\(South_Africa\)) portion of the [Johannesburg Ring Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg_Ring_Road).[^cite_note-\:1-1] From just north of the Vaal River the N1 changes from being a tarred road to a concrete road, until just after meeting the N12.



Road sign at the [R553 Golden Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R553_\(South_Africa\)) off-ramp before the Diepkloof Interchange

The N1 then becomes the [Western Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_Western_Bypass_\(South_Africa\)) portion of the same ring road, passing through Johannesburg's western and north-western suburbs (forming [Roodepoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roodepoort)'s eastern boundary and passing through [Randburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randburg,_Gauteng)) before bending east and meeting the northern termini of the [N3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N3_\(South_Africa\)) (the [Eastern Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N3_Eastern_Bypass_\(South_Africa\)) portion of the Johannesburg Ring Road, which connects to [Durban](313650.md)) and Johannesburg's own [M1 freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_\(Johannesburg\)) at the Buccleuch Interchange north-east of [Sandton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandton).[^cite_note-\:1-1]



The N1 between [Johannesburg](58893.md) and [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) as part of the [Ben Schoeman Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Schoeman_Highway) (2011).

The N1 then becomes the [Ben Schoeman Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Schoeman_Highway), heading northwards towards [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) (passing through [Midrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midrand)); this section carries 160,000 vehicles per day as of 2011[^cite_note-11] and is purported to be the busiest stretch of road in [South Africa](17416221.md).[^cite_note-12] At the Brakfontein Interchange in [Centurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centurion,_Gauteng), the N1 meets the [N14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N14_\(South_Africa\)) and they switch highways, with the N14 becoming the Ben Schoeman Highway northwards to [Pretoria Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria_CBD) and the N1 becoming the [Pretoria Eastern Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria_Bypass) (named the Danie Joubert Freeway) towards the north-east, proceeding to intersect with the [R21 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R21_\(Gauteng\)) coming from [O. R. Tambo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._R._Tambo_International_Airport) at the Flying Saucer Interchange before Pretoria East.[^cite_note-\:1-1] After the R21 interchange, the N1 proceeds in a more northerly direction through the eastern suburbs. East of Pretoria CBD, at the Proefplaas Interchange, the [N4 national route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N4_\(South_Africa\)) from [Witbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witbank) in the east joins the N1 and they are [one highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrency_\(road\)) for 12 km northwards before the N4 splits off to the west to become the [Platinum Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_Highway) ([Pretoria Northern Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria_Bypass)) towards [Brits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brits,_North_West) and [Rustenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustenburg,_North_West).[^cite_note-\:1-1] At this interchange with the Platinum Highway (N4) is the Pumulani Toll Plaza on the N1 north and the Doornpoort Toll Plaza on the N4 west.

From the interchange with the N4 Platinum Highway, the N1 is tolled for the remainder of its length, with various toll plazas located along it (including upon off-ramps and on-ramps). The N1 heads to the north, bypassing [Hammanskraal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammanskraal), and crosses into the [Limpopo](365944.md) province.[^cite_note-\:1-1]

### Limpopo

The N1 then passes near [Bela-Bela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bela-Bela) (previously Warmbaths) and [Modimolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modimolle) (previously Nylstroom).[^cite_note-\:1-1] At the Modimolle exit, the freeway ends; the section of freeway between the [Vaal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_River) and Modimolle is the longest freeway in South Africa by route number at approximately 265 km (although there are two changes in the alignment of freeway in [Gauteng](309661.md), at the Buccleuch and Brakfontein Interchanges; South Africa's longest continuous freeway is the [N3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N3_\(South_Africa\)) between [Durban](313650.md) and [Ladysmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladysmith,_KwaZulu-Natal), which is approximately 20 km shorter). The section from the Proefplaas Interchange in Pretoria to the [R516](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R516_\(South_Africa\)) Bela Bela exit is maintained by a private concessionaire, namely [Bakwena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakwena_Platinum_Corridor_Concessionaire?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] under license from [SANRAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SANRAL).

The N1 then heads past [Mokopane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokopane) (previously Potgietersrus), where the [N11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N11_\(South_Africa\)) intersects it at the Nyl Toll Plaza (north off-ramp only) (leaving the [N18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N18_\(South_Africa\)) and the [N17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N17_\(South_Africa\)) as the only [national roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Roads_in_South_Africa) that do not intersect with the N1), before heading to [Polokwane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polokwane) (previously known as Pietersburg).[^cite_note-\:1-1] The [Polokwane Eastern Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polokwane_Eastern_Bypass) is now used by traffic to loop around the Polokwane city centre (the N1 is no-longer the road passing through the city centre).[^cite_note-15]

After Polokwane, the N1 heads north, crossing the [Tropic of Capricorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Capricorn) before passing [Louis Trichardt](30861369.md). The N1 then winds through the [Soutpansberg Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soutpansberg) (containing two short tunnels) as the [Wyllie's Poort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyllie's_Poort) Pass before heading to [Musina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musina) (passing the last tollgate before Musina).[^cite_note-\:1-1] The Musina Western Bypass is now used by traffic to bypass the CBD to the west[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] (The N1 is no-longer the road passing through the town centre). The route then continues for 12 km to end at the [Beitbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beitbridge,_South_Africa) border crossing with [Zimbabwe](34399.md) on the [Limpopo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River), where it crosses the river as the [Alfred Beit Road Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Beit_Road_Bridge) before splitting into two routes, which are the [A4 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4_road_\(Zimbabwe\)) to [Harare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harare) and the [A6 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A6_road_\(Zimbabwe\)) to [Bulawayo](355237.md). The border town on the other side of the Limpopo River is also called [Beitbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beitbridge).

## Trans-African Highway Network

Main article: [Cairo-Cape Town Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo-Cape_Town_Highway)

The section of the N1 from [Cape Town](6653.md) to the split with the [N12 national route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N12_\(South_Africa\)) at [Three Sisters, Northern Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Sisters,_Northern_Cape) is declared part of the [Trans-African Highway Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-African_Highway_Network) no. 4 or [Cairo-Cape Town Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo-Cape_Town_Highway), which is the route designated by the [United Nations Economic Commission for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_Commission_for_Africa) between [Cairo](6293.md) and [Cape Town](6653.md). (The route continues as the N12 northwards from Three Sisters.)

## Old Route



A section of dual-carriageway freeway on the N1 near [Vanderbijlpark](1413961.md) in [Gauteng](309661.md) (2008)

In numerous places in South Africa, specifically near major cities, the N1 route has been rebuilt to freeway standards. The original routes usually carry the designation of R101 and are often alternative routes to the newer, sometimes tolled, highways. Two examples are the [R101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R101_\(Western_Cape\)) over [Du Toitskloof Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Toitskloof_Pass), where the new N1 highway bypasses the pass altogether by use of the [Huguenot Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot_Tunnel), and the R101 through [Polokwane Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polokwane), where the new N1 highway bypasses the city centre by use of the [Polokwane Eastern Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polokwane_Eastern_Bypass).

There are exceptions to the usual R101 alternative route designation:

- The old N1 route from Colesberg to Bloemfontein is designated [R717](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R717_road_\(South_Africa\)) up until [Reddersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddersburg), where it's designated [N6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N6_road_\(South_Africa\)) to Bloemfontein.

  [^cite_note-\:0-2]

- The old N1 route through [Bloemfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein) is designated as the [M30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M30_\(Bloemfontein\)).

- Between Bloemfontein and [Winburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winburg), the old route is designated firstly as the [R30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R30_\(South_Africa\)) between Bloemfontein and [Brandfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandfort) and then is not designated to Winburg.

  [^cite_note-\:0-2]

- Between [Kroonstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kroonstad) and [Parys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parys), the old route is designated firstly as the [R721](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R721_road_\(South_Africa\)) from Kroonstad to Vredefort and then as the [R59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R59_\(Free_State\)) through Parys to the interchange with the N1 at the Vaal Toll Plaza, approximately 10 km south of the [Vaal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_River).

  [^cite_note-\:0-2]

- Between the [Vaal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_River) and [Johannesburg](58893.md), several alternative routes exist, the official alternative route being via the Golden Highway ([R553](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R553_road_\(South_Africa\))). The old N1 route however followed the designation of the [R42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R42_\(Gauteng\)) through [Vanderbijlpark](1413961.md) to [Vereeniging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vereeniging), and then the [R82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R82_\(Gauteng\)) leading to Johannesburg's [M1 freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_\(Johannesburg\)).

  [^cite_note-\:0-2]

  &#x20;The M1 as well as Old Pretoria Road and [Louis Botha Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Botha_Avenue) (together designated as the [M11 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M11_\(Johannesburg\))) provide the alternative route through Johannesburg; near the Buccleuch Interchange (the point where the N1, M1 and [N3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N3_\(South_Africa\)) converge), the M11 becomes the R101, providing the alternative route through to [Polokwane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polokwane) via [Midrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midrand), [Centurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centurion,_Gauteng), [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria), [Bela-Bela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bela-Bela), [Modimolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modimolle) & [Mokopane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokopane).

### e-tolls in Gauteng

Main article: [E-toll (South Africa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-toll_\(South_Africa\))



An e-toll gantry on the N1 northbound near Lynnwood, Pretoria.

As a result of the [Gauteng Freeway Improvement Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng_Freeway_Improvement_Project?action=edit\&redlink=1), the section of the N1 from the [R553 Golden Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R553_\(South_Africa\)) off-ramp in-between the Misgund and Diepkloof interchanges in [Soweto](28573.md) to the Proefplaas Interchange with the [N4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N4_\(South_Africa\)) in [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) East was effectively declared an [e-toll highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-toll_\(Gauteng\)) (with [open road tolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_road_tolling)) from 3 December 2013 onwards.[^cite_note-19]

The South African government announced on 28 March 2024[^cite_note-20] that e-tolls in Gauteng would officially be shut down on 11 April 2024 at midnight.[^cite_note-\:3-21][^cite_note-\:4-22] As a result of the e-toll discontinuation, this section of the N1 (which includes the [Johannesburg Western Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_Western_Bypass_\(Johannesburg\)), part of the [Ben Schoeman Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Schoeman_Freeway) and part of the [Pretoria Eastern Bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria_Bypass)) became toll-free.[^cite_note-\:3-21][^cite_note-\:4-22]
