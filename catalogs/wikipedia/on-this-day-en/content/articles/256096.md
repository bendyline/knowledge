**Zamboanga del Sur** ([Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Habagatang Zamboanga;* [Subanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subanon_language): *S'helatan Sembwangan/Sembwangan dapit Shelatan*; [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanaon_language): *Salatan Sambuangan*, [Jawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_Alphabet): سلاتن سامبواڠن; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Katimugang Zamboanga*), officially the **Province of Zamboanga del Sur**, is a [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the [Zamboanga Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula) [region](216786.md) in [Mindanao](20546.md). Its capital is the city of [Pagadian](1694645.md).

Statistically grouped with Zamboanga del Sur is the highly urbanized [City of Zamboanga](25167165.md), which is geographically separated and a chartered city and governed independently from the province and also its largest city.

The province borders [Zamboanga del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_del_Norte) to the north, [Zamboanga Sibugay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Sibugay) to the west, [Misamis Occidental](260936.md) to the northeast, and [Lanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Norte) to the east. To the south is the [Moro Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Gulf).

## Etymology

The name of Zamboanga is the [Hispanicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanicization) spelling of the [Sinama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama-Bajau_languages) term for "mooring place" - *samboangan* (also spelled *sambuangan*; and in [Subanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subanen_language), *sembwangan*), from the root word *samboang* ("mooring pole"). "Samboangan" was the original name of [Zamboanga City](25167165.md), from where the name of the peninsula is derived from.[^cite_note-jubilado-4] "Samboangan" is well-attested in Spanish,[^cite_note-combes-5] British,[^cite_note-chall-6][^cite_note-wright-7] French,[^cite_note-prevost-8][^cite_note-roubaud-9] German,[^cite_note-meares-10] and American[^cite_note-pickering-11] historical records from as far back as the 17th century.[^cite_note-combes-5]

This is commonly contested by [folk etymologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymologies) which instead attribute the name of Zamboanga to the [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) word *jambangan* (claimed to mean "place of flowers", but actually means "pot" or "bowl"), usually with claims that all ethnic groups in Zamboanga were "[Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Malay)". However, this name has never been attested in any historical records prior to the 1960s.[^cite_note-12]

### Early history

The original inhabitants of the Zamboanga Peninsula were the [Subanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subanon_people), who settled along the riverbanks in inland areas; and the various [Sama-Bajau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama-Bajau) and [Yakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakan_people) ethnic groups who settled in coastal areas. [Tausūg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taus%C5%ABg_people) settlers from northeastern Mindanao also migrated to the region in the 13th century.[^cite_note-lsp24-13][^cite_note-Hoogervorst-14][^cite_note-kunapipi-15]

The region was additionally settled by migrants (mostly from the [Visayas](72418.md) islands) after World War II.[^cite_note-16] Together with the original settlers, these pioneers helped develop Zamboanga del Sur into the abundant and culturally diverse province, making it a melting pot of cultures..

### American colonial era

Historically, [Zamboanga](25167165.md) was the capital of the [Moro Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Province) in western [Mindanao](20546.md), which comprised five districts: [Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_\(province\)), [Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_\(province\)), [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Province), [Lanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_\(province\)), and [Zamboanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_\(province\)). In 1940, these districts became individual provinces. [Zamboanga City](25167165.md) became the capital of Zamboanga province.

### Philippine independence

Soon after [World War II](32927.md), the provincial capital was transferred to [Dipolog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipolog). [Molave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molave,_Zamboanga_del_Sur) was created as the provincial capital in 1948.

#### Foundation

On June 6, 1952, through *[Republic Act No.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Act) 711*, Zamboanga del Sur was carved out from the former [Zamboanga province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_\(province\)) that encompassed the entire [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula_\(geographical_region\)) in southwestern Mindanao.[^cite_note-17] As the 52nd province of the Philippines, it originally consisted of 11 towns with the City of [Zamboanga](25167165.md) and the Island of [Basilan](297003.md), which were later expanded into 42 municipalities with the City of [Pagadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagadian_City) as the capital.

This happened in the midst of the postwar period, a time when Mindanao was peaceful and increasingly progressive. Ethnic tensions were minimal, and there was essentially no presence of secessionists groups in Mindanao.[^cite_note-MiclatinArguillas-18] Tensions in Mindanao mostly began to rise only as the 1970s approached, as a result of social and economic tensions which affected the whole country.[^cite_note-Case-19][^cite_note-MackerrasMackerras2003-20][^cite_note-Rodis-21]

#### The Marcos era

Main articles: [Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos), [Ilaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaga), [Jabidah massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_massacre), and [Moro conflict](4602307.md)

The late 1960s in Mindanao saw a rise in land dispute conflicts arising from the influx of settlers from Luzon and Visayas,[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] and from the [Marcos administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Ferdinand_Marcos)’s encouragement of militia groups such as the [Ilaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaga).[^cite_note-Case-19][^cite_note-MackerrasMackerras2003-20] News of the 1968 [Jabidah massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_massacre) ignited a furor in the Moro community, and ethnic tensions encouraged with the formation of secessionist movements,[^cite_note-24] starting from the largely political [Muslim Independence Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Independence_Movement) and [Bangsamoro Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Liberation_Organization), and eventually the [Moro National Liberation Front](2821886.md) (MNLF), and the [Moro Islamic Liberation Front](51673.md) (MILF).[^cite_note-MackerrasMackerras2003-20] Additionally, an economic crisis in late 1969, violent crackdowns on student protests in 1970, and 1971, and eventually the declaration of Martial Law all led to the radicalization of many students.[^cite_note-Rodis-21] Many of them left schools in Manila and joined [New People's Army](1262096.md) units in their home provinces, bringing the [New People's Army rebellion](18748169.md) to Mindanao for the first time.[^cite_note-MiclatinArguillas-18]

The September 1972 declaration of Martial Law began a 14-year period historically remembered for [its human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship),[^cite_note-McCoy199909202-25][^cite_note-Abinales\&Amoroso20052-26] often involving the warrantless detention, murder, and physical, sexual, or mental torture of political opponents, student activists, journalists, religious workers, farmers, and others who fought against the Marcos dictatorship.[^cite_note-27] In Zamboanga del Sur, these were often attributed to [military-endorsed Militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Civilian_Home_Defense_Forces), which included the Ilaga and a number of armed cult groups, which were used to enhance the military's numbers as it fought various resisntance movements.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-hrw-29]

The year 1982 was a particularly bloody year for Zamboanga del Sur under the Marcos dictatorship, as two massacres happened in the province that year. On February 12, 1982, members of the [Ilaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaga) killed 12 persons in [Dumingag, Zamboanga del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumingag,_Zamboanga_del_Sur), allegedly to avenge the death of their leader, who they believed had been killed by the [NPA](1262096.md).[^cite_note-\:7-30] And on May 25, 1982, three people were killed and eight people were injured when the administration's airplanes dropped bombs on Barangay Dimalinao of [Bayog, Zamboanga del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayog,_Zamboanga_del_Sur), allegedly as reprisal for the killing of 23 soldiers by supposed rebels two days earlier. Days later, two more men from the community were picked up and killed, and a few months later, the residence of Bayog's [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) parish priest was strafed after he had written letters decrying the torture and harassment of the indigenous [Subanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subanon_people) people from his parish, whom government had tagged as communist supporters.[^cite_note-\:6-31]

### Contemporary

#### Separation of Zamboanga Sibugay

Political developments in February 2001 saw another major change in the territorial jurisdiction of Zamboanga del Sur. Its inhabitants voted to create a new province out of the [third congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Zamboanga_del_Sur), named [Zamboanga Sibugay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Sibugay).[^cite_note-CongressGovPH-RA8973-32]

## Geography

Zamboanga del Sur covers a total area of 4,499.46 square kilometres (1,737.25 sq mi)[^cite_note-nscb-33] occupying the southern section of the [Zamboanga peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula_\(geographical_region\)) in western [Mindanao](20546.md). It is located at longitude 122° 30"" and latitude 7° 15"" north. When Zamboanga City is included for statistical purposes, the province's land area is 591,416 hectares (5,914.16 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-nscb-33] The province is bordered on the north by [Zamboanga del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_del_Norte), west by [Zamboanga Sibugay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Sibugay), northeast by [Misamis Occidental](260936.md), east by [Lanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Norte), southeast by [Illana Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illana_Bay), and south by the [Moro Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Gulf).

### Topography



Dao Dao islands within the [Illana Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illana_Bay)

Stretching northward from Sibugay in the southwest and running along the northern boundary to Salug Valley in the east is the province’s mountainous countryside. The coastal plains extend regularly from south to west then spread into wide flat lands when reaching the coastal plains of the Baganian peninsula in the southeast.

The longest river in Region IX, the Sibugay River gets its water from the mountains of Zamboanga del Sur most specifically in [Bayog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayog,_Zamboanga_del_Sur) and [Lakewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakewood,_Zamboanga_del_Sur), from where it flows into [Sibuguey Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibuguey_Bay) which is now part of [Zamboanga Sibugay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Sibugay). Other notable rivers are the Kumalarang River, the Dinas River with its headwaters in the [Mount Timolan Protected Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Timolan_Protected_Landscape), and [Salug River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salug_River) in Molave.

### Climate

The province has a relatively high mean annual rainfall: 1,599 to 3,500 millimetres (63.0 to 137.8 in). Temperature is relatively warm and constant throughout the year: 22 to 35 °C (72 to 95 °F).

The population of Zamboanga del Sur in the 2024 census was 1,045,126 people,[^cite_note-40] with a density of 230 inhabitants per square kilometre or 600 inhabitants per square mile. When Zamboanga City is included for statistical purposes, the province's population is 2,063,975 people, with a density of 349/km<sup>2</sup> (904/sq mi).

### Religion

Most of the inhabitants in Zamboanga del Sur are Roman Catholics. Other Christian groups are [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists), [Methodists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodists), [Aglipayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aglipayan), [Church of Christ of Latter Day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Christ_of_Latter_Day_Saints), [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md), [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses), [Seventh-day Adventist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventist) and other [Evangelical Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Christian). There is a large Muslim minority.

### Languages

The most commonly spoken first language in the province is [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language), while [Chavacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano) is the majority language in and around Zamboanga City. [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) are also widely used and understood as the national and official language (Filipino) and co-official language (English) of the Philippines, with the former used as a lingua franca for and between various non-local ethnic groups or recent migrants and their families. Minority languages include [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanaon_language), [Subanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subanon_language), [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_language), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_language), and [Iranun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranun_language) as well as [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language), [Ibanag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibanag_language), [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people), [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language) and [Waray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray_language).

The economy is predominantly [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture). Products include coco oil, livestock feed milling, rice/corn milling, including the processing of fruits, gifts and housewares made from indigenous materials like handmade paper, roots, rattan, buri, and bamboo; wood-based manufacture of furniture and furniture components from wood, rattan, and bamboo; marine and aquaculture including support services; construction services and manufacture of marble, concrete, and wooden construction materials. There are also mining areas in the province, such as those found in the municipality of [Bayog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayog) managed by TVI, a Canadian-based mining firm which concentrates on gold mining, and the Cebu Ore Mining which is handling the Ore-Copper-Steel mines. There are also small-scale mines in the municipality of Dumingag.
