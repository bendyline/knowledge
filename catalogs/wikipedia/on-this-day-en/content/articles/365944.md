**Limpopo** ([/lɪmˈpoʊpoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is the northernmost [province](53215.md) of [South Africa](17416221.md). It is named after the [Limpopo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River), which forms the province's western and northern borders. The capital and largest city in the province is [Polokwane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polokwane), while the provincial legislature is situated in [Lebowakgomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebowakgomo).

The province is made up of three former [Bantustans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantustan) - [Lebowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebowa), [Gazankulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazankulu) and [Venda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_\(Bantustan\)) - and part of the former [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_\(province\)) province. The Limpopo province was established as one of nine provinces after the [1994 South African general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_South_African_general_election). The province's name was first **Northern Transvaal**, and was changed to **Northern Province** on 28 June 1995, with two other provinces. The name was changed again in 2002 to Limpopo Province. Limpopo is made up of three main ethnic groups: the [Pedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedi_people), the [Tsonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsonga_people) and the [Venda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_people).

Traditional leaders and chiefs still form a strong backbone of the province's political landscape. Established in terms of the Limpopo House of Traditional Leaders Act, Act 5 of 2005, the Limpopo House of Traditional Leaders' main function is to advise the government and the legislature on matters related to custom, tradition, and culture, including developmental initiatives that affect rural communities. On 18 August 2017, Kgosi Malesela Dikgale was re-elected as the Chairperson of the Limpopo House of Traditional Leaders.[^cite_note-gov.za-5]

## Name

The term "Limpopo" is derived from Rivombo (Livombo/Lebombo), a group of [Tsonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsonga_language) settlers led by Hosi Rivombo who settled in the mountainous vicinity and named the area after their leader. The Lebombo mountains are also named after them. The river has been called the Vhembe by local Venda communities of the area.[^cite_note-6]

Until 1994 it was the northern part of the former Transvaal Province. For a short period since 1994 it was called the province of Northern [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_\(province\)) and thereafter, through the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) of the public of South Africa Amendment Act No. 20 of 1995 from July 1995 initially *Northern Province*.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The province was named after the [Limpopo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River) by decision of the provincial assembly in 2002, and at the same time most of the cities on its territory were renamed.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] However, the renaming of the province required an amendment to the South African constitution, which was passed with an amending law was made in 2003.[^cite_note-11]

## History



Archaeological excavations in [Mapungubwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mapungubwe)

The world heritage site of [Mapungubwe National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapungubwe_National_Park) boasts archaeological finds from the period of [a powerful local kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mapungubwe) that ruled here between 800 CE and 1290 existed. The remains of a settlement from the period between the 15th and 17th century can be seen in Thulamela in the northern part of the Kruger National Park.

For a short period since 1994 it was called the Province of NorthReern [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_\(province\)) and thereafter, through the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) of the public of South Africa Amendment Act No. 20 of 1995 from July 1995 initially *Northern Province*.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The province was named after the [Limpopo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River) by decision of the provincial assembly in 2002, and at the same time most of the cities on its territory were renamed.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] However, the renaming of the province required an amendment to the South African constitution, which was passed with an amending law was made in 2003.[^cite_note-16]

## Geography



Sundown over one of the mountain ranges found in Limpopo.

Limpopo Province shares international borders with districts and provinces of three countries: [Botswana](3464.md)'s [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Botswana\)) and [Kgatleng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kgatleng_District) districts to the west and northwest respectively, [Zimbabwe](34399.md)'s [Matabeleland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matabeleland_South) and [Masvingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masvingo_\(province\)) provinces to the north and northeast respectively, and [Mozambique](19301.md)'s [Gaza Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Province) to the east.[^cite_note-17] On its southern edge, from east to west, it shares borders with the South African provinces of [Mpumalanga](313647.md), [Gauteng](309661.md), and [North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_\(South_African_province\)). Its border with Gauteng includes that province's [Johannesburg](58893.md)-[Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) axis, the most industrialised metropolis on the continent.



[Lowveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowveld) landscape

Limpopo contains much of the [Waterberg Biosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterberg_Biosphere), a [massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif) of approximately 15,000 km<sup>2</sup> (5,800 sq mi) which is the first region in the northern part of South Africa to be named a UNESCO [Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_Reserve).[^cite_note-Hogan-18]

The northern portion of the most well-known game reserve in South Africa, the [Kruger National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruger_National_Park), is located in the province. This includes famous gates like [Pafuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pafuri) and Punda Maria. This area is renowned for its distinctive landscapes which include a greater density of [Baobab trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adansonia_digitata) and [fever tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vachellia_xanthophloea) forests alongside the [Levubu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levubu_River). It is an important component of the greater [Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Limpopo_Transfrontier_Park), a vast conservation area that connects Zimbabwean and Mozambican national parks with Kruger.[^cite_note-19]

The far northern region is home to the [Soutpansberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soutpansberg) mountain range. A sizeable natural salt pan at its western end is the source of its name which translates to "Salt Pan Mountain" in Afrikaans. The range, which stretches roughly 107 kilometres (66 mi) from east to west, is a prominent feature of the area. In addition to being home to many plant and animal species that are unique to Earth, it is a recognised hotspot for biodiversity ([Vhembe Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vhembe_Biosphere_Reserve?action=edit\&redlink=1)) and has great cultural significance.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]



[Soutpansberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soutpansberg)

The [Magoebaskloof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magoebaskloof) is a [mountain pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_pass) that is accessible by the [R71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R71_\(South_Africa\)) road between [Tzaneen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzaneen) and [Haenertsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haenertsburg).[^cite_note-22] It literally translates to "Makgoba's ravine" and is named after [Chief Makgoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Makgoba).

It is a naturally occurring [evergreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen) [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics) [forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) that covers a verdant mountainous region. Before being conquered by [Afrikaners](18947965.md), the small kingdom of Makgoba existed in this region. The Debengeni (Dibekeni) waterfalls, Magoebaskloofdam, and numerous forest hiking trails are just a few of the natural wonders that make Magoebaskloof a popular tourist destination today.

## Economy



Man and his donkeys collecting wood in a rural area

Limpopo has a total population of 6.015 million with 1.641 million households.[^cite_note-\:0-23] The province has a relatively high Human Development Index (HDI) of 0.710, which is the third highest in South Africa.

### Agriculture

The bushveld is [beef cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef_cattle) country, where extensive ranching operations are often supplemented by controlled hunting. About 80% of South Africa's [game hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_hunting) industry is in Limpopo.

[Sunflowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower), [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut) are cultivated in the [Bela-Bela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bela-Bela) and [Modimolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modimolle) areas. Modimolle is also known for its table [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape). An embryotic [wine industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_wine) is growing in Limpopo.[^cite_note-24] Tropical fruit, such as [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana), [litchis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lychee), [pineapples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), [mangoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango) and [pawpaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya), as well as a variety of [nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_\(fruit\)), are grown in the [Tzaneen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzaneen) and [Louis Trichardt](30861369.md) areas. Tzaneen is also at the centre of extensive [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus), [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea), and [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) plantations and a major [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry) industry. Most of the farmers and households lack a water supply which makes them drill boreholes on their premises.[^cite_note-25]

### Housing

Most Limpopo residents live in rural areas; this has led to a new phenomenon of rural development, where the residents have invested in building lavish homes on their tribal land. Limpopo rural houses have been profiled by TV channels, lifestyle vloggers, social media influencers, and Africa's biggest facts brand, Africa Facts Zone. According to [^cite_note-\:0-23] 96.2% of Limpopo live in formal housing, above the national average of 84.0%. This makes Limpopo the province with the highest percentage of people living in formal housing in South Africa.

### Mining



[Ajoite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajoite) in [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz), from the Messina mine, Limpopo Province, South Africa. Scale at bottom is one inch, with a rule at one cm.

Limpopo's rich mineral deposits include the platinum group metals, iron ore, chromium, high- and middle-grade [coking coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coking_coal), diamonds, [antimony](898.md), [phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate), and copper, as well as mineral reserves like gold, emeralds, [scheelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheelite), [magnetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetite), [vermiculite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermiculite), [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon), and [mica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mica). Commodities such as [black granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_granite), [corundum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corundum), and [feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar) are also found. Mining contributes to over a fifth of the provincial economy.

Limpopo has the largest [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum) deposit in South Africa.[^cite_note-26] The [Waterberg Coalfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterberg_Coalfield), the eastern extension of [Botswana](3464.md)'s [Mmamabula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mmamabula) coalfields, is estimated to contain 40% of South Africa's coal reserves.[^cite_note-27]

### Tourism

The Limpopo Department of Economic Development, Environment and Tourism has targeted the province as a preferred [eco-tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco-tourism) destination. Its Environment and Tourism Programme encompasses tourism, protected areas, and community environment development to achieve sustainable economic growth.[^cite_note-search.gov.za-28]

While Limpopo is one of South Africa's poorest provinces, it is rich in wildlife, which gives it an advantage in attracting tourists. Both the private and public sectors are investing in tourism development.[^cite_note-29]

Near [Modjadjiskloof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modjadjiskloof), at [Sunland Baobab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunland_Baobab) farms, there is a large Baobab tree which has been fashioned into a relatively spacious pub.[^cite_note-30]

## Transportation and communications

The province has excellent road, rail, and air links. The N1 route from [Johannesburg](58893.md), which extends the length of the province, is the busiest overland route in Africa in terms of cross-border trade in raw materials and beneficiated goods. The port of [Durban](313650.md), South Africa's busiest, is served directly  by the province, as are the ports of [Richards Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richards_Bay) and [Maputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo). [Polokwane International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polokwane_International_Airport) is situated just north of [Polokwane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polokwane). Limpopo province contains approximately 56 airports and airstrips.[^cite_note-GAAL-31]

- [Rugby union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_South_Africa): Limpopo has no provincial rugby team of its own; it is represented in the domestic [Currie Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currie_Cup) by the [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria)-based [Blue Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Bulls). The Blue Bulls also operate a [Super Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Rugby) franchise, the [Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulls_\(rugby\)). Limpopo nonetheless produces its share of top players. Most notably, the second most-[capped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_\(sport\)) forward in the history of [the country's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_rugby_union_team), [Victor Matfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Matfield), is a native of Polokwane.

The population of Limpopo consists of several ethnic groups distinguished by culture, language, and race. 97.3% of the population is [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people), 2.4% is [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whites), 0.2% is [Coloured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coloured), and 0.1% is [Indian](14533.md)/[Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people). The province has the smallest percentage and second smallest total number of [White South Africans](4734206.md) in the country. However, there are several localities with a White majority, notably [Hoedspruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoedspruit) and [Modimolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modimolle). It also has the highest Black percentage out of all the provinces.

The [Pedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedi_people) comprise the largest percentage of the population, 52% of the province. The [Tsonga people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsonga_people) comprise about 24.0% of the province; the Tsonga also comprise about 11.5% of [Mpumalanga](313647.md) province since the southern part of their homeland, [Gazankulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazankulu), was cut off from Limpopo and allocated to Mpumalanga. The [Venda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_people) make up about 16.7%. [Afrikaners](18947965.md) make up the majority of Limpopo's White population, about 95,000 people; [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)-speaking Whites number just over 20,000. [Vhembe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vhembe) district has the smallest share of White people in Limpopo, about 5,000 total. In contrast, the [Waterberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterberg_District_Municipality) district has the largest share of Whites, with more than 60,000 Whites residing there. [Coloureds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coloureds) and [Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asians)/[Indians](14533.md) make up a tiny percentage of the province's total population.

### HIV/AIDS

At 18.5% (2007), Limpopo has a relatively high incidence of [HIV](14170.md) compared to other South African provinces. Cases rose from 14.5% to 21.5% between 2001 and 2005, with a slight fall between 2005 and 2007.[^cite_note-40] However, as at 2019, the Limpopo province HIV stats sat at (13.2%) which is one of the lowest in comparison with other provinces in South Africa.[^cite_note-41]
