The **World Baseball Classic** (**WBC**), also referred to as the **World Classic**[^cite_note-1] is a quadrennial international [baseball](3850.md) tournament sanctioned by the [World Baseball Softball Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Softball_Confederation) (WBSC),[^cite_note-2] the sport's global governing body, and organized by [World Baseball Classic Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Classic_Inc.), a partnership of the WBSC and [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB) in the United States and the [Major League Baseball Players Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_Players_Association) (MLBPA), also in the U.S. It is one of the two main adult baseball tournaments sanctioned by the WBSC, alongside the [WBSC Premier12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBSC_Premier12), but the only one to grant the winner the title of "world champion".[^cite_note-International_Baseball_Federation-3][^cite_note-wbsc.org-4][^cite_note-5] and award them the [World Baseball Classic Championship Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Classic_Championship_Trophy).

After a three-year gap between the first two installments of the tournament, plans were made for the WBC to be repeated every four years following the 2009 event. The 2021 event was delayed to 2023 as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic; the 2026 event proceeded as originally scheduled. Japan is the only team to win the tournament multiple times, with back-to-back wins in 2006 and 2009, and a third win at the 2023 tournament.

## History

### The early era (2006-2009)

#### First attempt

The 16-team field for the inaugural [2006 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_World_Baseball_Classic) was pre-selected, featuring the countries judged to be the "best baseball-playing nations" in the world; no qualifying competition was held.[^cite_note-6] The tournament format featured [round-robin](939742.md) group play in the first and second rounds, followed by [single-elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-elimination) semifinals and finals. The first game in WBC history saw [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_national_baseball_team) defeat [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_national_baseball_team) 2–0 before a crowd of 5,193 at the [Tokyo Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Dome) on March 3, 2006. South Korea went on to advance to the semifinals with a 6–0 record but lost to [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_national_baseball_team) (a team South Korea had beaten twice in the earlier rounds) for a berth in the final game. Meanwhile, [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_national_baseball_team) defeated the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_baseball_team) in the other semifinal.

Both countries had to go through two rounds of [group stages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournament#Multi-stage_tournaments) and the semi-finals in [knockout format](939680.md) to reach the final. Cuba lost only two games, once to [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_national_baseball_team) in the first round and once to the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_baseball_team) in the second round. However, Japan lost three times, twice to [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_national_baseball_team) in each round and the [United States](4119270.md) in the second round. This sparked a [format controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controversies_at_the_2006_World_Baseball_Classic#Format) since South Korea would have a better overall and head-to-head record than Japan by the end of the tournament. As such, Cuba was the favorite to win the final. The team with the higher winning percentage of games in the tournament was to be the home team. The match began progressing when Japan's starting pitcher, [Daisuke Matsuzaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisuke_Matsuzaka), gave up four [hits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit_\(baseball\)), five [strikeouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikeout) and one run by the end of the 4th inning using a [gyroball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroball) pitching style. Offensively, Japan scored 6 runs with the help of [Ichiro Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichiro_Suzuki)'s batting style of [contact hitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_hitter). Once the Japanese [bullpen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullpen) took the mound in the 6th inning, Cuba aggressively responded for the rest of the baseball game, using [power hitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_hitter). By the end of the eighth, the disparity would come down to one run in favor of Japan from [Frederich Cepeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederich_Cepeda)'s [home run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_run), who would record three [runs batted in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_batted_in) by the end of the game. In the ninth, Japan would counter by pushing their offensive limit over Cuba's, which would result in a final score of ten to six. The aftermath of the final most notably included notice from MLB, from Cuba's increase in [defection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_baseball_players_who_defected_from_Cuba) to Matsuzaka's impact for the [World Series champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_World_Series) [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Boston_Red_Sox_season) in the next year.

#### 2006-2009: Japanese dynasty

The next iteration of the tournament, taking place in 2009, featured the same 16 teams from 2006. However, the [controversial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_World_Baseball_Classic#Controversies) round-robin format from 2006 was replaced by a [modified double-elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-elimination) format for the first two rounds (the semifinals and final game remained single-elimination). The eight teams advancing from the first round were the same as in 2006, except for a ["Cinderella"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinderella_\(sports\)) performance by the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_baseball_team), which twice defeated the Dominican Republic to reach the second round. In the semifinals, South Korea defeated [Venezuela](4264306.md) while Japan defeated the [United States](4119270.md). [South Korea](27019.md) won the coin flip held after the second semifinal between [Japan](15573.md) and the United States, designating them as the home team for the final.[^cite_note-7]

Japan drew first blood, scoring on an [RBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runs_batted_in) [single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_\(baseball\)) by [Michihiro Ogasawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michihiro_Ogasawara) in the third inning. [Shin-Soo Choo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin-Soo_Choo) tied the score 1−all with a [home run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_run) in the fifth inning. With runners on first and third, [Hiroyuki Nakajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroyuki_Nakajima) hit an RBI single to bring [Seiichi Uchikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiichi_Uchikawa) home to give Japan the lead 2−1.[^cite_note-news-8] South Korea failed to take advantage of Japanese pitcher [Hisashi Iwakuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisashi_Iwakuma), who was visibly tired, when they failed to score in the seventh inning, when Iwakuma was relieved by [Toshiya Sugiuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiya_Sugiuchi) after two outs. Uchikawa hit a single to start the eighth. [Atsunori Inaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atsunori_Inaba) scored a [double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_\(baseball\)) to put Uchikawa in scoring position, and Uchikawa scored on [Akinori Iwamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akinori_Iwamura)'s [sacrifice fly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrifice_fly). [Hyun-wook Jong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jong_Hyun-wook) retired the remaining batters to close out the inning.[^cite_note-news-8]



Dodger Stadium hosting the [2009 World Baseball Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Baseball_Classic). The top of a ten-story elevator shaft bears the World Baseball Classic logo.

Japan brought out their [closer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closer_\(baseball\)), [Yu Darvish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu_Darvish), for the bottom of the ninth with a 3−2 lead. Darvish struck out [Keun-woo Jeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeong_Keun-woo), but [walked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk_\(baseball\)) [Hyun-soo Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyun-soo_Kim) and [Tae-kyun Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Tae-kyun_\(baseball,_born_1982\)) to put South Koreans on first and second with one out. Darvish then struck out Choo and was one out away from saving the game. But [Bum-ho Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Bum-ho) singled, driving in [Jong-wook Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Jong-wook_\(baseball\)) for the game-tying run to make it 3−all and send the game into [extra innings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_innings).[^cite_note-ESPN1-9] Japan batted first, with [Chang-yong Lim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Chang-yong) pitching for South Korea in the tenth. Uchikawa and Iwamura hit a single to put runners on first and third with two out. Ichiro was one strike away from ending the inning when he hit a line-drive single up the middle that scored Iwamura and Uchikawa. Lim then hit Nakajima with a pitch and intentionally walked [Norichika Aoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norichika_Aoki) to face [Kenji Johjima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenji_Johjima) who was hitless up to that point. Lim was able to strikeout Johjima and send the game to the bottom of the tenth. Darvish made short work of South Korea, capping with a strikeout of [Keun-woo Jeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeong_Keun-woo) to clinch Japan's successful defense of their 2006 championship.[^cite_note-ESPN1-9][^cite_note-10]

Pitcher [Daisuke Matsuzaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisuke_Matsuzaka) of Japan was awarded the tournament MVP for the second consecutive time, with a 3−0 [record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Win_\(baseball\)) and 2.45 [ERA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earned_run_average).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

After the match the team was congratulated immediately for their victory by Japanese prime minister [Taro Aso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taro_Aso), and South Korean president [Lee Myung-bak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Myung-bak) invited the South Korean team to come and encourage the team.

#### 2013: Direct qualification and "Plátano Power"

In 2013, the WBC was one of the two main senior baseball tournaments sanctioned by the WBSC, alongside the [Premier12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBSC_Premier12) but the Classic is the only one which grants to the winner the title of "World Champion".[^cite_note-International_Baseball_Federation-3][^cite_note-wbsc.org-4]

The buildup to the [2013 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Baseball_Classic) included a [qualifying round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Baseball_Classic_qualification) for the first time, with the four lowest finishers from 2009 having to re-qualify against 12 additional teams. This resulted in two new nations making their first appearances in the WBC, as [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_national_baseball_team) and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_baseball_team) respectively replaced [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_baseball_team) and [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_baseball_team). The round-robin format was revived for the tournament's first-round, while the second-round remained double-elimination. in Pool B, [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_national_baseball_team) went into the final game needing not only to win (which would cause a three-way tie for the two second-round berths) but to win by at least five runs in order to have a chance of advancing on tiebreakers. Although [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_national_baseball_team) lost its 2−0 lead in Korea's three-run eighth-inning rally, and then lost the game, they emerged as winners of the pool and of the second-round berth since Korea's margin of victory was only one run. [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_baseball_team) was the biggest surprise in the early stages of the tournament, making it to the second round with wins over Canada and Mexico.

The tournament ended in an all-Caribbean championship game, with the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_baseball_team) defeating [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_national_baseball_team). In the final, [Samuel Deduno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Deduno) started for the Dominican Republic, while [Giancarlo Alvarado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giancarlo_Alvarado) started for Puerto Rico.[^cite_note-aol-13][^cite_note-14] 35,703 fans attended the game at [AT\&T Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Park) in [San Francisco](49728.md). An additional 50,000 Dominican fans watched the game at [Estadio Quisqueya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Quisqueya) in [Santo Domingo, Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Domingo,_Dominican_Republic).[^cite_note-aol-13] In Puerto Rico, the final was the most watched sporting event for the past year with nearly 3⁄4 of all households tuning in.[^cite_note-Guardian-15][^cite_note-ESPN2-16] [Edwin Encarnación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Encarnaci%C3%B3n) hit a two-run [double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_\(baseball\)) in the first inning, giving the Dominican Republic the lead. [Erick Aybar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erick_Aybar) had the game's third [run batted in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_batted_in) for the Dominican Republic. Deduno recorded five [strikeouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikeout) in five scoreless [innings pitched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innings_pitched) and [Fernando Rodney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Rodney) completed the game with a [save](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_\(baseball\)), his seventh of the tournament.[^cite_note-aol-13] Four Dominican relief pitchers combined for 4 scoreless innings.[^cite_note-New_York_Times-17]

This was the third time in the tournament that the Dominican Republic defeated Puerto Rico.[^cite_note-aol-13] The Dominican Republic completed the tournament with an 8–0 record, becoming the first undefeated team to win the WBC.[^cite_note-ESPN2-16] [Robinson Canó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinson_Can%C3%B3) was named the [Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_Valuable_Player) of the Classic after he batted 15-for-32 (.469), the most hits in tournament history.

After the match, the team was congratulated for their victory by Dominican president [Danilo Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danilo_Medina).[^cite_note-ESPN2-16][^cite_note-18]

The [United States](4119270.md) won its first WBC championship, defeating Japan and [Puerto Rico](23041.md) in the semifinals and finals, respectively. [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_national_baseball_team) had been undefeated in the tournament before losing in the final.

The [2017 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Baseball_Classic) returned to the format used in 2006, where both the first and second rounds were round-robin, though with the addition of tiebreaker games if needed. [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_national_baseball_team) and [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_baseball_team) qualified for the first time, with Israel using a roster mostly of [Jewish American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_American) players. Prior to the start of the 2017 World Baseball Classic, [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) considered Israel, ranked 41st in the world, to be the biggest underdog in the tournament, referring to it as the "[Jamaican bobsled team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_national_bobsleigh_team) of the WBC".[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

On the other hand, defending champion [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_baseball_team) extended its WBC winning streak to 11 games, dating to the 2013 tournament, before being eliminated in the second round. Puerto Rico reached the championship undefeated in the tournament, winning all seven [games played](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_played). Puerto Rico had defeated the United States when they faced each other in [Pool F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Baseball_Classic_%E2%80%93_Pool_F).[^cite_note-21]

In the semi-final Japan reached the semifinals with wins in all six games played in the previous rounds.[^cite_note-22] [Tanner Roark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanner_Roark) started for the United States in the semifinal game,[^cite_note-23] while [Tomoyuki Sugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoyuki_Sugano) started for Japan.[^cite_note-24] Roark pitched four scoreless innings,[^cite_note-25] while Sugano allowed one run in six innings. The United States scored a run on an RBI [single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_\(baseball\)) by [Andrew McCutchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_McCutchen) in the fourth inning, and [Ryosuke Kikuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryosuke_Kikuchi) hit a home run for Japan in the sixth inning to tie the game. The U.S. scored another run in the eighth inning to take the lead, and [Luke Gregerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Gregerson) earned the [save](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_\(baseball\)). Though the Japanese team was considered the strongest defensive team in the WBC, mistakes by Kikuchi at second base and [Nobuhiro Matsuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobuhiro_Matsuda) at third base led to each of the United States's runs.[^cite_note-nyt_semi-26]

In the championship game, [Seth Lugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Lugo) started for Puerto Rico,[^cite_note-27] and [Marcus Stroman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Stroman) started for the United States. [Ian Kinsler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Kinsler) hit a two-run home run for the United States in the third inning, as Puerto Rico's performance faltered without earning a single run throughout the innings. Kinsler scored again in the fifth inning on a single by [Christian Yelich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Yelich), and Yelich scored on an infield single by McCutchen. Two more runs scored on a [bases loaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bases_loaded) single by [Brandon Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Crawford) in the seventh inning, and [Giancarlo Stanton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giancarlo_Stanton) scored the inning's third run with an RBI single. Meanwhile, Stroman did not allow a hit for the first six innings of play. The United States added another run in the eighth inning with an RBI single by McCutchen. The [United States](3434750.md) completed the [shutout](313346.md) to win the championship.[^cite_note-28] Stroman was named the tournament's MVP.[^cite_note-29]

#### 2023: Ohtani strikes out Trout, Threepeat, Japanese and American dominance

The championship game was one of the most watched games in [baseball history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_baseball).[^cite_note-30] The game was widely praised as an instant classic, with the ending duel between team captains Mike Trout and Shohei Ohtani being considered as one of the greatest moments in the history of the sport.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]



American military in Japan watching the two countries in the [2023 World Baseball Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic) final

In January 2020, MLB announced that the 2021 WBC would be expanded to 20 teams. The additional four participants would be determined through qualifying tournaments, which were originally planned to take place in March 2020.[^cite_note-33] However, on March 12, MLB announced that the 2021 tournament would be postponed due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-34]

The [collective bargaining agreement](12728750.md) (CBA) from the [2021–22 Major League Baseball lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Major_League_Baseball_lockout) planned for the next WBC to be held in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-auto5-36] [Qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic_qualification) for the tournament concluded on October 5, 2022, with [Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_national_baseball_team) claiming the final berth in a victory against Brazil.[^cite_note-37] The competition took place from March 8 to March 21, 2023.[^cite_note-MLB.com-38][^cite_note-39]

For the 2023 classic, the MLB urged its stars to participate. [Mike Trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Trout) announced his participation, and others followed, including [Trea Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trea_Turner), [Paul Goldschmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Goldschmidt), [Nolan Arenado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolan_Arenado), [J.T. Realmuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._T._Realmuto), [Mookie Betts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mookie_Betts). The [Samurai Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_national_baseball_team) team assembled their own roster, including [Shohei Ohtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shohei_Ohtani), [Roki Sasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roki_Sasaki), [Yu Darvish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu_Darvish), [Masataka Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masataka_Yoshida), [Munetaka Murakami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munetaka_Murakami), and [Yoshinobu Yamamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshinobu_Yamamoto).

The [United States](4119270.md) beat Venezuela to face [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_national_baseball_team) in the semifinals. Baseball is Cuba's most popular sport, and is played at a very high level. Therefore, the stakes of the encounter were high. Meanwhile, Japan faced [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_national_baseball_team) in the semifinals, who were on a [Cinderella run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinderella_\(sports\)), led by [Tampa Bay Rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Rays) outfielder [Randy Arozarena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Arozarena).

After defeating Cuba in the semifinals by a wide margin, the United States faced Japan in the [championship game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic_championship). The final was incredibly popular in Japan, drawing over 54 million Japanese viewers. [Shōta Imanaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shota_Imanaga) started for Japan, while [Merrill Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merrill_Kelly) started for the U.S.. With a 3–2 lead, two outs, and no runners on base in the bottom of the ninth inning, Japan's [Shohei Ohtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shohei_Ohtani), one of the finest pitchers in recent memory, stood on the mound as his [Angels](1360083.md) teammate [Mike Trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Trout), widely considered the greatest player of his generation, stepped up to the plate. After the first pitch of this matchup, [Fox Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports) commentator [Joe Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Davis_\(sportscaster\)) reflected on the spectacle, saying,

> "As [Benji Gil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benji_Gil) said last night, 'Baseball's already won.'"[^cite_note-Imperialism_at_the_World_Baseball_Classic-40][^cite_note-41]

&#x20;The two battled to a [full count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_count). Ohtani lured Trout to swing and miss with a [slider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slider_\(pitch\)) down and away, sealing the victory for Japan.[^cite_note-Imperialism_at_the_World_Baseball_Classic-40]

After the match, Japan was congratulated for their victory by Japanese prime minister [Fumio Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida). They became the second team to win the WBC without losing a single game the entire tournament, joining the Dominican Republic in 2013.[^cite_note-Axisa-42]

#### 2026: New heights and Venezuela first title

The event proved to be quite popular, however, providing many memorable moments including a first-round game between Venezuela and the Dominican Republic.&#x20;

Before the championship game of the 2023 tournament, MLB commissioner [Rob Manfred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Manfred) announced that the next WBC would be held in [2026](73255749.md).[^cite_note-43] Sixteen teams qualified by making the top four of their four respective pools during the [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic) tournament. The other four participants, Nicaragua, Chinese Taipei, Colombia, and Brazil were determined through a qualifying tournament in March 2025.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] In May 2024, it was announced that the pool play rounds will be played in [Miami, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LoanDepot_Park); [Houston, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minute_Maid_Park); [San Juan, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Bithorn_Stadium); and [Tokyo, Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Dome). The quarterfinals will be split between Miami and Houston, and the final round will be played in Miami.[^cite_note-46]

[Venezuela](4264306.md) had landslide victories over Japan and Italy in the quarterfinal and semifinal and won this year's World Baseball Classic tournament, beating the [United States](4119270.md) 3–2 in the [championship game](82034770.md), with [Eugenio Suarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Suarez) hitting a go-ahead double in the top of the 9th to clinch their first title. It was the first major international sporting competition won by Venezuela since the [1945 Amateur World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_Amateur_World_Series), and the second time in Latin America.[^cite_note-47] [Maikel García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maikel_Garc%C3%ADa) of Venezuela was named the MVP of the tournament.[^cite_note-48]

The tournament has hit new high levels in attendance and US Television viewership: the 2023 attendance record was broken before the pool play was ended, and US viewership of the final hit 10 million viewers for the first time ever.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

## Participating teams

Main article: [National team appearances in the World Baseball Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_team_appearances_in_the_World_Baseball_Classic)



Japan winning the inaugural World Baseball Classic

The first two iterations of the Classic featured the same 16 teams, chosen by invitation. A qualifying round was added leading into the 2013 tournament and takes place in the year before the main tournament. [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_national_baseball_team) and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_baseball_team) were the first new countries to earn berths in the WBC via qualification, and so far the addition of qualifying has allowed seven nations to play in the tournament who were not part of the original 16.&#x20;

The qualification setup for the 2013 and 2017 WBCs featured the top 12 finishing teams from the previous WBC being automatically entered in the following edition, while the four lowest finishers (the teams that finished in last place in their first-round pools) were relegated to the qualifying round. Qualifying consisted of four four-team [modified double-elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-elimination_tournament) tournaments, with the winners earning the last four slots in the main tournament.

With the 2023 WBC expanding to 20 teams, the qualifying format changed, as well. All 16 participants from the 2017 WBC received automatic bids. The restructured qualifying round consisted of a pair of six-team double-elimination tournaments, from which the winners and runners-up advanced to play in the 2023 WBC.

### Most Valuable Player

See also: [Baseball World Cup § Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_World_Cup#Most_Valuable_Player)

The inaugural winner of the [Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_Valuable_Player) award in 2006 was Japan's [Daisuke Matsuzaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisuke_Matsuzaka), who pitched 13 [innings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inning) and finished with a 3–0 record. Soon after this performance, Matsuzaka received a multi-million dollar contract to join the [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox) of MLB.[^cite_note-51] Again in the 2009 WBC, Matsuzaka received the world classic MVP, finishing with a record of 3–0 and an [ERA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earned_run_average) of 2.54. In 2013, [Robinson Canó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinson_Can%C3%B3) won MVP after hitting .469 with two home runs and six RBI in the tournament.[^cite_note-52] [Toronto Blue Jays](30847.md) pitcher [Marcus Stroman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Stroman) won the award in 2017 for the U.S. Stroman posted a 2.35 ERA over three starts and no-hit Puerto Rico through six innings in an 8–0 win in the Finals.[^cite_note-53] In 2023, [Los Angeles Angels](1360083.md) [two-way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-way_player#Baseball) superstar [Shohei Ohtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shohei_Ohtani) claimed MVP honors after [slashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_baseball_terms#slash_line) .435/.606/.739 as a hitter and posting a 1.86 ERA and 11 strikeouts in 92⁄3 innings as a pitcher, also pitching the final inning of Japan's win over the U.S. in the final.[^cite_note-Axisa-42] The 2026 MVP was [Kansas City Royals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Royals) third baseman [Maikel García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maikel_Garc%C3%ADa), who registered 10 hits and 7 RBI, including a key home run in Venezuela's upset quarterfinal win over Japan and an RBI in the championship game.[^cite_note-54]

### All-WBC teams

At the end of each tournament, an [all-star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-star) team is selected based on their play in the tournament. Three pitchers, eight other position players (one each at each position, including three outfielders), and a designated hitter are named to the team. Japanese pitcher [Daisuke Matsuzaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisuke_Matsuzaka), Puerto Rican catcher [Yadier Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadier_Molina) and Puerto Rican shortstop [Javier Báez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_B%C3%A1ez) are the only players to be named to the All-WBC team twice. Japanese two-way player [Shohei Ohtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shohei_Ohtani) is the only player to be named to the All-WBC team at two separate positions, having been named to the 2023 team as both a designated hitter and a pitcher.

## Coverage

In fact, the final series in 2006 and 2009 rank among the highest-rated sporting events in Japanese television history.[^cite_note-56]

The WBC championship game was televised in the United States on [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_on_Fox) in English and on [Fox Deportes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Deportes) in Spanish.[^cite_note-57] [Play-by-play](1662121.md#Main/play-by-play_commentator) announcer [Joe Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Davis_\(sportscaster\)) and [color analyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_commentator) [John Smoltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Smoltz) called the English language broadcast of the games for Fox, and were joined by [Ken Rosenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Rosenthal) and [Tom Verducci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Verducci) as [field reporters](1662121.md#Sideline_reporter).[^cite_note-58] The game averaged 10.8 million viewers on Fox and Fox Deportes combined, peaking at 12.1 million during the later stages of the game.[^cite_note-viewership1-59] The broadcasters in Venezuela were a [pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_pool) of TV channels, that included: [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN)/[Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+), [1Baseball Network](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/1%20Baseball%20Network), [Televen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televen), [IVC](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/IVC%20\(canal%20de%20televisi%C3%B3n\)), ByM Sport and [Venevisión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venevisi%C3%B3n).[^cite_note-60]

In Japan, [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) acquired the exclusive rights to the tournament for an estimated 15 billion [yen](34392.md).[^cite_note-\:0-61] As another workaround, some [karaoke parlors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaoke_box) enabled the ability for patrons to sign into the Netflix app in their private booths, so that they could watch the tournament with their friends.[^cite_note-\:2-62]

In Latin America, a first-round game between the United States and Mexico, was the third-most-watched game in the history of [ESPN Dos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_Dos), one of the three Spanish-language channels of ESPN in Latin America.

The Japan v. South Korea game averaged 62 million viewers in Japan.[^cite_note-63] The Korean rating in that game was the second-most-watched WBC game in South Korea, after the [2009 Japan-South Korea final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Baseball_Classic_championship).[^cite_note-front_office_sports_20230317-64]
