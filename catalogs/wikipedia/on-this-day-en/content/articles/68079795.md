**Joseph Clay Kent** (born April 11, 1980) is an American politician, former [United States Army](32087.md) warrant officer, and former [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) paramilitary officer who served as the director of the [National Counterterrorism Center](3231506.md) from 2025 to 2026. A member of the [Republican Party](32070.md), Kent was the nominee in the [United States House of Representatives election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Washington) for [Washington's third congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington's_3rd_congressional_district) in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Washington#District_3) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Washington#District_3).

Kent enlisted in the [75th Ranger Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75th_Ranger_Regiment) and applied for the [Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Forces) before the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md). He served eleven combat tours, primarily in [Iraq](7515928.md), and retired in 2018, becoming a paramilitary officer with the CIA. In January 2019, Kent's wife, [Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_M._Kent), was killed in [a suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Manbij_bombing) in [Manbij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij), Syria. He became involved in political advocacy after Shannon's death.

In 2022, Kent ran for Congress in [Washington's third congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington's_3rd_congressional_district). Running as a supporter of [Donald Trump](4848272.md), he eliminated incumbent Republican [Jaime Herrera Beutler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Herrera_Beutler) in the [nonpartisan primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonpartisan_primary) but lost to Democrat [Marie Gluesenkamp Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Gluesenkamp_Perez) in the general election. Kent ran again in 2024, losing to Gluesenkamp Perez in a rematch. Kent has ascribed to various political views ranging from libertarian to [far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right_politics_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-4]

In February 2025, Trump nominated Kent for director of the National Counterterrorism Center. That month, he began serving as the chief of staff to [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md), the [director of national intelligence](456082.md). Kent was confirmed by the [Senate](24909346.md) in July. In March 2026, Kent resigned as the director of the National Counterterrorism Center, citing disagreement over U.S. involvement in the [Iran war](82533582.md) and the influence of [Israel](9282173.md) and the [Israeli lobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_lobby_in_the_United_States) in [domestic politics](32021.md).

## Early life and education

Joseph Clay Kent[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJoseph_Clay_Kent_in_the_U.S.,_Index_to_Public_Records,_1994-2019-5] was born on April 11, 1980,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJoseph_C_Kent_in_the_U.S.,_Public_Records_Index,_1950-1993,_Volume_2-6] in a cabin in [Sweet Home, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Home,_Oregon).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanard2022-7] Kent was the first child of [Roman Catholic](606848.md) parents who later graduated from law school. He was raised in [Portland, Oregon](23503.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanard2022-7] Kent expressed an early interest in the military, constructing weapons out of [Lego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego) bricks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERyan2019-8] He was inspired to join the [United States Army](32087.md) after watching television coverage of the [Battle of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_\(1993\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeisman2022b-9] Kent graduated from [Norwich University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich_University) with a degree in strategic studies and defense analysis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECornfield2025-10]

## Career

### Military and intelligence service (1998–2019)



[Shannon Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_M._Kent), Kent's first wife, was killed in the [2019 Manbij bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Manbij_bombing).

At 17, Kent enlisted in the Army[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeisman2022b-9] on a Ranger contract,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanard2022-7] allowing him to enter the [75th Ranger Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75th_Ranger_Regiment).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrooks2025-11] He applied for the [Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Forces) before the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeisman2022b-9] and took a qualification course days before the attacks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanard2022-7] By September 2003, following his training,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanard2022-7] Kent was deployed to [Iraq](7515928.md), where he fought in the [First Battle of Fallujah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Fallujah),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeisman2022b-9] and searched for [Iraqi officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._list_of_most-wanted_Iraqis).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanard2022-7] He served eleven combat tours including deployments in [Yemen](350939.md) and [north Africa](21714.md) before retiring. Kent held the final rank of [chief warrant officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_officer_\(United_States\)). He later became a paramilitary officer with the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrynelson2021-12]

In December 2014, Kent married [Shannon Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_M._Kent), a cryptologist whom he had met at [Fort Belvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Belvoir), where he had been assigned to an [Army Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Operations_Command).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOppel2019-13] They had two children, born in 2015 and 2017.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERyan2019-8] In January 2019, Shannon was killed in [a suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Manbij_bombing) in [Manbij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij). After the bombing, Kent left government work,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrynelson2023-14] and began to write columns for [CNN](62028.md), *[Breitbart News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breitbart_News)*, and [Fox News](11121.md) speaking out against the [war on terror](13425800.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanard2022-7] He consulted with the [White House](33057.md) and volunteered for [Veterans for Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_for_Trump) and Concerned Veterans for America.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeisman2022b-9]

### U.S. congressional campaigns in Washington (2021–2022; 2023–2024)

Further information: [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Washington) and [2024 United States House of Representatives elections in Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Washington)



Kent in 2020

On February 18, 2021,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHair2021a-15] Kent announced his intention to run in the [United States House of Representatives election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Washington) for [Washington's third congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington's_3rd_congressional_district) as a [Republican](32070.md), citing [Jaime Herrera Beutler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Herrera_Beutler)'s decision to vote to impeach [Donald Trump](4848272.md) in [his second impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_impeachment_of_Donald_Trump) following the [January 6 Capitol attack](66307490.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2021a-16] Kent aligned himself with Trump in his campaign announcement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHair2021b-17] By July, he had raised US\$366,000, the most of any candidate in the election at that point,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2021b-18] and garnered financial support from [Steve Wynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Wynn) and [Peter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Thiel).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrynelson2021-12] In September, Trump endorsed Kent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2021c-19] His prominence was bolstered by [Tucker Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson), who had frequently had Kent as guest on the [Fox News](11121.md) program *[Tucker Carlson Tonight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson_Tonight)* (2016–2023).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHomans2023-20]

In March 2022, he entered into a dispute with [white supremacist](33178.md) commentator [Nick Fuentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Fuentes), who claimed during a livestream that Kent privately told him, "I love what you're doing." News agencies could not verify the claim; Kent denied that the statement had ever been made. After Kent disavowed Fuentes and stated he had not sought his endorsement over his views on race and religion, Fuentes chastised Kent for not being sufficiently conservative.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrynelson2022a-21] Kent was later interviewed by American Virtue, an organization associated with Fuentes, and stated American culture was "anti-white" and "anti-straight-white-male".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilson2025-22] The [Associated Press](18935732.md) reported in July the Kent campaign had paid a member of the [Proud Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proud_Boys), a far-right militia, as a consultant, that Kent was a political ally of [Joey Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Gibson_\(political_activist\)), a right-wing political activist, and that Kent had photographed himself with Greyson Arnold, a self-described [Christian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_nationalism_in_the_United_States). The campaign told the Associated Press Kent was unaware who Arnold was.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESlodysko2022-23] In September, [CNN](62028.md) reported Kent had given an interview to Arnold.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKaczynskiSteck2022-24] In his campaign, he made repeated references to [Sam Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Francis_\(writer\)), a [paleoconservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoconservatism) writer who identified as a [white nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism) late in life.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDent2022-25]

Kent defeated Herrera Beutler in the state's open primary in August, though he received fewer votes than [Marie Gluesenkamp Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Gluesenkamp_Perez), a [Democrat](5043544.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2022b-26] Gluesenkamp Perez shifted her campaign towards issues with broad Democratic appeal, including [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_the_United_States), as well as schools and jobs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2022c-27] Kent's views led to Gluesenkamp Perez garnering some Republican support.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2022d-28] She defeated Kent[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKarni2022-29] in one of the largest upsets that year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMutnickFerrisSchneider2022-30] He initially refused to concede,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrynelson2022b-31] but eventually did so in December.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"GOP's_Joe_Kent_concedes_Washington_state_Congressional_race"._Associated_Press-32] On January 11, 2023, Kent announced a second campaign for Washington's third congressional district.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEllenbecker2023-33] Kent married Heather Kaiser[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKarniEdmondson2024-34] that year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECornfield2025-10] By October 2024, polling between Gluesenkamp Perez and Kent indicated that the election was likely to be close. That month, they debated at [KATU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KATU)'s studio in [Portland, Oregon](23503.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECornfield2024a-35] Gluesenkamp Perez won the [2024 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Washington) in November.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFertig2024-36]

### Acting chief of staff to the director of national intelligence (February–July 2025)

By February 2025, Kent had been serving as the acting chief of staff to [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md), the [director of national intelligence](456082.md);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarnesHabermanSavage2025-37] his role was disclosed by *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* in March.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENakashimaSilverman2025-38] Responding to a request from the [Trump administration](78298108.md), Kent requested the [National Intelligence Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Intelligence_Council_\(United_States\)) conduct an intelligence assessment on connections between the [government of Venezuela](78643.md) and the organized crime syndicate [Tren de Aragua](70859925.md). After the report failed to yield associations between the government and the gang, he pressured Michael Collins, the acting chairman, to reassess its analysis after *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported on the internal report. The assessment conflicted with Trump's invocation of the [Alien Enemies Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_Enemies_Act), which requires a connection to a foreign state.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarnesHabermanSavage2025-37] Kent was a member of the [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_\(software\)) group chat involved in one of the [United States government group chat leaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_government_group_chat_leaks).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinsberg2025-39]

## Director of the National Counterterrorism Center (2025–2026)



Kent is sworn in as director of the [National Counterterrorism Center](3231506.md) in July 2025.

On February 3, 2025, Donald Trump named Kent as his nominee for director of the [National Counterterrorism Center](3231506.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE"Trump_nominates_Joe_Kent_as_director_of_National_Counter_Terrorism_Center"._Oregon_Public_Broadcasting-40] He appeared before the [Senate Select Committee on Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Select_Committee_on_Intelligence) on April 9, in which he defended his involvement in the Signal group chat and asserted the government was involved in the [January 6 Capitol attack](66307490.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2025-41] On July 30, Kent was confirmed by the [Senate](24909346.md) in a 52–44 vote along party lines.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchmitt2025-42] In October, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported Kent had obtained access to the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md)'s files on the [assassination of Charlie Kirk](81040139.md), alarming the bureau's director, [Kash Patel](62202727.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarnesHabermanBarrett2025-43] According to *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*, Kent had been sidelined from the team responsible for producing and delivering the [President's Daily Brief](584611.md) in the final months of his tenure.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVolzDawseyMcGraw2026-44]

### Resignation



Kent's resignation letter

On March 16, 2026, Kent told Trump, vice president [JD Vance](53396477.md), and White House chief of staff [Susie Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susie_Wiles) that he intended to resign as director of the National Counterterrorism Center over Trump's decision to initiate the [Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVolzDawseyMcGraw2026-44] The following day, he published a resignation letter, writing on [X](9988187.md) that the U.S. entered the war amid "pressure from [Israel](9282173.md) and its powerful [American lobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_lobby_in_the_United_States)". Kent denied that Iran was an "imminent threat" to the United States.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKimKlepper2026-45][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosenfeld2026-46] According to *[Semafor](72046207.md)*, Kent had been facing an investigation by the Federal Bureau of Investigation for allegedly leaking classified information. The inquiry began several months prior to his resignation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETalcott2026-47]

In an interview with [Tucker Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson) the following day, Kent said that he and other senior officials were "not allowed" to share concerns about the necessity of airstrikes with Trump. He alleged that the decision-making process had been limited to a select few advisors and continued to emphasize the involvement of Israeli officials.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlepper2026-48] Kent appeared on the podcasts of several political commentators associated with the [MAGA movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAGA_movement), including [Megyn Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megyn_Kelly), [Saagar Enjeti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saagar_Enjeti), and [Shawn Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Ryan_\(United_States_Navy\)), in an effort to encourage opposition to the war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELamothe2026-49]

Kent voted for [George W. Bush](3414021.md) in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush_2000_presidential_campaign) and [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush_2004_presidential_campaign) before shifting his political beliefs towards [libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism_in_the_United_States), supporting [Ron Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrynelson2021-12] According to voting records, he was registered with the [Libertarian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Party_\(United_States\)) in the [2012 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election) until 2019, when he became a [Democrat](5043544.md); Kent voted for [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) in the [2020 Democratic Party presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries) in order to support Republicans in [that year's presidential election](48410011.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2022a-50] A spokesman for Kent described his political philosophy as "inclusive populism", rejecting discrimination.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESlodysko2022-23]

Kent has embraced [false claims of fraud in the 2020 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_claims_of_fraud_in_the_2020_presidential_election).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeismanBroadwater2022-51] A month following the [January 6 Capitol attack](66307490.md), he stated Donald Trump had "no control" over the mob and attributed the severity of the attack to security planning.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2021a-16] Kent attended the [Justice for J6 rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_for_J6_rally) and compared the treatment of rioters to that of Iraqi citizens in the [Iraq War](5043324.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeismanRosenberg2021-52] He later called for releasing security footage of the attack,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeisman2022a-53] and claimed the mob was brought to violence by provocateurs associated with the [deep state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_state_conspiracy_theory_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKarni2024b-54] After the [FBI search of Mar-a-Lago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI_search_of_Mar-a-Lago), Kent told [Steve Bannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bannon) on *[War Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Room_\(political_podcast\))* the raid constituted "war".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeuer2022-55] He called for defunding the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) in response.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGutman2022-56]

In January 2022 Kent led a rally against a false claim that the Washington State Board of Health was set to forcefully quarantine citizens who did not get the [COVID-19 vaccine](63319438.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunnerTakahama2022-57] In March, he appeared at an event organized by the [Washington State Three Percenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Three_Percenters), a far-right militia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllam2022-58] Kent once advocated for a federal ban on [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_the_United_States), but later narrowed his position towards [states' rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States'_rights).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKarni2024b-54] By the following month, he had called for an investigation into [Anthony Fauci](1227250.md) over the [origin of SARS-CoV-2](66389098.md) and for an immigration moratorium.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2022a-50] In September, Kent stated Fauci should be charged with murder and described COVID-19 vaccines as "experimental gene therapy";[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGutman2022-56] he is unvaccinated against COVID-19.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanard2022-7] At a rally with [Matt Gaetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Gaetz) in September 2021, Kent advocated for [school vouchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_voucher) and per-child tax deductions for parents.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYaw2021-59] Kent opposed several proposals to replace the [Interstate Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Bridge), including tolling and light rail, in his 2024 campaign.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECornfield2024b-60]

### Military affairs

Kent is a [non-interventionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-interventionism), citing his military experience and the death of his wife.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELa_Corte2022-61] He began to question the management of the U.S. military during the [Iraq War](5043324.md), when officials sought to eliminate members of [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein)'s government. According to *[Mother Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Jones_\(magazine\))*, Kent read [David Hackworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hackworth)'s memoir *About Face* (1990), a book critical of the "clerks at the top" directing the U.S.'s involvement in the [Vietnam War](32611.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanard2022-7] He defended Trump's pardons of two [Army](32087.md) officers convicted of [Uniform Code of Military Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Code_of_Military_Justice) offenses, [Mathew L. Golsteyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathew_L._Golsteyn) and [Clint Lorance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Lorance), and his intervention in the case of [Eddie Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Gallagher_\(Navy_SEAL\)), a [Navy SEAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy_SEALs) involved in a high-profile war crimes case; in an interview with *[The New York Times](30680.md)* in November 2019, Kent compared Gallagher's case with that of [Chelsea Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Manning).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPhilippsOppelArango2019-62]

### Foreign policy

At a conference held at [Washington Marriott Marquis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Marriott_Marquis) in April 2022, Kent argued the "political establishment" seeks to initiate a conflict against Russia, including by providing military assistance to [Ukraine](31750.md)—a level of support he outright opposed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeilbrunn2022-63][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2022a-50] He stated Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md)'s demands for [Donetsk](494889.md) and [Luhansk Oblasts](494904.md) were "very reasonable".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeilbrunn2022-63] His comments on *[Tucker Carlson Tonight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson_Tonight)* denouncing support for Ukraine as deterring a peace deal were repeated by [TASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TASS), a Russian state-owned news agency.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompson2022-64] In September 2023, Kent described the Biden administration's strategy as immoral, arguing that the U.S. is fueling a prolonged war that is "unsustainable" for Ukraine. Kent has specifically stated that the policy uses the Ukrainian civilian population as "cannon fodder", describing [drafted Ukrainian soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_conscription_crisis)—whom he characterized as formerly everyday workers and students—as being sent to die in a "muddy ditch" in a war he believed they cannot win. He has argued that by providing continuous aid, the U.S. prevents a necessary, albeit likely painful, [peace deal](70255183.md) from being brokered.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKent2023-65]

In 2021, following the [U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan](66883576.md), Kent emerged as a vocal critic of what he described as Washington’s failed "nation-building" strategies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKimKlepper2026-45] In March 2026, Kent characterized [U.S.-Israeli strikes against Iran](82533582.md) as a "manufactured" war, arguing they did not serve American interests and risked repeating the "disastrous" mistakes of the [Iraq War](5043324.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStepansky2026-66]
