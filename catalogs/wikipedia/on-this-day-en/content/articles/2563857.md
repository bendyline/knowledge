The **Federal Ministry of Defence** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Bundesministerium der Verteidigung*, pronounced [\[ˈbʊndəsminɪsˌteːʁiʊm deːɐ̯ fɐˈtaɪdɪɡʊŋ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/9d/De-Bundesministerium_der_Verteidigung.ogg/De-Bundesministerium_der_Verteidigung.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/9d/De-Bundesministerium_der_Verteidigung.ogg/De-Bundesministerium_der_Verteidigung.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; abbreviated **BMVg**) is a top-level [federal agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_agency_\(Germany\)), headed by the Federal Minister of Defence as a member of the [Cabinet of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Germany). The ministry is headquartered at the [Hardthöhe barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardth%C3%B6he_\(barracks\)) itself located at the [Hardthöhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardth%C3%B6he) district in [Bonn](3295.md) and has a second office in the *[Bendlerblock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendlerblock)* building in [Berlin](3354.md), which is occasionally used as a [metonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonymy) to denote the entire Ministry.[^cite_note-4]

According to Article 65a of the [German Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_for_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany) (*Grundgesetz)*, the Federal Minister of Defence is [Commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the *[Bundeswehr](288188.md)*, the German armed forces, with around 260,953 active soldiers and civilians.[^cite_note-bundeswehr-5] Article 115b decrees that in the [state of defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Defence_\(Germany\)), declared by the [Bundestag](3768.md) with consent of the [Bundesrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesrat_of_Germany), the command in chief passes to the [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_Germany_\(Federal_Republic_of_Germany\)). The ministry currently has approximately 2,000 employees.[^cite_note-6]

## Organization

On April 1, 2012, the Federal Ministry of Defence (DEU MOD) changes its organization to the following general structure:

### Senior Management Level

- Federal Minister of Defence (acts as High Commander of the German armed forces in peacetime)&#x20;

  - 2 Parliamentary Secretaries of State

  - 2 Secretaries of State

- subordinated to the Senior Management&#x20;

  - Support Office

  - Press & Information Office

  - Politics Directorate

### Directorates

- Secretary of State #1&#x20;

  - Equipment Directorate (lost the Cyber & IT branch in 2016)

  - Cyber & IT Directorate (founded 2016)

- Secretary of State #2&#x20;

  - Financial & Controlling Directorate

  - Personnel Directorate

  - Infrastructure, Antipollution & Administrative Services Directorate

  - Legal Directorate

- [Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff,_Bundeswehr) of the Bundeswehr&#x20;

  - Plans & Policies Directorate

  - Strategy & Operations Directorate

  - Armed Forces Command & Control Directorate

### Departments of the Federal armed forces

The Bundeswehr is divided into a military part (armed forces or Streitkräfte) and a civil part with the armed forces administration (Wehrverwaltung) and consists of 11 Departments/Services:

- Armed Forces&#x20;

  - [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army) (Heer)

  - [German Navy](12960.md) (Marine)

  - [German Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Air_Force) (Luftwaffe)

  - [Bundeswehr Joint Medical Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundeswehr_Joint_Medical_Service) (Zentraler Sanitätsdienst)

  - [Joint Support Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streitkr%C3%A4ftebasis) (Streitkräftebasis) including the [Center for Military History and Social Sciences of the Bundeswehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Military_History_and_Social_Sciences_of_the_Bundeswehr)

  - [Cyber- and Information Domain Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber_and_Information_Domain_Service_\(Germany\)) (Cyber- und Informationsraum)(founded in 2017 from parts of the Joint Support Service)

- Armed Forces Administration&#x20;

  - Personnel Management (Personal)

  - Information Technology & In-Service Management (Ausrüstung, Informationstechnologie und Nutzung)

  - Infrastructure, Antipollution & Administrative Services (Infrastruktur, Umweltschutz und Dienstleistungen)

  - Judicature (Recht)

  - Military Chaplaincy (Militärseelsorge)

### Directly subordinated Offices & Agencies

### 19th century

From the [Unification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany) in 1871 until the end of [World War I](4764461.md), the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) did not have a national [Ministry of War](9473138.md). Rather, the larger German states (such as the kingdoms of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia), [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria), [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Saxony) and [Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg)), insisting on their autonomy, each had their own war ministry. According to the military agreements the Prussian minister-president [Otto von Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck) had forged with the South German states on the eve of the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War), the major states were also responsible for the defence of the smaller states. However, the [Imperial Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiserliche_Marine) from 1889 was overseen by a federal department, the [Imperial Naval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Imperial_Naval_Office).

### Weimar and Nazi Germany

See also: [Ministry of the Reichswehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Reichswehr)



Bendlerblock, Berlin-Tiergarten, secondary seat since 1993

After the war and the [German Revolution of 1918–19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Revolution_of_1918%E2%80%9319), the [Weimar Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Constitution) provided for a unified, national ministry of defence, which was created largely from the [Prussian Ministry of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Minister_of_War) and the Imperial Naval Office. The [Ministry of the Reichswehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Reichswehr) was established in October 1919, and had its seat in the [Bendlerblock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendlerblock) building.

In the context of the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) and the "Law for the Creation of a provisional [Reichswehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichswehr)" of March 1919, the Reichspräsident became the Commander-in-Chief of the armed forces, whilst the Reich Minister of Defence exercised military authority. Only in the [Free State of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Prussia) did military authority remain with the State Minister of War. After the Weimar Constitution came into force, the remaining war ministries in the states of Bavaria, Saxony, Württemberg and Prussia were abolished and military authority was concentrated in the [Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Reich) Minister of Defence. Command was exercised respectively by the Chief of the *Heeresleitung* (Army Command) and the Chief of the *Marineleitung* (Navy Command, see *[Reichsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsmarine)*). In 1929 a third office was established: the Ministerial Office, whose Chief functioned as the political representative of the Minister. The role of the General Staff was filled by the *Truppenamt*.

The [Social Democratic](60585.md) politician [Gustav Noske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Noske) became the first Minister of Defence of Germany. After the Nazi *[Machtergreifung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung)*, when the *Reichswehr* was recreated as the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) in 1935, the ministry was renamed *Reichskriegsministerium* (Reich Ministry of War); also, the *Heeresleitung* became the [Oberkommando des Heeres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkommando_des_Heeres) (OKH), the *Marineleitung* became the [Oberkommando der Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkommando_der_Marine) (OKM) and the [Oberkommando der Luftwaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkommando_der_Luftwaffe) (OKL) was newly created. The *Ministeramt* (Ministerial Office) was renamed the *Wehrmachtsamt*.

In 1938, following the [Blomberg-Fritsch Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blomberg-Fritsch_Affair), Hitler himself exercised the functions of the Reich War Minister. The *Wehrmachtsamt* was turned into the [Oberkommando der Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberkommando_der_Wehrmacht) (OKW; High Command of the Armed Forces), which formally existed until the end of [World War II](32927.md). The High Command was not a government ministry, but a military command, however.

### Post-WW2

After World War II, [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) started with preparations for rearmament (*[Wiederbewaffnung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiederbewaffnung)*) in 1950, as ordered by Chancellor [Konrad Adenauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Adenauer).[^cite_note-7] After the outbreak of the [Korean War](16772.md), the [United States](3434750.md) called for a West German contribution to the defence of Western Europe (against the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)). Initially [Gerhard Graf von Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Graf_von_Schwerin), a former Wehrmacht General, advised the Chancellor on these issues and led the preparations, but after Count Schwerin had talked to the press about his work, he was replaced by [Theodor Blank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Blank), who was appointed as "Special Representative" of the Chancellor. As the rearmament plans met with harsh opposition by a wide circle within the West German population and contradicted the [occupation statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_statute), the government office responsible for the rearmament acted secretly, unofficially known as *Amt Blank*. By 1955, the number of employees had surpassed 1,300. On 7 June 1955 the office became the Ministry of Defence, or *Bundesministerium für Verteidigung* in German. The [Bundeswehr](288188.md) was established and Germany joined the [NATO](21133.md) the same year. In 1956, Germany reintroduced [conscription](1285284.md), and the German military force quickly became the largest conventional military force in Western Europe. To confirm the ministry's importance, it was renamed *Bundesministerium **der** Verteidigung* on 30 December 1961, similar to the German names of the "classic" ministries of [Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Finance_\(Germany\)), [the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Germany\)) and [Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Justice_\(Germany\)) — though the federal minister is still denoted as *Bundesminister **für** Verteidigung* in Article 65a of the German Constitution.

Until 1960, the ministry had its seat in the Ermekeil barracks in Bonn. From 1960 onwards, it was moved to a new building complex at Hardthöhe.

### Post-reunification

After [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification), the Bendlerblock, former seat of its Weimar Republic predecessor, became the secondary seat of the ministry in 1993. The German military has become increasingly engaged in international operations since the early 1990s, and saw combat in the 1999 [Kosovo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NATO_bombing_of_Yugoslavia).

### 21st century



Map of Resolute Support Mission that documents the partition of responsibilities between allies: [TAAC – Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_Advise_Assist_Command_%E2%80%93_Capital), [TAAC – North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_Advise_Assist_Command_%E2%80%93_North), [TAAC – South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_Advise_Assist_Command_%E2%80%93_South), [TAAC – East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_Advise_Assist_Command_%E2%80%93_East), [TAAC – West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_Advise_Assist_Command_%E2%80%93_West)

Until the [Fall of Kabul](68481047.md) in August 2021, Germany deployed for nearly 20 years its armed forces in [Afghanistan](737.md) as part of the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) (2001-2015) and later the [Resolute Support Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_Support_Mission) (2015-2021). [German forces withdrew on 29 June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_Support_Mission#Contributing_nations).[^cite_note-dwaf-8]
