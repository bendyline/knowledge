In an [explosion](18993857.md) (top), force radiates away from a source. With implosion (bottom), the object collapses upon itself (generally being crushed by an outside force).



Colorized image of NIF “Bigfoot” deuterium-tritium implosion, February 7, 2016. [National Ignition Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Ignition_Facility)

**Implosion** is the collapse of an object into itself from a pressure differential or gravitational force. The opposite of [explosion](18993857.md) (which expands the [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume)), implosion reduces the volume occupied and concentrates [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter) and [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy). Implosion involves a difference between internal (lower) and external (higher) pressure, or inward and outward forces, that is so large that the structure collapses inward into itself, or into the space it occupied if it is not a completely solid object. Examples of implosion include a submarine being crushed by [hydrostatic pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_pressure#Hydrostatic_pressure)[^cite_note-1] and the collapse of a [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star) under its own [gravitational pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_collapse).&#x20;

In some but not all cases, an implosion propels material outward, for example due to the force of inward falling material rebounding, or peripheral material being ejected as the inner parts collapse. If the object was previously solid, then implosion usually requires it to take on a more [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) form—in effect to be more concentrated, compressed, or converted into a denser material.

## Examples

### Nuclear weapons

In an implosion-type [nuclear weapon design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_design), a sphere of [plutonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium), [uranium](31743.md), or other [fissile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fissile) material is imploded by a spherical arrangement of explosive charges. This decreases the material's volume and thus increases its density by a factor of two to three, causing it to reach [critical mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_mass) and create a [nuclear explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_explosion).

In some forms of [thermonuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear_weapons), the energy from this explosion is then used to implode a capsule of fusion fuel before igniting it, causing a [fusion reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion) (see [Teller–Ulam design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teller%E2%80%93Ulam_design)). In general, the use of radiation to implode something, as in a hydrogen bomb or in laser driven [inertial confinement fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_confinement_fusion), is known as [radiation implosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_implosion).

### Fluid dynamics

[Cavitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavitation) (bubble formation/collapse in a fluid) involves an implosion process. When a cavitation bubble forms in a liquid (for example, by a high-speed water [propeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller)), this bubble is typically rapidly collapsed—imploded—by the surrounding liquid.

### Astrophysics

Implosion is a key part of the [gravitational collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_collapse) of large [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star), which can lead to the creation of [supernovas](27680.md), [neutron stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_star) and [black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole).&#x20;

In the most common case, the innermost part of a large star (called the [core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_core)) stops burning and without this source of [heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat), the forces holding [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) and [protons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton) apart are no longer strong enough to do so. The core collapses in on itself exceedingly quickly, and becomes a [neutron star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_star) or [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole); the outer layers of the original star fall inwards and may rebound off the newly created neutron star (if one was created), creating a [supernova](27680.md).

### Cathode-ray tube and fluorescent lighting implosion

A high vacuum exists within all [cathode-ray tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode-ray_tube). If the outer glass envelope is damaged, a dangerous implosion may occur. The implosion may scatter glass pieces at dangerous speeds. While the last generations of CRTs used in televisions and computer displays had epoxy-bonded face-plates or other measures to prevent shattering of the envelope, CRTs removed from equipment must be handled carefully to avoid injury.[^cite_note-Bali-2]

### Controlled structure demolition

Main article: [Building implosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_implosion)

The [demolition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition) of large buildings using precisely placed and timed explosions so that the structure collapses on itself is often erroneously described as *implosion*.
