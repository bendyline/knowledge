The **United States Northern Command** (**USNORTHCOM**)[^cite_note-7] is one of the 11 [unified combatant commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command) of the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md). The command is tasked with providing military support for [non-military authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_authority) in the U.S., and protecting the territory and [national interests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_interests) of the United States within the [continental United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_America), [Puerto Rico](23041.md), [Canada](5042916.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), [the Bahamas](3451.md), [Greenland (Denmark)](12118.md) and the air, land and sea approaches to these areas.[^cite_note-8] It is the U.S. military command which, if applicable, would be the primary defender against an invasion of the U.S.

USNORTHCOM was created on 25 April 2002 when President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) approved a new [Unified Command Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Command_Plan), following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md). USNORTHCOM went operational on 1 October 2002.

## Creation

USNORTHCOM was established on 25 April 2002 when President George W. Bush approved a new [Unified Command Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Command_Plan),[^cite_note-ABA-p44-9][^cite_note-10] and attained [initial operating capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_operating_capability) on 1 October 2002.[^cite_note-11]

## Mission

According to the UCP, Northern Command's mission is to:[^cite_note-12]

- Conduct operations to deter, prevent, and defeat threats and aggression aimed at the United States, its territories, and [interests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_interest) within the assigned area of responsibility and,

- As directed by the [President](24113.md) or [Secretary of Defense](43998.md) provide military assistance to [non-military authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_authority) including consequence management operations

## Area of responsibility



NORTHCOM Area of Responsibility (in green)

USNORTHCOM's Area of Responsibility (AOR) includes air, land and sea approaches and encompasses the [continental United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_America), [Canada](5042916.md), [Mexico](3966054.md) and the surrounding water out to approximately 500 nautical miles (930 km). It also includes the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), the [Straits of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Florida), portions of the Caribbean region to include [The Bahamas](3451.md), [Puerto Rico](23041.md), the [U.S. Virgin Islands](32135.md), the [British Virgin Islands (United Kingdom)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Virgin_Islands), [Bermuda (United Kingdom)](3460.md), and the [Turks and Caicos Islands (United Kingdom)](30217.md).[^cite_note-13] The commander of USNORTHCOM is responsible for theater security cooperation with Canada, Mexico, and The Bahamas.[^cite_note-14] In May 2011, NORTHCOM was mobilized in the wake of the [BP oil spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepwater_Horizon_oil_spill) in the Gulf of Mexico[^cite_note-15] to provide air, ground, and logistical support.[^cite_note-16] In October 2014, NORTHCOM took administrative control of [Alaskan Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaskan_Command).[^cite_note-17] In June 2025, the area of responsibility of [Greenland (Denmark)](12118.md) was shifted from [USEUCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_European_Command) to USNORTHCOM.[^cite_note-18]

## Organizational structure

### Headquarters



NORAD-USNORTHCOM headquarters at the Eberhart-Findley Building on Peterson Space Force Base

Commander, U.S. Northern Command is concurrently Commander of the U.S.-Canadian [North American Aerospace Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aerospace_Defense_Command) (NORAD). The two are co-located at [Peterson Space Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterson_Space_Force_Base) in [Colorado Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Springs,_Colorado), [Colorado](5399.md).[^cite_note-19] General [Ralph Eberhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Eberhart) was the first CDRUSNORTHCOM.[^cite_note-20]

USNORTHCOM headquarters has approximately 1,200 uniformed and civilian staff.[^cite_note-21] In its first period of organising in 2002–03, one priority was to hire civilian staff which could help respond to a [Weapons of Mass Destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_of_Mass_Destruction) attack and to coordinate disaster recovery.[^cite_note-22]

## Commanders

Main article: [Leadership of the United States Northern Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_of_the_United_States_Northern_Command)



Attendees pay respects during the playing of the American national anthem at the NORAD-USNORTHCOM change of command ceremony on 23 May 2018.

The commander of United States Northern Command is a [four-star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-star_rank) [general](2965165.md) or [admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(United_States\)) in the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md) who serves as the head of all U.S. military forces within the command's geographical [area of responsibility](9452512.md). The commander of U.S. Northern Command concurrently serves as the [commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_North_American_Aerospace_Defense_Command) of [North American Aerospace Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aerospace_Defense_Command) (NORAD) and is the head of all [United States](3434750.md) and [Canadian](5042916.md) [joint aerospace military operational forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_operations), stationed within the Northern American territories. The commander of U.S. Northern Command is nominated for appointment by the [President of the United States](24113.md) and must be confirmed by the [United States Senate](24909346.md). The commander of U.S. Northern Command typically serves for two years.

**Note:** The National Defense Authorization Act of 2008 stipulates that at least one deputy commander of USNORTHCOM be a National Guard general officer unless the commander is already such an officer.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

## Planning and strategy

Northern Command has created several [classified](252857.md) "concept plans" (e.g. "Defense Support of Civil Authorities") that are intended to address the 15 National Planning Scenarios that NORTHCOM must be prepared to respond to.[^cite_note-34]

However, in 2012, the GAO found that the national strategy to defend the United States is several years out of date.[^cite_note-35]

On 20 January 2025, President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) issued an executive order instructing the Secretary of Defense to give Northern Command the mission to "seal the borders and maintain the sovereignty, territorial integrity, and security of the United States," requiring the secretary to revise the Unified Command Plan within 10 days in accordance with the order, and the commander of Northern Command to present a plan within 30 days on how NORTHCOM will achieve this mission.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

## Domestic operations and training

NORTHCOM operates extensive [domestic intelligence operations](81014.md) which both share and receive information from local, state and federal law enforcement agencies. Employees of the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md), [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md), [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency), [Defense Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Intelligence_Agency), [National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geospatial-Intelligence_Agency), and other agencies maintain offices at NORTHCOM and receive daily intelligence briefings.[^cite_note-38] The total of 14 agencies with representatives at NORTHCOM in December 2002 included the State Department, [NASA](18426568.md), and the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md).[^cite_note-39]

Northern Command has completed several joint training exercises with local, state and federal law enforcement agencies, the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md), and the [Federal Emergency Management Agency](73797.md) (FEMA).[^cite_note-Head-2009-p60-40]

In Exercise Vigilant Shield 2008, Northern Command, [Pacific Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Pacific_Command), the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md), and numerous law enforcement agencies across the U.S. conducted exercises to test their "response abilities against a variety of potential threats".[^cite_note-Head-2009-p60-40]

In January 2025 the Northern Command activated military police and combat engineer units from the Army and Marine Corps to support [Customs and Border Protection](757040.md) on the U.S. southern border.[^cite_note-41] In March 2025, the newly formed [Joint Task Force-Southern Border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Task_Force-Southern_Border?action=edit\&redlink=1), from the headquarters of the [10th Mountain Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Mountain_Division), took over control of the operation along the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border), to oversee joint forces and serve as the NORTHCOM land component command for the mission, which involves about 10,000 service members.[^cite_note-42]

## Related legislation

The [Posse Comitatus Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posse_Comitatus_Act) of 1878 and subsequent [Department of Defense](7279897.md) policy constrains any member of the [United States Army](32087.md), [Air Force](32090.md), [Navy](20518076.md), or [Marine Corps](17349325.md), and the [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Guard) under federal authority from domestically intervening in a law enforcement capacity on United States soil. Several exceptions to the law have been used in the past, including protecting the citizens' constitutional rights in the absence of state and/or local assistance, such as protecting the [Little Rock Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock_Nine) students in [Little Rock, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas) in 1957, and using the [Insurrection Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurrection_Act) to quell civil disorders, such as the [1992 Los Angeles riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Los_Angeles_riots).

The [Military Commissions Act of 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Commissions_Act_of_2006) lifted many restrictions placed on the military to support non-military authorities by the Posse Comitatus Act, however the United States Supreme Court ruled in June 2008 that significant portions of the MCA were unconstitutional. The "John Warner Defense Authorization Act of 2007" [H.R. 5122 (2006)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.R._5122_\(2006\)) effectively nullified the limits of the Insurrection Act[^cite_note-43] when it was passed; however, the bill was amended in 2008.

On 1 October 2008, the [3rd Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\))'s 1st [Brigade Combat Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_Combat_Team) was assigned to U.S. Northern Command, marking the first time an active unit had been given a dedicated assignment to Northern Command. The force will be known for the first year as a CBRNE Consequence Management Response Force, and will serve as an on-call federal response force for terrorist attacks and other natural or manmade emergencies and disasters.[^cite_note-44]
