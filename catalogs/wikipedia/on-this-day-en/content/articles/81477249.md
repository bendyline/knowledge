The relationship between British politician [Peter Mandelson](216970.md) and the American child sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md) spanned from at least 2002 to 2011, continuing after Epstein's first conviction in 2008. Their friendship had been public knowledge, but a fresh wave of scrutiny and public outrage erupted in September 2025 following the release of US court documents and a cache of private emails between the two men by the [US House Oversight Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Oversight_and_Government_Reform). The backlash created a [political scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_scandal) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) with significant ramifications for the governing British [Labour Party](19279158.md) and prime minister [Keir Starmer](18681491.md), who soon dismissed Mandelson as the [British Ambassador to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Ambassador_to_the_United_States) and oversaw his resignation from the Labour Party and from the [House of Lords](13658.md).

The [Epstein files](80415168.md) revealed the depth and nature of their relationship. Mandelson called Epstein his "best pal" in a 2003 [birthday book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein's_birthday_book) message; had reportedly expressed support for Epstein following his 2008 conviction in Florida for soliciting prostitution from a minor;[^cite_note-1] allegedly told Epstein he "thought the world of him" and advised him to "fight for early release" from his 18-month sentence; reportedly had his travel paid for by Epstein in 2003; received payments from Epstein alongside his husband; and alleged that, in 2009–10, he passed on confidential government information to Epstein amid the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) while Mandelson was serving as [business secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Business_and_Trade) in the [Gordon Brown ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_ministry).

Starmer and the then-[foreign secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_\(United_Kingdom\)) [David Lammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lammy) announced Mandelson's appointment as British Ambassador to the United States in December 2024[^cite_note-2] to strengthen the government's relationship with incoming [US president](24113.md) [Donald Trump](4848272.md). In September 2025, amid the first releases of the Epstein files, the British government claimed that newly published emails provided "materially different" information about the extent of the Mandelson–Epstein relationship, and dismissed Mandelson as ambassador. In February 2026, Starmer told the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons) that Mandelson "betrayed our country, our Parliament, and my party. He lied repeatedly" and that he regretted appointing him.[^cite_note-3]

The scandal brought down several other government figures, including Starmer's [Chief of Staff](4476964.md) [Morgan McSweeney](75790152.md) who took responsibility for Mandelson's appointment, and [Olly Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olly_Robbins), a top civil servant in the [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Office).[^cite_note-4] Several people have called on Starmer to resign as a result of the scandal, including [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(United_Kingdom\)) [Kemi Badenoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemi_Badenoch) who called the prime minister's position "untenable",[^cite_note-5] [Ed Davey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Davey) ([Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)) leader), [Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage) ([Reform UK](59757202.md) leader), [Zack Polanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Polanski) ([Green Party](79787.md) leader), [Liz Saville Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Saville_Roberts) ([Plaid Cymru](53343.md) parliamentary leader), and [John Swinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swinney) ([First Minister of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Scotland) and [SNP](28887.md) leader).[^cite_note-6]

Mandelson was stripped of honorary awards and titles, and for his part expressed deep regret for the association, claiming he was "taken in" by a "charismatic criminal liar", and profoundly sympathised with Epstein's victims. Mandelson resigned from the Labour Party and from the House of Lords, and the [Metropolitan Police](192450.md) launched a criminal investigation into alleged misconduct in a public office. On 23 February 2026, Mandelson was arrested on suspicion of misconduct in public office, and then released on [bail](170652.md) pending further investigation.[^cite_note-\:0-7][^cite_note-bailreleaseinvestigation-8]

In April 2026, it was reported that Mandelson had been denied security clearance by [security vetting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_vetting_in_the_United_Kingdom) back in January 2025. A government spokesperson said "the decision to grant developed vetting to Peter Mandelson against the recommendation of UK Security Vetting was taken by officials in the FCDO".[^cite_note-9] Starmer said he was not told that he had failed security vetting, and denied claims that he had misled the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons). [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_\(United_Kingdom\)) [Yvette Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvette_Cooper) dismissed the most senior civil servant in the Foreign Office [Olly Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olly_Robbins), who said he had been put under "serious pressure" to push through the appointment at a summons to the [Foreign Affairs Select Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_Select_Committee).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Mandelson was a friend of [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md), an American financier who pleaded guilty to soliciting prostitution from someone under the age of 18 in 2008, and was arrested again on federal charges for the [sex trafficking](2101268.md) of minors in 2019, later [dying in custody](61499571.md). They were friends while Mandelson was serving as a cabinet minister under Labour prime ministers [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) and [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown). The friendship lasted at least from 2002 to 2011, continuing even after Epstein's first conviction in 2008. Amid the wider "Epstein scandal" in 2019, UK's [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4) aired an episode of *[Dispatches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispatches_\(TV_programme\))* in which a source close to Epstein said that Mandelson, while serving as a cabinet minister, made a phone call to Epstein in order to set up a meeting with [Jamie Dimon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Dimon), CEO of [JPMorgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPMorgan). In June 2023, an internal JPMorgan report from 2019, filed with a New York court, found that "Jeffrey Epstein appears to maintain a particularly close relationship with [Prince Andrew the Duke of York](125267.md)[^cite_note-12] and Lord Peter Mandelson, a senior member of the British government."[^cite_note-\:12-13] In 2024, a picture of Mandelson with Epstein on his private island [Little Saint James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Saint_James) resurfaced in court documents.[^cite_note-bbc12sept-14] In September 2025 it emerged that an internal report by JPMorgan suggested that, in 2009, Mandelson stayed at Epstein's Manhattan apartment while Epstein was serving his 18-month prison sentence for soliciting prostitution.[^cite_note-15]

According to reports in September 2025, Mandelson flew on Epstein's private jet, the [Lolita Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolita_Express), on at least one occasion. A former Epstein employee, Valdson Vieira Cotrin, recalls Mandelson flying with Epstein on his private jet. The trip, in the early 2000s, was from St. Thomas in the US Virgin Islands to New York. A former employee believes Epstein made a short helicopter trip to meet Mandelson while he was staying on a nearby island.[^cite_note-16]

Additionally, Epstein paid for commercial flights for Mandelson. Financial records from the US House of Representatives Oversight Committee show that Epstein paid for two commercial flights for Mandelson in April 2003, totalling more than \$7,400 (£5,400). Emails from October 2005 show that, after Mandelson complained to Epstein about a lack of British Airways air miles, Epstein offered to pay for his plane tickets to the Caribbean. It is unknown if Mandelson accepted the offer.[^cite_note-bbc12sept-14] It also emerged that in 2002, Mandelson encouraged Prime Minister [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) to meet Epstein,[^cite_note-17] and that during his time as business secretary, Mandelson reportedly asked Epstein to broker a deal for the sale of a UK taxpayer-owned banking business.[^cite_note-18] Documents concerning Mandelson's 2002 memo and advice to Blair on Epstein were set to be released from the National Archives in September 2025 but were withheld due to an "international relations" exemption.[^cite_note-19]

#### Payments from Epstein

In January 2026, a release of documents relating to Epstein showed Mandelson and his boyfriend (since October 2023 husband) Reinaldo Avila da Silva received upwards of \$75,000 in three payments from Epstein, the first of such payments having been made to Mandelson in 2003 and the last in 2004.[^cite_note-20]

According to the documents, Avila da Silva was sent thousands of pounds between 2009 and 2010[^cite_note-21] as Epstein put the Cabinet minister's boyfriend on a monthly stipend of \$4,000 (£2,950), sent directly to his personal bank account.[^cite_note-22]

#### Support of Epstein



Mandelson spent nearly a week on Epstein's private Caribbean island, [Little Saint James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Saint_James), in 2003.

On 9 September 2025, the [US House Oversight Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Oversight_and_Government_Reform) published a series of documents from [Epstein's estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_of_Jeffrey_Epstein), which included a letter written by Mandelson to Epstein in 2003.[^cite_note-\:43-23][^cite_note-\:53-24][^cite_note-25] The letter was part of a [birthday book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein's_birthday_book) compiled by [Ghislaine Maxwell](32018562.md), for Epstein's 50th birthday in 2003.[^cite_note-\:43-23] Within that letter, Mandelson described Epstein as an "intelligent sharp-witted man" and as "my best pal".[^cite_note-\:53-24] A number of pictures of Mandelson, including one with Epstein, were published alongside the letter.[^cite_note-\:43-23] Alongside one picture of Mandelson with two women, whose faces are obscured, he writes about meeting Epstein's interesting – in inverted commas – friends.[^cite_note-26]

&#x20;On 10 September 2025, further communication from Mandelson to Epstein was published widely, including an email sent in June 2008 after Epstein's conviction for soliciting prostitution from someone aged under 18 and before his sentencing, where he wrote:[^cite_note-\:16-27][^cite_note-28]

> "You have to be incredibly resilient, fight for early release and be philosophical about it as much as you can. The whole thing has been years of torture and now you have to show the world how big a person you are, and how strong. I think the world of you and I feel hopeless and furious about what has happened. I can still barely understand it. It just could not happen in Britain. Everything can be turned into an opportunity and that you will come through it and be stronger for it. Your friends stay with you and love you."

His comments came after the release of US documents detailing his interactions with Epstein, including a 2003 message where Mandelson called Epstein his "best pal". During the interview, Mandelson said he deeply regrets continuing his association with Epstein for "far longer than I should have done" and that the association has felt like "an albatross around my neck" since Epstein's death. He stated that he was "taken in" and "fell for his lies," believing Epstein's assurances about his 2008 conviction for soliciting prostitution from an underage person, but now recognised Epstein as a "charismatic criminal liar". The BBC reported that Mandelson disputes the interpretation that he thought the conviction was "wrongful," instead believing the length of the sentence should be challenged. He maintains that he was truthful during the vetting process, but No. 10 sources said he was "economical with the truth".[^cite_note-60]

Starmer has faced significant political pressure regarding the scandal, particularly concerning his initial appointment and later dismissal of Mandelson as ambassador to the US. Starmer's response has included publicly expressing anger, stating that he would not have made the appointment had he known the full extent of Mandelson's ties to Epstein, and acknowledging flaws in the [vetting process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_vetting_in_the_United_Kingdom).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] On Wednesday, 10 September 2025, during [Prime Minister's Questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister's_Questions), Starmer publicly defended Mandelson amid repeated calls for him to resign as ambassador.[^cite_note-67] Starmer dismissed Mandelson the next day, 11 September, after emails emerged showing Mandelson encouraged Epstein to "fight for early release" from jail in 2008. Starmer described Mandelson's comments as "reprehensible".[^cite_note-68] Following usual [HR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_resources) procedure, as he was dismissed Mandelson received a payoff from the Foreign Office of £75,000.[^cite_note-JHWT-69][^cite_note-70]

Downing Street sources stated that the newly published emails revealed a relationship with Epstein that was[^cite_note-71] "materially different" from what was known at the time of the appointment. Starmer said he was not "at all" satisfied with Mandelson's responses to questions from government officials. Starmer claimed that while he knew at PMQs that the Foreign Office was investigating the emails, he was not made aware of their full content until later that evening, after PMQs. He told Channel 4 News he was "angry to have been put in that position". Some reports suggested Starmer was deliberately kept in the dark by his team. While the Foreign Office later acknowledged that Mandelson was not given in-depth vetting before the appointment, a full review of vetting processes has been avoided, leading to further questions from the Opposition.[^cite_note-72] Critics, including Leader of the Opposition and Conservative leader [Kemi Badenoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemi_Badenoch) and [Leader of the Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Liberal_Democrats) [Ed Davey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Davey), have accused Starmer of poor judgement, dishonesty, and hiding from scrutiny.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

A source close to Starmer revealed that the prime minister is "frustrated and a bit angry" at the scandal, because "he is having to deal with the conduct of others, rather than show what he is trying to do".[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] The scandal has been described as particularly damaging for the Starmer ministry, starting days after the [tax scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Rayner_tax_scandal) surrounding former [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom) [Angela Rayner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Rayner).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] It has also been described as damaging for the UK's negotiations with Trump, due to [Trump's own relationship with Epstein](80511968.md) which has also been the source of major controversy. In his first interview since Mandelson was dismissed, Starmer said: "I don't particularly think anger helps here, but I feel let down. I feel that the process was gone through and now information has come to light which had I known it at the time, I wouldn't have appointed him."[^cite_note-79] Starmer began facing leadership questions amid the scandal, with many Labour MPs expressing public and private frustration with the prime minister's leadership. MPs reportedly viewed underperformance in the May 2026 [United Kingdom local elections](79139854.md), [2026 Scottish Parliament election](65934215.md) and [2026 Senedd election](68245236.md) as a likely catalyst for a leadership challenge.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

Mandelson was dismissed by Starmer on 11 September 2025 following the publication in *[The Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_\(United_Kingdom\))* newspaper of supportive emails Mandelson sent to Epstein after he was arrested in 2008.[^cite_note-83] The emails show that Mandelson stated Epstein's first conviction was wrongful and tried to help Epstein to challenge the verdict.[^cite_note-84] Foreign Office minister [Stephen Doughty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Doughty) said these emails showed "the depth and extent of Peter Mandelson's relationship with Jeffrey Epstein is materially different from that known at the time of his appointment. In light of that, and mindful of the victims of Epstein's appalling crimes, he has been withdrawn as ambassador with immediate effect."[^cite_note-85] In his letter to US embassy staff after being dismissed, Mandelson said: "The circumstances surrounding the announcement today are ones which I deeply regret. I continue to feel utterly awful about my association with Epstein twenty years ago and the plight of his victims. I have no alternative to accepting the Prime Minister's decision and will leave a position in which I have been so incredibly honoured to serve." [James Roscoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Roscoe_\(diplomat\)) was appointed interim ambassador following Mandelson's dismissal, and [Christian Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Turner) was appointed ambassador in February 2026.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-usdec25-87]

In a February 2026 statement at Prime Minister's Questions following subsequent revelations about Mandelson in the [Epstein files](80415168.md), Starmer addressed the fallout from revealed links between Mandelson and Epstein, focusing on accountability and the protection of public standards.[^cite_note-88] He began by expressing solidarity with Epstein's victims and those affected by the 2008 financial crash, acknowledging their trauma and anger. Starmer accused Mandelson of repeatedly lying to his team during the vetting process for his appointment as US ambassador, specifically misrepresenting the nature of his relationship with Epstein. He characterised Mandelson's alleged actions—including leaking sensitive information during the 2008 financial crisis—as a betrayal of the country, Parliament, and the Labour Party.[^cite_note-89] Regarding legal consequences, Starmer confirmed that material had been referred to the police, resulting in an active criminal investigation into potential misconduct. He also announced plans to draft legislation to strip Mandelson of his title and to establish broader powers for removing disgraced peers from the House of Lords.[^cite_note-90] Finally, he stated he reached an agreement with [King Charles III](125248.md) to remove Mandelson from the Privy Council for bringing the body into disrepute. Starmer explicitly expressed regret for the appointment, stating that Mandelson would never have been considered for the role had the full extent of his ties to Epstein been known at the time.[^cite_note-91]

### Resignation of Morgan McSweeney

On 8 February 2026, following increasing internal pressures, McSweeney resigned as Starmer's chief of staff.[^cite_note-92] In a written statement, McSweeney took responsibility for the decision to appoint Mandelson, acknowledging it was wrong and had damaged the party and trust in politics. He stated that stepping aside was "the only honourable course". McSweeney expressed pride in the government's achievements, stating that his motivation was always to support a Labour government focused on ordinary people. He also highlighted the importance of remembering the victims of Epstein. Although he did not oversee vetting, he called for it to be fundamentally overhauled. He concluded by affirming his support for Starmer's mission.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

Starmer accepted McSweeney's resignation and issued a statement expressing gratitude for his service. Starmer stated that he and the Labour Party owe McSweeney a "debt of gratitude" for his years of service. He credited McSweeney with a central role in the party's electoral success, including the [2024 landslide majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election). Starmer praised McSweeney's "dedication, loyalty and leadership" and said it had been an honour to work with him.[^cite_note-95] Following McSweeney's resignation, major news outlets such as BBC and *The Guardian* described the move as a significant political shake-up, highlighting increased scrutiny of Downing Street's vetting procedures. The resignation also prompted discussions within the Labour Party about improving internal oversight and accountability. The resignation also led to [Tim Allan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Allan) resigning as [Director of Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_Communications_\(Office_of_the_Prime_Minister\)) the following day.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-ALCAM-97]

In October 2025, McSweeney, who was then the Downing Street Chief of Staff, reported his government-issued phone as stolen by a cyclist in Westminster.[^cite_note-98] The incident gained significant attention in March 2026 following a parliamentary order for the government to release communications between McSweeney and Mandelson regarding the latter’s appointment as US ambassador.[^cite_note-99] Critics, including Conservative opposition leader [Kemi Badenoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemi_Badenoch), have raised concerns about the timing of the theft, while Prime Minister Keir Starmer has dismissed suggestions of a "cover-up" as "far-fetched". To provide transparency, the Metropolitan Police released a transcript of McSweeney’s 999 call, which revealed he initially provided an incorrect street name, leading to the case being recorded in the wrong location and subsequently closed. The Met has since reopened the investigation to reassess evidence in the correct area.[^cite_note-100]

### Calls for resignation



[Anas Sarwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anas_Sarwar) publicly called for Starmer to resign

On 9 February 2026, Scottish Labour Party leader [Anas Sarwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anas_Sarwar) held a press conference in Glasgow to publicly call for Starmer to resign. Sarwar stated that "the distraction needs to end and the leadership in Downing Street has to change," citing a series of "mistakes" that he believed were undermining Labour's prospects in the elections. Despite describing Starmer as a "decent man" and a "friend," Sarwar argued that his primary loyalty was to Scotland and that the Prime Minister's continued leadership was sabotaging the party's future.[^cite_note-101]

Sarwar said he had spoken to Starmer earlier that day to inform him of his decision, stating that the two had "disagreed" on the necessity of his resignation. In the immediate aftermath, senior Cabinet members including Deputy Prime Minister [David Lammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lammy) and Chancellor [Rachel Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Reeves) rallied behind Starmer, emphasising his mandate and urging party unity. Within Scottish Labour, the move caused significant friction; while [Monica Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Lennon) supported Sarwar's "leadership," others like former Scottish Secretary [Ian Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Murray_\(Scottish_politician\)) branded the call a threat to party stability. Opposition leaders [John Swinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swinney) and [Russell Findlay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Findlay) characterised the situation as "opportunism" and a "meltdown."[^cite_note-102] Addressing the Parliamentary Labour Party later that evening, Starmer remained defiant, stating he had "won every fight I've ever been in" and refused to walk away.[^cite_note-103]

The scandal, amid mounting wider public dissatisfaction with the [Starmer ministry](77280613.md), contributed to Labour's poor results in the [2026 United Kingdom local elections](79139854.md).[^cite_note-104] This led to the [2026 Labour Party leadership crisis](83157483.md) in which over 95 Labour MPs had called on Starmer to resign or set out a departure timetable, and one [cabinet minister](162790.md) (Health Secretary [Wes Streeting](17169295.md)), four junior ministers (including [Jess Phillips](46646379.md)), and four [ministerial aides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_private_secretary) resigned in protest.[^cite_note-105]

### Dismissal of Olly Robbins

[Olly Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olly_Robbins) had been appointed [Permanent Under-Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Under-Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_Affairs) at the [Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office](59653.md) (FCDO) on 8 January 2025. On 16 April 2026, [Pippa Crerar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pippa_Crerar) at *[the Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported that Mandelson had been denied security clearance by [security vetting (UKSV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_vetting_in_the_United_Kingdom) on 28 January 2025 but nevertheless had been "confirmed" to be ambassador two days later. A government spokesperson told the Guardian that "the decision to grant developed vetting to Peter Mandelson against the recommendation of UK Security Vetting was taken by officials in the FCDO".[^cite_note-106]

During a summit in Paris, Starmer said "I was not told that he had failed security vetting, no minister was told", and denied that he had misled the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons) in February when saying that 'full due process had been followed.'[^cite_note-107] Both Starmer and [Foreign Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Secretary_\(United_Kingdom\)) [Yvette Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvette_Cooper) 'lost confidence' in Robbins and he was dismissed. [Darren Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Jones), the prime minister's [Chief Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Secretary_to_the_Prime_Minister) said that the prime minister had not been told about the denial of clearance until 14 April 2026, and announced he had suspended a government department's ability to overrule the vetting process.

#### Parliamentary inquiries

Starmer delivered a statement to the House of Commons on 20 April 2026, while [Emily Thornberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Thornberry) also invited Robbins to appear before the [Foreign Affairs select committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_Select_Committee) on 21 April to be questioned over his role in the appointment of Mandelson.[^cite_note-108] In his statement, Starmer maintained his position that blame lies firmly with the Foreign Office, claiming there had been a "deliberate decision" to withhold information about the vetting process from MPs.[^cite_note-109] Robbins delivered his evidence to the Foreign Affairs Select Committee the following morning, where he argued that the Foreign Office had been put under "serious pressure" to push through Mandelson's appointment.[^cite_note-110] During his appearance with the Committee, Robbins also claimed that Downing Street considered finding a job as a Head of Mission for [Matthew Doyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Doyle_\(political_adviser\)), the then-[director of communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downing_Street_Director_of_Communications) at Number 10.[^cite_note-111]

Following the appearance of Robbins at the committee, both [Morgan McSweeney](75790152.md) and [Philip Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Barton) were summoned to appear at the [Foreign Affairs select committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_Select_Committee) on 28 April.[^cite_note-112] The Conservative Party also requested that Starmer be investigated over claims he misled the Commons over comments regarding whether he followed "due process" and over his assertion that "no pressure whatsoever" was applied to officials at the Foreign Office. Other party leaders, including [Ed Davey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Davey), supported the vote whilst Labour grandees including [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown) issued public statements in support of the Prime Minister.[^cite_note-113]

## Other responses

### Virginia Giuffre's relatives

The surviving relatives of [Virginia Giuffre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Giuffre), the most outspoken victim of Epstein and Maxwell (and accuser of Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor) who died by suicide months earlier in 2025, disapproved of Mandelson's appointment and stated that he should never have been appointed to the role to begin with. Giuffre's sister-in-law, Amanda Roberts, said: "Why does it take us to have to pull out the skeletons for people to be held accountable? Our governments have allowed these people to hold their status and their title without shame. He should never have been given that title. We have to put the spotlight on them. It's unfair we continuously pull these skeletons out, that survivors have to continuously point the finger for us to do the right thing." Sky Roberts, Giuffre's younger brother, said that the firing of Mandelson was a "step in the right direction" but "the reality is that's not nearly enough." Referencing Epstein's birthday book, he added, "there are still people out there, still people in that book who could be doing this to other young women and children right now."[^cite_note-114]

### Labour ministers

Health Secretary [Wes Streeting](17169295.md)'s relationship with Mandelson has shifted from one of long-standing political mentorship to a public "defriending" following the revelations involving Epstein. Historically, the two were close allies; Mandelson reportedly viewed Streeting as a "keeper of the Blairite flame" and even canvassed for him to become Labour leader during the 2022 [Beergate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beergate) controversy. Their ties were further solidified by Streeting's husband, Joe Dancey, who previously worked as an adviser to Mandelson. As recently as September 2025, Streeting defended Mandelson after reports surfaced regarding Mandelson's ties to Epstein, stating that the public should not regard individuals as "guilty by association".[^cite_note-115] However, in February 2026, following new evidence that Mandelson allegedly leaked sensitive government information to Epstein, Streeting accused Mandelson of a "terrible betrayal" of the country and two Prime Ministers. Streeting has since stated that he regrets his association with Mandelson, describing his actions as "outrageous" and insisting he must suffer the legal and political consequences. Despite this distancing, some Labour insiders suggest Streeting remains "tainted" by his years of closeness to Mandelson, which could impact his future leadership ambitions. Streeting later released all of his conversations with Mandelson from 2024 to 2025 to the public, stating "I have nothing to hide."[^cite_note-116]

When Business Secretary [Peter Kyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kyle) was asked if Mandelson's appointment was a mistake, Kyle said: "They \[Giuffre's family] say it was a mistake. And retrospectively, if we had known the information that we know now, it is highly unlikely that he would have been appointed because what we know now is materially different to what we understood at the time."[^cite_note-117]

When asked about Mandelson's payout following dismissal as an ambassador, [Secretary of State for Work and Pensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Work_and_Pensions) [Pat McFadden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_McFadden) said he should probably give it back or give it to charity, suggesting one supporting victims of [violence against women and girls](3634424.md),[^cite_note-AGFT-118] a sentiment echoed by a source from the Prime Minister's office.[^cite_note-JHWT-69] The Foreign Office said it had launched a review into the severance pay.[^cite_note-119]

#### Angela Rayner

### Concern over potential impact on Trump state visit

Main article: [2025 state visit by Donald Trump to the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_state_visit_by_Donald_Trump_to_the_United_Kingdom)



The scandal created a diplomatic predicament for Trump, and his team were reportedly concerned about the timing.

As the initial revelations about Mandelson were revealed shortly before the [state visit by Donald Trump to the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_state_visit_by_Donald_Trump_to_the_United_Kingdom), it created a diplomatic predicament for Trump, and his team were reportedly concerned about the timing, as Trump faced major backlash at the time over [his own association with Epstein](80511968.md) and his initial refusal to release the [Epstein files](80415168.md) despite previously promising to do so; Trump later approved their release which resulted in the subsequent Mandelson revelations being revealed. A source with knowledge of the White House's discussions said that Trump's team was "nervy" about anything that could resurface the Epstein scandal, and they were concerned Mandelson's dismissal would overshadow the UK state visit. *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* noted that with Trump having his own well-documented links to Epstein, "there is no subject he wants to revisit less" than the scandal surrounding Mandelson's ties to him. For Trump, whose priority was to avoid distractions during the state visit, the controversy arrived at an "especially awkward" time.[^cite_note-Edwards-157][^cite_note-158]

On 15 September 2025, members of the UK collective [Everyone Hates Elon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everyone_Hates_Elon) installed a 400-square-metre print of a widely circulated photograph of Trump and Epstein on the lawns of Windsor Castle.[^cite_note-Edwards-157] On 17 September, journalists were detained for questioning after police confiscated an advertising van associated with the group People vs Elon that was displaying images of Trump and Epstein on the streets of Windsor.[^cite_note-Lyons-159] On 16 September, members of the [Led By Donkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_By_Donkeys) protest group, which had also targeted Trump during his [last visit to the UK](52890158.md), projected a nine-minute video discussing Trump and Epstein's relationship from a hotel room opposite Windsor. The video also included a photograph and video clip of [Virginia Giuffre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Giuffre), a widely circulated photograph of Andrew with Epstein, and photographs of Epstein's accomplice Maxwell.[^cite_note-Edwards-157][^cite_note-\:1-160][^cite_note-\:22-161] Officers reportedly investigated the projection to "swiftly" stop it. Four individuals were arrested the following day, which according to an official statement by [Thames Valley Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_Valley_Police), was done on the grounds of malicious communications and an "unauthorised projection".[^cite_note-Lyons-159][^cite_note-162][^cite_note-Guardian-Sep17-163][^cite_note-\:1-160] They included a 60-year-old from [East Sussex](67923.md), a 36-year-old and a 50-year-old from [London](17867.md), and a 37-year-old from [Kent](16766.md). All four were released on [conditional bail](170652.md) until 12 December.[^cite_note-\:32-164] The investigation concluded, with no further action taken, on 22 October.[^cite_note-165]

&#x20;According to Led By Donkeys, it was the first time anyone from the group had been arrested for a projection.[^cite_note-\:22-161][^cite_note-\:4-166] Led by Donkeys later told *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* that the arrests were "[Orwellian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orwellian)" and "ridiculous". They mentioned that they had made between 25 and 30 projections prior to these arrests.[^cite_note-\:4-166] They stated in their interview:

> "Forgive the cliche, but it is rather Orwellian for a piece of journalism, which raises questions about our guest's relationship with America's most notorious child sex trafficker to lead to arrests." "We're constantly told, you know, we need to see peaceful protests. Well, here's a peaceful protest ... We projected a piece of journalism on to a wall and now people have been arrested for malicious communications. I think that, frankly, says a lot more about the policing of Trump's visit than it does about what we did." "Trump is being welcomed to our country, being given the unique honour of a second state visit, and it's being housed at our expense at Windsor Castle," they said. "This is like The Emperor's New Clothes – you've got to point at it and say, 'Hang on. You know, this guy has incredibly close links to America's most notorious child sex trafficker. We probably need to talk about that.' And so we decided to build a film that would tell that story."[^cite_note-\:4-166]

The morning of 17 September, a van parked outside Windsor Castle displayed an image of Trump and Epstein alongside the text: "Welcome to the UK, Donald."[^cite_note-\:32-164] During a press conference held in the UK on 18 September 2025, Trump claimed he did not know who Mandelson was, despite having met him multiple times. Trump made the statement when asked if he had any sympathy for Mandelson after he was dismissed from his role as ambassador. In response, Trump stated: "I don't know him, actually." He added that it was a choice made by the prime minister and suggested Starmer was better placed to speak on the matter. Despite Trump's claim of not knowing Mandelson, multiple sources and photographic evidence show they have met on several occasions. In May 2025, Mandelson stood with Trump in the Oval Office during the announcement of a US-UK trade framework. Photos captured the two shaking hands and smiling together. Mandelson also received a signed note from Trump that read, "Peter, great job!"[^cite_note-POLITICO-2025-38][^cite_note-Sky_News-39] This was the second time Trump attempted to distance himself from an Epstein associate; in December 2019 he claimed to not know Andrew, despite multiple sources and photographic evidence showing they have met on several occasions.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]
