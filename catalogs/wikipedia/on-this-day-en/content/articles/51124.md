**Pangolins**, also called **scaly anteaters**,[^cite_note-12] are [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammals) of the order **Pholidota** ([/fɒlɪˈdoʊtə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)). The one [extant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neontology) family, the [Manidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manidae), has three genera: *[Manis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manis)*, *[Phataginus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phataginus)*, and *[Smutsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smutsia)*.[^cite_note-13] *Manis* comprises five species found in Asia, while *Phataginus* and *Smutsia* include two species each, all found in sub-Saharan Africa.[^cite_note-web2.utc.edu-14] These species range in size from 30 to 100 cm (12 to 39 in). Several [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct) pangolin species are also known. In September 2023, [a ninth species was reported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manis_mysteria), although it is only known from confiscated scales and has never been observed in the wild.[^cite_note-NYT-20230925-15]

Pangolins have large, protective [keratin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keratin) scales, covering their skin. Depending on the species, they live in hollow trees or [burrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrow). Pangolins are [nocturnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnal), and their diet consists of mainly [ants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ants) and [termites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termites), which they capture using their long tongues.[^cite_note-16] They tend to be solitary animals, meeting only to mate and produce a litter of one to three offspring, which they raise for about two years.&#x20;

Pangolins are [threatened by poaching](53302120.md) (for their meat and scales, which are used in [traditional medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_medicine)[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]) and heavy [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) of their natural habitats, and are also the most [trafficked](26238888.md) mammals in the world.[^cite_note-19] As of January 2020, there are eight species of pangolin whose [conservation status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_status) is listed in the [threatened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threatened_species) tier. Three (*[Manis culionensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manis_culionensis)*, *[M. pentadactyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manis_pentadactyla)* and *[M. javanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manis_javanica)*) are critically endangered, three (*[Phataginus tricuspis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phataginus_tricuspis)*, *[Manis crassicaudata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manis_crassicaudata)* and *[Smutsia gigantea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smutsia_gigantea)*) are endangered and two (*[Phataginus tetradactyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phataginus_tetradactyla)* and *[Smutsia temminckii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smutsia_temminckii)*) are vulnerable on the [Red List of Threatened Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_List_of_Threatened_Species) of the [International Union for Conservation of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_for_Conservation_of_Nature).[^cite_note-IUCN_Manidae-20]

## Etymology

The name of order Pholidota comes from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) Φολιδωτός – "clad in scales"[^cite_note-RWB-21] from φολίς *pholís* "[scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_\(anatomy\))".[^cite_note-22]

The name "pangolin" comes from the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) word *pengguling* meaning "one who rolls up"[^cite_note-COED-23] from *guling* or *giling* "to roll"; it was used for the [Sunda pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_pangolin) (*Manis javanica*).[^cite_note-Wilkinson-24] However, the modern name is *tenggiling*. In [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language),  it is *terenggiling*;[^cite_note-Wilkinson-24] and in the [Philippine languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_languages), it is *goling*, *tanggiling*, or *balintong* (with the same meaning).[^cite_note-Vergara-25]

In ancient India, according to [Aelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Aelianus), it was known as the *phattáges* (φαττάγης).[^cite_note-26]

## Description



Pangolin [skeletons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton) at the [Museum of Osteology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Osteology) (2009)



Schematic drawing of pangolin scale histology

The physical appearance of a pangolin is marked by large, hardened, overlapping, plate-like scales, which are soft on newborn pangolins, but harden as the animal matures.[^cite_note-27] They are made of [keratin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keratin), the same material from which human [fingernails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingernails) and [tetrapod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapod) [claws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claw#Tetrapods) are made, and are structurally and compositionally very different from the scales of reptiles.[^cite_note-Spearman2008-28] The pangolin's scaled body is comparable in appearance to a [pine cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_cone). It can curl up into a ball when threatened, with its overlapping scales acting as [armor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armour_\(anatomy\)), while it protects its face by tucking it under its tail. The scales are sharp, providing extra defense from predators.[^cite_note-Wang2016-29] Despite their appearance, they are not closely related to [armadillos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armadillo), having both evolved scales by [convergent evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_evolution).

Pangolins can emit a noxious-smelling chemical from [glands near the anus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anal_gland), similar to the spray of a [skunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skunk).[^cite_note-30] They have short legs, with sharp claws which they use for burrowing into [ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant) and [termite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termite) mounds and for climbing.[^cite_note-31]

The tongues of pangolins are extremely long, and like those of the [giant anteater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_anteater) and the [tube-lipped nectar bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tube-lipped_nectar_bat), the root of the tongue is not attached to the [hyoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyoid) bone but is in the [thorax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorax) between the [sternum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sternum) and the [trachea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate_trachea).[^cite_note-32] Large pangolins can extend their tongues as much as 40 cm (16 in), with a diameter of only about 0.5 cm (1⁄5 in).[^cite_note-Mondadori-33]

## Behaviour



[Ground pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_pangolin) in defensive posture



[Indian pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_pangolin) defending itself against [Asiatic lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_lion)

Most pangolins are [nocturnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnal) animals[^cite_note-34] which use their well-developed sense of smell to find insects. The [long-tailed pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-tailed_pangolin) is also active by day, while other species of pangolins spend most of the daytime sleeping, curled up into a ball ("[volvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvation)").[^cite_note-Mondadori-33]

[Arboreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboreal) pangolins live in hollow trees, whereas the ground-dwelling species dig tunnels to a depth of 3.5 m (11 ft).[^cite_note-Mondadori-33]

Some pangolins walk with their front claws bent under the foot pad, although they use the entire foot pad on their rear limbs. Furthermore, some exhibit a [bipedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipedalism) stance for some behavior, and may walk a few steps bipedally.[^cite_note-Mohapatra-35] Pangolins are also good swimmers.[^cite_note-Mondadori-33]

### Diet

Pangolins are [insectivorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insectivory). Most of their diet consists of various species of ants and termites and may be supplemented by other insects, especially larvae. They are somewhat particular and tend to consume only one or two species of insects, even when many species are available. A pangolin can consume 140–200 g (5–7 oz) of insects per day.[^cite_note-36] Pangolins are an important regulator of termite populations in their natural habitats.[^cite_note-37]

Pangolins have very poor [vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_perception). They also lack teeth. They rely heavily on [smell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfaction) and [hearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing), and they have other physical characteristics to help them eat ants and termites. Their skeletal structure is sturdy and they have strong front legs used for tearing into termite mounds.[^cite_note-Rose-2010-38] They use their powerful front claws to dig into trees, soil, and vegetation to find prey,[^cite_note-39] then proceed to use their long tongues to probe inside the insect tunnels and to retrieve their prey.

The structure of their tongue and stomach is key to aiding pangolins in obtaining and digesting insects. Their [saliva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saliva) is sticky,[^cite_note-Rose-2010-38] causing ants and termites to stick to their long tongues when they are hunting through insect tunnels. However, without teeth, pangolins cannot chew;[^cite_note-Gutteridge2008-40] so while [foraging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foraging), they ingest small stones ([gastroliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroliths)), which accumulate in their stomachs to help to grind up ants.[^cite_note-Publishing2015-41] This part of their stomach is called the [gizzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gizzard), and it is also covered in keratinous spines.[^cite_note-42] These spines further aid in the grinding up and digestion of the pangolin's prey.

Some species, such as the [tree pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_pangolin), use their strong, [prehensile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehensile) tails to hang from tree branches and strip away bark from the trunk, exposing insect nests inside.[^cite_note-Prothero2016-43]

### Reproduction



A [Philippine pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_pangolin) pup and its mother, a [critically endangered](7451605.md) species [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic) to the [Palawan](240395.md) island group. It is threatened by illegal poaching for the [pangolin trade](53302120.md) to China and [Vietnam](202354.md), where it is regarded as a luxury medicinal delicacy.

[^cite_note-Fabro-44]

Pangolins are solitary and meet only to reproduce, with mating typically taking place at night after the male and female pangolin meet near a watering hole. Males are larger than females, weighing up to 40% more. While the mating season is not defined, they typically mate once each year, usually during the summer or autumn. Rather than the males seeking out the females, males [mark their location with urine or feces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scent_marking) and the females find them. If competition over a female occurs, the males use their tails as clubs to fight for the opportunity to mate with her.[^cite_note-45]

[Gestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestation) periods differ by species, ranging from roughly 70 to 140 days.[^cite_note-Hua-2015-46] African pangolin females usually give birth to a single offspring at a time, but the Asiatic species may give birth to from one to three.[^cite_note-Mondadori-33] Weight at birth is 80 to 450 g (2+3⁄4 to 15+3⁄4 oz), and the average length is 150 mm (6 in). At the time of birth, the scales are soft and white. After several days, they harden and darken to resemble those of an adult pangolin. During the vulnerable stage, the mother stays with her offspring in the burrow, nursing it, and wraps her body around it if she senses danger. The young cling to the mother's tail as she moves about, although, in burrowing species, they remain in the burrow for the first two to four weeks of life. At one month, they first leave the burrow riding on the mother's back. [Weaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaning) takes place around three months of age, when the young begin to eat insects in addition to nursing. At two years of age, the offspring are sexually mature and are abandoned by the mother.[^cite_note-EoM-47]

### Phylogeny

#### Among placentals

See also: [Pholidotamorpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pholidotamorpha)

The order Pholidota was long considered to be the [sister taxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_taxon) to [Xenarthra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenarthra) (neotropical [anteaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anteater), [sloths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloth), and [armadillos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armadillo)), but recent genetic evidence indicates their closest living relatives are the [carnivorans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnivora), with which they form a [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade), the [Ferae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferae).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-AmrineMadsen2003-60][^cite_note-Beck_et_al_2006-61][^cite_note-Du_Toit2014-62] [Palaeanodonts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeanodonta) are even closer relatives to pangolins, being classified with pangolins in the clade [Pholidotamorpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pholidotamorpha).[^cite_note-KondrashovAgadjanian2012-63] The split between carnivoran and pangolin lineages is estimated to have occurred 75.31 [Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega-annum) (million years) ago.[^cite_note-Wangmo2025-64]

#### Among Manidae

The first dichotomy in the [phylogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogeny) of extant Manidae separates Asian pangolins (*Manis*) from African pangolins (*Smutsia* and *Phataginus*).[^cite_note-McKenna-66][^cite_note-Du_Toit2014-62] Within the former, *Manis pentadactyla* is the [sister group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_group) to a clade comprising *M. crassicaudata* and *M. javanica*. Within the latter, a split separates the large terrestrial African pangolins of the genus *Smutsia* from the small arboreal African pangolins of the genus *Phataginus*.[^cite_note-Gaubert2018-67]

Asian and African pangolins are thought to have diverged about 38.05 Ma ago.[^cite_note-Wangmo2025-64] Moreover, the [basal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_\(phylogenetics\)) position of *Manis* within Pholidota[^cite_note-Du_Toit2014-62][^cite_note-du_Toit2017-68][^cite_note-Heighton2023-69] suggests the group originated in Eurasia, consistent with their [laurasiatherian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurasiatheria) phylogeny.[^cite_note-Du_Toit2014-62]

## Threats

See also: [Pangolin trade](53302120.md)



Confiscated black market pangolin scales, which are in high demand in [traditional Chinese medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_traditional_medicine),

[^cite_note-cnnchangethelist-70]

&#x20;which were destroyed by authorities in [Cameroon](5447.md) in 2017

Pangolins are in high demand in southern China and [Vietnam](202354.md) because their scales are believed to have medicinal properties in [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine) and [Vietnamese medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Vietnamese_medicine).[^cite_note-71] Their meat is also considered a delicacy.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-javanica-74][^cite_note-75] 100,000 are estimated to be trafficked a year to China and Vietnam,[^cite_note-Haenlein-2018-76] amounting to over one million over the past decade.[^cite_note-pangolinsg-77][^cite_note-78] This makes them the most [trafficked animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_trade) in the world.[^cite_note-pangolinsg-77][^cite_note-79] This, coupled with [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), has led to a large decrease in the numbers of pangolins. Some species, such as *[Manis pentadactyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_pangolin)* have become commercially extinct in certain ranges as a result of overhunting.[^cite_note-80]

In November 2010, pangolins were added to the [Zoological Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoological_Society_of_London)'s list of evolutionarily distinct and endangered mammals.[^cite_note-81] All eight species of pangolin are assessed as threatened by the [IUCN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN), while three are classified as [critically endangered](7451605.md).[^cite_note-IUCN_Manidae-20] All pangolin species are currently listed under Appendix I of [CITES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CITES) which prohibits international trade, except when the product is intended for non-commercial purposes and a permit has been granted.[^cite_note-CITES-82]

China had been the main destination country for pangolins until 2018, where it was surpassed by Vietnam. In 2019, Vietnam was reported to have seized the largest volumes of pangolin scales, surpassing Nigeria that year.[^cite_note-83]

Pangolins are also hunted and eaten in Ghana and are one of the more popular types of [bushmeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushmeat), while local healers use the pangolin as a source of traditional medicine.[^cite_note-84] A 2025 study in *Nature Ecology & Evolution* found that opportunistic hunting for meat, rather than hunting for scales used in traditional medicine, is the primary driver of pangolin population declines in Nigeria.[^cite_note-85]

Though pangolins are protected by an international ban on their trade, populations have suffered from illegal trafficking due to beliefs in East Asia that their ground-up scales can stimulate [lactation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactation) or cure cancer or [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma).[^cite_note-86] In the past decade, numerous seizures of illegally trafficked pangolin and pangolin meat have taken place in Asia.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] In one such incident in April 2013, 10,000 kg (22,000 pounds) of pangolin meat were seized from a Chinese vessel that ran aground in the [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] In another case in August 2016, an Indonesian man was arrested after police raided his home and found over 650 pangolins in freezers on his property.[^cite_note-92] The same threat is reported in [Nigeria](21383.md), where the animal is on the verge of extinction due to [overexploitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overexploitation).[^cite_note-93] The overexploitation comes from hunting pangolins for game meat and the reduction of their forest habitats due to deforestation caused by [timber harvesting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging).[^cite_note-Sodeinde-2009-94] The pangolin are hunted as game meat for both medicinal purposes and food consumption.[^cite_note-Sodeinde-2009-94]

## Virology

### COVID-19 infection

See also: [Origin of SARS-CoV-2](66389098.md)

The [nucleic acid sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid_sequence) of a specific receptor-binding domain of the [spike protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_spike_protein) belonging to [coronaviruses](201983.md) taken from pangolins was found to be a 99% match with SARS coronavirus 2 ([SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2)), the virus which causes [COVID-19](63030231.md) and is responsible for the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-Cyranoski-2020-95][^cite_note-96] Researchers in [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), China, hypothesized that SARS-CoV-2 had originated in bats, and prior to infecting humans, was circulating among pangolins. The illicit Chinese trade of pangolins for use in [traditional Chinese medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine) was suggested as a [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_\(epidemiology\)) for human transmission.[^cite_note-Cyranoski-2020-95][^cite_note-Live-97] However, whole-genome comparison found that the pangolin and human [coronaviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template\:SARS-CoV-2_related_coronavirus) share only up to 92% of their RNA.[^cite_note-Nature-coronavirus-mystery-98][^cite_note-99] Ecologists worried that the early speculation about pangolins being the source may have led to mass slaughters, endangering them further, which was similar to what happened to [Asian palm civets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_palm_civet) during the [SARS](196741.md) outbreak.[^cite_note-Nature-coronavirus-mystery-98][^cite_note-100] It was later proved that the testing which suggested that pangolins were a potential host for the virus was flawed, when [genetic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_analysis) showed that the [spike protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_spike_protein) and its binding to receptors in pangolins had minimal effect from the virus, and therefore were not likely mechanisms for COVID-19 infections in humans.[^cite_note-Partin-101]

### *Pestivirus* and *Coltivirus*

In 2020, two novel RNA viruses distantly related to [pestiviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pestivirus) and [coltiviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coltivirus) have been detected in the genomes of dead *Manis javanica* and *Manis pentadactyla*.[^cite_note-Gao2020-102] To refer to both sampling site and hosts, they were named Dongyang pangolin virus (DYPV) and Lishui pangolin virus (LSPV). The DYPV pestivirus was also identified in *[Amblyomma javanense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amblyomma_javanense)* nymph [ticks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tick) from a diseased pangolin.[^cite_note-Gao2020-102]

In addition to pestiviruses and coltiviruses, genomic surveys of healthy pangolins have revealed the presence of multiple potentially zoonotic viruses, including coronaviruses, flaviviruses, and circoviruses, indicating that pangolins naturally harbor diverse viral communities without showing disease symptoms.[^cite_note-103]

## Folk medicine



Pangolins are threatened by illegal hunting and trading due to their use in folk medicine.

Pangolin scales and flesh are used as ingredients for various [traditional Chinese medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine) preparations.[^cite_note-jak-104] While no [scientific evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence-based_medicine) exists for the efficacy of those practices, and they have no logical [mechanism of action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanism_of_action),[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-Novella2012-106][^cite_note-Jin2005-107] their popularity still drives the [black market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market) for animal body parts, despite concerns about toxicity, transmission of diseases from animals to humans, and species extermination.[^cite_note-jak-104][^cite_note-Zhang2012-108] The ongoing demand for parts as ingredients continues to fuel [pangolin poaching, hunting and trading](53302120.md).[^cite_note-109]

The first record of pangolin scales occurs in *Ben Cao Jinji Zhu* ("Variorum of Shennong's Classic of Materia Medica", 500 CE), which recommends pangolin scales for protection against ant bites; burning the scales as a cure for people crying hysterically during the night.[^cite_note-PanMed-110] During the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty), a recipe for expelling evil spirits with a formulation of scales, herbs, and minerals appeared in 682, and in 752 CE the idea that pangolin scales could also stimulate milk secretion in lactating women, one of the main uses today, was recommended in the *Wai Tai Mi Yao* ("Arcane Essentials from the Imperial Library").[^cite_note-PanMed-110] In the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty), the notion of penetrating and clearing blockages was emphasized in the *Taiping sheng hui fan* ("Formulas from Benevolent Sages Compiled During the Era of Peace and Tranquility"), compiled by Wang Huaiyin in 992.[^cite_note-PanMed-110]

In the 21st century, the main uses of pangolin scales are [quackery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quackery) practices based on unproven claims the scales dissolve [blood clots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_clot), promote blood circulation, or help [lactating women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactation) secrete milk.[^cite_note-jak-104][^cite_note-PanMed-110] The supposed health effects of pangolin meat and scales claimed by [folk medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine) practitioners are based on their consumption of ants, long tongues, and protective scales.[^cite_note-jak-104] The *[Pharmacopoeia of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacopoeia_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China)* included Chinese pangolin scales as an ingredient in traditional Chinese medicine formulations.[^cite_note-PanMed-110] Pangolins were removed from the *Pharmacopoeia* starting from the first half of 2020.[^cite_note-ng_maron_2020-09-09-111] Although pangolin scales have been removed from the list of raw ingredients, the scales are still listed as a key ingredient in various medicines.[^cite_note-112]

Pangolin parts are also used for medicinal purposes in other Asian countries such as India, Nepal and Pakistan. In some parts of India and Nepal, locals believe that wearing the scales of a pangolin can help prevent pneumonia.[^cite_note-113] Pangolin scales have also been used for medicinal purposes in Malaysia, Indonesia and northern Myanmar. Indigenous people in southern [Palawan](240395.md), Philippines, have held the belief that elders could avoid prostate illnesses by wearing belts made with the scales.[^cite_note-114]

## Conservation



A coat of [armour made of gilded pangolin scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_armour) from India, presented in 1875–76 to the then [Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales), the later [Edward VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VII).

As a result of increasing threats to pangolins, mainly in the form of illegal, international trade in pangolin skin, scales, and meat, these species have received increasing conservation attention in recent years.[^cite_note-115] As of January 2020, the IUCN considered all eight species of pangolin on its [Red List of Threatened Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN_Red_List) as threatened.[^cite_note-IUCN_Manidae-20] The IUCN SSC Pangolin Specialist Group launched a global action plan to conserve pangolins, dubbed "Scaling up Pangolin Conservation", in July 2014. This action plan aims to improve all aspects of pangolin conservation with an added emphasis on combating poaching and [trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafficking) of the animal while educating communities on its importance.[^cite_note-pangolinsg-77]

Another suggested approach to fighting pangolin (and general wildlife) trafficking consists in "following the money" rather than "the animal", which aims to disrupt smugglers' profits by interrupting money flows. Financial intelligence gathering could thus become a key tool in protecting these animals, although this opportunity is often overlooked.[^cite_note-Haenlein-2018-76] In 2018, a Chinese NGO launched the Counting Pangolins movement, calling for joint efforts to save the mammals from trafficking.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] Wildlife conservation group [TRAFFIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_\(conservation_programme\)) has identified 159 smuggling routes used by pangolin traffickers and aims to shut these down.[^cite_note-119]



Pangolins (in rectangular cages) in an illegal [wildlife market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_market) in [Myanmar](19457.md)

Many attempts have been made to breed pangolins in captivity, but due to their reliance on wide-ranging habitats and very particular diets, these attempts are often unsuccessful.[^cite_note-Hua-2015-46][^cite_note-120] Pangolins have significantly decreased immune responses due to a genetic dysfunction, making them extremely fragile.[^cite_note-121] They are susceptible to diseases such as [pneumonia](52135.md) and the development of [ulcers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_ulcer) in captivity, complications that can lead to an early death.[^cite_note-Hua-2015-46] In addition, pangolins rescued from illegal trade often have a higher chance of being infected with parasites such as [intestinal worms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestinal_worms), further lessening their chance for rehabilitation and reintroduction to the wild.[^cite_note-Hua-2015-46]

The idea of farming pangolins to reduce the number being illegally trafficked is being explored with little success.[^cite_note-122] The third Saturday in February is promoted as World Pangolin Day by the conservation NPO Annamiticus.[^cite_note-123] World Pangolin Day has been noted for its effectiveness in generating awareness about pangolins.[^cite_note-124]

In 2017, [Jackie Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Chan) made a public service announcement called *[WildAid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WildAid): Jackie Chan & Pangolins (Kung Fu Pangolin)*.[^cite_note-pangolins-125]

In December 2020, a study found that it is "not too late" to establish conservation efforts for Philippine pangolins (*Manis culionensis*), a species that is only found on the island province of Palawan.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

### Taiwan

[Taiwan](25734.md) is one of the few conservation grounds for pangolins in the world after the country enacted the 1989 Wildlife Conservation Act.[^cite_note-128] The introduction of Wildlife Rehabilitation Centers in places like Luanshan ([Yanping Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanping_Township)) in [Taitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taitung_County) and [Xiulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiulin,_Hualien) townships in [Hualien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hualien_County) became important communities for protecting pangolins and their habitats and has greatly improved the survival of pangolins. These centers work with local aboriginal tribes and forest police in the National Police Agency to prevent poaching, trafficking, and smuggling of pangolins, especially to black markets in China. These centers have also helped to reveal the causes of death and injury among Taiwan's pangolin population.[^cite_note-129] Today, Taiwan has the highest population density of pangolins in the world.[^cite_note-130]
