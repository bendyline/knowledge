The **Vegas Golden Knights** are a professional [ice hockey](14790.md) team based in the [Las Vegas metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley). The Golden Knights compete in the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) as a member of the [Pacific Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Division_\(NHL\)) in the [Western Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Conference_\(NHL\)). Founded in 2017 as an [expansion team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_team), the team is the first major sports franchise to represent Las Vegas. The franchise is primarily owned by Black Knight Sports and Entertainment, a [consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consortium) led by [Bill Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Foley), with [Adrienne Maloof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrienne_Maloof) holding a minority stake. Their home games are played at [T-Mobile Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Mobile_Arena) on the [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip) in [Paradise, Nevada](124384.md).

One of the few expansion franchises to experience immediate success in North American professional sports, the Golden Knights have qualified for the [Stanley Cup playoffs](10490910.md) in eight of their first nine seasons and reached the [Stanley Cup Final](3314237.md) in their first season. Their 13 playoff wins en route to the [2018 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_Final) are the most for a team during their inaugural postseason run.[^cite_note-PostseasonWins-7] In [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_Final), the club returned to the Stanley Cup Final and won their first championship in their sixth season of existence, becoming the fastest expansion team to win the Stanley Cup in NHL history.

## History

### Background and establishment

The NHL has had a presence in Las Vegas since 1991; that year, the city hosted the [first outdoor game between two NHL teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_outdoor_NHL_game_in_Las_Vegas) – a preseason exhibition between the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) and [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) outside [Caesars Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesars_Palace). The Kings would subsequently organize "[Frozen Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frozen_Fury)" – a series of annual preseason games in Las Vegas against the [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md). The [NHL Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_Awards) ceremonies have been held in Las Vegas since 2009.[^cite_note-8] In 2009, the media speculated about a plan involving [Hollywood](418334.md) producer [Jerry Bruckheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Bruckheimer) to move the [Phoenix Coyotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Coyotes) to Nevada.[^cite_note-9]

Rumors of a Las Vegas expansion team surfaced again in August 2014, pointing to a [new indoor arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Mobile_Arena) on the Strip (built as a joint venture between [Anschutz Entertainment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschutz_Entertainment_Group), owners of the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings), and [MGM Resorts International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM_Resorts_International)) as the potential home arena,[^cite_note-10] although these rumors were denied by the league.[^cite_note-lvs-nhlexpansion14-11][^cite_note-torstar-2014rumors-12][^cite_note-theprovince-bettmantune-13] In November 2014, an unconfirmed report stated that the league had selected billionaire businessman [Bill Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Foley) and the [Maloof family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maloof_family) (former owners of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md)'s [Sacramento Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Kings), and founders of the [Palms Casino Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palms_Casino_Resort)) to lead the ownership group for a Las Vegas expansion team.[^cite_note-nyp-vegasowners-14][^cite_note-lvrv-maloofnhl-15] In December 2014, the NHL's board of governors decided to allow Foley to hold a season ticket drive to gauge interest in a Las Vegas team, though league commissioner [Gary Bettman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Bettman) also asked the media not to "make more out of this than it is".[^cite_note-si-seriousaboutlv-16][^cite_note-cp-nhllvdrive-17] The season ticket drive began in February 2015, with interested parties placing ten percent deposits for the 2016–17 season.[^cite_note-snellas-drive-18] The drive drew 5,000 deposits in its first day and a half, and reached its goal of 10,000 deposits by April 2015.[^cite_note-snellas-drive-18][^cite_note-19]

In June 2015, the league officially opened the window for prospective owners to bid on expansion teams.[^cite_note-20] By this point, Foley had secured more than 13,200 season-ticket deposits.[^cite_note-EarlyExpansion-21] Two expansion applications were submitted: Foley's application for a Las Vegas team, and a bid from [Quebecor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebecor) to revive the [Quebec Nordiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Nordiques) at a [new arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_Vid%C3%A9otron) in [Quebec City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City).[^cite_note-EarlyExpansion-21] Both Las Vegas and Quebec were invited to move into Phase II of the league expansion bid in August 2015, which involved providing additional details about the Las Vegas market to the league.[^cite_note-22] Later in the same month, both bids proceeded to Phase III, which involved a review of ownership financials.[^cite_note-23]

At the league owners' meeting on June 22, 2016, in Las Vegas, the Las Vegas expansion bid was approved by a unanimous vote, with play to begin in the [2017–18 NHL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NHL_season).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-expansion_confirmed-25] The team became the first [major professional sports franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) to be [based in Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_the_Las_Vegas_metropolitan_area), and the first NHL expansion team since [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NHL_expansion_draft).[^cite_note-26] Foley committed to pay the league's \$500 million expansion fee[^cite_note-27] and began the process of hiring the team's principal staff and determining its official identity. Foley announced that former [Washington Capitals](73125.md) general manager [George McPhee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McPhee) would be the franchise's first general manager.[^cite_note-28] [Murray Craven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Craven), who had acted as an adviser to Foley for two years, was named senior vice president on August 18,[^cite_note-29] and lauded as the team's "master builder" by *[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated)* the following January.[^cite_note-30] On November 22, the name was revealed as the Vegas Golden Knights.[^cite_note-31]

### Start of operations and the inaugural season (2017–2018)

On March 1, 2017, the team completed its expansion fee payments and filings, making it eligible to formally begin operations such as free agent acquisition, and participation in league meetings.[^cite_note-lvrj-paperworkfiled-32] Five days later, the Golden Knights made their first personnel move by signing [Reid Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reid_Duke_\(ice_hockey\)) to a three-year, entry-level contract.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

The team announced inaugural head coach [Gerard Gallant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Gallant) on April 13, 2017.[^cite_note-GallantHired-35] Over the next two months, the Golden Knights developed their [farm system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_team#Ice_hockey), announcing affiliations with the [Chicago Wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Wolves) of the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) and the [Quad City Mallards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quad_City_Mallards) of the [ECHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHL).[^cite_note-AHLWol-36][^cite_note-ECHLMal-37]

The team participated in the [2017 NHL expansion draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NHL_expansion_draft) on June 21, 2017, selecting an available player from all 30 teams in the NHL. The draft picks were announced at T-Mobile Arena during the [NHL Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_Awards) ceremony. Some notable selections included goaltender [Marc-André Fleury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc-Andr%C3%A9_Fleury) from the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) and winger [James Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Neal_\(ice_hockey\)) from the [Nashville Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Predators).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] At the [2017 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NHL_entry_draft), [Cody Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Glass) was the first player selected by the Golden Knights.[^cite_note-40] The Golden Knights played their first game on October 6 against the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars) with Neal scoring the franchise's first two goals en route to their first victory.[^cite_note-41]

During October 2017, the Golden Knights were forced to start four different goaltenders due to injuries.

The team's inaugural home game at [T-Mobile Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Mobile_Arena) was played on October 10, 2017, hosting the [Arizona Coyotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Coyotes) as the second game of a home-and-home series. In the aftermath of the [October 1 mass shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Las_Vegas_shooting), the pre-game ceremonies honored the victims of the attack, and the team issued an appeal for donations to its charitable arm.[^cite_note-42] With their 5–2 win, the Golden Knights increased their unbeaten streak to start the season to 3–0, an NHL record for an expansion team.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The Golden Knights are the first team in NHL history to start their inaugural season winning eight of their first nine games.[^cite_note-46] During their tenth game, goaltender [Oscar Dansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Dansk) was injured becoming the third Golden Knights' goaltender to be injured during the season after losing starters Fleury and the recently acquired [Malcolm Subban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Subban), forcing the team to start a fourth goaltender in 11 games with [Maxime Lagacé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxime_Lagac%C3%A9).[^cite_note-47]

Fifteen games into the season, [Vadim Shipachyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Shipachyov) became the first player to retire from the NHL as a Golden Knight when he decided to return to the [Kontinental Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontinental_Hockey_League).[^cite_note-48] In December, the Golden Knights set another NHL expansion team record of six straight wins, a record they previously missed when they lost their tenth game of the season, and established a new NHL record with eight straight wins.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] On February 1, 2018, the Golden Knights set the expansion team record for wins in a debut season with 34 wins after only 50 games,[^cite_note-52] and then on February 21, 2018, set a record for most points by an expansion team in the inaugural season with 84.[^cite_note-53] Clinching a berth for the [2018 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) on March 26, the Golden Knights became the first team since the [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers) and [Hartford Whalers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_Whalers) in the [1979–80 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979%E2%80%9380_NHL_season) to make the playoffs in their inaugural season in the league.[^cite_note-54] On March 31, the Golden Knights clinched the Pacific Division title, becoming the first true expansion team in the [four major sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) to win its division in its inaugural season (not counting all-expansion divisions, as was the case in the [1967–68 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967%E2%80%9368_NHL_season)).[^cite_note-55] Vegas' inaugural season was widely considered the most successful of any North American expansion team,[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] with much attention given to the breakout seasons of their expansion draft selections,[^cite_note-59] dubbed the "Golden Misfits."[^cite_note-60]

On April 11, the franchise won its first playoff game in a [1–0 victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(P1\)_Vegas_Golden_Knights_vs._\(WC1\)_Los_Angeles_Kings) over the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) in the first game of the series.[^cite_note-61] Six days later on April 17, the franchise earned their first playoff series win against the Kings, winning the fourth game by a 1–0 score, which also became their first playoff series sweep. The Golden Knights became the first team in NHL history to sweep their first playoff series in their inaugural season.[^cite_note-62] On May 6, 2018, the Golden Knights defeated the [San Jose Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks) four-games-to-two, becoming the third team in NHL history to win multiple playoff series in their inaugural season, and advanced to the conference finals.[^cite_note-63] On May 20, Vegas defeated the [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) to win the conference finals in five games, becoming the third NHL team to advance to the [Stanley Cup Final](3314237.md) in its inaugural season, after the [Toronto Arenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Arenas) in [1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1918_Stanley_Cup_Final) and the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Stanley_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-64] The Golden Knights were defeated in the Stanley Cup Final by the [Washington Capitals](73125.md) in five games;[^cite_note-65] despite this loss, the team won 13 postseason games that year, breaking the record for the most wins by an expansion team in their first playoff appearance.[^cite_note-PostseasonWins-7]

### Continued contention and first Stanley Cup championship (2018–present)

Fleury, forward Max Pacioretty, and defenseman Colin Miller during a game against the Washington Capitals in October 2018

Fleury, [Max Pacioretty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Pacioretty), and [Colin Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Miller_\(ice_hockey,_born_1992\)) during a game against the [Washington Capitals](73125.md) in October 2018.

Prior to the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_NHL_season), the Golden Knights acquired winger [Max Pacioretty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Pacioretty) from the [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md) and signed free-agent center [Paul Stastny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Stastny),[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] filling the roster holes left by the free-agency departures of [James Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Neal_\(ice_hockey\)) and [David Perron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Perron).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] Additionally, Vegas acquired future captain [Mark Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Stone) from the [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators) at the trade deadline,[^cite_note-70] subsequently signing him to an eight-year extension.[^cite_note-71] Despite dropping to 93 standings points from the prior season's 109, the Golden Knights returned to the playoffs as the third seed in the Pacific Division. They ultimately suffered [their first-ever first-round elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(P2\)_San_Jose_Sharks_vs._\(P3\)_Vegas_Golden_Knights), losing to the [San Jose Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks) in seven games [after leading the series 3 games to 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_teams_to_overcome_3%E2%80%931_series_deficits#Successful_comebacks_3).[^cite_note-SJSGame7-72] Game seven was particularly notable; after taking a 3–0 lead into the third period, [Cody Eakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Eakin) delivered a [cross-check](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-checking) to Sharks captain [Joe Pavelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Pavelski), resulting in a controversial 5-minute major penalty call that saw the Sharks score four goals and take a 4–3 lead.[^cite_note-73] The Sharks would eventually win the game, after a late game-tying goal by [Jonathan Marchessault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Marchessault) for Vegas followed by [Barclay Goodrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclay_Goodrow)'s series winner in overtime.[^cite_note-SJSGame7-72]

In May 2019, Vegas modified their front-office staff, with the promotions of [George McPhee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McPhee) from general manager to president of hockey operations and assistant general manager [Kelly McCrimmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_McCrimmon) to general manager.[^cite_note-McCrimmon_promotion-74] During the remainder of the off-season and [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_NHL_season), the Golden Knights' roster experienced considerable turnover. In June, original Golden Knights [Erik Haula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Haula) and [Colin Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Miller_\(ice_hockey,_born_1992\)) were traded to the [Carolina Hurricanes](70523.md) and [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md),[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] respectively, with recently signed [KHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KHL) standout [Nikita Gusev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Gusev) also sent to the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils).[^cite_note-77] The team additionally acquired center [Chandler Stephenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Stephenson) from the [Washington Capitals](73125.md) in early December 2019.[^cite_note-78] After an up-and-down start resulting in a 24–19–6 record and capped by a four-game losing streak, the team fired head coach Gallant, replacing him with recently fired former Sharks head coach [Peter DeBoer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_DeBoer) on January 15, 2020.[^cite_note-DeBoerHired-79]

During the following month in the lead-up to the trade deadline, Vegas further acquired defenseman [Alec Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Martinez) from the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings),[^cite_note-80] forward [Nick Cousins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Cousins) from Montreal,[^cite_note-VGKMTL-CHITORVGK-81] and goaltender [Robin Lehner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Lehner) from the [Chicago Blackhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks),[^cite_note-VGKMTL-CHITORVGK-81] as well as trading original Golden Knights [Cody Eakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Eakin) and [Malcolm Subban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Subban) to the [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) and Chicago.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-VGKMTL-CHITORVGK-81] Due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the NHL regular season was suspended on March 12, 2020,[^cite_note-83] and officially concluded on May 26;[^cite_note-2020Playoffs-84] Vegas, holding the 3rd-best points percentage in the Western Conference, automatically qualified for the [restructured playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), playing in a round-robin to determine the top four seeds in the Western playoff bracket.[^cite_note-2020Playoffs-84] After sweeping the round-robin round over the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars), [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues), and [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md), Vegas earned the first seed,[^cite_note-85] proceeding to defeat Chicago in the first round in five games.[^cite_note-86] In the second round, Vegas defeated the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) in seven games, despite once again having previously led the series 3–1.[^cite_note-87] Their playoff run would end in the conference finals, however, as the Golden Knights lost to Dallas in five games.[^cite_note-88]

Prior to the shortened [2020–21 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_NHL_season), [Mark Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Stone) was named the first [captain](502359.md) in franchise history.[^cite_note-StoneCaptain-89] Additionally, alternate captain and team leader [Deryk Engelland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deryk_Engelland) retired, stepping into a front-office role with the team.[^cite_note-90] The Golden Knights further overhauled the roster in the off-season, notably signing defenseman [Alex Pietrangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Pietrangelo) to a seven-year contract,[^cite_note-91] as well as trading [Paul Stastny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Stastny) to Winnipeg and original Golden Knight [Nate Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Schmidt) to Vancouver.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] The shortened schedule saw a temporary realignment where teams only played against their own division in the regular season, with Vegas being placed in a new eight-team [West Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_West_Division).[^cite_note-94] The Golden Knights ultimately finished second in both the division and league; despite being tied in points with the [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md), the Avalanche had five more regulation wins than Vegas.[^cite_note-95] In first round of [the playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), Vegas was forced to a game seven for the third consecutive year after leading 3–1, but defeated the [Minnesota Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Wild) thanks in part to a hat trick from trade-deadline acquisition [Mattias Janmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattias_Janmark).[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

In the second round, the Golden Knights defeated the Avalanche in six games despite initially going down 2–0;[^cite_note-98] however, Vegas' playoff run would again end one round short of the Stanley Cup Final, as the Golden Knights were then upset by the [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md) in six games in the Stanley Cup semifinals.[^cite_note-99] Goaltender [Marc-André Fleury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc-Andr%C3%A9_Fleury) was named the winner of the [Vezina Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vezina_Trophy) as the league's best goaltender at season's end, with he and Lehner also sharing a [William M. Jennings Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_M._Jennings_Trophy) win for allowing the fewest goals against of any team.[^cite_note-100]

The 2021 off-season began with a swap of former first-round picks, as Vegas acquired [Nolan Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolan_Patrick) from the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) as part of a three-team trade that sent [Cody Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Glass) to the [Nashville Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Predators).[^cite_note-101] Vegas also acquired forward [Brett Howden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Howden) from the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers),[^cite_note-102] later sending [Ryan Reaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reaves) to the Rangers in a separate trade.[^cite_note-103] Most controversially, Fleury was traded to the [Chicago Blackhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks) for minor-league forward [Mikael Hakkarainen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Hakkarainen) due to salary-cap constraints.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] Vegas later traded for [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators) forward [Evgenii Dadonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgenii_Dadonov),[^cite_note-106] as well as signing [Laurent Brossoit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Brossoit) to back up [Robin Lehner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Lehner).[^cite_note-107] Approximately one month after the start of the [2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NHL_season), Vegas acquired star center [Jack Eichel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Eichel) from the [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md), in exchange for [Alex Tuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Tuch), [Peyton Krebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Krebs), and two draft picks.[^cite_note-108] During the course of the season, the Golden Knights suffered a rash of injuries across the roster,[^cite_note-Eliminated-109] with a total of 478 man-games lost;[^cite_note-110] owing to this, [Ben Hutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Hutton_\(ice_hockey\)),[^cite_note-111] [Michael Amadio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Amadio),[^cite_note-112] [Adam Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Brooks_\(ice_hockey\)),[^cite_note-113] and [Derrick Pouliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Pouliot) were signed or claimed off waivers for depth,[^cite_note-114] while rookies such as [Jake Leschyshyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Leschyshyn), [Jonas Rondbjerg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Rondbjerg), and [Logan Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Thompson) received significant playing time. The trade deadline also witnessed a voided trade, as an attempt to send Dadonov to the [Anaheim Ducks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Ducks) fell through due to non-compliance with his no-trade clause.[^cite_note-115] Due in part to the injuries, as well as lackluster play, the Golden Knights ultimately missed the playoffs for [the first time in team history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Vegas_Golden_Knights_seasons),[^cite_note-Eliminated-109] finishing three points behind the Nashville Predators in the Western Conference.

#### 2022–2023: Stanley Cup champions

During the 2022 off-season, Vegas fired head coach DeBoer,[^cite_note-DeBoerFired-116] replacing him with former [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins) head coach [Bruce Cassidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Cassidy) one month later.[^cite_note-CassidyHired-117]

Goaltender Adin Hill during a game against the Seattle Kraken in October 2022.

Goaltender Laurent Brossoit during a game against the Seattle Kraken in April 2023.

[Adin Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adin_Hill) (left) and [Laurent Brossoit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Brossoit) (right) during games against the [Seattle Kraken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Kraken) in October 2022 and April 2023, respectively. The two would combine to start all 22 of Vegas' 2023 playoff games, with Brossoit starting the first eight and Hill the final 14, including the entire [2023 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_Final).

Besides the hiring of Cassidy, the Golden Knights experienced comparatively few changes during the off-season; most notably, [Mattias Janmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattias_Janmark) left in free agency, while [Max Pacioretty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Pacioretty) and [Dylan Coghlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Coghlan) were traded to the Carolina Hurricanes,[^cite_note-118] and [Evgenii Dadonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgenii_Dadonov) was traded to the [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md).[^cite_note-119] The team also signed veteran forward [Phil Kessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Kessel) to a one-year contract.[^cite_note-120] Change did occur in the goaltender's net, however; with starting goaltender [Robin Lehner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Lehner) ruled out for the season due to hip surgery,[^cite_note-121] and backup [Laurent Brossoit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Brossoit) set to miss time due to surgery of his own,[^cite_note-122] the team turned to rookie [Logan Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Thompson) as starter.[^cite_note-123] Additionally, the Golden Knights acquired [Adin Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adin_Hill) from the [San Jose Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks) to serve as Thompson's backup.[^cite_note-124]

The Golden Knights began the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NHL_season) leading the Pacific Division, losing just two games during the month of October, and holding a 17–6–1 record at the end of November.[^cite_note-125] However, the team slowed down afterwards, posting a combined 12–12–3 record through December and January, including a 1–5–2 slump in the eight games prior to the [All-Star break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NHL_All-Star_Game).[^cite_note-126] The Golden Knights recovered after the break, however; despite a variety of injuries sidelining Thompson, Hill, and a returning Brossoit for varying periods,[^cite_note-March-127] the Golden Knights went on a tear through the final three months of the season, finishing out 22–4–5.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] In addition, the team acquired forwards [Ivan Barbashev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Barbashev) and [Teddy Blueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Blueger) at the trade deadline for scoring depth,[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] as well as longtime [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) goaltender [Jonathan Quick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Quick) to address the multitude of goaltender injuries.[^cite_note-132] During this period, the Golden Knights also became the first team to win four consecutive games with four different starting goaltenders,[^cite_note-March-127] as rookie [Jiri Patera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Patera_\(ice_hockey\)) became the fifth goaltender to start for Vegas during the season.[^cite_note-133] After clinching a playoff berth on March 30, 2023,[^cite_note-134] Vegas defeated the [Seattle Kraken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Kraken) in their final regular season game on April 13, narrowly beating out the [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers) for both the Pacific Division championship and first seed in the Western Conference.[^cite_note-135]



The Golden Knights with [U.S. President](24113.md) [Joe Biden](145422.md) at the [White House](33057.md) following their Stanley Cup victory in 2023.

In [the playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Golden Knights faced off against the [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) in the first round, in a rematch of the 2018 conference finals; after a 5–1 loss in game 1,[^cite_note-136] the Golden Knights won four straight to win the series in five games.[^cite_note-137] Proceeding to the second round, Vegas defeated the division rival Oilers in six games,[^cite_note-138] despite an injury to Brossoit in game 3 necessitating Hill taking over as goaltender.[^cite_note-139] The conference finals saw a rematch of the [2020 series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#Western_Conference_Final), as the Golden Knights faced the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars) once again; avenging their prior loss, Vegas won the series and their second Western Conference championship in six games,[^cite_note-WCFGame6-140] despite Dallas forcing two additional games after Vegas led the series 3–0.[^cite_note-141] The Golden Knights ultimately faced the [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers) in the [Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-WCFGame6-140] Vegas won the Cup Final in five games, including a dominant 9–3 victory in game 5, to win their first [Stanley Cup](66968.md) championship,[^cite_note-142] as well as Las Vegas' first championship in any of the "[big 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada)" North American sports leagues.[^cite_note-143] [Jonathan Marchessault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Marchessault), who led the team in playoff goals, would win the [Conn Smythe Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conn_Smythe_Trophy) as the most valuable player during the playoffs.[^cite_note-144] Additionally, the Golden Knights fulfilled the prediction of owner [Bill Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Foley_\(businessman\)), who had previously stated the franchise's goals were "playoffs in three, cup in six," with the 2022–23 season being the franchise's sixth.[^cite_note-145] The Knights became the youngest team to win the Stanley Cup since the NHL's first major expansion in [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_NHL_expansion), beating the previous record set by the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers), who won the Cup in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Stanley_Cup_Final), their seventh season.[^cite_note-146]

The Golden Knights entered the [2023–24 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_NHL_season) retaining the majority of their Cup-winning roster,[^cite_note-147] agreeing to extensions with Barbashev,[^cite_note-148] Hill,[^cite_note-149] and [Brayden Pachal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brayden_Pachal) several weeks after the Stanley Cup Final.[^cite_note-150] However, Blueger, Brossoit, and Quick departed in free agency, while original Golden Knight [Reilly Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reilly_Smith) was traded to the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins), ending his six-year tenure with the franchise.[^cite_note-151] For the second consecutive season, Vegas started the season at the top of the Pacific Division, going 11–0–1 over their first twelve games, with no regulation losses in October;[^cite_note-152] their seven-game season-opening win streak set the record for the longest season-opening winning streak of any defending champion, surpassing the record of five previously set by the [1985–86 Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Edmonton_Oilers_season) and [1920–21 Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920%E2%80%9321_Ottawa_Senators_season).[^cite_note-153]

The team again faltered in the months afterward, though, with another rash of injuries between November and February resulting in the NHL debuts of [Lukas Cormier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukas_Cormier),[^cite_note-154] [Mason Morelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason_Morelli),[^cite_note-155] and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_NHL_entry_draft) first-round pick [Brendan Brisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Brisson).[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] January also saw the Golden Knights compete in the [2024 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NHL_Winter_Classic) on [New Year's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year's_Day), facing off against Seattle at [T-Mobile Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Mobile_Park) in their second outdoor game; Vegas ultimately lost 3–0, becoming the first team to be shut out in the Winter Classic.[^cite_note-158] Business changes also occurred mid-season, as the [Maloof family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maloof_family) sold the bulk of their shares to majority owner Foley in early January, with only [Adrienne Maloof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrienne_Maloof) maintaining a minority stake.[^cite_note-sportsbusinessjournal.com-6] After a short winning period in late January that featured Hill's return from a two-month injury,[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160] as well as Pachal's loss to the [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames) on waivers,[^cite_note-161] the team celebrated its first [1,000th-game ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NHL_players_with_1,000_games_played) on February 12, 2024, as defenseman [Alex Pietrangelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Pietrangelo) played his 1,000th NHL game.[^cite_note-162] However, the team slumped heavily after the [All-Star break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NHL_All-Star_Game), recording a 2–8–1 record between February 12 and March 7,[^cite_note-163] endangering their playoff chances.[^cite_note-164] In an attempt to bolster the roster, the Golden Knights acquired [Noah Hanifin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Hanifin),[^cite_note-165] [Tomáš Hertl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Hertl),[^cite_note-166] and [Anthony Mantha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Mantha) at the trade deadline,[^cite_note-167] later signing Hanifin to an eight-year extension to begin the following season;[^cite_note-168] this was followed by a 6–0–1 surge through late March,[^cite_note-169] culminating in the Golden Knights clinching a playoff berth on April 12.[^cite_note-170] As the second [wild card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_card_\(sports\)), Vegas again faced off against the [Central Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Division_\(NHL\)) champion Dallas Stars in a rematch of the past year's conference finals; despite winning the first two games of the series, the Golden Knights would ultimately lose the series in seven games, ending their title defense in the first round.[^cite_note-171]

In March 2026, with eight games left in the season, Cassidy was fired and replaced with [John Tortorella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tortorella).[^cite_note-TortorellaHired-172] Tortorella would go 7–0–1 to finish the season, and the Golden Knights would win their fifth division title and clinch a playoff spot.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] The Golden Knights beat the [Utah Mammoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Mammoth) and the Anaheim Ducks in the first two rounds of the [Stanley Cup playoffs](80157146.md), respectively.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176] They would then upset the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy)-winning Colorado Avalanche by sweeping them, and reached the [Stanley Cup Final](80157147.md) for the third time in franchise history.[^cite_note-177] In the Stanley Cup Final, Vegas played against the Carolina Hurricanes.[^cite_note-178] The Golden Knights win game 1 and lost game 2, heading to Vegas with the series tied 1–1.[^cite_note-179] In game 3, they would score four goals in 6\:26 in the second period, including a hat trick from [Mitch Marner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_Marner), to go up 4–0. However, they would give away the lead in the third period, sending the game into overtime, in which [Shea Theodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shea_Theodore) scored the game winner in double overtime, and the Golden Knights took a 2–1 series lead.[^cite_note-180] However, they would lose the next three games to lose the Cup Final series 4–2 to the Hurricanes.[^cite_note-181]

## Team identity

### Logos, colors, and uniforms

A four-pointed red star, with a white and gold inner border and black outer border. Underneath the star are two crossed swords with gold hilts and a black outline.

The team's secondary logo

The team's primary logo is a [barbute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbute) helmet, superimposed on a black and gold shield, with a V-shaped opening.[^cite_note-si20170109-182][^cite_note-lvs20161123-183] The secondary logo is two crossing swords behind a red star, designed to resemble the star found on the landmark [Welcome to Fabulous Las Vegas sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Fabulous_Las_Vegas_sign).[^cite_note-lvs20161123-183]

The team's primary color is [steel gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_gray), which is said to represent "strength and durability".[^cite_note-Introducing-1][^cite_note-lvs20161123-183] The other team colors are [gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_\(color\)), [red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red) (found in the Las Vegas skyline and at [Red Rock Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Rock_Canyon_National_Conservation_Area)), and [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black) (for "power and intensity").[^cite_note-Introducing-1][^cite_note-184]

The first uniforms in Golden Knights team history were unveiled publicly on June 20, 2017. Home uniforms are steel gray with black, gold and red stripes, while road uniforms are white with steel gray, gold and red stripes. Shoulders feature the alternate swords logo.[^cite_note-185] On October 2, 2020, the Golden Knights introduced a gold alternate uniform, essentially a palette swap of the road uniforms with gold and white switching places.[^cite_note-186] On February 11, 2021, the Golden Knights debuted shiny gold helmets as an alternate to their home gray helmets.[^cite_note-187] Starting with the 2022–23 season, the gold uniforms became the primary, while the gray uniforms became the alternate.[^cite_note-188]

The Golden Knights also released a special "Reverse Retro" alternate uniform. Because the Golden Knights did not have a long NHL history to draw from, their retro design was inspired in part by [Manon Rhéaume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manon_Rh%C3%A9aume), the first female NHL player, who played for the now-defunct [International Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hockey_League_\(1945%E2%80%932001\))'s [Las Vegas Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Thunder). The uniform employs a red base and features the "crossing swords" logo in front.[^cite_note-189] Their second "Reverse Retro" uniform was a faux-back design from 1995, featuring a black base and a diagonal "VEGAS" wordmark inspired by various vintage hotels in the strip. The wordmark also has a glow-in-the-dark feature when shown in a dark background.[^cite_note-190]

Two ice hockey players, a skater and a goaltender, in off-white jerseys with a gray "V" crest, gold shoulders/stripes, and gray equipment. Both are wearing off-white, gray, and gold toques. The skaters has his back to the camera, while the goaltender faces it.

The crest is a stylized gray "V" with petal accents and gold trim.[^cite_note-191] Prior to its official unveiling on November 22, 2023, the uniform was accidentally leaked to the public four days earlier during an [All Elite Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Elite_Wrestling) event, with AEW host [Renee Paquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renee_Paquette) wearing the uniform during the [Full Gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Gear_\(2023\)) pay-per-view.[^cite_note-192]

### Name

The team's name includes "Knights" as a homage to the [Black Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Black_Knights) of the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy), Foley's alma mater, and because [knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights) were, according to Foley, "the epitome of the warrior class".[^cite_note-si20170109-182][^cite_note-lvrj20161123-193] Foley had hoped to name his team the Black Knights, but dropped that plan after encountering resistance from federal officials. Foley was unable to call the team the "Vegas Knights" because the [London Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Knights) owned the "Knights" name in Canada.[^cite_note-lvrj20161123-193][^cite_note-wapo20161201-194][^cite_note-195]

"Golden" was included in the name because [gold](12240.md) is, as Foley stated, the "No. 1 precious metal", and because Nevada is the [largest gold-producing state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_mining_in_Nevada) in the country.[^cite_note-lvs20161123-183][^cite_note-lvrj20161123-193] "Las" was omitted from the team's name because, according to Foley, residents tend to refer to the city simply as "Vegas", and because a four-word name would have been too long.[^cite_note-lvs20161123-183][^cite_note-VGKTeamName-2]

The [United States Army](32087.md) opposed the team's [trademark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trademark) registration because [their exhibition parachute team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Parachute_Team) uses the same nickname;[^cite_note-196] they dropped their opposition after negotiating a [trademark coexistence agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trademark_coexistence_agreement) with the team.[^cite_note-197] An objection was also raised by the [College of Saint Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Saint_Rose) because its sports teams use the same name; the Vegas team's initial trademark application was denied as a result, but was later approved on appeal.[^cite_note-198] The team did clear the name with [Clarkson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarkson_University), which also uses the name Golden Knights.[^cite_note-199]

Beginning in the 2024–25 season, the Golden Knights' television territory was extended to Arizona following the deactivation and relocation of the [Arizona Coyotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Coyotes); selected Golden Knights games were picked up by KMCC's sister stations KASW in Phoenix and KWBA in Tucson, airing alongside the re-located [Utah Mammoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Mammoth).[^cite_note-\:1-208][^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211][^cite_note-\:2-209]

From its inaugural season through 2022–23, [AT\&T SportsNet Rocky Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_SportsNet_Rocky_Mountain) (ATTSN) was the regional television rightsholder for all Golden Knights games not broadcast exclusively by the NHL's national television partners.[^cite_note-lvrj-rootsports-212][^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214] Golden Knights games on KMCC, and formerly on ATTSN, are called by former [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins) radio announcer [Dave Goucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Goucher) on [play-by-play](1662121.md), [Shane Hnidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Hnidy), who previously worked color for the [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) on [TSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network), and Ashali Vise, who is the rinkside reporter.[^cite_note-sun-voices-215]

#### Radio

The team has a three-year radio deal with [Lotus Broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Broadcasting).[^cite_note-lvrj-knightsradio-216] Lotus airs the team's games on its [Fox Sports Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports_Radio) affiliate, [KKGK 1340/94.7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KKGK).[^cite_note-lvrj-knightsradio-216] KKGK fronts a network of nine stations across Nevada, California, Arizona, and Utah.[^cite_note-217]The FM radio broadcasts were aired on 98.9 until December 18, 2025, when KKGK switched signals with [KXPT-HD2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KXPT).[^cite_note-218] Dan D'uva does the play by play and Gary Lawless does analysis.

One game a week is also aired on [KENO 1460](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KENO_\(AM\)), a Spanish-language sports radio station, making the team one of only three in the NHL to offer Spanish-language broadcasts.[^cite_note-lvsun-spanishradio-219]

### Minor league affiliates

On May 16, 2017, the Golden Knights entered a multi-year affiliation agreement with a [minor league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_league) team, the [Chicago Wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Wolves) of the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League). Like most NHL–AHL affiliation arrangements, the Golden Knights were able to transfer players to and from the Wolves.[^cite_note-AHLWol-36] Although the Wolves were the Golden Knights affiliate, the two teams do not share the same ownership. In addition to the Wolves, the Golden Knights were affiliated with the [Quad City Mallards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quad_City_Mallards) of the [ECHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHL) for the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_ECHL_season),[^cite_note-ECHLMal-37] however, the Mallards ceased operations after the one season.[^cite_note-220]

On August 21, 2018, the Golden Knights entered a one-year affiliation agreement with the [Fort Wayne Komets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne_Komets) of the ECHL for the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_ECHL_season).[^cite_note-221]

On February 6, 2020, [Spurs Sports & Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spurs_Sports_&_Entertainment) announced the sale of the [San Antonio Rampage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Rampage) franchise to the Golden Knights organization. It was later announced on February 28, that the Rampage franchise was approved to relocate to the Henderson/Las Vegas area by the AHL. On May 28, the name of the relocated franchise was revealed to be the [Henderson Silver Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_Silver_Knights), mimicking the logo style and aura of their parent club. The shield-shaped logo features a silver-colored [Destrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destrier), with eyes that are the same color gold used in the logo for the Golden Knights.[^cite_note-222] The Silver Knights began play in the 2020–21 season at [Orleans Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orleans_Arena), while [Dollar Loan Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollar_Loan_Center) was under construction.[^cite_note-223]

On May 19, 2022, the Golden Knights announced a one-year affiliation agreement with the expansion [Savannah Ghost Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah_Ghost_Pirates) of the ECHL, through the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_ECHL_season).[^cite_note-224]

On July 16, 2024, the Golden Knights announced a multi-year affiliation agreement with the expansion [Tahoe Knight Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahoe_Knight_Monsters) of the ECHL, resulting in Nevada hosting both of the franchise's minor-league affiliates.[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226]

### Retired numbers

The number 58 was retired by the team on March 31, 2018, in honor of the 58 victims killed in the [October 2017 Las Vegas shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Las_Vegas_shooting).[^cite_note-retn58-235]
