The **North American Aviation T-28 Trojan** is a [radial-engined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial_engine) military [trainer aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainer_aircraft) manufactured by [North American Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aviation) and used by the [United States Air Force](32090.md) and [United States Navy](20518076.md) beginning in the 1950s. Besides its use as a trainer, the T-28 was successfully employed as a [counter-insurgency aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-insurgency_aircraft), primarily during the [Vietnam War](32611.md). It has continued in civilian use as an [aerobatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobatics) and [warbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warbird) performer.

## Design and development

On 24 September 1949, the **XT-28** (company designation **NA-159**) was flown for the first time, designed to replace the [T-6 Texan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-6_Texan). The T-28A arrived at the [Air Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Proving_Ground), [Eglin Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eglin_Air_Force_Base), Florida, in mid-June 1950, for suitability tests as an advanced trainer by the 3200th Fighter Test Squadron, with consideration given to its transition, instrument, and gunnery capabilities.[^cite_note-2] Found satisfactory, a contract was issued and between 1950 and 1957, in total 1,948 were built.

Following the T-28's withdrawal from U.S. military service, a number were remanufactured by [Hamilton Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Aircraft?action=edit\&redlink=1) into two versions called the **Nomair**. The first refurbished machines, designated **T-28R-1** were similar to the standard T-28s from which they were adapted, and were supplied to the [Brazilian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Navy). Later, a more ambitious conversion was undertaken as the **T-28R-2**, which transformed the two-seat tandem aircraft into a five-seat cabin monoplane for general aviation use. Other civilian conversions of ex-military T-28As were undertaken by [PacAero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PacAero?action=edit\&redlink=1) as the **Nomad Mark I** and **Nomad Mark II**[^cite_note-IEA-3]

## Operational history



[West Virginia Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_Air_National_Guard) T-28A in 1957

After becoming adopted as a primary trainer by the U.S. Air Force (USAF), the U.S Navy (USN) and U.S. Marine Corps (USMC) adopted it, as well. Although the USAF phased out the aircraft from primary pilot training by the early 1960s, continuing use only for limited training of special-operations aircrews and for primary training of select foreign military personnel, the aircraft continued to be used as a primary trainer by the USN (and by default, the USMC and U.S. Coast Guard) well into the early 1980s.

The largest single concentration of this aircraft was employed by the USN at [Naval Air Station Whiting Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Whiting_Field) in [Milton, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton,_Florida), in the training of student naval aviators. The T-28's service career in the U.S. military ended with the completion of the phase-in of the [T-34C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_T-34_Mentor) turboprop trainer. The last USN training squadron to fly the T-28 was [VT-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VT-27) "Boomers", based at [Naval Air Station Corpus Christi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Corpus_Christi), Texas, flying the last T-28 training flight in early 1984. The last T-28 in the Training Command, [BuNo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_aircraft_serials#United_States_Navy_and_Marine_Corps) 137796, departed for Naval District Washington on 14 March 1984, to be displayed permanently at [Naval Support Facility Anacostia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Facility_Anacostia), DC.[^cite_note-4]

### Vietnam War combat



[RVNAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RVNAF) T-28Cs over Vietnam

In 1963, a [Royal Lao Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Lao_Air_Force) T-28 piloted by Lieutenant Chert Saibory, a Thai national, defected to [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam). Saibory was immediately imprisoned and his aircraft was impounded. Within six months, the T-28 was refurbished and commissioned into the [North Vietnamese Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_People's_Air_Force) (NVAF) as its first fighter aircraft.[^cite_note-5] Lt. Saibory later trained NVAF pilot Nguyen Van Ba in the operation of the T-28, where Nguyen flew the T-28 in its first successful interception against a [Republic of Vietnam Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Vietnam_Air_Force) (RVNAF) [C-123 Provider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_C-123_Provider) on 15 February 1964, earning the NVAF its first aerial victory.[^cite_note-6] The NVAF unit that operated the T-28 in the respective military operation has later evolved into the modern [Vietnam Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Airlines).[^cite_note-\:1-7]

T-28s were supplied to the RVNAF in support of [ARVN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARVN) ground operations, seeing extensive service during the Vietnam War in RVNAF hands, as well as the [Secret War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_Civil_War) in [Laos](17752.md). A T-28 Trojan was the first US fixed-wing attack aircraft (nontransport type) lost in South Vietnam, during the Vietnam War. Capt. Robert L. Simpson, USAF, Detachment 2A, [1st Air Commando Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Special_Operations_Wing), and Lt. Hoa, RVNAF, were shot down by ground fire on 28 August 1962, while flying close air support. Neither crewman survived. The USAF lost 23 T-28s to all causes during the war, with the last two losses occurring in 1968.[^cite_note-8]

### Other combat uses

T-28s were used by the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) in the former [Belgian Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Congo) during the 1960s.[^cite_note-9]

The T-28B and D were the primary ground-attack aircraft of [Khmer Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Air_Force) in [Cambodia](334751.md) during the war there, largely provided from the U.S. Military Equipment Delivery Team and maintained by [Air America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_America_\(airline\)).[^cite_note-10] On the night of 21 January 1971, PAVN sappers managed to get close enough to destroy the majority at Pochentong Airbase. Replacements were quickly shipped in. On 17 March 1973, a pilot of a T-28, said to be Capt. So Petra, a common-law husband of one of the daughters of the overthrown Prince [Norodom Sihanouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norodom_Sihanouk), machine gunned and bombed the palace of [Lon Nol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lon_Nol) in an attempt to assassinate him, killing at least 20 and wounding 35, before defecting to Khmer Rouge-held lands.[^cite_note-11]

France's [Armée de l'Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arm%C3%A9e_de_l'Air) used locally remanufactured Trojans, *T-28S Fennec*, for close-air support missions in [Algeria](358.md).[^cite_note-T-28_FENNEC_History-12]

Nicaragua replaced its fleet of more than 30 former Swedish P-51s with T-28s in the early 1960s,[^cite_note-13] with more aircraft acquired in the 1970s and '80s.[^cite_note-14]

The [Philippines](23440.md) used T-28s (colloquially known as "Tora-toras") during the [1989 Philippine coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Philippine_coup_attempt). The aircraft were often deployed as [dive bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dive_bombers) by rebel forces.

### Civilian use

[AeroVironment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AeroVironment) modified and armored a T-28A to fly [weather research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_research) for [South Dakota School of Mines & Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_School_of_Mines_&_Technology) (SDSM\&T), funded by the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), and operated in this capacity from 1969 to 2005.[^cite_note-weath-15][^cite_note-nsf-16] SDSM\&T was planning to replace it with another modified, but more modern, former military aircraft, specifically a [Fairchild Republic A-10 Thunderbolt II](12502446.md).[^cite_note-17] This plan was found to carry too many risks associated with the costly modifications required, and the program was cancelled in 2018.[^cite_note-18]

[MicroProse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MicroProse) purchased a T-28B in 1988 for use in marketing their flight-simulator video games, and named it *Miss MicroProse*. USAF reserve pilot [Bill Stealey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Stealey) would fly with game journalists for this purpose, and ran a competition called "I Cheated Death with Major Bill", which selected three fans to fly with him on a "stunt-filled flight lesson". The plane was later sold to a flight school in Cincinnati.[^cite_note-MeierMemoir-19]

### Aerobatics and warbird display

Many retired T-28s were subsequently sold to private civilian operators, and due to their reasonable operating costs, they are often found flying or displayed as [warbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warbird) today.

## Specifications (T-28D)

North American T-28B Trojan 3-view drawing

North American T-28B Trojan 3-view drawing

North American T-28C Trojan 3-view drawing

*Data from* Encyclopedia of World Military Aircraft[^cite_note-WMAp333-124]

**General characteristics**

- **Crew:** 2

- **Length:** 33 ft 0 in (10.06 m)

- **Wingspan:** 40 ft 1 in (12.22 m)

- **Height:** 12 ft 8 in (3.86 m)

- **Wing area:** 268.0 sq ft (24.90 m

  2

  )

- **[Aspect ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio_\(aeronautics\)):** 6.0\:1

- **Empty weight:** 6,424 lb (2,914 kg) (equipped)

- **Max takeoff weight:** 8,500 lb (3,856 kg)

- **Powerplant:** 1 × [Wright R-1820](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_R-1820)-86 Cyclone 9-cylinder air-cooled [radial engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial_engine), 1,425 hp (1,063 kW)

**Performance**

- **Maximum speed:** 343 mph (552 km/h, 298 kn) at 10,000 ft (3,000 m)

- **Ferry range:** 1,060 mi (1,710 km, 920 nmi)

- **Service ceiling:** 35,500 ft (10,800 m)

- **Rate of climb:** 3,540 ft/min (18.0 m/s)

**Armament**\


- **Hardpoints:** 6 with a capacity of 1,200 lb (540 kg) total
