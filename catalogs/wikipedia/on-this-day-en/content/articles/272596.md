The **Spanish Socialist Workers' Party** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Partido Socialista Obrero Español* [\[paɾˈtiðo soθjaˈlista oˈβɾeɾo espaˈɲol\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/85/Es_partido-socialista-obrero-espanol_001.ogg/Es_partido-socialista-obrero-espanol_001.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/85/Es_partido-socialista-obrero-espanol_001.ogg/Es_partido-socialista-obrero-espanol_001.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, **PSOE** [\[peˈsoe\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/1e/Es_psoe_001.ogg/Es_psoe_001.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/1e/Es_psoe_001.ogg/Es_psoe_001.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> or [\[ˈsoe\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is a [social democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy)[^cite_note-nordsieck-4][^cite_note-socdem-5] [political party in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Spain). The PSOE has been in government longer than any other political party in modern democratic Spain: from 1982 to 1996 under [Felipe González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Gonz%C3%A1lez), 2004 to 2011 under [José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero](19614411.md), and since 2018 under [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md).

The PSOE was founded in 1879, making it the oldest party currently active in Spain. The PSOE played a key role during the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic), being part of the [coalition government](6036.md) from 1931 to 1933 and 1936 to 1939, when the republic was defeated in the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War). The party was then banned under the [Francoist dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain) and its members and leaders were persecuted or exiled; the ban was only lifted in 1977 in the [transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy). Historically [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism), it abandoned the ideology in 1979.[^cite_note-6] Like most mainstream Spanish political organizations since the mid–1980s, the PSOE has been considered by experts to have embraced a positive outlook towards [European integration](683704.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERuiz_JiménezEgea_de_Haro2011110-7][^cite_note-8]

The PSOE has historically had strong ties with the [General Union of Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uni%C3%B3n_General_de_Trabajadores) (UGT), a major Spanish trade union. For a couple of decades, UGT membership was a requirement for PSOE membership. Since the 1980s, the UGT has frequently criticised the economic policies of the PSOE, even calling for [general strikes](41138909.md) against the PSOE governments on 14 December 1988,[^cite_note-History-9] 28 May 1992, 27 January 1994, and 29 September 2010, jointly with the [Workers' Commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Commissions), another major trade union in Spain. The PSOE is a member of the [Party of European Socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_European_Socialists), [Progressive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance) and the [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_International).[^cite_note-History-9]

Both the trade unions and the [left](18499.md) have often criticised the economic policies of the PSOE for their [economically liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism) nature. They have denounced policies including [deregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deregulation) and the increase in [precarious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precarity) and [temporary work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_work), cuts in unemployment and retirement benefits, and the [privatisation](24661.md) of large state-owned organisations and [public services](1291782.md).[^cite_note-10] The PSOE has traditionally attracted a higher share of female voters than its rivals.[^cite_note-11] [Same-sex marriage](92656.md) and [adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_adoption) were legalised in 2005 under the [Zapatero Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_government_of_Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Zapatero), and in 2023 a transgender rights bill was passed to allow more freedom in regards to gender identity.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

The PSOE is a member of the [Party of European Socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_European_Socialists), [Progressive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance) and the [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_International).[^cite_note-History-9] The PSOE's 20 [Members of the European Parliament](258909.md) sit in the [Socialists and Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance_of_Socialists_and_Democrats) [European parliamentary group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_groups_of_the_European_Parliament).

## History

### Restoration regime (1879–1931)



[Pablo Iglesias Posse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Iglesias_Posse) addressing the workers during a 1905 demonstration in Madrid

The PSOE was founded by [Pablo Iglesias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Iglesias_Posse) on 2 May 1879 in the [Casa Labra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Labra) tavern in Tetuán Street near the [Puerta del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerta_del_Sol) at the centre of [Madrid](41188263.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVadillo200732-17][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEÁlvarez_Junco2018414–415-18] Iglesias was a typesetter who had previously come in contact with the Spanish section of the [International Workingmen's Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workingmen's_Association) and with [Paul Lafargue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Lafargue).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEÁlvarez_Junco2018414–415-18] The first program of the new political party was passed in an assembly of 40 people on 20 July of that same year. The bulk of the growth of the PSOE and its affiliated trade union, the [Unión General de Trabajadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uni%C3%B3n_General_de_Trabajadores) (UGT) was chiefly restricted to the Madrid-[Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscay)-[Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturias) triangle up until the 1910s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETuñón_de_Lara1990239-19] The obtaining of a seat at the Congress by Pablo Iglesias at the [1910 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1910_Spanish_general_election) in which the PSOE candidates presented within the broad [Republican–Socialist Conjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican%E2%80%93Socialist_Conjunction) became a development of great symbolical transcendence and gave the party more publicity at the national level.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobles_Egea2015-20]



[Julián Besteiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_Besteiro), Daniel Anguiano, Andrés Saborit and [Francisco Largo Caballero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Largo_Caballero) in the prison of Cartagena in 1918

The PSOE and the UGT took a leading role in the general strike of August 1917 in the context of the events leading to the [Spanish crisis of 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_crisis_of_1917) during the conservative government of [Eduardo Dato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Dato). The strike was crushed by the army as a result of further undermining of the constitutional order.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERomero_Salvadó201079–80-21] The members of the organizing committee ([Julián Besteiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_Besteiro), [Francisco Largo Caballero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Largo_Caballero), [Daniel Anguiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Anguiano) and Andrés Saborit) were accused of [sedition](324091.md) and sentenced to [life imprisonment](148681.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECasanovaGil_Andrés201463-22] Sent to the prison of [Cartagena](38101530.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECasanovaGil_Andrés201463-22] they were released a year later after being elected to the [Cortes](352254.md) in the [1918 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1918_Spanish_general_election). During the 1919−1921 crisis of the socialist internationals, the party experienced tensions between the members endorsing the [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_International) and the advocates for joining the [Communist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_International). Two consecutive splits of dissidents willing to join the Communist International, namely the [Spanish Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Communist_Party) in 1920[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeywood200256-23] and the [Spanish Communist Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Communist_Workers'_Party_\(1921\)) in 1921,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeywood200225-24] broke away from the PSOE and soon merged to create the [Communist Party of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Spain) (PCE). The PSOE was a member of the [Labour and Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_and_Socialist_International) between 1923 and 1940.[^cite_note-25]

After the death of Pablo Iglesias in 1925, Julián Besteiro replaced him as president of both the PSOE and the UGT. During the 1923–1930 dictatorship of [Miguel Primo de Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Primo_de_Rivera), [corporatist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporatism) PSOE and UGT elements were willing to engage in limited collaboration with the regime, against the political stance defended by other socialists such as [Indalecio Prieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indalecio_Prieto) and [Fernando de los Ríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_los_R%C3%ADos), who instead advocated a closer collaboration with [republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_Spain) forces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEgido_León201129–30-26] The last years of the dictatorship saw a divergence emerge among the corporatist which was personified by [Francisco Largo Caballero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Largo_Caballero), who began to endorse the rapport with bourgeois republicans; and Julián Besteiro, who continued to show great distrust towards them.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJuliá198344-27] Besteiro's refusal to participate in the Revolutionary Committee led to his resignation as president both of the party and the trade union in February 1931.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeywood2002117-28] He was replaced as president of the party by [Remigio Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remigio_Cabello).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeywood2002119-29]

### Second Republic and Civil War (1931–1939)



The PSOE entered the provisional government of the Second Republic in 1931 with [Indalecio Prieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indalecio_Prieto), [Fernando de los Ríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_los_R%C3%ADos) and Largo Caballero as ministers

After the proclamation of the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic) on 14 April 1931, three PSOE members were included in the cabinet of the [provisional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Government_of_the_Second_Spanish_Republic), namely [Indalecio Prieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indalecio_Prieto) (Finance), [Fernando de los Ríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_de_los_R%C3%ADos) (Education) and [Francisco Largo Caballero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Largo_Caballero) (Labour). The socialist presence remained in the rest of cabinets of the [Social-Azañist Biennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Biennium) (1931–1933).

After the [November 1933 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_Spanish_general_election) which marked a win for the centre-right forces in a climate of increasing polarization and growing unemployment, along with a desire to make amends for the mistake of not having sided with the republicans in the election against the united right, Largo Caballero adopted a [revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary) rhetoric, calling for violent revolution and a transitionary [dictatorship of the proletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship_of_the_proletariat).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPreston197894–95-30][^cite_note-31] Indalecio Prieto had also participated in the increasingly aggressive rhetoric, having already condemned the heavy-hand repression of the December 1933 largely anarchist uprising by the government, that has been cheered on by the Spanish Confederation of Autonomous Rights ([CEDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEDA)) leaders in parliament.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPreston1978101-32] The [Socialist Youth of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Youth_of_Spain) (JSE) also engaged in a shrill revolutionary rhetoric, while Besteiro firmly opposed the insurrectionary drift of the militancy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPreston1978102–105-33]



Workers arrested by [civil guards](325008.md) and [assault guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuerpo_de_Seguridad_y_Asalto) during the [1934 Asturian revolutionary strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturian_miners'_strike_of_1934)

The formation of a new cabinet in October 1934 (including CEDA ministers) was perceived among the left as a [reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-revolutionary),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGil_Pecharromán201514-34] because the CEDA party was perceived as being indistinguishable from contemporary [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) to most workers[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPreston1978100-35], while CEDA leader [Gil-Robles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Gil-Robles_y_Qui%C3%B1ones) had advocated the establishment of a corporative state in the 1933 electoral campaign.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPreston197892–93-36] The UGT called for a nationwide general strike for 5 October, which developed into a full-blown insurrection (the [Revolution of 1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_1934)) in the mining region of Asturias, and was vocally supported by socialists such as Largo Caballero and Prieto. Most PSOE and UGT leaders were jailed [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPreston1978129,_132–132-37] after the end of the revolt, whose repression was entrusted to generals [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco) and [Manuel Goded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Goded_Llopis).&#x20;



Francisco Largo Caballero chairing a meeting of the Council of Ministers during wartime

A growing rift between Prieto and Largo Caballero (with disparate views of politics, albeit sharing a general pragmatist approach) formed in 1935, while Besteiro's hold on the party diminished significantly.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPreston1978133-38] Followers of Indalecio Prieto would ultimately become "estranged from the party left".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraham1988177-39] The PSOE formed part of the broad left-wing [Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_\(Spain\)) electoral coalition that stood for election in the [1936 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Spanish_general_election), and achieved a victory in seats over the right.

In September 1936, a few months into the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) (which lasted until 1939), a cabinet presided over by Largo Caballero was formed (he also held the functions of Minister of War). In November, Largo Caballero succeeded in bringing some National Confederation of Labor ([CNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederaci%C3%B3n_Nacional_del_Trabajo)) members into his government. The left socialist *caballeristas* were revolutionary in rhetoric, although in reality they proposed moderate reformist policies while in government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraham1988177-39] The [May Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Days) of 1937 in Barcelona destabilised the government which was replaced by a new cabinet led by [Juan Negrín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Negr%C3%ADn), another socialist.

### Clandestinity and exile (1939–1974)



[Rodolfo Llopis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Llopis) led the PSOE in exile for nearly three decades

With the PSOE reduced to clandestinity during the [Francoist dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_dictatorship), its members were persecuted, with many leaders, members and supporters being imprisoned or exiled and even executed. Prime Minister Negrín fled to France in March 1939 after the final collapse of the Republican front and his fall from office.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeevor2006393-40] The aging and ill Julián Besteiro, who preferred to stay in Spain over exile, died in a Francoist prison in 1940. [Julián Zugazagoitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_Zugazagoitia), government minister in 1937–1938, was captured with others in exile in Paris by the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo), handed over to Spain, tried and found guilty of rebellion, and executed by firing squad in 1940. The party was legalised again only in 1977 during the [Spanish transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy).

Disputes between the followers of Indalecio Prieto (who had exiled to Mexico) and Juan Negrín over the political strategy of the [Republican government in exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Republican_government_in_exile) soon arose. Negrín, whose 1937–1939 spell at the government in wartime was seen negatively by large elements of both *caballerista* and *prietista* extraction, had become vilified.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoyos_Puente2016316–317-41] The party was re-organized along new lines in 1944 in the 1st Congress in Exile that took place in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) and in which [Rodolfo Llopis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Llopis) became the party's new secretary-general.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoyos_Puente2016318-42]

The PSOE congresses in exile during the post-war period were marked by strong [anti-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communism) positions as a reflection of how the exiles remembered the last events of the Civil War (which featured bitter strifes with the communists) and in line with the stance of other parties of the [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_International) during the [Cold War](325329.md), neglecting any kind of rapprochement with the [Communist Party of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Spain) (PCE).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBueno_Aguado2016334–335-43] The relative void left in Spain by the PSOE, with a Toulouse-based direction lacking in dynamism and innovation, was filled by the PCE and other new clandestine organizations such as the Agrupación Socialista Universitaria (ASU), the [Popular Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Liberation_Front_\(Spain\)) (FELIPE) or later the [Enrique Tierno Galván](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Tierno_Galv%C3%A1n)'s [Socialist Party of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Socialist_Party_\(Spain\)#History).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBueno_Aguado2016335–336-44] The Toulouse executive board became increasingly detached from the party in Spain in the 1960s an insurmountable chasm between the former and the party in the interior was already defined by 1972.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeywood1987198–199-45]

### Return to democracy

#### González leadership (1974–1996)



[Felipe González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Gonz%C3%A1lez) during a speech in 1977

The 25th party congress was held in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) in August 1972. In 1974, [Felipe González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Gonz%C3%A1lez) was elected Secretary-General at the 26th party congress in [Suresnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suresnes), replacing Llopis. González was from the reform wing of the party and his victory signaled a defeat for the historic and veteran wing of the party. The direction of the PSOE shifted from the exiles to the young people in Spain who had not fought the war.[^cite_note-History-9] Llopis led a schism to form the [Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (historic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Socialist_Workers'_Party_\(historic\)). González showed intentions to move the party away from its Marxist and socialist background, turning the PSOE into a social democratic party, similar to those of the rest of western Europe. In 1977, the PSOE became the unofficial [opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_\(parliamentary\)) leading party with 29.2% of the vote and 118 seats in the [Cortes Generales](519703.md) (which until then it had been the PCE, leading more aggressively among a larger representation of underground parties since the last free popular vote during the Civil War on Republican territory). Their standing was further boosted in 1978 when the [Popular Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Socialist_Party_\(Spain\)) agreed to merge into the PSOE.

At the 27th party congress in May 1979, González resigned because the party would not abandon its Marxist character. In September of that year, the extraordinary 28th congress was called in which González was re-elected when the party agreed to move away from Marxism. Western European social democratic parties supported González's stand and the [Social Democratic Party of Germany](60585.md) granted them money. PSOE party symbol was changed from the anvil with the book to the social democratic [fist and rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fist_and_rose) created in the French [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)), redrawn for the PSOE by [José María Cruz Novillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Cruz_Novillo). In the [1978 Spanish constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Spanish_constitutional_referendum), the PSOE supported the [Spanish constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Constitution_of_1978) which was approved. In the [1979 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Spanish_general_election), the PSOE gained 30.5% of the vote and 121 seats, remaining the main opposition party. In the [1982 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Spanish_general_election), the PSOE was victorious with 48.1% of the vote (10,127,392 total). González became [Prime Minister of Spain](203556.md) on 2 December, a position he held until May 1996.

Although the party had opposed [NATO](21133.md), most party leaders supported keeping Spain inside the organisation after reaching the government. The González administration organised a referendum on the question in 1986, calling for a favourable vote, and won. The administration was criticised for avoiding the official names of *North Atlantic Treaty Organisation* and *NATO*, using the unofficial *Atlantic Alliance* terms. A symbol of this U-turn is [Javier Solana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Solana) who campaigned against NATO but ended up years later as its Secretary General. The PSOE supported the United States in the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) (1991). PSOE won the 1986, 1989 and 1993 general elections. Under the Gonzalez administration, public expenditure on education, health, and pensions rose in total by 4.1 points of the country's GDP between 1982 and 1992.[^cite_note-46]

Economic crisis and [state terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_terrorism) ([GAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupos_Antiterroristas_de_Liberaci%C3%B3n)) against the violent separatist group [ETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETA_\(separatist_group\)) eroded the popularity of González. In the [1996 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Spanish_general_election), the PSOE lost to the conservative [People's Party](404727.md) (PP) (PP). Between 1996 and 2001, the PSOE weathered a profound structural and leadership crisis, with Gonzalez resigning in 1997. The PSOE suffered a heavy defeat in the [2000 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Spanish_general_election), with 34.7% of the popular vote. However, the PSOE remained as the ruling party in the [autonomous communities](55002.md) of [Andalusia](2736.md), [Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturias), [Castilla-La Mancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilla-La_Mancha) and [Extremadura](68204.md).

#### Zapatero and Rubalcaba leadership (2000–2014)

Main article: [Governments of Rodríguez Zapatero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governments_of_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Zapatero)

In 2000, [José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero](19614411.md) was elected as the new [Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_\(title\)#Secretary-general), reforming the party with "Nueva Vía" (New Way). Later, the PSOE won the [2003 Spanish local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Spanish_local_elections). The PSOE strongly opposed the [Iraq War](5043324.md) which was supported by the [Aznar government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_government_of_Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Aznar).



[José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero](19614411.md) during the 2010 Progressive Governance Conference

In the [2003 Catalan regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Catalan_regional_election), the PSOE's [Socialists' Party of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialists'_Party_of_Catalonia) (PSC) increased its vote total, but ended up in second place after [Convergence and Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_and_Union). After a period of negotiations, the party formed a pact with the [Initiative for Catalonia Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initiative_for_Catalonia_Greens), the [Republican Left of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Left_of_Catalonia) and the [United and Alternative Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_and_Alternative_Left), governing Catalonia until 2010.

In the [2004 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Spanish_general_election), the PSOE won with almost 43% of the votes following the [11M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Madrid_train_bombings) terrorist (11 March) attacks. It was alleged by PP that the PSOE, with the help of the national newspaper *[El País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pa%C3%ADs)*, did not observe the "reflection journey" which forbade political parties from trying to sway public opinion (forbidden by Spanish law), calling the opposing political party "assassins" and blaming the terrorist attack on them. The PSOE maintained their lead in the [2004 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_European_Parliament_election_in_Spain).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

In 2005, the PSOE called for a yes vote on the [European Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_establishing_a_Constitution_for_Europe). The PSOE also favoured the negotiations between the government and the ETA during the 2006 cease-fire which had a *de facto* end with the [Madrid–Barajas Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid%E2%80%93Barajas_Airport) [terrorist attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Madrid%E2%80%93Barajas_Airport_bombing). In the [2008 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Spanish_general_election), the PSOE won again, with Zapatero remaining prime minister. The PSOE increased their share of seats in the [Congress of Deputies](352254.md) from 164 to 169 after the latest election.



PSOE leading figures during the [38th Federal Congress of the PSOE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38th_Federal_Congress_of_the_PSOE) in which [Alfredo Pérez Rubalcaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_P%C3%A9rez_Rubalcaba) (centre) was elected as Secretary-General

After waning popularity throughout their second term, the PSOE was defeated in the [2011 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Spanish_general_election) by the conservative People's Party. Shortly after, an [extraordinary congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38th_Federal_Congress_of_the_PSOE) was held in which [Alfredo Pérez Rubalcaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_P%C3%A9rez_Rubalcaba), former Deputy to Zapatero and Minister of the Interior, was elected Secretary-General defeating [Carme Chacón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carme_Chac%C3%B3n), the other candidate, who stood for the Zapatero platform. This victory caused huge internal divisions and weakened the party's external image.

In 2013, the PSOE held a political conference which introduced a completely new platform, widely seen as a move to the left in an attempt to regain votes from parties such as the [United Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Left_\(Spain\)), whose popularity rose steadily due to the general discontent with the two-party system and spending cuts. That platform was the basis for the [2014 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_European_Parliament_election_in_Spain) manifesto, promoted as a solid alternative to the conservative plan for Europe. ThePSOE suffered another defeat, winning 14 seats. Shortly thereafter, Rubalcaba resigned as Secretary-General and an [Extraordinary Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Extraordinary_Federal_Congress_of_the_PSOE) was convoked.

#### Sánchez leadership (2014–present)

[This party congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Extraordinary_Federal_Congress_of_the_PSOE) was the first to use a [primary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisan_primary) system with three candidates, namely [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md), [Eduardo Madina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Madina) and [José Antonio Pérez Tapias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Antonio_P%C3%A9rez_Tapias). Sánchez was elected with 49% of the vote of the affiliates and therefore became Secretary-General on 27 July 2014.

In the [2015 Spanish municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Spanish_municipal_elections), the PSOE won 25% of the vote, one of its worst results since the restoration of democracy. Together with the fall of the People's Party which won 27% of votes, it meant the end of the two-party system in Spain in favor of new parties. The PSOE alone lost 943 councilors. The [2015 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Spanish_general_election) produced a [hung parliament](652214.md) broken into four major parties. The PSOE got about 20% of the vote, its worst result since democracy was restored. The parliament was so fragmented that no government could be formed and six months later new elections were held. The [2016 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Spanish_general_election) resulted in the PSOE losing a further five seats despite gaining 0.6% of the vote (still the party's second-worst popular vote total after 2015 since the restoration of democracy), leaving the party with 85 seats in the parliament, their lowest total since the restoration of democracy and the fewest since the [1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_Spanish_general_election) in [Republican Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic) left the party with 59 seats in the 473-member parliament.



[Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) (who led the party through its [crisis in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_PSOE_crisis)) singing *[The Internationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Internationale)* after winning the 2017 primary election for Secretary-General

With the exception of the [2015 Andalusian regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Andalusian_regional_election), elections held during the early leadership of Sánchez were losses for the PSOE. In addition, the policy of pacts conducted by Sánchez after the 2016 general election, based on Sánchez's outright refusal to facilitate a People's Party government, caused a faction within the party critical of Sánchez to gain momentum, led by President of [Andalusia](2736.md) [Susana Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susana_D%C3%ADaz). On 28 September 2016, the Secretary of Federal Policy Antonio Pradas went to the party's headquarters and presented the *en bloc* resignation of 17 members of the Federal Executive and the demands of those who resigned for the party to be run by an interim manager and to pressure Sánchez to resign as secretary-general. The Executive later lost two more members in the *en bloc* resignation, bringing the total number of resignations to 19. Resigning executives included the president of the party [Micaela Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micaela_Navarro), the former Minister [Carme Chacón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carme_Chac%C3%B3n), the President of [Valencia](63861.md) [Ximo Puig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ximo_Puig) and the President of [Castilla–La Mancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilla%E2%80%93La_Mancha) [Emiliano García-Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiliano_Garc%C3%ADa-Page). This launched the [2016 PSOE crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_PSOE_crisis). On the afternoon of 1 October 2016, after holding a tense Federal Committee meeting, Sánchez resigned as party General-Secretary, forcing an [extraordinary party congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Spanish_Socialist_Workers'_Party_leadership_election) to choose a new General-Secretary. That night, it was reported that an interim manager would be chosen, later confirmed to be the President of [Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturias) [Javier Fernández Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Fern%C3%A1ndez_Fern%C3%A1ndez). Sánchez announced his intention to run for General-Secretary of the party as did Susana Díaz (one of the leaders of the anti-Sánchez faction of the party) and [Patxi López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patxi_L%C3%B3pez), former President of the [Basque Autonomous Community](20514897.md). At the 39th federal congress in June 2017, Díaz received 48.3% of endorsements, outpacing both Sánchez (43.0% of endorsements) and López (8.7% of endorsements), but Sánchez won an absolute majority of the party's popular vote at 50.3% (Díaz received 39.9% and López 9.8%). Both Díaz and López withdrew before the delegate vote, returning Sánchez as the General-Secretary and ending the crisis.&#x20;

In mid-2018, the [National Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_National_Court) found that the conservative People's Party profited from the illegal kickbacks-for-contracts scheme of the [Gürtel affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCrtel_affair), confirming the existence of an illegal accounting and financing structure that ran in parallel with the party's official one since 1989 and that sentenced that the PP helped to establish "a genuine and effective system of institutional corruption through the manipulation of central, autonomous and local public procurement". The PSOE Parliamentary Group in the Congress of Deputies filed a [motion of no confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_vote_of_no_confidence_in_the_government_of_Mariano_Rajoy) against the government of Prime Minister [Mariano Rajoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Rajoy), presenting Sánchez as alternative candidate. The PSOE's motion passed with the support of [Unidos Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidos_Podemos) (UP), [Republican Left of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Left_of_Catalonia) (ERC), [Catalan European Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_European_Democratic_Party) (PDeCAT), [Basque Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Nationalist_Party) (PNV), [Coalició Compromís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalici%C3%B3_Comprom%C3%ADs), [EH Bildu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EH_Bildu) and [New Canaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Canaries) (NCa), bringing down the Rajoy government. The PP voted against the proposal, joined by [Citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_\(Spanish_political_party\)) (C's), the [Navarrese People's Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarrese_People's_Union) (UPN) and the [Asturias Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturias_Forum) (FAC). The [Canarian Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_Coalition) (CC) abstained. Following the successful motion of no confidence, Sánchez became prime minister on 2 June 2018 in a minority government. In December 2018, the PSOE's [branch in Andalusia](45257246.md) was defeated in the [2018 Andalusian regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Andalusian_regional_election) for the first time since the restoration of democracy, with a centre-right coalition of PP, C's and the resurgent [right-wing nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_nationalists) [Vox](41928488.md) taking power in the region.

For most of his first term as prime minister, Sánchez relied on support from the UP and the NC to get his agenda passed, occasionally being forced into negotiating with the Catalan separatist parties the ERC and the PDeCAT and the PNV on individual issues. In February 2019, the ERC, the PDeCAT and [En Marea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_Marea) withdrew their support of Sánchez's government by voting against and helping defeat the 2019 [General State Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_State_Budget) and Sánchez called an early election for 28 April 2019. The [April 2019 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2019_Spanish_general_election) resulted in victory for the PSOE, with the party winning 123 seats on 28.7% of the vote in the Cortes and an absolute majority of 139 in the Senate, gains of 38 and 79 seats respectively. The PSOE also finished eight percentage points ahead of the PP which finished second in both seats and in the popular vote. At election night, party supporters demanded Sánchez to reject any coalition with Cs.[^cite_note-49] On the same day as the April 2019 general election, the [2019 Valencian regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Valencian_regional_election) resulted in the [Valencian branch of the PSOE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_the_Valencian_Country) being re-elected in coalition with the [Valencianist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencianism) party [Compromís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalici%C3%B3_Comprom%C3%ADs) and UP.

On 26 May 2019, the PSOE became the largest Spanish party in the [European Parliament](9581.md) following the [2019 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_Parliament_election_in_Spain). The PSOE gained six seats to bring their total to 20 and won all but eight provinces in the country. 26 May also saw [regional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Spanish_regional_elections) for every region in the country except Valencia, Catalonia, Andalusia, the Basque Country and Galicia. In every region, the PSOE gained seats and votes from the 2015 regional elections. The PSOE finished first in terms of votes and seats in every region except for [Cantabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Cantabrian_regional_election), where the [Regionalist Party of Cantabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalist_Party_of_Cantabria) (PRC) finished first and the PSOE third behind the PP; and [Navarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Navarrese_regional_election), where the conservative regionalist [NA+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navarra_Suma) finished first and the [Socialist Party of Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Navarre) finished second. PSOE governments were re-elected in [Castilla-La Mancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Castilian-Manchegan_regional_election) and [Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Extremaduran_regional_election), with the party receiving an absolute majority of seats in both regions. The party took over the Presidency of the [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Canarian_regional_election) with the support of [New Canaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Canaries) and [Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podemos_\(Spanish_political_party\)), ending 26 years of [Canarian Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_Coalition) government. On the same date, the PSOE became the largest party in the [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_of_Spain) following the [local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Spanish_local_elections).

Following months of political deadlock, Sánchez called a second general election in seven months. In the [November 2019 Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2019_Spanish_general_election), the PSOE lost three members of parliament and 0.7% of the popular vote in the election, while the PP and VOX gained 23 and 28 seats respectively, further worsening the deadlock. As of 23 December, there was still no government in place, although members of PSOE, PSC and UP had voted to join in a coalition government, agreed to by Sánchez and UP Secretary-General [Pablo Iglesias Turrión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Iglesias_Turri%C3%B3n). On 5 January 2020, the PSOE–UP government failed its first investiture vote, with 166 votes in favor and 165 opposed with 18 abstentions and one UP parliamentarian absent, therefore the government fell short of an absolute majority. On 7 January, the investiture motion, this time requiring only a [simple majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority), passed with 167 votes in favour and 165 against. PSOE, UP, [En Comú Podem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_Com%C3%BA_Podem), [Grupo Común da Esquerda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupo_Com%C3%BAn_da_Esquerda), PNV, [Más País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1s_Pa%C3%ADs), Compromís, NCa, the [Galician Nationalist Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_Nationalist_Bloc) (BNG) and [Teruel Existe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teruel_Existe) (TE) voted in favor of the government, with PP, Vox, Cs, [Together for Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_for_Catalonia_\(2017\)) (JxCat), the [Popular Unity Candidacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Unity_Candidacy) (CUP), NA+, CC, PRC and FAC voting against while ERC and EH Bildu both abstained.

In 2021, PSOE started a [podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast) called *Donde hay partido*.[^cite_note-50]

Throughout 2025, the party was rocked by [allegations of sexual harassment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932026_Spanish_sexual_misconduct_allegations#PSOE_and_party-held_administrations) by some members—with political backlash expanding to the party's handling of the cases, seeing internal outcry being compared to the [MeToo movement](55551931.md)—that prompted the resignation or dismissal of a number of officials at the local and regional level, as well as in the [prime minister's office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Prime_Minister_\(Spain\)).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

As of 2026, two former party's organization secretaries, [José Luis Ábalos](31311432.md) and [Santos Cerdán](81252951.md), are currently the subject of a corruption probe on the [Koldo case](76268688.md).[^cite_note-54]

## Ideology

### From Marxism to social democracy



Pablo Iglesias founded the party in 1879

The PSOE was founded with the purpose of representing and defending the interests of the [proletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletariat) formed during the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) in the 19th century. In its beginnings, the PSOE's main objective was the defense of workers' rights and the achievement of the ideals of [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism), emerging from contemporary philosophy and [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist) politics, by securing [political power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_\(social_and_political\)) for the [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) and establishing a [dictatorship of the proletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship_of_the_proletariat) in order to achieve [social ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_ownership) of the [means of production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means_of_production). The ideology of the PSOE has evolved throughout the 20th century according to relevant historical events and the evolution of Spanish society.

In 1979, the party abandoned its definitive Marxist thesis at the hands of its Secretary-General [Felipe González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Gonz%C3%A1lez), not before overcoming great tensions and two party congresses, the first of which preferred to maintain Marxism. Before this situation, notable internal leaders such as [Pablo Castellano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Castellano?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Luis Gómez Llorente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_G%C3%B3mez_Llorente?action=edit\&redlink=1) founded the internal faction of [Left Socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Socialists) which included the militants who would not renounce Marxism. This allowed for the consolidation of the leftist forces in the PSOE. From this moment, the diverse events both outside and within the party led to projects that resembled those of other European [social democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy) parties and acceptance of the defence of the market economy. The [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialist) faction has been especially critical of the party's [Third Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Way) move to the centre starting in the 1980s for its [economic liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism) nature, denouncing the policies of [deregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deregulation), cuts in social benefits, and [privatisations](24661.md).

The PSOE defines itself as social democratic, [left-wing](18499.md), and [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] It is grouped with other self-styled socialists, social democrats, and labour parties in the [Party of European Socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_European_Socialists), and supports [pro-Europeanism](631596.md).[^cite_note-58] During his shift to the left in 2017, party leader [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) stood for a refoundation of social democracy, in order to transition to a [post-capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-capitalism) society and end [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism) capitalism,[^cite_note-59] as well as for the indissoluble link between social democracy and Europe.[^cite_note-60]

### Federalism

During the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic), the structure of the state was still open with in the party, with two differing views, namely a [centralist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralisation) view as well as a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism) one competing against one another.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMolina's_Jimenez2019259-61] The later years of the [Francoist dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain) saw a period in which the PSOE defended the right to "self-determination of the peoples of Spain", as a reflection of a newer ideological and a [pragmatist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatism) approach of the party.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuiroga_Fernández_de_Soto2008100-62] Ultimately, the party, while sticking to its preference for a federal system, gradually ceased to mention the notion of [self-determination](29269.md) during the [Spanish transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuiroga_Fernández_de_Soto2008101-63] Ideas in support of to the independence of the [autonomous territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) of [Catalonia](6822.md), the [Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_\(greater_region\)) and [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\)), have been adopted by some elements of the party, while the others in the PSOE are heavily critical of said notions, because, as they see it, the principle of territorial equality among the autonomous communities would be under threat if the autonomous territories became [independent](62450.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuiroga_Fernández_de_Soto2008108-64]

#### Terms

- *Baron*: unofficial term for the party's regional leaders. They can be very powerful, especially if they run an autonomous community. There have been conflicts between barons and the central directorate in the past. Some *barons* were [Pasqual Maragall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasqual_Maragall) ([Catalonia](6822.md)), who did not run for re-election in 2006; [Juan Carlos Rodríguez Ibarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Ibarra) ([Extremadura](68204.md)), who did not run for re-election in 2007; [Manuel Chaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Chaves_\(politician\)) ([Andalusia](2736.md)), who renounced Andalucia's presidency in 2009 to assume the Third Vice Presidency of the Spanish Government; and [José Montilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Montilla) ([Catalonia](6822.md)). The term *baron* is more colloquial than official, representing the great power regional leaders have in the party, but it has been falling out of use since 2016.

- *Compañero* ("companion", "comrade"): a term of address among Socialists, analogous to the English *[comrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comrade)* and the Russian *tovarisch*.

#### Factions

There have been several currents or internal factions within the PSOE based on personal or ideological affinities. Some of them have ended in splits from the PSOE. Examples of currents include the *Terceristas* (an historical faction that wished to enter the [Third International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_International)) and, more recently, *Izquierda Socialista* (Socialist Left, the left wing of the party since 1979). Some factions have brought infighting to the party, more notably:&#x20;

- The divide between *Guerristas* (followers of [Alfonso Guerra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_Guerra)), and *Renovadores* (Renewers, right wing of the party).

- More recently, the divide has been between the *Sanchistas* (followers of [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md)) and *Susanistas* (followers of [Susana Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susana_D%C3%ADaz)). The *Sanchistas* won in the [2017 primary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_PSOE_leadership_election).
