# Atomic energy

Wiktionary logo

Look up ***[atomic energy](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/atomic%20energy)*** in Wiktionary, the free dictionary.

**Atomic energy** or **energy of atoms** is energy carried by [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoms). The term originated in [1903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1903_in_science) when [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford) began to speak of the possibility of atomic energy.[^cite_note-Asimov,_Isaac_Page_125-1] [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells) popularized the phrase "splitting the atom", before discovery of the [atomic nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus).&#x20;

Atomic energy includes:

- [Nuclear binding energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_binding_energy), the energy required to split a nucleus of an atom.

- [Nuclear potential energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_potential_energy), the potential energy of the particles inside an atomic nucleus.

- [Nuclear reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reaction), a process in which nuclei or nuclear particles interact, resulting in products different from the initial ones; see also [nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission) and [nuclear fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion).

- [Radioactive decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay), the set of various processes by which unstable atomic nuclei (nuclides) emit subatomic particles.

Atomic energy is the source of [nuclear power](22153.md), which uses sustained nuclear fission to boil water. It is also the source of the explosive force of an [atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bomb).
