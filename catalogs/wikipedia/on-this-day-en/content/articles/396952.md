***The Australian***, including its Saturday edition ***The Weekend Australian***, is a six-days-a-week [broadsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadsheet)-format [newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_newspaper) published by [News Corp Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Corp_Australia) since 14 July 1964.[^cite_note-sinclair-6][^cite_note-taylor-7][^cite_note-Archer-8] It is one of two Australian daily newspapers distributed nationally. Its readership as of September 2019, of both print and [online editions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_newspaper), was 2,394,000. Its editorial line has been self-described over time as [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics).[^cite_note-CrikeyBias-4][^cite_note-The_Media_Report-9]

## Owners

*The Australian* is published by News Corp Australia,[^cite_note-Cryle-10] an asset of [News Corp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Corp), which also owns the sole daily newspapers in [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane), [Adelaide](1148.md), [Hobart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart), and [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory), and the most circulated metropolitan daily newspapers in [Sydney](27862.md) and [Melbourne](17306237.md).[^cite_note-Manning-11] News Corp's chairman and founder is [Rupert Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Murdoch).

*The Australian* integrates content from overseas newspapers owned by News Corp Australia's international owner News Corp, including *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* and *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* of London.[^cite_note-Manning-11]

## History

The first edition of *The Australian* was published by Rupert Murdoch on 15 July 1964, becoming the third [national newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper) in Australia following shipping newspaper *[Daily Commercial News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd's_List_Australia)* (1891)[^cite_note-12] and *[Australian Financial Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Financial_Review)* (1951). Unlike other original Murdoch newspapers, it is not in [tabloid format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabloid_\(newspaper_format\)).[^cite_note-Cryle-10] At its inauguration, a national paper was considered commercially unfeasible, since newspapers mostly relied on local advertising for their revenue. *The Australian* was printed in Canberra, from where plates were flown to other cities for copying.[^cite_note-OzAt45-13] From its inception, the paper struggled for profitability, and ran at a loss for several decades.[^cite_note-Cryle-10]

A Sunday edition, *The Sunday Australian*, was established in 1971[^cite_note-14] but was discontinued the following year because [printing-press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press) capacity was insufficient to print it in addition to *[The Sunday Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Telegraph_\(Sydney\))* and the *[Sunday Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Mirror_\(Sydney\))*.[^cite_note-15]

*The Australian's* first editor was [Maxwell Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_Newton), who left the newspaper within a year.[^cite_note-OzAt45-13] He was succeeded first by Walter Kommer and then by [Adrian Deamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Deamer). Under Deamer's editorship, *The Australian* encouraged female journalists and was the first mainstream daily newspaper to hire an [Aboriginal](2912594.md) reporter, [John Newfong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Newfong).[^cite_note-Cryle-10]<sup>: 174 </sup>

During the [1975 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Australian_federal_election), campaigning against the [Whitlam government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitlam_government) by owner Rupert Murdoch led to the newspaper's journalists striking over editorial direction.[^cite_note-OzAt45-13]

Editor-in-chief [Chris Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Mitchell_\(journalist\)) was appointed in 2002 and retired on 11 December 2015; he was replaced by [Paul Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Whittaker_\(newspaper_editor\)), formerly the editor-in-chief of Sydney's *[Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph_\(Sydney\))*.[^cite_note-16]

In 2010, the newspaper launched the first Australian newspaper [iPad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPad) application.[^cite_note-alfi-17]

In 2011, *The Australian* launched a [paywall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paywall) with a AU\$2.95/week charge (with a free 3-month trial) for access to its online content.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] As of October 2025, the subscription was AU\$44 every four weeks after a reduced-price 3-month trial.[^cite_note-20]

In September 2017, *The Australian* launched a Chinese language website.[^cite_note-21]

In October 2018, [Chris Dore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Dore), former editor of *The Daily Telegraph*, *[The Courier-Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Courier-Mail)* and *[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Times_\(Western_Australia\))* (Western Australia), was announced as editor-in-chief.[^cite_note-22] He resigned in November 2022 following what was reported as "lewd comments" towards a woman at an event in the United States.[^cite_note-23]

The following month, the newspaper's editorial board began investigating a senior editor after women employees filed complaints about his physical and verbal conduct at an after-work event.[^cite_note-24]

Michelle Gunn was appointed in January 2023 as editor-in-chief – the first woman to assume that position.[^cite_note-gunnnamed-2]

Since 2019, *The Australian* has published an annual study, called *[Australia's Richest 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia's_Richest_250)*, which ranks the country's wealthiest people from one to 250, in order of their net worth.[^cite_note-25]

## Coverage

The Australian is one of only two daily newspapers distributed nationally in Australia, the other being the [Australian Financial Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Financial_Review). Daily sections include national news ("The Nation"), world news ("Worldwide"), sport, and business ("Business"). Contained within each issue is a prominent opinion/editorial (op/ed) section, including regular columnists and occasional contributors. Other regular sections include technology ("Australian IT"), media (edited by [Darren Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Davidson) since 2015), features, legal affairs, aviation, defence, horse-racing ("Thoroughbreds"), the arts, health, wealth, and higher education. A *Travel + Luxury* lift-out magazine is included on Saturdays, along with "Inquirer", an in-depth analysis of major stories of the week, alongside much political commentary. Saturday lift-outs include *Culture* (named *Review* until late October 2025[^cite_note-26]) focusing on books, arts, film, and television, and *The Weekend Australian Magazine*, the only national weekly glossy insert magazine. A glossy magazine, *Wish*, is published on the first Friday of the month.

"*The Australian* has long maintained a focus on issues relating to [Aboriginal](2912594.md) disadvantage."[^cite_note-Manning-11] It also devotes attention to the [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology), [defence](67538.md) and [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) industries,[^cite_note-Manning-11] as well as the science, economics, and [politics of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_climate_change). It has also published numerous special reports into Australia's energy policy, legal affairs, and research sector.[^cite_note-27]

The *[Australian Literary Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Literary_Review)* was a monthly supplement from September 2006 to October 2011.[^cite_note-28]

The tone and nature of *The Australian'*&#x73; coverage has changed over time, but since the late 20th century under the ownership of Rupert Murdoch and with Chris Mitchell as editor-in-chief, it has taken a markedly [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Australia) direction.[^cite_note-sinclair-6][^cite_note-taylor-7][^cite_note-Archer-8]

Former editor [Paul Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kelly_\(journalist\)) stated in 1991, "*The Australian* has established itself in the marketplace as a newspaper that supports economic libertarianism".[^cite_note-29] Laurie Clancy asserted in 2004 that the newspaper "is generally conservative in tone and heavily oriented toward business; it has a range of columnists of varying political persuasions, but mostly to the right."[^cite_note-30] It has been described in *[Politico Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politico_Magazine)* as "conservative", and [Claire Lehmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claire_Lehmann) has described it as "partisan and narrow" in comparison with her intentions for her own magazine, *[Quillette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quillette)*.[^cite_note-31] Former editor-in-chief Chris Mitchell has said that the editorial and op-ed pages of the newspaper are [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right) but "claims it is down the middle in its news coverage".[^cite_note-The_Media_Report-9][^cite_note-CrikeyBias-4]

In 2007, *[Crikey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crikey)* described the newspaper as generally in support of the [Liberal Party of Australia](18453.md) and the [then-Coalition government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Howard_ministry), but has pragmatically supported Labor governments in the past as well.[^cite_note-CrikeyBias-4] In 2007, *The Australian* announced their support for [Kevin Rudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Rudd) of the [Australian Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party) in the [Federal election.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Australian_federal_election)[^cite_note-32] Along with other Australian papers owned by News Ltd, *The Australian* has been highly and repeatedly critical of the Labor Party.[^cite_note-33]

*The Australian* has presented varying views on [climate change](5042951.md), such as those of [Ian Plimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Plimer), [Tim Flannery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Flannery) and [Bjørn Lomborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rn_Lomborg).[^cite_note-34] A 2011 study of the previous seven years of articles claimed that four out of every five articles were opposed to taking action on climate change.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

In 2010, ABC's *[Media Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_Watch_\(TV_program\))* presenter [Paul Barry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Barry) accused *The Australian* of waging a campaign against the [Australian Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Greens), and the Greens' federal leader [Bob Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Brown) wrote that *The Australian* has "stepped out of the [fourth estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_estate) by seeing itself as a determinant of democracy in Australia". In response, *The Australian* opined that "Greens leader Bob Brown has accused *The Australian* of trying to wreck the alliance between the Greens and Labor. We wear Senator Brown's criticism with pride. We believe he and his Green colleagues are hypocrites; that they are bad for the nation; and that they should be destroyed at the ballot box."[^cite_note-GunningForTheGreens-37]

In late 2022, editor-in-chief [Chris Dore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Dore) resigned from *The Australian* citing health issues.[^cite_note-gunnnamed-2][^cite_note-38] After acting as editor-in-chief following the departure of Dore, Michelle Gunn become the first female editor-in-chief at the newspaper in January 2023. Gunn was replaced as editor by Kelvin Healey.[^cite_note-gunnnamed-2]

## Notable stories

### AWB kickback scandal

[Caroline Overington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Overington), a senior journalist writing for *The Australian*, reported in 2005 about the [Australian Wheat Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Wheat_Board) funneling hundreds of millions of dollars to Iraq and the government of [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) before the start of the [Iraq War](5043324.md).[^cite_note-39] This story became known as the [AWB oil-for-wheat scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWB_oil-for-wheat_scandal), and resulted in a commission of inquiry into the matter.[^cite_note-40] Overington received a [Walkley Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkley_Awards) for her coverage.[^cite_note-41]

### Stimulus Watch

In 2009, *The Australian* ran a large number of articles about the [Rudd government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudd_government_\(2007%E2%80%932010\))'s [Building the Education Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_the_Education_Revolution) policy, which uncovered purported evidence of overpricing, financial waste, and mismanagement of the building of improvements to schools such as halls, gymnasia, and libraries. On the newspaper's website, a section named "Stimulus Watch", subtitled "How your Billions Are Being Spent", contained a large collection of such articles.[^cite_note-jgam-42]

The following year, other media outlets also reported these issues and the policy turned into a political embarrassment for the government, which until then had been able to ignore *The Australian'*&#x73; reports. Along with the government's [insulation stimulus policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Efficient_Homes_Package), it contributed to criticisms, perceptions of incompetence, and general dissatisfaction with the government's performance.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

On 16 July 2010, [Julia Gillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Gillard) was reported to have admitted that the school-building program was flawed and that errors had been made because the program was designed in haste to protect jobs during the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

### AWU Affair

In 2011, [Glenn Milne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Milne) reported on the allegations against Prime Minister [Julia Gillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Gillard) concerning the [AWU affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWU_affair), including a claim regarding Gillard's living arrangements with [Australian Workers' Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Workers'_Union) official Bruce Wilson. Gillard contacted the chief executive of *The Australian*, resulting in the story being removed and an apology and retraction posted in its place.[^cite_note-Bombshell-47]

On 18 August 2012, [Hedley Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedley_Thomas) reported that Gillard had left her job as a partner with law firm [Slater & Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slater_&_Gordon) as a direct result of a secret internal investigation in 1995 into corrupt conduct on behalf of her then-boyfriend Ralph Blewett.[^cite_note-48] The story was ignored for a long time by other media outlets until after Gillard held a press conference to respond to the allegations against her.[^cite_note-49] In 2013, the [Fair Work Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Work_Commission) commenced initial inquiries into allegations of improper union financial conduct, and the government initiated a judicial inquiry into the AWU affair in December of that year as part of a royal commission into trade unions.[^cite_note-50]

### *The Teacher's Pet*

[*The Teachers Pet*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Teacher's_Pet), an investigation into the [disappearance of Lynette Dawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearance_of_Lynette_Dawson), is a [podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast) written by Hedley Thomas and Slade Gibson that ran in 2018. It was credited with generating new leads that led to the subsequent arrest of [Chris Dawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Dawson_\(rugby_league\)) for the murder of his wife,[^cite_note-51] and the setting up of police enquiry Strike Force Southwood to explore claims of sexual assaults and student-teacher relationships at several Sydney high schools brought up on the podcast.[^cite_note-52] The series has had 28 million downloads,[^cite_note-\:1-53] was the number-one Australian podcast and reached number one in the UK, Canada, and New Zealand.[^cite_note-last-54] Both Thomas and Gibson received [Gold Walkleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Walkley) for their work on the series.[^cite_note-55]

## Australian of the Year Award

In 1971, *The Australian* instituted its own "Australian of the Year award" separate and often different from the [Australian of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_of_the_Year) chosen by the government's [National Australia Day Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Australia_Day_Council). Starting in 1968, the official award had long had links to the Victorian Australia Day Council, and at the time a public perception arose that it was state-based. As a national newspaper, *The Australian* felt it was better situated to create an award that more truly represented all of Australia.[^cite_note-71] Nominees are suggested by readers, decided upon by an editorial board, and awarded in January of every year.[^cite_note-72]

In the June quarter of 2013, the average print circulation for *The Australian* on weekdays was 116,655, and 254,891 for *The Weekend Australian*. Both were down (9.8 and 10.8%, respectively) compared to the June quarter the previous year.[^cite_note-Knott2013-73]

As of March 2015, the weekday edition circulation was 104,165 and the weekend edition was 230,182, falling 6.5% and 3.3%, respectively, compared to the same period in 2014. *The Australian* had 67,561 paid digital subscribers in the same period.[^cite_note-74]

As of August 2015, according to third-party [web analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_analytics) providers [Alexa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexa_Internet) and [Similarweb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similarweb), *The Australian*'s website was the 72nd- and 223rd-most visited websites in Australia, respectively.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-similar-76] SimilarWeb rates the site as the 23rd-most visited news website in Australia, attracting almost 3 million visitors per month.[^cite_note-similar-76][^cite_note-77]

In June 2018, according to [Roy Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Morgan), *The Australian* had a readership of 292,000 for the Monday–Friday editions and 576,000 for the Saturday edition.[^cite_note-78]

In June 2019, Roy Morgan reported figures of 851,000 (June 2018: 831,000) for the print version (total, weekend, and weekday editions); digital versions 1,965,000 (June 2018: 1,965,000); total cross-platform 2,421,000 (June 2018: 2,564,000). (By way of comparison, *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald)* total figure was 4,125,000; *[The Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Age)* (Melbourne) 2,782,000, *[Herald Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_Sun)* (Melbourne) 2,729,000. The only other nationally distributed daily newspaper, the business-focused *[Australian Financial Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Financial_Review)*, had 1,587,000 cross-platform readers.)[^cite_note-rmsep2019-79]

The paper had a 4-week-[reach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reach_\(advertising\)) in June 2024 of 1.82 million in print, 3.56 million on its digital platform, and 4.06 million cross-platform.[^cite_note-newscorp2024-5]
