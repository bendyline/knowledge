The office of **Crown Steward and Bailiff of the Chiltern Hundreds** functions as a procedural device by which a [member of Parliament](3776360.md) (MP) may [resign](192296.md) from the [House of Commons](13828.md) of the United Kingdom. The House of Commons, by its own resolution, does not allow its members to resign. An MP who wishes to leave the House therefore effects their resignation by requesting and being appointed to the stewardship, which the [House of Commons Disqualification Act 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_Disqualification_Act_1975) designates as an office disqualifying its holder from membership of the House; the act designates the office of [Steward of the Manor of Northstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steward_of_the_Manor_of_Northstead) in the same way.[^cite_note-hcio-1] The [chancellor of the exchequer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_exchequer) traditionally makes the appointment. The MP wishing to resign is directly appointed to rather than offered the office, and hence becomes disqualified and vacates the Commons seat.

The use of the stewardship for this purpose began as a [legal fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_fiction): historically, appointment to an "[office of profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_profit) under [the Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown)" disqualified an individual from sitting as an MP, and several such offices were used to allow MPs to resign before the disqualification was placed on its present statutory footing.[^cite_note-hcio-1] The position was reworked in 1861 by [William Ewart Gladstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ewart_Gladstone), who was worried about the honour conferred by appointment to people such as [Edwin James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_James_\(barrister\)), who had fled to the United States while over £100,000 in debt; the standard appointment letter by the [chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer) was rewritten to omit any references to honour.

The office was first used in this way on 9 February 1850 to allow [Sir George Smyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_George_Smyth,_6th_Baronet), MP for [Colchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchester_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), to resign his seat. Appointees to the offices of Steward of the Manor of Northstead and Steward of the Chiltern Hundreds are alternated so that two MPs can resign at once (as happened on 23 January 2017 when [Tristram Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristram_Hunt) and [Jamie Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Reed) resigned). However, every new appointment revokes the previous one, so there is no difficulty in situations in which more than two resign, such as [the 1985 walkout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Irish_Agreement#Unionist_and_Loyalist_opposition) of [Ulster Unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Unionist) MPs when several separate appointments were made on a single day.[^cite_note-hcio-1] If a resigning MP wishes to contest the following by-election, as [Douglas Carswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Carswell) did in 2014, they must resign the stewardship to avoid further disqualification.[^cite_note-2]

The steward of the Chiltern Hundreds as of September 2026 is [Keir Starmer](18681491.md), formerly the MP for [Holborn and St Pancras](1344871.md) and, until July 2026, the [Prime Minister of the United Kingdom](24150.md).
