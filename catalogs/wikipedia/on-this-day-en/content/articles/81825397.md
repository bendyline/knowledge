The **76th annual [Berlin International Film Festival](18948039.md)**, usually called the **Berlinale**, took place from 12 to 22 February 2026 in [Berlin](3354.md), Germany. German filmmaker [Wim Wenders](32902.md) was named the Jury President for the main competition.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-\:5-2]

*[Yellow Letters](82175912.md)* by [İlker Çatak](76181560.md) won the festival's top prize, the [Golden Bear](21547166.md). The [Silver Bear Grand Jury Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_Grand_Jury_Prize) was awarded to *[Salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation_\(2026_film\))* by [Emin Alper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emin_Alper). While the [Silver Bear for Best Leading Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Bear_for_Best_Leading_Performance) was awarded to [Sandra Hüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_H%C3%BCller) for *[Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_\(2026_film\))*.[^cite_note-3]

The festival opened with German-Afghan drama film *[No Good Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Good_Men)* by [Shahrbanoo Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrbanoo_Sadat).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] During the opening ceremony, Malaysian actress [Michelle Yeoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Yeoh) was awarded the [Honorary Golden Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Golden_Bear).[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-\:2-7][^cite_note-8]

## Controversies

The festival was marked for a number of controversies regarding the ongoing [Gaza war](74998519.md) and [Germany](11867.md)'s government role in it.

During the press conference for the main competition jury, Wenders, in response to a question about the festival's government-based funding and its official response to the ongoing [Gaza genocide](75668427.md) in comparison to its full support to [Ukraine](70149799.md) and [Iranian democratic protests](81976180.md), stated that the jury "has to stay out of politics".[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

The statement created controversy throughout the festival,[^cite_note-11] resulting in the withdrawal of the films *The Dislocation of Amber* (1975) by Sudanese filmmaker [Hussein Shariffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Shariffe) and *Sad Song of Touha* (1972) by Egyptian filmmaker [Atteyat El Abnoudy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ateyyat_El_Abnoudy),[^cite_note-12] as well as the writer [Arundhati Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arundhati_Roy), who was scheduled to appear with a restoration of the Indian film *[In Which Annie Gives It Those Ones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Which_Annie_Gives_It_Those_Ones)* (1989). Roy said that "To hear them say that art should not be political is jaw-dropping. It is a way of shutting down a conversation about a crime against humanity even as it unfolds before us in real time—when artists, writers and film-makers should be doing everything in their power to stop it".[^cite_note-13]

On 17 February, more than 80 directors, actors and producers including [Tilda Swinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilda_Swinton), [Javier Bardem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Bardem), [Adam McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_McKay) and [Mike Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Leigh) signed an open letter criticising the festival for “censoring artists who oppose Israel’s ongoing genocide against Palestinians in Gaza and the [German state’s key role](6545438.md) in enabling it”, the letter urges the Festival to condemn the ongoing massacre of civilians in a similar position adopted to the ongoing massacre of civilians in Ukraine and Iran.[^cite_note-14]

Responding to the open letter, Berlinale's artistic director [Tricia Tuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricia_Tuttle) stated that the festival would not change its position on [Israel](9282173.md), but added that "people are realizing that maybe [staatsräson](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/staatsr%C3%A4son#Israel_in_der_bundesdeutschen_Staatsr%C3%A4son) is holding us back from having important conversations about the government that is currently in power in Israel".[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

During the closing ceremony, numerous winners and jury members from sidebars sections, who were mostly filmmakers from the [Middle East](19323.md), including [Emin Alper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emin_Alper), [Abdallah Al-Khatib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdallah_Al-Khatib), Marie-Rose Osta and [Ameer Fakher Eldin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ameer_Fakher_Eldin), took the stage to criticize the festival's lack of support to Palestinian civilians and condemn the ongoing [genocide in Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_in_Gaza).[^cite_note-\:9-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

Palestinian-Syrian filmmaker [Abdallah Al-Khatib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdallah_Al-Khatib) won the GWFF Best First Feature Award for *[Chronicles from the Siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicles_from_the_Siege)*. This film was the only Palestinian feature selected to the festival. The filmmaker, who lives in Germany, criticized the German and Israeli governments and their involvements in the Gaza War during his speech, prompting Germany's [Federal Minister for the Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_for_the_Environment,_Climate_Action,_Nature_Conservation_and_Nuclear_Safety) [Carsten Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsten_Schneider) ([SPD](60585.md)) to leave the ceremony.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

In the aftermath of the closing ceremony, controversy ensued around Germany's [Minister of State for Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Government_Commissioner_for_Culture_and_the_Media) led by [Wolfram Weimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_Weimer) ([CDU](10796483.md)), the festival's main funder. An extraordinary meeting over the future of the festival was called by the Minister cabinet, while artist director [Tricia Tuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricia_Tuttle) faced imminent risk of dismissal after posing for pictures alongside a [flag of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Palestine) held by *[Chronicles from the Siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicles_from_the_Siege)* crew. Tuttle had been hired after the [74th edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/74th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival), on a 5-year deal, following the dismissal of [Carlo Chatrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Chatrian) and Mariette Rissenbeek for similar reasons.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Shortly after, new open letters were created, this time supporting Tuttle and expressing concern with the German government censorship attempts, and were signed by industry members,[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Israeli filmmakers,[^cite_note-27] international festival directors,[^cite_note-28] and the Berlinale staff.[^cite_note-29] Following these events, [Weimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_Weimer) announced that the German government would not dismiss Tuttle, and instead planned to develop guidelines for publicly funded cultural institutions aimed at balancing artistic freedom with Germany’s responsibilities regarding [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_Germany) and [support for Israel](6545438.md).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Yeoh's refusal to comment on the U.S. government's ongoing [mass deportation program](78314324.md) was also met with further controversy.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

The refusal of the [German Embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_Germany) in [Cairo](6293.md) to grant entry visas to a delegation of Sudanese filmmakers caused a stir. Sudanese filmmaker Mohammed Alomda, his producer [Amjad Abu Alala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amjad_Abu_Alala) and writer Paula Thabet were invited to attend the [European Film Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Market), but pulled out after visa rejection over "migration risk". They had intended to present their film project *Blue Card* at the event.[^cite_note-34]
