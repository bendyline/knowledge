**[Capital punishment](5902.md)** was abolished[^cite_note-1] in **[Lebanon](17771.md)** on August 11, 2026. Lebanon was the first Arab country in the Middle East to do so.[^cite_note-2] The last execution was carried out in 2004.[^cite_note-3]

In February 2026, a bill to abolish the death penalty and replace it with life imprisonment was approved by a parliamentary committee. The bill needed to be approved by the general assembly.[^cite_note-4] In August 2026, the Lebanese Parliament abolished the death penalty.[^cite_note-5]

The methods prescribed by law were [hanging](155019.md) and [firing squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firing_squad), although hanging had been the predominant method throughout Lebanese history. Carrying out an execution required approval from the Minister of Justice, the Prime Minister and the President. [^cite_note-6]

The statute books of Lebanon carried a long list of capital crimes, including [murder](20171.md), [espionage](10501.md), [treason](31292.md), [terrorism](30636.md), collaborating with Israeli forces and if the crime is especially heinous enough, [rape](7980471.md), child [rape](7980471.md), gang-[robbery](54849.md) or gang-[assault](1466.md) involving torture, [arson](184695.md) against certain types of structures or sabotage of communications, transportation or industrial facilities causing death, aggravated [assault](1466.md) involving torture, life-eligible crimes with recidivism, importing nuclear/toxic wastes, polluting rivers or waterways with harmful substances and some military offenses (e.g. [desertion](488824.md)).[^cite_note-7]
