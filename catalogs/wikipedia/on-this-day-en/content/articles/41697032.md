**Hybe Co., Ltd.**[^cite_note-Company_established-11] ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 주식회사 하이브), commonly known as simply **Hybe** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 하이브; stylized in [all caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_caps)), is a South Korean [multinational](214491.md) conglomerate.[^cite_note-12] Founded as Big Hit Entertainment, Hybe has grown from a traditional music entertainment company into a tech-driven lifestyle platform corporation.

## History

### 2005–2021: Big Hit Entertainment Co., Ltd.

Big Hit Entertainment was founded on February 1, 2005,[^cite_note-13] and signed the vocal trio [8Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8Eight) in 2007.[^cite_note-\:0-14] In 2010, the company signed a contract with [JYP Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JYP_Entertainment) to jointly manage the boy group [2AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2AM_\(band\)).[^cite_note-\:2-15] That year, Bang Si-hyuk signed [RM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RM_\(rapper\)) as the first member of [BTS](39862325.md) and launched nationwide auditions to recruit other members for the group.[^cite_note-16] BTS made their debut under Big Hit on June 13, 2013.[^cite_note-17]

In 2012, the company signed [Lim Jeong-hee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lim_Jeong-hee),[^cite_note-\:1-18] and formed the girl group [Glam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLAM_\(band\)) as a collaboration with [Source Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_Music).[^cite_note-19] The group was active until 2014, when it was disbanded due to legal actions against one of its members Kim Da-hee. Kim was sentenced to prison after being found guilty of blackmailing actor [Lee Byung-hun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Byung-hun).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

Following the end of the joint contract between Big Hit and JYP in April 2014, three members of 2AM returned to JYP. [Lee Chang-min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Chang-min_\(singer\)) remained with Big Hit to continue with his solo career and as part of the duo [Homme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homme_\(duo\)).[^cite_note-\:2-15] 8Eight disbanded later that year, after Baek Chan and Joo Hee's contracts with Big Hit ended.[^cite_note-\:0-14]

In May 2015, Lim parted ways with the agency, following the expiration of her three-year contract,[^cite_note-\:1-18] and [Signal Entertainment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Entertainment_Group), a [KOSDAQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOSDAQ)-listed company specializing in artist management and television production, acquired stakes in Big Hit through a [₩](298884.md)6 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)5.24 million) [convertible bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convertible_bond).[^cite_note-edaily-23] In early 2016, Big Hit ended their stake relationship with Signal and made a full settlement of the bonds.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

In February 2018, Homme disbanded after member Changmin's contract ended. He left the company to start his own agency, while [Lee Hyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hyun) continued on as a solo artist.[^cite_note-Lee_Hyun_now_solo-26] In August, Big Hit and [CJ ENM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJ_ENM_Entertainment_Division) released information stating plans to create a joint entertainment agency, with ownership split 52% to the latter and 48% to the former.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Filed under the name Belift Lab, the agency debuted its first boy group [Enhypen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhypen) in November 2020.[^cite_note-29] In October, BTS renewed and extended their contract with the agency for seven more years.[^cite_note-30] Big Hit was voted Best Investment Company of the Year at the 2018 Korea VC Awards in December.[^cite_note-31]

Big Hit debuted its second male group, [Tomorrow X Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TXT_\(band\)) (TXT), in March 2019.[^cite_note-32] Also that month, former [CBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_business_officer) Lenzo Yoon was appointed co-CEO alongside Bang.[^cite_note-33] Yoon would focus on the business components of the company, while Bang's focus would be on creative production.[^cite_note-34] In July 2019, the company acquired [Source Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_Music),[^cite_note-35] and in August, the video game firm Superb.[^cite_note-36] Thanks to the [Weverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weverse) and Weverse Shop[^cite_note-38] apps developed by its subsidiary beNX, Big Hit was named the fourth most innovative company of 2020 worldwide by *[Fast Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Company)*.[^cite_note-39]

In May 2020, Big Hit became the majority shareholder of [Pledis Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pledis_Entertainment). The label would retain its independence; however, its artists would now receive greater financial support and global promotions.[^cite_note-40] The [Fair Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Trade_Commission_\(South_Korea\)) (KFTC) approved the acquisition in October.[^cite_note-41] Big Hit acquired KOZ Entertainment, a record label founded by rapper [Zico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zico_\(rapper\)), the following month.[^cite_note-Benjamin-42]

In January 2021, news media reported that Big Hit and beNX had invested a combined [₩](298884.md)70 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)61.19 million) in [YG Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YG_Plus), acquiring a 17.9% stake in the company in a merchandising and distribution deal that would see YG Plus' artists join the Weverse social media and shop platforms.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] [Naver Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naver_Corporation) later invested [₩](298884.md)354.8 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)310.15 million) in beNX, acquiring 49% of the subsidiary. In return, it transferred its [V Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_Live) video streaming service to the latter for the development of a new integrated fan community platform. BeNX was eventually renamed Weverse Company.[^cite_note-KJAD_Naver_benx-46] The merger was approved by the KFTC in May.[^cite_note-47]

On February 17, Big Hit and [Universal Music Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Music_Group) (UMG) announced a strategic partnership between the two companies to collaborate on various music and technology-related endeavors.[^cite_note-48] At the forefront of the partnership was a joint venture between Big Hit and [Geffen Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interscope_Geffen_A\&M_Records), a UMG flagship label, that will debut a global boy group through a new, [Los Angeles](18110.md)-based label following a global audition program set to air in 2022. Big Hit would be responsible for selecting and training the artists, while UMG would be in charge of music production, global distribution, and marketing.[^cite_note-49] Additionally, other UMG artists were set to join Weverse, already home to [Gracie Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracie_Abrams), [New Hope Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope_Club), and Alexander 23.[^cite_note-50] On February 25, Big Hit announced a [₩](298884.md)4 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)3.5 million) investment in a Korea-based AI company, Supertone, that specializes in creating hyper-realistic voices using technology.[^cite_note-51]

### 2021–present: Hybe, expansions, and investigations

Big Hit announced its rebranding into an entertainment lifestyle platform company under the name Hybe in March 2021.[^cite_note-52] The company underwent a complete organizational restructuring, which also saw the recording and management arm of Big Hit Entertainment spun off into an independent label named [BigHit Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BigHit_Music) under Hybe's new Labels division. The renaming was approved at a shareholders meeting on March 30.[^cite_note-hybe_presentation-53][^cite_note-54] The company relocated to its new headquarters in the Yongsan Trade Center in [Yongsan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongsan_District) on March 22,[^cite_note-hybe_presentation-53][^cite_note-yongsan-4] and the rebrand went into effect on March 31.[^cite_note-55] Hybe acquired [Scooter Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scooter_Braun)'s Ithaca Holdings and all its properties, including SB Projects[^cite_note-56] and [Big Machine Label Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Machine_Records#Imprints), through its subsidiary Hybe America[^cite_note-Hybe_America-57] in April.[^cite_note-Variety_Ithaca-58][^cite_note-59] The corporation invested [₩](298884.md)1.07 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)935.36 million) into the America branch to finance the buyout;[^cite_note-60] \$1.05 billion was paid to Ithaca shareholders and bondholders.[^cite_note-61] BH Odyssey Merger Sub, a newly created subsidiary of Hybe America, facilitated the acquisition, and was later dissolved after the deal's finalization.[^cite_note-KJAD_Ithaca-62][^cite_note-63] The Carlyle Group, a minority shareholder and original investor, sold its stake in Ithaca as part of the deal. Braun joined Hybe's board, while [Scott Borchetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Borchetta) retained his position as CEO of Big Machine Label Group.[^cite_note-Variety_Ithaca-58][^cite_note-64] That same month, *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* named Hybe one of the "100 Most Influential Companies of 2021", under the "Pioneers" section.[^cite_note-65]

Japanese streaming platform [Showroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showroom_\(streaming_service\)) announced a business and capital partnership with Hybe in May 2021 to improve its domestic services and expand access to Japanese content in South Korea, the United States, and globally.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] Hybe was added to the [MSCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSCI) Korea index on May 12, and the index updated on May 28.[^cite_note-68] Following a second organizational restructuring, Bang resigned as CEO on July 1 to return his focus to music production. He was replaced by Jiwon Park but retained his position as chairman of the board of directors.[^cite_note-Bang_resigns_CEO-69][^cite_note-BSH_resign_MBW-70] In November, Hybe entered into an equity-based partnership with Korean [fintech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_technology) company Dunamu to develop a joint venture [NFT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-fungible_token) business for the creation of digital assets.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-Pulse-72] It acquired a 2.6% stake in the company for [₩](298884.md)500 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)437.08 million), while Dunamu received a 5.6% stake in Hybe for [₩](298884.md)700 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)611.91 million).[^cite_note-Pulse-72][^cite_note-73] On November 11, Hybe won a [Red Dot Design Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Dot_Design_Award) in the Brands & Communication Design category at the 2021 Red Dot Design Awards for its new [corporate identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_identity) (CI) design.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] The establishment of a new music label, ADOR,[^cite_note-77] was announced on November 12. [Chief brand officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_brand_officer) (CBO) [Min Hee-jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Hee-jin) was appointed as its president and tasked with overseeing the management of the label's first girl group,[^cite_note-78] later announced as [NewJeans](71357738.md), which debuted in July 2022.[^cite_note-79]

In January 2022, Hybe published a series of "original stories" featuring boy groups BTS, Enhypen, and Tomorrow X Together in collaboration with [Webtoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webtoon_\(publisher\)) and [Wattpad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wattpad) as part of a planned IP expansion.[^cite_note-80] The BTS comic alone earned over 15 million views within two days of release, becoming the fastest title in Webtoon's history to reach that milestone.[^cite_note-81] In March, *Fast Company* named Hybe the 11th most innovative company of the year globally on its annual top-50 list[^cite_note-82]—ranking first in the Media category[^cite_note-83]—while *Time* included Hybe on its 100 Most Influential Companies list for a second consecutive year.[^cite_note-84] The company topped the "Leaders" category and was dubbed "The Pop Powerhouse" by the outlet for its acquisitions, expansion into the digital frontier, and transformative strides in the music industry during the pandemic era.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] The following month, Hybe won an [iF Product Design Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IF_Product_Design_Award) for "Company Branding" in the Communication category at the [International Forum Design Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Forum_Design) held in Germany.[^cite_note-87] Hybe signed a multi-year deal with [The Walt Disney Company Asia Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_assets_owned_by_The_Walt_Disney_Company#The_Walt_Disney_Company_Asia_Pacific) in July 2022 to provide content showcasing the Korean music and entertainment industries to a global market. Five programs will be available worldwide on [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+) and other Disney streaming platforms—a [4K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4K_resolution) cinematic film of BTS' [Permission to Dance on Stage concert in Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permission_to_Dance_on_Stage#Film); *[In the Soop: Friendcation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Soop:_Friendcation)*; and *BTS Monuments: Beyond the Star*, a [docuseries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_documentary) of BTS' history from debut to present set to premiere in 2023. Additional content featuring other Hybe artists will also be released.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

Hybe became the largest shareholder of [SM Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_Entertainment) in February 2023, after it acquired founder [Lee Soo-man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Soo-man)'s 14.8% stake in the company for approximately ₩422.8 billion.[^cite_note-90] The company subsequently acquired [Galaxia SM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxia_SM)'s 1% stake on March 3.[^cite_note-91] On March 12, Hybe announced that they no longer planned to own the majority stakes of SM Entertainment, said that the bidding war with [Kakao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakao) could "damage shareholder value."[^cite_note-92] On March 24, Hybe announced that it will sell its entire stake in SM Entertainment to Kakao by accepting its tender offer launched by Kakao.[^cite_note-93] However, on March 28, Hybe instead sold half of its stakes in SM to Kakao, leaving it only for 8.81%.[^cite_note-94] On May 27, 2025, Hybe announced it would sell all of its shares in SM Entertainment to [Tencent Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent_Music).[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

In May 2023, HYBE entered into a music distribution and strategic cooperation agreement with China's [Tencent Music Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent_Music_Entertainment). Under the agreement, music by HYBE artists became available on Tencent Music's streaming platforms, including [QQ Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QQ_Music), KuGou Music, Kuwo Music and WeSing. The partnership covered music copyright and artist promotion and expanded HYBE's distribution presence in China, a market that had been difficult for South Korean entertainment companies to access following tensions between South Korea and China.[^cite_note-97]

In August 2023, [HYBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HYBE) agreed to acquire [CJ ENM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJ_ENM)'s 51.5% stake in [Belift Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belift_Lab) for [₩](298884.md)150 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)131.12 million), making Belift Lab a wholly owned subsidiary of HYBE. The transaction was completed on 1 September 2023.[^cite_note-hybefully-98][^cite_note-enhypenbacker-99]

In November 2023, HYBE strengthened its global expansion strategy by acquiring Exile Music, the Latin music label affiliated with Spanish-language entertainment company Exile Content. The deal marked HYBE’s entry into the Latin music market and led to the creation of HYBE Latin America, a Mexico-based division designed to develop artists and content for the region.[^cite_note-100]

In August 2024, ADOR, HYBE's label for [NewJeans](71357738.md), replaced its chief executive officer [Min Hee-jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Hee-jin) with then-CHRO and board member Kim Ju-young]] amid an ongoing dispute between Min and HYBE. Min remained on ADOR's board and was initially announced as continuing to produce music for NewJeans, while ADOR separated its production and management functions. HYBE, which held 80% of ADOR, had previously gained control of the label's board by appointing three directors at a May 2024 shareholders' meeting. Min disputed her removal as a unilateral dismissal and argued that it violated her shareholders' agreement with HYBE.[^cite_note-101]

In December 2024, South Korea's Financial Supervisory Service began investigating HYBE chairman [Bang Si-hyuk](54718511.md) over shareholder agreements made before the company's 2020 [initial public offering](63879.md). According to reports, the agreements with three private equity funds allowed the funds to sell shares following the IPO while entitling Bang to 30% of their profits. HYBE stated that the agreements had been provided to the IPO underwriters and denied wrongdoing.[^cite_note-102] In May 2025, the Financial Supervisory Service reportedly decided to refer Bang Si-hyuk to prosecutors over allegations that he had misled early HYBE shareholders before the company's 2020 IPO. The allegations concerned statements made in 2019 about the company's plans to go public and Bang's subsequent receipt of a share of profits from private equity investors. HYBE maintained that the transactions were conducted lawfully and with legal oversight.[^cite_note-103] On 29 May 2025, prosecutors investigated a HYBE employee over suspected insider trading and searched the company's headquarters. The investigation concerned allegations that the employee had used non-public information relating to HYBE's business before trading shares.[^cite_note-104]

On May 28, 2025, HYBE America sold Big Machine Rock, a Nashville-based rock label and imprint of [Big Machine Label Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Machine_Label_Group), to Gebbia Media, a subsidiary of Siebert Financial. [Scott Borchetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Borchetta), founder of Big Machine Label Group, continued to oversee the label's operations, while general manager Heather Luke-Husong remained in her position. Artists signed to Big Machine Rock remained with the label following the transaction. The sale followed HYBE's acquisition of Big Machine Label Group through its 2021 purchase of [Ithaca Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca_Holdings) for approximately US\$1.05 billion.[^cite_note-105] followed the next day by the announcement of the establishment of HYBE China, a Beijing-based subsidiary launched on April 2 as the company's fourth overseas branch after Japan, the United States and Latin America. The subsidiary was established to support HYBE artists' activities and expansion in China. HYBE stated that it did not initially plan to launch a China-based idol group or audition programme, instead focusing on its existing artists.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]

On July 1, Hybe announced plans to expand its operations into India,[^cite_note-109] with the subsidiary establish on September 23.[^cite_note-110] In the same month, one of Hybe's largest shareholders, [Netmarble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netmarble) considered issuing approximately [₩](298884.md)2.5 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)2.19 billion) in exchangeable bonds (EB), using part of its stake in Hybe as collateral to secure liquidity for repaying loans incurred during its acquisition of the U.S. social casino game company SpinX. While a formal underwriting agreement had not yet been signed, NH Investment & Securities emerged as the likely arranger, and NH Hedge Asset Management was reportedly forming a project fund with institutional investors to support the deal. Netmarble has previously utilized its Hybe holdings for financing, including a 2024 price return swap involving 2.6% equity and a 2023 block trade of approximately 6% of its shares.[^cite_note-111] However, by August, Netmarble abandoned its plan, a decision attributed to the volatility and sharp decline in Hybe's stock price caused by an "owner risk" scandal involving Hybe's founder and Chairman Bang Si‑hyuk. The funding was intended to pay down debt from Netmarble's acquisition of SpinX ([₩](298884.md)1.6 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)1.4 billion) in debt), which is now reduced to mid‑[₩](298884.md)200 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)174.83 million).[^cite_note-112]

On August 20, Mirae Asset Securities was preparing to sell the remaining portion of Hybe convertible bonds (CBs) worth approximately 1 trillion KRW, capitalizing on the recent surge in Hybe's stock price, which had risen to around 280,000 KRW—well above the CB conversion price of 218,000 KRW. The CBs, issued in October 2024 as zero-coupon, zero-interest bonds, were initially underwritten mainly by Mirae Asset, but some remained unsold due to concerns over the deal's structure and perceived favoritism. With Hybe's stock boosted by positive market sentiment, including anticipation of BTS's return and the easing of Chinese content restrictions, Mirae Asset sees a lucrative opportunity to offload the remaining CBs at a premium, maximizing returns through both underwriting fees and capital gains.[^cite_note-113] The same day, Hybe subsidiary ADOR appointed Lee Do-kyung as its new president. This leadership change follows the dismissal of former president Min Hee-jin in 2024 and a period of interim leadership under Kim Joo-young, who focused on stabilizing the company and restructuring operations. With that process complete, Hybe determined that Lee—who has been at Hybe since 2019 and has experience in strategic planning and IP projects—was the right person to lead ADOR into its next phase.[^cite_note-114]

In December 2025, [HYBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HYBE) announced a global management partnership with Brandon Hixon and Colin Gayle, the managers of South African singer [Tyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyla), to develop African artists for international markets. Under the partnership, Hixon and Gayle would lead the artist-development initiative, while HYBE would provide support in areas including touring, marketing, digital strategy, multimedia production and brand partnerships. Tyla became the first artist supported under the initiative while remaining managed by Hixon and Gayle.[^cite_note-115]

In May 2026, Hybe unveiled a new corporate identity and updated mission statement, introducing "Discover a New Universe, Unlock an Immersive Journey" as its official mission, while retaining its previous music-focused principle, "We believe in Music", for internal use. The new branding emphasizes immersive fan experiences across content and services, reflecting a shift toward leveraging technology in its entertainment offerings. The company also adjusted its logo usage, allowing the wordmark and its derived "H" symbol to be used independently.[^cite_note-116] On May 15, Hybe announced that former CHRO, Kim Joo-young, left the company after a recent filing revealed her shares, along with former CEO of Hybe America, Scooter Braun, and former CEO of Hybe Latin America, Kah Jong-hyun, dropped to zero,[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-ShareDump-118] with Braun's own departure later revealed on July 1st.[^cite_note-119]

In June, HYBE announced a strategic partnership with Japanese music production company INCS together to launch Girls Archives, a five-member Japanese-Korean girl group combining real-world performers with virtual characters and a transmedia storyline. HYBE's Next Entertainment Business (NEB) division provided support for the group's concept and story development, while INCS toenter served as the project's lead production company.[^cite_note-120]

On July 23, HYBE disclosed that its subsidiary Supertone had been approved for dissolution following a resolution adopted at an extraordinary shareholders' meeting on July 15th.[^cite_note-121] Although the corporate entity is being dissolved, HYBE announced that parts of Supertone's business would continue under a new operating company. Its AI voice services Shift, Clear, and Air were to be transferred to a new company and continue without interruption, with existing licences and purchased voices remaining valid. Meanwhile, Play, the Supertone API, and Voice Builder were discontinued.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

## Company value and investments

Big Hit Entertainment initially operated as a private company with Bang Si-hyuk as the largest shareholder of its stock.[^cite_note-\:3-125] In 2018, the Korean mobile gaming company [Netmarble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netmarble_Games) acquired the second largest percentage of the company, paying a reported [₩](298884.md)201.4 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)176.06 million) for 25.71%.[^cite_note-126] In October 2018, private investment firm [STIC Investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STIC_Investments) received an undisclosed share of Big Hit for an investment of roughly [₩](298884.md)104 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)90.91 million).[^cite_note-127]

In 2007, Big Hit had four employees and was near bankruptcy, but Bang was able to keep the company afloat after the local success of [8Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8Eight)'s *Without a Heart* in 2009.[^cite_note-128] Over the years, its value rose in tandem with the increasing global popularity of its first boy group BTS.[^cite_note-129] In March 2018, Big Hit's earnings were publicized for the first time. The company reported revenue of [₩](298884.md)92.4 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)80.77 million) and operating profit of [₩](298884.md)32.5 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)28.41 million) for 2017.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-theinvestor.co.kr-131] Initial estimates valued Big Hit at up to [₩](298884.md)700 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)611.91 million) if entered into an [IPO](63879.md) that very month, making CEO Bang the richest in the South Korean entertainment business due to his large shareholding position, and leaving him with personal ownership value of roughly [₩](298884.md)350 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)305.96 million).[^cite_note-132] By October 2018, the company was valued at more than [₩](298884.md)1 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)874.16 million).[^cite_note-theinvestor.co.kr-131]

Big Hit's success has been attributed to its innovative management style, more like an IT company than an entertainment company, which has become the new standard in the K-pop industry; it includes a wide usage of [social media](5897742.md) to capture people's interest and transform it into sales, the creation of artist-related multimedia content, and the full use of [fandom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fandom) energy.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] Financials published in March 2019 for 2018, showed a 132% increase in sales compared to 2017, with Big Hit making approximately [₩](298884.md)214.2 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)187.25 million). Operating profit rose 97% to [₩](298884.md)64.1 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)56.03 million), while net profit rose 105% to [₩](298884.md)50.2 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)43.88 million).[^cite_note-135] By June 2019, the company was valued between [₩](298884.md)1.28 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)1.12 billion) and [₩](298884.md)2.22 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)1.94 billion)[^cite_note-136] As of March 2020, its total value was roughly [₩](298884.md)6 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)5.24 billion).[^cite_note-137] Big Hit commenced plans to go public on May 21, and applied for a pre-IPO consultation with the [Korea Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Exchange). This is required under South Korean law before a company can file paperwork for an IPO.[^cite_note-138] One week later, the company filed for a preliminary review of their planned IPO.[^cite_note-139] On October 15, Big Hit was listed in the [KOSPI](1352321.md) index and began trading stocks.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

Per the company's 2020 annual report, its first since going public, Big Hit recorded net income of [₩](298884.md)86.2 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)75.35 million), a 19.1% increase compared to 2019's [₩](298884.md)72.4 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)63.29 million)[^cite_note-yonhap-142] Operating profit rose 44.3% to [₩](298884.md)142.4 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)124.48 million), while sales increased 35.6% to {{KRWConvert|796.3 billion[^cite_note-yonhap-142]—bolstered in part by album revenue and various acquisitions—with the company experiencing its best quarterly performance yet in the fourth quarter of 2020.[^cite_note-BH_2020-143] Though concert revenue decreased in the wake of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_South_Korea), merchandise, content, and fan club-related revenue increased 53%, 71%, and 66%, respectively.[^cite_note-BH_2020-143][^cite_note-144] In 2021, following its rebranding as Hybe, the company became the first South Korean music agency to surpass the [₩](298884.md)1 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)874.16 million) benchmark in annual sales. It recorded annual revenue of [₩](298884.md)1.2 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)1.05 billion) operating profit of [₩](298884.md)190.3 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)166.35 million), and net income of [₩](298884.md)141 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)123.26 billion).[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

## Disputes and legal actions

### Dispute with Min Hee-jin

On April 22, 2024, Hybe requested for [Min Hee-jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Hee-jin), the then-CEO of their sub-label ADOR, to step down from her position following an [audit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audit) of the label amidst accusations of Min attempting to make Ador independent.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206] Min released a statement dismissing those claims and also accused Hybe of overlooking the similarities between [NewJeans](71357738.md) and [Illit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illit), a girl group that debuted in March 2024 under Hybe's sub-label Belift Lab. In her statement, she accused Hybe and Belift of refusing to acknowledge the claims of [Illit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illit) plagiarizing [NewJeans](71357738.md)' image from music to style, with the former's debut causing confusion amongst fans online.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208] The value of Hybe's shares fell by 8.03% following the news.[^cite_note-209] In response, Hybe submitted evidence to judicial authorities to refute the claims of plagiarism. A later court case dismissed the plagiarism claims.[^cite_note-210]

Hybe officially reported Min Hee-jin to the police for "breach of trust" on April 25 following evidence retrieved from the audit that she is attempting to gain management of the label. Hybe later released text messages between Min and ADOR's vice president listing a series of methods to take control of the label. Hybe has also stated that they plan to meet with the legal representatives of NewJeans' members to discuss providing them with psychological and emotional care to support them for a successful return in the future.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212][^cite_note-\:4-213] It was also announced that NewJeans, who are set to release a new single in May, will carry out activities as planned.[^cite_note-214]

On April 25, seven hours after the report was filed, Min held an emergency press conference to deny the accusations against her. During the conference, she shared text messages exchanged between her, Bang Si-hyuk, and Hybe's former CEO Jiwon Park, some of which included Bang mentioning groups outside of Hybe. She accused Hybe of fabricating her words and revealed that she had filed an internal report in the past because the company failed to address her concerns over their treatment of NewJeans and her as a producer, however, this was ignored by the management.[^cite_note-\:5-215][^cite_note-\:4-213][^cite_note-216] Issues regarding Hybe's leadership also came to light as Min accused Bang of trying to control all of Hybe's labels and removing their uniqueness. Min also stated that she's willing to talk to either Bang or Park but they never agreed.[^cite_note-\:5-215][^cite_note-\:4-213]

Hybe requested for Ador to organize a board meeting on April 30. If the board members refuse to attend, then a request would be filed in court to open a shareholders' meeting for the label. The company also refuted all of Min's claims, additionally stating that she should respond to the audit and promptly step down from her position.[^cite_note-\:4-213][^cite_note-217] Following a court case held on May 30, Min's injunction to stop Hybe from exercising their voting rights to dismiss her was accepted by the court. Meanwhile, the ruling noted that Min had sought to weaken Hybe's control over ADOR by attempting to remove NewJeans from Hybe's influence or by pressuring Hybe to sell its stake in ADOR, thereby enabling herself to take independent control of the label[^cite_note-218] In respect of the court's decision, Hybe did not exercise its voting rights.[^cite_note-219] If Hybe were to violate this and fire her, they would have to pay a compensation of [₩](298884.md)20 [billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1,000,000,000) ([US\$](18717338.md)17.48 million).[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221]

In July 2024, Min filed police complaints against five Hybe executives on charges of defamation and business obstruction. The police ultimately dismissed the case.[^cite_note-222]

On November 20, 2024, Min resigned from all her positions (including the board internal director) at both ADOR and Hybe.[^cite_note-223]

On January 9, 2025, Chairman Park Jung-kyu of the tech company Davolink alleged in an interview that Min had made efforts to "poach" NewJeans. He also mentioned that there had been previous discussions between him and Min regarding potential investment and the possibility of NewJeans leaving Ador to sign exclusive contracts with another company.[^cite_note-224]

In early 2025, Min Hee-jin continued to face scrutiny over allegations of workplace misconduct and breach of trust. In January, the Ministry of Employment and Labor dismissed initial complaints of workplace harassment against her.[^cite_note-KoreaTimes2025-01-225] However, in March, the Seoul Regional Employment and Labor Office issued an administrative fine against Min, determining that certain actions met the threshold for workplace harassment under South Korean labor law. Min appealed the decision.[^cite_note-JoongAng2025-03-226]

In June, new developments emerged during ongoing court proceedings between Hybe and Min. Evidence presented in court included claims that Min had a hand in crafting emails that were publicly released by the families of NewJeans (then known as NJZ), raising questions about her role in shaping public narratives.[^cite_note-TOI2025-06-227]

A major legal decision occurred on July 15, when the Seoul Yongsan Police Station cleared Min of all breach of trust allegations that had been filed by Hybe in April 2024. Authorities concluded there was insufficient evidence to pursue criminal charges.[^cite_note-KoreaTimes2025-07-228]

Following the decision, Hybe announced its intent to appeal, stating that it had uncovered new evidence and legal grounds to challenge the police's conclusion.[^cite_note-MBW2025-07-229][^cite_note-Reuters-230]

On February 12, 2026, it was reported that the court ruled in favor of Min in both her lawsuit seeking payment for shares under a put option agreement and Hybe's lawsuit seeking confirmation that the shareholder agreement was terminated. After the validity of Min's put option right is finalized, Hybe will have to pay Min ₩25.5 billion (US\$17.7 million).[^cite_note-231]

### Employee insider trading

In June 2024, South Korean prosecutors charged three employees of subsidiaries affiliated with Hybe with insider trading. The individuals were accused of selling company shares shortly before the public announcement on June 14, 2022, that members of the K-pop group BTS would be taking a hiatus. Prosecutors stated that the employees had access to non-public information about the announcement, which enabled them to sell their shares in advance and avoid financial losses estimated between US\$24,000 and US\$108,000,[^cite_note-232] which the three denied during their first hearing at trial.[^cite_note-233]

On July 22, 2025, the three individuals—who were no longer employed at Hybe at the time of sentencing—received suspended prison sentences ranging from six months to two years. In addition to the suspended terms, the court imposed financial penalties ranging from approximately [₩](298884.md)51 million ([US\$](18717338.md)44,582.37) to [₩](298884.md)231 million ([US\$](18717338.md)201,931.9).[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235]

Separately, on May 29, prosecutors from the Seoul Southern District Prosecutors Office raided the headquarters of Hybe as part of an investigation into allegations of fraudulent trading. A former Hybe executive was accused of earning approximately [₩](298884.md)240 million ([US\$](18717338.md)209,799.38) in profits through insider trading by purchasing shares in [YG Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YG_Plus) after obtaining non-public information about Hybe's planned investment in the company.[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237] Hybe stated that it was fully cooperating with authorities and reiterated its commitment to legal compliance and transparency.[^cite_note-238]

### Leaked audit documents

On October 24, 2024, Hybe came under public scrutiny during a National Assembly audit, when Representative Min Hyung-bae of the Democratic Party questioned Hybe's Chief Operating Officer, Taeho Kim, about the company's internal practices. During the session, Min disclosed the existence of an internal company document titled Weekly Music Industry Report—a collection reportedly totaling approximately 18,000 pages. The report allegedly contained derogatory remarks, unverified rumors, and harsh commentary about various K-pop idols. Portions of the document were later leaked online, prompting widespread criticism.

In response, on October 29, Hybe CEO Jason Jaesang Lee issued a public apology. He described the report as a compilation of external opinions and reactions related to K-pop trends, intended for internal analysis by a limited number of executives. Lee acknowledged that the content was "highly inappropriate and unacceptable" and expressed regret over the harm caused. He stated that Hybe was in the process of directly contacting affected agencies to offer apologies, and he also personally apologized to the company's artists who had been criticized in the leaked material.[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241]

### Tax investigation

On July 29, 2025, the National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea conducted a search and seizure operation at Hybe's headquarters in Yongsan, Seoul. This action was part of a broader investigation by the NTS's 4th Investigation Bureau into alleged tax evasion and irregular financial activities involving 27 companies. Hybe was among the businesses suspected of engaging in unfair stock practices and underreporting tax liabilities.[^cite_note-TI_-_JoonAng-242]

The probe specifically focused on Hybe's 2020 initial public offering (IPO) and transactions that preceded it. Authorities are investigating claims that Hybe and its executives, including founder and chairman Bang Si-hyuk, misled investors in 2019 by publicly stating that there were no plans for a stock market listing, while secretly preparing for an IPO. The company later proceeded with the listing, and Bang is alleged to have gained hundreds of billions of won in profits through a special purpose vehicle (SPV) that acquired shares on his behalf. Prosecutors believe Bang may have received as much as 30% of the proceeds from this SPV-linked transaction.[^cite_note-TI_-_JoonAng-242]

This marked the first tax raid on Hybe since June 2022, when a routine audit by the Seoul Regional Tax Office led to the imposition of a "a multi-billion-won penalty."[^cite_note-243] The 2025 raid, however, signaled a significant escalation, as it followed two previous legal actions within the same month: one involving insider trading by Hybe executives and another centered on IPO-related fraud allegations relating to the founder, Bang Si-hyuk. Combined, these investigations reflect mounting legal and regulatory pressure on the entertainment conglomerate.[^cite_note-TI_-_JoonAng-242]

On April 21, 2026, South Korean police sought an arrest warrant for Hybe founder and chairperson Bang Si-hyuk over alleged violations of capital market laws related to Hybe's initial public offering, accusing him of misleading early investors and receiving profits through a related private equity fund.[^cite_note-244]
