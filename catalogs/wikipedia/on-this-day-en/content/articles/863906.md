The **National Liberal Party** ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Partidul Național Liberal, PNL*) is a major [liberal conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_conservatism) and [Christian democratic](328969.md) political party in [Romania](25445.md). As of early 2026, it stands as the nation's third-largest political force within the [Romanian Parliament](4462971.md). While the modern organization was formally re-founded in January 1990, emerging in the immediate aftermath of the [Revolution of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_revolution) and the collapse of the [communist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania), it serves as the direct successor to the [political party of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberal_Party_\(Romania,_1875\)) that dominated the political landscape of the [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) between 1875 and 1947.

Drawing upon this extensive historical legacy, the PNL frequently asserts its status as the first formally constituted [political party in Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Romania) and the oldest surviving member within the family of [European liberal parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_parties_by_country#Europe).[^cite_note-20] Internationally, the party is a member of the [European People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party) (EPP). Over the last two decades, the PNL has played a central role in Romanian governance, frequently leading or participating in governing coalitions and giving one of the country's post-communist presidents, [Klaus Iohannis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Iohannis), who served from late 2014 to early 2025.

## Recent historical overview

Until 2014, the PNL was a member of the [Alliance of Liberals and Democrats for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Liberals_and_Democrats_for_Europe_Party) (ALDE).[^cite_note-21] Following an update to its statutes in June 2014, the party's [MEPs](258909.md) joined the [EPP Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party_Group). On September 12, 2014, the PNL was admitted as a full member of the EPP[^cite_note-22] and subsequently merged with the [Democratic Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Liberal_Party_\(Romania\)) (PDL). Previously a member of the [Liberal International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_International) (LI),[^cite_note-23] the party later moved to the [Centrist Democrat International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrist_Democrat_International) (CDI).[^cite_note-24]

In the 2020 elections, under the leadership of [Ludovic Orban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludovic_Orban), the PNL achieved its best results since the fall of communism, securing its highest score in over 30 years across both the local and parliamentary levels and establishing itself as the dominant force in [Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transylvania), [Banat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat), and [Bukovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina). While finishing second in the [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_parliamentary_election), the party successfully led the formation of a [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics) government. However, internal friction led to Orban being overthrown in 2021; after its initial alliance with the [Save Romania Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_Romania_Union) (USR) collapsed (along with the [Cîțu Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%AE%C8%9Bu_Cabinet)), the PNL formed a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition) with its historical rival, the [Social Democratic Party](407177.md) (PSD), which governed through a scheduled power rotation between [Nicolae Ciucă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ciuc%C4%83) and [Marcel Ciolacu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Ciolacu).

Following the [2024 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_parliamentary_election), the political landscape shifted with the appointment of [Ilie Bolojan](78639092.md) (PNL) as Prime Minister in June 2025. Leading a broad [pro-European](631596.md) coalition, Bolojan pursued a program of rigorous fiscal discipline. However, the PSD withdrew its support in April 2026, allegedly over disagreements regarding these reforms. On May 5, 2026, the government fell after a no-confidence motion initiated and supported by the PSD and the [Alliance for the Union of Romanians](64666536.md) (AUR), with additional support stemming from other members of the parliament (totalling 281 votes),[^cite_note-25] leaving the PNL to lead a caretaker administration as of May 11 while negotiations for a new parliamentary majority continue.

## History

### Re-establishment and first governing experiences (1990–2000)



Flowchart showcasing the liberal political groups which seceded from and were subsequently integrated within the National Liberal Party during the 1990s (all with the exception of PNL-C)

The National Liberal Party (PNL) was re-established in January 1990, just days after the violent Romanian Revolution brought an end to communist rule. The process was spearheaded by a group of liberals who had survived the communist period, including many former [political prisoners](37307.md), or who had returned from exile. In its early years, the party was led by [Radu Câmpeanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radu_C%C3%A2mpeanu) and [Mircea Ionescu-Quintus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Ionescu-Quintus), both of whom served as a living bridge to the party's pre-communist history, having been liberal youth leaders during the [interwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period) and early [post-World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-war) eras. This continuity allowed the PNL to reclaim its position as the direct institutional successor to the historic party founded in 1875.

At the [1990 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Romanian_general_election), the PNL emerged as the third-largest parliamentary force. Radu Câmpeanu finished second in the presidential race with 10.6% of the vote, trailing behind [Ion Iliescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Iliescu). To bolster its ranks, the party integrated the Socialist Liberal Party (PSL) in December 1990, with its leader, [Niculae Cerveni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niculae_Cerveni), becoming a vice-president of the PNL.[^cite_note-26]

Shortly thereafter, at the behest of and primarily alongside the [Christian Democratic National Peasants' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_National_Peasants'_Party) (PNȚCD), and supported by smaller center-right parties and NGOs, the PNL helped form the [Romanian Democratic Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Democratic_Convention) (CDR). This alliance sought to establish a robust collective opposition and a governing alternative to the ruling [National Salvation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Salvation_Front_\(Romania\)) (FSN), which was widely viewed as the institutional successor to the Romanian Communist Party (PCR). However, prior to the [1992 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Romanian_general_election), Câmpeanu withdrew the PNL from the CDR to compete as a standalone force. A primary catalyst for this exit was Câmpeanu's refusal to run on unified electoral lists with the [Democratic Union of Hungarians in Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Union_of_Hungarians_in_Romania) (UDMR/RMDSZ).

This ultimately proved to be a major strategic error; the PNL failed to surpass the electoral threshold required for parliamentary representation and was consequently relegated to extra-parliamentary opposition for the 1992–1996 term. Furthermore, this decision triggered a wave of fragmentation within the liberal movement. Several splinter factions broke away from the main party: some opted to remain within the CDR, while others followed Câmpeanu into a new entity titled [PNL-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberal_Party%E2%80%93C%C3%A2mpeanu) ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Partidul Național Liberal-Câmpeanu*). The primary factions that defected to align with the CDR included PNL-CD (led by Niculae Cerveni), PNL-AT, and [PL '93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_'93). Other minor liberal parties, such as the PAC and UFD (which would eventually merge into the main PNL) also maintained their presence within the CDR throughout the late 1990s.

Following a leadership change that saw Mircea Ionescu-Quintus elected as the new party president in 1995, the PNL contested the [1996 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Romanian_general_election) as a reinstated member of the CDR.[^cite_note-RoseMunro2009-27] These elections marked the first [peaceful transition of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaceful_transition_of_power) in post-1989 Romania; the PNL joined the PNȚCD, the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Romania\)) (PD), and the UDMR/RMDSZ in forming a [broad governing coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition). This alliance successfully relegated the PDSR (the successor to the FSN and [FDSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Salvation_Front)) to the opposition for the 1996–2000 term. Additionally, the presidency was secured by the CDR's joint candidate, [Emil Constantinescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Constantinescu), who received the unified endorsement of all the alliance's constituent groups, including the various liberal factions then under the PNL umbrella.

### Opposition and second governing experiences (2000–2010)

Between 1996 and 2000, the governing CDR coalition was hampered by a lack of political coherence and frequent cabinet reshuffles. In response to this instability, the PNL once again decided to withdraw from the alliance shortly before the [2000 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Romanian_general_election), opting to compete as an independent force. While the party successfully maintained its parliamentary presence, finishing fourth in the legislative races and third in the presidential election, it failed to secure a place in a center-right government. This period also saw another internal schism: a splinter group founded by [Dan Amedeo Lăzărescu](https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan%20Amedeo%20L%C4%83z%C4%83rescu) and led by [Decebal Traian Remeș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decebal_Traian_Reme%C8%99), known as PNL-T ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *PNL Tradițional*), broke away to remain within the 'CDR 2000' alliance, endorsing [Mugur Isărescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugur_Is%C4%83rescu) for the presidency.

Consequently, in early 2003, the PNL joined forces with the Democratic Party (PD) to form the [Justice and Truth Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Truth_Alliance) (D.A.).[^cite_note-RoseMunro2009-27] This coalition was designed as a powerful center-right alternative to the ruling PSD (formerly PDSR) ahead of the [2004 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Romanian_general_election). The alliance achieved significant success, finishing second in the popular vote for Parliament and securing the presidency through their joint candidate, [Traian Băsescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traian_B%C4%83sescu). Following the elections, the D.A. Alliance successfully formed a center-right cabinet, marking the PNL's return to the executive and inaugurating a period of significant liberal influence in Romanian governance.

Until April 2007, the PNL served as the senior partner within the governing Justice and Truth (D.A.) Alliance, which commanded a parliamentary majority through a broader coalition with the Democratic Party (PD), the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(Romania\)) (PC), and the UDMR/RMDSZ.[^cite_note-28] However, the relationship between the PNL and PD deteriorated, culminating in April 2007 when Prime Minister and PNL President [Călin Popescu-Tăriceanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83lin_Popescu-T%C4%83riceanu) ousted the PD ministers from the cabinet. He subsequently formed a minority government alongside the UDMR/RMDSZ, supported externally by the PSD. This maneuver triggered significant internal dissent and led to a major scission: a splinter group led by [Theodor Stolojan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Stolojan) broke away to form the [Liberal Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democratic_Party_\(Romania\)) (PLD). The PLD eventually merged with the PD to create the [Democratic Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Liberal_Party_\(Romania\)) (PDL), effectively splitting the liberal electorate between two rival camps.

Following the [2008 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Romanian_parliamentary_election), the PNL placed third and entered official opposition, holding 19.74% of parliamentary seats. They were eclipsed by a new 'grand coalition' formed between their former allies, the Democratic Liberal Party (PDL), and the [Social Democratic Party](407177.md) (PSD), which together commanded roughly 70% of the legislature. At the [2009 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Romanian_presidential_election), the PNL's newly elected leader, [Crin Antonescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crin_Antonescu), finished third in the first round. Despite this significant electoral showing, the party remained in parliamentary opposition for the next three years, eventually returning to power in mid-2012 following the formation and accession of the [Social Liberal Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Liberal_Union) (USL).

During this period, [Klaus Iohannis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Iohannis), then serving solely as the president of the [FDGR/DFDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Forum_of_Germans_in_Romania), emerged as a central political figure. He was twice nominated for the premiership by the PNL in 2009, receiving the backing of a powerful opposition bloc that included the PSD and the PC. Despite this broad parliamentary support, his nomination was twice rejected by President Traian Băsescu. This impasse proved to be a critical precursor to the formal cooperation between the liberals and social democrats, while simultaneously elevating Iohannis's profile as a major national alternative to the incumbent administration.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

### Transition from USL to ACL and third governing experiences (2010–2020)



Central headquarters of the PNL located on Modrogan Alley, Bucharest (September 2014)

On 5 February 2011, the PNL formed the [Social Liberal Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Liberal_Union) (USL), a major political alliance with the PSD, the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(Romania\)) (PC), and the [National Union for the Progress of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_the_Homeland,_Military_and_Police) (UNPR).[^cite_note-31] This partnership lasted until 25 February 2014, when the PNL officially exited the USL, leading to the alliance's dissolution and the party's return to the opposition.[^cite_note-32] Following the [2014 European elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_European_Parliament_election_in_Romania), PNL President [Crin Antonescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crin_Antonescu) announced a significant ideological shift, seeking membership for the party within the [European People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party) (EPP).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Consequently, at the start of the [8th European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_European_Parliament), the majority of PNL [MEPs](258909.md) joined the EPP Group, though one remained with [ALDE Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Liberals_and_Democrats_for_Europe_group) as an independent.[^cite_note-35] By late May 2014, the PNL agreed to a merger with the Democratic Liberal Party (PDL); their immediate strategic objective was to field a joint candidate for the upcoming presidential election under the banner of the [Christian Liberal Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Liberal_Alliance) (ACL).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

On 27 June 2014, former PNL Chairman [Călin Popescu-Tăriceanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83lin_Popescu-T%C4%83riceanu) announced his intention to establish a separate liberal party to contest the presidency, citing his opposition to the impending merger with the PDL.[^cite_note-38] This breakaway faction, named the [Liberal Reformist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Reformist_Party_\(Romania\)) (PLR), was officially founded on 3 July 2014.[^cite_note-39] Meanwhile, the unification process continued; on 17 July 2014, it was confirmed that the merged entity would retain the National Liberal Party (PNL) name while relocating to the PDL's Bucharest headquarters.[^cite_note-40] The merger was formally ratified on 26 July 2014 during a joint party congress, with the legal registration of the 'New PNL' finalized by the end of the year.[^cite_note-41]



Klaus Iohannis, the 4th President of post-communist Romania

In the first round of the [2014 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Romanian_presidential_election), held on 2 November, the ACL candidate [Klaus Iohannis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Iohannis), then PNL President and Mayor of [Sibiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibiu), finished as the runner-up.[^cite_note-42] However, in a major political upset during the runoff on 16 November, Iohannis secured the presidency with 54.5% of the vote.[^cite_note-43] Despite this executive victory, the party faced significant challenges in the 2016 [local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Romanian_local_elections) and [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Romanian_parliamentary_election) elections, finishing second behind the PSD. As a result, the PNL remained in opposition for the next three years, eventually regaining executive power in late 2019 following the collapse of the [Dăncilă Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%83ncil%C4%83_Cabinet).

In anticipation of the [2019 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Romanian_presidential_election), the PNL formalized its strategy as early as March 2018, officially endorsing incumbent Klaus Iohannis for a second term. Simultaneously, the party designated its then-president, [Ludovic Orban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludovic_Orban), as the official candidate for [Prime Minister](266262.md) should the PNL prevail in the [2020 legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Romanian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] This dual-track leadership was cemented in June 2018 when Iohannis, during a press conference in his hometown of Sibiu, publicly confirmed his intention to seek re-election.[^cite_note-46] This early mobilization allowed the PNL to present a unified front against the ruling PSD, linking the presidency and the prospective premiership in a single political platform.

Throughout 2019, the PNL expanded its ranks by absorbing two minor parties: the [National Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Party_\(Romania\)) (PND), led by [Daniel Fenechiu](https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83t%C4%83lin-Daniel%20Fenechiu), and PACT, led by [Sebastian Burduja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Burduja). By late 2019, the National Liberal Party returned to power, forming a minority standalone government under Ludovic Orban. This cabinet secured parliamentary approval twice, sustained primarily through 'confidence and supply' agreements with the [USR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_Romania_Union), the [PMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Movement_Party), and various [ethnic minority parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_ethnic_minority_parties), most notably the [FDGR/DFDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Forum_of_Germans_in_Romania). At national level, the Orban administration was immediately defined by its efforts to navigate two unprecedented crises: the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Romania) and the [resulting economic recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_recession).

### Brief alliance with USR PLUS and fourth governing experiences (2020–present)



Electoral banner of the PNL (bottom) for the [2020 Romanian legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Romanian_legislative_election) displayed in Bucharest (November 2020). The Romanian caption translates to: 'We develop Romania #Succeeding! Together'.

The PNL entered several electoral alliances with the [2020 USR-PLUS Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USR_PLUS) for the [2020 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Romanian_local_elections), most notably by endorsing [Nicușor Dan](35543359.md), the successful independent candidate for [General Mayor of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Bucharest). Alongside this victory, the PNL secured multiple sector mayorships in the capital and numerous municipalities across the country. Shortly thereafter, the party established local administrative alliances with partners including USR PLUS, the PMP, the FDGR/DFDR, the UDMR/RMDSZ, and the PNȚCD. Following the [2020 legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Romanian_parliamentary_election), the PNL formalized its national strategy by forming a center-right coalition government with USR PLUS and the UDMR/RMDSZ, aiming to provide a stable governing framework for the four-year term.

In the immediate aftermath of the elections in early December 2020, incumbent party president Ludovic Orban stepped down as Prime Minister. [Nicolae Ciucă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ciuc%C4%83) was subsequently appointed to serve in an acting capacity until the new coalition could secure a formal vote of confidence. The PNL then put forward [Florin Cîțu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florin_C%C3%AE%C8%9Bu), who had served as [Minister of Public Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ministers_of_finance_of_Romania) in both Orban cabinets, as the nominee for Prime Minister. On 23 December 2020, the [Cîțu Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%AE%C8%9Bu_Cabinet) officially took office after receiving a decisive confidence vote in Parliament, with 260 votes in favor and 186 against.[^cite_note-guvernul_citu_a_fost_votat_2020-47]

As the new year unfolded, the PNL announced a national congress for 25 September 2021, convening 5,000 delegates to determine the party's leadership at every level nationwide.[^cite_note-48] At this summit, incumbent president Ludovic Orban faced a challenge from Prime Minister Florin Cîțu for control of the party's future; although rumors suggested former Minister of Agriculture [Dan Motreanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Motreanu) might also run, he ultimately declined to move forward with a candidacy.[^cite_note-49] While Orban remained the official president leading up to the vote, the escalating power struggle between the two rival camps significantly hindered the government's legislative pace, effectively stalling the implementation of key reforms.

#### 2021 governmental crisis

Main article: [2021 Romanian political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Romanian_political_crisis)

In early September 2021, the governing coalition collapsed after USR PLUS withdrew its ministers from the Cîțu Cabinet. The exit was prompted by Prime Minister Florin Cîțu's dismissal of the Minister of Justice, a move USR PLUS characterized as a provocation.[^cite_note-50] The party subsequently entered the opposition, declaring Cîțu responsible for a major governmental crisis and pledging to support any motion of no confidence against him.

Central to the dispute was the "Anghel Saligny" (PNDL 3) investment program, a 50 billion lei (\$12 billion) infrastructure scheme. Cîțu defended the project as essential for rural modernization, accusing the Justice Ministry of obstructionism. Conversely, USR PLUS alleged the program was a vehicle for public procurement fraud designed to "bribe" local PNL mayors, referred to in press reports as "local barons", for their support in the upcoming party congress.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

Despite losing its parliamentary majority, the PNL maintained a minority government alongside the UDMR/RMDSZ. An initial motion of no confidence filed by USR PLUS and AUR was stalled through parliamentary boycotts which were facilitated by both the PSD and UDMR/RMDSZ. During this period of instability, the Prime Minister faced significant public backlash, particularly regarding social media content where he was portrayed as "[Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman)".[^cite_note-53] This drew widespread mockery and prompted Ludovic Orban to suggest that the Prime Minister required a psychiatric consultation.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] In late September, the National Anticorruption Directorate (DNA) opened a criminal investigation into allegations of abuse of office regarding Cîțu's actions.[^cite_note-56]

The crisis resulted in a broader political realignment as President Klaus Iohannis maintained his firm endorsement of Cîțu, while Orban warned that relying on the tacit support of the PSD would be catastrophic for the party's future. At the local level, the PNL lost several former allies as PMP shifted toward center-left alliances in various counties,[^cite_note-57] and most local-level partnerships between the PNL and USR PLUS dissolved. The prolonged instability had immediate economic repercussions, with the [National Bank of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Romania) (BNR) reporting a steady depreciation of the [leu](642267.md) against the [Euro](9472.md) and [US Dollar](18717338.md),[^cite_note-58] while [Bloomberg](3057255.md) noted that inflation had reached a three-year high by early September 2021.[^cite_note-59]

##### Leadership under Florin Cîțu (September 2021–April 2022)

On 25 September 2021, Florin Cîțu was elected the 10th post-1989 president of the PNL, defeating incumbent Ludovic Orban. While the congress was marred by allegations of voting irregularities from the Orban camp,[^cite_note-60] Orban subsequently declared the end of his partnership with President Klaus Iohannis and resigned as [President of the Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Chamber_of_Deputies_of_Romania). Despite Cîțu's victory, political analysts labeled it a "[Pyrrhic victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhic_victory)," as the government faced an imminent collapse following USR PLUS's exit and the Social Democratic Party's (PSD) stated intent to support a motion of no confidence.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

On 5 October 2021, the Cîțu Cabinet was ousted by a record-breaking 281 votes in Parliament, the largest margin for a no-confidence motion in Romania's post-1989 history.[^cite_note-63] While Cîțu remained as acting, his approval ratings plummeted, reportedly reaching a low of 7% by late October.[^cite_note-64] During this period of transition, the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (PNRR, part of the [Next Generation EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_EU) package) was officially adopted by the [European Commission](9974.md), a project largely negotiated by the outgoing USR PLUS ministers.[^cite_note-65]

Following the government's dismissal, President Klaus Iohannis nominated [Dacian Cioloș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacian_Ciolo%C8%99) to form a new cabinet,[^cite_note-66] but the proposal was rejected by Parliament. Iohannis subsequently nominated then-acting Prime Minister [Nicolae Ciucă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ciuc%C4%83) for the premiership.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] This period marked a fundamental shift in PNL strategy; despite Cîțu's previous claims that a coalition with the PSD was a "major compromise",[^cite_note-69] the party entered negotiations to form the [National Coalition for Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coalition_for_Romania) (CNR) — a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition) with their traditional rivals, the PSD. Following the successful formation of this coalition, Ciucă was confirmed as Prime Minister by Parliament on 25 November 2021. This realignment triggered a formal schism within the PNL; on 23 November 2021, Ludovic Orban officially resigned from the party along with 16 other MPs to found the [Force of the Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_of_the_Right) (FD).[^cite_note-70]

Cîțu's tenure as party president remained unstable as the PNL sought to absorb smaller parties like ALDE and PMP to consolidate its position within the new grand coalition.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] However, internal pressure continued to mount, leading to Florin Cîțu's resignation as PNL president on 2 April 2022.[^cite_note-73] He was succeeded on an interim basis by [Gheorghe Flutur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Flutur) until a new congress was convened on 10 April 2022 to formalize the leadership of Nicolae Ciucă.[^cite_note-74]

##### Leadership under Nicolae Ciucă (April 2022–November 2024)

On 10 April 2022, at an extraordinary party congress, Nicolae Ciucă was elected the 11th post-1989 president of the PNL, receiving 1,060 votes out of 1,120 cast.[^cite_note-75] His election marked the first time a military leader held the party's presidency. Ciucă's primary objective was to maintain the stability of the National Coalition for Romania (CNR) grand coalition until the conclusion of his term as prime minister in June 2023. Following the governmental rotation, the PNL maintained the CNR coalition solely with the Social Democratic Party (PSD), removing the UDMR/RMDSZ from the executive. However, the coalition retained a parliamentary majority by maintaining a confidence and supply agreement with the political group of national minorities to endorse the former [Ciolacu I cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Ciolacu_cabinet).

Under Ciucă's leadership, the government's foreign policy was dominated by the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), with the administration expressing grave concern and aligning closely with [NATO](21133.md) and [EU](9317.md) responses. On the domestic front, Romania faced accusations of [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding).[^cite_note-76] [The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist) ranked the country last in the European Union in its democracy index, placing it [behind Viktor Orbán's Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding_under_Viktor_Orb%C3%A1n).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

The tenures of both Ciucă and his predecessor, Florin Cîțu, were marked by significant internal fragmentation; numerous [ MPs](98066.md) and local officials departed the party to form splinter organizations. By early 2024, opinion polls indicated a significant decline in the PNL's electoral support ahead of the [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_parliamentary_election).

#### Leadership under Bolojan (2024–present)



Ilie Bolojan, the current leader of PNL

Following the National Liberal Party's poor performance in the first round of the subsequently annulled [2024 Romanian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_presidential_election), and the resulting resignation of Nicolae Ciucă, [Ilie Bolojan](78639092.md) assumed the role of the party's acting president on 25 November 2024. Shortly thereafter, on 23 December 2024, he was elected [President of the Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Senate_of_Romania).[^cite_note-Senate-79]

The early part of his leadership was defined by significant institutional upheaval. On 12 February 2025, Bolojan assumed the role of acting [President of Romania](273659.md) following the resignation of incumbent Klaus Iohannis. In accordance with the Romanian Constitution, his duties as President of the Senate were suspended during this interim presidency. Bolojan served as the acting head of state until 26 May 2025, overseeing the transition to the administration of [Nicușor Dan](35543359.md). Following the conclusion of his interim mandate, Bolojan was appointed [Prime Minister of Romania](266262.md) on 23 June 2025.

## Ideology

The party had officially adhered to [conservative liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_liberalism)[^cite_note-Close-91] and [liberal conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_conservatism)[^cite_note-Nordsieck-92] before becoming simply [conservative](6675.md) since 2021.[^cite_note-newsweek.ro-93][^cite_note-Bogdan-94][^cite_note-euractiv-95][^cite_note-pora-96][^cite_note-ziare-97] In recent years, it has focused more on [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism) and a shift to a more [catch all](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_tent) platform. The National Liberal Party (PNL) also advocates for conservative initiatives and policies and the state in moral and religious issues, as well as the [privatisation](24661.md) and denationalisation of the economy, a trend which is currently  taking place quite rapidly in Romania, as in other [post-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-communism) economies in [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe). Unlike its Western counterparts, the party is more [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_nationalism)[^cite_note-98] and traditionalist on social issues, such as [LGBT rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Romania).[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

The party has factions of adherence to [Christian democracy](328969.md),[^cite_note-auto4-3][^cite_note-auto6-4][^cite_note-auto5-5] [ethnic nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_nationalism),[^cite_note-102] [neoliberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism), and [social conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism).[^cite_note-117] The party has also been described as [populist](8354382.md),[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] while former president [Florin Cîțu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florin_C%C3%AE%C8%9Bu) rejects this qualification.[^cite_note-121] However, after joining the [European People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party) (EPP) and especially under Cîțu and Ciucă's leadership, the party became more [conservative](6675.md),[^cite_note-newsweek.ro-93][^cite_note-Bogdan-94][^cite_note-euractiv-95][^cite_note-pora-96][^cite_note-ziare-97] [Radio Free Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe/Radio_Liberty) calling it "liberal only in the name".[^cite_note-auto-122] PNL opposes [same-sex marriage](92656.md) as well as [civil unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_union).[^cite_note-auto4-3]

In economic regards, it favours lowering [taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax) and expanding the [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector) of the national economy through a series of new laws in order to generate more value.[^cite_note-123] It also advocates a decentralisation of Romania's political structure, with greater autonomy given to the eight [development regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_regions_of_Romania). However, under Ciucă's rule, the party also shifted more from a [liberal-oriented economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism) towards [economic patriotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_patriotism).[^cite_note-auto-122][^cite_note-auto3-124][^cite_note-auto1-125][^cite_note-auto2-126] Former opposition leader [Cătălin Drulă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83t%C4%83lin_Drul%C4%83), former president of the [Save Romania Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_Romania_Union) (USR), accused the party of being [statist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statism).[^cite_note-127]

## Organization and Structure

The National Liberal Party operates through a hierarchical system of national and local bodies defined by its statute, most recently updated to reflect a more streamlined, executive-heavy leadership model. These organs manage the party's political strategy, internal discipline, and administrative logistics.

### National Leading Organs

**The Congress** ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Congresul*): The supreme authority of the party, the Congress (or General Assembly) meets every four years or in extraordinary circumstances. It is responsible for electing the Party President, the 4 First Vice-presidents, the 8 Regional Vice-presidents, and the 15 functional vice-presidents of the National Political Bureau.

**The National Council (CN)** ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Consiliul Naţional*): Serving as the primary forum for debate between Congresses, the Council meets at least twice a year. Its key functions include adopting the party's governing program and officially nominating the PNL candidate for the Romanian Presidency.

**The National Political Bureau (BPN)** ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Biroul Politic Național*): This is a broad leadership body that approves major political alliances and evaluates the performance of territorial branches. It is composed of the national leadership and the presidents of the county branches.

**The Executive Bureau (BEx)** ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Biroul Executiv*): This is the operative leadership core responsible for the party's day-to-day political decisions and tactical management. It consists of the President, First Vice-presidents, the Secretary-General, and the Regional Vice-presidents.

**The Secretary-General**: This office oversees the communication between central organisms and territorial branches, manages party assets, and handles the administrative information system.

### Auxiliary Organizations

The PNL maintains several specialized organizations designed to engage specific social and demographic groups:

[National Liberal Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberal_Youth_\(Romania\)) (TNL): Coordinates activities for members under the age of 35.

[Liberal Women Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Women_Organisation_\(Romania\)) (OFL): Focuses on policies regarding gender equality and the representation of women in politics.

League of the Local Elected Officeholders (LAL): A specialized body that coordinates the activity of PNL mayors, county council presidents, and local councillors.

[Liberal Student Clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Student_Clubs_\(Romania\)) (CSL): Promotes liberal ideology within higher education institutions.

### Local Structure

The party is decentralized into territorial branches, typically corresponding to Romania's 41 counties and the Municipality of Bucharest. Each branch maintains its own General Assembly and Standing Bureau. Under the 2025 reforms, these branches are grouped under 8 Development Regions, each overseen by a Regional Vice-president to ensure better coordination between the national executive and local administrations.
