The history of [immigration](4599312.md) to [Argentina](18951905.md) can be divided into several major stages:

- [Spanish colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas) between the [16th](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siglo%20XVI) and [18th](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siglo%20XVIII) century, mostly male,

  [^cite_note-1]

  &#x20;largely assimilated with the natives through a process called [miscegenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscegenation). Although, not all of the current territory was effectively colonized by the Spaniards. The [Chaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaco_Province) region, Eastern [Patagonia](65433.md), the current province of [La Pampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Pampa_Province), the south zone of [Córdoba](241269.md), and the major part of the current provinces of [Buenos Aires](255919.md), [San Luis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Province), and [Mendoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendoza,_Argentina) were maintained under indigenous dominance—[Guaycurúes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaycuru_peoples) and [Wichís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wich%C3%AD) from the Chaco region; [Huarpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huarpe) in the Cuyana and north Neuquino; Ranqueles in the east of Cuyo and north from the Pampean region; Tehuelches and Mapuches in the Pampean and Patagonian regions, and Selknam and Yámanas in de Tierra del Fuego archipelago—which were taken over by the Mapuches; first to the east of Cordillera de los Andes, mixing interracially with the Pehuenches in the middle of the 18th century and continuing until 1830 with the indigenous Pampas and north from [Patagonia](65433.md), which were conquered by the Argentine State after its [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_War_of_Independence).

- The African population, forcibly introduced from [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md) (mainly of [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples_of_South_Africa) origin), taken to work as slaves in the colony between the 17th and 19th centuries in great numbers.

- Immigration mostly European and to a lesser extent from [Western Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Asia), including considerable [Arab](2185.md) and [Jewish](25955086.md) currents, produced between the end of the 19th century and the first half of the 20th century (particularly [Italians](385155.md)

  [^cite_note-2]

  &#x20;and [Spaniards](963152.md) in that quantitative order), promoted by the Constitution of 1852 that prohibited establishing limitations to enter the country to those "strangers that bring through the purpose of working the land, bettering the industries, and introducing and teaching the sciences and the arts"

  [^cite_note-3]

  &#x20;and order the State to promote "European" immigration, even though after predomination of [Mediterranean](19006.md) immigrants, from [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) and the western Asia. Added to this is the [Alberdian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberdian) precept of "to govern is to populate." These politics were destined to generate a rural social fabric and to finalize the occupation of the Pampean, Patagonian, and Chaco territories, that until the 1880s, were inhabited by diverse indigenous cultures.

  [^cite_note-4]

- [European immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_European_immigration_wave_to_Argentina) in the 19th century and early 20th century (mainly [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Argentine) and [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Argentine)), focused on colonization and sponsored by the government (sometimes on lands conquered from the native inhabitants by the [Conquest of the Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Desert) in the last quarter of the century).

  [^cite_note-5]

- The immigration from nearby countries ([Uruguay](31843.md), [Chile](5489.md), [Brazil](3383.md), [Bolivia](3462.md), and [Paraguay](23420.md)) from the 19th to 21st century. These immigration streams date back to the first agro-pottery civilizations that appeared in Argentine territory.

  [^cite_note-6]

  [^cite_note-7]

- From the 1980s and 1990s, the migration currents especially come from [Chile](5489.md), [Paraguay](23420.md), [Bolivia](3462.md), [Peru](170691.md), [Asia](689.md) (particularly from [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea), [China](5405.md), and [Japan](15573.md) in this period) and [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe).

- During the 21st century, a part of Argentine migrants and their descendants returned from [Europe](9239.md) and the [United States](3434750.md). In addition, immigrants from [Bolivia](3462.md), [Paraguay](23420.md), and [Peru](170691.md); now there are also migratory streams from [China](5405.md), [Brazil](3383.md),

  [^cite_note-8]

  [^cite_note-9]

  &#x20;[Colombia](5222.md), [Cuba](5042481.md), [Venezuela](32374.md), [Senegal](27278.md), [Ecuador](9334.md), [Dominican Republic](8060.md), and [Haiti](13373.md).

- Mostly urban immigration during the era of rapid growth in the late 19th century (from 1880 onwards) and the first half of the 20th century, before and after [World War I](4764461.md) and also after the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War).

## History

### Colonial era

The [Spanish migration flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Argentina) which conquered and colonised the area that is now Argentina were mainly three:

- The one which came from the northwest — those Peruvian lands conquered by [Diego de Almagro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_de_Almagro) and [Francisco Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Pizarro) — being the cities of [Lima](85423.md), [Cusco](72735.md) and [Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potos%C3%AD) the scattering centres.

- The one which came from the west, from Chile, across the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes); from the cities of [Santiago](51572.md) and [Coquimbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquimbo).

- The one which came from the east, that used the [Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) and its tributaries, especially the [Paraná River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_River) to settle on the banks thereof. Also, this migration flow settled in [Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n), Paraguay where they colonised much of the region.

The Spanish [conquistadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) and settlers were mainly from [Biscay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscay), as well from [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\)) and [Portugal](23033.md), founding cities and establishing [estancias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estancia) for supplies of agricultural and livestock products. The scale of operations was reduced, mainly focused on the domestic market and the provision of the [crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Spain).

Another important population center were the cities of [Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n) del Paraguay and [Corrientes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrientes), founded in the area of the [Guaraní people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaran%C3%AD_people), and with important [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) on navigable rivers. At the same time, Andean migration from Chile settled in San Juan and Mendoza.

Later, with the rise of smuggling and the multiplication without human intervention of wild cattle on the Pampean plains, [Buenos Aires](255919.md) and other cities on the [Mesopotamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia,_Argentina) coast began to boom.

The exact number of Hispanic migrants to the Americas is difficult to determine, due to the fragmentary nature of the sources available to date. However, there are several estimates based on different sources and calculations. In any case, their percentages were low with respect to the total population, around 1–2%, in no case exceeding 5%.

The Argentine government reports that in 1810, about 6,000 peninsular Spaniards lived in the territory of the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata, out of a total population of between 500–700,000 inhabitants,[^cite_note-repetida_6-11] representing approximately 1% of the population.

The indigenous population decreased drastically in a very short period of time, both because of the deaths caused by the conquest and the contagion of diseases -such as [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox)- hitherto unknown in the continent, and because of the heavy cost in human lives of the [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) operations in the Andean region; The exact figures are unknown, and it is probably impossible to establish them reliably, but most studies[^cite_note-12] agree that this was one of the main features of the [demographic catastrophe in America after the arrival of the Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_genocide_in_the_United_States), although the rest was linked to the Hispanic or Creole element.[^cite_note-13]

During the colonial period, the current Argentine territory was also populated by [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples), who remained independent from the dominion of the Spanish Empire, in the Chaqueña region, the Pampean plains, the [Altiplano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano) and Patagonia.

## Support and control of immigration



Copy of a colonization contract in the history museum of [San José, Entre Ríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jos%C3%A9,_Entre_R%C3%ADos)

Since its unification as a country, Argentine rulers intended the country to welcome immigration. Article 25 of the 1853 [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Argentina) reads:

> The Federal Government will encourage European [immigration](4599312.md), and it will not restrict, limit or burden with any taxes the entrance into Argentine territory of foreigners who come with the goal of working the land, improving the industries and teach the sciences and the arts.

The Preamble of the Constitution dictates a number of goals (justice, peace, defence, welfare and liberty) that apply "to all men in the world who wish to dwell on Argentine soil". The Constitution incorporates, along with other influences, the thought of [Juan Bautista Alberdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Bautista_Alberdi), who expressed his opinion on the matter in succinct terms: *"to govern is to populate"*.

As a result of policies promoting immigration to the once sparsely populated country 11% of the Argentinian population and 50% of the population of Buenos Aires was made up of newly arrived immigrants by 1869.[^cite_note-14]

The legal and organisational precedents of today's [National Directorate for Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Directorate_for_Migration_\(Argentina\)) (DNM) can be found in 1825, when [Rivadavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardino_Rivadavia) created an Immigration Commission. After the commission was dissolved, the government of [Rosas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_de_Rosas) continued to allow immigration. [Urquiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justo_Jos%C3%A9_de_Urquiza), under whose sponsorship the Constitution was drawn, encouraged the establishment of [agricultural colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_colonies_in_Argentina) in the Littoral (western [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia,_Argentina) and north-eastern [Pampas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampa)).

The first law dealing with immigration policies was Law 817 of Immigration and Colonization, of 1876. The General Immigration Office was created in 1898, together with the [Hotel de Inmigrantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_de_Inmigrantes) (Immigrants' Hotel), in Buenos Aires.

The liberal rulers of the late 19th century saw immigration as the possibility of bringing people from supposedly more civilised, enlightened countries into a sparsely populated land, thus diminishing the influence of aboriginal elements and turning Argentina into a modern society with a dynamic economy. These politicians and intellectuals favored northern European immigration in particular.[^cite_note-15] However, immigrants did not bring only their knowledge and skills.

In 1902, a Law of Residence (*Ley de Residencia*) was passed, mandating the expulsion of foreigners who "compromise national security or disturb public order", and, in 1910, a Law of Social Defence (*Ley de Defensa Social*) explicitly named ideologies deemed to have such effects. These laws were a reaction by the ruling elite against imported ideas such as [labor unionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_union), [anarchism](12.md) and other forms of popular organisation.

The modern National Directorate for Migration was created by decree on 4 February 1949, under the Technical Secretariat of the Presidency, in order to deal with the new post-war immigration scenario. New regulations were added to the Directorate by Law 22439 of 1981 and a decree of 1994, but the current regulations are the Law 25871 of 2004 and the decree 616 of 2010.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Features of immigration

The majority of immigrants, since the 19th century, have come from Europe, mostly from [Italy](14532.md) and [Spain](26667.md). Also notable were Jewish immigrants escaping persecution, giving Argentina the highest Jewish population in Latin America, and the 7th in all the world. The total population of Argentina rose from 4 million in 1895 to 7.9 million in 1914, and to 15.8 million in 1947; during this time the country was settled by 1.5 million Spaniards and 3.8 million Italians between 1861 and 1920[^cite_note-18] but not all remained. Also arrived were [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), [Russians](59510.md), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) (more than 100,000 each), [Germans](152735.md) and [Austrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrians) (also more than 100,000), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people), [Greeks](42056.md), [Ukrainians](145144.md), [Croats](69801.md), [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs), [Irish](775859.md), [British](19097669.md), [Swiss](7351032.md), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), [Hungarians](17106358.md), [Scandinavians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavians) (the vast majority being [Danes](419786.md)), and people from other [European](9239.md) and [Middle Eastern](19323.md) countries, prominently [Syria](7515849.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)), [Egypt](8087628.md), [Armenia](10918072.md), [Georgia](48768.md), and [Turkey](11125639.md). These trends made Argentina the country with the second-largest number of immigrants, with 8 million, second only to the United States with 27 million. In addition, Argentine immigrant documents also show immigrants from [Canada](5042916.md), [Australia](4689264.md), [South Africa](17416221.md) and [The United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_United_States) arriving in Argentina.[^cite_note-CELS_Inmigrantes-19][^cite_note-Sánchez-Alonso-20]

Most immigrants arrived through the port of [Buenos Aires](255919.md) and stayed in the capital or within [Buenos Aires Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Province), as it still happens today. In 1895, immigrants accounted for 52% of the population in the Capital, and 31% in the province of Buenos Aires (some provinces of the [littoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral), such as [Santa Fe](520560.md), had about 40%, and the [Patagonian](65433.md) provinces about 50%). In 1914, before [World War I](4764461.md) caused many European immigrants to return to their homeland in order to join the respective armies, the overall rate of foreign-born population reached its peak, almost 30%.

A significant number of immigrants settled in the countryside in the interior of the country, especially the littoral provinces, creating [agricultural colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_colonies_in_Argentina). These included many Jews, fleeing pogroms in Europe and sponsored by [Maurice de Hirsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_de_Hirsch)'s [Jewish Colonization Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Colonization_Association); they were later termed "[Jewish gauchos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_gauchos)". The first such Jewish colony was Moïseville (now the village of [Moisés Ville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mois%C3%A9s_Ville)). Through most of the 20th century, Argentina held one of the largest Jewish communities (near 500,000) after the US, France, Israel and Russia, and by far the largest in Latin America (see [History of the Jews in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Argentina)). Argentina is home to a large community from the Arab world, made up mostly of immigrants from [Syria](7515849.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)), [Israel](9282173.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), [Egypt](8087628.md), [Armenia](10918072.md), [Georgia](48768.md), and [Turkey](11125639.md). Most are [Christians](5211.md) of the [Eastern Orthodox](10186.md) and [Eastern Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic) ([Maronite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite)) Churches, with smaller [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jews) and [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) minorities. Many have gained prominent status in national business and politics, including former president [Carlos Menem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Menem), the son of Syrian settlers from the province of La Rioja. (see [Arab Argentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Argentine)).

Nationalities settled in particular areas of the country, such as Irish in Formosa and the Mesopotamia region, the [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_of_Argentina) in [Misiones](233567.md) where they constitute approximately 9% of the population.[^cite_note-21]

Well-known and culturally strong are the German-speaking communities such as those of German-descendants themselves (both those from Germany itself, and those ethnic Germans from other parts of Europe, such as [Volga Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_German)), Austrian, and Swiss ones. Strong German-descendant populations can be found in the Mesopotamia region (especially [Entre Ríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entre_R%C3%ADos_Province) and [Misiones](233567.md) provinces), many neighborhoods in Buenos Aires city (such as [Belgrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrano,_Buenos_Aires) or [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo,_Buenos_Aires)), the Buenos Aires Province itself (strong German settlement in [Coronel Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronel_Su%C3%A1rez_Partido), [Tornquist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornquist_Partido) and other areas), [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba_Province_\(Argentina\)) (the [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest) celebration in [Villa General Belgrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_General_Belgrano) is specially famous) and all along the Patagonian region, including important cities such as [San Carlos de Bariloche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Carlos_de_Bariloche) (an important tourist spot near the Andes mountain chain, which was especially influenced by German settlements).

The South African Boers Patagonia houses a unique community of South African Boers who settled there after the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War) against the [United Kingdom](31717.md), which ended in 1902. Between 1903 and 1909, up to 800 Boer families trekked by ship to this lonely spot on Argentina's east coast, about 1500 km north of Tierra del Fuego. There are an estimated 100–120 Boer families still living on the land assigned to them by General Julio Roca. They are mainly an agricultural community.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

Other nationalities, such as Spaniards, although they have specific localities (such as the centre of Buenos Aires), they are more uniformly present all around the country and form the general background of Argentine population today.

## Legacy of immigration



A statue honoring the immigrants, in [Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario)

Argentine popular culture, especially in the [Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) basin, was heavily marked by Italian and Spanish immigration.

Post-independence politicians tried to steer Argentina consistently away from identification with monarchical Spain, perceived as backward and ultraconservative, towards relatively progressive national models like those of France or the United States. Millions of poor peasants from [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\)) arriving in Argentina not only did little to alter this position but also immigrated to Argentina because of it, steering clear of the United States and Britain.

[Lunfardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunfardo), the jargon enshrined in tango lyrics, is laden with Italianisms, often also found in the mainstream colloquial dialect ([Rioplatense Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rioplatense_Spanish)). Common dishes in the central area of the country (*milanesa*, *fainá*, *polenta*, *pascualina*) have Italian names and origins.

Immigrant communities have given [Buenos Aires](255919.md) some of its most famous landmarks, such as the *[Torre de los Ingleses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_de_los_Ingleses)* (Tower of the English) or the *[Monumento de los Españoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_Carta_Magna_and_Four_Regions_of_Argentina)* (Monument of the Spaniards). Ukrainians, Armenians, Swiss, and many others built monuments and churches at popular spots throughout the capital.

Argentina celebrates Immigrant's Day on 4 September since 1949, by a decree of the Executive Branch. The National [Immigrant's Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant's_Festival) is celebrated in [Oberá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ober%C3%A1), [Misiones](233567.md), during the first fortnight of September, since 1980. There are other celebrations of ethnic diversity throughout the country, such as the [National Meeting and Festival of the Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encuentro_y_Fiesta_Nacional_de_Colectividades_\(Rosario,_Argentina\)) in [Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario) (typically at the beginning of November). Many cities and towns in Argentina also feature monuments and memorials dedicated to immigration. There are also Immigrant's Festivals (or Collectivities Festivals) throughout the country, for example: [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordoba,_Argentina), [Bariloche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bariloche), [Berisso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berisso), [Esperanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanza,_Santa_Fe), [Venado Tuerto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venado_Tuerto), and [Comodoro Rivadavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comodoro_Rivadavia) have their own Immigrant's festivals. These festivals tend to be local, and they are not advertised or promoted nationally like the festivals in [Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario) and [Oberá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ober%C3%A1).

## Immigration in recent times

Besides substantial immigration from neighboring countries during the middle and late 1990s, Argentina received significant numbers of people from Asian countries such as Korea (both North and South), China, Vietnam, Cambodia, and Japan who joined the previously existing Sino-Japanese communities in Buenos Aires. Despite the [economic and financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_economic_crisis_\(1999%E2%80%932002\)) Argentina suffered at the start of the 21st century, people from all over the world continued arriving to the country, because of Argentina's immigration-friendly policies and high standard of living.

According to official data, between 1992 and 2003 an average of 13,187 people per year immigrated legally into Argentina. The government calculates that 504,000 people entered the country during the same period, giving about 345,000 undocumented immigrants. The same source gives a plausible total figure of 750,000 undocumented immigrants currently residing in Argentina.

From 2004 onwards, after Immigration Law 25871[^cite_note-40] was sanctioned, which makes the State responsible for guaranteeing access to health and education for immigrants, many foreigners have chosen Buenos Aires as their destination to work or study. Between 2006–2008 and 2012–2013 a relatively large group of [Senegal](27278.md) nationals (4500 in total) have immigrated to Argentina, 90 percent of which have refugee status.[^cite_note-41]

In April 2006, the national government started the *Patria Grande* plan to regularize the migratory situation of undocumented immigrants. The plan attempts to ease the bureaucratic process of getting documentation and residence papers, and is aimed at citizens of [Mercosur](25869317.md) countries and its associated states (Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Paraguay, Peru, Uruguay and Venezuela). The plan came after a scandal and a wave of indignation caused by fire in a Buenos Aires sweatshop, which revealed the widespread utilization of undocumented Bolivian immigrants as cheap labor force in inhumane conditions, under a regime of virtual [debt slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt_bondage).

As of 2020, Argentina counted with a positive [net migration rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_migration_rate) (one of the only three such countries in the region, alongside Chile and [Costa Rica](5551.md)) and remains a major destination for migrants within Latin America and the Caribbean.[^cite_note-42]

However, since the start of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022, in light of the international sanctions on Russian passports, Russian couples and pregnant Russian women have started to [emigrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_tourism) to Argentina in hopes of acquiring an [Argentine passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_passport) in order to continue their journey onto [Europe](9239.md), or to permanently establish themselves in Argentina with their children.[^cite_note-43] As of July 2023, more than 18,500 Russians have come to Argentina after Russia invaded Ukraine. Argentina does not require a visa for Russian citizens to enter the country as tourists and it also allows the parents of children born on Argentinian soil to receive residency, and, later, a passport. This opportunity has led to approximately 10,500 Russians travelling to Argentina to give birth in 2022.[^cite_note-44]
