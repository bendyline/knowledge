**Diammonium phosphate** (**DAP**; [IUPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUPAC) name **diammonium hydrogen phosphate**; chemical formula (NH<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub>(HPO<sub>4</sub>)) is one of a series of [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water)-[soluble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluble) [ammonium phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium_phosphate) [salts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_\(chemistry\)) that can be produced when [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia) reacts with [phosphoric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphoric_acid).&#x20;

Solid diammonium phosphate shows a dissociation pressure of ammonia as given by the following expression and equation:[^cite_note-2]

(NH<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub>HPO<sub>4</sub>(s) ⇌ NH<sub>3</sub>(g) + (NH<sub>4</sub>)H<sub>2</sub>PO<sub>4</sub>(s)

At 100 °C, the dissociation pressure of diammonium phosphate is approximately 5 mmHg.[^cite_note-3]

According to the diammonium phosphate [MSDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_data_sheet) from CF Industries, Inc., decomposition starts as low as 70 °C: "Hazardous Decomposition Products: Gradually loses ammonia when exposed to air at room temperature. Decomposes to ammonia and [monoammonium phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoammonium_phosphate) at around 70 °C (158 °F). At 155 °C (311 °F), DAP emits phosphorus oxides, [nitrogen oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_oxide) and ammonia."

## Uses



DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate) in a 1 kg commercial package&#x20;



DAP (Diammonium Phosphate) Granules

DAP is used as a [fertilizer](37401.md).[^cite_note-IPNI-2015-4] When applied as plant fertilizer, it temporarily increases the [soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil) [pH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH), but over a long term the treated ground becomes more [acidic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acidic) than before, upon nitrification of the ammonium. It is incompatible with [alkaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaline) chemicals because its [ammonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium) ion is more likely to convert to [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia) in a high-pH environment. The average pH in solution is 7.5–8.[^cite_note-IPNI-2012-5] The typical [NPK ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labeling_of_fertilizer) is 18-46-0 (18% N, 46% P<sub>2</sub>O<sub>5</sub>, 0% K<sub>2</sub>O).[^cite_note-IPNI-2012-5]

DAP can be used as a [fire retardant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_retardant). It lowers the combustion temperature of the material, decreases maximum weight loss rates, and causes an increase in the production of residue or char.[^cite_note-6] These are important effects in fighting [wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildfires) as lowering the [pyrolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrolysis) temperature and increasing the amount of [char](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Char_\(chemistry\)) formed reduces that amount of available fuel and can lead to the formation of a [firebreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firebreak).&#x20;

DAP is also used as a [yeast nutrient in winemaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast_assimilable_nitrogen) and [mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mead)-making; as an additive in some brands of cigarettes purportedly as a [nicotine](38272.md) enhancer; to prevent afterglow in matches, in purifying [sugar](27712.md); as a [flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_\(metallurgy\)) for soldering tin, copper, zinc and brass; and to control [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(chemistry\)) of alkali-soluble and acid-insoluble colloidal dyes on [wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool).[^cite_note-Patnaik-2002-1]

## Natural occurrence

The compound occurs in the nature as the exceedingly rare mineral phosphammite.[^cite_note-mindat-7][^cite_note-ima-mineralogy-2011-8] The related dihydrogen compound occurs as the mineral biphosphammite.[^cite_note-sciencepool-2024-9][^cite_note-ima-mineralogy-2011-8] Both are related to guano deposits.[^cite_note-mindat-7][^cite_note-sciencepool-2024-9]
