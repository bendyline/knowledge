[Iran](14653.md) is subdivided into thirty-one [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province) ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): استان, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *Ostân*), which are the first-level [administrative divisions of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Iran). Each province is governed from a local centre, usually the largest local city, which is called the capital ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): مرکز, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *[Markaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markaz_\(country_subdivision\))*) of the province. The provincial authority is headed by a [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_Iran_governors-general) ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): استاندار, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *Ostândâr*), who is appointed by the [Minister of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_\(Iran\)) subject to approval of the cabinet. The provinces are subdivided into [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Iran), districts ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): بخش, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *[bakhsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhsh_\(administrative_division\))*) and villages.[^cite_note-1]

## Modern history



Map of administrative subdivisions of Iran in 1911 – [Tehran](57654.md), *eyalats*, and *velayats*

Iran has held its modern territory since the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1857\)) in 1857. Prior to 1937, Iran had maintained its feudal administrative divisional structure, dating back to the time the modern state was centralized by the [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) in the 16th century, although the boundaries, roles, and rulers changed often. On the eve of the [Persian Constitutional Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Constitutional_Revolution) in 1905, Iran was composed of [Tehran](57654.md), being directly ruled by the monarch; four *[eyalats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyalet)* ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): ایالات *eyâlât* pl., ایالت *eyâlat* sin.), ruled by [Qajar princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty); and various *[velayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velayat)* ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): ولایات *velâyât* pl., ولایت *velayat* sin.). Nomadic tribal confederations, such as the [Bakhtiari people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhtiari_people) and [Qashqai people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qashqai_people), were largely independent of the domestic administrative divisions and were autonomous.

With the Constitutional Revolution, and the formation of the first [National Consultative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Consultative_Assembly), Iran's administrative subdivisions were legally defined in 1907.[^cite_note-1907Law-2] Any change in the boundaries of *eyalats*, *velayats*, or their respective sub-districts was banned by the Iranian constitution, except with the passage of a new law by the assembly. Per the 1907 law, the following were defined:[^cite_note-1907Law-2]

**.ماده ۱** ــ مملکت محروسه ایران برای تسهیل امور سیاسی بایالات و ولایات منقسم می‌شود&#x20;

**ماده ۲** ــ ایالت قسمتی از مملکت است که دارای حکومت مرکزی و ولایات حاکم‌نشین جزء است و فعلاً منحصر به چهار ایالت است: آذربایجان، کرمان و بلوچستان، فارس، خراسان

&#x20;**ماده ۳** ــ ولایات قسمتی از مملکت است که دارای یک شهر حاکم‌نشین و توابع باشد اعم از اینکه حکومت آن تابع پایتخت یا تابع مرکز ایالتی باشد

**Article 1** – Guarded Domain of Iran, for the facilitation of political affairs, will be subdivided into *Eyalats* and *Velayats*&#x20;

**Article 2** – *Eyalat* is a part of the kingdom which includes a central government and subordinate governor-ruled *Velayats* and at the moment there only are four *Eyalat*: Azerbaijan, Kerman and Baluchistan, Fars, Khurasan.

&#x20;**Article 3** – *Velayat* is a part of the kingdom which includes a governor-residence city and subordinate areas, whether its governance is subordinate to the capital \[Tehran], or to the capital of an *Eyalat*.

On 22 October 1911, the National Consultative Assembly passed another law, titled "The law of Election of National Consultative Assembly" ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): قانون انتخابات مجلس شورای ملی). This law presented a complete list of all *eyalats* and *velayats* of the country, as well as their constituent districts and cities. This list presented the grouping of various towns and districts into [electoral districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district) for the purpose of the election. According to this law, in 1911, Iran was made up of 27 administrative subdivisions, the region of [Tehran](57654.md), 4 *eyalats*, and 22 *velayats*.[^cite_note-1911Law-3] Below is a list:

In 1937, Iran was reorganized to form ten numbered provinces with subordinate governorates: Gilan, Mazandaran, East Azerbaijan, West Azerbaijan, Kermanshah, Khuzestan, Pars, Kerman, Khorasan, and Isfahan.[^cite_note-statoid-4]

Iran has had a historical claim to [Bahrain](18933277.md) as its 14th province: [Bahrain province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_province), which was under British colonial occupation until 1971. Prior to 1957, Bahrain was placed under [Pars province](448049.md).[^cite_note-5] During the [Safavid era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran), Bahrain was subordinate to [Bushehr governorship](448037.md) and [Zubarah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zubarah) (located in modern-day country of [Qatar](8238258.md)) was its capital city. In 1737, during the [Afsharid era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_Iran), Bahrain was made subject to Pars governorship.[^cite_note-6] This claim was reasserted by the new theocratic Iranian leadership after 1979 with the famous [1981 coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Bahraini_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) that occurred.[^cite_note-7]

From 1960 to 1981, the governorates were gradually raised to provincial status one by one. Since then several new provinces have been created, most recently in 2010 when the new [Alborz province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_province) was split from Tehran province, and before that in 2004 when the province of [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan_province) was divided into three provinces.[^cite_note-jazeera-8]

## Provinces by population and GDP

According to *[Donya-e-Eqtesad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donya-e-Eqtesad)*, between 2017 and 2019, some 11 of the 20 poorest Iranian cities were in the province of [Sistan and Baluchestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistan_and_Baluchestan). Three other markedly poor cities were located in [Kerman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_province).[^cite_note-9]

### Safavid Dynasty

The *Tadhkirat Al-Muluk*, a work made circa 1725 (1137) which details the Safavid administration mentions that Iran had four territories governed by *[Valis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velayat)*: ([Arabistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Khuzestan), [Luristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Lorestan), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Georgia) and [Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Kurdistan)).&#x20;

And thirteen provinces governed by *[Beglarbegis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beylerbeylik_\(Safavid_Persia\))*: ([Azarbayjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_province_\(Safavid_Iran\)) (also called Tabriz Province), [Chukhur-i Sa'd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erivan_province_\(Safavid_Iran\)), [Qarabagh-Ganja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Karabakh), [Shirvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Shirvan), [Astarabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Astarabad), [Mashhad](406512.md), [Marv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merv), [Herat](14128.md), [Qandahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Kandahar), [Kirman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_province), [Kuh-Giluya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohgiluyeh_and_Boyer-Ahmad_province), [Qazvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazvin) and [Hamadan](43610555.md)).

In other places such as [Isfahan](223149.md) (the capital) and nearby regions, [Yazd](451385.md), [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran), [Gilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilan), [Saveh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saveh), [Shiraz](39622.md) and many districts of [Persian Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Iraq), they were administered by the *Khāssa*, the central royal branch of the government; in opposition to the provincial *Mamālik* government branch.[^cite_note-al-Muluk-15]
