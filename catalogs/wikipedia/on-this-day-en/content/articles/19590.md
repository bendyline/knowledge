**Minnesota**[^cite_note-9] is a [state](18618239.md) in the [Upper Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest) region of the United States. It is bordered by the Canadian provinces of [Manitoba](18926.md) and [Ontario](22218.md) to the north and east and by the U.S. states of [Wisconsin](33127.md) to the east, [Iowa](26810748.md) to the south, and [North Dakota](21651.md) and [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota) to the west. The northeast corner has a water boundary with [Michigan](18859.md) across [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior). It is the [12th-largest U.S. state in area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) and the [22nd-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population), with about 5.8 million residents. Minnesota is known as the "Land of 10,000 Lakes"; it has 14,420 bodies of fresh water covering at least ten acres each.[^cite_note-10] Roughly a third of the state is [forested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover_by_state_and_territory_in_the_United_States). Much of the remainder is [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie) and farmland. More than 60% of Minnesotans (about 3.71 million) live in the [Minneapolis–Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis%E2%80%93Saint_Paul) metropolitan area, known as the "Twin Cities", which is Minnesota's main [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Minnesota), [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Minnesota), and [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Minnesota) hub[^cite_note-11] and the [16th-largest metropolitan area in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_statistical_areas) Other minor [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) and [micropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micropolitan_statistical_area) statistical areas include [Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth,_Minnesota), [Mankato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankato,_Minnesota), [Moorhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorhead,_Minnesota), [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Minnesota), and [St. Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Cloud,_Minnesota).[^cite_note-\:2-12]

Minnesota, which derives its name from the [Dakota language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_language), has been inhabited by various [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) since the [Woodland period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_period) of the 11th century BCE. Between roughly 200 and 500 CE, two areas of the indigenous [Hopewell tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_tradition) emerged: the [Laurel complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_complex) in the north, and Trempealeau Hopewell in the [Mississippi River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River_Valley) in the south. The [Upper Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mississippian), consisting of the [Oneota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneota) people and other [Siouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siouan) speakers, emerged around 1000 CE and lasted through the arrival of Europeans in the 17th century. French explorers and [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionaries) were the earliest Europeans to enter the region, encountering the [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people), [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe), and various [Anishinaabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anishinaabe) tribes. Much of what is now Minnesota formed part of the [vast French holding of Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)), which [the United States purchased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) in 1803. After several territorial reorganizations, the [Minnesota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Territory) was [admitted to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) as the 32nd state in 1858. Minnesota's official motto, *L'Étoile du Nord* ("The Star of the North"), is the only [state motto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._state_and_territory_mottos) in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language).[^cite_note-auto1-2] This phrase was adopted shortly after statehood and reflects both the state's early French explorers and its position as the northernmost state in the contiguous U.S.

As part of the [American frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_frontier), Minnesota attracted settlers and [homesteaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Acts) from across the country. Its growth was initially based on timber, agriculture, and railroad construction. Into the early 20th century, European immigrants arrived in significant numbers, particularly from [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), Germany, and [Central Europe](5188.md). Many were linked to the failed [revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848), which partly influenced the state's development as a center of [labor and social activism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_labor_movement).[^cite_note-The_transition_of_a_new_world_Bohemia.-13] Minnesota's rapid industrialization and urbanization precipitated [major social, economic, and political changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Era) in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; the state was at the forefront of [labor rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_rights_in_the_United_States), [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage_in_the_United_States), and political reform.[^cite_note-14] Consequently, Minnesota is relatively unique among Midwestern states in being a reliable base for the [Democratic Party](5043544.md), having voted for every Democratic presidential nominee since 1976, longer than any other U.S. state.[^cite_note-15]

Since the late 20th century, Minnesota's economy has diversified away from traditional industries such as agriculture and resource extraction to services, finance, and health care. Minnesota ranks highly among national averages in terms of [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_life_expectancy), healthcare standards, and education, and above average in income per capita. Minnesota is home to 11 federally recognized [Native American reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reservation) (seven Ojibwe, four Dakota), and its culture, demographics, and religious landscape reflect [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_American) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) influence. This heritage continues to affect the state's racial demographics, making it one of the country's least diverse states,[^cite_note-CensusDiversity-16] but in recent decades, Minnesota has become more multicultural, due to both larger domestic migration and immigration from Latin America, Asia, the [Horn of Africa](62142.md), and the Middle East. The state has the nation's largest population of [Somali Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Somalis_in_Minneapolis%E2%80%93Saint_Paul) and the second-largest [Hmong community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Hmong_in_Minneapolis%E2%80%93Saint_Paul).[^cite_note-MNHmong-17]

## Etymology

The word *Minnesota* comes from the [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_language)[^cite_note-18] name for the [Minnesota River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_River), which got its name from one of two words in Dakota: *mní sóta*, which means "clear blue water",[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] or *Mníssota*, which means "cloudy water".[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-mnhs-name-22] Early explorers interpreted the Dakota name for the Minnesota River in various ways, and four spellings of the state's name were considered before "Minnesota" was established in 1849, when the Territory of Minnesota was formed.[^cite_note-23] [Dakota people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people) demonstrated the name to early settlers by dropping milk into water and calling it *_mní sóta_*.[^cite_note-mnhs-name-22]

Many places in the state have similar Dakota names, such as [Minnehaha Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnehaha_Park_\(Minneapolis\)) ("curling water" or waterfall), [Minneiska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneiska,_Minnesota) ("white water"), [Minneota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneota,_Minnesota) ("much water"), [Minnetonka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Minnetonka) ("big water"), [Minnetrista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnetrista,_Minnesota) ("crooked water"), and Minneapolis, a [hybrid word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_word) combining Dakota *mní* ("water") and *-polis* ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) for "city").[^cite_note-24] The [state seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_Minnesota) features the phrase *Mni Sóta Makoce* ("the land where the water reflects the skies"), the Dakota name for the larger region.[^cite_note-25]

## History

Main article: [History of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Minnesota)



A map of [Minnesota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Territory) 1849–1858

When Europeans arrived in North America, the [Dakota people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people) lived in what is now Minnesota. The first Europeans to enter the region were French [voyageurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyageurs) and [fur traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) who arrived in the 17th century. They used the [Grand Portage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Portage_National_Monument) to access trapping and trading areas further into Minnesota. The [Anishinaabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anishinaabe) (also known as [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe) or Chippewa) were migrating into Minnesota, and formed an Alliance with the Dakota. European powers destabilized the region economically and politically, resulting in the alliance's collapse and the [Dakota-Ojibwe War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota-Ojibwe_War),[^cite_note-timepieces-26] and dislocated the [Mdewakanton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mdewakanton) from its homelands along [Mille Lacs Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mille_Lacs_Lake). Explorers such as [Daniel Greysolon, Sieur du Lhut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Greysolon,_Sieur_du_Lhut), Father [Louis Hennepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Hennepin), [Jonathan Carver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Carver), [Henry Schoolcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Schoolcraft), and [Joseph Nicollet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Nicollet) mapped the state.

The region was part of [Spanish Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_Spain\)) from 1762 to 1802.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The portion of the state east of the Mississippi River became part of the United States at the end of the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), when the [Second Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)) was signed. Land west of the Mississippi was acquired with the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase), though the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company) disputed the [Red River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Valley) until the [Treaty of 1818](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1818), when the border on the [49th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north) was agreed upon.[^cite_note-Lass-29]

In 1805, [Zebulon Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebulon_Pike) bargained with Native Americans to acquire land at the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the Minnesota and Mississippi rivers to create a military reservation. The construction of Fort Snelling followed between 1819 and 1825.[^cite_note-Gilman-30] Its soldiers built a [grist mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grist_mill) and a [sawmill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawmill) at [Saint Anthony Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Anthony_Falls), which were harbingers of the water-powered industries around which Minneapolis later grew. Meanwhile, squatters, government officials, and others had settled near the fort; in 1839 the army forced them off military lands, and most moved downriver, just outside the military reservation, to the area that became St. Paul.[^cite_note-hfs-31]

Minnesota was part of several territorial organizations between acquisition and statehood. From 1812 to 1821 it was part of the [Territory of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Missouri) that corresponded with much of the Louisiana Purchase. It was briefly an unorganized territory ([1821–1834](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States#Formerly_unorganized_territories)) and was later consolidated with Wisconsin, Iowa and half the Dakotas to form the short-lived [Territory of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Michigan) (1834–1836). From 1836 to 1848, Minnesota and Iowa were part of the [Territory of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Wisconsin). From 1838 to 1846, Minnesota west of the Mississippi River was part of the [Territory of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Iowa). Minnesota east of the Mississippi was part of Wisconsin until 1848.[^cite_note-auto-32]

### Statehood

When Iowa gained statehood, western Minnesota was in an Unorganized Territory again. [Minnesota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Territory) was formed on March 3, 1849. The first territorial legislature, held on September 2, 1849,[^cite_note-33] was dominated by men of [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) ancestry.[^cite_note-34] Minnesota became the [32nd U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union) on May 11, 1858. The founding population was so overwhelmingly of New England origins that the state was dubbed "the New England of the West".[^cite_note-auto-32][^cite_note-35]



Mixed Dakota-Europeans who were rescued by "non-hostile" Dakota. The girl in the foreground wrapped in the striped blanket is Elise Robertson, the sister of Thomas Robertson, a mixed blood who acted as an intermediary between the Dakota and the European-Americans during the [Dakota War of 1862](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_War_of_1862)

Treaties between the U.S. government and the eastern Dakota and Ojibwe gradually forced the natives off their lands and onto [reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reservation). As conditions deteriorated for the eastern Dakota, tensions rose, leading to the [Dakota War of 1862](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_War_of_1862).[^cite_note-36] The conflict was ignited when four young Dakota men, searching for food, killed a family of white settlers on August 17. That night, a faction of [Little Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Crow)'s eastern Dakota decided to try to drive all settlers out of the Minnesota River valley. In the weeks that followed, Dakota warriors killed hundreds of settlers, causing thousands to flee the area.[^cite_note-anderson2019-37] The six-week war ended with the defeat of the eastern Dakota and 2,000 in custody, who were eventually exiled to the [Crow Creek Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crow_Creek_Reservation) by the [Great Sioux Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Sioux_Reservation) in [Dakota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Territory). The remaining 4,500 to 5,000 Dakota mostly fled the state into [Rupert's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert's_Land).[^cite_note-Lass-29] As many as 800 settlers were killed during the war.[^cite_note-38]

Minnesota Governor [Alexander Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Ramsey) subsequently declared that "the Sioux Indians of Minnesota must be exterminated or driven forever beyond the borders of the state"[^cite_note-39] and placed a bounty of \$25/scalp on the heads of the eastern Dakota men. Over 1,600 eastern Dakota women, children, and elderly walked from the Lower Sioux Agency to [Fort Snelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Snelling) to be held until the spring thaw allowed riverboats to take them out of Minnesota to Crow Creek Indian Reservation.[^cite_note-aftermath-40] [William Crooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Crooks_\(colonel\)), commander of [6th Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Minnesota_Infantry_Regiment), had a [palisade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palisade) erected around the encampment on the river bottom flats directly below Fort Snelling, to protect native people from the soldiers and settlers.[^cite_note-41] Conditions there were poor and between 125 and 300 died of disease.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-Bounty-43] Around 400 Dakota men were tried after the war. 303 were sentenced to death, but [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) reviewed the convictions and approved 39 of the death sentences. In December 1862, 38 of them were hanged.[^cite_note-Lass-29]

In early 1863, Ramsey resigned as governor to become the Federal [Indian Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Commissioner). His successor, Governor [Henry Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Adoniram_Swift), raised the bounty to \$200/scalp. A total of \$325 was paid out to four people collecting bounties, including for Little Crow who was killed in July 1863.[^cite_note-Bounty-43] Upon becoming Indian Commissioner, Ramsey set out to get Ojibwe lands too. In 1863 he negotiated the [Treaty of Old Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Old_Crossing), whereby the Ojibwe ceded all their land in northern Minnesota and moved to reservations.

Logging, farming, and railroads were mainstays of Minnesota's early economy. The sawmills at Saint Anthony Falls and logging centers of [Pine City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_City,_Minnesota), [Marine on St. Croix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_on_St._Croix,_Minnesota), [Stillwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillwater,_Minnesota), and [Winona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winona,_Minnesota) processed vast quantities of timber. These cities were on rivers that were ideal for transportation.[^cite_note-Lass-29] St. Anthony Falls was later tapped to provide power for flour mills. Innovations by Minneapolis millers led to the production of Minnesota "patent" flour, which commanded almost double the price of "bakers'" or "clear" flour which it replaced.[^cite_note-44] By 1900, Minnesota mills, led by [Pillsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillsbury_Company), [Northwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Consolidated_Milling_Company), and the Washburn-Crosby Company, an ancestor of [General Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Mills), were grinding 14.1% of the nation's grain.[^cite_note-45]



[Phelps Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phelps_Mill) in [Otter Tail County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otter_Tail_County,_Minnesota)

The state's iron-mining industry was established with the discovery of iron in the [Vermilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermilion_Range_\(Minnesota\)) and [Mesabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesabi_Range) ranges in the 1880s, followed by the [Cuyuna Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyuna_Range) in the early 1900s. The ore went by rail to [Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth,_Minnesota) and [Two Harbors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Harbors,_Minnesota) for ship transport east via the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes).[^cite_note-Lass-29]

### 20th and 21st centuries

Industrial development and the rise of manufacturing caused the population to shift gradually from rural areas to cities during the early 20th century. Nevertheless, farming remained prevalent. Minnesota's economy was hit hard by the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), resulting in lower prices for farmers, layoffs among iron miners, and labor unrest. Compounding the adversity, western Minnesota and the Dakotas were hit by drought from 1931 to 1935. [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal) programs provided some economic turnaround. The [Civilian Conservation Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_Conservation_Corps) and other programs around the state established some jobs for Indians on their reservations, and the [Indian Reorganization Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Reorganization_Act) of 1934 provided the tribes with a mechanism of self-government. This gave Natives a greater voice within the state and promoted more respect for tribal customs because religious ceremonies and [native languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_language) were no longer suppressed.[^cite_note-Gilman-30]

After World War II, industrial development quickened. New technology increased farm productivity through automation of [feedlots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedlot) for hogs and cattle, machine milking at dairy farms, and raising chickens in large buildings. Planting became more specialized, with [hybridization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_\(biology\)) of corn and wheat, and farm machinery such as [tractors](152692.md) and [combines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combine_harvester) became the norm. [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) professor [Norman Borlaug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Borlaug) contributed to these developments as part of the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution).[^cite_note-Gilman-30] Increased mobility enabled more specialized jobs.[^cite_note-Gilman-30]

Minnesota became a center of technology after World War II. [Engineering Research Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_Research_Associates) was formed in 1946 to develop computers for the [United States Navy](20518076.md). It later merged with [Remington Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remington_Rand), and then became [Sperry Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sperry_Rand). [William Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Norris_\(CEO\)) left Sperry in 1957 to form [Control Data Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_Data_Corporation) (CDC).[^cite_note-46] [Cray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cray) Research was formed when [Seymour Cray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Cray) left CDC to form his own company. Medical device maker [Medtronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medtronic) also started business in the Twin Cities in 1949. The nonprofit [Mayo Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_Clinic), which was founded in 1864 in [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Minnesota), grew to become one of the country's leading medical systems, and, by the 21st century, Minnesota's largest private employer.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

In 1957, the legislature created a planning commission for the Twin Cities metropolitan area, which became the [Metropolitan Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Council_\(Minnesota\)) in 1967.[^cite_note-\:3-49] In 1971, under Governor [Wendell Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_R._Anderson), a series of legislation called the "Minnesota Miracle" led to a broad reform in financing of Minnesota public schools and local governments that created a fairer distribution in taxation and education.[^cite_note-\:4-50][^cite_note-51] Two postwar Minnesota governors, former dentist [Rudy Perpich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Perpich) and former professional wrestler [Jesse Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Ventura), were both known for their unconventional manner, but enjoyed some popularity within the state.[^cite_note-\:3-49][^cite_note-\:4-50]

In the 2020s, the state drew national and international attention for the [murder of George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd) and the subsequent [George Floyd protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests); a [series of cases of fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_fraud_scandal), most notably the [Feeding Our Future](77081996.md) scandal; and [Operation Metro Surge](82105033.md).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-\:22-53][^cite_note-54] After a period of mostly divided government during the 21st century, the [Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic%E2%80%93Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party) (DFL) won control of all three branches of Minnesota's government in 2022 and passed significant reforms in the [2023 legislative session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/93rd_Minnesota_Legislature), moving the state in a progressive direction.[^cite_note-\:02-55][^cite_note-56]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Minnesota)



Scalable map of Minnesota, showing roads and major bodies of water

Minnesota is the second northernmost U.S. state (after [Alaska](624.md)) and northernmost contiguous state, as the isolated [Northwest Angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Angle) in [Lake of the Woods County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_the_Woods_County) is the only part of the 48 [contiguous states](83759.md) north of the [49th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north). The state is part of the U.S. region known as the [Upper Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest) and part of North America's [Great Lakes region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_region_\(North_America\)). It shares a [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior) water border with [Michigan](18859.md) and a land and water border with [Wisconsin](33127.md) to the east. [Iowa](26810748.md) is to the south, [North Dakota](21651.md) and [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota) are to the west, and the [Canadian provinces](75763.md) of [Ontario](22218.md) and [Manitoba](18926.md) are to the north. With 86,943 square miles (225,180 km<sup>2</sup>),[^cite_note-57] or approximately 2.25% of the United States,[^cite_note-58] Minnesota is the 12th-largest state.[^cite_note-59]

### Geology

Main article: [Geology of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Minnesota)

See also: [List of lakes in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_in_Minnesota) and [List of Minnesota rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Minnesota_rivers)



Tilted beds of the Middle [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) Thomson Formation in [Jay Cooke State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Cooke_State_Park)

[^cite_note-MNGeog-60]

Minnesota has some of the earth's oldest rocks, [gneisses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gneiss) that are about 3.6 billion years old (80% as old as the planet).[^cite_note-MNGeog-60][^cite_note-autogenerated1-61] About 2.7 billion years ago [basaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) poured out of cracks in the floor of the primordial [ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean); the remains of this [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano) rock formed the [Canadian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Shield) in northeast Minnesota.[^cite_note-MNGeog-60][^cite_note-Compass-62] The roots of these volcanic mountains and the action of [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) seas formed the [Iron Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Range) of northern Minnesota. Since a period of [volcanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanism) 1.1 billion years ago, Minnesota's geological activity has been more subdued, with no volcanism or mountain formation, but with repeated incursions of the sea, which left behind multiple strata of [sedimentary rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock).[^cite_note-MNGeog-60]

In [more recent times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_history_of_Minnesota), massive ice sheets at least one kilometer thick ravaged the state's landscape and sculpted its terrain.[^cite_note-MNGeog-60] The [Wisconsin glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_glaciation) left 12,000 years ago.[^cite_note-MNGeog-60] These glaciers covered all of Minnesota except the far southeast, an area characterized by steep hills and streams that cut into the [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock). This area is known as the [Driftless Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulee_Region) for its absence of [glacial drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drift_\(geology\)).[^cite_note-63] Much of the remainder of the state has 50 feet (15 m) or more of [glacial till](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_till) left behind as the last glaciers retreated. Gigantic [Lake Agassiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Agassiz) formed in the northwest 13,000 years ago. Its flatbed now is the fertile [Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_of_the_North) valley, and its outflow, [glacial River Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_River_Warren), carved the valley of the [Minnesota River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_River) and the Upper Mississippi downstream from [Fort Snelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Snelling).[^cite_note-MNGeog-60] Minnesota is geologically quiet today; it experiences [earthquakes](10106.md) infrequently, most of them minor.[^cite_note-64]



[Palisade Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palisade_Head) on [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior) was formed from a [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) [rhyolitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyolitic) [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) flow.

[^cite_note-MNGeog-60]

The state's high point is [Eagle Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Mountain_\(Minnesota\)) at 2,301 feet (701 m), which is only 13 miles (21 km) away from the low point of 601 feet (183 m) at the shore of Lake Superior.[^cite_note-Compass-62][^cite_note-65] Notwithstanding dramatic local differences in elevation, much of the state is a gently rolling [peneplain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peneplain).[^cite_note-MNGeog-60]

Two major [drainage divides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_divide) meet in Minnesota's northeast in rural [Hibbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibbing,_Minnesota), forming a triple [watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin). [Precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) can follow the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) south to the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), the [Saint Lawrence Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_Seaway) east to the Atlantic Ocean, or the [Hudson Bay watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Hudson_Bay_drainage) to the Arctic Ocean.[^cite_note-66]

The state's nickname "Land of 10,000 Lakes" is apt, as there are 11,842 [Minnesota lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_lakes) over 10 acres (4 ha) in size.[^cite_note-dnr-67] Minnesota's portion of Lake Superior is the largest (at 962,700 acres (389,600 ha; 3,896 km<sup>2</sup>)) and deepest (at 1,290 ft (390 m)) body of water in the state.[^cite_note-dnr-67] Minnesota has 6,564 natural rivers and streams that cumulatively flow for 69,000 miles (111,000 km).[^cite_note-dnr-67] The Mississippi River begins its journey from its [headwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headwaters) at [Lake Itasca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Itasca) and crosses the Iowa border 680 miles (1,090 km) downstream.[^cite_note-dnr-67] It is joined by the [Minnesota River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_River) at Fort Snelling, by the [St. Croix River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Croix_River_\(Wisconsin-Minnesota\)) near [Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings,_Minnesota), by the [Chippewa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chippewa_River_\(Wisconsin\)) at [Wabasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabasha,_MN), and by many smaller streams. The Red River drains the northwest part of the state northward toward Canada's Hudson Bay. Approximately 10.6 million acres (4,300,000 ha; 43,000 km<sup>2</sup>) of wetlands are within Minnesota's borders, the most of any state outside Alaska.[^cite_note-weatheralmanac-68]

### Flora and fauna

Main article: [Natural history of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history_of_Minnesota)

Minnesota has four ecological provinces: [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie) parkland, in the southwestern and western parts of the state; the [eastern broadleaf forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_deciduous_forest) ([Big Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Woods)) in the southeast, extending in a narrowing strip to the state's northwestern part, where it transitions into [tallgrass aspen parkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallgrass_Aspen_Parkland); and the northern [Laurentian mixed forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurentian_Mixed_Forest_Province), a transitional forest between the northern [boreal forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiga) and the broadleaf forests to the south.[^cite_note-69] These northern forests are a vast wilderness of [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine) and [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce) trees mixed with patchy stands of [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch) and [poplar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populus).

Much of Minnesota's northern forest has undergone logging, leaving only a few patches of [old growth forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_growth_forest) today in areas such as the [Chippewa National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chippewa_National_Forest) and the [Superior National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_National_Forest), where the [Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Waters_Canoe_Area_Wilderness) has some 400,000 acres (162,000 ha) of unlogged land.[^cite_note-Heinselman-70] Although logging continues, regrowth and replanting keep about [a third of the state forested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover_by_state_and_territory_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-71] Nearly all Minnesota's prairies and [oak savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_savanna) have been fragmented by farming, grazing, logging, and suburban development.[^cite_note-72]

While loss of habitat has affected native animals such as the [pine marten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_marten), [elk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elk), [woodland caribou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migratory_Woodland_Caribou), and [bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_bison),[^cite_note-73] others like [whitetail deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitetail_deer) and [bobcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobcat) thrive. Minnesota has the nation's largest [population of timber wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repopulation_of_wolves_in_Midwestern_United_States) outside Alaska,[^cite_note-74] and supports healthy populations of [black bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_black_bear), [moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose), and [gophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopher). Located on the [Mississippi Flyway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_Flyway), Minnesota hosts migratory waterfowl such as [geese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose) and [ducks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck), and game birds such as [grouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouse), [pheasants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheasant), and [turkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_\(bird\)). It is home to [birds of prey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_of_prey), including the largest number of breeding pairs of [bald eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_eagle) in the lower 48 states as of 2007,[^cite_note-75] [red-tailed hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-tailed_Hawk), and [snowy owls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowy_owl). [Hawk Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawk_Ridge,_Duluth) is one of the premier birdwatching sites in North America. The lakes teem with sport fish such as [walleye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walleye), [bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_\(fish\)), [muskellunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskellunge), and [northern pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_pike), while [brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brook_trout), [brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_trout), and [rainbow trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_trout) populate streams in the southeast and northeast.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Minnesota)



Minnesota's [Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)

Minnesota experiences [temperature extremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Minnesota_weather_records) characteristic of its [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate), with cold winters and hot summers. The lowest temperature recorded was −60 °F (−51 °C) at [Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower,_Minnesota) on February 2, 1996. The highest was 114 °F (46 °C) at [Moorhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorhead,_Minnesota) on July 6, 1936.[^cite_note-extremes-76] Meteorological events include rain, snow, blizzards, thunderstorms, hail, [derechos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derecho), tornadoes, and high-velocity [straight-line winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downburst). The growing season varies from 90 days in the far northeast to 160 days in southeast Minnesota near the Mississippi River. Average temperatures range from 37 to 49 °F (3 to 9 °C).[^cite_note-noaa-77] Average summer [dewpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dew_point) range from about 58 °F (14 °C) in the south to about 48 °F (9 °C) in the north.[^cite_note-noaa-77][^cite_note-78] Average annual precipitation ranges from 19 to 35 inches (48 to 89 cm). Droughts occur every 10 to 50 years.[^cite_note-noaa-77]

Minnesota has been affected by climate change and warmed over the past few years. Rising temperatures have affected natural habitats and many species that live in them. For example, the lakes' water is warming, which affects fish populations: trout, a cold-water fish, is losing its habitat, while the habitat of bass, a warm-water fish, is growing.[^cite_note-79]

### Protected lands



Pose Lake in the [Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Waters_Canoe_Area_Wilderness)

Minnesota's first state park, [Itasca State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itasca_State_Park), was established in 1891, and is the [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_\(river_or_stream\)) of the Mississippi River.[^cite_note-81] Today Minnesota has [72 state parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Minnesota_state_parks) and recreation areas, [58 state forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Minnesota_state_forests) covering about four million acres (16,000 km<sup>2</sup>), and numerous state wildlife preserves, all managed by the [Minnesota Department of Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Department_of_Natural_Resources). The [Chippewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chippewa_National_Forest) and [Superior national forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_National_Forest) comprise 5.5 million acres (22,000 km<sup>2</sup>). The Superior National Forest in the northeast contains the [Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Waters_Canoe_Area_Wilderness), which encompasses over a million acres (4,000 km<sup>2</sup>) and a thousand lakes. To its west is [Voyageurs National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyageurs_National_Park). The [Mississippi National River and Recreation Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_National_River_and_Recreation_Area) (MNRRA) is a 72-mile-long (116 km) corridor along the Mississippi River through the Minneapolis–St. Paul Metropolitan Area connecting a variety of sites of historic, cultural, and geologic interest.[^cite_note-82]

## Cities and towns

See also: [List of cities in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Minnesota), [List of townships in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_townships_in_Minnesota), and [List of counties in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Minnesota)



[National Farmers Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Farmers_Bank) in [Owatonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owatonna,_Minnesota) by [Louis Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Sullivan)



[Minnesota cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Minnesota), [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Minnesota), and [towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_townships_in_Minnesota).

[Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota), in east-central Minnesota along the banks of the Mississippi River, has been Minnesota's [capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) since 1849, first as capital of the [Territory of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Minnesota), and then as the state capital since 1858.

Saint Paul is adjacent to Minnesota's most populous city, Minneapolis; they and their suburbs are collectively known as the [Twin Cities metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis%E2%80%93Saint_Paul#Combined_Statistical_Area), the country's 16th-largest metropolitan area and home to about 55% of the state's population.[^cite_note-83] The remainder of the state is known as "[Greater Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Minnesota)" or "Outstate Minnesota".[^cite_note-\:2-12]

The state has 17 cities with populations above 50,000 as of the 2020 census. In descending order of population, they are [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis,_Minnesota), [Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota), [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Minnesota), [Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth,_Minnesota), [Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Minnesota), [Brooklyn Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Park,_Minnesota), [Plymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth,_Minnesota), [Saint Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Cloud,_Minnesota), [Woodbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodbury,_Minnesota), [Eagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagan,_Minnesota), [Maple Grove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_Grove,_Minnesota), [Coon Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coon_Rapids,_Minnesota), [Eden Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Prairie,_Minnesota), [Minnetonka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnetonka,_MN), [Burnsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnsville,_Minnesota), [Apple Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Valley,_Minnesota), [Blaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaine,_Minnesota), and [Lakeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeville,_Minnesota).[^cite_note-popest-84] Of these, only Rochester, Duluth, and Saint Cloud are outside the Twin Cities metropolitan area.

Minnesota's population continues to grow, primarily in the urban centers. The populations of metropolitan [Sherburne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherburne_County,_Minnesota) and [Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_County,_Minnesota) counties doubled between 1980 and 2000, while 40 of the state's 87 counties lost residents over the same period.[^cite_note-EIA-85]&#x20;

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) and the Minnesota State Demographic Center, Minnesota had a population of about 5.7 million in 2020, making it the 22nd-most populous U.S. state.[^cite_note-PopEstUS-89] Its fertility rate in 2021 was slightly below the replacement rate at 1.75, but the state has seen growth over the past century through more births than deaths, and significant immigration.[^cite_note-Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention_2018_y464-90] A destination for European immigrants in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, primarily from [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), [Germany](11867.md), and [Ireland](147575.md), it now attracts people from [Latin America](18524.md), primarily [Mexico](3966054.md); [East Africa](260987.md), particularly [Somalis](1571696.md) and [Ethiopians](6204551.md); and [South](21566765.md) and [Southeast Asia](28741.md), especially [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_people), [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people), [Karen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_people), and [Indian people](7564733.md).[^cite_note-Immigration_Language_MN-8] The state has a diverse population in terms of age, birthplace, ancestry, and socioeconomic status, with a well-educated populace and a median household income around \$77,000.[^cite_note-Census_Bureau_QuickFacts_2022_f936-91]

As of 2020, according to U.S. census data, the white population had fallen to 77.5% from over 98% in the early to mid-20th century.[^cite_note-92] Concurrently, other racial populations have markedly increased. The Black population has risen to 7%, the Asian population to 5.3%, and those identifying as two or more races to 6.1%.

### Immigration

Since the 1960s, Minnesota's immigrant population has been shaped by its status as a major area for refugee resettlement. As of 2018, Minnesota had the largest refugee population per capita of any state, with 2% of the country's population but 13% of its refugees.[^cite_note-100] The largest groups of refugees over the past decades have been Hmongs, Somalis, Ethiopians, and Vietnamese; other major refugee groups that have recently been settling in Minnesota include Burmese, Liberians, Ecuadorians, Congolese, Russians, and Ukrainians.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] Minnesota also receives large numbers of non-refugee immigrants, primarily from Mexico, India, China, Korea, and Canada.

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Minnesota)



The [French Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Renaissance_architecture) style [Cathedral of St. Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Paul,_National_Shrine_of_the_Apostle_Paul) in the city of St. Paul

Minnesota's religious landscape is also diverse, having evolved significantly over its history. The area's first Christian influence came from Catholic missionaries in the 17th and 18th centuries. 19th-century European settlers, especially Scandinavians, established Protestant denominations, particularly [Lutheranism](23371382.md). [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) also continued to be significant due to Irish immigrants, and the [Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Saint_Paul_and_Minneapolis) serves a substantial Catholic community. The 20th and 21st centuries witnessed growth in other Christian denominations and non-Christian religions due to further immigration, leading to the establishment of [Buddhist](3267529.md), [Hmong folk religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_folk_religion), [Muslim](6037917.md), and [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) communities, as well as a sizable [Jewish](15624.md) community.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-autogenerated2-107] A growing number of people identify as [non-religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion), in line with national trends. As of 2014, 74% of Minnesotans identified as Christian, 5% belonged to non-Christian faiths, and 20% identified as religiously unaffiliated, according to the Pew Research Center.[^cite_note-108]

### Languages

Further information: [Category\:Indigenous languages of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Indigenous_languages_of_Minnesota)

Minnesota does not have an official language, although [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) is dominant, spoken by about 90% of residents.[^cite_note-Bureau-109] Other languages spoken include [Spanish](26825.md), [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language), [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_language), [Vietnamese](32511.md), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic), and [Karen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karenic_languages).[^cite_note-Immigration_Language_MN-8]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Minnesota)

See also: [List of Minnesota locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Minnesota_locations_by_per_capita_income)

Once primarily a producer of raw materials, Minnesota's economy has transformed to emphasize finished products and services. Perhaps the most significant characteristic of the economy is its diversity; the relative outputs of its business sectors closely match the United States as a whole.[^cite_note-110]

In 2025, Minnesota's [gross domestic product](12594.md) was \$531.4 billion,[^cite_note-111] with 33 of the United States' top 1,000 publicly traded companies by revenue headquartered in Minnesota,[^cite_note-112] including [Target](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_Corporation), [UnitedHealth Group](1845551.md), [3M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3M), [General Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Mills), [U.S. Bancorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bancorp), [Ameriprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ameriprise), [Hormel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormel), [Land O' Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_O'_Lakes), [SuperValu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SuperValu_\(United_States\)), [Best Buy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Buy), and [Valspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valspar). Private companies based in Minnesota include [Cargill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill), the largest privately owned company in the United States,[^cite_note-113] and [Carlson Companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlson_Companies), the parent company of [Radisson Hotels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radisson_Hotels).[^cite_note-114]

Minnesota's [per capita personal income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_income) was \$78,538 in 2025,[^cite_note-115] the 13th-highest in the nation.[^cite_note-116] Its 2019 [median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_household_income) was \$74,593, ranking thirteenth in the U.S. and fifth among the 36 states not on the Atlantic coast.[^cite_note-117] As of May 2025, the [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rate) was 3.3%.[^cite_note-118]

### Industry and commerce



The [IDS Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDS_Tower), designed by [Philip Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Johnson), is the state's tallest building,

[^cite_note-119]

&#x20;reflecting [César Pelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Pelli)'s [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco)-style [Wells Fargo Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo_Center_\(Minneapolis\)).

Minnesota's earliest industries were fur trading and agriculture. Minneapolis grew around the flour mills powered by [St. Anthony Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anthony_Falls). Although less than 1% of the population is now employed in the agricultural sector,[^cite_note-120] it remains a major part of the state's economy, ranking sixth in the nation in the value of products sold.[^cite_note-121] The state is the nation's largest producer of [sugar beets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet), [sweet corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_corn), [peas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pea) for processing, and farm-raised [turkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_\(bird\)). Minnesota is a large producer of corn and soybeans,[^cite_note-resources-122] and has the most food [cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_food_cooperatives#United_States) per capita in the United States.[^cite_note-123]

[Forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry) remains strong, including [logging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging), [pulpwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulpwood) processing and paper production, and forest products manufacturing. Minnesota was famous for its soft-ore mines, which produced a significant portion of the world's [iron ore](145352.md) for more than a century. Although the high-grade ore is now depleted, [taconite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taconite) mining continues, using processes developed locally to save the industry. In 2016 the state produced 60% of the country's usable iron ore.[^cite_note-resources-122] The mining boom created the port of Duluth, which continues to be important for shipping ore, coal, and agricultural products.

The manufacturing sector now includes technology and [biomedical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical) firms, in addition to the older food processors and heavy industry. The nation's first indoor [shopping mall](235723.md) was [Edina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edina,_Minnesota)'s [Southdale Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southdale_Center), and its largest is Bloomington's [Mall of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mall_of_America). Minnesota is one of 45 U.S. states with its [own lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Lottery); its games include [multi-jurisdiction draws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-State_Lottery_Association) and in-house draws.

### Energy use and production

Further information: [List of power stations in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Minnesota)

Minnesota produces [ethanol fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel) and is the first to mandate its use, a 10% mix ([E10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E10_fuel)).[^cite_note-125] In 2019, there were more than 411 service stations supplying [E85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E85) fuel, comprising 85% ethanol and 15% gasoline.[^cite_note-126] A 2% [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel) blend has been required in [diesel fuel](81761.md) since 2005. Minnesota is ranked in the top ten for wind energy production. In 2018, Minnesota generated nearly one-fifth of its electrical energy from wind.[^cite_note-127]

[Xcel Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xcel_Energy) is the state's largest utility and is headquartered in the state;[^cite_note-128] it is one of five investor-owned utilities.[^cite_note-\:0-129] There are also a number of municipal utilities.[^cite_note-\:0-129] There are 44 electric distribution cooperatives serving retail electric consumers throughout the state.[^cite_note-130]

### State taxes

&#x20;Minnesota has a [progressive income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_income_tax) structure. The four brackets of state [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) rates are 5.35%, 7.05%, 7.85%, and 9.85%.[^cite_note-131] In 2025, Minnesota ranked 10th in the nation in per capita total state and local taxes.[^cite_note-TF2025-132] In 2022, Minnesotans paid 12.1% of their income in state and local taxes; the U.S. average was 11.2%.[^cite_note-TF2025burden-133] The state [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) in Minnesota is 6.875%. Clothing, prescription drug medications and food items for home consumption are exempt.[^cite_note-salestax-134] The [state legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Legislature) may allow municipalities to institute local sales taxes and special local taxes, such as the 0.5% supplemental sales tax in Minneapolis.[^cite_note-135] [Excise](11404033.md) taxes are levied on alcohol, tobacco, and motor fuel. The state imposes a [use tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_tax) on items purchased elsewhere but used in Minnesota.[^cite_note-salestax-134] Owners of [real property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_property) in Minnesota pay [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) to their county, municipality, school district, and special taxing districts.

### Literature

The rigors and rewards of pioneer life on the [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie) are the subject of [*Giants in the Earth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_in_the_Earth_\(novel\)) by [Ole Rolvaag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Rolvaag) and the [*Little House*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_House_on_the_Prairie) series of children's books by [Laura Ingalls Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Ingalls_Wilder). Small-town life is portrayed grimly by [Sinclair Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinclair_Lewis) in the novel [*Main Street*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Street_\(novel\)), and more gently and affectionately by [Garrison Keillor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison_Keillor) in his tales of [Lake Wobegon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Wobegon).

St. Paul native [F. Scott Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Scott_Fitzgerald) wrote of the social insecurities and aspirations of the young city in stories such as *[Winter Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Dreams)* and *The Ice Palace*, published in *[Flappers and Philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flappers_and_Philosophers)*. [Henry Wadsworth Longfellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wadsworth_Longfellow)'s epic poem *[The Song of Hiawatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Song_of_Hiawatha)* was inspired by Minnesota and names of many of the state's places and bodies of water. Minnesota native [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) won the 2016 [Nobel Prize in Literature](23385442.md). Science fiction writer [Marissa Lingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Lingen) lives here.

### Popular culture

See also: [List of television shows and movies in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_shows_and_movies_in_Minnesota)



A youth [fiddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddle) performance at the [Minnesota State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Fair).

Stereotypical traits of Minnesotans include "[Minnesota nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_nice)", [Lutheranism](23371382.md), a strong sense of community and shared culture, and a distinctive brand of [North Central American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_American_English) featuring [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) expressions. [Potlucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potluck), usually with a variety of [hotdishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotdish), are popular small-town church activities. A small segment of the Scandinavian population attend a traditional [lutefisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutefisk) dinner to celebrate Christmas.

Life in Minnesota has also been depicted or used as a backdrop, in movies such as *[Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo_\(1996_film\))*, *[Grumpy Old Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumpy_Old_Men_\(film\))*, *[Grumpier Old Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumpier_Old_Men)*, *[Juno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_\(film\))*, *[Drop Dead Gorgeous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_Dead_Gorgeous_\(film\))*, *[Young Adult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Adult_\(film\))*, *[A Serious Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Serious_Man)*, *[New in Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_in_Town)*, *[Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_\(2011_film\))*, *[The Mighty Ducks films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mighty_Ducks_\(film_series\)),* and in famous television series like *[Little House on the Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_House_on_the_Prairie_\(1974_TV_series\))*, *[The Mary Tyler Moore Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mary_Tyler_Moore_Show)*, *[The Golden Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Golden_Girls)*, *[Coach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_\(TV_series\))*, *[The Rocky and Bullwinkle Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rocky_and_Bullwinkle_Show)*, *[How I Met Your Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_I_Met_Your_Mother)* and *[Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo_\(TV_series\))*. Major movies shot on location in Minnesota include *[That Was Then... This Is Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Was_Then..._This_Is_Now)*, *[Purple Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Rain_\(film\))*, *[Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_\(1970_film\))*, *[Beautiful Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Girls_\(film\))*, *[North Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Country_\(film\))*, *[Untamed Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untamed_Heart)*, *[Feeling Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeling_Minnesota)*, *[Jingle All The Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingle_All_The_Way)*, and *[A Simple Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Simple_Plan_\(film\))*.

The [Minnesota State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Fair), advertised as *The Great Minnesota Get-Together*, is an icon of state culture. In a state of 5.5 million people, there were more than 1.8 million visitors to the fair in 2014, setting a new attendance record.[^cite_note-139] The fair covers the variety of Minnesota life, including [fine art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_art), [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), food preparation, [4-H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-H) displays, music, [the midway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midway_\(fair\)), and corporate merchandising. It is known for its displays of [seed art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_art), [butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter) sculptures of [dairy princesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Kay_of_the_Milky_Way), the birthing barn, and the "fattest pig" competition. In September 1927, [John Philip Sousa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Philip_Sousa) and his band gave the premiere performance of "[The Minnesota March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Minnesota_March)" at the fair before a grandstand crowd of 12,000.[^cite_note-\:03-140] One can also find dozens of varieties of food on a stick, such as [Pronto Pups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pronto_Pup), [cheese curds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_curds#Fried_cheese_curds), and deep-fried candy bars. On a smaller scale, many of these attractions are offered at numerous county fairs.

Other large annual festivals include the [Saint Paul Winter Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul_Winter_Carnival), the [Minnesota Renaissance Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Renaissance_Festival), Minneapolis' [Aquatennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_Aquatennial) and Mill City Music Festival, [Moondance Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moondance_Jam) in [Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker,_Minnesota), the [Judy Garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Garland) Festival in [Grand Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Rapids,_Minnesota), the [Eelpout Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eelpout_Festival) on Leech Lake, and the [WE Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WE_Fest) in [Detroit Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Lakes,_Minnesota).

## Health



The [Mayo Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_Clinic) in [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Minnesota)

Minnesotans have low rates of premature death, infant mortality, cardiovascular disease, and occupational fatalities.[^cite_note-UHC-141][^cite_note-142] They have long life expectancies,[^cite_note-143] and high rates of health insurance and regular exercise.[^cite_note-UHC-141][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-\:5-145] These and other measures have led two groups to rank Minnesota as the healthiest state in the nation; however, in one of these rankings, Minnesota descended from first to sixth in the nation between 2005 and 2009 because of low levels of public health funding and the prevalence of [binge drinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binge_drinking).[^cite_note-UHC-141][^cite_note-146] While overall health indicators are strong, Minnesota has significant health disparities in minority populations.[^cite_note-147]

In October 2007, the [Freedom to Breathe Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_to_Breathe_Act) took effect, outlawing smoking in restaurants and bars in Minnesota.[^cite_note-148]

The [Minnesota Department of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Department_of_Health) is the primary state health agency responsible for public policy and regulation. Medical care in the state is provided by a comprehensive network of hospitals and clinics operated by a number of large providers including [Allina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allina) Hospitals & Clinics, [CentraCare Health System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CentraCare_Health_System), [Essentia Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essentia_Health), [HealthPartners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HealthPartners), [M Health Fairview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M_Health_Fairview) and the [Mayo Clinic Health System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_Clinic_Health_System). There are two teaching hospitals and medical schools in Minnesota. The [University of Minnesota Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Medical_School) is a high-rated teaching institution that has made a number of breakthroughs in treatment, and its research activities contribute significantly to the state's growing [biotechnology](4502.md) industry.[^cite_note-149] The [Mayo Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_Clinic), a world-renowned hospital based in Rochester, was founded by [William Worrall Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Worrall_Mayo), an immigrant from England.[^cite_note-Welbourn1990-150][^cite_note-Haddy2011-151]

*U.S. News & World Report*'s 2020–21 survey ranked 4,554 hospitals in the country in 12 specialized fields of care, and placed the Mayo Clinic in the top four in most fields. The hospital ranked first on the best hospitals honor roll. The only specialty where it fell outside the top ten was ophthalmology.[^cite_note-152] The Mayo Clinic and the University of Minnesota are partners in the Minnesota Partnership for Biotechnology and Medical Genomics, a state-funded program that conducts research into [cancer](105219.md), [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease), [heart health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_heart_disease), [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity), and other areas.[^cite_note-153]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Minnesota)

See also: [List of colleges and universities in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Minnesota), [List of high schools in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_Minnesota), and [List of school districts in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Minnesota)



The [Richardsonian Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richardsonian_Romanesque) Pillsbury Hall (1889) is one of the oldest buildings on the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) Minneapolis campus.

One of the first acts of the Minnesota Legislature when it opened in 1858 was the creation of a [normal school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_school) in Winona. Minnesota's commitment to education has contributed to a literate and well-educated populace. In 2009, according to the U.S. Census Bureau, Minnesota had the second-highest proportion of high school graduates, with 91.5% of people 25 and older holding a [high school diploma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_diploma), and the tenth-highest proportion of people with bachelor's degrees.[^cite_note-154]

In 2015, Minneapolis was named the nation's "Most Literate City", while St. Paul placed fourth, according to a major annual survey.[^cite_note-155] In a 2013 study conducted by the National Center for Educational Statistics comparing the performance of eighth-grade students internationally in math and science, Minnesota ranked eighth in the world and third in the United States, behind Massachusetts and Vermont.[^cite_note-156]

In 2014, Minnesota students had the nation's tenth-highest average composite score on the [ACT exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_\(examination\)).[^cite_note-157] In 2013, Minnesota ranked 21st nationwide in per-student public education spending.[^cite_note-158] Minnesota has chosen not to implement [school vouchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_voucher),[^cite_note-vouchers-159] but is home to the first [charter school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_school).[^cite_note-Charter-160]

The state supports a network of public universities and colleges, including 37 institutions in the [Minnesota State Colleges and Universities System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Colleges_and_Universities_System) and five major campuses of the [University of Minnesota system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_system). It is also home to more than 20 private colleges and universities, six of which rank among the nation's top 100 [liberal arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts) colleges, according to *[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report)*.[^cite_note-161]

## Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Minnesota)



The [Aerial Lift Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_Lift_Bridge) in [Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth,_Minnesota)

Transportation in Minnesota is overseen by the [Minnesota Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Department_of_Transportation) (MnDOT) at the state level and by regional and local governments at the local level. Principal transportation corridors radiate from the [Twin Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities) metropolitan area and along interstate corridors in Greater Minnesota. The major [Interstate highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System) are [Interstate 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_35_in_Minnesota) (I-35), [I-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90_in_Minnesota), and [I-94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_94_in_Minnesota), with I-35 and I-94 connecting the Minneapolis–St. Paul area, and I-90 traveling east–west along the state's southern edge.[^cite_note-162]

In 2006, a [constitutional amendment](261586.md) was passed that required sales and use taxes on motor vehicles to fund transportation, with at least 40% dedicated to [public transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transit).[^cite_note-163] There are nearly two dozen [rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) corridors in Minnesota, most of which go through Minneapolis–St. Paul or Duluth.[^cite_note-164] There is water transportation along the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) system and from the ports of [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior).[^cite_note-165]



Two [Metro Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Green_Line_\(Minnesota\)) trains on the University of Minnesota–Twin Cities campus

Minnesota's principal airport is [Minneapolis–St. Paul International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis%E2%80%93St._Paul_International_Airport) (MSP), a major passenger and freight hub for [Delta Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines) and [Sun Country Airlines](1241139.md). Most other domestic carriers serve the airport. Large commercial jet service is provided at Duluth and Rochester, with scheduled commuter service to four smaller cities via [Delta Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Connection) carriers [SkyWest Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyWest_Airlines), [Compass Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_Airlines_\(North_America\)), and [Endeavor Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endeavor_Air).[^cite_note-DeltaRoutes-166]

Public transit services are available in the regional urban centers in Minnesota including [Metro Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Transit_\(Minnesota\)) in the Twin Cities, opt-out suburban operators [Minnesota Valley Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Valley_Transit_Authority), [SouthWest Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SouthWest_Transit), [Plymouth Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Metrolink), [Maple Grove Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_Grove_Transit) and others. In Greater Minnesota transit services are provided by city systems such as [Duluth Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth_Transit_Authority), [Mankato Transit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankato_Transit_System), [MATBUS (Fargo-Moorhead)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MATBUS_\(Fargo-Moorhead\)), [Rochester Public Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Minnesota#Transportation), [Saint Cloud Metro Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Cloud,_Minnesota#Transportation), Winona Public Transit and others. Dial-a-Ride service is available for persons with disabilities in a majority of Minnesota counties.[^cite_note-167]

Two daily [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) trains provide [inter-city rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail) service. The *[Empire Builder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Builder)* (Chicago–Seattle/Portland) train runs through Minnesota, calling at the [Saint Paul Union Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul_Union_Depot) and five other stations.[^cite_note-168] The [Borealis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borealis_\(train\)) (Chicago–Saint Paul) train runs from [Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota) to [Chicago](6886.md), on a portion of the tracks used by the Empire Builder.[^cite_note-169] [Intercity bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_bus_service) providers include [Jefferson Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Lines), [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Bus_Lines), and [Megabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabus_\(North_America\)). Local public transit is provided by [bus](4146.md) networks in the larger cities and by two rail services. From [Target Field station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_Field_station) in downtown Minneapolis, [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) runs to Saint Paul Union Depot on the [Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Minnesota\)), and to the MSP airport and the [Mall of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mall_of_America) via the [Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Minneapolis-St._Paul_Metro\)).

## Law and government

See also: [Law of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Minnesota)



The [historical coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_coats_of_arms_of_the_U.S._states) of Minnesota in 1876



Logo of the Minnesotan government

Minnesota is governed pursuant to the [Minnesota Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Constitution), which was adopted on October 13, 1857, roughly one year before statehood.[^cite_note-\:1-170] Like all U.S. states and the federal government, Minnesota has a [republican system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism_in_the_United_States) of political representation with power divided into three branches: executive, legislative, and judicial.[^cite_note-171] The state constitution includes a bill of rights that reaffirms many of the same rights and freedoms as its [federal counterpart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bill_of_Rights), with some protected more strongly and explicitly.[^cite_note-\:1-170]

### Executive

Main article: [Governor of Minnesota](214877.md)



Governor [Tim Walz](2216593.md)

The executive branch is led by Minnesota's [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_\(United_States\)), currently [Tim Walz](2216593.md), a [DFLer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic%E2%80%93Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party) who took office on January 7, 2019. Walz was also [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris)'s running mate in the [2024 United States presidential election](59568546.md). As chief executive, the [governor](214877.md) appoints the heads of state agencies and is responsible for faithful execution of the law. As commander-in-chief of the state's armed forces, the governor also has command and control over the [Minnesota National Guard](4562088.md).[^cite_note-Article_V,_Minnesota_Constitution-172] A [cabinet](214877.md#Cabinet) consisting of the [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Minnesota) and the heads of Minnesota's 22 state agencies consults and assists the governor in the business of state government.[^cite_note-173]

Aside from the governor and lieutenant governor, who are elected on a [joint ticket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_ticket), Minnesotans separately elect three other constitutional officers: a [secretary of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_of_Minnesota), an [attorney general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Attorney_General), and a [state auditor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Auditor).[^cite_note-Article_V,_Minnesota_Constitution-172][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-177][^cite_note-181] These five "executive officers" together constitute the [Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Executive_Council), which has certain statutory responsibilities in matters of state finance, emergency management, and public lands administration.[^cite_note-182]

### Legislature

Main article: [Minnesota Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Legislature)



The [Minnesota State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Capitol) in Saint Paul, designed by [Cass Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cass_Gilbert)

The [Minnesota Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Legislature) is a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) body consisting of the [Minnesota Senate](254415.md) and the [Minnesota House of Representatives](254414.md). The state has 67 legislative districts, each with about 85,000 people. Each district has one senator and two representatives, with each senatorial district divided into *A* and *B* sections for members of the House. Representatives serve two-year terms, and senators are up for election every four years and in the first state election after a census.

After the 2022 state elections, both the House and Senate had a slim DFL majority.[^cite_note-Faircloth_2022-183] Since the [2024 Minnesota House of Representatives election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Minnesota_House_of_Representatives_election), the House has had 67 DFLers and 67 Republicans.

### Judiciary

Minnesota's court system has three levels. Most cases start in the [district courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_District_Courts), which are courts of general jurisdiction. There are 279 district court judgeships in ten judicial districts. Appeals from the trial courts and challenges to certain governmental decisions are heard by the [Minnesota Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Court_of_Appeals), consisting of 19 judges who typically sit in three-judge panels. The seven-justice [Minnesota Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Supreme_Court) hears all appeals from the tax court, the [workers' compensation court of appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Workers'_Compensation_Court_of_Appeals), first-degree murder convictions, and [discretionary appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certiorari#State_courts) from the court of appeals; it also has [original jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction) over election disputes.[^cite_note-184]

Two specialized courts within administrative agencies have been established: the workers' compensation court of appeals, and the tax court, which deals with non-criminal tax cases.

### Regional

In addition to the city and county levels of government found in the United States, Minnesota has other entities that provide governmental oversight and planning. [Regional development commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Development_Commissions) (RDCs) provide technical assistance to local governments in the broad multi-county areas of the state. Along with this [Metropolitan Planning Organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Planning_Organizations) (MPOs), such as the [Metropolitan Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Council_\(Minnesota\)), provide planning and oversight of land use actions in metropolitan areas. Many lakes and rivers are overseen by [watershed districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watershed_district_\(Minnesota\)) and [soil and water conservation districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_and_water_conservation_district).

### Tribal

The State of Minnesota was created by the United States federal government in the traditional and cultural range of lands occupied by the [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people) and [Anishinaabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anishinaabe) peoples as well as other Native American groups. After many years of unequal treaties and forced resettlement by the state and federal government, the tribes re-organized into sovereign tribal governments. Today, the tribal governments are divided into 11 semi-autonomous [reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reservation) that negotiate with the U.S. and the state on a bilateral basis:

Four Dakota [Mdewakanton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mdewakanton) communities:

- [Prairie Island Indian Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Island_Indian_Community)

- [Shakopee Mdewakanton Sioux Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakopee_Mdewakanton_Sioux_Community)

- [Lower Sioux Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Sioux_Indian_Reservation)

- [Upper Sioux Community – Pejuhutazizi Oyate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Sioux_Community,_Minnesota)

Seven Anishinaabe reservations:

- [Bois Forte Band of Chippewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bois_Forte_Band_of_Chippewa)

- [Fond du Lac Band of Lake Superior Chippewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fond_du_Lac_Band_of_Lake_Superior_Chippewa)

- [Grand Portage Band of Chippewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Portage_Band_of_Chippewa)

- [Leech Lake Band of Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leech_Lake_Band_of_Ojibwe)

- [Mille Lacs Band of Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mille_Lacs_Band_of_Ojibwe)

- [White Earth Band of Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Earth_Band_of_Ojibwe)

- [Red Lake Band of Chippewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Lake_Band_of_Chippewa)

The first six of the Anishinaabe bands compose the [Minnesota Chippewa Tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Chippewa_Tribe), the collective federally recognized tribal government of the Bois Forte, Fond du Lac, Grand Portage, Leech Lake, Mille Lacs, and White Earth reservations.

Minnesota is known for a politically active citizenry, and [populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism) has been a long-standing force among the state's [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party).[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188] Minnesota has a consistently high [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout). In the [2008 U.S. presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election), 78.2% of eligible Minnesotans voted – the highest percentage of any U.S. state – versus the national average of 61.2%.[^cite_note-189] That figure was surpassed in 2020, when 79.96% of registered voters participated in the general election.[^cite_note-190] Voters can register on [election day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Day_\(United_States\)) at their [polling places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polling_places) with evidence of residency.[^cite_note-191]

[Hubert Humphrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey) brought national attention to the state with his address at the [1948 Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Democratic_National_Convention). Minnesotans have consistently cast their Electoral College votes for Democratic presidential candidates since 1976, longer than any other state. Minnesota is the only state in the nation that did not vote for [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) in either of his presidential campaigns. Minnesota has voted for the Democratic nominee in all but one presidential election since 1960 (Republican [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) won Minnesota in 1972).

Both the Democratic and Republican parties have major-party status in Minnesota, but its state-level Democratic party has a different name, officially known as the [Minnesota Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Democratic%E2%80%93Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party) (DFL). It was formed out of a 1944 alliance of the Minnesota Democratic and [Farmer–Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Farmer%E2%80%93Labor_Party) parties.

The state has had active third-party movements. The [Reform Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Party_of_the_United_States), now the [Independence Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Party_of_Minnesota), was able to elect former mayor of [Brooklyn Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Park,_Minnesota) and [professional wrestler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestler) [Jesse Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Ventura) to the [governorship in 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_gubernatorial_election,_1998). The [Independence Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Party_of_Minnesota) has received enough support to keep major-party status. The [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Minnesota), while no longer having major-party status, has a large presence in municipal government,[^cite_note-GrnOffHldrs-192] notably in Minneapolis and Duluth, where it competes directly with the DFL party for local offices. Major-party status in Minnesota (which grants state funding for elections) is reserved for parties whose candidates receive five percent or more of the vote in any statewide election (e.g., governor, secretary of state, U.S. president).

The state's [U.S. Senate](24909346.md) seats was split in the early 1990s and in the [108th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/108th_United_States_Congress) and [109th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/109th_United_States_Congress) Congresses, Minnesota's congressional delegation was split, with four representatives and one senator from each party. In the 2006 mid-term election, Democrats were elected to all state offices, except governor and lieutenant governor, where Republicans [Tim Pawlenty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Pawlenty) and [Carol Molnau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Molnau) narrowly won reelection.

The DFL posted double-digit gains in both houses of the legislature, elected Amy Klobuchar to the U.S. Senate, and increased the party's U.S. House caucus by one. Keith Ellison was the first [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) U.S. representative elected from Minnesota and the first [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) elected to Congress nationwide.[^cite_note-193] In 2008, DFLer and former comedian and radio talk show host [Al Franken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Franken) defeated incumbent Republican [Norm Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_Coleman) in the U.S. Senate race by 312 votes out of three million cast.

In the 2010 election, Republicans took control of both chambers of the Minnesota legislature for the first time in 38 years and, with Mark Dayton's election, the DFL party took the governor's office for the first time in 20 years. Two years later, the DFL regained control of both houses, and with Dayton in office, the party had same-party control of both the legislative and executive branches for the first time since 1990. Two years later, the Republicans regained control of the Minnesota House,[^cite_note-194] and in 2016, the GOP also regained control of the State Senate.[^cite_note-195]

In 2018, the DFL retook control of the Minnesota House, while electing DFLer [Tim Walz](2216593.md) as Governor.

In a 2020 study, Minnesota was ranked as the 15th easiest state for citizens to vote in.[^cite_note-196]

## Media

Main article: [List of media in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_media_in_Minnesota)

See also: [List of newspapers in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_Minnesota)



[KSTP studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSTP-TV)

The Twin Cities area is the fifteenth largest [media market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_market) in the United States, as ranked by [Nielsen Media Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research). The state's other top markets are [Fargo–Moorhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo%E2%80%93Moorhead) (118th nationally), [Duluth–Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Ports) (137th), Rochester–Mason City–Austin (152nd), and Mankato (200th).[^cite_note-197]

[Broadcast television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_television) in Minnesota and the Upper Midwest started on April 27, 1948, when [KSTP-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSTP-TV) began broadcasting.[^cite_note-198] [Hubbard Broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubbard_Broadcasting), which owns KSTP, is now the only locally owned television company in Minnesota. Twin Cities [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) station [WCCO-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCCO-TV) and [FOX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company) station [KMSP-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KMSP-TV) are [owned-and-operated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owned-and-operated) by their respective networks. There are [39 analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_stations_in_Minnesota_\(by_channel_number\)) broadcast stations and 23 [digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_television) channels broadcast over Minnesota.

The four largest daily newspapers are the *[Star Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Tribune)* in Minneapolis, the *[Pioneer Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Pioneer_Press)* in Saint Paul, the *[Duluth News Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth_News_Tribune)* in Duluth, and the *[Post-Bulletin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Bulletin)* in Rochester. *[The Minnesota Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Daily)* is the largest student-run newspaper in the U.S.[^cite_note-199] Sites offering daily news on the Web include *[The UpTake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_UpTake)*, *[MinnPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MinnPost.com)*, the Twin Cities *Daily Planet*, business news site *[Finance and Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_&_Commerce)* and Washington D.C.–based *[Minnesota Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Independent)*. Weekly and monthly publications such as *Minnesota Monthly* are available.

Two of the largest [public radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_radio) networks, [Minnesota Public Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Public_Radio) (MPR) and [Public Radio International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Radio_International) (PRI), are based in the state. MPR has the largest audience of any regional public radio network in the nation, broadcasting on 46 radio stations as of 2019.[^cite_note-200][^cite_note-201] PRI weekly provides more than 400 hours of programming to almost 800 affiliates.[^cite_note-202] The state's oldest radio station, [KUOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KUOM)-AM, was launched in 1922 and is among the 10-[oldest radio stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_radio_stations) in the United States. The University of Minnesota-owned station is still on the air, and since 1993 broadcasts a [college rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_radio) format.

## Sports, recreation and tourism

Minnesota has an active program of organized amateur and professional sports. Tourism has become an important industry, especially in the Lake region. In the North Country, what had been an industrial area focused on mining and timber has largely been transformed into a vacation destination. Popular interest in the environment and [environmentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism), added to traditional interests in [hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting) and fishing, has attracted a large urban audience within driving range.[^cite_note-203]

Humphrey Metrodome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_H._Humphrey_Metrodome) from 1982 until its demolition after the 2013 season for the construction of the team's new and current home, [U.S. Bank Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bank_Stadium).[^cite_note-204] This stadium hosted [Super Bowl LII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LII) in February 2018. [Super Bowl XXVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVI) was played in the Metrodome in 1992. The Vikings have advanced to the Super Bowl [Super Bowl IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_IV), [Super Bowl VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_VIII), [Super Bowl IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_IX), and [Super Bowl XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XI), losing all four games to their AFC/AFL opponent.

The [Minnesota Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Twins) have played in the Major League Baseball in the Twin Cities since 1961. The Twins began play as the original [Washington Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Twins), a founding member of the American League in 1901, relocating to Minnesota in 1961. The Twins won the 1987 and [1991 World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_World_Series) in seven-game matches where the home team was victorious in all games. The Twins also advanced to the [1965 World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_World_Series), where they lost to the [Los Angeles Dodgers](18213.md) in seven games. The team has played at [Target Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_Field) since 2010.

The [Minneapolis Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_Lakers) of the National Basketball Association played in the [Minneapolis Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_Auditorium) from 1947 to 1960, after which they relocated to Los Angeles. The [Minnesota Timberwolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Timberwolves) joined the NBA in 1989, and have played in [Target Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_Center) since 1990.

The National Hockey League's [Minnesota Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Wild) play in St. Paul's [Grand Casino Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Casino_Arena), and reached 300 consecutive sold-out games on January 16, 2008.[^cite_note-205] Previously, the [Minnesota North Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_North_Stars) competed in the NHL from 1967 to 1993, which played in and lost the 1981 and 1991 [Stanley Cup Finals](3314237.md).

[Minnesota United FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_United_FC) joined [Major League Soccer](71802.md) as an expansion team in 2017, having played in the lower-division [North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_\(2010\)) from 2010 to 2016. The team plays at [Allianz Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz_Field) in St. Paul.[^cite_note-206] Previous professional soccer teams have included the [Minnesota Kicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Kicks), which played at [Metropolitan Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Stadium) from 1976 to 1981, and the [Minnesota Strikers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Strikers) from 1984 to 1988.

Minnesota also has minor-league professional sports teams. The [Minnesota Swarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Swarm) of the [National Lacrosse League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lacrosse_League) played at the Xcel Energy Center until the team moved to Georgia in 2015. The [St. Paul Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul_Saints), who play at [CHS Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHS_Field) in St. Paul, are the [Triple-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple-A_\(baseball\)) minor league affiliate of the Minnesota Twins.

Professional women's sports include the [Minnesota Lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Lynx) of the Women's National Basketball Association, winners of the 2011, 2013, 2015, and 2017 WNBA Championships, [Minnesota Aurora FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Aurora_FC) of the [United Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Soccer_League) [W-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_W-League_\(1995%E2%80%932015\)), the [Minnesota Vixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Vixen) of the [Independent Women's Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Women's_Football_League), the [Minnesota Valkyrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Valkyrie) of the Legends Football League, the [Minnesota Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Frost) of the [Professional Women's Hockey League](74699992.md), and the [Minnesota Whitecaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Whitecaps) of the National Women's Hockey League.

The Twin Cities campus of the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) is a National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) Division I school competing in the [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference). Four additional schools in the state compete in NCAA Division I ice hockey: the [University of Minnesota Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Duluth); [Minnesota State University, Mankato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_University,_Mankato); [St. Cloud State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Cloud_State_University) and [Bemidji State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemidji_State_University). There are nine [NCAA Division II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_II) colleges in the [Northern Sun Intercollegiate Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sun_Intercollegiate_Conference), and twenty [NCAA Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III) colleges in the [Minnesota Intercollegiate Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Intercollegiate_Athletic_Conference) and [Upper Midwest Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest_Athletic_Conference).[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208]

Minneapolis has hosted the [NCAA Men's Division I Basketball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Men's_Division_I_Basketball_Championship) in [1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_NCAA_Men's_Division_I_Basketball_Tournament), [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_NCAA_Men's_Division_I_Basketball_Tournament), [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_NCAA_Men's_Division_I_Basketball_Tournament), and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_NCAA_Men's_Division_I_Basketball_Tournament).

The [Hazeltine National Golf Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazeltine_National_Golf_Club) has hosted the [U.S. Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_\(golf\)), [U.S. Women's Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Women's_Open), [U.S. Senior Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senior_Open) and [PGA Championship](224616.md). The course also hosted the [Ryder Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryder_Cup) in the fall of 2016, when it became one of two courses in the U.S. to host all major golf competitions. The Ryder Cup is scheduled to return in 2028.[^cite_note-209]

[Interlachen Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interlachen_Country_Club) has hosted the [U.S. Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_\(golf\)), [U.S. Women's Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Women's_Open), and Solheim Cup.

[Winter Olympic Games](34069.md) medalists from the state include twelve of the twenty members of the [gold medal](526488.md) [1980 ice hockey team](295721.md) (coached by Minnesota native [Herb Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb_Brooks)) and the bronze medalist [U.S. men's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling_at_the_2006_Winter_Olympics#Men's) [curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling) team in the [2006 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Olympics), as well as the gold medal-winning team from [Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth,_Minnesota) at the [2018 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling_at_the_2018_Winter_Olympics#Men's). Swimmer [Tom Malchow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Malchow) won an Olympic gold medal in the [2000 Summer games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics) and a silver medal in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-210]

[Grandma's Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandma's_Marathon) is run every summer along the scenic [North Shore of Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Shore_\(Lake_Superior\)),[^cite_note-211] and the [Twin Cities Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities_Marathon) winds around lakes and the Mississippi River during the peak of the [fall color season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_change_in_leaves). Farther north, [Eveleth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eveleth,_Minnesota) is the location of the [United States Hockey Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Hockey_Hall_of_Fame).[^cite_note-212]

### Outdoor recreation



Fishing on [Bde Maka Ska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bde_Maka_Ska) in Minneapolis

[^cite_note-213]

Minnesotans participate in high levels of physical activity,[^cite_note-\:5-145] and many of these activities are outdoors. The strong interest of Minnesotans in environmentalism has been attributed to the popularity of these pursuits.[^cite_note-214]

In the warmer months, these activities often involve water. Weekend and longer trips to family [cabins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottage) on Minnesota's numerous lakes are a way of life for many residents. Activities include [water sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_sports) such as [water skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_skiing), which originated in the state,[^cite_note-215] [boating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boating), [canoeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoeing), and [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing). More than 36% of Minnesotans fish, second only to Alaska.[^cite_note-216]



An old [sauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauna) cabin of [Listening Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listening_Point) on the shores of [Burntside Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burntside_Lake) in [Morse Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_Township,_St._Louis_County,_Minnesota), Minnesota

Fishing does not cease when the lakes freeze; [ice fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_fishing) has been around since the first Scandinavian immigrants arrived.[^cite_note-217] During the long, harsh winters, Minnesotans play ice sports such as [skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_skating), [hockey](14790.md), [curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling), and [broomball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broomball), and snow sports such as [cross-country skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-country_skiing), [alpine skiing](39576.md), [luge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luge), [snowshoeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowshoeing), and [snowmobiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmobile).[^cite_note-218] Minnesota is the only U.S. state where [bandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandy) is played.[^cite_note-219]

State and national forests and the 72 state parks are used year-round for hunting, [camping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camping), and [hiking](147313.md). There are almost 20,000 miles (32,000 km) of snowmobile trails statewide.[^cite_note-220] Minnesota has more miles of [bike trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_trail) than any other state,[^cite_note-221] and a growing network of [hiking trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail), including the 235-mile (378 km) [Superior Hiking Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Hiking_Trail) in the northeast.[^cite_note-222] Many hiking and bike trails are used for cross-country skiing during the winter.
