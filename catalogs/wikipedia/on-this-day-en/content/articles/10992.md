**Frankfurt am Main**,[^cite_note-9] usually shortened to **Frankfurt**, is the most populous city in the [German state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse). Its 778,589 inhabitants as of 2025[^cite_note-Statistikportal_Frankfurt-10] make it the [fifth-most populous city in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Germany_by_population).[^cite_note-11] Located in the [foreland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreland_basin) of the [Taunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunus) on its namesake river [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)), the city forms a continuous [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) with [Offenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offenbach_am_Main); [its urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Rhein-Main_Regional_Authority) has a population of more than 2.7 million. Frankfurt is the heart of the larger [Rhine-Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Main) metropolitan region, which has a population of more than 5.8 million and is Germany's [fourth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_regions_in_Germany) metropolitan region after the [Rhine-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr_metropolitan_region) region, the [Berlin/Brandenburg Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin/Brandenburg_Metropolitan_Region) and the [Munich Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Metropolitan_Region). Home to the [European Central Bank](9474.md), the city serves as one of the four [institutional seats of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_seats_of_the_European_Union). Frankfurt is classified by the [GaWC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network) as an [Alpha world city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network#Alpha).

Frankfurt was first mentioned in a document in 794 and has been an imperial city since 1372. Frankfurt was a [city state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state), the [Free City of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Frankfurt), for nearly five centuries, and was one of the most important cities of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), as a site of [Imperial coronations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_the_Holy_Roman_Emperor); it lost its [sovereignty](28791.md) upon the collapse of the empire in 1806, regained it in 1815 and then lost it again in 1866, when it was annexed by the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) following the [Austro-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Prussian_War). It has been part of the state of Hesse since 1945. Frankfurt is culturally, ethnically and religiously diverse, with half of its population, and a majority of its young people, [having a migrant background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Germany). A quarter of the population consists of foreign nationals, including many [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate). In 2015, Frankfurt was home to 1,909 [ultra high-net-worth individuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-net-worth_individual), the sixth-highest number of any city.

Frankfurt is a global hub for commerce, culture, education, tourism and transportation, and is the site of many global and European corporate headquarters. Due to its central location in former [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) became the [busiest in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Germany), one of [the busiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports) in the world, the airport with the most direct routes in the world, and the primary hub for [Lufthansa](97733.md), the [national airline](465816.md) of Germany and Europe's largest airline. [Frankfurt Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Main\)_Hauptbahnhof) is Germany's second-busiest railway station after [Hamburg Hbf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Hbf), operated by [Deutsche Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn), the world's largest railway company,[^cite_note-12] whose Frankfurter division [DB InfraGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB_InfraGO) manages the largest railway network in Europe.[^cite_note-13] [Frankfurter Kreuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Kreuz) is the most-heavily used [interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchange_\(road\)) in the EU. Frankfurt is one of the major financial and business centers of Europe, with the headquarters of the [European Central Bank](9474.md), [Deutsche Bundesbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bundesbank), [Frankfurt Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Stock_Exchange), [Deutsche Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank), [DZ Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DZ_Bank), [KfW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KfW), [Commerzbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerzbank), [DekaBank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DekaBank), [Helaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helaba), several [cloud](19541494.md) and [fintech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fintech) startups, and other institutes. Automotive, technology and research, services, consulting, media and [creative industries](674483.md) complement the economic base. Frankfurt's [DE-CIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DE-CIX) is the world's largest [internet exchange point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_exchange_point). [Messe Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messe_Frankfurt) is one of the world's largest [trade fairs](1129368.md). Other major fairs include the [Music Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musikmesse_Frankfurt) and the [Frankfurt Book Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Book_Fair), the world's largest book fair. The city also has 93 consulates, among which the largest is the [US Consulate General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate_General_of_the_United_States,_Frankfurt).

Frankfurt is home to influential educational institutions, including the [Goethe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University) with the [Universitätsklinikum Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A4tsklinikum_Frankfurt?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Hesse's largest hospital), the [FUAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_University_of_Applied_Sciences), the [FUMPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_University_of_Music_and_Performing_Arts), and graduate schools like the [FSFM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_School_of_Finance_&_Management). The city is one of two seats of the [German National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Library) (alongside [Leipzig](17955.md)), the largest library in the German-speaking countries and one of the largest in the world. Its renowned cultural venues include the concert hall [Alte Oper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alte_Oper), continental Europe's [largest English theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_English_Theatre_Frankfurt) and many museums, including the [Städel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A4del), [Liebieghaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liebieghaus), [German Film Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Film_Museum), [Senckenberg Natural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senckenberg_Natural_History_Museum), [Goethe House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_House) and [Schirn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schirn_Kunsthalle_Frankfurt) art venue. [Frankfurt's skyline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt's_skyline) is shaped by some of Europe's tallest skyscrapers, which has led to the term *[Mainhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainhattan)*.

The city has many parks and major botanical gardens. Roughly 52% of the city area is green. Frankfurt is a founding member of the Climate Alliance of European Cities and has pledged to work reducing carbon emissions by 50% by 2030.[^cite_note-14] Frankfurt is the seat of the [German Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Football_Association), is home to the first division association football club [Eintracht Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eintracht_Frankfurt), the [Löwen Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B6wen_Frankfurt) [ice hockey](14790.md) team, and the basketball club [Frankfurt Skyliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyliners_Frankfurt), and is the venue of the [Frankfurt Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Marathon) and the [Ironman Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironman_Germany).

## Etymology



"The Discovery of Frankfurt by Charlemagne" by [Leopold Bode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Bode) (1888)

*Frankonovurd* (in [Old High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_High_German)) or *Vadum Francorum* (in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin)) were the first names mentioned in written records from 794.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] It transformed to *Frankenfort* during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) and then to *Franckfort* and *Franckfurth* in the [modern era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period). The name is derived from the *Franconofurd*, from the [Germanic tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) of the [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) and *Furt* ([cf.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cf.) English *[ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_\(river\))*) where the river was shallow enough to be crossed on foot.

[Thietmar of Merseburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thietmar_of_Merseburg) in the 11th century reported a [founding legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_myth) in which Charlemagne and his army of Franks discovered the ford at Frankfurt when a [doe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer) crossed over it, showing them the way; in honor of this, the city was named "ford of the Franks". By the 19th century, the name *Frankfurt* had been established as the official spelling. The older English spelling of *Frankfort* is now rarely seen in reference to Frankfurt am Main, although more than a dozen other towns and cities, mainly in the United States, use this spelling, including [Frankfort, Kentucky](57834.md), [Frankfort, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfort_\(town\),_New_York), and [Frankfort, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfort,_Illinois). *The New York Times* first used the Frankfurt spelling for Frankfurt am Main on 24 October 1953 and last used the Frankfort spelling on 10 June 1954.

The suffix *am Main* has been used regularly since the 14th century. In English, the city's full name of *Frankfurt am Main* means "Frankfurt on the Main" (pronounced like English *mine* or German *mein*). Frankfurt is located on an ancient ford (German: *_Furt_*) on the river [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)). As a part of early [Franconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia), the inhabitants were the early [Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks), thus the city's name reveals its legacy as "the ford of the Franks on the Main".[^cite_note-17]

Among English speakers, the city is commonly known simply as Frankfurt, but Germans occasionally call it by its full name to distinguish it from the other (significantly smaller) German city of [Frankfurt an der Oder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Oder\)) in the [*Land*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of [Brandenburg](3765.md) on the Polish border. The common abbreviations for the city, primarily used in railway services and on road signs, are *Frankfurt (Main)*, *Frankfurt (M)*, *Frankfurt a. M.*, *Frankfurt/Main* or *Frankfurt/M*. The common [abbreviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbreviation) for the name of the city is "FFM". Also in use is "FRA", the [IATA code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association_airport_code) for Frankfurt Airport.

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Frankfurt).

### Early history and Holy Roman Empire



Remains of the medieval city wall

At the western borders of Frankfurt lies the [Kapellenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelsberg_culture#Settlements) as part of the Taunus with one of the first Stone Age cities in Europe.[^cite_note-18] The Celts had different settlements in the Taunus mountains north of Frankfurt, the biggest one the [Heidetrank Oppidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidetrank_Oppidum). The first traces of [Roman settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania) established in the area of the [river Nidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidda_River) date to the reign of Emperor [Vespasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespasian) in the years 69 to 79 AD. [Nida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nida_\(Roman_town\)) (modern [Heddernheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heddernheim), [Praunheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praunheim)) was a Roman [civitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civitas) capital (*Civitas Taunensium*).

[Alemanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni) and Franks [lived there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francia), and by 794, [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) presided over an [imperial assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Frankfurt) and church synod, at which *Franconofurd* (alternative spellings end with -furt and -vurd) was first mentioned. It was one of the two capitals of Charlemagne's grandson [Louis the German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_the_German), together with [Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regensburg). Louis founded the [collegiate church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_church), rededicated in 1239 to [Bartholomew the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_the_Apostle) and now [Frankfurt Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Cathedral).[^cite_note-19]

Frankfurt was one of the most important cities in the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). From 855, the [German kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_kings) were elected and crowned in [Aachen](1520.md). In 1372, Frankfurt became a *Reichsstadt* ([Imperial Free City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Free_City)) and was as such directly subordinate to the [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor) rather than a regional ruler or a local nobleman. From 1562 onward the kings and [emperors were crowned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_the_Holy_Roman_Emperor) and [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_election) in Frankfurt, starting with [Maximilian II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). This tradition ended in 1792, when [Francis II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) was elected. His coronation was deliberately held on 14 July [Bastille Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastille_Day), the anniversary of the [storming of the Bastille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storming_of_the_Bastille). The elections and coronations took place in [St. Bartholomäus Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Cathedral), known as the *Kaiserdom* (Imperial Cathedral), or the four other structures known to have existed at this location. The cathedral spire was constructed according to plans by [Madern Gerthener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madern_Gerthener) in 1415 and is 95 meters tall.[^cite_note-20]



Coronation of [Archduke Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) in Frankfurt Cathedral, 3 April 1764

The [Messe Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messe_Frankfurt) ('Frankfurt Trade Fair') was first mentioned in 1150. In 1240, [Emperor Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Frederick_II) granted an imperial privilege to its visitors, meaning they would be protected by the empire. The fair became particularly important when similar fairs in French Beaucaire lost attraction around 1380. [Book trade fairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Book_Fair) began in 1478. In 1585, Frankfurt traders established a system of [exchange rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_rate) for the various currencies that were circulating to prevent cheating and extortion. Therein lay the early roots for the [Frankfurt Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Stock_Exchange).

Frankfurt managed to remain neutral during the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War), but suffered from the [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) that refugees brought to the city. After the war, Frankfurt regained its wealth. In the late 1770s the theater principal [Abel Seyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Seyler) was based in Frankfurt, and established the city's theatrical life.[^cite_note-21]

### Impact of French revolution and the Napoleonic Wars

Following the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), Frankfurt was occupied or bombarded several times by French troops. It remained a [Free city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_imperial_city) until the collapse of the Holy Roman Empire in 1805/6. In 1806, it became part of the [principality of Aschaffenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Aschaffenburg) under the *Fürstprimas* ([Prince-Primate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Primate)), [Karl Theodor Anton Maria von Dalberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Theodor_Anton_Maria_von_Dalberg). This meant that Frankfurt was incorporated into the [Confederation of the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_the_Rhine). In 1810, Dalberg adopted the title of a [Grand Duke of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Frankfurt). [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I_of_France) intended to make his adopted son [Eugène de Beauharnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Beauharnais), already *Prince de Venise* ("[prince of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Venice)", a newly established primogeniture in Italy), Grand Duke of Frankfurt after Dalberg's death (since the latter as a Catholic bishop had no legitimate heirs). The Grand Duchy remained a short episode lasting from 1810 to 1813 when the military tide turned in favor of the Anglo-Prussian-led allies that overturned the Napoleonic order. Dalberg abdicated in favor of Eugène de Beauharnais, which of course was only a symbolic action, as the latter effectively never ruled after the ruin of the French armies and Frankfurt's takeover by the allies.

### Frankfurt as a fully sovereign state



View of Frankfurt am Main from *Sachsenhaeuser Berg* (Mountain of Sachsenhausen) showing the spire of Frankfurt Cathedral in 1845

After Napoleon's final defeat and abdication, the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) (1814–1815) dissolved the grand-duchy and Frankfurt became a fully sovereign city-state with a republican form of government. Frankfurt entered the newly founded [German Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Confederation) (till 1866) as a free city, becoming the seat of its *Bundestag*, the confederal parliament where the nominally presiding [Habsburg Emperor of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) was represented by an Austrian "presidential envoy".

After the ill-fated [revolution of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_revolutions_of_1848%E2%80%931849), Frankfurt was the seat of the first democratically elected German parliament, the [Frankfurt Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Parliament), which met in the *[Frankfurter Paulskirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Paulskirche)* (St. Paul's Church) and was opened on 18 May 1848. In the year of its existence, the assembly developed a common constitution for a unified Germany, with the Prussian king as its monarch. The institution failed in 1849 when the [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) king, [Frederick William IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_William_IV_of_Prussia), declared that he would not accept "a crown from the gutter".

### Frankfurt after the loss of sovereignty



The [Frankfurt Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Parliament) at [St. Paul's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul's_Church,_Frankfurt) in 1848

Frankfurt lost its independence following the [Austro-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Prussian_War) of 1866, when Prussia annexed several smaller states, including the [Free City of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Frankfurt). The city was subsequently incorporated into the Prussian province of [Hesse-Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse-Nassau). The occupation and annexation were widely regarded in Frankfurt as a grave injustice, yet the city retained its distinctly Western European, urban, and cosmopolitan character. The formerly independent towns of [Bornheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Bockenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bockenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) were incorporated in 1890.

In 1914, the citizens founded the University of Frankfurt, later named [Goethe University Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt). This marked the only civic foundation of a university in Germany; today it is one of Germany's largest.

From 6 April to 17 May 1920, following military intervention to put down the [Ruhr uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_uprising), [Frankfurt was occupied by French troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_occupation_of_Frankfurt).[^cite_note-22] The French claimed that Articles 42 to 44 of the [peace treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles_\(1919\)) concerning the demilitarization of the [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_Province) had been broken.[^cite_note-23] In 1924, [Ludwig Landmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Landmann) became the first Jewish mayor of the city, and led a significant expansion during the following years. During the [Nazi era](21212.md), the synagogues of the city were destroyed and the vast majority of the Jewish population fled or was killed.[^cite_note-frankfurt-tourismus.de-24]

During [World War II](32927.md), Frankfurt was the location of a Nazi prison for underage girls with several [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) camps,[^cite_note-25] a camp for [Sinti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinti) and [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) (see *[Romani Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_Holocaust)*),[^cite_note-26] the [Dulag Luft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulag_Luft) West transit camp for [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) prisoners of war,[^cite_note-27] and a subcamp of the [Natzweiler-Struthof concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natzweiler-Struthof_concentration_camp).[^cite_note-28]



Aerial view of the cathedral in May 1945

Frankfurt was severely [bombed in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Frankfurt_am_Main_in_World_War_II) (1939–1945). About 5,500 residents were killed during the raids, and the once-famous [medieval city center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), by that time one of the largest in Germany, was almost completely destroyed. It became a [ground battlefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Frankfurt) on 26 March 1945, when the Allied advance into Germany was forced to take the city in contested urban combat that included a river assault. The [5th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) and the [6th Armored Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Armored_Division_\(United_States\)) of the [United States Army](32087.md) captured Frankfurt after several days of intense fighting, and it was declared largely secure on 29 March 1945.[^cite_note-29] In spite of the wartime destruction, more than 40% of Frankfurt's buildings still date from before the Second World War.[^cite_note-zensus2011.de-30]

After the end of the war, Frankfurt became a part of the newly founded state of Hesse, consisting of the old [Hesse-(Darmstadt)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_State_of_Hesse) and the [Prussian Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Kurhessen) provinces. The city was part of the [American Zone of Occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Occupation_Zones_in_Germany#American_Zone_of_Occupation) of Germany. The Military Governor for the United States Zone (1945–1949) and the United States High Commissioner for Germany (HICOG) (1949–1952) had their headquarters in the [IG Farben Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IG_Farben_Building), intentionally left undamaged by the Allies' wartime bombardment.

Frankfurt was the original choice for the provisional capital city of the newly founded state of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in 1949. The city constructed a parliament building that was never used for its intended purpose (it housed the radio studios of [Hessischer Rundfunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessischer_Rundfunk)). In the end, [Konrad Adenauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Adenauer), the first postwar [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_Germany_\(Federal_Republic\)), preferred the town of [Bonn](3295.md), for the most part because it was close to his hometown, but also because many other prominent politicians opposed the choice of Frankfurt out of concern that Frankfurt would be accepted as the permanent capital, thereby weakening the West German population's support for a [reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) with [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) and the eventual return of the capital to [Berlin](3354.md).



Reconstructed [Römer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6mer) in the 1950s

Postwar reconstruction took place in a sometimes simple modern style, thus changing Frankfurt's architectural face. A few landmark buildings were reconstructed historically, albeit in a simplified manner (e.g., [Römer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6mer), [St. Paul's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul's_Church,_Frankfurt_am_Main), and [Goethe House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_House)). The collection of historically significant [Cairo Genizah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Genizah) documents of the Municipal Library was destroyed by the bombing. According to [Arabist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabist) and Genizah scholar [S.D. Goitein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.D._Goitein), "not even handlists indicating its contents have survived."[^cite_note-31]

The end of the war marked Frankfurt's comeback as Germany's leading financial hub. In 1948, the Allies founded the [Bank deutscher Länder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_deutscher_L%C3%A4nder), the forerunner of the [Deutsche Bundesbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bundesbank). Following this decision, more financial institutions were re-established, such as [Deutsche Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank) and [Dresdner Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresdner_Bank). In the 1950s, [Frankfurt Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Stock_Exchange) regained its position as the country's leading stock exchange. In 1998, the [European Central Bank](9474.md) was founded in Frankfurt, followed by the [European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Insurance_and_Occupational_Pensions_Authority) in 2011, and the [Anti-Money Laundering Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Money_Laundering_Authority) in 2024.

Frankfurt also reemerged as Germany's transportation hub and [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) became Europe's second-busiest airport behind [London Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport) in 1961. During the 1970s, the city created one of Europe's most efficient underground transportation systems.[^cite_note-32] That system includes a suburban rail system ([S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_S-Bahn)) linking outlying communities with the city center, and a deep underground light rail system with smaller coaches ([U-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_U-Bahn)) also capable of travelling above ground on rails.

## Geography



Frankfurt on the mouth of the [Nidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidda_\(river\)) into the [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)), which flows into the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) between the [Rhineland-Palatine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland-Palatine) capital of [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz) and the [Hessian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) (historically [Nassauian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_\(region\))) capital of [Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesbaden). Also visible the [Taunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunus) suburbs of the districts of [High Taunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochtaunuskreis) and [Main-Taunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-Taunus-Kreis), two of the wealthiest districts in Germany.

Frankfurt is the largest city in the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) in the western part of Germany.

### Site

Frankfurt is located on both sides of the river [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)), south-east of the [Taunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunus) mountain range. The southern part of the city contains the [Frankfurt City Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_City_Forest), Germany's largest city forest. The city area is 248.31 km<sup>2</sup> (95.87 sq mi) and extends over 23.4 km (14.54 mi) east to west and 23.3 km (14.48 mi) north to south. Its downtown is north of the river Main in [Altstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district (the historical center) and the surrounding [Innenstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innenstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district. The geographical center is in [Bockenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bockenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district near [Frankfurt West station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_West_station).

Frankfurt at the heart of the densely populated [Frankfurt Rhine-Main Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Rhine-Main) with a population of 5.5 million. Other important cities in the region are [Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesbaden) (capital of [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse)), [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz) (capital of [Rhineland-Palatinate](26239.md)), [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt), [Offenbach am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offenbach_am_Main), [Hanau](442088.md), [Aschaffenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aschaffenburg), [Bad Homburg vor der Höhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Homburg_vor_der_H%C3%B6he), [Rüsselsheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCsselsheim), [Wetzlar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetzlar) and [Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg).

### Districts



The 46 *Stadtteile* (city districts) of central Frankfurt, 2010

The city is divided into 46 city districts (*Stadtteile*), which are in turn divided into 121 city boroughs (*Stadtbezirke*) and 448 electoral districts (*Wahlbezirke*). The 46 city districts combine into 16 area districts (*[Ortsbezirke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortsbezirk_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\))*), which each have a district committee and chairperson.

The largest city district by population and area is [Sachsenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), while the smallest is [Altstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), Frankfurt's historical center. Three larger city districts (Sachsenhausen, [Westend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Nordend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\))) are divided for administrative purposes into a northern (*-Nord*) and a southern (*-Süd*) part, respectively a western (*-West*) and an eastern (*-Ost*) part, but are generally considered as one city district (which is why often only 43 city districts are mentioned, even on the city's official website).[^cite_note-33]

Some larger housing areas are often falsely called city districts, even by locals, like Nordweststadt (part of [Niederursel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niederursel_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Heddernheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heddernheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Praunheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praunheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\))), Goldstein (part of [Schwanheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwanheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\))), Riedberg (part of [Kalbach-Riedberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalbach-Riedberg_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\))) and [Europaviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europaviertel) (part of [Gallus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallus_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\))). The [Bankenviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankenviertel) (*banking district*), Frankfurt's financial district, is also not an administrative city district (it covers parts of the western [Innenstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innenstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district, the southern [Westend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district and the eastern [Bahnhofsviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahnhofsviertel_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district).

Many city districts are incorporated suburbs (*[Vororte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vororte)*) or were previously independent cities, such as [Höchst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6chst_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)). Some like [Nordend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Westend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) arose during the rapid growth of the city in the [Gründerzeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%BCnderzeit) following the [Unification of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Germany), while others were formed from territory which previously belonged to other city district(s), such as [Dornbusch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornbusch_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Riederwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riederwald_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)).

### History of incorporations

Until the year 1877 the city's territory consisted of the present-day inner-city districts of [Altstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Innenstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innenstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Bahnhofsviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahnhofsviertel_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Gutleutviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutleutviertel_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Gallus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallus_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Westend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Nordend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Ostend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Sachsenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)).

At the end of the 19th century the city of Frankfurt was enlarged. The central train station was built in 1888 to support the incorporation of existing population centers.[^cite_note-34] [Bornheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) was incorporated in 1877, followed by [Bockenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bockenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) in 1895. [Seckbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seckbach_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Niederrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niederrad_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Oberrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberrad_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) followed on 1 July 1900. The *Landkreis Frankfurt* was finally dispersed on 1 April 1910, and therefore [Berkersheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkersheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Bonames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonames_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Eckenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Eschersheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschersheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Ginnheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginnheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Hausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Heddernheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heddernheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Niederursel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niederursel_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Praunheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praunheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Preungesheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preungesheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Rödelheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6delheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) joined the city. In the same year a new city district, [Riederwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riederwald_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), was created on territory that had formerly belonged to Seckbach and Ostend.

On 1 April 1928 the City of [Höchst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6chst_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) became part of Frankfurt, as well as its city districts [Sindlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindlingen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Unterliederbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterliederbach_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Zeilsheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeilsheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)). Simultaneously the *Landkreis Höchst* was dispersed with its member cities either joining Frankfurt ([Fechenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fechenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Griesheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griesheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Nied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nied_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Schwanheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwanheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Sossenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sossenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\))) or joining the newly established *Landkreis* of [Main-Taunus-Kreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-Taunus-Kreis). [Dornbusch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornbusch_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) became a city district in 1946. It was created on territory that had formerly belonged to Eckenheim and Ginnheim.

On 1 August 1972, Hesse's smaller suburbs of [Harheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Kalbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalbach_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Nieder-Erlenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieder-Erlenbach_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), and [Nieder-Eschbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieder-Eschbach_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) became districts while other neighboring suburbs chose to join the Main-Taunus-Kreis, the [Landkreis Offenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landkreis_Offenbach), the [Kreis Groß-Gerau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreis_Gro%C3%9F-Gerau), the [Hochtaunuskreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochtaunuskreis), the [Main-Kinzig-Kreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-Kinzig-Kreis) or the [Wetteraukreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetteraukreis). [Bergen-Enkheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen-Enkheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) was the last suburb to become part of Frankfurt on 1 January 1977.

[Flughafen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flughafen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) became an official city district in 1979. It covers the area of Frankfurt Airport that had belonged to Sachsenhausen and the neighboring city of [Mörfelden-Walldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6rfelden-Walldorf).

Frankfurt's youngest city district is [Frankfurter Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Berg). It was part of Bonames until 1996. Kalbach was officially renamed [Kalbach-Riedberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalbach-Riedberg) in 2006 because of the large residential housing development in the area known as Riedberg.

Together with these towns (and some larger nearby towns, e.g., [Hanau](442088.md), [Rodgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodgau), [Dreieich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreieich), [Langen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langen,_Hesse)) Frankfurt forms a contiguous built-up urban area called *Stadtregion Frankfurt* which is not an official administrative district. The urban area had an estimated population of 2.3 million in 2010, and is the [13th-largest urban area in the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_the_European_Union).

### Climate

Frankfurt has a [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperateness) [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dobk*). Its climate features cool winters with frequent rain showers and overcast skies, and warm summers. The average annual temperature is 11.4 °C (52.5 °F), with monthly mean temperatures ranging from 2.7 °C (36.9 °F) in January to 20.8 °C (69.4 °F) in July. The city is one of the warmest in [Germany](11867.md) in winter.[^cite_note-35]

The [growing season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growing_season) is longer when compared to the rest of Germany, thus resulting in an early arrival of springtime in the region, with trees typically leafing out already toward the end of March.

Winters in Frankfurt are generally mild or at least not freezing with a small possibility of snow, especially in January and February but dark and often overcast. Frankfurt is, on average, covered with snow only for around 10 to 20 days per year.[^cite_note-36] The temperature falls below 0 °C on about 64 days and the daily maximum stays below freezing for about 10 days on average per year.

Because of the mild climate in the region, there are some well-known wine regions in the vicinity such as [Rhenish Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhenish_Hesse), [Rheingau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheingau), [Franconia (wine region)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia_\(wine_region\)) and [Bergstraße (route)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergstra%C3%9Fe_\(route\)). There is also a microclimate on the northern bank of the river [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)) which allows [palms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_tree), [fig trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fig_trees), [lemon trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon_trees) and southern European plants to grow in that area. The area is called the "[Nizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizza,_Frankfurt)" (the German word for the southern French town [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice)) and is one of the biggest parks with Mediterranean vegetation north of the [Alps](981.md).[^cite_note-37]

With a population of 763,380 (2019) within its administrative boundaries[^cite_note-42] and of 2,300,000 in the actual [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_of_the_European_Union),[^cite_note-European_Union-43] Frankfurt is the [fifth-largest city in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Germany_by_population), after [Berlin](3354.md), [Hamburg](13467.md), [Munich](19058.md) and [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne). Central Frankfurt has been a *[Großstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gro%C3%9Fstadt)* (a city with at least 100,000 residents by definition) since 1875. With 414,576 residents in 1910, it was the ninth largest city in Germany and the number of inhabitants grew to 553,464 before [World War II](32927.md). After the war, at the end of the year 1945, the number had dropped to 358,000. In the following years, the population grew again and reached an all-time-high of 691,257 in 1963. It dropped again to 592,411 in 1986 but has increased since then. According to the demographic forecasts for central Frankfurt, the city will have a population up to 813,000 within its administrative boundaries in 2035[^cite_note-44] and more than 2.5 million inhabitants in its urban area.

As of 2015, Frankfurt had 1909 [ultra high-net-worth individuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_high-net-worth_individual), the sixth-highest number of any city in the world.[^cite_note-45]

During the 1970s, the state government of [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse) wanted to expand the city's administrative boundaries to include the entire urban area. This would have made Frankfurt officially the second-largest city in Germany after Berlin with up to 3 million inhabitants.[^cite_note-46] However, because local authorities did not agree, the administrative territory is still much smaller than its actual urban area.

### Immigration and foreign nationals

According to data from the city [register of residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_registration#Germany), 51.2% of the population had a *migration background* as of 2015, which means that a person or at least one of their parents was born with foreign citizenship. For the first time, a majority of the city residents had an at least part non-German background.[^cite_note-47] Moreover, three of four children in the city under the age of six had full or partial immigrant backgrounds,[^cite_note-48] and 27.7% of residents had a foreign citizenship.[^cite_note-49]

According to statistics, 46.7% of immigrants in Frankfurt come from other countries in the EU; 24.5% come from European countries that are not part of the EU; 15.7% come from Asia (including Western Asia and South Asia); 7.3% come from Africa; 3.4% come from North America (including the [Caribbean](18956035.md) and Central America); 0.2% come from [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_\(continent\)) and [New Zealand](4913064.md); 2.3% come from South America; and 1.1% come from Pacific island nations. Because of this the city is often considered to be a multicultural city, and has been compared to [New York City](645042.md) and [London](17867.md).[^cite_note-50]

Frankfurt has the largest Moroccan community in Germany, numbering about 8,000 people, and the [Rhine-Main area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Rhine-Main) has about 20,000. Many [Moroccans](5639315.md) came as guest workers in the 1970s.

### Religion

Frankfurt is historically a [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant)-dominated city. However, during the 19th century, an increasing number of [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Germany) moved to Frankfurt. As of 2013, the largest Christian denominations were Catholicism (22.7% of the population) and Protestantism, especially Lutheranism (19.4%).[^cite_note-52]

The Jewish community has a history dating back to medieval times and has always ranked among the largest in Germany. More than 7,200 inhabitants are affiliated with the Jewish community, making it the third largest in Germany after [Berlin](3354.md) and [Munich](19058.md).[^cite_note-frankfurt-tourismus.de-24] Frankfurt has four active synagogues.[^cite_note-Jewish_Community_Frankfurt_am_Main-53]

Frankfurt has a Muslim community estimated at 12% in 2006.[^cite_note-54] According to calculations based on census data for 21 countries of origin, the number of Muslim migrants in Frankfurt amounted to about 84,000 in 2011, making up 12.6% of the population.[^cite_note-55] The most prevalent countries of origin were [Turkey](11125639.md) and [Morocco](19291.md). The Ahmadiyya Noor Mosque, constructed in 1959, is the city's largest mosque and the third largest in Germany. In 2020, the number of Muslims in Frankfurt's total population was estimated at 18%.[^cite_note-56]

### Landtag election

For elections to the [Hesse State Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Hesse), Frankfurt am Main is split up into six constituencies. In total 15 delegates represent the city in the [Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag) in [Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesbaden). The last election took place in October 2018. Six members of parliament were directly elected in their respective constituencies: Uwe Serke (CDU, Frankfurt am Main I), [Miriam Dahlke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miriam_Dahlke) (Greens, Frankfurt am Main II), Ralf-Norbert Bartel (CDU, Frankfurt am Main III), [Michael Boddenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Boddenberg) (CDU, Frankfurt am Main IV), Markus Bocklet (Greens, Frankfurt am Main V) and [Boris Rhein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Rhein) (CDU, Frankfurt am Main VI).

Delegates from Frankfurt often serve high-ranking positions in Hessian politics, e.g. [Michael Boddenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Boddenberg) is Hessian Minister of Finance and Boris Rhein was elected President of the Landtag of Hesse in 2019.

## Economy



[Deutsche Börse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_B%C3%B6rse) trading floor in Frankfurt

Frankfurt is one of the world's most important financial hubs and Germany's financial capital, followed by [Hamburg](13467.md) and [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart). Frankfurt was ranked eighth at the [International Financial Centers Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_centre#Xinhua%E2%80%93Dow_Jones_Index_\(2010%E2%80%932014\)) (2013), eighth at the [Worldwide Centres of Commerce Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldwide_Centres_of_Commerce_Index) (2008), ninth at the [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index) (September 2013),[^cite_note-57] tenth at the [Global Power City Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city#Global_Power_City_Index) (2011), 11th at the [Global City Competitiveness Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city#Global_City_Competitiveness_Index) (2012), 12th at the Innovation Cities Index (2011),[^cite_note-58] 14th at the [World City Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city#World_City_Survey) (2011) and 23rd at the [Global Cities Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city#Global_Cities_Index_\(Kearney\)) (2012).[^cite_note-Foreign_Policy-59]

The city's importance as a financial hub has risen since the [eurozone crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone_crisis). Indications are the establishment of two institutions of the [European System of Financial Supervisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_System_of_Financial_Supervisors) ([European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Insurance_and_Occupational_Pensions_Authority) and [European Systemic Risk Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Systemic_Risk_Board)) in 2011, and the entry into force in 2014 of [European Banking Supervision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Banking_Supervision), by which the European Central Bank has become the central supervisory authority for the euro area banking sector.

According to an annual study by [Cushman & Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushman_&_Wakefield), the European Cities Monitor (2010), Frankfurt has been one of the top three cities for international companies in Europe, after London and Paris, since the survey started in 1990.[^cite_note-60] It is the only German city considered to be an [alpha world city (category 3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city#GaWC_study) as listed by the [Loughborough University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough_University) group's 2010 inventory,[^cite_note-Beaverstock-61] which was a promotion from the group's 2008 inventory when it was ranked as an alpha minus world city (category 4).[^cite_note-62]

With more than 922 jobs per 1,000 inhabitants, Frankfurt has the highest concentration of jobs in Germany. On work days and Saturdays, one million people commute from all over the [Rhein-Main-Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhein-Main_Region). The GRP per capita was €96,670 in 2019.[^cite_note-63]

The city is expected to benefit from international banks relocating jobs from London to Frankfurt as a result of [Brexit](41688778.md) to retain access to the EU market.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-Bloomberg.com-65] Thus far, Morgan Stanley, Citigroup Inc., Standard Chartered Plc and Nomura Holdings Inc. announced they would move their EU headquarters to Frankfurt.[^cite_note-Bloomberg.com-65]

### Central banks



The new headquarters of the European Central Bank in the Ostend district

Frankfurt is home to two important [central banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank): the German Bundesbank and the European Central Bank (ECB).[^cite_note-66]

#### European Central Bank



[Euro-Skulptur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro-Skulptur)

The European Central Bank (*Europäische Zentralbank*) is one of the world's most important central banks with a balance sheet total of around 7 trillion.[^cite_note-67] The ECB sets monetary policy for the Eurozone, consisting of 19 [EU member states](10890716.md) that have adopted the [Euro](9472.md) (€) as their common currency. From 1998 the ECB Headquarters have been located in Frankfurt, first in the [Eurotower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurotower_\(Frankfurt\)) at Willy-Brandt-Platz and in two other nearby high-rises. The new [Seat of the European Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_the_European_Central_Bank) in the [Ostend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district, consisting of the former wholesale market hall (*[Großmarkthalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gro%C3%9Fmarkthalle)*) and a newly built 185-meter skyscraper, was completed in late 2014. The new building complex was designed to accommodate up to 2,300 ECB personnel. The location is a few kilometers away from downtown and borders an industrial area as well as the Osthafen (*East Harbor*), It was primarily chosen because of its large premises which allows the ECB to install security arrangements without high fences.

The city honors the importance of the ECB by officially using the slogan "The City of the Euro" since 1998.

#### Deutsche Bundesbank

The [Deutsche Bundesbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bundesbank) (German Federal Bank), located in [Ginnheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bockenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), was established in 1957 as the central bank for the Federal Republic of Germany. Until the euro (€) was introduced in 1999, the Deutsche Bundesbank was responsible for the monetary policy of Germany and for the German currency, the [Deutsche Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mark) (DM). The Bundesbank was greatly respected for its control of inflation through the second half of the 20th century. Today the Bundesbank is an integral part of the [European System of Central Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_System_of_Central_Banks) (ESCB) which is formed by all 27 EU member states. Its balance sheet total is around 2,7 trillion,[^cite_note-68] making it the 4th biggest central bank.

### Frankfurt Stock Exchange

Main article: [Frankfurt Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Stock_Exchange)



[Bull and bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_trend) in front of the Frankfurt Stock Exchange

The Frankfurt Stock Exchange (*Frankfurter Wertpapierbörse*) began in the ninth century. By the 16th century Frankfurt had developed into an important European hub for trade fairs and financial services. Today the Frankfurt Stock Exchange is by far the largest in Germany, with a turnover of more than 90 percent of the German [stock market](52328.md) and is the third-largest in Europe after the [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stock_Exchange) and the [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronext_Paris). The most important [stock market index](23408957.md) is the [DAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAX), the index of the 30 largest German business companies listed at the stock exchange. The stock exchange is owned and operated by [Deutsche Börse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_B%C3%B6rse), which is itself listed in the DAX. Deutsche Börse also owns the European [futures exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futures_exchange) [Eurex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurex) and clearing company [Clearstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearstream). On 1 February 2012 European Commission blocked the proposed merger of Deutsche Börse and [NYSE Euronext](21560.md). "The merger between Deutsche Börse and NYSE Euronext would have led to a near-monopoly in European financial derivatives worldwide. These markets are at the heart of the financial system and it is crucial for the whole European economy that they remain competitive. We tried to find a solution, but the remedies offered fell far short of resolving the concerns."[^cite_note-71] European competition commissioner [Joaquín Almunia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaqu%C3%ADn_Almunia) said.

It is located downtown at the Börsenplatz. Deutsche Börse's headquarters are formally registered in Frankfurt, but it moved most of its employees to a high-rise called "The Cube" in [Eschborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschborn) in 2010, primarily due to significantly lower local [corporate taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_tax).

### Frankfurt Trade Fair

Main article: [Messe Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messe_Frankfurt)



[Messeturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messeturm) seen from the trade fair premises

Frankfurt Trade Fair (*Messe Frankfurt*) has the third-largest exhibition site in the world with a total of 578,000 m<sup>2</sup> (6,220,000 sq ft). The trade fair premises are located in the western part between [Bockenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bockenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), the [Westend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and the [Gallus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallus_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district. It houses ten exhibition halls with a total of 321,754 m<sup>2</sup> (3,463,330 sq ft) of space and 96,078 m<sup>2</sup> (1,034,170 sq ft) of outdoor space.

Frankfurt and the river [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) tributary [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)) acquired transnational economic importance in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). In 1337 the [Holy Roman Emperor Louis IV of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) issued a diploma privilege so that any other city was not allowed to establish a market or trade fair if this could harm the Frankfurt Trade Fair in any way. This imperial backing allowed Frankfurt to retain the commanding importance of the Frankfurt Trade Fair for centuries. In the Middle Ages the full range of manufactured and semi-processed commodities were trade at Frankfurt Fairs.[^cite_note-72]

Hosted in Frankfurt are the [Frankfurt Book Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Book_Fair) (*Frankfurter Buchmesse*), the world's largest book fair, the Ambiente Frankfurt, the world's largest [consumer goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_good) fair, the Achema, the world's largest plant engineering fair, and many more like Paperworld, Christmasworld, Beautyworld, Tendence Lifestyle or Light+Building. Messe Frankfurt GmbH, the owner and operator company, organized 87 exhibitions in 2010, of which 51 were in foreign countries. It is one of the largest trade fair companies with commercial activities in more than 150 countries.

### Aviation



Two Lufthansa Airbus A380s at [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport)

[Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) is one of the busiest airports in the world and is also the single largest place of work in Germany with more than 500 companies, which employ 71,500 people (2010).[^cite_note-73]

[Fraport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraport) is the owner and operator of Frankfurt Airport. It is the airport's second-largest employer (19,800 workers in 2010).[^cite_note-74] Fraport also operates other airports worldwide, e.g., [King Abdulaziz International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_International_Airport) in [Jeddah](277718.md), [Jorge Chávez International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Ch%C3%A1vez_International_Airport) in Lima and [Antalya Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antalya_Airport).

The largest company at Frankfurt Airport is Lufthansa, Germany's [flag carrier](465816.md) and Europe's largest airline. Lufthansa employs 35,000 people in Frankfurt.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] The Lufthansa Aviation Center (LAC) is the main operation base of Lufthansa at Frankfurt Airport. The airport serves as Lufthansa's primary [hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_hub) with 157 worldwide destinations (compared to 110 destinations at [Munich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport), Lufthansa's second-largest hub). [Lufthansa Cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Cargo) is based in Frankfurt and operates its largest cargo center (LCC) at Frankfurt Airport. [Lufthansa Flight Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lufthansa_Flight_Training) is also based here.

[Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor_Flugdienst) is a German airline based at Frankfurt Airport.

### Media

#### Newspapers



Editorial department building of *[Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung](513356.md)*

Two important daily newspapers are published. The conservative *[Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung](513356.md)*, also known as *FAZ*, was founded in 1949 and is the German newspaper with the widest circulation outside of Germany, with its editors claiming to deliver the newspaper to 148 countries every day. The FAZ has a circulation of more than 380,000 copies daily. The other important newspaper, the *[Frankfurter Rundschau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Rundschau)*, was first published in 1945 and has a daily circulation of more than 181,000.

#### Magazines

Several magazines also originate from Frankfurt. The local *Journal Frankfurt* is the best-known magazine for events, parties, and "insider tips". *Öko-Test* is a consumer-oriented magazine that focuses on ecological topics. [*Titanic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic_\(magazine\)) is a well-known and often criticized [satirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satire) magazine with a circulation of approximately 100,000.

#### Radio and TV

Frankfurt's first radio station was the Südwestdeutsche Rundfunkdienst AG (Southwest German Broadcast Service), founded in 1924. Its successor service is the public broadcaster [Hessischer Rundfunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessischer_Rundfunk) (Hessian Broadcast Service). It is located at the "[Funkhaus am Dornbusch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting_House_Dornbusch)" in the [Dornbusch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornbusch_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district and is one of the most important radio and television [broadcasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting) in Hesse, with additional studios in [Kassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassel), [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt) and [Fulda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulda).

[Bloomberg TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_TV) and [RTL Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTL_Television) have regional studios.

Other radio broadcasters include Main FM and Radio X.

From August 1945 to October 2004, the [American Forces Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Forces_Network) (AFN) had broadcast from Frankfurt ([AFN Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFN_Frankfurt)). Due to troop reductions the AFN's location has been closed with AFN now broadcasting from [Mannheim](99627.md).

#### News agency

Frankfurt is home to the German office of [Reuters](18998750.md), a global [news agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_agency). [Associated Press](18935732.md) and US-based international news agency Feature Story News have bureaux in Frankfurt.

#### Property and real estate

Frankfurt has Germany's highest concentration of homeowners. This is partly attributed to the financial sector, but also to its cosmopolitan nature, with expatriates and immigrants representing one-fourth of its population. For this reason, Frankfurt's property market often operates differently than the rest of the country where the prices are generally flatter.

#### Tourism

Frankfurt is one of Germany's leading tourist destinations. In addition to its infrastructure and economy, its diversity supports a vibrant cultural scene. This blend of attractions led 4.3 million tourists (2012) to visit Frankfurt.[^cite_note-81] The Hotels in central Frankfurt offer 34,000 beds in 228 hotels, of which 13 are luxury hotels and 46 are first-class hotels.[^cite_note-82]

## Landmarks

### Römer



[Römer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6mer), the city hall

[Römer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6mer), the German word for [Roman](25458.md), is a complex of nine houses that form the Frankfurt [city hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_hall) (*Rathaus*). The houses were acquired by the city council in 1405 from a wealthy merchant family. The middle house became the city hall and was later connected with its neighbors. The *Kaisersaal* ("Emperor's Hall") is located on the upper floor and is where the newly crowned emperors held their banquets. The Römer was partially destroyed in World War II and later rebuilt. The surrounding square, the Römerberg, is named after the city hall.



The [New Frankfurt Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom-R%C3%B6mer_Project) was completed in 2018, including 15 reconstructed historical buildings



[Römerberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6merberg_\(Frankfurt\))

The former [Altstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) (old town) quarter between the Römer and the Frankfurt Cathedral was redeveloped as the [Dom-Römer Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom-R%C3%B6mer_Project) from 2012 to 2018, including 15 [reconstructions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_\(architecture\)) of historical buildings that were destroyed during World War II.

### Frankfurt Cathedral



Frankfurt Cathedral

[Frankfurt Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Cathedral) (*Frankfurter Dom*) is not a cathedral, but the main Catholic church, dedicated to [St. Bartholomew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_the_Apostle). The [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) building was constructed in the 14th and 15th centuries on the foundation of an earlier church from the [Merovingian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merovingian) time. From 1356 onwards, kings of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) were elected in this church, and from 1562 to 1792, [Roman-German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) emperors were crowned there.

Since the 18th century, St. Bartholomew's has been called *Dom*, although it was never a bishop's seat. In 1867 it was destroyed by fire and rebuilt in its present style. It was again partially destroyed in [World War II](32927.md) and rebuilt in the 1950s. Its height is 95 m (312 ft). The cathedral tower has a viewing platform open to the public at a height of 66 m (217 ft), accessed through a narrow spiral staircase with 386 steps.

### Central Station

[Frankfurt Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Central_Station) (*Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof*), which opened in 1888, was built as the central train station for Frankfurt to replace three smaller downtown train stations and to boost the needed capacity for travellers. It was constructed as a [terminus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_station) and was the largest train station in Europe by floor area until 1915 when [Leipzig Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_Hauptbahnhof) was opened. Its three main halls were constructed in a [neo-Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Revival_architecture) style, while the later enlargement with two outer halls in 1924 was constructed in [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) style.

### Alte Oper



[Alte Oper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alte_Oper), now a concert hall, at Opernplatz

The [Alte Oper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alte_Oper) is a former [opera house](155040.md), hence the name "Old Opera". The opera house was built in 1880 by architect Richard Lucae. It was one of the major opera houses in Germany until it was heavily damaged in World War II. Until the late 1970s, it was a ruin, nicknamed "Germany's most beautiful ruin". Former Frankfurt [Lord Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Mayor) Rudi Arndt called for blowing it up in the 1960s, which earned him the nickname "Dynamite-Rudi". (Later on, Arndt said he never had meant his suggestion seriously.)

Public pressure led to its refurbishment and reopening in 1981. Today, it functions as a famous concert hall, while operas are performed at the "new" Frankfurt Opera. The inscription on the [frieze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frieze) of the Alte Oper says: "*Dem Wahren, Schönen, Guten*" ("To the true, the beautiful, the good").

### St. Paul's Church



[St. Paul's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul's_Church,_Frankfurt)

[St. Paul's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul's_Church,_Frankfurt) is a national historic monument in Germany because it was the seat of the first democratically elected parliament in 1848. It was established in 1789 as a [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) church, but was not completed until 1833. Its importance has its roots in the [Frankfurt Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Parliament), which met in the church during the revolutionary years of 1848/49 in order to write a constitution for a united Germany. The attempt failed because the monarchs of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) and Austria did not want to lose power. In 1849, Prussian troops ended the democratic experiment by force and the parliament dissolved; the building was once more used for religious services.

St. Paul's was partially destroyed in World War II with its interior significantly damaged. It was quickly and symbolically rebuilt after the war albeit with modern alterations particularly to the interior; today it is used mainly for exhibitions and events.

### Archäologischer Garten Frankfurt

The Archaeological Garden contains small parts of the oldest recovered buildings: an ancient Roman settlement and the Frankfurt Royal Palace (*Kaiserpfalz Frankfurt*) from the sixth century. The garden is located between the Römerberg and the cathedral. It was discovered after World War II when the area was heavily bombed and later partly rebuilt. The remains were preserved and are now open to the public. From 2013 until 2015 an event building, the Stadthaus ("City house"), has been built on top of the garden, but it remains open to the public free of charge.

### Haus Wertheim

Wertheim House is the only [timbered house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber_framing) in the [Altstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district that survived the heavy bombings of World War II undamaged. It is located on the Römerberg next to the Historical Museum.

### Saalhof

The [Saalhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saalhof?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the oldest conserved building in the [Altstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district and dates to the 12th century. It was used as an exhibition hall by Dutch [clothiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloth_merchant) when trade fairs were held during the 14th and 15th centuries. The Saalhof was partly destroyed in World War II and later rebuilt. Today it serves as a part of the [Historical Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_museum,_Frankfurt).

### Eiserner Steg

The Eiserner Steg (Iron Bridge) is a pedestrian-only bridge across the Main that connects Römerberg and Sachsenhausen. It was built in 1868 and was the second bridge to cross the river. After World War II, when it was blown up by the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht), it was quickly rebuilt in 1946. Today some 10,000 people cross the bridge on a daily basis.

### German National Library



DNB building in Frankfurt

Frankfurt is one of two sites of the [German National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Library) (*Deutsche Nationalbibliothek*), the other being [Leipzig](17955.md). The Deutsche Nationalbibliothek is a [universal library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_library) in Germany. Its task, unique in Germany, is to collect, permanently archive, comprehensively document and record bibliographically all German and German-language publications from 1913 on, foreign publications about Germany, translations of German works and the works of German-speaking emigrants published abroad between 1933 and 1945, and to make them available to the public.[^cite_note-86]

The Eschenheim Tower (*[Eschenheimer Turm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschenheimer_Turm)*) was erected at the beginning of the 15th century and served as a city gate as part of late-medieval fortifications. It is the oldest and most unaltered building in the [Innenstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innenstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district.

### St. Catherine's Church

[St. Catherine's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catherine's_Church,_Frankfurt) is the largest [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Church_in_Germany) church, dedicated to [Catherine of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Alexandria), a [martyred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr) early Christian saint. It is located downtown at the entrance to the Zeil, the central pedestrian shopping street.

### Hauptwache



[Hauptwache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauptwache_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [St. Catherine's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catherine's_Church,_Frankfurt)

Although today [Hauptwache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauptwache_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) is mostly associated with the inner-city underground train station [of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Main\)_Hauptwache_station), the name originates from a [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) building on the square above the station. The Hauptwache building was constructed in 1730 and was used as a prison, therefore the name that translates as "main guard-house". Today the square surrounding the building is also called "Hauptwache" (formal "An der Hauptwache"). It is situated downtown opposite to St. Catherine's Church.

### Frankfurter Hof

The Frankfurter Hof is a landmark downtown hotel at Kaiserplatz, built from 1872 to 1876. It is part of [Steigenberger Hotels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steigenberger_Hotels) group and is considered the city's most prestigious.

### St. Leonhard

[St. Leonhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Leonhard,_Frankfurt), on the Main close to the bridge Eiserner Steg, is a Catholic [late Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) [hall church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_church), derived from a [Romanesque style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture) [basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica) beginning in 1425. It is the only one of nine churches in the Old Town that survived World War II almost undamaged. The parish serves the English-speaking community. The church has been under restoration from 2011 until 2019.[^cite_note-87]

### History of high-rise buildings

See also: [List of tallest buildings in Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Frankfurt)



The original Henninger Turm in 2007



Skyline seen from Deutschherrnbrücke (2026)

Frankfurt Skyline at River Main

Bridges over River Main in Frankfurt 2023

The [Henninger Turm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henninger_Turm) was opened in the 1960s and the silo was topped by a [revolving restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolving_restaurant) designed to be a landmark. The Henninger Turm was extensively refurbished and is now a residential high-rise.[^cite_note-93]

The first high-rise building boom came in the 1970s when [Westend Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westend_Gate) (then called *Plaza Büro Center*) and [Silberturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silberturm) were constructed and became the tallest buildings in Germany with a height of 159.3 meters and 166.3 meters, respectively. Around the same time, [Frankfurter Büro Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_B%C3%BCro_Center) and [City-Haus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-Haus) (142.4 meters and 142.1 meters) were constructed at Mainzer Landstraße and [Eurotower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurotower_\(Frankfurt\)) (148.0 meters) and [Garden Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Tower) (127.0 meters; then called *Helaba-Hochhaus*) were constructed in the financial district.

None of the buildings constructed during the 1980s surpassed Silberturm. The most famous buildings from this decade are the [Deutsche Bank Twin Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bank_Twin_Towers) at Taunusanlage, both 155.0 meters tall.

The 1990s featured a second wave. [Messeturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messeturm), built on the trade fair site, reached a height of 256.5 meters (842 ft) and became the tallest building in Europe by 1991. It was overtaken by the 259 m-high (850 ft) [Commerzbank Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerzbank_Tower) in 1997. Other tall buildings from this decade are [Westendstrasse 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westendstrasse_1) (208 meters (682 ft)), [Main Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Tower) (200 meters (660 ft)) and [Trianon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trianon_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) (186 meters (610 ft)).

In 21st-century Frankfurt, more high-rise buildings and skyscrapers (e.g., [Skyper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyper), [Opernturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opernturm), [Tower 185](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_185), [Seat of the European Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_the_European_Central_Bank), [Taunusturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunusturm)) emerged, but none have surpassed Commerzbank Tower.

### Other tall structures



Top of the [Europaturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europaturm), a 337 m (1,106 ft) communications tower

- [Europaturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europaturm) — The Europe Tower is a [telecommunications tower](2044496.md), also known as the Frankfurt TV Tower, built from 1974 to 1979. With a height of 337.5 meters it is the tallest tower and the second tallest structure in Germany after the [Fernsehturm Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernsehturm_Berlin). It was open to the public until 1999, with an entertainment establishment in the revolving top. It is normally referred to by locals as the "Ginnheimer Spargel" (*Ginnheim Asparagus*), but stands a few meters within [Bockenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bockenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district.

- [Henninger Turm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henninger_Turm) — The Henninger Tower was a 120-mete-high [grain silo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_silo) built from 1959 to 1961 and owned by [Henninger Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henninger_Brewery). It was the highest structure until 1974. The Henninger Tower had two rotating restaurants at the height of 101 and 106 meters and an open-air observation deck at the height of 110 meters. The tower closed to the public in October 2002 and was demolished in 2013 to be replaced by a 140 m (459 ft) tall residential tower, which is externally inspired by the old Henninger Turm. The cornerstone for this project was laid in June 2014 and construction was completed in summer 2017. The new tower offers 207 luxury flats

  [^cite_note-94]

  &#x20;and houses the non-rotating restaurant "Franziska". From 1962 to 2008 a famous yearly [cycling race](1755460.md) was named after the tower, the "Radrennen Rund um den Henninger Turm" (*Cycling race around Henninger Tower*). The now-renamed race is still a yearly event.

- [Goetheturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_Tower) – The Goethe Tower was a 43 m-high (141 ft) tower on the northern edge of the [Frankfurt City Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_City_Forest) in [Sachsenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)). It was the fifth tallest wood construction structure in Germany. It was built in 1931 and was a popular place for day-trippers until it burned down in 2017. A faithful reconstruction has been opened to the public on 12 October 2020, exactly three years after the original's destruction.

## Parks and green spaces



[Frankfurt City Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_City_Forest)

With a large forest, many parks, the Main riverbanks and the two botanical gardens, Frankfurt is considered a "green city": More than 50 percent of the area within the city limits are protected green areas.[^cite_note-96]

- Frankfurter Grüngürtel – The Green Belt is a ring-shaped public green space around the city. With 8,000 [ha](24919513.md) it covers a third of the administrative area. It includes the Frankfurter Stadtwald (*[Frankfurt City Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_City_Forest)*, Germany's largest forest within a city), the Schwanheimer Düne (*[Schwanheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwanheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) [Dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune)*), the Niddatal (*[Nidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidda_\(river\)) [Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley)*), the Niddapark, the Lohrberg (*Lohr Mountain*, Frankfurt's only [vineyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyard)), the Huthpark, the Enkheimer Ried (*[Enkheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt-Bergen-Enkheim) [Marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh)*), the Seckbacher Ried (*[Seckbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seckbach_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) [Marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh)*) and the Fechenheimer Mainbogen (a S-shaped part of the [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)) river in [Fechenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fechenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\))). The Green Belt is a [protected area](100406.md) which means that housing is not allowed. The Green Belt was formally created in 1991 with its own constitution.

- Mainuferpark – The Mainuferpark (*Main Riverbanks Park*) is the common term to describe the inner-city Main riverbanks. It is an auto-free zone with large green areas that is popular with strollers and tourists, especially in the summertime, when it can become crowded. The southern riverbank, which continues further to [Offenbach am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offenbach_am_Main) and [Hanau](442088.md), offers the best skyline views. The northern riverbank ends in the west at the former Westhafen (*West Harbor*, a residential housing area) and is growing to the east: A former industrial-used area between the new [Seat of the European Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_the_European_Central_Bank) and the Osthafen (*East Harbor*) has become a park named Hafenpark (*Harbor Park*), which offers outdoor courts for basketball, soccer and a skatepark.

- Wallanlagen – The [Wallanlagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallanlagen) (former *[ramparts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_wall)*) relate to the former ring-shaped [city wall fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_wall) around the [Altstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and the [Innenstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innenstadt_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district (abolished 1804–1812), now a series of parks. Building is not allowed, with a few exceptions, the most famous being the [Alte Oper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alte_Oper) (built 1880) at the Opernplatz. The part between the northern Main riverbank and the Opernplatz, referred to officially as Taunusanlage and Gallusanlage, is locally known as "Central Park" (a reference to the [famous park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park) in [Manhattan](45470.md)), because of the skyscrapers which stand on both sides.

- Nizza Park – At the juncture of the northern Main riverbank and the Wallanlagen is a famous small park called Nizza. The name of the park recalls [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) in southern France, because it is one of the warmest areas with a nearly [mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate). Numerous [Mediterranean flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_flora) grow there and can survive outside during the winter.

- Garten des Himmlischen Friedens – "Garden of Heavenly Peace", named after the [Tiananmen Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiananmen_Square) in Beijing, is a Chinese-styled park in the [Nordend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district and part of the larger Bethmannpark. It contains Chinese buildings, with building materials imported from China and built by Chinese workers in the 1980s. Hosts traditional Chinese plants and herbs.

- Niddapark – after the economic failure of a Federal Garden Show the area was opened to the public as a public park in 1990 without elaborately designed plantings. With 168 hectares, the Niddapark is the largest public park in Frankfurt. The park is popular with runners, walkers, cyclists, dog owners, families, and is used for weekend leisure activities. Apart from sports fields, one fitness trail is open to the general public.

  [^cite_note-97]

- Other parks – the Ostpark (32 ha) and the [Grüneburgpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%BCneburgpark) (29 ha).

### Performing arts

#### Music

[Eurodance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurodance) and [Trance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trance_music) originated in Frankfurt. In 1989 German producers Michael Münzing and Luca Anzilotti (under the pseudonyms Benito Benites and John "Virgo" Garrett III) formed the [Snap!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap!) project. Snap! songs combined [Rap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop) and [Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music) vocals adding rhythm by using computer technology and mixing electronic sounds, bass and drums. By doing so a new genre was born: Eurodance.[^cite_note-102] In the early 1990s, DJs including [Sven Väth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven_V%C3%A4th) and DJ DAG (of [Dance 2 Trance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_2_Trance)) first played a harder, deeper style of [acid house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_house) that became popular worldwide over the next decade as Trance music. Some of the early and most influential Eurodance, Trance and [Techno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno_music) acts, e.g., [La Bouche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Bouche), [Jam and Spoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jam_and_Spoon), [Magic Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Affair), [Culture Beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_Beat), [Snap!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap!), [Dance 2 Trance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_2_Trance), [Oliver Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Lieb) and [Hardfloor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardfloor), and record labels such as [Harthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harthouse) and [Eye Q](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_Q_\(record_label\)), were based in the city in the early 1990s.

### Festivals



The Museumsuferfest in 2026

- Museumsuferfest – *Museums Riverbank Festival* is one of Germany's biggest cultural festivals, attracting more than 3 million visitors over three days at the end of August along the [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)) riverbank downtown. The 20 museums there open far into the night. It offers live music, dance shows, booths for crafts, jewelry, clothes and food stands from around the world.



Dippemess in Spring 2026

- Dippemess – Frankfurt's oldest folk festival is the *Festival of Stoneware*, which takes place semi-annually around Easter and the end of September in the eastern area. "Dippe" is a regional [Hessian dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessian_dialects) word meaning "pot" or "jar" which would not be understood in most other German regions. Mentioned for the first time in the 14th century as an annual marketplace it is now more of an amusement park. The name of the festival derives from its original purpose when it was a fair where traditionally crafted jars, pots and other stoneware were on offer.

- Luminale — The "festival of light" has taken place biannually since 2000, parallel to the *Light + building* exhibition at the trade fair. Many buildings are specially lit for the event. In 2008, more than 220 light installations could be seen, attracting 100,000 visitors.



Wäldchestag in 2026

- Wäldchestag – *Day of the forest* is known as a regional holiday because until the 1990s it was common that Frankfurt's shops were closed on this day. The festival takes place over four days after [Pentecost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecost) with the formal Wäldchestag on Tuesday. Its unique location is in the [Frankfurt City Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_City_Forest), south-west of downtown in [Niederrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niederrad). "Wäldches" is a regional dialect of the German word "Wäldchen", meaning "small forest".

- Nacht der Museen – *Night of the museums* takes place every year in April or May. 50 museums in Frankfurt and in the neighboring city of [Offenbach am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offenbach_am_Main) are open until 2\:00 am surrounded by special music events, dance performances, readings and guided tours. A free shuttle operates between the museums. In 2010, approximately 40,000 visitors attended.

- Nacht der Clubs – *Night of the clubs* is an event similar to Nacht der Museen: On one night as many as 20 clubs can be visited with a single ticket for €12. Usually, club-door policies are loosened to attract new customers. A free shuttle runs between the clubs. 15,000 people participated in 2008.

- Wolkenkratzer Festival — The *Skyscraper Festival* is unique in Germany. It takes place irregularly, lately in May 2013, and attracted around 1.2 million visitors. For two days most skyscrapers are open to the public. Sky-divers, base jumpers, fireworks and laser shows are extra attractions.

### Nightlife

Frankfurt offers a variety of restaurants, bars, pubs and clubs. Clubs concentrate in and around downtownand in the [Ostend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district, mainly close to Hanauer Landstraße. Restaurants, bars and pubs concentrate in [Sachsenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Nordend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordend_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), [Bornheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) and [Bockenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bockenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)).

In [electronic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music), Frankfurt was a pioneering city in the late 1980s and early 1990s, with renowned DJs including [Sven Väth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven_V%C3%A4th), [Marc Trauner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Trauner), [Scot Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scot_Project) and [Kai Tracid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Tracid). One of the main venues of the early [Trance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trance_music) sound was the [Omen nightclub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omen_nightclub?action=edit\&redlink=1) from 1988 to 1998. Another popular disco club of the 1980s–1990s and a hotspot for Techno/Trance music was the [Dorian Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorian_Gray_\(club\)), which was located within Terminal 1 at Frankfurt Airport from 1978 to 2000. Further popular venues were the [U60311](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U60311?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1998–2012) and the [Coocoon Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoon_\(club\)) in [Fechenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fechenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) (2004–2012). Notable live music venues of the past include the [Sinkkasten Arts Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinkkasten_Arts_Club?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1971–2011) and the [King Kamehameha Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Kamehameha_Club?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1999–2013).

Among the most popular active rock and pop concert venues is the [Batschkapp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batschkapp) in [Seckbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seckbach_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), which opened in 1976 as a center for autonomous and left-wing counterculture.

### Culinary specialties

See also: [Hessian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessian_cuisine)



"Bembel" (jug) and "Geripptes" (glass)



Original *Frankfurter Würstchen* served with [potato salad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato_salad)

- [Apfelwein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apfelwein) – *Apple wine* or *hard cider* is regionally known as "Ebbelwoi", "Äppler" or "Stöffsche". It has an alcohol content of 5.5%–7% and a tart, sour taste. It is traditionally served in a glass, typically decorated with lozenges, called "Geripptes", a full glass is then called "Schoppen". Apfelwein is also available in a stoneware jar locally known as "Bembel". A group normally orders a "Bembel" and shares the contents. Apfelwein can be ordered as "sauergespritzer", which is apfelwein blended with 30% mineral water or as "süssgespritzer", which is Apfelwein blended with lemon soda, orange soda or fresh-pressed apple juice (lemon soda being the most common). Most of the pubs which serve Apfelwein are located in [Sachsenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), which is therefore known as "Ebbelwoi district". Due to its national drink Frankfurt is sometimes called "Big Ebbel" (pronunciation with [Hessian dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessian_dialects)), an homage to [Big Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Apple), the famous nickname of New York City.

- [Grüne Soße](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_sauce#German_Gr%C3%BCne_So%C3%9Fe) – *Green sauce* is a sauce made with hard-boiled eggs, oil, vinegar, salt and a generous amount of seven fresh herbs, namely borage, sorrel, garden cress, chervil, chives, parsley and salad burnet. Variants, often due to seasonal availability include dill, lovage, lemon balm and spinach. Original green sauce Frankfurt-style is made of herbs that were gathered only on fields within the city limits.

- [Frankfurter Würstchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_W%C3%BCrstchen) – "short Frankfurter" is a small [sausage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sausage) made of smoked pork. They are similar to [hot dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_dog). The name Frankfurter Würstchen has been trademarked since 1860.

- [Frankfurter Rindswurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Rindswurst) – Sausage made of pure beef.

- [Frankfurter Rippchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Rippchen) – Also known as Rippchen mit Kraut, this is a traditional dish which consists of cured pork cutlets, slowly heated in [sauerkraut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerkraut) or meat broth, and usually served with sauerkraut, mashed potatoes and yellow mustard.

- [Handkäs mit Musik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handk%C3%A4se) – German regional [sour milk cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sour_milk_cheese) (similar to [Harzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harzer)) and a culinary specialty in the [Rhine Main Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Rhine-Main). The traditional way of producing it is by hand. When it is topped with chopped onions it becomes "Handkäs mit Musik" (with music) because the onions are supposed to stimulate flatulence.

- [Frankfurter Kranz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Kranz) – Cake speciality believed to originate from Frankfurt.

- [Bethmännchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethm%C3%A4nnchen) – "A little Bethmann" is a pastry made from marzipan with almond, powdered sugar, rosewater, flour, and egg. It is usually baked for Christmas.

## Quality of life

In a 2001 ranking by the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool), Frankfurt was rated the richest city in Europe by [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita), followed by [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe), [Paris](22989.md) and [Munich](19058.md).[^cite_note-108]

Frankfurt was voted the seventh in the [Mercer Quality of Living Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_most_livable_cities) by the Mercer Quality of Living Survey (2012),[^cite_note-109] seventh in the Mercer Quality of Living Survey (2010) and 18th at the [Economist's](15303116.md) World's Most Liveable Cities Survey (2011).[^cite_note-110] According to an annual citizen survey (2010), arranged by the city council, 66 percent inhabitants are satisfied or highly satisfied with the city, while only 6 percent said that they are dissatisfied. Compared to the 1993's survey the number of satisfied inhabitants has grown about 22 percent while the number of dissatisfied inhabitants was reduced by 8 percent. 84 percent of the inhabitants like to live in Frankfurt, 13 percent would rather choose to live somewhere else. 37 percent are satisfied with the public safety (1993: only 9 percent), 22 percent are dissatisfied (1993: 64 percent).[^cite_note-111]

Frankfurt consistently has the highest levels of crime per 100,000 inhabitants in Germany (15.976 crimes per annum in 2008) and is therefore dubbed the German "crime capital".[^cite_note-112] However, this statistic is often criticized because it ignores major factors: It is calculated based on the administrative 680,000-inhabitant figure while the urban area has 2.5 M inhabitants and on weekdays adds another million people (not counting the 53 million passengers passing through the airport each year). The rate for personal safety-relevant crimes such as murder, manslaughter, rape or bodily harm, is 3.4 percent, placing Frankfurt twelfth in the ranking (related to the official 680,000-inhabitant figure) or number 21 (related to the one-million-figure).[^cite_note-113] In 2018, the state of Hesse, where Frankfurt is located, was ranked the third-safest state in Germany.[^cite_note-114]

## Transport

### Airports

#### Frankfurt Airport

Main article: [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport)



[Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) (with the fourth runway under construction in 2010) and the [Frankfurter Kreuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Kreuz) (lower right corner)

The city can be accessed from around the world via [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport) (*Flughafen Frankfurt am Main*) located 12 km (7 mi) southwest of downtown. The airport has four [runways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway) and serves 265 nonstop destinations. Run by transport company [Fraport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraport) it ranks among the [world's busiest airports by passenger traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic) and is the [busiest airport by cargo traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_busiest_airports_by_cargo_traffic) in Europe. The airport also serves as a hub for [Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor_Flugdienst) and as the main hub for German [flag carrier](465816.md) [Lufthansa](97733.md). It is the busiest airport in Europe in terms of cargo traffic, and the fourth busiest in Europe in terms of passenger traffic behind [London Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport), [Paris Charles de Gaulle Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport) and [Amsterdam Airport Schiphol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Airport_Schiphol). Passenger traffic at Frankfurt Airport in 2018 was 69,510,269 passengers.

A third terminal is being constructed (planned to open in 2026). The third terminal will increase the capacity of the airport to more than 90 million passengers per year.[^cite_note-115]

The airport can be reached by car or bus and has two railway stations, one for regional and one for long-distance traffic. The [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn) lines S8 and S9 (direction *Offenbach Ost* or *Hanau Hbf*) departing at the [regional station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport_regional_station) take 10–15 minutes from the airport to [Frankfurt Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Central_Station) and onwards to [Hauptwache station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Hauptwache_station) downtown. The [IC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity) and [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity-Express) trains departing at the [long-distance station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport_long-distance_station) take 10 minutes to Frankfurt Central Station.

#### Frankfurt Hahn Airport

Despite the name, [Frankfurt Hahn Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt%E2%80%93Hahn_Airport) (*Flughafen Frankfurt-Hahn*) is situated approximately 120 km (75 mi) from the city in [Lautzenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lautzenhausen) ([Rhineland-Palatinate](26239.md)). Hahn Airport is a major base for [low-cost carrier](428422.md) [Ryanair](262878.md). This airport can only be reached by car or bus. An hourly bus service runs from [Frankfurt Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Hauptbahnhof), taking just over 2 hours.[^cite_note-116] Passenger traffic at Hahn Airport in 2010 was 3.5 million.

#### Frankfurt Egelsbach Airport

[Frankfurt Egelsbach Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Egelsbach_Airport) (*Flugplatz Frankfurt-Egelsbach*) is a busy [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) airport located south-east of Frankfurt Airport, near [Egelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egelsbach).

### Roads



[Frankfurter Kreuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Kreuz)

Frankfurt is a traffic hub for the German motorway (*[Autobahn](1764276.md)*) system. The [Frankfurter Kreuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurter_Kreuz) is an Autobahn interchange close to the airport, where the [Bundesautobahn 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_3) (A3), [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) to [Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg), and the [Bundesautobahn 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_5) (A5), [Basel](4911.md) to [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover), meet. With approximately 320,000 cars passing through it every day, it is Europe's most heavily used interchange. The [Bundesautobahn 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_66) (A66) connects Frankfurt with [Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesbaden) in the west and [Fulda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulda) in the east. The [Bundesautobahn 661](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_661) (A661) is mainly a commuter motorway that starts in the south (Egelsbach), runs through the eastern part and ends in the north ([Oberursel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberursel)). The [Bundesautobahn 648](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_648) (A648) is a very short motorway in the western part which primarily serves as a fast connection between the A 66 and the [Frankfurt Trade Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Trade_Fair). The A5 in the west, the A3 in the south and the A661 in the northeast form a [ring road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road) around the inner city districts and define a [Low-emission zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-emission_zone) (*Umweltzone*; established in 2008), meaning that vehicles have to meet certain emission criteria to enter the zone.

The streets of central Frankfurt are usually congested with cars during [rush hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_hour). Some areas, especially around the shopping streets Zeil, Goethestraße and Freßgass, are pedestrian-only streets.

### Railway stations

#### Frankfurt Central Station



[Frankfurt Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Main\)_Hauptbahnhof)



[S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn) at [Central Station (underground)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Central_Station_\(underground_station\))

Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof *(*&#x6F;ften abbreviated as *Frankfurt (Main) Hbf* or *F-Hbf*) is the largest railway station in Germany by railway traffic. By daily passenger volume, it ranks second (493,000 each) after [Hamburg Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Hauptbahnhof) (550,000). It is located between the [Gallus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallus_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)), the [Gutleutviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutleutviertel) and the [Bahnhofsviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahnhofsviertel_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) district, not far away from the trade fair and the financial district. It serves as a major hub for long-distance trains ([InterCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity), [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCityExpress)) and regional trains as well as for Frankfurt's public transport system. It is a stop for most of ICE high-speed lines, making it Germany's most important ICE station. ICE Trains to London via the [Channel Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Tunnel) were planned for 2013.[^cite_note-117] All [Rhine-Main S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Main_S-Bahn) lines, two [U-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_U-Bahn) lines (U4, U5), several tram and bus lines stop there. Regional and local trains are integrated in the Public transport system [Rhein-Main-Verkehrsverbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhein-Main-Verkehrsverbund) (RMV), the second-largest integrated public transport system in the world, after [Verkehrsverbund Berlin-Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkehrsverbund_Berlin-Brandenburg).

#### Frankfurt Airport stations



[ICE 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICE_3) departing westward from [Frankfurt Airport long-distance station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport_long-distance_station) underneath [The Squaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Squaire)

Frankfurt Airport can be accessed by two railway stations: [Frankfurt Airport long-distance station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport_long-distance_station) (*Frankfurt Flughafen Fernbahnhof*) is only for long-distance traffic and connects the airport to the main rail network, with most of the [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCityExpress) services using the [Cologne-Frankfurt high-speed rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne-Frankfurt_high-speed_rail_line). The long-distance station is located outside the actual airport ground but has a connecting bridge for pedestrians to Terminal 1, concourse B. [Frankfurt Airport regional station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport_regional_station) (*Frankfurt Flughafen Regionalbahnhof*) is for local [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Main_S-Bahn) trains (lines S8, S9) and regional trains. The regional station is located within Terminal 1, concourse B.

#### Frankfurt South station

Frankfurt's third long-distance station is [Frankfurt South station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_South_station) (*Frankfurt Südbahnhof*, often abbreviated as *Frankfurt (Main) Süd* or *F-Süd*), located in [Sachsenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)). It is an important destination for local trains and trams (lines 15, 16 and 18) and the terminal stop for four U-Bahn lines (U1, U2, U3, U8) as well as three S-Bahn lines (S3, S4, S5). Line S6 also serves the station.

#### Messe stations

The [Frankfurt Trade Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Trade_Fair) offers two railway stations: [Messe station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Messe_station) is for local [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Main_S-Bahn) trains (lines S3-S6) and is centrally located amid trade fair premises, while Festhalle/Messe station is served by U-Bahn line U4 and is located at the north-east corner of the premises.

#### Konstablerwache station and Hauptwache station

Two other major downtown railway stations are Konstablerwache and Hauptwache, located on each end of the Zeil. They are the main stations to change from east-to-west-bound S-Bahn trains to north-to-south-bound U-Bahn trains. Konstablerwache station is the second-busiest railway station regarding daily passenger volume (98,000) after the central station. The third-busiest railway station is Hauptwache station (93,000).[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119]

#### Frankfurt West Station



[DBAG Class 423](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DBAG_Class_423) approaching the [elevated section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevated_railway) of [Frankfurt West station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_West_station)

This Station, located in Bockenheim, is served by north-heading Long-Distance ICE trains, multiple regional trains, and four commuter S-Bahn lines (S3, S4, S5, S6). Additionally, it is an important terminal stop for three "Metrobus" lines (M32, M36, M73).

### Public transport

Main article: [Public transport in Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_in_Frankfurt_am_Main)



Public transport network



Rhein-Main-Verkehrsverbund (RMV)

The city has two [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) systems: the U-Bahn and the S-Bahn, as well as an above-ground tram system. Information about the U- and S-Bahn can be found on the website of the [RMV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhein-Main-Verkehrsverbund).[^cite_note-121]

#### S-Bahn

Main article: [Rhine-Main S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Main_S-Bahn)

Nine [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn) lines (S1 to S9) connect Frankfurt with the densely populated [Rhine Main Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Rhein-Main_Region). Most routes have at least 15-minute service during the day, either by one line running every 15 minutes, or by two lines servicing one route at a 30-minute interval. All lines, except line S7, run through the [Frankfurt City Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_City_Tunnel) and serve stations at [Ostendstraße](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Ostendstra%C3%9Fe_station), [Konstablerwache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Main\)_Konstablerwache_station), [Hauptwache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Main\)_Hauptwache_station), [Taunusanlage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taunusanlage_station) and [Frankfurt Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Central_Station). When leaving the city the S-Bahn travels above ground. It provides access to the trade fair (S3, S4, S5, S6), the airport (S8, S9), the stadium (S7, S8, S9) and nearby cities such as [Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesbaden), [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz), [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt), [Rüsselsheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCsselsheim), [Hanau](442088.md), [Offenbach am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offenbach_am_Main), [Oberursel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberursel), [Bad Homburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Homburg), [Kronberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronberg), [Friedberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedberg,_Hesse) and smaller towns that are on the way. The S8/S9 runs 24/7.

#### U-Bahn



Underground line *U7* running as a *[Stadtbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadtbahn)* amidst [Ludwig-Landmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Landmann)-Straße in [Frankfurt-Rödelheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6delheim)

Main article: [Frankfurt U-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_U-Bahn)

The [U-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-Bahn) has nine lines (U1 to U9) serving Frankfurt and the larger suburbs of Bad Homburg and Oberursel in the north. The trains that run on the U-Bahn are in fact [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) (*[Stadtbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadtbahn)*) as many lines travel along a track in the middle of the street instead of underground. The minimum service interval is 2.5 minutes, although the usual pattern is that each line runs at 7.5- to 10-minute intervals, which produce between 3- and 5-minute intervals on downtown tracks shared by more than one line.

#### Tram

Main article: [Trams in Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Frankfurt_am_Main)

Frankfurt has ten tram lines (11, 12, 14 to 21), with trams arriving usually every 10 minutes. Many sections are served by two lines, combining to run at 5-minute intervals during rush-hour. Trams only run above ground and serve more stops than the U-Bahn or the S-Bahn.

#### Bus

Main article: [Public transport in Frankfurt am Main § Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_in_Frankfurt_am_Main#Bus)

A number of bus lines complete the Frankfurt public transport system. Night buses replace U-Bahn and tram services between 1\:30 am and 3\:30 am.[^cite_note-122] The central junction for the night bus service is at the downtown square of Konstablerwache, where all night bus lines start and end.

### Taxis

[Taxicabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab) can usually be found outside the major S-Bahn and U-Bahn stations, at the central station, the south station, the airport, the trade fair and in the crowded inner-city shopping streets. The common way to obtain a taxi is to either call a taxi operator or to go to a taxi rank. However, although not the norm, one can hail a passing taxi on the street.

[Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber) ceased operations in Frankfurt on 9 November 2015 after operating in the city for 18 months.[^cite_note-123] However, [UberX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UberX) and local cabs are available through the Uber app.[^cite_note-124]

### Bicycles



Velotaxi at the Zeil

[Deutsche Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn) makes bicycles available for hire through their [Call a Bike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_a_Bike) service. The bicycles are stationed all over the city, including at selected railway stations. They can easily be spotted because of their eye-catching silver-red color. To rent a specific bike, riders either call a service number to get an unlock code or reserve the bike via the smartphone application. To return the bike, the rider locks it within a designated return area (and calls the service number, if not booked via the app).[^cite_note-125]

[Nextbike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nextbike) also makes bicycles available for hire in Frankfurt. They are stationed all over the city. These can be spotted with their blue color scheme.

[Cycle rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_rickshaw) (velotaxis), a type of [tricycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricycle) designed to carry passengers in addition to the driver, are also available. These are allowed to operate in pedestrian-only areas and are therefore practical for sightseeing.

Frankfurt has a network of cycle routes. Many long-distance bike routes into the city have cycle tracks that are separate from motor vehicle traffic. A number of downtown roads are "bicycle streets" where the cyclist has the right of way and where motorized vehicles are only allowed access if they do not disrupt the cycle users. In addition, cyclists are allowed to ride many cramped one-way streets in both directions. As of 2015, 15 percent of citizens used bicycles.[^cite_note-126]

### E-Scooters

Since 15 June 2019, the use of [e-scooters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-scooters) was officially permitted by the German federal government. In Frankfurt, companies like [Lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lime_\(transportation_company\)), TIER, [Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_\(transportation_company\)), voi., [Dott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dott_\(transportation_company\)) or [Bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolt_\(company\)) are offering their electric micro mobility vehicles for lease. However, their use is being regarded with increasing weariness due to frequent abuse (parking, speeding, vandalism, accidents) and has sparked a public debate about the need of further regulation of the e-scooter market.[^cite_note-127]

## Financial authorities



[Westhafen Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westhafen_Tower), home to the European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority (EIOPA)

### European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority

The [European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Insurance_and_Occupational_Pensions_Authority) (EIOPA) is an institution of the EU and part of the [European System of Financial Supervisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_System_of_Financial_Supervisors) that was created in response to the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis). It was established on 1 January 2011.

### Federal Financial Supervisory Authority

Frankfurt is one of two locations of the German [Federal Financial Supervisory Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Financial_Supervisory_Authority) (*Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht*, short: *BaFin*). The BaFin is an independent federal institution and acts as Germany's [financial regulatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_regulation) authority.

### Anti-Money Laundering Authority

The Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA) of the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) is based in Frankfurt.[^cite_note-135]

## Education and research

### Universities and schools

Frankfurt hosts two universities and several specialist schools. The two business schools are [Goethe University Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt)'s [Goethe Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_Business_School) and [Frankfurt School of Finance & Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_School_of_Finance_&_Management).

#### Johann Wolfgang Goethe University



[Johann Wolfgang Goethe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Wolfgang_Goethe_University)

The oldest and best-known university is the [Johann Wolfgang Goethe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Wolfgang_Goethe_University), with locations in Bockenheim, Westend, and Riedberg, and the university hospital in Niederrad. [Goethe Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_Business_School) is part of the university's [House of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Finance) at Campus Westend. The Business School's Full-Time MBA program has more than 70% international students.

#### Frankfurt University of Applied Sciences

The [Frankfurt University of Applied Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_University_of_Applied_Sciences) was created out of several older organisations in 1971, and offers more than 38 study areas, in the arts, sciences, engineering and law. Some of the most important research projects: Planet Earth Simulator, [FraLine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FraLine)-IT-School-Service, quantitative analysis of [methane](18582230.md) in human corpses with the help of a mass spectrometer, software engineering (e.g., fraDesk), analysis of qualitative and quantitative gas in human lungs, long-term studies on [photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic) modules (to name only a few).

#### Frankfurt School of Finance and Management

The city is also home to a business school, [Frankfurt School of Finance & Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_School_of_Finance_&_Management), formerly known as the *_Hochschule für Bankwirtschaft_* (Institution of Higher Learning for Banking Economics), with its new campus near Deutsche Nationalbibliothek U-Bahn stop (recently moving from its previous location in the Ostend (Eastend) neighborhood). In 2001, it became a specialist institution for Economics and Management, or FOM. Frankfurt School is consistently ranked among the best business schools in the world, attributed to its high research output and quality of undergraduate and graduate training.[^cite_note-136]

#### Städelschule

Frankfurt has the State Institution of Higher Learning for Artistic Education known as the [Städelschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A4delschule), founded in 1817 by [Johann Friedrich Städel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Friedrich_St%C3%A4del). It was taken over by the city in 1942 and turned into a state art school.

#### Music schools and conservatory

Music institutions are the [Frankfurt University of Music and Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_University_of_Music_and_Performing_Arts), and the [Hoch Conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoch_Conservatory) (Dr. Hoch's Konservatorium) which was founded in 1878. The International [Ensemble Modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_Modern) Academy is a significant institution for the study of contemporary music.[^cite_note-137]

#### Other notable schools

The [Sankt Georgen Graduate School of Philosophy and Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankt_Georgen_Graduate_School_of_Philosophy_and_Theology) (German:*Philosophisch-Theologische Hochschule Sankt Georgen*), a private institution with membership in the German Jesuit Association, has been located in [Sachsenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) since 1950.

### Research institutes



[Max Planck Institute for Brain Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Brain_Research)

The city is home to three [Max Planck Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society) institutes: the [Max Planck Institute for Legal History and Legal Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Legal_History_and_Legal_Theory), [Max Planck Institute for Biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Biophysics), and the [Max Planck Institute for Brain Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Brain_Research).

The [Frankfurt Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies), sponsored by several institutional and private sources, is involved in theoretical research in physics, chemistry, neuroscience, and computer science.

Frankfurt is host to the *Römisch-Germanische-Kommission* (RGK), the [German Archaeological Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Archaeological_Institute) branch for [prehistoric archeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory) in Germany and Europe. The RGK is involved in a variety of research projects. Its library, with more than 200,000 items, is one of the largest archeological libraries in the world.[^cite_note-138]

[Goethe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt) and [Frankfurt University of Applied Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_University_of_Applied_Sciences) are involved in the Hessian Center for Artificial Intelligence ("hessian.AI").

### International Finance Corporation

Frankfurt is home to the German office of the [International Finance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Finance_Corporation) (IFC), which is part of the [World Bank](36752.md) Group. The IFC promotes sustainable private sector investment in [developing countries](78449.md).

### Consulates

Frankfurt hosts 93 diplomatic missions (consulates and [consulates-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate-_general)).[^cite_note-142] The [Consulate General of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate_General_of_the_United_States_in_Frankfurt) in [Eckenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckenheim_\(Frankfurt_am_Main\)) is the largest American consulate in the world.[^cite_note-143]
