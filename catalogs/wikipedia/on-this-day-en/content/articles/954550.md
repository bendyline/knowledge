A **mining accident** is an accident that occurs during the process of [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) [minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) or metals. Thousands of miners die from mining accidents each year, especially from [underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_mining_\(hard_rock\)) [coal mining](208413.md), although accidents also occur in [hard rock mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_mining_\(hard_rock\)). Coal mining is considered much more hazardous than hard rock mining due to flat-lying rock strata, generally incompetent rock, the presence of [methane](18582230.md) gas, and [coal dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_dust). Most of the deaths these days occur in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries), and rural parts of [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_countries) where safety measures are not practiced as fully. A **mining disaster** is an incident where there are five or more fatalities.[^cite_note-1]

## Causes

Mining accidents can occur from a variety of causes, including leaks of [poisonous gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisonous_gas) such as [hydrogen sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide)[^cite_note-2] or explosive natural gases, especially [firedamp](505455.md) or [methane](18582230.md),[^cite_note-3] [dust explosions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_explosions), collapsing of mine [stopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoping_\(mining_method\)), mining-[induced seismicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_seismicity), flooding, or general mechanical errors from improperly used or malfunctioning mining equipment (such as [safety lamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_lamp) or [electrical equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_equipment)). The use of improper [explosives](10192.md) underground can also cause methane and [coal dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_dust) explosions.

## Worst mining disaster in history

On April 26, 1942, in the [Benxihu (Honkeiko)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benxihu_Colliery) [coal mine](208413.md) in Liaoning Province, China, what is believed to be the worst mining disaster in history occurred when a [coal dust explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_dust_explosion) killed over 1,500 people.[^cite_note-ten_worst-4]

The disaster occurred in an area that is now within the borders of modern-day China but was at the time part of the [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) of [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo) established by Japan after it invaded and occupied northeast China in the 1930s. The Japanese administrators of the mine forced Chinese labourers to conduct the mining work under harsh conditions. The disaster began with a fire in the mine. In order to suppress it, the Japanese operators cut off the air in the ventilation shafts and blocked off the mine so as to deprive the blaze of oxygen. Most workers were not evacuated before these actions, and they were trapped within the sealed-off area of the mine; they suffocated to death as the fire burned off oxygen and led to [carbon monoxide poisoning](488743.md). Once the fire died out and the mine was re-opened, ten days were required for workers to remove debris and reach the bodies of those who had been trapped inside the mine.[^cite_note-worst_mining_disasters-5]

The dead consisted of 1,518 Chinese and 31 Japanese. Most of the bodies were later buried in a mass grave. After the war and liberation of China by the Soviet Union, the disaster was investigated. The Soviet report concluded that the majority of the deaths were not caused directly by the initial fire but were the result of carbon monoxide poisoning and suffocation resulting from the decisions of the Japanese.[^cite_note-chinazhaoge-6]

### 20th century

Main category: [20th-century mining disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:20th-century_mining_disasters)

- **May 19, 1902:** 216 miners were killed in the [Fraterville Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraterville_Mine_disaster) in [Fraterville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraterville,_Tennessee)

  [^cite_note-85]

- **March 10, 1906:** [Courrières mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courri%C3%A8res_mine_disaster) in [Courrières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courri%C3%A8res), France. 1,099 workers died in the worst mine accident in European history.

- **December 1, 1907:** [Naomi Mine Explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Mine_explosion) in [Fayette City, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayette_City,_Pennsylvania). 34 workers died.

- **December 4, 1907** [Giroux Mining Accidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giroux_Mining_Accidents) [Ely, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ely,_Nevada). 2 dead; 3 trapped.

- **December 6, 1907:** [Monongah Mining disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongah_Mining_disaster) in [Monongah, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongah,_West_Virginia). Official death toll is 362, but due to inadequate record keeping, the true death toll could be around 500.

  [^cite_note-86]

  &#x20;Victims were mostly [Italian immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_American) workers, including children. The disaster is widely considered the worst coal mining accident in American history.

  [^cite_note-87]

- **December 16, 1907**: Yolande mine explosion near [Birmingham, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama). Fifty-seven killed.

  [^cite_note-88]

- **December 19, 1907:** [Darr Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darr_Mine_disaster) in [Rostraver Township, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostraver_Township,_Pennsylvania). 239 workers died, including children.

- **August 18, 1908:** [Maypole Colliery disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maypole_Colliery_disaster) in Abram, Greater Manchester, England. 75 men and boys died.

  [^cite_note-89]

- **November 28, 1908**: Marianna mine explosion near [Marianna, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianna,_Pennsylvania). 154 men killed, one survivor.

  [^cite_note-marianna-90]

- **November 13, 1909:** [Cherry Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Mine_disaster) in [Cherry, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry,_Illinois). 259 workers, some as young as eleven, died in this mine fire, which had the most fatalities of any mine fire in the United States.

- **December 21, 1910**: [The Pretoria Pit disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria_Pit_disaster) in Westhoughton, Lancashire, 344 men and boys lost their lives in this explosion, which is the worst mining disaster on one day in England.

- **January 20, 1911**: 40 coal miners die in [Sosnowiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosnowiec), [Russian Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland).

  [^cite_note-91]

- **April 8. 1911** [Banner Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banner_Mine_disaster) near Littleton, Alabama. Of the 128 men killed, most were leased Black convicts.

- **December 9, 1911:** [Cross Mountain Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Mountain_Mine_disaster) killed 84 miners in [Briceville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briceville,_Tennessee)

- **October 14, 1913:** [Senghenydd colliery disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senghenydd_colliery_disaster), the worst mining accident in the United Kingdom; 439 workers died in Wales.

- **October 22, 1913**: [Dawson Stag Canon Number 2 Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson,_New_Mexico#Disasters), near [Dawson, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson,_New_Mexico),

  [^cite_note-92]

  &#x20;where 263 workers were killed due to illegal use of dynamite.

- **April 28, 1914**: The Eccles mine disaster was an explosion of coal-seam, in Eccles, West Virginia. The explosion took the lives of at least 180 men and boys

- **June 8, 1917**: [Speculator Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculator_Mine_disaster) in [Butte, Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butte,_Montana). An electric cable being lowered into the mine was accidentally ignited at 2,500 feet below the surface. The fire quickly climbed the cable and ignited the mine's wooden shaft. The shaft became a chimney, eliminating the mine's primary source of oxygen. Nearly all of the 168 fatalities were due to asphyxia, from carbon monoxide poisoning. This is the deadliest underground hard rock mining disaster in United States history.

- **January 12, 1918** [Minnie Pit disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnie_Pit_disaster) in [Staffordshire](58733.md), England was a coal mining accident in which 155 men and boys died (144 from carbon monoxide poisoning and 11 from violence, plus carbon monoxide poisoning). The disaster, which was caused by an explosion due to firedamp, is the worst ever recorded in the North Staffordshire Coalfield. An official investigation never established what caused the ignition of flammable gases in the pit.

* **September 19, 1921** [Mount Mulligan mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mulligan_mine_disaster) in [Mount Mulligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mulligan), [Far North Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_North_Queensland), [Australia](4689264.md) killed 75. The disaster was caused by the accidental or negligent firing of an explosive charge on top of a block of coal, apparently in order to split it.

- **February 8, 1923** The [Dawson Stag Canon #1 Mine Explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson,_New_Mexico#Disasters) killed 123; many were descendants of men killed in the 1913 explosion at the same mine. As a mine car derailed, it caused sparks and ignited coal dust, causing the explosion.

- **March 8, 1924** The [Castle Gate Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Gate_Mine_disaster) killed 171 miners and one rescue worker. At least 24 mules, 16 canaries, and an unknown number of horses died in the disaster and rescue & recovery efforts. The Castle Gate mine disaster currently ranks as the 10th worst mining disaster in United States history

  [^cite_note-93]

  &#x20;and the second worst mining disaster in the history of the state of Utah, following the [Scofield Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scofield_Mine_disaster) of 1900, which killed 200 miners.

22 miners drowned in December 1882 in the Australasian number 2 deep lead gold mine at Creswick in Victoria.[^cite_note-127] The mine was violently flooded by a burst of water when miners digging a new drive approached too close to the abandoned and flooded Australasian number 1 workings, due to an error in surveying by the manager of the mine.[^cite_note-128] 5 miners survived and were rescued after 50 hours trapped underground. This disaster, with the highest death toll from a gold mine disaster in Australian history, left 63 children without fathers and 18 widows.[^cite_note-129]

The [Bulli Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulli,_New_South_Wales) of 23 March 1887 involved a [gas explosion](1919621.md) in the mine that killed 81 men and boys, leaving 50 women widows and 150 children without fathers.

The [Mount Kembla Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kembla#Mine_disaster) of 31 July 1902 was an explosion resulting in the death of 96 miners, including two engaged in rescue work. It remains the worst mining disaster in Australian history.

Tasmania's [Beaconsfield Mine collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaconsfield_Mine_collapse) occurred on 25 April 2006. Of the 17 people who were in the mine at the time, 14 escaped immediately following the collapse, one was killed and the remaining two were found alive after five days. The survivors were trapped in a 1.5m x 1.2m cherry picker cage, which had saved them from being crushed by rocks.[^cite_note-130] As it was not safe for rescuers to blast their way through, a special borer was brought in to drill an escape shaft. They were finally released on 9 May after 14 days underground.

Three mining disasters occurred at Moura in a 20-year period. The first of these was in 1975, at the Kianga Mine, where 13 men died in an underground explosion. The mine was sealed without their bodies being retrieved. In 1986 a second disaster occurred, as an underground explosion, which took the lives of 12 miners.[^cite_note-131] The bodies of all those persons were retrieved. In Moura on 7 August 1994 a third major mining accident occurred with an explosion at Moura No. 2 Mine. A second explosion at the mine approximately a day and a half later saw rescue attempts abandoned, and the mine was sealed, with the bodies of the 11 miners unretrieved.[^cite_note-132]

In the 1996 Gretley coal mine disaster, near Newcastle, four men were killed when their mining machine broke into the flooded workings of an old coalmine, abandoned over 80 years earlier.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

Four miners were killed in a windblast incident at the [Northparkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northparkes) mine outside the New South Wales town of Parkes in 1999.[^cite_note-abc-135]

A mine collapsed at [Ballarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballarat) Gold Mine in Victoria on March 14, 2024, resulting in a man dead, and another in critical condition. The two had been 'air legging' in a prohibited area under unsupported ground when the collapse occurred at 4\:50pm. 29 other miners took refuge in a safety pod and were later brought to safety. The air legging technique is to be no longer used temporarily while a work safe investigation is underway. [Australian Workers' Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Workers'_Union) organiser Ross Kenna said the geology of the mine is not suitable for the technique.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

### China

Main article: [List of coal mining accidents in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_coal_mining_accidents_in_China)

According to one source, in 2003 China accounted for the largest number of coal-mining fatalities, accounting for about 80% of the world's total, although it produced only 35% of the world's coal.[^cite_note-Xiaohui-145] Between January 2001 and October 2004, there were 188 accidents that had a death toll of more than 10, about one such accident every 7.4 days.[^cite_note-Xiaohui-145] After the [2005 Sunjiawan mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Sunjiawan_mine_disaster), which killed at least 210 miners, a meeting of the State Council was convened to work on measures to improve work safety in coal mines. The meeting's statement indicated serious problems such as violation of safety standards and overproduction in some coal mines. Three billion yuan (360 million US dollars) were dedicated for technological renovation on work safety, gas management in particular, at state-owned major coal mines. The government also promised to send safety supervision teams to 45 coal mines with serious gas problems and invite colliery safety experts to evaluate safety situations in coal mines and formulate prevention measures.[^cite_note-146]

In 2006, according to the State Work Safety Supervision Administration, 4,749 Chinese coal miners were killed in thousands of blasts, floods, and other accidents. For example, a gas explosion at the [Nanshan Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshan_Colliery_disaster) killed 24 people on November 13, 2006; the mine was operating without any safety license and the [Xinhua News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhua_News_Agency) claimed the cause was incorrect usage of explosives. However, the 2006 rate was 20.1% less than 2005 despite an 8.1% increase in production.[^cite_note-147]

*[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that China's lack of a free press, independent trade unions, citizen watchdog groups and other checks of official power has made cover-ups of mining accidents more possible, even in the Internet age. As a result, Chinese bureaucrats habitually hide scandals (such as mine disasters, chemical spills, the 2003 SARS epidemic, and tainted milk powder) for fear of being held accountable by the ruling [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) or exposing their own illicit deals with companies involved. Under China's authoritarian system, superiors reward subordinates for strict compliance with goals established by authorities, like reducing mine disasters. Indeed, should a mining accident occur, the incentive to hide it is often stronger than the reward for managing it well, as any disaster is almost surely considered a liability.[^cite_note-148]

In November 2009, [a mining accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Heilongjiang_mine_explosion) in [Heilongjiang](173816.md) killed at least 104 people. It is thought to have been caused by a [methane explosion](505455.md) followed by a [coal dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_dust) [explosion](18993857.md). Three top officials involved with the mining company were promptly dismissed.

On August 30, 2012, an [explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaojiawan_coal_mine_disaster) killed 45 people at the Xiaojiawan coal mine in Sichuan province.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] A few days later on September 3, 2012, 14 miners were killed at Gaokeng Coal Mine in Jiangxi province.[^cite_note-151]

On 4 January 2014 The [Chinese Government](23239.md) stated that 1,049 people died in the year 2013, down 24 percent from 2012.[^cite_note-go-153]

Nearly 900 rescuers, including a team from [Ministry of Emergency Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Emergency_Management), were sent to search for people. [General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) ordered “all-out efforts in search and rescue” of all people.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

### France

Main category: [Mining disasters in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mining_disasters_in_France)



*[Le Petit Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Petit_Journal_\(newspaper\))* illustration of the Courrières mine disaster

The [Courrières mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courri%C3%A8res_mine_disaster) was the worst ever pit mine disaster in Europe. It caused the death of 1,099 miners (including many children) in Northern France on 10 March 1906. It seems that this disaster was surpassed only by the [Benxihu Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benxihu_Colliery) accident in China on April 26, 1942, which killed 1,549 miners. A dust explosion, the cause of which is not known with certainty, devastated a coal mine operated by the Compagnie des mines de houille de Courrières (founded in 1852) between the villages of Méricourt (404 killed), Sallaumines (304 killed), Billy-Montigny (114 killed), and Noyelles-sous-Lens (102 killed) about two kilometres (one mile) to the east of Lens, in the Pas-de-Calais département (about 220 km, or 140 miles, north of Paris).

A large explosion was heard shortly after 06\:30 on the morning of Saturday 10 March 1906. An elevator cage at Shaft 3 was thrown to the surface, damaging pit-head workings; windows and roofs were blown out on the surface at Shaft 4; an elevator cage raised at Shaft 2 contained only dead and unconscious miners.

### Netherlands

The twelve mines in the Netherlands, four of which were [state owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership), were considered among the safest in the world, with only three larger accidents occurring during 70 years of mining:

- On 13 July 1928 a [methane gas](18582230.md) explosion killed 13 miners in the state-owned mine [Hendrik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatsmijn_Hendrik) in [Brunssum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunssum).

- On 24 March 1947 13 miners from [Staatsmijn Hendrik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatsmijn_Hendrik) were killed in a fire caused by an overheated [conveyor belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conveyor_belt).

- On 3 March 1958 7 miners lost their lives when a [cave-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave-in) occurred at [Staatsmijn Maurits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatsmijn_Maurits) in [Geleen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geleen).

### Poland

Main article: [List of mining disasters in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mining_disasters_in_Poland)

On November 25, 2006, the worst [mining disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Halemba_Coal_Mine_disaster) occurred in modern Polish history, 23 miners lost their lives at [Halemba Coal Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halemba_Coal_Mine), a colliery in the town of [Ruda Śląska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruda_%C5%9Al%C4%85ska) in the southern industrial province of [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia). A methane explosion at a depth of 1,030 meters caused the November 21 tragedy. The miners were attempting to retrieve €17 million (\$US22 million) worth of equipment from a tunnel when a blast caused the shaft to collapse. The tunnel was supposed to have been closed in March due to dangerously high methane concentrations, but was kept active because of the value of the equipment left behind.[^cite_note-165]

### United Kingdom

#### England

In England, [The Oaks explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oaks_explosion) remains the worst mining accident, claiming 388 lives on 12–13 December 1866 near [Barnsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnsley) in [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire) although in the first and main explosion only 340 died, fewer than at the Hulton colliery, but subsequent explosions claimed other lives during the night and the following day. [The Hulton Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria_Pit_Disaster) explosion at [Westhoughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westhoughton), [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire), in 1910 claimed the lives of 344 miners.[^cite_note-bolton-182] An explosion in 1878, at the Wood Pit, [Haydock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haydock), [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire), killed over 200 workers, although only 189 were included in the 'official list'.[^cite_note-genuki-183] Another disaster that killed many miners was the [Hartley Colliery Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartley_Colliery_Disaster), which occurred in January 1862 when the beam of the pumping engine broke suddenly and fell into the single shaft serving the pit. The beam blocked the shaft and entombed hundreds of miners. The final death toll was 204, most of whom were suffocated by the lack of [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen).

In the [metalliferous](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/metalliferous) mines of Cornwall, some of the worst accidents were at [East Wheal Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Wheal_Rose) in 1846, where 39 workers were killed by a sudden flood; at [Levant mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant_mine) in 1919, where 31 were killed and many injured in a failure of the [man engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_engine); 12 killed at [Wheal Agar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheal_Agar?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1883 when a cage fell down a shaft and seven killed at [Dolcoath mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolcoath_mine) in 1893 when a large [stull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoping_\(mining_method\)) collapsed.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186]

#### Scotland

The worst mining accident in [Scotland](26994.md) is the 1877 [Blantyre mining disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blantyre_mining_disaster) in [Blantyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blantyre,_South_Lanarkshire), [Lanarkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanarkshire), which claimed 207 lives. Other fatal incidents occurred in the town in 1878 and 1879.[^cite_note-187]

Another serious incident occurred in the small [Ayrshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrshire) mining village of [Knockshinnoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knockshinnoch_Disaster) in September 1950. For several tense days rescuers battled bravely against all odds to reach the 129 men trapped deep underground when a field above where they were working caved-in, flooding the mine workings with thick liquid peat, cutting off all means of escape. 116 were rescued but 13 died.[^cite_note-infomine-188] A film, *[The Brave Don't Cry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brave_Don't_Cry)*, was made about the disaster in 1952.[^cite_note-189]

The worst Scottish mining disaster in the 20th century took place at Auchengeich by [Moodiesburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moodiesburn) in September 1959, with 47 men killed.[^cite_note-190] The total surpassed the 40 who had [died in flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redding_mining_disaster) at [Redding, Falkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redding,_Falkirk) in September 1923.[^cite_note-191]

#### Wales



Crowd gathering at the [pit head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headframe) of the Senghenydd Colliery after the explosion in October 1913

During the period 1850 to 1930 the [South Wales coalfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Wales_coalfield) had the worst disaster record. This was due to the increasing number of mines being sunk to greater depths into gas-containing strata, combined with poor safety and management practices. As a result, there were nearly forty underground explosions in the [Glamorgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamorgan) and [Monmouthshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monmouthshire_\(historic\)) areas of the coalfield during this time. Each accident resulted in the deaths of twenty or more workers – either directly in the explosion or by suffocation by the poisonous gases formed. The total death toll from these disasters was 3,119 people. The four worst accidents in Wales were:

- 439 deaths at the [Senghenydd Colliery Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senghenydd_Colliery_Disaster) at [Universal Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Colliery) in [Senghenydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senghenydd), Glamorgan, in a gas explosion in 1913.

- 290 deaths at the [Albion Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion_Colliery) in [Cilfynydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilfynydd), Glamorgan, in a gas explosion on 25 June 1894.

- 272 deaths at the [Prince of Wales Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abercarn_colliery_disaster), [Abercarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abercarn), Monmouthshire, in an explosion of 11 September 1878.

  [^cite_note-192]

- 266 deaths in the [Gresford Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresford_Disaster) near [Wrexham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrexham) in [North Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wales) on 22 September 1934.

Some collieries, e.g. [Morfa Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morfa_Colliery?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-welshcoalmines-193] near [Port Talbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Talbot), [Glamorgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamorgan), and [Black Vein Colliery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Vein_Colliery?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Risca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risca), [Monmouthshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monmouthshire_\(historic\)), suffered three disasters before they were closed for being unsafe.

### United States

Main category: [Mining disasters in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Mining_disasters_in_the_United_States)

The [Scofield Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scofield_Mine_disaster) occurred on May 1, 1900, near [Scofield, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scofield,_Utah). At least 200 men died making it the worst mining disaster in the United States at that point.[^cite_note-194]

The [Fraterville mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraterville_mine_disaster) occurred on May 19, 1902, killing 216 miners making it one of the worst in American history. Fraterville is located in western Anderson County, Tennessee. Also in the same year on July 10, 1902, the [Rolling Mill Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Mill_Mine_Disaster) happened in Johnstown, Pa. It killed 112, many of whom had just arrived in town. At the time it was one of the region's most productive mines.

The [Monongah Mining Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongah_Mining_Disaster) was the worst mining accident of American history; 362 workers were killed in an underground explosion on December 6, 1907, in [Monongah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongah), [West Virginia](32905.md).

The Marianna Mine Disaster occurred on November 28, 1908, in a coal mine near [Marianna, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianna,_Pennsylvania) resulting in the death of 154 men from the explosion. The explosion occurred during shift change, as men entered the mine before the previous shift had left. Consequently, the mine contained many more miners than usual. Another accident occurred in the same mine on September 23, 1957, when an explosion killed 6 of 11 men in the mine.[^cite_note-marianna-90]

The [Cross Mountain Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Mountain_Mine_disaster) occurred on December 9, 1911, near the community of [Briceville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briceville,_Tennessee), killing 84 miners.

The First Dawson Disaster was a mining accident on October 22, 1913, in [Dawson, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson,_New_Mexico) in which 263 men died (146 were Italian and 36 were Greek).

The Second Dawson Disasters was a mining accident on February 8, 1923, in Dawson, New Mexico in which 123 men died.

The [Speculator Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculator_Mine_disaster) occurred in the copper mines of [Butte, Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butte,_Montana) on June 8, 1917. An electric cable being lowered into the mine was accidentally ignited at 2,500 feet below the surface. The fire quickly climbed the cable, in turn igniting the shaft. The shaft thus became a chimney, eliminating the mine's primary source of oxygen. Nearly all of the 168 fatalities were due to asphyxia. It remains the deadliest underground hard rock mining event in American history.

The [Hastings mine explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_mine_explosion) was a fire at the Victor-American Fuel Company coal mine in Hastings, Las Animas Country, Colorado, On April 27, 1917, in which 121 people died.

The Cherry Mine disaster was a fire in the [Cherry, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry,_Illinois), [coal mine](208413.md) in 1909, and surrounding events, in which 259 men and boys died.

The [Millfield Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millfield_Mine_Disaster) 1930 in Ohio killed 82 men.

From 1880 to 1910, mine accidents claimed thousands of fatalities. Where annual mining deaths had numbered more than 1,000 a year during the early part of the 20th century, they decreased to an average of about 500 during the late 1950s, and to 93 during the 1990s.[^cite_note-195] In addition to deaths, many thousands more are injured (an average of 21,351 injuries per year between 1991 and 1999), but overall there has been a downward trend of deaths and injuries.

In 1959, the [Knox Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knox_Mine_Disaster) occurred in [Port Griffith, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Griffith,_Pennsylvania). The swelling [Susquehanna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susquehanna_River) collapsed into a mine under it and resulted in 12 deaths. In [Plymouth, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth,_Pennsylvania), the [Avondale Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avondale_Mine_Disaster) of 1869 resulted in the deaths of 108 miners and two rescue workers after a fire in the only shaft eliminated the oxygen in the mine. Federal laws for mining safety resulted from this disaster. Pennsylvania suffered another disaster in 2002 at [Quecreek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quecreek_Mine_Rescue), 9 miners were trapped underground and subsequently rescued after 78 hours. During 2006, 72 miners died at work, 47 by coal mining. The majority of these fatalities occurred in [Kentucky](16846.md) and [West Virginia](32905.md), including the [Sago Mine Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sago_Mine_Disaster).[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197] On April 5, 2010, in the [Upper Big Branch Mine disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Big_Branch_Mine_disaster) an underground explosion caused the deaths of 29 miners. The [Mine Safety and Health Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_Safety_and_Health_Administration) recorded 8 coal mining deaths in the United States in 2025.[^cite_note-198]

The [U.S. Bureau of Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bureau_of_Mines) was created in 1910 to investigate accidents, advise industry, conduct production and safety research, and teach courses in accident prevention, first aid, and [mine rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_rescue). The [Federal Coal Mine Health and Safety Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Coal_Mine_Health_and_Safety_Act) of 1969 and 1977 set further safety standards for the mining. Since the closure of the U.S. Bureau of Mines in 1996, this research function has been carried on by the [National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Occupational_Safety_and_Health) (NIOSH). NIOSH maintains a list of mine disasters which occurred in the United States since 1839.[^cite_note-199]
