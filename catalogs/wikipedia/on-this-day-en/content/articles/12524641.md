***Israel Hayom*** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): יִשְׂרָאֵל הַיּוֹם, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Israel Today') is an Israeli [Hebrew-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew-language) [free daily newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_newspaper). Distributed for free around Israel,[^cite_note-IndyUK2011iHaYom-6] it is the country's most widely distributed newspaper.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-iHaYomLargestLAT2016-2] Owned by the family of [Sheldon Adelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Adelson), the casino mogul and [political donor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_donor), *Israel Hayom* has been noted for its [right-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Israel) editorial stance and coverage of [Israeli politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Israel), often sparking debate about its political alignment.

## History

*Israel Hayom*'s print edition, "financed by the American casino billionaire Sheldon Adelson,"[^cite_note-iHayomNetObsHISimageNYT2018-8] was launched on 30 July 2007, competing directly with *[Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_\(newspaper\))*, another free daily. That same year, *[Maariv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maariv_\(newspaper\))* editor [Dan Margalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Margalit_\(journalist\)) left the newspaper to write for *Israel Hayom*. A weekend edition was launched in October 2009. In 2014, *Israel Hayom* bought Israeli media outlets *[Makor Rishon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makor_Rishon)* and *[nrg מעריב](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nrg_\(website\))*.[^cite_note-makornrg-5]



The *Israel Hayom* headquarters in [Tel Aviv](31453.md)

In May 2014 the name מעריב (Maariv) was removed from nrg log,[^cite_note-9] and it was rebranded as nrg. Following the acquisition, an antitrust complaint was filed against *Israel Hayom*, which resulted in a court order that requested the transfer of the maariv [domain name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_name) to [*Maariv* weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maariv_\(newspaper\)).[^cite_note-10] In 2017, nrg was renamed to nrg360, and on 10 January 2018 the website closed; all of its content was merged into the [Makor Rishon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makor_Rishon) website.

Later that year, it is believed, was when [Sheldon Adelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Adelson) invested at least \$50 million in *Israel Hayom*.[^cite_note-11] Critics of Adelson's involvement claimed that the distribution of free newspapers hurt free enterprise and targeted Israel's print newspaper industry, as *Israel Hayom* was then Israel's only free-of-charge national newspaper. The bill passed the first reading in the Knesset, but not subsequent ones.[^cite_note-12] In 2016, Adelson's attorney announced that although it was commonly believed that Adelson owned the newspaper, it was owned by a relative of his.[^cite_note-13]

In January 2021, Saudi-American independent academic researcher, Najat Al-Saied, became the first person from the [Arab states of the Persian Gulf](3088555.md) to join *Israel Hayom*.[^cite_note-14]

In June 2024, Israel Hayom announced a significant partnership with the tabloid [Bild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bild), Germany's biggest [Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axel_Springer_SE) news outfit. It involves joint articles and investigations that are published simultaneously in both countries.[^cite_note-15]

This endorsement reinforced the perception of the paper as a supporter of right-wing ideologies not only in Israel but also internationally.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Market share



*Israel Hayom* readers in [Jerusalem](16043.md)

In July 2010, *Israel Hayom* surpassed *[Yedioth Ahronoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yedioth_Ahronoth)* in weekday exposure rate in the semi-annual [Target Group Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_Group_Index) (TGI) survey.[^cite_note-18]

As of 1 January 2024, a TGI survey indicated that *Israel Hayom*, distributed for free, is Israel's most read newspaper, with a 27.4% weekday readership exposure, followed by *[Yedioth Ahronoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yedioth_Ahronoth)*, with 22.4%, *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* with 5.4%, *[Maariv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maariv_\(newspaper\))* with 3.5%*, and [Globes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globes_\(newspaper\))* with 3.2%.[^cite_note-19]

As of 31 July 2023, a TGI survey had indicated that *Israel Hayom*, was Israel's most read newspaper, with a 29.4% weekday readership exposure, followed by *[Yedioth Ahronoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yedioth_Ahronoth)*, with 22.3%, *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* with 4.8%, *[Globes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globes_\(newspaper\))* with 4% and *[Maariv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maariv_\(newspaper\))* with 3.9%.[^cite_note-20]

In January 2016, citing internal records from *Israel Hayom*, *Haaretz* revealed that between 2007 and 2014, the newspaper lost about 730 million shekels (\$190 million), approximately equal to a shekel per copy printed.[^cite_note-21]
