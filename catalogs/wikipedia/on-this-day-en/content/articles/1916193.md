[^cite_note-sdousa-4]

**Terrorism in India**, according to the [Ministry of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Home_Affairs_\(India\)), poses a significant threat to the people of India. Compared to other countries, India faces a wide range of terror groups. Terrorism found in India includes [Islamist terrorism](566821.md), [ultranationalist terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_terrorism), and [left-wing terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_terrorism).[^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-bh-6][^cite_note-satp11lw-7] India is one of the countries most impacted by terrorism.[^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-8]

A common [definition of terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definition_of_terrorism) is the systematic use or threatened use of violence to intimidate a population or government for political, religious, or [ideological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology) goals.[^cite_note-britannica-9][^cite_note-10]

In 2022, India ranked 13th on the [Global Terrorism Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Terrorism_Index).[^cite_note-11] India continues to face a number of terror attacks from Islamist separatist groups in [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir), [Sikh separatists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalistan_movement) in [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India), and secessionist groups in [Assam](186162.md).[^cite_note-\:0-5] The regions with long term [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) activities have been [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md), east-central and south-central India ([Naxalism](4760079.md)) and the [Seven Sister States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sister_States). In August 2008, National Security Advisor M K Narayanan said that as many as 800 [terrorist cells](14558092.md) are operating in the country.[^cite_note-12] As of 2013, 205 of the country's 608 districts were affected by terrorist activity.[^cite_note-13] Terror attacks caused 231 civilian deaths in 2012 in India, compared to 11,098 terror-caused deaths worldwide, according to the State Department of the United States; or about 2% of global terror fatalities while it accounts for 17.5% of the global population.[^cite_note-sdousa-4]

Reports have alleged and implicated terrorism in India to be sponsored by Pakistan.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Speigel-15][^cite_note-16] In July 2016, the [Government of India](553883.md) released data on a string of terror strikes in India since 2005 that claimed 707 lives and left over 3,200 injured.[^cite_note-17]

## Definition

The 8th report on [terrorism](30636.md) in India published in 2008 defined terrorism as the peacetime equivalent of war crime.[^cite_note-arc08-18] An act of terror in India includes any intentional act of violence that causes death, injury or property damage, induces fear, and is targeted against any group of people identified by their political, philosophical, ideological, racial, ethnic, religious or any other nature. This description is similar to one provided by the [United Nations](31769.md)' in 2000.[^cite_note-19]

The [Indian government](553883.md) uses the following working definition of terrorism, same as one widely used by Western nations as well as the United Nations, proposed by Schmid and Jongman in 1988.[^cite_note-arc08-18]

> Terrorism is an anxiety-inspiring method of repeated violent action, employed by (semi-) clandestine individual, group or state actors, for idiosyncratic, criminal or political reasons, whereby the direct targets of violence are not the main targets. The immediate human **victims of violence are generally chosen randomly** (targets of opportunity) **or selectively** (representative or symbolic targets) from a target population, and serve as message generators. Threat and violence-based communication processes between terrorist organisation, victims, and main targets are used to manipulate the main target (audience(s)), turning it into a target of terror, a target of demands, or a target of attention, depending on whether intimidation, coercion, or propaganda is primarily sought.

— Alex Schmid and Albert Jongman[^cite_note-20]

India subdivides terrorism into four major groups:[^cite_note-arc08-18][^cite_note-satpaao-21]

1. Ethno-[nationalist terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_terrorism) – This form of terror focuses either (a) on creating a separate State within India or independent of India or in a neighboring country, or (b) on emphasising the views/response of one ethnic group against another. Violent Tamil Nationalist groups from India to address the condition of Tamils in Sri Lanka, as well as insurgent tribal groups in North East India are examples of ethno-nationalist terrorist activities.

   [^cite_note-bh-6]

2. [Religious terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_terrorism) – This form of terror focuses on religious imperatives, a presumed duty or in solidarity for a specific religious group, against one or more religious groups. The Mumbai 26/11 terror attack in 2008 from an Islamist group in Pakistan is an example of religious terrorism in India. The examples are [Hindu terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_terrorism), [Islamic terrorism](566821.md), [Right-wing terrorism](5210960.md), etc.

   [^cite_note-22]

3. [Left-wing terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_terrorism) – This form of terror focuses on economic ideology, where all the existing socio-political structures are seen to be economically exploitative in character and a revolutionary change through violent means is essential.

   [^cite_note-bh-6]

   [^cite_note-23]

   &#x20;The ideology of Marx, Engel, Mao, Lenin and others is considered as the only valid economic path. Maoist violence in Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh are examples of left wing terrorism in India.

   [^cite_note-satp11lw-7]

4. [Narcoterrorism](221350.md) – This form of terror focuses on creating illegal narcotics traffic zones.

   [^cite_note-24]

   &#x20;Drug violence in northwest India is an example of narco-terrorism in India.

   [^cite_note-sh13-25]

SATP (South Asia Terrorism Portal) has listed 180 terrorist groups that have operated within India over the last 20 years, many of them co-listed as transnational terror networks operating in or from neighboring South Asian countries such as [Bangladesh](3454.md), [Nepal](171166.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-26] Of these, 38 are on the current list of terrorist organisations banned by India under its First Schedule of the UA(P) Act, 1967.[^cite_note-27] As of 2012, many of these were also listed and banned by the United States and European Union.[^cite_note-28]

### Andhra Pradesh

#### 2000 Church bombings of South India

Main article: [2000 Church bombings of South India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Church_bombings_of_South_India)

The 2000 Church bombings refers to the serial bombings of churches in the southern [Indian](14533.md) states of [Karnataka](16880.md), [Goa](40010153.md) and [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) by the [Islamist extremist](8734632.md) group [Deendar Anjuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deendar_Anjuman) in the year 2000. On 21 May 2000, a Christian congregation at [Machilipatnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machilipatnam), Andhra Pradesh was bombed. On 8 July 2000, two churches were bombed in [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md), Gewett Memorial Baptist Church in [Ongole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ongole) and the Mother Vannini Catholic Church in [Tadepalligudem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadepalligudem) town. The blast in the Ongole church injured three persons.

#### Kurnool train crash

Main article: [Kurnool train crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurnool_train_crash)

A passenger train derailed in [Kurnool](1039379.md) district in [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md), on 21 December 2002.[^cite_note-29] During the repair, it was discovered that one of the railway tracks had been deliberately severed, which caused the derailment. Later reports agreed that the crash was caused by sabotage. Thirteen months after the attack, police in Hyderabad arrested a man named Syed Abdul Nayeem, a [Lashkar-e-Taiba](649718.md) activist, who failed a 'brainwave fingerprinting test' after being questioned by Indian police. He was charged both in this case and a bombing which killed two people in the Sai Baba Temple.

### Assam

After [Nagaland](408677.md), Assam is the most volatile state in the region. Beginning in 1979, the indigenous people of Assam demanded that the illegal immigrants who had emigrated from [Bangladesh](3454.md) to [Assam](186162.md) be detected and deported. The movement led by the [All Assam Students Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Assam_Students_Union) began non-violently with [satyagraha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satyagraha), boycotts, picketing, and courting arrests.

Those protesting frequently came under police action. In 1983 an election was conducted, which was opposed by the movement leaders. The election led to widespread violence. The movement finally ended after the movement leaders signed an agreement (called the [Assam Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_Accord)) with the central government on 15 August 1985.

Under the provisions of this accord, anyone who entered the state illegally between January 1966 and March 1971 was allowed to remain but was disenfranchised for ten years, while those who entered after 1971 faced expulsion. A November 1985 amendment to the Indian citizenship law allows non-citizens who entered Assam between 1961 and 1971 to have all the rights of citizenship except the [right to vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_vote) for ten years.

New Delhi also gave special administration autonomy to the [Bodos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodo_people) in the state. However, the Bodos demanded a separate Bodoland, which led to a clash between the Bengalis, the Bodos, and the [Indian military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_India) resulting in hundreds of deaths.

Several organisations advocate the independence of [Assam](186162.md). The most prominent of these is the [United Liberation Front of Asom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Liberation_Front_of_Asom) (ULFA). Formed in 1979, the ULFA has two main goals: the independence of [Assam](186162.md) and the establishment of a socialist government.

The ULFA has carried out several terrorist attacks in the region targeting the Indian Military and non-combatants. The group assassinates political opponents, attacks police and other security forces, blasts railroad tracks, and attacks other infrastructure facilities. The ULFA is believed to have strong links with the *Nationalist Socialist Council of Nagaland (NSCN)*, [Maoists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoists), and the [Naxalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxalites).

It is also believed that they carry out most of their operations from the Kingdom of [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan). Because of ULFA's increased visibility, the Indian government outlawed the group in 1986 and declared [Assam](186162.md) a troubled area. Under pressure from New Delhi, Bhutan carried out a massive operation to drive out the ULFA militants from its territory.

Backed by the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), [Thimphu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thimphu) was successful in killing more than a thousand terrorists and [extraditing](239648.md) many more to India while sustaining only 120 casualties. The Indian military undertook several successful operations aimed at countering future ULFA terrorist attacks, but the ULFA continues to be active in the region. In 2004, the ULFA targeted a public school in [Assam](186162.md), killing 19 children and 5 adults.

[Assam](186162.md) remains the only state in the northeast where terrorism is still a major issue. On 18 September 2005, a soldier was killed in Jiribam, Manipur, near the Manipur-Assam border, by members of the ULFA. On 14 March 2011, Bodo militants of the Ranjan Daimary-led faction ambushed patrolling troops of [BSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Security_Force) when on their way from Bangladoba in the Chirang district of Assam to Ultapani in Kokrajhar killing 8 jawans.[^cite_note-30]

On 5 August 2016, a terrorist attack was reported in the market area of Balajan Tinali of the city of [Kokrajhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokrajhar) that resulted in the death of 14 civilians and injuries to 15 others. Three terrorists, suspected to be Bodo militants, were reported to have attacked using [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47) and a grenade.[^cite_note-31] OP Singh. Director General of Police said in a press conference the terrorist from the Hizbul Mujahideen group arrested in the city of Kanpur was Qamar-uz-Zama. He is 37 years old and is a resident of Assam.[^cite_note-firstpost.com-32]

#### Brahmaputra Express Train bombing

Main article: [Brahmaputra Mail train bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_Mail_train_bombing)

The Brahmaputra Express Train bombing was a terrorist attack on a train travelling in [Western Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Assam) in Eastern [India](14533.md) on 30 December 1996. Most of the passengers were believed to be vacationers heading for Delhi to celebrate the New Year.[^cite_note-\:2-33] The train was carrying an estimated 1,200 passengers.[^cite_note-\:2-33] Police in Kokrajhar said a bomb planted on the rails went off shortly after the New Delhi-bound Brahmaputra Express left Kokrajhar in the jungle terrain of Assam state.[^cite_note-\:2-33] "The blast took place at 7\:15 p.m., a few minutes after the train left the Kokrajhar station for New Delhi." police said.[^cite_note-\:2-33] The train's engine and its first coach car were derailed by the blast, and the next three rail cars were severely damaged, an official said.[^cite_note-\:2-33] The bomb totally wrecked three carriages of the train and derailed six more, killing at least 33 people.[^cite_note-\:2-33] No one has claimed responsibility, though the separatist Bodo rebel faction was believed to be involved.[^cite_note-\:2-33]

#### 2004 Dhemaji school bombing

Main article: [2004 Dhemaji school bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Dhemaji_school_bombing)

On the occasion of Independence Day, 15 August 2004, people, mostly were school children and their mothers, gathered at [Dhemaji College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhemaji_College?action=edit\&redlink=1) ground for an Independence Day parade. At around 09\:30 am, a bomb went off killing 18 schoolchildren and injuring 40. According to police, the bomb was planted near the college gate and triggered by a remote-controlled device. It exploded when the students and teachers of various schools were passing through the gate. Police blamed [ULFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ULFA), a banned Assamese militant group, which had initially called for a boycott of the event. But on 13 December 2009, [Paresh Barua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paresh_Barua), the C-in-C of the group, sought public apology and forgiveness for the blast. He stated in an e-mail that the ULFA leadership was misled by some of their cadres and junior leaders about the blast, which is why the leadership had to deny its involvement.

### Manipur

Like its sister states in Northeast, Manipur has experienced years of insurgency and inter-ethnic violence while it was part of Assam and sought more rights.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] The state joined India on 21 September 1949, when [Maharaja Budhachandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_Budhachandra) signed a Treaty of Accession merging the kingdom into India; this merger was disputed by various groups in Manipur as having been completed without consensus and under duress. Manipur was part of Assam after 1949, became a Union Territory in 1956.[^cite_note-73] The first armed opposition group in Manipur, the United National Liberation Front (UNLF), was founded in 1964, which declared that it wanted to gain more rights or outright independence from India. After several rounds of negotiations, Manipur became a full state in 1972 along with several other sister states of the Northeast.[^cite_note-74] Post statehood, more groups continued to form in Manipur, each with different goals, and deriving support from diverse ethnic groups in Manipur. For example, in 1977 the People's Revolutionary Party of Kangleipak (PREPAK) was formed, the [People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army_of_Manipur) (PLA) was formed in 1978. In 1980, the Kangleipak Communist Party (KCP) was formed. These groups began a spree of bank robberies and attacks on police officers and government buildings. The state government appealed to the central government in New Delhi for support in combating this violence.[^cite_note-hrwm-75] In 1980, the central government brought the entire state of Manipur under the Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act (AFSPA) because its state government claimed that the use of the Armed Forces in aid of the state and local police is necessary to prevent violent deaths and to maintain law and order.

The violence in Manipur includes significant inter-ethnic tribal rivalry. There is violence between the Meiteis, Nagas, Kukis and other tribal groups.[^cite_note-hrwm-75] They have formed splinter groups who disagree with each other. Other than UNLF, PLA and PREPAK mentioned above, other Manipuri insurgent groups include Revolutionary Peoples Front (RPF), Manipur Liberation Front Army (MLFA), Kanglei Yawol Khnna Lup (KYKL), Revolutionary Joint Committee (RJC), Peoples United Liberation Front (PULF), Kuki National Front (KNF), Kuki National Army (KNA), Kuki Defence Force (KDF), Kuki Democratic Movement (KDM), Kuki National Organisation (KNO), Kuki Security Force (KSF), Chin Kuki Revolutionary Front (CKRF), Kom Rem Peoples Convention (KRPC), Zomi Revolutionary Volunteers (ZRV), Zomi Revolutionary Army (ZRA), Zomi Reunification Organisation (ZRO), and Hmar Peoples Convention (HPC).[^cite_note-hrwm-75]

According to SATP (the South Asia Terrorism Portal),[^cite_note-76] there has been a large decline in fatalities in Manipur in recent decades.[^cite_note-satp9413-77] Since 2010, about 25 civilians have died in militant-related violence (about 1 per 100,000 people), dropping further to 21 civilian deaths in 2013 (or 0.8 per 100,000 people).[^cite_note-satp9413-77] Most of these deaths have been from inter-factional violence. Elections have been held regularly over recent decades. The last state assembly elections were held in 2012, with 79.2% voter turnout and the incumbent re-elected to power.[^cite_note-78]

### Mizoram

In 1947, Mizoram was part of Assam, and its districts were controlled by hereditary tribal chiefs. The educated elites among the Mizos campaigned against the tribal [chiefdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiefdom) under the banner of [Mizo Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizo_Union). As a result of their campaign, the hereditary rights of the 259 chiefs were abolished under the Assam-Lushai District (Acquisition of Chief's Rights) Act, 1954.[^cite_note-Suhas1994-79][^cite_note-misai-80] Village courts, which were disbanded by the colonial authorities during their re-structuring of Assam, were re-implemented in Mizo region. All of these regions were frustrated by these arrangements and centralized Assam governance. The Mizos were particularly dissatisfied with the government's inadequate response to the 1959–60 [mautam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mautam) famine. The Mizo National Famine Front, a body formed for famine relief in 1959, later developed into a new political organisation, the [Mizo National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizo_National_Front) (MNF) in 1961.[^cite_note-Braja1998-81] A period of protests and armed insurgency followed in the 1960s, with MNF seeking independence from India.[^cite_note-dommen-82]

In 1971, the government agreed to convert the Mizo Hills into a Union Territory, which came into being as Mizoram in 1972. Following the [Mizoram Peace Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram_Peace_Accord) (1986) between the Government and the MNF, Mizoram was declared a full-fledged state of India in 1987.[^cite_note-AlfredJuan2011-83] Mizoram got two seats in the [Parliament](410238.md), one each in the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) and in the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md).[^cite_note-84] Per the accord, insurgents surrendered their arms. The first election of Mizoram Legislative Assembly was held on 16 February 1987.[^cite_note-misai-80] Elections have been held at 5 year intervals since then. The most recent [Mizoram elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Mizoram_Legislative_Assembly_election) were held for 40 seats of the legislative assembly on 25 November 2013. The voter turnout was 81%. The Indian National Congress led by [Lal Thanhawla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Thanhawla) was re-elected to power.[^cite_note-85] The region has been peaceful in recent decades. Between 2006 and 2013, between 0 and 2 civilians died each year from any protest-related violence (or less than 0.2 people per 100,000).[^cite_note-86]

### Nagaland

After the [independence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) in 1947, the area remained a part of the province of Assam. Nationalist activities arose amongst a section of the Nagas. [Phizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angami_Zapu_Phizo)-led [Naga National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_National_Council) and demanded a political union of their ancestral and native groups. The movement led to a series of violent incidents, that damaged government and civil infrastructure, attacked government officials and civilians. The Union government sent the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) in 1955, to restore order. In 1957, an agreement was reached between Naga leaders and the Indian government, creating a single separate region of the Naga Hills. The Tuensang frontier was united with this single political region, Naga Hills Tuensang Area (NHTA),[^cite_note-Nagaland_Legislative_Assembly-87] and it became a [Union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory) directly administered by the Central government with a large degree of autonomy. This was not satisfactory to the tribes, however, and agitation with violence increased across the state – including attacks on army and government institutions, and banks, as well as non-payment of taxes. In July 1960, following a discussion between the then [Prime Minister](24452.md) Nehru and the leaders of the Naga People Convention (NPC), a 16-point agreement was arrived at whereby the Government of India recognised the formation of Nagaland as a full-fledged state within the Union of India.[^cite_note-88]

Nagaland became the 16th state of the Indian Union on 1 December 1963.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] After elections in January 1964, the first democratically elected [Nagaland Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaland_Legislative_Assembly) was constituted on 11 February 1964.[^cite_note-Nagaland_Legislative_Assembly-87][^cite_note-91] The rebel activity continued, in the form of banditry and attacks, motivated more by inter-factional tribal rivalry and personal vendetta than by political aspiration. In November 1975, the leaders of largest rebellion groups agreed to lay down their arms and accept the Indian constitution, a small group did not agree and continued their insurgent activity.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

#### 1996 Dimapur car bombing

Main article: [1996 Dimapur car bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Dimapur_car_bombing)

On Christmas Day, 25 December 1996, the [NSCN-IM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Socialist_Council_of_Nagaland) detonated a powerful [car bomb](155282.md) triggered by remote control in the Ara Mile neighbourhood of [Dimapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimapur), Nagaland in an attempted assassination on [Kihoto Hollohon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kihoto_Hollohon), the then Minister of Industries & Commerce of Nagaland. Hollohon escaped as he was not in the vehicle. The blast killed his wife, daughter, granddaughter, grandson and one other on the spot.

#### 2004 Dimapur bombings

Main article: [2004 Dimapur bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Dimapur_bombings)

On 2 October 2004, two powerful bombs were set off—one at the [Dimapur Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimapur_railway_station) and the other at the Hong Kong Market killing 30 and injuring over 100 others.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

Over the 5 year of 2009 to 2013, between 0 and 11 civilians died per year in Nagaland from rebellion related activity (or less than 1 death per year per 100,000 people), and between 3 and 55 militant deaths per year in inter-factional killings (or between 0 and 3 deaths per 100,000 people).[^cite_note-96] The most recent Nagaland Legislative Assembly election took place on 23 February 2013 to elect the Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLA) from each of the 60 Assembly Constituencies in the state. The voter turnout was 83% and Nagaland People's Front was elected to power with 37 seats.[^cite_note-97]

### Odisha

### Punjab

In the 1980s, an insurgent movement turned to violence, seeking a separate state called [Khalistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalistan), independent of India. They were led by Jarnail Singh [Bhindranwale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhindranwale) who was neutral on the establishment of a new state.[^cite_note-98]

In 1984, [Operation Blue Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Blue_Star) was conducted by the Indian government to confront the movement. It involved an assault on the Golden Temple complex, which Sant Bhindranwale had fortified in preparation of an army assault. [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi), [India](14533.md)'s then prime minister, ordered the military to storm the temple, who eventually had to use tanks. After a 74-hour firefight, the army successfully took control of the temple. In doing so, it damaged some portions of the [Akal Takht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akal_Takht), the [Sikh Reference Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Reference_Library), and the [Golden Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmandir_Sahib) itself. According to Indian government sources, 83 army personnel were killed and 249 were injured. Militant casualties were 493 killed and 86 injured.

During the same year, the assassination of [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi) by two Sikh [bodyguards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodyguard), believed to be driven by the Golden Temple affair, resulted in widespread anti-Sikh riots, especially in New Delhi. Following [Operation Black Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Black_Thunder) in 1988, [Punjab Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Police_\(India\)), first under Julio Ribeiro and then under [KPS Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanwar_Pal_Singh_Gill), together with the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), eventually succeeded in pushing the movement underground.

In 1985, Sikh terrorists bombed an [Air India](26457880.md) flight from Canada to India, killing all 329 people on board [Air India Flight 182](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_182). It was the worst [mass murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_murder) in Canadian history.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

The ending of Sikh militancy and the desire for a Khalistan catalysed when the then-Prime Minister of [Pakistan](23235.md), [Benazir Bhutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Bhutto), handed all intelligence material concerning Punjab militancy to the Indian government, as a goodwill gesture.[^cite_note-101] The Indian government used that intelligence to arrest those who were behind attacks in India and militancy.

The ending of overt Sikh militancy in 1993 led to a period of relative calm, punctuated by militant acts (for example, the assassination of Punjab CM, Beant Singh, in 1995) attributed to half a dozen or so operating Sikh militant organisations. These organisations include [Babbar Khalsa International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babbar_Khalsa_International), [Khalistan Commando Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalistan_Commando_Force), [Khalistan Liberation Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalistan_Liberation_Force), and [Khalistan Zindabad Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalistan_Zindabad_Force).[^cite_note-102]

#### 1991 Punjab killings

Main article: [1991 Punjab killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Punjab_killings)

The **1991 Punjab killings** was a massacre of train passengers that occurred on 15 June 1991 in the [Ludhiana district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludhiana_district) of the Indian State of [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_\(India\)), where [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) Khalistani militants killed at least 80 to 126 passengers travelling in two trains. The militants stopped the two trains about a kilometre from Ludhiana station by pulling their [emergency cords, triggering emergency brakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_brake_\(train\)#India). They proceeded to open fire inside the trains at around 9\:35 p.m. ([IST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Standard_Time)), killing at least 80 passengers. On the second train, the Dhuri-Hisar passenger train, the militants fired indiscriminately. After the attackers fled, the train moved back to Badduwal station, where the rescue team arrived with doctors. Local villagers helped the survivors with food, water, medicine, and mental support. The attacks came less than five hours after polling closed in a national election already marred by violence and interrupted by the assassination of ex-Prime Minister [Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi) around a month prior.

### Tamil Nadu

Militants belonging to the [Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Tigers_of_Tamil_Eelam) (LTTE) were operating in the Tamil Nadu state up until the assassination of former Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi. The LTTE had given many speeches in Tamil Nadu led by [Velupillai Prabhakaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velupillai_Prabhakaran), [S. P. Thamilselvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._P._Thamilselvan), and other Eelam members. The LTTE, now a banned organisation, had been receiving many donations and support from India in the past. #### Meenambakkam bomb blast

The [Tamil Eelam Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Eelam_Army) was suspected. Several members were convicted in 1998.[^cite_note-104]

#### Assassination of Rajiv Gandhi

Main article: [Assassination of Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Rajiv_Gandhi)

The [assassination of Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Rajiv_Gandhi), former [Prime Minister of India](24452.md), occurred as a result of a [suicide bombing](18835454.md) in [Sriperumbudur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sriperumbudur), [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), in [Tamil Nadu](29918.md), [India](14533.md) on 21 May 1991.[^cite_note-105] At least 14 others, in addition to [Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi), were killed.[^cite_note-106] It was carried out by Thenmozhi Rajaratnam (also known as Kalaivani Rajaratnam or Dhanu),[^cite_note-107] a member of the [Sri Lankan Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamils) separatist organization [Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Tigers_of_Tamil_Eelam) (LTTE).

#### 1993 bombing of RSS office in Chennai

Main article: [1993 bombing of RSS office in Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_bombing_of_RSS_office_in_Chennai)

**1993 bombing of the RSS office in Chennai** refers to the bombing of the head office of the [Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtriya_Swayamsevak_Sangh) in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai) in [Tamil Nadu](29918.md) on 8 August 1993 by [terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorists). The bombings left eleven people dead and seven others injured.

The special [CBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Investigation) court tried eighteen of the accused under the now-defunct [Terrorist and Disruptive Activities (Prevention) Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_and_Disruptive_Activities_\(Prevention\)_Act). They had been earlier given life imprisonment by a TADA court in Chennai for their involvement in the blast on 8 August 1993 at the RSS office in Chennai. The CBI has announced a reward of Rs. 10 lakh for providing credible information about Mushtaq Ahmed, one of the main accused in the blast.

Main article: [August 2007 Hyderabad bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2007_Hyderabad_bombings)

The [25 August 2007 Hyderabad bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2007_Hyderabad_bombings) consisted of two synchronised blasts. The blast was caused by a [cellphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellphone)-triggered [pipe bomb](233935.md).[^cite_note-deccan-113] Fourteen people were reported dead in the immediate aftermath, of whom five (official record\:disputed) were killed by the police firing after the incident while trying to quell the mob.[^cite_note-deccan-113]

The [2013 Hyderabad blasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Hyderabad_blasts) occurred around 19\:00 IST. The two blasts occurred in the Indian city of Hyderabad's Dilsukhnagar. The simultaneous blasts occurred near a bus stop and a cinema.

### Tripura

#### Mandai massacre

[Mandai massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandai_massacre) refers to the general massacre of the [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis) of Mandai village near [Agartala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agartala) in the Indian state of Tripura on 8 June 1980, by tribal insurgents. According to official figures 255 Bengalis were massacred in Mandwi, while foreign presses, independent sources and eyewitnesses put the figure anywhere between 350 and 400. Many of the victims had their heads crushed and their limbs severed. The children were spiked through.[^cite_note-114] Pregnant women had their stomachs slit open. *[Amrita Bazar Patrika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amrita_Bazar_Patrika)* described the Mandwi massacre as [My Lai massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Lai_Massacre) put into shade.[^cite_note-115] According to Major R. Rajamani, the commander of the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) contingent that arrived on 9 June, the My Lai massacre was not even half as gruesome as in Mandai.

#### 2006 Varanasi blasts

A series of blasts occurred across the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) holy city of [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi) on 7 March 2006. Fifteen people are reported to have been killed and as many as 101 others were injured. On 5 April 2006 the Indian police arrested six Islamic militants, including a cleric who helped plan bomb blasts. The cleric is believed to be a commander of a banned [Bangladeshi](3454.md) Islamic militant group, Harkatul Jihad-al Islami, and is linked to the [Inter-Services Intelligence](2159021.md), the [Pakistani](23235.md) spy agency.[^cite_note-118]

#### 2005 Ayodhya attacks

Main article: [2005 Ram Janmabhoomi attack in Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Ram_Janmabhoomi_attack_in_Ayodhya)

The long simmering Ayodhya crisis finally culminated in a terrorist attack on the site of the 16th century [Babri Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babri_Mosque). The ancient Masjid in [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya) was demolished on 5 July 2005. Following the two-hour gunfight between [Lashkar-e-Toiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashkar-e-Toiba) terrorists based in Pakistan and Indian police, in which six terrorists were killed, opposition parties called for a nationwide strike with the country's leaders condemning the attack, believed to have been masterminded by [Dawood Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawood_Ibrahim).

#### 2022 Gorakhpur attack

In April 2022, a man armed with a dagger forcibly tried to enter the temple premises. Chanting religious slogans, the man identified as Ahmad Murtaza Abbasi approached the constables at the gate and attacked them as a result of which two constables were injured. As the police attempted to arrest him he evaded them for a while before being captured. Abbasi is an engineering graduate from [IIT Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIT_Bombay). The temple had a sizeable crowd of devotees as it was a Sunday afternoon. However no civilians were harmed in the attack. The case is being investigated by the Uttar Pradesh Anti Terrorist Squad.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

### West Bengal

#### 1993 Bowbazar bombing

Main article: [1993 Bowbazar bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Bowbazar_bombing)

The **Bowbazaar bomb blast** was an explosion which occurred in the central business district of [Bowbazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowbazar), Calcutta, on the night of 16 March 1993. In total, it claimed the lives of 69 people.

In 1993, Mohammed Rashid Khan ran gambling establishments in the Bowbazaar area and had a workshop above his office where he made small bombs. After the [demolition of the Babri Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_of_the_Babri_Masjid) and the riots which followed, Khan and his accomplices made plans to bomb parts of the city in order to kill Hindu residents. He began stockpiling explosives. Preparations were made secretly until, on the night of 16 March, the entire stockpile blew up accidentally. If the blast had occurred in the daytime, the death toll would have been much higher. According to some witnesses, the sound of the blast could be heard 3 miles away.

Khan and five others were sentenced to life imprisonment in 2001 by the [Calcutta High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_High_Court) under the [Terrorist And Disruptive (Prevention) Act (TADA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_and_Disruptive_Activities_\(Prevention\)_Act).
