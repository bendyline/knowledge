**Popular Renewal** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Renovación Popular*, **RP**) is a [right-wing](56522.md) to [far-right](221220.md) Peruvian political party. Founded in 2020, the party is the successor of the former [National Solidarity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_\(Peru\)) founded and led by former [Lima Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Lima) [Luis Castañeda Lossio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Casta%C3%B1eda_Lossio). Following its poor results at the [2020 snap parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Peruvian_parliamentary_election), leader [Rafael López Aliaga](65483323.md) announced the party's re-foundation under Popular Renewal.[^cite_note-2]

## History

As [Rafael López Aliaga](65483323.md) was elected in mid-2019 as Secretary General of [National Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Solidarity_\(Peru\)) following [Luis Castañeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Casta%C3%B1eda)'s resignation due to the pretrial detention on the [Odebrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odebrecht) scandal in [Peru](170691.md), the party shifted to a Christian conservative ideology and support for the current constitution of 1993. At the [2020 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Peruvian_parliamentary_election), National Solidarity received 1.5% of the popular vote, placing nineteenth out of twenty-one participating lists, thus failing to attain representation.[^cite_note-3] Following its poor results in the election, the party underwent a formal reconstruction.[^cite_note-4] During this period, [Rafael López Aliaga](65483323.md) formally announced his candidacy for the presidency in the [2021 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Peruvian_general_election), stating that he intended to gather all possible support from the country's conservative circles.[^cite_note-5]

Under this new platform, López Aliaga announced the dissolution of National Solidarity, effectively re-founding the party under the name Popular Renewal, in October 2020.[^cite_note-6] Assuming the position of party president, he stated that the party is "re-founded with principles of solidarity and anchored in God, Christ."[^cite_note-7]

The party's name-change was officially recognized in December 2020.[^cite_note-8] Simultaneously, López Aliaga was formally declared the party's presidential nominee for the [2021 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Peruvian_general_election), alongside [Neldy Mendoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neldy_Mendoza?action=edit\&redlink=1), chair of the Family and Life Institute, and [Jorge Montoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Montoya?action=edit\&redlink=1), former [Joint Command Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Joint_Command_\(Peru\)), as his first and second running mates.[^cite_note-9] López Aliaga affirmed that there will be no alliances with other political parties for those elections,[^cite_note-10] he also announced that the proposals they give will be based on five axes: health, work, security, education and anti-corruption.[^cite_note-11]

On Election Day, Lopez Aliaga placed third in a race of 18 candidates, while in the congressional election, the party gained 13 seats being the third most voted party and the fifth largest force in Congress.

## Ideology and position

Popular Renewal holds onto an [ultraconservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraconservatism) platform, mainly based in opposition to [abortion](765.md), [LGBT rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights), and "[gender ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_ideology)".[^cite_note-12] It has been described as holding a [far-right](221220.md),[^cite_note-17] [right-wing](56522.md),[^cite_note-23] [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism),[^cite_note-30] and [neo-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-fascism) political position.[^cite_note-33] Its members in the [Congress of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Peru) have been described as being part of a "far-right" bloc allied with [Go on Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_on_Country_%E2%80%93_Social_Integration_Party) and [Popular Force](15247524.md).[^cite_note-FAR-38] Internationally, the party has aligned with far-right groups, with Peruvian investigative journalism website *[OjoPúblico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OjoP%C3%BAblico)* writing in an article discussing far-right alliances in the Americas that members of the far-right Spanish political party [Vox](41928488.md) travelled to Peru to obtain signatures for the [anti-leftist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-leftist) manifesto known as the [Madrid Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Charter), with Popular Renewal, along with the parties Go on Country of [Hernando de Soto](285909.md) and Popular Force of [Keiko Fujimori](4643445.md), signing the document.[^cite_note-\:7-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

López Aliaga also opposed the secret effective collaboration agreement with the [Odebrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odebrecht) company, considering it a "harmful issue" for the Peruvian government.[^cite_note-42] He has also been against controversial businessmen such as José Graña and Jorge Barata.[^cite_note-43]
