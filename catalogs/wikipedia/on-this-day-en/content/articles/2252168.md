**Qeshm** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): قشم) is an [Iranian island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Iran) in the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) of the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), with an area of about 1,500 square kilometres (580 sq mi).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-AJE20260317-2] Separated from the Iranian mainland by the [Clarence Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Strait_\(Iran\)), Qeshm is the largest island in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). The island is part of [Qeshm County](7349780.md), Daraghan Province. There is a [hidden missile city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_underground_missile_bases) underneath the island, which contains various missiles, rockets, and drones.[^cite_note-AJE20260317-2] The island is home to the Namakdan Salt Cave, one of the longest known salt caves in the world, which is part of the Qeshm Island [UNESCO Global Geopark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Global_Geoparks).

## Etymology

Iranian [etymologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymologist) and [linguist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguist) Zana Salehrad ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): زانا صالح​راد) proposed that the [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponym) Qeshm reflects a layered history of linguistic adaptation in the Persian Gulf region. Salehrad suggests that the name originates from a pre-Islamic Iranian root, *Kašm* or *Kaš*, meaning "fortified place" or "enclosed land", reflecting the island’s strategic position along ancient maritime trade routes. Another explanation suggests that the island name has [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) roots and is derived from the phrase "a place to settle".[^cite_note-3] A third theory proposes that the island name derives from the Portuguese word ["queixume"](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/queixume#Portuguese).[^cite_note-4]

## Geography



Satellite image of Qeshm



A beach on Qeshm

Qeshm Island is located a few kilometers off of the southern Iranian shoreline, in the Persian Gulf, opposite the port cities of [Bandar Abbas](464348.md) and [Bandar Khamir](24750251.md). The island, which hosts a 300-square-kilometre (116-square-mile) free zone jurisdiction, is 135 km long and lies strategically in the Strait of Hormuz, 60 kilometres (37 miles) from the [Omani](22316.md) port of [Khasab](1658322.md) and about 180 kilometres (112 miles) from the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md)'s [Mina Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mina_Rashid).&#x20;

At its widest point, near its centre, the island spans 40 kilometres (25 miles). At its narrowest point, it measures approximately 9.4 kilometres (5.8 miles). The island has a [surface area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_area) of 1,491 square kilometres (576 square miles) and is almost twice the size of [Bahrain](18933277.md). The main population center of [Qeshm](32901385.md), located at the easternmost point of the island, is 22 kilometres (14 miles) from Bandar Abbas. The closest point of the island is two kilometres (1 mile) from the mainland.

### Namakdan Salt Cave

At 6.6 km long, Namakdan Salt Cave is one of the longest known [salt caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_cave) in the world. Both [salt](1605200.md) and [sulphur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulphur) were traditionally mined here ('namak' is salt in [Farsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language)). It now forms the key [geosite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoheritage) within the Qeshm Island [UNESCO Global Geopark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Global_Geoparks). The [cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave) formed within a large [salt dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_dome) from a succession of [strata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratum) that date from the late [Proterozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proterozoic). It is a candidate site for inscription on the list of [World Heritage Sites](44940.md).[^cite_note-5] In October 2022, it was included in a list of the "First 100 IUGS Geological Heritage Sites", on account of its significance to human understanding of [tectonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonics) and active [geological processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology).[^cite_note-6]

## Weather

The average temperature on the island is approximately 27 °C (81 °F). The warmest months are June to August. October to January are the coldest months. The average [rainfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainfall) is 183.2 mm (7+3⁄16 in).[^cite_note-7]

## History



The attack by the British on the fort of *[Luft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laft)*, 27 November 1809 during the [Persian Gulf campaign of 1809](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_campaign_of_1809)

The earliest evidence of human presence at Qeshm dates back to the [Paleolithic Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic), from when stone tools have been found at Bam-e-Qeshm.[^cite_note-8] Historical records concerning Qeshm date far back into the pre-Islamic era. Names such as *Qeshm*, *Keshm* (کشم), [*Kish*](847277.md) (کیش) and [*Tunb*](931816.md) (تنب) mark the lengthy stay of [Elamites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elam) in the area, spanning several centuries BC. It is, apparently, the island called Alexandria or Aracia by [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy)[^cite_note-9] in the 2nd century [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era) and Alexandria by [Ammianus Marcellinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus) (xxiii.6.42) in the 4th century.[^cite_note-1984MartinHinds-10]

On account of its strategic geopolitical situation near the mouth of the Persian Gulf, it has been frequently attacked by invaders, including Ilamids ([Elamites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elamites)), [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_dynasty) and [Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_dynasty), and the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England), and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Empire). Under [Sasanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) rule, between CE 224 and 651, the island was called *Abarkawan* and was part of the [Ardashir-Khwarrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir-Khwarrah) administrative division.[^cite_note-1984MartinHinds-10]

According to historical records, Qeshm Island has been known as a trade and navigation center. Its economy flourished during the [Dailamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylamites) and [Buyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyid_dynasty) eras, as trade vessels sailed past and stopped on Qeshm Island from places including China, India, and [Eastern Africa](260987.md).[^cite_note-1984MartinHinds-10] Qeshm, along with other islands in the Strait of Hormuz, was also used in the [Indian Ocean slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_slave_trade), in which [enslaved people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enslaved_people) were transported from Africa to various Arab countries. The inhabitants today retain some of the beliefs and cultural mores of their ancestors, such as the traditions and ceremonies of the [Zār](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C4%81r).[^cite_note-boetker-11][^cite_note-colberg-12]

In 1622, explorer [William Baffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Baffin) was mortally wounded on Qeshm during the [Anglo-Persian capture of Qeshm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Persian_capture_of_Qeshm) from Portuguese forces.

A nineteenth-century English [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible), edited by [John Cassell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cassell), posited that the [Garden of Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_of_Eden) was in Qeshm.[^cite_note-13]

### Modern era

On 3 July 1988, during the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), a civilian [Iran Air](168635.md) [Airbus A300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300) ([Iran Air Flight 655](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_Air_Flight_655)) was shot down by the United States Navy [cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser) [USS *Vincennes* (CG-49)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Vincennes_\(CG-49\)) just south of the island, killing all 290 people on board. The wreckage fell into the sea approximately 2.5 kilometres off the southern coast of Qeshm Island.[^cite_note-14]

In 2013, the IRGC Navy established a UAV base on the island, with a runway and a drone hangar.[^cite_note-15]

In March 2021 the [IRGC Navy](4676345.md) revealed what it called an “underground missile city,” with large numbers of [anti-ship missiles](140725.md) and [Fajr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fajr-5) rockets inside a tunnel complex close to the southern shores of the island.[^cite_note-contest-16] The IRGC 112th Zolfaghar Surface Combat Brigade was also on the island.[^cite_note-contest-16]

According to *[Iran International](58555280.md)*, during the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md), protesters chanted "[Death to the dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_to_the_dictator)" on Qeshm Island.[^cite_note-17]

#### 2026 Iran war

Main article: [2026 Iran war](82533582.md)

On 7 March 2026, the government of Iran claimed that the [water desalination](156787.md) plant on Qeshm Island was bombed by the United States military in the opening days of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), which it said had disrupted the water supply to 30 villages.[^cite_note-APNews8March-18] The United States, Israel, and the UAE all denied striking the plant.[^cite_note-APNews8March-18][^cite_note-TimeToxic-19] Various sources, including *[Al Jazeera](30204052.md)* reported that tunnels were built on the island to protect [Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Revolutionary_Guard_Corps) weapons, including [antiship missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiship_missile), mines, Nasir underwater drones, and small attack craft.[^cite_note-AJE20260317-2][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

On 31 March 2026, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that American or Israeli forces had conducted an airstrike on the island.[^cite_note-22]

According to *[Bloomberg News](3057255.md)*, Iran was forcing ships wishing to transit the Strait of Hormuz in March 2026 to pass through the narrow strait of Khuran between Qeshm and the mainland.[^cite_note-23]

## Economy



[Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin) in the strait between Qeshm and [Hengam Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengam_Island)

[Fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing) is a major source of income. [Dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm) and [melons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melon) are also grown on the island, and [salt](1605200.md) is mined on the southeastern coast. Qeshm offers [ecotourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) attractions such as the [Hara marine forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hara_forests). According to environmentalists, about 1.5% of the world's birds and 25% of Iran's native birds annually migrate to the forests, which are the first national [geopark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopark) in Iran.[^cite_note-iranreview-24]

Tourist attractions on the island include an [early modern-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_era) [Portuguese](23033.md) fort, historic mosques, the Seyyed Mozaffar and Bibi Maryam shrines, ponds, and [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) forests. Additional attractions comprise of [Salt caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_cave), a hatching area for [Hawksbill turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawksbill_sea_turtle) in [Shibderaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shib_Deraz) village, as well as numerous docks and [wharfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharf) along the coast.[^cite_note-iranreview-24]

Qeshm

The [Nazz Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazz_Islands) are located at the south wing of Qeshm Island. Oysters, corals, colorful fish, and seabirds in these islands are a tourist attraction.[^cite_note-iranreview-24]

In the first ten-year plan of the Islamic Republic, the law provided for the creation of [free trade zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_zone). In 1991, three locations were identified—[Kish Island](847277.md), Qeshm Island, and [Chabahar](1658958.md). Qeshm Island was then transformed into a "Trade and Industrial Free Area".[^cite_note-25]

To that end, the island was granted considerable leeway to set its own policies, independent of the central government, which had often been seen as an impediment to growth in many sectors of the economy. The island retains the advantages associated with its connection to the mainland, including the rights to explore and develop the oil and gas reserves.

## Demographics

The island, comprising 59 towns and villages, had a population of 117,774 in the 2011 census. The main population center is [Qeshm](32901385.md). The local population is engaged in fishing, [dhow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhow) vessel construction, trade, and services. An additional 30,000 are administrative or industrial workers, or students.

## In the arts

[Iranian Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Australian) photographer [Hoda Afshar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoda_Afshar)'s book *Speak the Wind* (2021), which includes an essay by anthropologist [Michael Taussig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Taussig), contains many photographs of the people of the Qeshm, [Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormuz_Island), and [Hengam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengam_Island). Some of her photographs illustrate their belief in the [zār](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C4%81r), a harmful spirit-wind that possesses people's bodies. One reviewer sees her images as a metaphor for "possession of land and of bodies in the real world, and of the transgenerational trauma that possession constructs".[^cite_note-boetker-11][^cite_note-colberg-12]
