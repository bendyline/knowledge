The **National Salvation Movement** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Movimiento de Salvación Nacional*, **MSN**) is a Colombian [far-right](221220.md) [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) founded in 1990 by [Álvaro Gómez Hurtado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_G%C3%B3mez_Hurtado). Originally established by dissident members of the [Colombian Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Conservative_Party), the party emerged as a conservative alternative to the traditional party leadership and became one of Colombia's most prominent minor political movements during the 1990s.&#x20;

In the [1990 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Colombian_presidential_election), Gómez finished second, outperforming the official Conservative Party candidate. The party subsequently maintained representation in both the [Senate](645242.md) and the [Chamber of Representatives](645347.md).&#x20;

Despite its defeat in the election, the party achieved its greatest political success in the election to the [1991 National Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_Colombia), which was tasked with drafting a [new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1991) for Colombia, where it won 11 seats. The election, held in December 1990, took place under exceptional political circumstances amid a broader process of constitutional reform. For the campaign, [Álvaro Gómez Hurtado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_G%C3%B3mez_Hurtado) sought support beyond traditional party lines, assembling what he described as a "supra-partisan" [political movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_movement).[^cite_note-5]

Following electoral decline in the 2000s, the party lost its parliamentary presence and was dissolved after the [2006 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Colombian_parliamentary_election).&#x20;

The party was re-established in 2021 under the leadership of [Enrique Gómez Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_G%C3%B3mez_Mart%C3%ADnez), a nephew of its founder. It contested the [2022 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Colombian_parliamentary_election) and nominated Gómez as its candidate in the [2022 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Colombian_presidential_election), where he received 0.23% of the vote.&#x20;

The National Salvation Movement has positioned itself in opposition to the government of [Gustavo Petro](13391962.md). For the [2026 presidential election](79603998.md), the party supported lawyer [Abelardo de la Espriella](81665680.md) as its presidential candidate, with [José Manuel Restrepo Abondano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Restrepo_Abondano) as its candidate for vice president. To support the campaign, the party adopted a new logo featuring a [tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger) and three claw marks in place of the rainbow previously used in its emblem.[^cite_note-\:02-6]

In the [2026 parliamentary election](79604693.md), the party fielded an open Senate list headed by chairman [Enrique Gómez Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_G%C3%B3mez_Mart%C3%ADnez).[^cite_note-7] The party won four seats in the [Senate](645242.md) and one seat in the [Chamber of Representatives](645347.md), marking a significant increase in its electoral support compared with the previous election and allowing it to retain its legal status.&#x20;

In the first round of the presidential election, held on 31 May 2026, de la Espriella received 10,361,413 votes (43.74% of the vote), placing first among the candidates and advancing to the runoff election against [Iván Cepeda](66799376.md).[^cite_note-8]
