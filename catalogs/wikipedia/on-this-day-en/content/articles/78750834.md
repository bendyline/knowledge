[Georgia](48768.md) is currently undergoing a [political crisis](52674452.md) due to the disputed legitimacy of the [October 2024 Georgian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_parliamentary_election), which was conducted with significant [irregularities](382165.md) and described by observers as "fundamentally flawed".[^cite_note-IRI_final_report_Georgia_Dec2024-2] The crisis continued with the unconstitutional self-convening of [Parliament](3471146.md)[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_Pres_parl_meeting_unconstitutional-10][^cite_note-OCMedia_GD_opens_new_parl-11] and escalated with the decision of the ruling party to suspend preparations for [EU accession negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Georgia_to_the_European_Union),[^cite_note-OpenDemoc_witnessing_revolutionn_GE-1] which was seen as contradicting Article 78 of the [Georgian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Georgia_\(country\)).[^cite_note-OpenDemoc_witnessing_revolutionn_GE-1] The crisis entered another phase with the [election of a new president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_presidential_election) by the [Georgian Electoral Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Electoral_Assembly)[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_I_remain_President_Zurabishvili-12] and its 29 December 2024 inauguration of [Mikheil Kavelashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikheil_Kavelashvili).[^cite_note-OCMedia_Zourabichvili_leave_Orbeliani-13] [Salome Zourabichvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salome_Zourabichvili) stated on 29 December and during the following weeks that she remained the president of Georgia.[^cite_note-OCMedia_Zourabichvili_leave_Orbeliani-13][^cite_note-AgendaGE_Zourabichvili_relocate_2018-14][^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_attend_inauguration-15]

[Protests against the ruling party](78322562.md) have continued since the election, with hundreds of protesters arrested, beaten or tortured[^cite_note-16] by the police and the ruling-party affiliated [violent groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titushky#Georgia), who also attacked journalists.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

## Background

See also: [Democratic backsliding in Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding_in_Georgia)

By 2024, former prime minister and billionaire [Bidzina Ivanishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidzina_Ivanishvili) was widely seen as the *de facto* ruler of Georgia, with most Georgian government officials and institutions following his orders, with the exception of President [Salome Zourabichvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salome_Zourabichvili), who maintained her independent positions.[^cite_note-TransparencyGE_Oligarch_Bidzina-21][^cite_note-DW_Oligarch_GE_pivot-22][^cite_note-Guardian_Ivanishvili_puppet_master-23][^cite_note-Meduza_Ivanishvili_latest_comeback-24] In late December 2024, political scientist [Stephen F. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_F._Jones) stated that Ivanishvili had become "the unaccountable and unchecked ruler" of Georgia.[^cite_note-OpenDemoc_witnessing_revolutionn_GE-1]

## 2024 October parliamentary election

Main articles: [2024 Georgian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_parliamentary_election), [2024 Georgian post-election protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_post-election_protests), and [Next Georgian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Georgian_parliamentary_election)

Prior to the October [2024 Georgian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_parliamentary_election), in 2023 and again in April 2024, [Georgian Dream](35807631.md) (GD) proposed two successive versions of a [foreign agent law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%932024_Georgian_protests#Foreign_agent_bill) in relation to non-governmental organisations receiving foreign funding. Both versions were controversial, leading to [successive phases of street protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%932024_Georgian_protests) in opposition to the law[^cite_note-25] and significantly weakening popular support for GD.[^cite_note-OpenDemoc_witnessing_revolutionn_GE-1]

Prior to the election, [Ivanishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidzina_Ivanishvili) stated that he wanted a three-quarters majority in parliament so that GD would be able to make [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Georgia_\(country\)) changes to "ban the opposition".[^cite_note-Reuters_GD_founder_ban_opposition-26][^cite_note-OpenDemoc_witnessing_revolutionn_GE-1] The October 2024 election was held. [Salome Zourabichvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salome_Zourabichvili) and the four main opposition groups considered the election to be legally invalid[^cite_note-27] because of significant irregularities in the conduct of the election.[^cite_note-IRI_final_report_Georgia_Dec2024-2][^cite_note-ODIHR_Georgian26Oct2024_observation_mission_final-28] Mass street protests took place daily following the election, together with police repression.

On 18 December 2024, the [Public Defender of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Defender_of_Georgia) stated that his representatives had visited 327 detainees, among which 225 stated that they had been ill-treated, and 157 had visible signs of physical injuries. Representatives from [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International) Georgia, Georgian European Orbit and Rule of Law Center stated that Georgian Dream had "planned the systemic torture of peaceful demonstrators", with a "system of torture \[planned] in advance". They stated that detainees had been "beaten in the face, head, eye sockets, ribs, \[and] kidneys" and that the detainees had been robbed of personal belongings by the security forces.[^cite_note-GeorgiaToday_along_with_beatings-3]

Opposition media described the resignations of government officials in early December as a "collapse of the system". According to Gocha Beridze, former head of [Batumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batumi) Coast Guard Marine Safety Department, 49 employees of the [Ministry of Internal Affairs of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Internal_Affairs_of_Georgia) resigned: Irakli Shaishmelashvili, the head of a department in the Ministry with responsibility for dispersing protests, and four of his deputies; 16 special forces instructors; the head of the psychological training service and twelve of its members; and all 16 water cannon operators.[^cite_note-Charter97_system_collapse-29] In late December 2024, after leaving Georgia for his and his family's safety, Shaishmelashvili gave an extensive interview. He stated that police violence was systematic and done under orders given by [Zviad Kharazishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zviad_Kharazishvili), the head of the [Special Tasks Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Tasks_Department_\(Georgian_police\)) of the Ministry and by Georgian Dream founder [Bidzina Ivanishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidzina_Ivanishvili), who according to Shaishmelashvili are close colleagues of one another. Shaishmelashvili stated that no investigations into police violence were being conducted and that none were planned.[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_interview_senior_official-30]

In late December 2024, Zourabichvili announced plans to create a council with broad participation to organise a [re-run of the election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Georgian_parliamentary_election),[^cite_note-OCMedia_regime_collapsing-31][^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_Pres_opposition_CSOs_vision-32] as recommended by the [European Parliament](9581.md).[^cite_note-EuroParl_GE_worsening_democratic_crisis-33]

## Self-convening of Parliament and EU accession suspension

Main article: [Accession of Georgia to the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Georgia_to_the_European_Union)

Following the October election, Zourabichvili refused to convene a session of the [Parliament of Georgia](3471146.md) with the members elected per the official results of the election. Parliament self-convened, though Zourabichvili and Georgia's top legal and constitutional experts considered the action unconstitutional.[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_Pres_parl_meeting_unconstitutional-10][^cite_note-OCMedia_GD_opens_new_parl-11][^cite_note-auto1-34]

Forty staff members of the [Georgian Public Defender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Public_Defender)'s office published an open letter protesting against the Public Defender's attendance at the self-convened opening of parliament. Lawyers and human rights activists stated that Article 86 (5a) of the rules for parliamentary procedures prevent electees from starting their mandates if the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Georgia) is considering legal disputes about the election. The staff members' letter stated that the self-convening of parliament "violate\[d] the constitutional and legal order, undermine\[d] the Public Defender's role as an independent constitutional body, and erode\[d] trust in the institution". Levani Ioseliani, the Public Defender, stated that the "convening of the Parliament of Georgia was entirely legitimate, in accordance with Article 38 of the Constitution."[^cite_note-OCMedia_public_defender_admonished-35]

The 28 November announcement by Kobakhidze of suspending [EU accession negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Georgia_to_the_European_Union) was widely seen as unconstitutional, with two hundred [National Bank of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Georgia) employees declaring the suspension to be inconsistent with Article 78 of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Georgia_\(country\)). A [new round of protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_Georgian_post-election_protests#Second_wave_\(28_November%E2%80%93present\)) followed the announcement.[^cite_note-OpenDemoc_witnessing_revolutionn_GE-1]

## Presidential election

Main article: [2024 Georgian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_presidential_election)

Parliament [elected a new president of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_presidential_election) on 14 December 2024. Zourabichvili considered the election to be invalid. She stated that she would retain her status as president because of the invalidity of the election.[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_I_remain_President_Zurabishvili-12] Protests continued after the presidential election, including a [human chain](484000.md) on 28 December.[^cite_note-FR24_human_chains_28Dec-36]

On 29 December 2024, [Mikheil Kavelashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikheil_Kavelashvili) was inaugurated as president. Zourabichvili left [Orbeliani Palace](68206220.md), which in 2018 she had chosen as her presidential residence instead of [Avlabari Presidential Residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_Palace_of_Georgia),[^cite_note-AgendaGE_Zourabichvili_relocate_2018-14] stating that she remained the legitimate President, as the palace is only a symbol.[^cite_note-KyivPost_Kavelashvili_exMan_City_striker-37][^cite_note-OCMedia_Zourabichvili_leave_Orbeliani-13] Zourabichvili described the inauguration as a "mockery of democracy" and stated that [Georgian Dream](35807631.md) (GD) was "locked up, scared, corrupt, illegitimate, unrecognized, subject to sanctions".[^cite_note-KyivIndep_Kavelashvili_sworn_in-38] On 9 January 2025, Zourabichvili held a press briefing at her new office, stating that she would continue to work to solve the crisis, meeting people around Georgia and internationally. She stated that she would attend the [second inauguration of Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump) and hold high-level meetings in [Washington](108956.md). She described the Georgian state as being "practically on the verge of collapse", with [state capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_capture) by a single party and single person, which she viewed as a "betrayal of the Constitution". Zourabichvili stated that she remained president and would remain so if a [new election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Georgian_parliamentary_election) were held.[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_attend_inauguration-15]

## Civil service resignations and dismissals

In addition to the December 2024 resignations of 49 members of the Ministry of Internal Affairs,[^cite_note-Charter97_system_collapse-29][^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_interview_senior_official-30] several civil service employees were dismissed. On 31 December, [Central Election Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Administration_of_Georgia) (CEC) advisor Vako Maisuradze stated that he and other CEC employees had been fired for criticising GD on [online social media](2041117.md).[^cite_note-OCMedia_CEC_fires_adviser-4] On 3 January 2025, *[OC Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OC_Media)* described the overall pattern of dismissals as "purges of public sector employees critical of the government".[^cite_note-OCMedia_GD_public_sector_against-5]

## Wave of repressive laws

The crisis was accompanied by a wave of repressive laws passed by the Georgian Dream government from early 2025. More than twenty laws were passed that greatly increased the powers of the state to regulate and suppress dissent in various forms. Changes include regulation and limitation of funding for civil society organization, limitations on the right of assembly, vastly increased fines of [₾](904025.md)5,000 for administrative misdemeanours, proposed bans on opposition parties, electoral changes including the removal of the possibility of the Georgian diaspora voting abroad, increased punishments for insults to public officials, restrictions on media coverage of court proceedings, and many additional measures.[^cite_note-OC_Media_--_laws-39] These laws were widely criticized by local and international organizations.[^cite_note-IEP-40][^cite_note-JAM_News-41] A law on creating a central database for people with mental health challenges was criticized for its repressive potential.[^cite_note-42] In December 2025, protesting by standing on the sidewalk was prohibited, with sanctions escalating to criminal prosecution.[^cite_note-Tiflis24-43] Lawyers viewed the December law as unconstitutional.[^cite_note-Tiflis24-43]

A proposal for the [2025–2026 restructuring of Georgia's education system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_restructuring_of_Georgia's_education_system) was widely seen as an attempt to subjugate the higher education sector to executive control.[^cite_note-Kapanadze-GEO-44]

## International reactions

The [European Parliament](9581.md) adopted 5 resolutions regarding Georgia since [2024 Georgian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Georgian_parliamentary_election). On 28 November 2024, the [European Parliament](9581.md), by a majority of 444 in favour, 72 against, and 82 abstentions, declared that it did not recognise the October parliamentary election.[^cite_note-EuroParl_GE_worsening_democratic_crisis-33] Since then, the [European Parliament](9581.md) demanded the release of Georgian journalist, [Mzia Amaglobeli](79038175.md) citing that "the authorities ignored procedural safeguards, imposed pre-trial detention without a clear legal basis, contested by the Public Defender, and assigned a presiding judge lacking qualifications in criminal law"[^cite_note-45] and pointing out that "she is being punished for exposing corruption and reporting on election fraud during the 2024 elections".[^cite_note-46] In its resolutions, the [European Parliament](9581.md) also demands the release of all political prisoners and encourages the European Commission and EU member states to step up the support of Georgian people. The [European Parliament](9581.md) awarded [the 2025 Sakharov Prize for Freedom and Thought](1055590.md) to Mzia Amaglobeli for her fight for freedom of expression and democratic future of Georgia.[^cite_note-47]

On 19 December 2025, the [European Commission](9974.md) published its 8th report under visa suspension mechanism where a chapter was dedicated to Georgia's lack of compliance on respect for fundamental rights benchmark adopted during visa liberalisation dialogue.[^cite_note-\:0-48] The [Commission](9974.md) highlighted that Georgia failed to address any recommendations of previous report in this regard. The report concludes that "Georgia continues to be in breach of the benchmarks underpinning the visa-free regime granted by the EU. In light of these developments, appropriate measures should be considered under the revised Visa Suspension Mechanism".[^cite_note-\:0-48] On 6 March 2026, The [Commission](9974.md) adopted an implementing regulation under revised visa-suspension mechanism to suspend the visa-free regime for Georgian holders of diplomatic, service or official passports.[^cite_note-\:1-49] The Commission cited "Georgia's deliberate and persisting violation of the commitments taken under its visa-free regime in key areas of democracy and fundamental rights"[^cite_note-\:1-49] as cause for this move. The [Commission's](9974.md) implementing act is mandatory for all EU member states.

According to the [Weimar Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Triangle) (France, Germany, Poland) foreign ministers, the electoral irregularities and the violence against protestors, journalists and opposition politicians constituted [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding). The ministers stated that they would implement a [European Union](9317.md) (EU) decision to end visa-free travel for Georgian officials.[^cite_note-WeimarTriangle_statement_GE_2024Dec31-50]

### Constitutional law

Several of Georgia's top legal and constitutional experts, including Vakhushti Menabde, Vakhtang Khmaladze and Sandro Baramidze, stated that [GD](35807631.md)'s conduct violated the [Georgian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Georgia_\(country\)), as well as parliament's own rules and procedures, and that the resulting parliament or the president elected by such a parliament cannot be considered legitimate.[^cite_note-OCMedia_GD_opens_new_parl-11][^cite_note-auto1-34] Constitutional scholar Vakhtang Khmaladze, who is one of the authors of Georgia's current constitution and is himself a former member of GD, has described the election of Kavelashvili as "illegitimate".[^cite_note-KyivPost_Kavelashvili_exMan_City_striker-37] In addition to the issue of fraud during the parliamentary elections and the fact that none of the violations were addressed, Khmaladze stated that the parliament members had illegally approved their mandates when they had no actual authority to do so, because the election results were still pending in court.[^cite_note-auto1-34]

Two hundred members of the [National Bank of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Georgia) stated that the suspension of preparations for [EU accession negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Georgia_to_the_European_Union) was unconstitutional because it opposed Article 78 of the constitution.[^cite_note-OpenDemoc_witnessing_revolutionn_GE-1]

### Authoritarian overreach point of view

On 3 December 2024, political scientist [Stephen F. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_F._Jones) stated that [Ivanishvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidzina_Ivanishvili) had become "the unaccountable and unchecked ruler" of Georgia, and that Ivanishvili's government was "on the brink of collapse". Jones saw GD as having made three main errors of political judgment. In April 2024, GD [reintroduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%932024_Georgian_protests#April) the [Foreign agent bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%932024_Georgian_protests#Foreign_agent_bill), leading to massive protests by citizens and institutions such as the [Venice Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Commission) of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe). Jones argued that in October 2024, GD could quite likely "have secured a small parliamentary majority without massively falsifying the elections", but falsified the elections because Ivanishvili wanted a three-quarters majority in order to be able to unilaterally make constitutional changes. This led to a [new round of protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_Georgian_post-election_protests#First_wave_\(28_October%E2%80%9325_November\)), "angrier" from both the citizens' and government's side, according to Jones. The third mistake in Jones' view was the 28 November announcement by Kobakhidze of suspending [EU accession negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Georgia_to_the_European_Union), which he saw as "the fatal error", "enrag\[ing] a Georgian populace that for two centuries has believed it is Europe", and leading to a [third round of intensified protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_Georgian_post-election_protests#Second_wave_\(28_November%E2%80%93present\)).[^cite_note-OpenDemoc_witnessing_revolutionn_GE-1]

Jones explained what he saw as GD's strategic errors in terms of Ivanishvili's centralised control not tolerating criticism, with critics typically being "banish\[ed] from the inner circle". He viewed GD as losing the support of elites and being "forced by its own errors into a corner", with the only options being either "survival by brutal suppression (already an unlikely scenario), or flight".[^cite_note-OpenDemoc_witnessing_revolutionn_GE-1]

Irakli Pavlenishvili of [Unity – National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_%E2%80%93_National_Movement) predicted on 3 January 2025 that Ivanishvili would not be able to "gather the critical mass that is necessary for even an authoritarian regime to function because ... a very large part of public servants are against establishing a dictatorship".[^cite_note-OCMedia_GD_public_sector_against-5]

### Pro-Russia versus pro-EU policies

After coming to power in the [2012 Georgian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Georgian_parliamentary_election), Georgian Dream continued relations with the US and the EU and improved economic relations with Russia by 2017. Following the 2022 full-scale [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), GD supported the expulsion of Russia from the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) and the [International Criminal Court investigation in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_investigation_in_Ukraine). It did not join economic sanctions on Russia and did not send arms to Ukraine. Trade between Russia and Georgia expanded.[^cite_note-quincy_West_Georgia_crisis-51] GD denies being "pro-Russia"[^cite_note-Reuters_protesters_clash_GE-52] and states that it supports joining the [European Union](9317.md) (EU). At a meeting of Georgian ambassadors on 29 December 2024, Kavelashvili, [Shalva Papuashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalva_Papuashvili) and GD-appointed prime minister [Irakli Kobakhidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irakli_Kobakhidze) stated that Georgia was continuing to progress towards membership of the EU.[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_2024_ambassadorial-53] Earlier, on 28 November, Kobakhidze announced that the government would not pursue the opening of EU accession negotiations until late 2028.[^cite_note-CivilGeorgia_GD_aborts_EU-54]
