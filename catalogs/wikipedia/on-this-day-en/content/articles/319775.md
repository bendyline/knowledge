**South Gyeongsang Province** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 경상남도; Korean pronunciation: [\[kjʌŋ.saŋ.nam.do\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Korean)), also known as **Gyeongnam** (경남), is a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_South_Korea) in the southeast of [South Korea](27019.md). The provincial capital is at [Changwon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changwon). It is adjacent to the major metropolitan center and port of [Busan](68115.md). The UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md) [Haeinsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haeinsa), a [Buddhist temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Buddhist_temples) that houses the *[Tripitaka Koreana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripitaka_Koreana)* and tourist attraction, is located in this province. Automobile and petrochemical factories are largely concentrated along the southern part of the province, extending from [Ulsan](323111.md) through Busan, [Changwon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changwon), and [Jinju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinju).

## Etymology

The name derives from [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language)&#x20;*&#x20;Gyeongsang* 'joyous furthermore'; from&#x20;*&#x20;gyeong (Korean 경, Hanja [慶](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%85%B6))* 'celebrate' and&#x20;*&#x20;sang (Korean 상, Hanja [尚](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%B0%9A))* 'append to'. The name derives from the names of the principal cities of [Gyeongju](426047.md) (경주; 慶州) and [Sangju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangju) (상주; 尙州).

### Joseon

In 1407, for military purposes, the administrative districts were reorganized, with Gyeongsang-do being divided into Gyeongsangjwa-do (or Gyeongsangjwa Province; left) and Gyeongsangwu-do (or Gyeongsangwu Province; right) as the reference points of the [Nakdong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakdonggang_River).[^cite_note-3]

Before 1895, the area corresponding to modern-day South Gyeongsang Province was part of [Gyeongsang Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongsang_Province), one of the [Eight Provinces of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Provinces_of_Korea) during the [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) dynastic kingdom. In 1895, southern Gyeongsang was replaced by the districts of [Jinju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinju) in the west and Dongnae (modern-day [Busan](68115.md)) in the east. In 1896, they were merged to form South Gyeongsang Province.

### Today

The provincial capital was originally in Jinju; it moved to Busan in 1925. During [Imperial Japanese colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), it was known as [Keishōnan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keish%C5%8Dnan_Province).

In 1948, South Gyeongsang became part of [South Korea](27019.md). In 1963, Busan separated from South Gyeongsang Province to become a [Directly Governed City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_cities_of_South_Korea) (*Jikhalsi*). In 1983, the provincial capital moved from Busan to Changwon. In 1995, Busan became a Metropolitan City (*Gwangyeoksi*), and [Ulsan](323111.md) separated from South Gyeongsang Province to become a Metropolitan City in 1997.

## Geography

The province is part of the [Yeongnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongnam) [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Korea), on the north by [North Gyeongsang Province](314848.md), on the west by [North Jeolla Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Jeolla_Province) and [South Jeolla Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jeolla_Province), and on the south by the [Korea Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Strait) far from [Nagasaki Prefecture](221380.md), Japan. Most of the province is drained by the [Nakdong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakdong_River) and its tributaries. The total area of the province is 10,533 square kilometres (4,067 sq mi).[^cite_note-4]

The tail of the [Taebaek Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taebaek_Mountains) reaches the east of the Province, and the [Nakdong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakdonggang_River) flows in the middle of the mountain range. The Province is bordered by the [Sobaek Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobaek_Mountains) and [Honam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honam) to the west. The central lowland spreads along the Nakdonggang River basin. The Nakdonggang River is collecting tributaries of Gyeongsangnam-do to develop the [Gimhae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimhae) Delta Plain near the downstream. [Jirisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiri_Mountain), [Deogyusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deogyusan), and [Bagunsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baegunsan_\(South_Jeolla\)) are lined up in the west.[^cite_note-5]

The Nakdong delta plain around [Gimhae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimhae) is one of the best granaries in South Korea. Agricultural products form Gyeongsangnam-do include [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beans), [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potatoes), and [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley). The area is renowned for its [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [sesame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame), and [fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruits) which are grown along the southern seaside. A number of marine products are caught. The province is one of the country's leading fisheries.

## Attractions



[Yeongnamnu in Miryang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongnamnu_\(Miryang\)), South Gyeongsang Province. A pavilion from the [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon).

Gyeongsangnam-do is the home of [Haeinsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haeinsa), a [Buddhist temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Buddhist_temples) that houses the *[Tripitaka Koreana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripitaka_Koreana)* and attracts many tourists. It is in the national park around [Jirisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirisan) (1,915 m) on the border with Jeollabuk-do. The temple was first built in 802.

[Changnyeong County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changnyeong_County) contains three major tourist attractions for the province: [Upo Wetland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upo_Wetland), the natural hotsprings of [Bugok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugok), and [Hwawangsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwawangsan).

[Yangsan](968885.md)-si contains two major temples for the province: [Tongdosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongdosa) and Naewon Temple

## Culture

### Festival

Beginning in 2002, the **[Tongyeong International Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongyeong_International_Music_Festival)** is one of the most important music events in Asia. Held every spring, the festival consists of stages from well-known [classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music) figures and aspiring musicians. It aims to foster young composers and performers.[^cite_note-9]

**Jinhae Naval Port Festival**, one of the biggest cherry blossom festivals in South Korea, is held in [Jinhae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinhae).[^cite_note-10]

The **Haman Water Fireworks Festival** takes place around [Buddha's Birthday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha's_Birthday) in April every year. It is one of the cultures that has existed since the [Joseon Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon_Dynasty). It originated from the heart of praying for peace in the country.[^cite_note-11]

## Tourism

The **[Jinju National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinju_National_Museum)** opened in 1984 as the first national museum in South Gyeongsang Province. Visitors can experience cultural heritages in the Province, including [Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaya_confederacy).[^cite_note-12]

**[Haeinsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haeinsa)**, one of Korea's top three temples, was built in 802 by two monks Seung-woon and Lee Jung. The temple houses various treasures along with [Woodblocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodblock_printing) of national treasures, including the [Tripitaka Koreana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripitaka_Koreana) and Janggyeong Panjeon.[^cite_note-13]

## Economy

The province accounts for 43% of the national space industry production, and is home to [Korea Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Aerospace_Industries) and [Hanwha Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanwha_Aerospace), as well as 125 industrial complexes. The [National Aerospace Industrial Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aerospace_Industrial_Complex) is currently being developed, with completion targeted for June 2025.[^cite_note-14]

The shipyards of [Geoje](968842.md) are one of the largest shipyards in the world and host shipbuilders such as [Samsung Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Heavy_Industries)[^cite_note-15]
