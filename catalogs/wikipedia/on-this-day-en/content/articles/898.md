**Antimony** is a [chemical element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element) with the [symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_symbol) **Sb** (from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language)&#x20;*&#x20;stibium*) and [atomic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_number) 51. A lustrous grey metal or [metalloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metalloid), it occurs in nature mainly in the form of the [sulfide mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfide_mineral) [stibnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stibnite) (Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>). Antimony compounds have been known since [ancient history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history) and were powdered for use as cosmetics (often known by the Arabic name [kohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohl_\(cosmetics\))) and as medicine.

China is the largest producer of antimony and its compounds, with most production coming from the [Xikuangshan Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xikuangshan_Mine) in Hunan. The industrial methods for refining antimony from [stibnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stibnite) are [roasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roasting_\(metallurgy\)) followed by [reduction with carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbothermic_reaction), or direct reduction of stibnite with iron.

The most common applications for metallic antimony are in [alloys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy) with [lead](17747.md) and [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin), which have improved properties for [solders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solder), [bullets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet), and [plain bearings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_bearing). It improves the rigidity of lead-alloy plates in [lead–acid batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead%E2%80%93acid_battery). [Antimony trioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trioxide) is a prominent additive for [halogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halogen)-containing [flame retardants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flame_retardant). Antimony is used as a [dopant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopant) in [semiconductor devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_device).

Antimony is a member of [group 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_\(periodic_table\)) of the [periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table). As one of the elements called [pnictogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pnictogen), it has an [electronegativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronegativity) of 2.05. In accordance with periodic trends, it is more electronegative than [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) or [bismuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismuth), and less electronegative than [tellurium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tellurium) or [arsenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic). As a metalloid, it has a [Mohs scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohs_scale) hardness of 3.

Antimony is a silvery, lustrous gray solid that is stable in air at room temperature.[^cite_note-13] If heated, it reacts with [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) to produce [antimony trioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trioxide),Sb<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>.[^cite_note-w758-14] Antimony is attacked by [oxidizing acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidizing_acid).

The stable [allotrope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotropy) of antimony crystallises in a [trigonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal) cell, [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_\(crystallography\)) with [bismuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismuth) and the gray allotrope of [arsenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic).

A yellow allotrope of antimony, assumed to be analogous to yellow arsenic, forms by oxidation of [stibine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stibine) (SbH<sub>3</sub>) with air or oxygen at −90 °C.[^cite_note-allotropes-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] At ambient temperatures and in ambient light, it transforms into the more stable black allotrope.[^cite_note-kirk-18][^cite_note-19] A rare [explosive form of antimony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_form_of_antimony) can be formed from the electrolysis of [antimony trichloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trichloride), but it always contains appreciable chlorine and is not really an antimony allotrope.[^cite_note-allotropes-15] When scratched with a sharp implement, an [exothermic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exothermic) reaction occurs and white fumes are given off as metallic antimony forms; when rubbed with a [pestle in a mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_and_pestle), a strong detonation occurs.

Elemental antimony adopts a layered structure ([space group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_group) R3m No. 166) whose layers consist of fused, ruffled, six-membered rings. The nearest and next-nearest neighbors form an irregular octahedral complex, with the three atoms in each double layer slightly closer than the three atoms in the next. This relatively close packing leads to a high density of 6.697 g/cm<sup>3</sup>, but the weak bonding between the layers leads to the low hardness and brittleness of antimony.[^cite_note-w758-14]

### Isotopes

Main article: [Isotopes of antimony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotopes_of_antimony)

Antimony has two stable [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope): <sup>121</sup>Sb with a natural abundance of 57.21% and <sup>123</sup>Sb with a natural abundance of 42.79%. There are 37 artificial [radioactive isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_isotopes) known with mass numbers 104 to 142, of which the longest-lived is the [fission product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fission_product) <sup>125</sup>Sb with a [half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-life) of 2.758 years. Numerous [meta states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_isomer) are known, of which the most stable is <sup>120m1</sup>Sb with a [half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-life) of 5.76 days. Isotopes that are lighter than the stable <sup>123</sup>Sb tend to undergo [β<sup>+</sup> decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron_emission), and those that are heavier experience [β<sup>−</sup> decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_decay), with some exceptions.[^cite_note-20]

### Occurrence

See also: [Category\:Antimonide minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Antimonide_minerals) and [Category\:Antimonate minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Antimonate_minerals)



[Stibnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stibnite), China CM29287 Carnegie Museum of Natural History specimen on display in [Hillman Hall of Minerals and Gems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillman_Hall_of_Minerals_and_Gems)

The abundance of antimony in the Earth's crust is estimated at 0.2 [parts per million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_per_million),[^cite_note-g548-21] comparable to [thallium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thallium) at 0.5 ppm and silver at 0.07 ppm. It is the 63rd most abundant element in the crust. Even though this element is not abundant, it is found in more than 100 mineral species.[^cite_note-22] Antimony is sometimes found natively (e.g. on [Antimony Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_Peak)), but more frequently it is found in the sulfide [stibnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stibnite) (Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>) which is the predominant ore mineral.[^cite_note-g548-21]

## Compounds

See also: [Category\:Antimony compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Antimony_compounds)

Antimony compounds are often classified according to their oxidation state: Sb(III) and Sb(V). The +5 [oxidation state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation_state) is more common.[^cite_note-23]

### Oxides and hydroxides

[Antimony trioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trioxide) is formed when antimony is burnt in air.[^cite_note-24] In the gas phase, the molecule of the compound is Sb<sub>4</sub>O<sub>6</sub>, but it [polymerizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerization) upon condensing.[^cite_note-w758-14] [Antimony pentoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_pentoxide) (Sb<sub>4</sub>O<sub>10</sub>) can be formed only by oxidation with concentrated [nitric acid](21655.md).[^cite_note-house-25] Antimony also forms a mixed-valence oxide, [antimony tetroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_tetroxide) (Sb<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>), which features both Sb(III) and Sb(V).[^cite_note-house-25] Unlike oxides of [phosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus) and [arsenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic), these oxides are [amphoteric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphoteric), do not form well-defined [oxoacids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxoacid), and react with acids to form antimony salts.

Antimonous acid Sb(OH)<sub>3</sub> is unknown, but the conjugate base sodium antimonite (\[Na<sub>3</sub>SbO<sub>3</sub>]<sub>4</sub>) forms upon fusing [sodium oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_oxide) and Sb<sub>4</sub>O<sub>6</sub>.[^cite_note-26] Transition metal antimonites are also known.[^cite_note-norman-27]<sup>: 122 </sup> Antimonic acid exists only as the hydrate HSb(OH)<sub>6</sub>, forming salts as the antimonate anion \[Sb(OH)]<sub>6</sub><sup>−</sup>. When a solution containing this anion is dehydrated, the precipitate contains mixed oxides.[^cite_note-norman-27]<sup>: 143 </sup>

The most important antimony ore is [stibnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stibnite) (Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>). Other [sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfide) minerals include [pyrargyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrargyrite) (Ag<sub>3</sub>SbS<sub>3</sub>), [zinkenite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinkenite), [jamesonite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamesonite), and [boulangerite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulangerite).[^cite_note-28] [Antimony pentasulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_pentasulfide) is [non-stoichiometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-stoichiometric_compound), which features antimony in the +3 [oxidation state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation_state) and S–S bonds.[^cite_note-29] Several thioantimonides are known, such as Sb<sub>6</sub>S<sub>10</sub><sup>2−</sup> and Sb<sub>8</sub>S<sub>13</sub><sup>2−</sup>.[^cite_note-30]

### Halides

Antimony forms two series of [halides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halide): SbX<sub>3</sub> and SbX<sub>5</sub>. The trihalides [SbF<sub>3</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trifluoride), [SbCl<sub>3</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trichloride), [SbBr<sub>3</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_tribromide), and [SbI<sub>3</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_triiodide) are all molecular compounds having [trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal_pyramidal_molecular_geometry). The trifluoride is prepared by the reaction of [antimony trioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trioxide) with [hydrofluoric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrofluoric_acid):[^cite_note-31]

Sb<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> + 6 HF → 2 SbF<sub>3</sub> + 3 H<sub>2</sub>O

It is [Lewis acidic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_acid) and readily accepts fluoride ions to form the complex anions SbF<sub>4</sub><sup>−</sup> and SbF<sub>5</sub><sup>2−</sup>. Molten antimony trifluoride is a weak [electrical conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_conductor). The trichloride is prepared by dissolving [stibnite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stibnite) in [hydrochloric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrochloric_acid):[^cite_note-Ullmann-32]

Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub> + 6 HCl → 2 SbCl<sub>3</sub> + 3 H<sub>2</sub>S

Arsenic sulfides are not readily attacked by the hydrochloric acid, so this method offers a route to As-free Sb.



Structure of gaseous SbF<sub>5</sub>

The pentahalides [SbF<sub>5</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_pentafluoride) and [SbCl<sub>5</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_pentachloride) have [trigonal bipyramidal molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal_bipyramidal_molecular_geometry) in the gas phase, but in the liquid phase, SbF<sub>5</sub> is [polymeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer), whereas SbCl<sub>5</sub> is monomeric.[^cite_note-33] Antimony pentafluoride is a powerful Lewis acid used to make the [superacid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superacid) [fluoroantimonic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluoroantimonic_acid) (H<sub>2</sub>F<sup>+</sup>·SbF<sub>6</sub><sup>−</sup>).

[Oxohalides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxohalide) are more common for antimony than for arsenic and phosphorus. [Antimony trioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trioxide) dissolves in concentrated acid to form oxoantimonyl compounds such as [SbOCl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_oxychloride) and (SbO)<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>.[^cite_note-34]

### Antimonides, hydrides, and organoantimony compounds

Compounds in this class generally are described as derivatives of Sb<sup>3−</sup>. Antimony forms [antimonides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimonide) with metals, such as [indium antimonide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indium_antimonide) (InSb) and silver antimonide (Ag<sub>3</sub>Sb).[^cite_note-35] The alkali metal and zinc antimonides, such as Na<sub>3</sub>Sb and Zn<sub>3</sub>Sb<sub>2</sub>, are more reactive. Treating these antimonides with acid produces the highly unstable gas [stibine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stibine), SbH<sub>3</sub>:[^cite_note-36]

Sb<sup>3−</sup> + 3 H<sup>+</sup> → SbH<sub>3</sub>

Stibine can also be produced by treating Sb<sup>3+</sup> salts with hydride reagents such as [sodium borohydride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_borohydride). Stibine decomposes spontaneously at room temperature. Because stibine has a positive [heat of formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_of_formation), it is [thermodynamically unstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_stability) and thus antimony does not react with [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) directly.[^cite_note-37]

[Organoantimony compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organoantimony_chemistry) are typically prepared by alkylation of antimony halides with [Grignard reagents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grignard_reagent).[^cite_note-38] A large variety of compounds are known with both Sb(III) and Sb(V) centers, including mixed chloro-organic derivatives, anions, and cations. Examples include [triphenylstibine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triphenylstibine) (Sb(C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)<sub>3</sub>) and [pentaphenylantimony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentaphenylantimony) (Sb(C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>)<sub>5</sub>).[^cite_note-39]

## History

An unshaded circle surmounted by a cross.

One of the [alchemical symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemical_symbol) for antimony

[Antimony(III) sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony\(III\)_sulfide), Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub>, was recognized in [predynastic Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predynastic_Egypt) as an eye cosmetic ([kohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohl_\(cosmetics\))) as early as about [3100 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_millennium_BC), when the [cosmetic palette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmetic_palette) was invented.[^cite_note-40]

An artifact, said to be part of a vase, made of antimony dating to about 3000 BC was found at [Telloh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girsu), [Chaldea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaldea) (part of present-day Iraq), and a copper object plated with antimony dating between 2500 BC and 2200 BC has been found in [Egypt](8087628.md).[^cite_note-kirk-18] Austen, at a lecture by [Herbert Gladstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Gladstone,_1st_Viscount_Gladstone) in 1892, commented that "we only know of antimony at the present day as a highly brittle and crystalline metal, which could hardly be fashioned into a useful vase, and therefore this remarkable 'find' (artifact mentioned above) must represent the lost art of rendering antimony malleable."[^cite_note-moorey-41]

The British archaeologist [Roger Moorey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Moorey) was unconvinced the artifact was indeed a vase, mentioning that Selimkhanov, after his analysis of the Tello object (published in 1975), "attempted to relate the metal to Transcaucasian natural antimony" (i.e. native metal) and that "the antimony objects from Transcaucasia are all small personal ornaments."[^cite_note-moorey-41] This weakens the evidence for a lost art "of rendering antimony malleable".[^cite_note-moorey-41]

The Roman scholar [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) described several ways of preparing antimony sulfide for medical purposes in his treatise [*Natural History*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_History_\(Pliny\)), around 77 AD.[^cite_note-mellor-42] Pliny the Elder also made a distinction between "male" and "female" forms of antimony; the male form is probably the sulfide, while the female form, which is superior, heavier, and less [friable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friability), has been suspected to be native metallic antimony.[^cite_note-43]

The Greek naturalist [Pedanius Dioscorides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedanius_Dioscorides) mentioned that antimony sulfide could be roasted by heating by a current of air. It is thought that this produced metallic antimony.[^cite_note-mellor-42]



The Italian metallurgist [Vannoccio Biringuccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vannoccio_Biringuccio) described a procedure to isolate antimony in 1540.

Antimony was frequently described in alchemical manuscripts, including the *Summa Perfectionis* of [Pseudo-Geber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-Geber), written around the 14th century.[^cite_note-44] A description of a procedure for isolating antimony is later given in the 1540 book *[De la pirotechnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_la_pirotechnia)* by [Vannoccio Biringuccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vannoccio_Biringuccio),[^cite_note-45] predating the more famous 1556 book by [Agricola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Agricola), *[De re metallica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_re_metallica)*. In this context Agricola has been often incorrectly credited with the discovery of metallic antimony. The book *Currus Triumphalis Antimonii* (The Triumphal Chariot of Antimony), describing the preparation of metallic antimony, was published in Germany in 1604. It was purported to be written by a [Benedictine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine) monk, writing under the name [Basilius Valentinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilius_Valentinus) in the 15th century; if it were authentic, which it is not, it would predate Biringuccio.[^cite_note-priesner-48][^cite_note-49]

The metal antimony was known to German chemist [Andreas Libavius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Libavius) in 1615 who obtained it by adding iron to a molten mixture of antimony sulfide, salt, and potassium [tartrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartrate). This procedure produced antimony with a crystalline or starred surface.[^cite_note-mellor-42]

With the advent of challenges to [phlogiston theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phlogiston_theory), it was recognized that antimony is an element forming sulfides, oxides, and other compounds, as do other metals.[^cite_note-mellor-42]

The first discovery of naturally occurring pure antimony in the [Earth's crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_crust) was described by the Swedish scientist and local mine district engineer [Anton von Swab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_von_Swab?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1783; the [type-sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_locality_\(geology\)) was collected from the [Sala Silver Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sala_Silver_Mine) in the Bergslagen mining district of [Sala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sala_Municipality,_Sweden), [Västmanland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4stmanland_County), Sweden.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

Coins of antimony were issued in China's [Guizhou](173815.md) in 1931; durability was poor, and minting was soon discontinued because of its softness and toxicity.[^cite_note-52]

### Etymology

The [medieval Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Latin) form, from which the modern languages and late [Byzantine Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Greek) take their names for antimony, is *_antimonium_*.[^cite_note-53] The origin of that is uncertain, and all suggestions have some difficulty either of form or interpretation. The [popular etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_etymology), from ἀντίμοναχός *anti-monachos* or French *antimoine*, would mean "monk-killer", which is explained by the fact that many early [alchemists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemist) were monks, and some antimony compounds they used were poisonous.[^cite_note-54]

Another popular etymology is the hypothetical Greek word ἀντίμόνος *antimonos*, "against aloneness", explained as "not found as metal", or "not found unalloyed".[^cite_note-kirk-18] However, ancient Greek would more naturally express the pure negative as *α-* ("not").[^cite_note-55] [Edmund Oscar von Lippmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Oscar_von_Lippmann) conjectured a hypothetical Greek word ανθήμόνιον *anthemonion*, which would mean "floret", and cites several examples of related Greek words which describe chemical or biological [efflorescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efflorescence).[^cite_note-Lippmann-56]

The early uses of *antimonium* include the translations, in 1050–1100, by [Constantine the African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_African) of Arabic medical treatises.[^cite_note-Lippmann-56] Several authorities believe *antimonium* is a scribal corruption of some Arabic form; Meyerhof derives it from *ithmid*;[^cite_note-57] other possibilities include *athimar*, the Arabic name of the metalloid, and a hypothetical *as-stimmi*, derived from or parallel to the Greek.[^cite_note-e28-58]<sup>: 28 </sup>

The standard chemical symbol for antimony (Sb) is credited to [Jöns Jakob Berzelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B6ns_Jakob_Berzelius), who derived the abbreviation from *stibium*.[^cite_note-59]

The ancient words for antimony mostly have, as their chief meaning, *kohl*, the sulfide of antimony.[^cite_note-60]

The Egyptians called antimony *mśdmt*[^cite_note-61]<sup>: 230 </sup>[^cite_note-62]<sup>: 541 </sup> or *stm*.[^cite_note-etym-63]

The Arabic word for the substance, as opposed to the cosmetic, can appear as إثمد *ithmid, athmoud, othmod*, or *uthmod*. [Littré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littr%C3%A9) suggests the first form, which is the earliest, derives from *stimmida*, an accusative for *stimmi*.[^cite_note-e28-58][^cite_note-64] The Greek word στίμμι (stimmi) is used by [Attic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attica) [tragic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragedy) poets of the 5th century BC, and is possibly a loan word from Arabic or from Egyptian *stm*.[^cite_note-etym-63]

## Production

### Process

The extraction of antimony from ores depends on the quality and composition of the ore. Most antimony is mined as the sulfide; lower-grade ores are concentrated by [froth flotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Froth_flotation), while higher-grade ores are heated to 500–600 °C, the temperature at which stibnite melts and separates from the [gangue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangue) minerals. Antimony can be isolated from the crude antimony sulfide by reduction with scrap iron:[^cite_note-usgs2-65]

Sb<sub>2</sub>S<sub>3</sub> + 3 Fe → 2 Sb + 3 FeS

The sulfide is converted to an oxide by roasting. The product is further purified by vaporizing the volatile antimony(III) oxide, which is recovered.[^cite_note-Ullmann-32] This sublimate is often used directly for the main applications, impurities being arsenic and sulfide.[^cite_note-Norm-66][^cite_note-67] Antimony is isolated from the oxide by a carbothermal reduction:[^cite_note-usgs2-65][^cite_note-Norm-66]

2 Sb<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> + 3 C → 4 Sb + 3 CO<sub>2</sub>

The lower-grade ores are reduced in [blast furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_furnace) while the higher-grade ores are reduced in [reverberatory furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverberatory_furnace).[^cite_note-usgs2-65]

## Applications

In 2017, approximately 48% of antimony was consumed in [flame retardants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flame_retardant), 33% in [lead–acid batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead%E2%80%93acid_battery), and 8% in plastics.[^cite_note-usgs2-65]

### Flame retardants

Antimony is mainly used as the [trioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trioxide) for [flame-proofing compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flame_retardant), always in combination with halogenated flame retardants except in halogen-containing polymers. The flame retarding effect of antimony trioxide is produced by the formation of halogenated antimony compounds,[^cite_note-82] which react with hydrogen atoms, and probably also with oxygen atoms and OH radicals, thus inhibiting fire.[^cite_note-83] Markets for these flame-retardants include children's clothing, toys, aircraft, and automobile seat covers. They are also added to [polyester resins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyester_resin) in [fiberglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass-reinforced_plastic) [composites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_material) for such items as light aircraft engine covers. The resin will burn in the presence of an externally generated flame, but will extinguish when the external flame is removed.[^cite_note-Ullmann-32][^cite_note-84] Antimony trioxide is also used as a synergist with brominated flame retardants in housings and plastic parts for electrical and electronic equipment (e.g., HIPS/ABS enclosures) to meet flammability standards such as UL 94.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

### Alloys

Antimony forms a highly useful [alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy) with lead, increasing its hardness and mechanical strength. When casting it increases fluidity of the melt and reduces shrinkage during cooling.[^cite_note-88] For most applications involving lead, varying amounts of antimony are used as alloying metal. In [lead–acid batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead%E2%80%93acid_batteries), this addition improves plate strength and charging characteristics.[^cite_note-Ullmann-32][^cite_note-89] For sailboats, lead keels are used to provide righting moment, ranging from 600 lbs to over 200 tons for the largest sailing superyachts; to improve hardness and tensile strength of the lead keel, antimony is mixed with lead between 2% and 5% by volume. Antimony is used in antifriction alloys (such as [Babbitt metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babbitt_metal)),[^cite_note-90] in bullets and [lead shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_shot), [electrical cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_cable) sheathing, [type metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_metal) (for example, for [Linotype machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linotype_machine)[^cite_note-91]), [solder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solder) (some "[lead-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoHS)" solders contain 5% Sb),[^cite_note-92] in [pewter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pewter),[^cite_note-93] and in hardening alloys with low [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) content in the manufacturing of [organ pipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_pipe).

### Other applications



InSb infrared detector manufactured by [Mullard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullard) in the 1960s

Three other applications consume nearly all the rest of the world's supply.[^cite_note-usgs2-65] One application is as a stabilizer and catalyst for the production of [polyethylene terephthalate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyethylene_terephthalate).[^cite_note-usgs2-65] Another is as a fining agent to remove microscopic bubbles in glass, mostly for TV screens;[^cite_note-94] antimony ions interact with oxygen, suppressing the tendency of the latter to form bubbles.[^cite_note-95] This also prevents discolouration. The third application is pigments.[^cite_note-usgs2-65] Antimony also helps to maintain color stability and surface smoothness when it is used with certain types of ceramics and enamels.

In the 1990s antimony was increasingly being used in [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) as a [dopant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopant) in [n-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-type_semiconductor) [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon) [wafers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wafer_\(electronics\))[^cite_note-96] for [diodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diode), [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared) detectors, and [Hall-effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_effect) devices. In the 1950s, the emitters and collectors of n–p–n [alloy-junction transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy-junction_transistor) were doped with tiny beads of a lead-antimony alloy.[^cite_note-97] [Indium antimonide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indium_antimonide) (InSb) is used as a material for mid-[infrared detectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_detector).[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

The material [Ge<sub>2</sub>Sb<sub>2</sub>Te<sub>5</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeSbTe) is used for [phase-change memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase-change_memory), a type of [computer memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_memory).

Biology and medicine have few uses for antimony. Treatments containing antimony, known as [antimonials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimonial), are used as [emetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emetic).[^cite_note-101] Antimony compounds are used as [antiprotozoan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiprotozoal_agent) drugs. [Potassium antimonyl tartrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_antimonyl_tartrate), or tartar emetic, was once used as an anti-[schistosomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schistosomiasis) drug from 1919 on. It was subsequently replaced by [praziquantel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praziquantel).[^cite_note-102] Antimony and its compounds are used in several [veterinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterinary) preparations, such as [anthiomaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthiomaline?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [lithium antimony thiomalate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_antimony_thiomalate?action=edit\&redlink=1), as a skin conditioner for [ruminants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruminant).[^cite_note-103] Antimony has a nourishing or conditioning effect on [keratinized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keratinized) tissues in animals.

Antimony-based drugs, such as [meglumine antimoniate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meglumine_antimoniate), are also considered the drugs of choice for treatment of [leishmaniasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leishmaniasis). Early treatments used antimony(III) species ([trivalent antimonials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivalent_antimonial?action=edit\&redlink=1)), but in 1922 [Upendranath Brahmachari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upendranath_Brahmachari) invented a much safer antimony(V) drug and, since then, so-called [pentavalent antimonials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentavalent_antimonial) have been the standard first-line treatment. However, *[Leishmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leishmania)* strains in [Bihar](442482.md) and neighboring regions have developed resistance to antimony.[^cite_note-104] Elemental antimony in the form of an [antimony pill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_pill) was once used as a medicine. It could be reused by others after ingestion and elimination.[^cite_note-105]

[Antimony(III) sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony\(III\)_sulfide) is used in the heads of some [safety matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_match).[^cite_note-Trends-106][^cite_note-107] Antimony sulfides help to stabilize the friction coefficient in automotive brake pad materials.[^cite_note-108] Antimony is used in bullets, bullet tracers,[^cite_note-109] paint, glass art, and as an [opacifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opacifier) in [enamel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitreous_enamel).

The powder derived from crushed antimony sulfide (*[kohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohl_\(cosmetics\))*) has been used for millennia as an eye cosmetic. Historically it was applied to the eyes with a metal rod and with one's [spittle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spittle), and was thought by the ancients to aid in curing eye infections.[^cite_note-110] The practice is still seen in [Yemen](350939.md) and in other Muslim countries.[^cite_note-111]

[Antimony-124](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony-124) is used together with [beryllium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryllium) in [neutron sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_source); the [gamma rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_ray) emitted by antimony-124 initiate the [photodisintegration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photodisintegration) of beryllium.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] The emitted neutrons have an average energy of 24 keV.[^cite_note-114] Natural antimony is used in [startup neutron sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_neutron_source).

Antimony and many of its compounds are [toxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxic), and the effects of antimony poisoning are similar to [arsenic poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic_poisoning). The toxicity of antimony is far lower than that of arsenic; this might be caused by the significant differences of uptake, metabolism, and excretion between arsenic and antimony. The uptake of antimony(III) or antimony(V) in the gastrointestinal tract is at most 20%. Antimony(V) is not quantitatively reduced to antimony(III) in the cell (in fact antimony(III) is oxidised to antimony(V) instead).[^cite_note-116]

Since [biomethylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomethylation) of antimony does not occur, the excretion of antimony(V) in urine is the main way of elimination.[^cite_note-117] Like arsenic, the most serious effect of acute antimony poisoning is [cardiotoxicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiotoxicity) and the resulting [myocarditis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocarditis); however, it can also manifest as [Adams–Stokes syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams%E2%80%93Stokes_syndrome), which arsenic does not. Reported cases of intoxication by antimony equivalent to 90 mg [antimony potassium tartrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_potassium_tartrate) dissolved from enamel has been reported to show only short-term effects. An intoxication with 6 g of antimony potassium tartrate was reported to result in death after three days.[^cite_note-118]

Inhalation of antimony dust is harmful and in certain cases may be fatal; in small doses, antimony causes headaches, [dizziness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizziness), and depression. Larger doses such as prolonged skin contact may cause dermatitis, or damage the kidneys and the liver, causing violent and frequent vomiting, leading to death in a few days.[^cite_note-Sundar2010-119]

Antimony is incompatible with strong [oxidizing agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidizing_agent), [strong acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_acid), [halogen acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrohalic_acid), [chlorine](5667.md), or [fluorine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorine). It should be kept away from heat.[^cite_note-120]

Antimony [leaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaching_\(chemical_science\)) from [polyethylene terephthalate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyethylene_terephthalate) (PET) bottles into liquids.[^cite_note-121] While levels observed for [bottled water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottled_water) are below [drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_water) guidelines,[^cite_note-shotyk-122] [fruit juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_juice) concentrates (for which no guidelines are established) produced in the UK were found to contain up to 44.7 μg/L of antimony, well above the EU limits for [tap water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tap_water) of 5 μg/L.[^cite_note-123] The guidelines are:

- [World Health Organization](33583.md): 20 μg/L

  [^cite_note-who-124]

- Japan: 15 μg/L

  [^cite_note-125]

- [United States Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md), [Health Canada](216516.md), and the [Ontario Ministry of Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Ministry_of_Environment): 6 μg/L

  [^cite_note-canada-126]

- EU and German Federal Ministry of Environment: 5 μg/L

  [^cite_note-shotyk-122]

The [tolerable daily intake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolerable_daily_intake) (TDI) proposed by WHO is 6 μg antimony per kilogram of body weight.[^cite_note-who-124] The [immediately dangerous to life or health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immediately_dangerous_to_life_or_health) (IDLH) value for antimony is 50 mg/m<sup>3</sup> (50 μg/L).[^cite_note-127]

### Toxicity

Certain compounds of antimony appear to be toxic, particularly antimony trioxide and antimony potassium tartrate.[^cite_note-atsdr.cdc.gov-128] Effects may be similar to [arsenic poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic_poisoning).[^cite_note-129] Occupational exposure may cause respiratory irritation, [pneumoconiosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumoconiosis), antimony spots on the skin, gastrointestinal symptoms, and cardiac arrhythmias. Antimony trioxide is potentially carcinogenic to humans.[^cite_note-Sundar2010-119]

Adverse health effects have been observed in humans and animals following inhalation, oral, or dermal exposure to antimony and antimony compounds.[^cite_note-atsdr.cdc.gov-128] Antimony toxicity typically occurs either due to occupational exposure, during therapy, or from accidental ingestion. It is unclear if antimony can enter the body through the skin.[^cite_note-atsdr.cdc.gov-128] The presence of low levels of antimony in saliva may also be associated with [dental decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dental_decay).[^cite_note-Davis_et_al_Sci_Rep-130]
