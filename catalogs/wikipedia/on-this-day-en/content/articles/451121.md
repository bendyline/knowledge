**AFC Bournemouth** ([/ˈbɔːrnməθ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d1/En-uk-Bournemouth.ogg/En-uk-Bournemouth.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d1/En-uk-Bournemouth.ogg/En-uk-Bournemouth.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*BORN-məth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a professional [football](10568.md) club based in [Kings Park, Boscombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park,_Boscombe), a suburb of [Bournemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth), [Dorset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset), England. The club competes in the [Premier League](11250.md), the top tier of [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system). Formed in 1899 as **Boscombe**, the club adopted their current name in 1971. Nicknamed "The Cherries", and commonly referred to as **Bournemouth**, they have played their home games at [Dean Court](1821645.md) since 1910.&#x20;

The club competed in regional football leagues before going up from the [Hampshire League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_League) to the [Southern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League) in 1920. By then, known as **Bournemouth & Boscombe Athletic**, they were elected into the [Football League](14419588.md) in 1923. They remained in the [Third Division South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_South) for 35 years, winning the [Third Division South Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_South_Cup) in 1946. Placed in the newly reorganised [Third Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division) in 1958, they suffered relegation in 1970, but would win an immediate promotion in 1970–71. Relegated back into the [Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Fourth_Division) in 1975, Bournemouth were promoted again in 1981–82 and after lifting the [Associate Members' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Trophy) in [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Associate_Members'_Cup_Final) would go on to win the Third Division title in 1986–87. They spent three seasons in the second tier but entered [administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administration_\(British_football\)) in 1997 and ended up back in the fourth tier with relegation in 2002, though immediately gained promotion by winning the play-offs in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Football_League_Third_Division_play-off_final).

Bournemouth entered administration for a second time and were relegated back into [League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_Two) in 2008, but ended the year by appointing [Eddie Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Howe) as manager. Under Howe's stewardship, Bournemouth won three promotions in six years to win a place in the first tier of English football for the first time in their history. This was achieved with a second-place finish in League Two in 2009–10, a second-place finish in [League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_One) in 2012–13 and a [Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) title in 2014–15. The club remained in the [Premier League](11250.md) for five seasons before suffering relegation in 2020, but they returned in 2022 as Championship runners-up, this time under the management of [Scott Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Parker). Following their return to the top flight, the club achieved their highest league finish to date, by finishing 6th in 2025–26, thus earning European football and qualifying for the [UEFA Europa League](232175.md) for the first time in their history, under the management of [Andoni Iraola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andoni_Iraola).

## History

### Boscombe

Although the exact date of the club's foundation is not known, there is proof that it was formed in the autumn of 1899 out of the remains of the older Boscombe St. John's Institute Football Club.[^cite_note-12] The club was originally known as Boscombe Football Club. The first president was Mr. J. C. Nutt.[^cite_note-13]

In their first season, 1899–1900, Boscombe competed in the Bournemouth and District Junior League. They also played in the Hants Junior Cup. During the first two seasons, they played on a football pitch in Castlemain Avenue, Pokesdown. From their third season, the team played on a pitch in [King's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Park,_Boscombe). In the 1905–06 season, Boscombe graduated to senior amateur football.[^cite_note-14]

In 1910, the club was granted a long lease over some wasteland next to Kings Park as the club's football ground by local businessman J.E. Cooper-Dean. With their own ground, named Dean Court after the benefactor, the club continued to thrive, and dominated the local football scene. The same year the club signed its first professional player [Baven Penton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baven_Penton).[^cite_note-15]

Around about this time, the club obtained their nickname "The Cherries". There are two leading explanations of how the club gained the nickname: from the cherry-red striped shirts that the team played in, and, perhaps less plausible, because Dean Court was built adjacent to the Cooper-Dean estate, which, it is believed, may have contained many cherry trees.[^cite_note-16]

For the first time, during the 1913–14 season, the club competed in the [FA Cup](11237.md). The club's progress, however, was halted in 1914 with the outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md), and Boscombe returned to the [Hampshire League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_League).

In 1920, the Third Division of the [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League) was formed, and Boscombe were promoted to the [Southern League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Football_League), finding moderate success.



[Dean Court](1821645.md)

### Bournemouth & Boscombe Athletic

To make the club more representative of the district, the name was changed to Bournemouth & Boscombe Athletic Football Club in 1923. During the same year, the club was elected to the newly expanded [Third Division South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Third_Division_South). The first league match was at [Swindon Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swindon_Town_F.C.) on 25 August 1923, which Bournemouth lost 3–1. The first league game at Dean Court was also against Swindon, where Bournemouth gained their first league point after a 0–0 draw.

Bournemouth struggled in the Football League but eventually established themselves as a Third Division club. Bournemouth remains on the records as the longest continuous members of the Third Division.[^cite_note-17]

As a league club, Bournemouth had to wait until after the Second World War before winning their first trophy. This was accomplished as they beat [Walsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsall_F.C.) in the Third Division (South) Cup in the final at [Stamford Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_\(stadium\)).

### AFC Bournemouth

The club adopted the name *AFC Bournemouth* for the start of the 1971–72 season,[^cite_note-FCHD-18] with the intention that the club would appear first in alphabetical lists of English clubs whilst also keeping the initialism of AFC (Athletic Football Club) as part of their name.[^cite_note-19] A year later, the club adopted a new badge as a symbol of its progress.[^cite_note-20] The stripes in the background were based on the club shirt, while in the foreground is the profile of a player heading the ball, in honour of [Dickie Dowsett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickie_Dowsett), a prolific scorer for the club in the 1950s and 1960s.[^cite_note-21]

The team's red-and-black kit, introduced in 1971, was based on the [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan) strip.[^cite_note-22] This was the era of [Ted MacDougall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_MacDougall), a prolific goalscorer who, in an FA Cup tie in November 1971, scored nine goals in an 11–0 win against [Margate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margate_F.C.).[^cite_note-23]

### Late 20th century



Chart of yearly table positions of Bournemouth in the League.

Bournemouth recorded a famous victory over holders [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) in the [FA Cup](11237.md) in January 1984, while they were managed by [Harry Redknapp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Redknapp).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The club won its second piece of silverware by winning the [Associate Members' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Members'_Cup) in its [inaugural season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_Associate_Members'_Cup), beating [Hull City](451358.md) 2–1 at [Boothferry Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boothferry_Park) on 24 May 1984 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Associate_Members'_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-26]

Redknapp took Bournemouth into the second tier of the English league for the first time in their history as Third Division champions in 1987. After comfortably surviving in their first season in the Second Division, Bournemouth made a serious challenge for promotion to the top flight in the [1988–89 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_in_English_football); they ultimately fell away after a poor run late in the season, but their eventual finish of 12th place remained their highest-ever in the Football League until the [2013–14 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_AFC_Bournemouth_season).

On 5 May 1990, the final day of the [1989–90 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_in_English_football), [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.) had the chance to win the Second Division and gain promotion into the First Division by beating Bournemouth at Dean Court. Some United fans had already caused trouble in the town during the morning and the atmosphere was tense as Leeds won the match by a single goal. Combined with the results of other matches, this meant that Leeds were promoted while Bournemouth were relegated. The violence and destruction by visitors to Bournemouth continued over the holiday weekend, causing more than £1 million worth of damage and injury to opposing fans and police officers.[^cite_note-archive.bournemouthecho.co.uk-27] The town's *[Daily Echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth_Daily_Echo)* newspaper reported that "spectators, including many young children, had to run to safety as missiles were hurled and riot police waded in to control the crowds."[^cite_note-archive.bournemouthecho.co.uk-27] The matter was raised in Parliament by one of the town's MPs. Financially, the Leeds trouble affected the club for more than a decade, as Bournemouth were prevented by local police from staging home games on [Bank Holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_holiday) (traditionally a popular day for football) until a game against [Shrewsbury Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury_Town_F.C.) on 21 April 2003.

Redknapp remained at the club for two more seasons, both of which ended with the club falling three points short of the play-offs. However, mounting financial pressures caused him to resign his position at the end of the [1991–92 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_in_English_football), and he subsequently re-joined former club [West Ham United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.) as a coach. He was replaced by [Tony Pulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Pulis), who built a much cheaper squad that could only manage two consecutive 17th-place finishes before Pulis walked out of the club, blaming financial pressures.

Bournemouth went the first few months of the [1994–95 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_in_English_football) without a permanent manager in place, and a dreadful start saw them bottom of the table for much of the first half of the season. Despite a minor upturn in form when [Mel Machin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Machin) was appointed as manager, they looked highly unlikely to survive, given that there were five relegation spots in Division Two for that season due to league reconstruction. However, a late run of form combined with collapses by relegation rivals [Cambridge United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_United_F.C.) and [Plymouth Argyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Argyle_F.C.) saw them survive on the last day of the season by two points.

Machin ultimately remained in charge for six years, most of which were marked by unremarkable mid-table finishes. The [1998–99 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_in_English_football) proved to be arguably the highlight of his tenure, with the club making a serious playoff challenge for most of the season, but ultimately falling short and finishing seventh. However, a drop to 16th place in the [1999–2000 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_AFC_Bournemouth_season) followed by a poor start to the following season saw Machin removed from his position and given the role of director of football.

### Early 21st century

[Sean O'Driscoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_O'Driscoll) was promoted from the coaching staff in place of Mel Machin at the start of the [2000–01 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_AFC_Bournemouth_season). In O'Driscoll's first season as manager, Bournemouth narrowly missed out on the Division Two playoffs but were relegated a year later in the new stadium (in the early part of the 2001–02 season, they played their home matches at [Dorchester Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorchester_Town_F.C.)'s ground while their own stadium was being redeveloped). The board kept faith in O'Driscoll and they were rewarded with promotion via the Division Three playoffs in [2002–03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_in_English_football). The club became the first to score five goals at the [Millennium Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Stadium) when they beat [Lincoln City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_City_F.C.) 5–2 in the [2002–03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_in_English_football) Division Three play-off final with goals from [Steve Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Fletcher), [Carl Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Fletcher_\(Welsh_footballer\)) (2), [Stephen Purches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Purches) and [Garreth O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garreth_O'Connor). Under O'Driscoll, Bournemouth narrowly missed out on the play-offs for the [2003–04](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_in_English_football) and [2004–05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_in_English_football) seasons, and just avoided relegation in the [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_in_English_football) season.

Long-serving player [James Hayter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hayter_\(footballer\)) scored the fastest league hat-trick in English Football League history during the [2003–04](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_in_English_football) season. The Cherries were leading 3–0 against [Wrexham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrexham_F.C.) thanks to goals from Stephen Purches, [Warren Cummings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Cummings) and [Warren Feeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Feeney) when Hayter was brought onto the field as a substitute. With 86 minutes gone, Hayter managed to net three goals in the space of two minutes and 21 seconds, making the final score 6–0 to Bournemouth.[^cite_note-28]

In September 2006, with the team in eighth in the League, Sean O'Driscoll left to become manager of [Doncaster Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doncaster_Rovers_F.C.). He was replaced by [Kevin Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Bond_\(English_footballer\)).[^cite_note-29]

### Decline and administration (2008–2009)

In February 2008, Bournemouth were forced into administration, suffering a ten-point deduction which put them in relegation trouble. Bournemouth had debts of around £4 million and almost went out of business.[^cite_note-30] The off-field uncertainty continued throughout the season, with only one, ultimately unsuccessful, bid for the club accepted,[^cite_note-31] and the club ended the season being relegated to [League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_Two).[^cite_note-FCHD3-32]

Ahead of the [2008–09 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_in_English_football), the team's future in the Football League was put into doubt when the league threatened to block Bournemouth's participation in League Two, due to problems with the club's continuing administration and change in ownership. The league ordered both Bournemouth and [Rotherham United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotherham_United_F.C.) to demonstrate that they could fulfil all of their fixtures and find a way out of administration,[^cite_note-33] eventually allowing the club to compete with a 17-point penalty for failing to follow the Football League insolvency rules. The new company was also ordered to pay unsecured creditors the amount offered at the time of the original [CVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_while_insolvent#Company_voluntary_agreement) (around ten pence in the pound) within two years.[^cite_note-34]

Early into the season, manager Bond was sacked and was replaced by former player [Jimmy Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Quinn_\(Northern_Irish_footballer\)), who would himself leave the club only a few months later.[^cite_note-35] Former player [Eddie Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Howe) took over as manager with the club still ten points adrift at the bottom of the league and initially on a caretaker basis, becoming the youngest manager in the Football League at the age of 31.[^cite_note-36]

At the end of 2008, it was announced that local businessman Adam Murray had completed the purchase of 50% of the club's shares from previous chairman, Paul Baker. However, in January 2009, Murray missed the deadline to buy Baker's shares.[^cite_note-37]

In the final home game of the 2008–09 season, the Cherries guaranteed their Football League status by beating [Grimsby Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimsby_Town_F.C.) 2–1 with a winning goal ten minutes from time by Bournemouth's Steve Fletcher, sparking celebrations after an ending to "The Great Escape." They finished their season with a 4–0 victory at [Morecambe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morecambe_F.C.).

In June 2009, a consortium including Adam Murray finally took over Bournemouth. The consortium included Jeff Mostyn, former vice-chairman Steve Sly, Neill Blake and former [Dorchester Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorchester_Town) chairman [Eddie Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Mitchell).

### Rise to the Premier League (2009–2015)

Howe's first full season in charge brought success as Bournemouth finished second in League Two to earn promotion with two games to spare. Howe left the club for [Burnley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnley_F.C.) during the following season; his successor, another former Bournemouth player, [Lee Bradbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Bradbury), led Bournemouth to the League One play-offs. The two-legged semi-final against [Huddersfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_Town_F.C.) finished 3–3 after extra time, and Huddersfield went through the final by winning the penalty shoot-out 4–2. Bradbury was unable to lead Bournemouth to another promotion challenge in the [2011–12 Football League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Football_League_One), placing 11th after a season of indifferent results, and was replaced by youth team coach [Paul Groves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Groves_\(footballer\)) for the final games of the season.

Groves remained in charge at the start of the [2012–13 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_AFC_Bournemouth_season), only to be sacked in October 2012 following a start which left the club near the bottom of the table. Eddie Howe returned as manager, and not only did he pull the club away from their early-season relegation battle, they achieved promotion to the [Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship), returning to the second-tier of English football for the first time since 1990. The club also revealed a new club crest.[^cite_note-38] After a promising start to life in the Championship, the club was handed a fourth Round [FA Cup](11237.md) tie with [Premier League](11250.md) club [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) which ended in a 2–0 loss. Bournemouth finished their first season back in the Championship in tenth place, their highest ever position in the Football League.

On 25 October 2014, Bournemouth won 8–0 away at [St. Andrew's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrew's_\(stadium\)) against [Birmingham City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_F.C.). It was the first time that the Cherries had ever scored eight goals in a league game and their largest winning margin in the league (not counting a 10–0 win over [Northampton Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton_Town_F.C.) in September 1939, which was discounted after the league was abandoned due to the Second World War).[^cite_note-bbc_2014-10-25-39] The club followed up this success with a 2–1 victory over [Premier League](11250.md) side [West Bromwich Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C.) in the League Cup, reaching the quarter-finals of the competition for the first time. Bournemouth were again drawn against Liverpool but lost 3–1. The club spent most of the [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_AFC_Bournemouth_season) near the top of the table, and a 3–0 win away at [Charlton Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlton_Athletic_F.C.) on the final day of the season was enough to clinch the Championship title and a first-ever promotion to the top flight of English football.[^cite_note-40]

### First Premier League era (2015–2020)

In Bournemouth's [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_AFC_Bournemouth_season) in the Premier League, the team was beset by a number of crippling injuries, including to [Callum Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callum_Wilson_\(English_footballer\)), star striker from the previous season. The team struggled for most of the first half of the season but an upturn in form during the second half of the season saw a reversal of fortunes. Bournemouth eventually finished 16th in the league, avoiding relegation.[^cite_note-41]

The club was widely tipped to suffer [second season syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_season_syndrome), but the [2016–17 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_A.F.C._Bournemouth_season) was largely successful. Despite a weak start, which saw them in the relegation zone for the first three weeks, the team quickly recovered and went on to finish 9th.[^cite_note-42] Star loan player [Nathan Aké](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Ak%C3%A9) was signed permanently from Chelsea for a club-record fee in June 2017, reportedly in the region of £20 million.[^cite_note-43] Despite another slow start in [2017–18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_A.F.C._Bournemouth_season), a run of good form through late December and January saw them steer clear of the relegation zone, and earn Howe his second Premier League Manager of the Month award.[^cite_note-44] Bournemouth went on to gain 19 points from losing positions in the second half of the season – a Premier League record – helping the team finish in 12th place.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

The [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_AFC_Bournemouth_season) saw the club break their transfer record again on [Jefferson Lerma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Lerma) during the summer,[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] and contrasting with the previous season, the club had a strong start, sitting in 6th place after the first 12 games. However, their form regressed for the remainder of the season due to many injury problems. In the end, Bournemouth finished in 14th place, securing a 5th season in the Premier League.

A bright start to the [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_AFC_Bournemouth_season) saw the team sitting in 7th place at the beginning of November.[^cite_note-49] However, continuing injury problems and a poor run of results followed, and the club dropped into the relegation zone in January. Poor performances continued after the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_England) had interrupted the season, with key losses to [Manchester City](165813.md) and [Southampton](81576.md) putting the club on the brink.[^cite_note-50] Despite a 3–1 victory over [Everton](91155.md) away at [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park) on the final day, the club's relegation was confirmed because Aston Villa drew against West Ham.[^cite_note-51] On 1 August 2020, Howe left the club by mutual consent, ending his 8-year second spell as manager.[^cite_note-52]

#### Financial Fair Play violation and punishment

In 2016, Bournemouth were found guilty of violating the Football League's Financial Fair Play regulations during 2014–15, the season it secured promotion to the Premier League. The club's over-spend broke the 'maximum deviation', with a £38.3 million financial loss in 2014–15. This followed a loss of £10.3 million in 2013–2014. The club was originally fined £7.6 million by the Football League, but subsequently negotiated a settlement with a fine of £4.75 million for breaching Financial Fair Play rules.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

### Back to EFL Championship (2020–2022)

On 8 August, [Jason Tindall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Tindall), a former Bournemouth player and Howe's longtime assistant, was promoted to manager.[^cite_note-56] Despite sitting second in mid-December, Tindall was sacked on 3 February 2021 after a run with one win in eight games, which saw the team fall to 6th in the table.[^cite_note-57] He was replaced by first team coach [Jonathan Woodgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Woodgate), initially as caretaker.[^cite_note-58] Woodgate remained as manager for the remainder of the season, as the club finished in 6th and entered the playoffs, but lost 3–2 to Brentford on aggregate in the semi-final.[^cite_note-59] Woodgate's contract was not renewed after the season and on 28 June 2021, former [Fulham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulham_F.C.) manager [Scott Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Parker) was appointed as the new head coach, with [Matt Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Wells_\(football_coach\)) as the assistant coach.[^cite_note-60] Parker led the club on an impressive 15-game unbeaten start to the [2021–22 Championship season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_EFL_Championship), going on to clinch promotion in the penultimate match of the season, a 1–0 victory against promotion rivals [Nottingham Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Forest_F.C.), amassing 88 points.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

### Second Premier League era, new ownership and European football (2022–present)

The club's return to the Premier League got off to a tough start. A breakdown in the club's relationship with Parker and a Premier League record-equalling [9–0 defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C._9%E2%80%930_AFC_Bournemouth) to [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) at [Anfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anfield) resulted in Parker's dismissal after just four league games on 30 August 2022.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] First team coach [Gary O'Neil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_O'Neil) was made interim head coach and presided over an upturn in form in his 12 games in interim charge before being appointed as the permanent head coach on 27 November 2022.[^cite_note-66]

On 13 December 2022, the club was purchased from previous owner [Maxim Demin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Demin) by Black Knight Football Club, a partnership led by American businessman [Bill Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Foley) and Cannae Holdings.[^cite_note-67] The minority ownership group is led by Hollywood actor [Michael B. Jordan](2310146.md) and Kosmos founder Nullah Sarker.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] On 19 June 2023, the club announced the departure of manager O'Neil, with [Andoni Iraola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andoni_Iraola) coming in to replace him.[^cite_note-70] In the [2023–24 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Premier_League), Bournemouth, led by Iraola, achieved their best points tally in the Premier League, accumulating 48 points to finish 12th.[^cite_note-71] The following season, in the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Premier_League), Bournemouth once again broke their record Premier League points total, achieving 56 points, whilst equalling their best ever league finish of 9th in the Premier League. As well as this, the club achieved their most wins in a Premier League season (15), fewest defeats (12), most goals scored (58), and fewest goals conceded (46).[^cite_note-72]

On 19 May 2026, Bournemouth qualified for [European football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competition_records_and_statistics) for the first time in their history, after their 1–1 draw with [Manchester City](165813.md).[^cite_note-73] On 24 May 2026, they drew 1–1 with [Nottingham Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Forest_F.C.) to confirm sixth place, the highest league finish in the club's history, which, after Manchester City lost to [Aston Villa](42173.md) preventing the EPS from dropping down, meant Bournemouth qualified for the [2026–27 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_UEFA_Europa_League). With 57 points, the club also broke their Premier League points total for the third season running, whilst also only losing seven games, their fewest defeats in a Premier League season.[^cite_note-74] This game was also the club's eighteenth draw of the season, breaking the record for the [most draws](33287218.md#Draws) in a thirty-eight game Premier League season, and extended the club's record unbeaten run to eighteen games, having not lost in the league since a 3–2 loss at home to [Arsenal](2174.md) on 3 January 2026.[^cite_note-75]

## Colours

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [AFC Bournemouth kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/AFC%20Bournemouth%20kits).

The team's colours have varied slightly throughout the club's history. Starting off playing in red-and-white stripes, Bournemouth have also played in all-red shirts, red with white sleeves, and mostly, since 1990, in red-and-black stripes.[^cite_note-81] A predominantly red shirt was chosen for the [2004–05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_in_English_football) and [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_in_English_football) seasons, but – due to fan demand – a return was made to the stripes for the [2006–07](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_in_English_football) season.[^cite_note-82]

Since 2026 the team's kit has been manufactured by [Hummel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hummel_International). Previously it has been made by [Umbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbro) (1974–78, 1983–86, 2017–26), [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) (1978–81), Osca (1982–83), Henson (1986–87), Scoreline (1987–90), Ellgren (1990–92), Matchwinner (1993–95), [Le Coq Sportif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Coq_Sportif) (1995–96), [Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_\(sportswear_company\)) (1996–2000), Super League (2000–01), TFG Sportswear (2001–03), Bourne Red (2003–08), [Carbrini Sportswear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbrini_Sportswear) (2008–11, 2014–15), [Fila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fila) (2011–14) and [JD Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Sports) (2015–17).

## Rivalries

According to a poll named 'The League of Love and Hate' in August 2019, Bournemouth supporters named their club's near neighbour [Southampton](81576.md) as its biggest rival, with [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C.), [Brighton and Hove Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_and_Hove_Albion_F.C.), [Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_F.C.) and [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.) following.[^cite_note-87]

The most intense rivalry is with Leeds United, due to an incident on 5 May 1990, where Leeds fans caused widespread disorder and damage in Bournemouth over the May Day bank holiday weekend.

## Records and statistics

For a more detailed list, see [List of AFC Bournemouth records and statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AFC_Bournemouth_records_and_statistics).

[Steve Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Fletcher) holds the record for Bournemouth appearances, having played 726 first-team matches between 1992 and 2013.[^cite_note-88] Fletcher also holds the record for most league appearances, making 628.[^cite_note-89] Ron Eyre holds the record for the most goals 229 in a Bournemouth shirt having played 337 first-team matches between 1924 and 1933.[^cite_note-90] [Ted MacDougall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_MacDougall) holds the record for the most goals scored in a single season, 42 in the [1970–71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970%E2%80%9371_in_English_football) season in the [Fourth Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Fourth_Division).[^cite_note-91]

The highest transfer fee received for a Bournemouth player to date is £65 million, from [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) for [Dominic Solanke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Solanke) in August 2024,[^cite_note-92] whilst the highest transfer fee paid by the club to date is £40.2 million, for [Evanilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanilson_\(footballer,_born_1999\)) from [Porto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Porto), also in August 2024.[^cite_note-93]

The club's highest league finish to date is 6th place in the [Premier League](11250.md), achieved in the [2025–26](77711894.md) season.[^cite_note-94] Their best performance in the [FA Cup](11237.md) is reaching the quarter-finals of the competition in the [1956–57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956%E2%80%9357_FA_Cup), [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_FA_Cup) and [2024–25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_FA_Cup) seasons, whilst in the [EFL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Cup), Bournemouth's best performance is reaching the quarter-finals in the [2014–15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Football_League_Cup), [2017–18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_EFL_Cup) and [2018–19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_EFL_Cup) seasons.[^cite_note-95]
