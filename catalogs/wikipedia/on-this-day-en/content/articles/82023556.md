# International reactions to the 2026 United States intervention in Venezuela



Reactions to the intervention:

&#x20; Countries that supported the U.S. operation

&#x20; Countries that called for a democratic transition in Venezuela

&#x20; Countries that called for respect for international law

&#x20; Countries that condemned the intervention

On 3 January 2026, the [United States](3434750.md) launched a [military intervention in Venezuela](82021123.md) and captured [Venezuelan](32374.md) president [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md) and his wife, [Cilia Flores](9530834.md), leading to reactions from across the world.[^cite_note-1] Several governments condemned the intervention, with some describing them as a violation of [international law](8195726.md), while others expressed support, vague apprehension or were noncommittal. Most condemnations for the strikes came from countries in Africa, Asia, and most of Latin America, whereas most countries in Europe and North America, particularly [NATO](21133.md) member states, supported the airstrikes on Venezuela and capture of Maduro.&#x20;

-  [South Africa](17416221.md): The [Department of International Relations](14036979.md) released a press statement which viewed the US' actions "as a manifest violation of the [Charter of the United Nations](88805.md)". It, furthermore, called on the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) (UNSC) to urgently convene to address the situation.

## International organizations

- [United Nations](31769.md): [Stéphane Dujarric](10302459.md), the spokesperson for Secretary-General [António Guterres](471806.md), said that Guterres was "deeply alarmed by the recent escalation in Venezuela", and that it constituted a "dangerous precedent", emphasizing the full respect "by all – of international law, including the UN Charter".

  [^cite_note-143]

  &#x20;The [UN High Commissioner for Human Rights](22463.md), [Volker Türk](71721726.md), requested restraint while respecting international law. Türk stated that "the protection of the people of Venezuela is paramount and must guide any further action". The UN [Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_International_Fact-Finding_Mission_on_Venezuela) raised concerns over Venezuela's human rights situation and urged that accountability cannot be ignored by the developing situation. The [Human Rights Council](635790.md)-appointed chair for the mission, [Marta Valiñas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Vali%C3%B1as?action=edit\&redlink=1), echoed Türk's concerns of "grave human rights violations and crimes against humanity" in spite of the US' justification for its actions. At the request of Venezuela and Colombia, with the support of permanent members Russia and China, the [UN Security Council](31956.md) convened on 5 January to discuss the situation.

  [^cite_note-144]

  [^cite_note-UNNews050126-145]

  &#x20;Representatives of Russia and China called for the immediate release of President Maduro while the US representative rejected characterisations of US actions as military aggression, describing the operation as a targeted law enforcement measure to arrest an indicted fugitive.
