**Kyiv Oblast** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Київська область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Kyivska oblast*, IPA: [\[ˈkɪjiu̯sʲkɐ ˈɔblɐsʲtʲ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian)), also called **Kyivshchyna** (Київщинa, IPA: [\[ˈkɪjiu̯ʃtʃɪnɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian)), is an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine) (province) in central and northern [Ukraine](31750.md). It surrounds, but does not include, the city of [Kyiv](585629.md), which is administered as a [city with special status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_with_special_status). However, Kyiv also serves as the [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) of the oblast. The [Kyiv metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_metropolitan_area) extends out from Kyiv city into parts of the oblast, which is significantly dependent on the urban economy and transportation of Kyiv.

The population of Kyiv Oblast is 1,795,079 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-8] Its largest city is [Bila Tserkva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bila_Tserkva), with a population over 200,000.

The [Chernobyl Exclusion Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_Exclusion_Zone) is in the northern part of Kyiv Oblast. It is administered separately from the oblast and public access is prohibited.

## History

Kyiv Oblast was created as part of the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) on 27 February 1932 among the first five original oblasts in Ukraine. It was established on territory that had been known as Ruthenian land.[^cite_note-9]

Earlier historical administrative units that became the territory of the oblast include the [Kiev Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Voivodeship) under the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) and [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania), and the [Kiev Viceroyalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Viceroyalty) and [Kiev Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Governorate) under the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). The northern part of the oblast belongs to the historical region of [Polesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polesia) (*Polissia*).

In Kyiv region, there was a specific folk icon-painting style much influenced by the [Kyiv Pechersk Lavra](478837.md) painting school. Saints were depicted on the deep purple or black background, their clothes dark, their haloes dark blue, dark green or even black, outlined by thin white dotted contours. The Kyiv region's icons collection is the part of the exhibition of the [Museum of Ukrainian home icons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Ukrainian_home_icons) in the Historical and cultural complex "[The Radomysl Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radomysl_Castle)".[^cite_note-10]

The current borders of the oblast were set following the [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) in 1986. Administrative oversight of the new city of [Slavutych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavutych), which was constructed as part of the [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast), was then transferred to the Kyiv Oblast (*see [Chernobyl zone](#Chernobyl_zone) below*).

On 24 February 2022, the [Russian Armed Forces](25709.md) [invaded Kyiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_occupation_of_Kyiv_Oblast) as part of its [Russian invasion of Ukraine](70149799.md).[^cite_note-aljazeera2022/4/3/ukraine-11] Ukraine launched a counter-offensive to retake the region in March 2022. The oblast was declared free of invaders on 2 April 2022 by the Ukrainian Deputy Minister of Defense [Hanna Maliar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanna_Maliar).[^cite_note-aljazeera2022/4/3/ukraine-11][^cite_note-12] According to the [Ukrainian Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Ministry_of_Defense) its troops had retaken more than 30 towns and villages around Kyiv.[^cite_note-aljazeera2022/4/3/ukraine-11] However, on 9 April 2022 the Russians attacked the Oblast again, even destroying a railway station in [Bucha](3310001.md).[^cite_note-13]

## Geography

Kyiv Oblast has a total area of 28,100 km<sup>2</sup> (10,849 sq mi) (approximately 35 times the area of Kyiv city) and is located in north-central Ukraine. On the west it borders the [Zhytomyr Oblast](1567596.md), on the southwest – [Vinnytsia Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia_Oblast), on the south – [Cherkasy Oblast](494894.md), on the southeast – [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md), on the east and northeast – [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast), and on the north – [Homyel Voblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homiel_Voblast) of [Belarus](3457.md).

The oblast is equally split between both banks of the [Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River) (*Dnipro*) north and south of Kyiv. Other significant rivers in the oblast are the [Dnieper's tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River#Tributaries_of_the_Dnieper): [Pripyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pripyat_River) (*Prypiat*) (R), [Desna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desna_River) (L), [Teteriv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teteriv_River) (R), [Irpin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irpin_River) (R), [Ros'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ros'_River) (R) and [Trubizh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trubizh) (L).

The length of the Dnipro River within the boundaries of the oblast totals 246 km (153 mi). The oblast has a total number of 177 rivers intersecting the region; 13 reservoirs (the most notable ones being [Kyiv Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Reservoir) and the [Kaniv Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniv_Reservoir)), over 2000 ponds, and approximately 750 small lakes.

### Climate

The climate of Kyiv Oblast is characteristic of the [Polesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polesia) area and other neighboring forested areas. The oblast has a moderately [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) with relatively mild winters and warm summers. The temperatures range from −6.1 °C (21.0 °F) in January to 19.2 °C (66.6 °F) degrees in July.



View of the historical [landscape park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_garden) "[Oleksandriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboretum_Oleksandriya)" in the city of [Bila Tserkva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bila_Tserkva).

### Vegetation

Kyiv Oblast has small mountains and slopes on the right bank of the Dnieper River. This entire area is surrounded by a continuous belt by greenery and forests. The oblast's "green area" covers 436 km<sup>2</sup> (168 sq mi), characterized by 250 different sorts of trees and bushes.

According to the [2001 Ukrainian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Ukrainian_census), [ethnic Ukrainians](145144.md) accounted for 92.5% of the population of Kyiv Oblast, and [ethnic Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Ukraine) for 6.0%.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

The current estimated population (excluding Kyiv) is around 1.72 million (as of 2013). The population density is 63.01/km<sup>2</sup>.

The urban population, according to the [2001 Ukrainian Census data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Census_\(2001\)), accounted for 1,053,500 people, or 57.6%, and the rural population – for 774,400 people, or 42.4%.[^cite_note-18]

According to the data, the number of men accounted for 845,900 people, or 46.3%, that of women – 982,000 people, or 53.7%.

[Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) is the only official language on the whole territory of Kyiv Oblast.[^cite_note-27]

According to a poll conducted by *[Rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rating_\(sociological_group\))* from 16 November to 10 December 2018 as part of the project «Portraits of Regions», 68% of the residents of Kyiv Oblast believed that the Ukrainian language should be the only state language on the entire territory of Ukraine. 9% believed that Ukrainian should be the only state language, while Russian should be the second official language in some regions of the country. 19% believed that Russian should become the second state language of the country. 4% found it difficult to answer.[^cite_note-28]

On 21 March 2023, [Kyiv Oblast Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Oblast_Council) approved the «Programme for the Development and Functioning of the Ukrainian Language as the State Language in All Spheres of Public Life in the Kyiv Oblast for 2023—2025», the main objectives of which are to strengthen the positions of the Ukrainian language in various spheres of public life in the oblast and to [Ukrainianize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainization) the [refugees from other regions of Ukraine](70199806.md).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

According to the research of the *Content Analysis Centre*, conducted from 15 August to 15 September 2024, the topic of which was the ratio of Ukrainian and Russian languages in the Ukrainian segment of [social media](5897742.md), 87.2% of posts from Kyiv Oblast were written in Ukrainian (83.6% in 2023, 75.9% in 2022, 29.3% in 2020), while 12.8% were written in Russian (16.4% in 2023, 24.1% in 2022, 70.7% in 2020).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

### The "exclaves"

The municipality of [Slavutych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavutych) is within the borders of the neighboring [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast) on the eastern bank of the [Dnieper](380138.md) river and the municipality has no common border with the Kyiv Oblast. Still, Slavutych is administered by the Kyiv Oblast authorities (being a kind of administrative [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave)).

Similarly, the town of [Kotsiubynske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotsiubynske), which is within the borders of [Kyiv](585629.md) city (which is surrounded by the Kyiv Oblast), is administered by the Kotsiubynske Settlement Council.

### Chernobyl zone

Main article: [Chernobyl Exclusion Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_Exclusion_Zone)

The north-western end of the oblast is a part of the [Chernobyl Exclusion Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_Exclusion_Zone) due to the [radioactive contamination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_contamination) caused by the [Chernobyl nuclear reactor accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_accident). The largest cities within zone are [Chernobyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_\(city\)) and [Prypiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prypiat,_Ukraine), which are now [abandoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_town). The city of Slavutych was built outside of the zone to host evacuated residents of Prypyat and personnel of the zone installations.

## Economy

### Industry

Kyiv Oblast's main industry's include: power production, food, chemical and petrochemical industries, mechanical engineering and metal-working. The national share of tire production for automobiles constitutes – 63%, [excavators](312903.md) – 53%, paper and cardboard – 40%, hoisting cranes – 39%. In general, the oblast has 330 licensed industrial enterprises and 742 smaller industrial enterprises.[^cite_note-CMU-47] The Boryspil Bus Plant in [Prolisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolisky) produces 1,700 buses per year of various modifications, and several other auto-industry factories are also located in the oblast'.

### Agriculture

In addition to industry, the oblast also has a developed agriculture production. In 1999, the gross grain yield in the region was about 1,118,600 tons, sugar-beets – 1,570,900 tons, sunflower seeds – 18,1 thousand tons, potatoes – 669,200 tons. The region also produced 156,900 tons of meat, 738,500 tons of milk and 855,2 million eggs. At the beginning of 1999, there were 1,130 registered farms within the oblast.[^cite_note-CMU-47]

### Transportation

See also: [Transport in Kyiv](40288955.md)



Simplified map of the major railway lines in Kyiv Oblast.

Kyiv Oblast has a highly developed rail transport system. The total length of the oblast's working railway lines is 88 km (as of 1985). Through the territory of the oblast pass the: Moscow—Kyiv—[Lviv](49968.md), Kyiv—[Dnipro](989795.md)—[Donetsk](562951.md) and other railway routes. In addition to inter-Ukraine and international rail routes, local [Elektrychka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektrichka) lines also pass through the oblast: Kyiv—Fastiv—Koziatyn, Kyiv—Fastiv—Myronivka, Kyiv—Teteriv, Kyiv—Nizhyn, and Kyiv—Yahotyn.

The length of the oblast's roads totals 7,760 km, including 7,489 km of paved roads. The main roads passing thorough the oblast include:

- [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md)—Kyiv—[Odesa](55545.md) ([M-01](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M01_highway_\(Ukraine\))/[M-05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M05_highway_\(Ukraine\)) route)

- Kyiv-[Kovel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovel)-[Lublin](74594.md) (M-07 route)

- [Voronezh](467218.md)—[Kharkiv](2788558.md)-Kyiv-[Lviv](49968.md)-[Kraków](16815.md) ([E-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E40), M-06/M03 route)

- [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk)-[Dnipro](989795.md)-Kyiv (M-04 route)

  [^cite_note-48]

  [^cite_note-49]

The oblast's main airports include two international airports: the [Boryspil Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boryspil_Airport) and the [Hostomel (Antonov) Cargo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostomel_Airport). [Ukrainian military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Ukraine) airbases are located in the cities of [Bila Tserkva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bila_Tserkva) and [Uzyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzyn).

Strategic gas-pipelines in the oblast include [Urengoy–Pomary–Uzhhorod pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urengoy%E2%80%93Pomary%E2%80%93Uzhhorod_pipeline), and [Shebelynka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebelinka_gas_field)—[Poltava](38581.md)—Kyiv.

## Education

Kyiv Oblast has 795 state-run schools of general education, 219 (27.5%) of which are situated in urban areas and 576 (72.5%) of which – in rural areas.[^cite_note-KRSA_education-50] These schools are attended by 232,260 students, 141,416 (60.6%) which attend urban schools, and 98,944 (39.4%) which attend rural area schools.[^cite_note-KRSA_education-50] In addition, there are 12 evening schools with an enrollment of over 6,000 students, 15 private institutions teaching about 7,000 students, 23 vocational schools teaching over 14,300 students, 22 higher schools with an enrollment of over 34,900 students), and 52 [home-school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling) institutions containing over 48,700 children.[^cite_note-KRSA_education-50] There are also 756 institutions of [pre-school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursery_school) education attended by a total of 44,400 children, 52 home-school institutions, 22 vocational institutions, having an enrollment of 17,300 students.[^cite_note-KRSA_education-50]



Aerial view of [Boryspil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boryspil), home of the [Boryspil Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boryspil_Airport) from an airplane.

Also, educational institutions for orphans, physically and mentally disabled children represent an important component of Kyiv Oblast's educational system.

In addition to general education schools, the oblast has educational institutions specifically for gifted children, including:

- Fastiv Regional Natural and Mathematic School

- Kyiv Regional Lyceum of Physical Education and Sports

- Stritiv Higher Pedagogical School of [Kobza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobza) Music

- Brovary Higher School of Physical Education (training ground for several world- and Olympic champions).

## Nomenclature

See also: [Kyiv § Name](585629.md#Name)

Most of Ukraine's oblasts are named after their capital cities, officially referred to as "oblast centers". The name of each oblast is a [relative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_clause) [adjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective), formed by adding a feminine [suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffix) to the name of respective center city: Kyiv (in transliterated Ukrainian) is the center of the *Kyivs’ka oblast'* (Kyiv Oblast). Most oblasts are also sometimes referred to in a feminine noun form, following the convention of traditional regional place names, ending with the suffix "-shchyna", as is the case with the Kyiv Oblast, *Kyivshchyna*.
