**Jubayt** or **Gebeit** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جبيت) is a small town in eastern [Sudan](27421.md).

## History

Gold mining of [quartz veins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz_vein) in the area dates from the [New Kingdom of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Egypt) Period. A large New Kingdom settlement is located northeast of the main Gebeit mine in Wadi Gebeir-Shariq, which includes 15 buildings, and numerous oval [grinding mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mills_\(grinding\)) and [anvil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anvil) stones. Mining was resumed under the British in 1903, after an evaluation by the [Royal Geographical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Geographical_Society) in 1896. Total gold production when operations ceased in 1956 was about 4,200 kg from 63.5 grams per ton (g/t) [ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore). Then, the British Minex Mineral (Sudan) operated for a time until 1990. Some archaic extraction of ore was still underway in the 2000s by miners living in the makeshift village.[^cite_note-Klemm-1]

Gebeit was the location of an attempted assassination of [Abdel Fattah al-Burhan](60480545.md) in which five people were killed at a military graduation ceremony.[^cite_note-2]

## Transport

It is served by a station on the mainline of the Sudan railway network.
