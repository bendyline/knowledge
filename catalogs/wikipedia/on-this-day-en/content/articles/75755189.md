# Munitions factory



People working in [Explosives Factory Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosives_Factory_Salisbury), 1943

A **munitions factory**, also called an **ordnance factory** or a **munitions manufacturing base**, is a factory that produces [explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosives), [ammunition](18940621.md), [missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missiles), and similar products. They are used by the [defence industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_industry) to produce equipment for military use, as well as for public consumption in countries which allow citizens to carry [firearms](11966.md).

## In the United States

In the [United States](3434750.md), munitions factories that are used to produce equipment for the military are all government owned, though some are operated by contractors.[^cite_note-1] In 2020, there were 5 active plants.[^cite_note-army-2] The government office typically responsible for munitions funding is the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense)'s Office of the Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Industrial Base Resilience. In September of 2023, that office announced a plan to begin funding more factories, providing \$50 million worth in shared equipment and \$25 million in monetary funding.[^cite_note-3]

## In the United Kingdom

The majority of [British military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_military) munitions are produced by [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems), a manufacturing company in [London](17867.md), though some are standard [NATO](21133.md) equipment manufactured elsewhere.[^cite_note-4] According to a 2020 article from *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, the United Kingdom was the second-largest exporter of arms in the world.[^cite_note-5]

## Hazards

Due to the nature of devices being produced in munitions factories, working conditions are typically dangerous. Ordnance can [explode](18993857.md), and if the factory is producing [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Weapons) or [biological weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_weapons), yet more risks are added. Also, concerns have been raised over possible [contamination of drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_water) due to [runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_pollution).[^cite_note-6]

One notable incident with unexploded ordnance going off in the plant was the April 11, 2017 incident at [Lake City Army Ammunition Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_City_Army_Ammunition_Plant), which killed worker Lawrence Bass. The cause is thought to be improper working conditions and untaken precautions. Since 2010, Bass and one other person have died due to exploding parts.[^cite_note-army-2][^cite_note-7]
