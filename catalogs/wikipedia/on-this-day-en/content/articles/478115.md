The **New Zealand men's national cricket team** represents [New Zealand](4913064.md) in men's international [cricket](25675557.md). Nicknamed the **Black Caps** ([Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language): *Pōtae Pango*),[^cite_note-12] they played their first [Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket) in 1930 against [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_cricket_team) in [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch), becoming the fifth country to play Test cricket. From 1930, New Zealand had to wait until 1956, more than 26 years, for its first Test victory, against the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_cricket_team) at [Eden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Park) in [Auckland](18660332.md).[^cite_note-Beard-13] They played their first [ODI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International) in the 1972–73 season against [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_national_cricket_team) in Christchurch. New Zealand are the inaugural champions of [ICC World Test Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Test_Championship) which they won in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_World_Test_Championship_final) and they have also won [ICC Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_KnockOut_Trophy) in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_ICC_KnockOut_Trophy). They were also runners-up in the [ICC Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup) in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Cricket_World_Cup) and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Cricket_World_Cup), as well as the [ICC T20 World Cup](8258172.md) in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Men's_T20_World_Cup) and [2026](69284234.md).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

[Tom Latham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Latham_\(cricketer\)) is the current captain of the team in Test cricket and [Mitchell Santner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_Santner) is the current captain of the team in ODI and T20I cricket. The national team is organised by [New Zealand Cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Cricket).

The New Zealand cricket team became known as the Black Caps in January 1998, after its sponsor at the time,[^cite_note-16] [Clear Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clear_Communications), held a competition to choose a name for the team.[^cite_note-17] This is [one of many](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_national_team_nomenclature_based_on_the_%22All_Blacks%22) national team nicknames related to the [All Blacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Blacks).

As of 22 July 2026, the team is ranked 3rd in [Tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket), 2nd in [ODIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International) and 4th in [T20Is](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty20_International) by the [ICC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council).[^cite_note-ICC_rankings_-_ICC_Test,_ODI_and_Twenty20_rankings-18]

As of 2026, the team has participated in all the 35 ICC Men's events taking place from 1975 onwards and have made eight final appearances out of which they won two titles. In October 2000, they won the [ICC Knockout Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Champions_Trophy) (now called ICC Champions Trophy). In June 2021, they won the inaugural [ICC World Test Championship 2019–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_World_Test_Championship_final).

## History

### Beginnings of cricket in New Zealand

See also: [History of cricket in New Zealand to 1890](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cricket_in_New_Zealand_to_1890)

The reverend [Henry Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Williams_\(missionary\)) provided history with the first report of a game of cricket in New Zealand, when he wrote in his diary in December 1832 about boys in and around [Paihia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paihia) on Horotutu Beach playing cricket. In 1835, [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin) and [HMS *Beagle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Beagle) called into the [Bay of Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Islands) on its epic circumnavigation of the Earth and Darwin witnessed a game of cricket played by freed Māori slaves and the son of a missionary at Waimate North. Darwin in *[The Voyage of the Beagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Voyage_of_the_Beagle)* wrote:[^cite_note-19]

> several young men redeemed by the missionaries from slavery were employed on the farm. In the evening I saw a party of them at cricket.

The first recorded game of cricket in New Zealand took place in [Wellington](33804.md) in December 1842. The *Wellington Spectator* reports a game on 28 December 1842 played by a "Red" team and a "Blue" team from the Wellington Club. The first fully recorded match was reported by the *Examiner* in [Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson,_New_Zealand) between the Surveyors and Nelson in March 1844.

The first team to tour New Zealand was Parr's all England XI in 1863–64. Between 1864 and 1914, 22 foreign teams toured New Zealand. England sent 6 teams, Australia 15 and one from [Fiji](10707.md).

### First national team



Australia v Canterbury, Lancaster Park, Christchurch, New Zealand, 1 February 1894

See also: [History of cricket in New Zealand from 1890–91 to 1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cricket_in_New_Zealand_from_1890%E2%80%9391_to_1918)

On 15–17 February 1894 the first team representing New Zealand played New South Wales at Lancaster Park in Christchurch. New South Wales won by 160 runs. New South Wales returned again in 1895–96 and New Zealand won the solitary game by 142 runs, its first victory. The New Zealand Cricket Council was formed towards the end of 1894.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

New Zealand played its first two internationals (not Tests) in 1904–05 against a star-studded Australia team containing such players as [Victor Trumper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Trumper), [Warwick Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_Armstrong) and [Clem Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clem_Hill). Rain saved New Zealand from a thrashing in the first match, but not the second, which New Zealand lost by an innings and 358 runs – currently the second-largest defeat in New Zealand first-class history.[^cite_note-TWR-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

### Inter-war period

See also: [History of cricket in New Zealand from 1918–19 to 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cricket_in_New_Zealand_from_1918%E2%80%9319_to_1945)

In 1927 [they toured England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_cricket_team_in_England_in_1927) for the first time. They played 26 first-class matches, mostly against county teams. They won seven matches, including those against [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire_County_Cricket_Club), [Glamorgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamorgan_County_Cricket_Club), [Somerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerset_County_Cricket_Club) and [Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire_County_Cricket_Club). On the strength of the performances of this tour New Zealand was granted [Test status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_status).

In 1929/30 the [English team toured the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_cricket_team_in_New_Zealand_in_1929%E2%80%9330) and played 4 Tests all of 3 days in duration. New Zealand lost its first Test match but drew the next 3. In the second Test [Stewie Dempster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewie_Dempster) and [Jackie Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mills_\(cricketer,_born_1905\)) put on 276 for the first wicket. This is still the highest partnership for New Zealand against England. New Zealand first played South Africa in 1931–32 in a three match series but were unable to secure Test matches against any teams other than England before [World War II](32927.md) ended all Test cricket for 7 years. A Test tour by Australia, planned for February and March 1940, was cancelled after the outbreak of the war.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

### After World War II

See also: [History of cricket in New Zealand from 1945–46 to 1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cricket_in_New_Zealand_from_1945%E2%80%9346_to_1970)

New Zealand's first Test after the war was against Australia in 1945/46. This game was not considered a "Test" at the time but it was granted Test status retrospectively by the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) in March 1948.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The New Zealand players who appeared in this match probably did not appreciate this move by the ICC as New Zealand were dismissed for 42 and 54. The [New Zealand Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Cricket_Council)'s unwillingness to pay Australian players a decent allowance to tour New Zealand ensured that this was the only Test Australia played against New Zealand between 1929 and 1972.

In 1949 New Zealand sent one of its best-ever teams to England. It contained [Bert Sutcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Sutcliffe), [Martin Donnelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Donnelly_\(cricketer\)), [John R. Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Richard_Reid) and [Jack Cowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Cowie). However, 3-day Test matches ensured that all 4 Tests were drawn. Many have regarded the 1949 tour of England among New Zealand's best ever touring performances. All four tests were high-scoring despite being draws and Martin Donnelly's 206 at Lord's hailed as one of the finest innings ever seen there.[^cite_note-nzhistory.net.nz-30] Despite being winless, New Zealand did not lose a test either. Prior to this, only the legendary 1948 Australian team, led by the great [Don Bradman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bradman), had achieved this.

New Zealand played its first matches against the West Indies in 1951–52, and Pakistan and India in 1955/56.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In 1954/55 New Zealand recorded the lowest ever innings total, 26 against England.[^cite_note-34] The following season New Zealand achieved its first Test victory. The first 3 Tests of a 4 Test series were won easily by the West Indies but New Zealand won the fourth to notch up its first Test victory. It had taken them 45 matches and 26 years to attain.[^cite_note-35]

In the next 20 years, New Zealand won only seven more Tests. For most of this period New Zealand lacked a class bowler to lead their attack although they had two excellent batsmen in [Bert Sutcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Sutcliffe) and [Glenn Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Turner) and a great all-rounder in [John R. Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Richard_Reid).

Reid captained New Zealand on a tour to South Africa in 1961–62 where the five-test series was drawn 2–2. The victories in the third and fifth tests were the first overseas victories New Zealand achieved. Reid scored 1,915 runs in the tour, setting a record for the most runs scored by a touring batsman of South Africa as a result.[^cite_note-Outstanding_Achievements-36]

New Zealand won their first test series in their three match 1969/70 tour of Pakistan 1–0.[^cite_note-37] This was the first ever series win by New Zealand after almost 40 years and 30 consecutive winless series.[^cite_note-38]

### 1970 to 2000

See also: [History of cricket in New Zealand from 1970-71 to 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cricket_in_New_Zealand_from_1970-71_to_2000)



Scoreboard – [Basin Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basin_Reserve), February 1978. NZ's first win over England

In 1973 [Richard Hadlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hadlee) debuted and the rate at which New Zealand won Tests picked up dramatically. Hadlee was one of the best pace bowlers of his generation, playing 86 Tests for New Zealand before he retired in 1990. Of the 86 Tests that Hadlee played in New Zealand won 22 and lost 28. In 1977/78 New Zealand won its first Test against England, at the 48th attempt. Hadlee took 10 wickets in the match.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

During the 1980s New Zealand also had the services of batsman [Martin Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Crowe) and a number of other players such as [John Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wright_\(cricketer,_born_1954\)), [Bruce Edgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Edgar), [John F. Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fulton_Reid), [Andrew Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jones_\(cricketer,_born_1959\)), [Geoff Howarth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Howarth), [Jeremy Coney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Coney), [Ian Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Smith_\(New_Zealand_cricketer\)), [John Bracewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bracewell), [Lance Cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Cairns), [Stephen Boock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Boock), and [Ewen Chatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewen_Chatfield), who were capable of playing the occasional match-winning performance and making a valuable contribution to a Test match.

The best example of New Zealand's two star players (R. Hadlee and M. Crowe) putting in match-winning performances and other players making good contributions is New Zealand versus Australia, 1985 at Brisbane.[^cite_note-41] In Australia's first innings Hadlee took 9–52.[^cite_note-42] In New Zealand's only innings, M Crowe scored 188 and John F. Reid 108. Edgar, Wright, Coney, Jeff Crowe, V. Brown, and Hadlee scored between 17 and 54\*. In Australia's second innings, Hadlee took 6–71 and Chatfield 3–75. New Zealand won by an innings and 41 runs.

One-day cricket provided New Zealand with opportunities to play more frequently against other international teams than Test cricket allowed. In this format, a team can win without individual batsmen scoring centuries or bowlers taking all ten wickets. Victories can be achieved through a combination of partial batting contributions, economical bowling, and effective fielding. New Zealand players met these performance requirements, establishing a competitive record in one-day international cricket.

Perhaps New Zealand's most infamous one-day match was the ["under arm" match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underarm_bowling_incident_of_1981) against Australia at the [MCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_ground) in 1981. Requiring six runs to tie the match off the final ball, Australian captain [Greg Chappell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Chappell) instructed his brother [Trevor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Chappell) to bowl the ball underarm along the wicket to prevent New Zealand batsman Brian McKechnie from hitting a six. The Australian umpires ruled the move as legal even though to this day many believe it was one of the most unsporting decisions made in cricket.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

When New Zealand next played in the tri-series in Australia in 1983, [Lance Cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Cairns) became a cult hero for his one-day batting. In one match against Australia, he hit six sixes at the [MCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_ground), one of the world's largest grounds. Few fans remember that New Zealand lost this game by 149 runs. However, Lance's greatest contribution to New Zealand cricket was his son [Chris Cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Cairns).

Chris Cairns made his debut one year before Hadlee retired in 1990. Cairns, one of New Zealand's best all-rounders, led the 1990s bowling attack with [Danny Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Morrison_\(cricketer\)). [Stephen Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Fleming), New Zealand's most prolific scorer, led the batting and the team into the 21st century. [Nathan Astle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Astle) and [Craig McMillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_McMillan) also scored plenty of runs for New Zealand, but both retired earlier than expected.

[Daniel Vettori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Vettori) made his debut as an 18-year-old in 1997, and when he took over from Fleming as captain in 2007 he was regarded as the best spinning all-rounder in world cricket. On 26 August 2009, Daniel Vettori became the eighth player and second left-arm bowler (after [Chaminda Vaas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaminda_Vaas)) in history to take 300 wickets and score 3000 test runs, joining the illustrious club. Vettori decided to take an indefinite break from international short form cricket in 2011 but continued to represent New Zealand in Test cricket and returned for the [2015 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Cricket_World_Cup).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

On 4 April 1996, New Zealand achieved a unique world record, where the whole team was adjudged Man of the Match for team performance against 4-run victory over the West Indies. This is recorded as the only time where whole team achieved such an award.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

### 21st century

See also: [History of cricket in New Zealand from 2000–01](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cricket_in_New_Zealand_from_2000%E2%80%9301)

New Zealand started the new millennium by winning the [2000 ICC KnockOut Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_ICC_KnockOut_Trophy) in Kenya to claim their first ICC tournament.[^cite_note-51] This was a knockout tournament where teams were seeded according to their performance in Cricket World Cup 1999, the top five teams from that world cup gained direct entry to quarter-finals and while remaining six teams had to play the pre-quarter finals. New Zealand gained direct entry to quarter-finals where they faced Zimbabwe against whom they had recently lost an ODI series, after a nervy start they pulled things back and romped to a crushing 64-run victory to get through to the semis. In semis they faced Pakistan, a team who had managed to knock New Zealand out from last World Cup at this very stage. New Zealand beat Pakistan this time in a thrilling run-chase to enter the final.[^cite_note-52] In the final, they faced India who had knocked out world champions Australia and defending champions South Africa. New Zealand won the toss and opted to bowl but the decision seemed to backfire as India romped to a 141 run opening partnership in 27 overs, New Zealand somehow managed to pull things back but the target was a daunting 265, and in reply they struggled for the most part of their innings but in the end, it was a 122-run partnership between Chris Cairns and Chris Harris that took them close the target before Cairns finished the game with two balls to spare as New Zealand won its first-ever ICC event.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

[Shane Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Bond) played 18 Tests for NZ between 2001 and 2009 but missed far more through injury. When fit, he added a dimension to the NZ bowling attack that had been missing since Hadlee retired, taking 87 wickets at an average of 22.09.

Vettori stood down as Test captain in 2011 leading to star batsman [Ross Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Taylor) to take his place. Taylor led New Zealand for a year which included a thrilling win in a low scoring Test match against Australia in [Hobart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart), their first win over Australia since 1993. In 2012/13 [Brendon McCullum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendon_McCullum) became captain and new players such as [Kane Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kane_Williamson), [Corey Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Anderson_\(cricketer\)), [Doug Bracewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Bracewell), [Trent Boult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Boult) and [Jimmy Neesham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Neesham) emerged as world-class performers. McCullum captained New Zealand to series wins against the West Indies and India in 2013/14 and both Pakistan and Sri Lanka in 2014/15 increasing New Zealand's rankings in both Test and ODI formats. In the series against India McCullum scored 302 at Wellington to become New Zealand's first Test [triple centurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Test_cricket_triple_centuries).[^cite_note-56]

In early 2015 New Zealand made the final of the Cricket World Cup, going through the tournament undefeated until the final, where they lost to Australia by seven wickets.[^cite_note-57] In 2015 the New Zealand national cricket team played under the name of [Aotearoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aotearoa) for their first match [against Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_cricket_team_in_Zimbabwe_in_2015) to celebrate [Māori Language Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_Language_Week).[^cite_note-Aotearoa-58]

In mid-2015 New Zealand toured England,[^cite_note-59] performing well, drawing the Test series 1–1, and losing the One Day series, 2–3. From October to November 2015, and in February 2016, New Zealand played Australia in two Test Series, three and two games respectively. With a changing of an era in the Australian team, New Zealand was rated as a chance of winning especially in New Zealand. New Zealand lost both series by 2–0[^cite_note-60]

In February 2016, Kane Williamson was appointed as the captain of the team after Brendon McCullum's retirement who played his 101st and final ever test against Australia at Christchurch. Williamson's first international series as the full-time captain was Men's T20 World Cup 2016 in India in which the team won all four of its group games but lost to England in the semi-final at Delhi. After the annual rankings update on 4 May 2016 the team was awarded the No. 1 ranking in T20Is. The team then got into a rough patch after the T20 World Cup, where they would go onto lose away series to South Africa, India and Australia. In their home season they managed to beat Pakistan for the first time in a Test series in 32 years, whitewashed Bangladesh across formats, won the Chappell-Hadlee Trophy against Australia but went onto lose to South Africa in T20I, ODI and Test series.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

New Zealand started the 2017 international season with a tri-series involving hosts Ireland, and Bangladesh as a preparation for upcoming Champions Trophy in England.[^cite_note-Milne-64] New Zealand managed to win the tri-series as they finished at the top of the points table but the Champions Trophy turned out to be a disastrous campaign as they got knocked out by Bangladesh and ended the tournament without a single win, the worst performance for them in an ICC Event. After that the team had to wait four months for their next tour which was to India in which both the ODI and T20I series were closely contested but New Zealand lost both by a scoreline of 2–1. The home season started with whitewash of the West Indies across formats followed by whitewash of Pakistan in the ODI series but after that New Zealand lost the T20I series to Pakistan and in the process lost their No. 1 ranking in T20Is. Then they took part in the first ever T20I tri-series involving full-members the Trans-Tasman T20I Tri-Series in which they ended up runners-up to Australia and England finished third. Then they played England where they lost the ODI series but then managed to win the test series. This was their first test series win against England after 19 years and 4th overall in their 87 year old rivalry.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

In 2018–19 they began with a tour of UAE in to play Pakistan. The tour started with New Zealand suffering a whitewash in the T20I series but they drew the ODI series, 1–1.[^cite_note-67] New Zealand produced a stunning act of resilience to register their first away test series win against Pakistan after 49 years.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] This put New Zealand 3rd on the test rankings table. After this in their home season they beat Sri Lanka across formats, lost to India in the ODI series before managing to beat them in the T20I series and lastly they beat Bangladesh across formats and consequently they climbed to No. 2 spot in Test rankings.

New Zealand started the 2019 season with the Cricket World Cup in England & Wales. New Zealand had an impressive start to their World Cup campaign as they remained unbeaten and top of the table for their first six games. Their formed dipped after that initial burst as they managed to lose their next three group games convincingly and only just managed to get through to the semi-finals on net run-rate. In the semi-final, they stunned favourites India on the reserve day to reach a second consecutive final. In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Cricket_World_Cup_final), the scores were tied after 50 overs and again after the Super Over.[^cite_note-70] England won by having hit more boundaries.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] This boundary countback rule was criticised and a couple of months later ICC abolished the rule.[^cite_note-73]

In December 2022, [Kane Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kane_Williamson) stepped down as Test captain and was replaced by [Tim Southee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Southee); Williamson remained white-ball captain. On 4 June 2025, [Gary Stead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Stead) was let go as head coach, as NZ Cricket opted to keep a single coach to oversee all three formats.[^cite_note-74] On 6 June 2025, [Rob Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Walter) was named as his successor.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

In November 2024, New Zealand won their first ever test series in India 3–0 to break India's 12 year test series winning streak at home, and inflict India's first clean sweep home defeat since 2000.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] In November 2025, after helping his team whitewash England and West Indies, [Daryl Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daryl_Mitchell_\(New_Zealand_cricketer\)) became the second New Zealand cricketer (second only to Glenn Turner) to become the top ranked ODI batsman.[^cite_note-79] In January 2026, New Zealand won their first ever ODI series in India aided by Mitchell who scored 352 runs in the 3 matches, including back to back match winning centuries with [Glenn Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Phillips_\(cricketer\)) also scoring a century in the decider. From this series, Daryl Mitchell rose to being the number 1 ranked ODI batsman for the second time.[^cite_note-80]

New Zealand's kit is manufactured by [Canterbury of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_of_New_Zealand), who replaced previous manufacturer WStar in 2009. When playing Test cricket, New Zealand's [cricket whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_clothing_and_equipment) feature logo of the sponsors [Gillette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillette) on the left of the shirt, the [ANZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANZ_Bank_New_Zealand) logo on the left sleeve and on the middle of the shirt and the Canterbury logo on the right sleeve. New Zealand fielders may wear a black cap (in the style of a [baseball cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_cap) rather than the baggy cap worn by some teams) or a white sun hat with the New Zealand Cricket logo in the middle. Helmets are also coloured black (although until 1996, they used to be white with the silver fern logo encased in a black circle).

In [limited overs cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_overs_cricket), New Zealand's ODI and Twenty20 shirts feature the ANZ logo across the centre, with the silver fern badge on the left of the shirt, Canterbury logo on the right sleeve and the Ford logo on the right. In ODIs, the kit comprises a black shirt with blue accents and black trousers, whilst the Twenty20 kit comprises a beige shirt with black accents and black trousers. In [ICC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) limited-overs tournaments, a modified kit design is used with sponsor's logos moving to the sleeve and 'NEW ZEALAND' printed across the front.

In ODI, New Zealand wore Beige and brown between 1980 World Series Cricket and 1988 World Series Cricket. The 1983–1984 version was made popular by the Black Caps supporter group [Beige Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beige_Brigade), who sells the version of this uniform to the general public together with a "moral contract" which explains the expectations that come with being a Beige Brigadier. and was also worn in the inaugural Twenty20 international between New Zealand and Australia. Between 1991 and 1997 grey or silver (with some splashes of black or white) was worn instead. Until 2000, the ODI uniform was teal with black accents.

Previous suppliers were Adidas (World Series Cricket 1980–1990), ISC (World Cup World Cup 1992 and 1996, World Series 1993–97) Canterbury (1998–1999), Asics (who supplied all the 1999 Cricket World Cup participating teams) and WStar (2000–2009).

Previous sponsors were [DB Draught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB_Draught) (1990–1994 in the front, 1995–1997 in the sleeve), [Bank of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_New_Zealand) (1993–94 and 1997–99 in the front), [Clear Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clear_Communications), later [TelstraClear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TelstraClear) (1997–2000 in the front, 2001–2005 in the sleeve), [National Bank of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_New_Zealand) (2000–2014) and [Dheeraj and East Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dheeraj_and_East_Coast) (2009–2010),[^cite_note-84] since 2014 [ANZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_and_New_Zealand_Banking_Group) is the current sponsor, due to National Bank's rebranding as ANZ. [Amul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amul) became the new sponsor in May 2017 for the [ICC CT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Champions_Trophy_2017).

[ICC World Cup 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup) started on 5 October 2023.[^cite_note-85] They have ended their journey to this tournament by getting defeated to India by 70 runs in the semi-final.

In December 2023, there had been a six-year deal with [Castore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castore) to manufacture their kits starting from October 2024.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

## Records

Main articles: [List of New Zealand Test cricket records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_New_Zealand_Test_cricket_records), [List of New Zealand One Day International cricket records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_New_Zealand_One_Day_International_cricket_records), and [List of New Zealand Twenty20 International cricket records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_New_Zealand_Twenty20_International_cricket_records)

### Individual records

- [Richard Hadlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hadlee), one of New Zealand and the world's best all-rounders, took the world record for [most Test wickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Test_cricket_records#Most_wickets_in_a_career) (374) vs India at [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore) in 1988. Hadlee was the first bowler to reach 400 Test wickets, vs India at [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch) in 1990, and finished his career with 431 wickets. He subsequently lost the record to [Kapil Dev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapil_Dev).

- [Corey Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Anderson_\(cricketer\)) holds record for the second [fastest century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_One_Day_International_cricket_records#Fastest_centuries) in [One Day Internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International) (or any other format of international cricket). Playing against [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_cricket_team), he scored his ton in just 36 balls. Corey Anderson lost the record to [AB de Villiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AB_de_Villiers) when AB scored a century in just 31 balls against West Indies.

- In a One Day International in 1996, the entire New Zealand team were awarded man of the match against the West Indies, the first such occasion.

- [Andrew Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jones_\(cricketer,_born_1959\)) and [Martin Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Crowe) held the highest ever 3rd-wicket [partnership in Tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Test_cricket_records#Partnership_records), with 467 against Sri Lanka in 1991, which at the time was the highest partnership for any wicket.

  [^cite_note-cricinfo.com-132]

- [Brian Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Hastings) and [Richard Collinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Collinge) together scored 151 runs for the 10th-wicket [against Pakistan in 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_cricket_team_in_New_Zealand_in_1972%E2%80%9373), the [highest 10th-wicket partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Test_cricket_records#Highest_partnership_\(for_each_wicket\)) at the time.

  [^cite_note-133]

- [Nathan Astle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Astle) scored Test cricket's [fastest ever double century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Test_cricket_records#Centuries) [versus England, at Christchurch in 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_cricket_team_in_New_Zealand_in_2001%E2%80%9302#1st_Test).

He knocked the first hundred off 114 balls. Astle smashed the record by 59 balls, previously held by [Adam Gilchrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Gilchrist) Australia vs South Africa [Johannesburg](58893.md) 2002.

- [Brendon McCullum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendon_McCullum) holds the world record for the [fastest Test hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastest_Test_hundred) in terms of balls faced. It was scored off 54 balls on 20 February 2016, [against Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_cricket_team_in_New_Zealand_in_2015%E2%80%9316#2nd_Test) during his final Test match, in Christchurch.

  [^cite_note-135]

- Brendon McCullum holds the world record for the [most sixes in Test cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_sixes_in_Test_cricket) with 107.

- Brendon McCullum was the first batsman to score two centuries in Twenty20 International Cricket (116\* v. Australia and 123 v. Bangladesh).

- [Chris Cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Cairns) and his father [Lance Cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Cairns) are one of the two father-son combinations to each claim 100 Test wickets, South Africa's [Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pollock) and [Shaun Pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaun_Pollock) being the other.

  [^cite_note-138]

  [^cite_note-139]

- [Martin Guptill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Guptill) scored the [highest score in World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Cricket_World_Cup_records#Batting_records) with 237\* [in 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Cricket_World_Cup).

- Guptill holds the record for [most career runs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Twenty20_International_records#Most_career_runs) (2,271) and [most sixes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Twenty20_International_records#Most_career_sixes) (103, equal with [Chris Gayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Gayle)) in Twenty20 Internationals, both records previously held by Brendon McCullum.

  [^cite_note-140]

- [John Bracewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bracewell) was the first – and so far only – [substitute fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_fielder) to take four catches in a One Day International, vs Australia in [Adelaide](1148.md) on 23 November 1980.

  [^cite_note-141]

- [Daniel Vettori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Vettori) was the first cricketer to take four wickets and score a half-century in each innings of a Test match, a feat he achieved against [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_national_cricket_team) in October 2008 at [Chittagong](380451.md). His figures were 5/95 and 4/74 with the ball and 55\* and 76 with the bat.

- [Chris Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Harris_\(cricketer\)) holds the record for the most ODI [caught and bowled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caught_and_bowled) dismissals, with 29.

### Team records

- New Zealand has the record of taking the most number of Tests to register their first win in the format (45 Tests)

- New Zealand became the second team after Australia to win their debut ODI (beat Pakistan at Christchurch in 1973)

- New Zealand hosted Australia to play the first-ever T20 international match at Eden Park in Feb 2005

- New Zealand became the sixth team to achieve No. 1 ranking in all three formats by ICC (T20I 2016–2018, Test 2021–2021, ODI 2021–2023)

- New Zealand won the inaugural WTC Title on 23 Jun 2021 beating India at Hampshire Bowl, Southampton

- New Zealand became the first team to appear in two men's Finals in ICC Events in a calendar year (WTC & T20 WC in 2021)

- New Zealand became the first team to whitewash India in India in a Test series of 3 or more matches

- New Zealand has appeared in the joint-most Semi-Finals in ICC ODI & T20I World Cups (14)

- New Zealand has appeared in the joint-fourth Finals in ICC Events (8)

- New Zealand has won two ICC Titles, joint-seventh with South Africa

### Notable

- Ross Taylor has the 8th highest ODI batting average among batsmen to have played at least 100 ODIs, and Kane Williamson has the 10th highest.

- New Zealand dismissed Zimbabwe ([Harare 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_cricket_team_in_Zimbabwe_in_2005#First_Test)) twice in the same day for totals of 59 and 99. Zimbabwe became only the second team (after India at Manchester in 1952) to be dismissed twice in the same day. The whole Test was completed inside two days.

  [^cite_note-144]

  &#x20;This feat was then repeated [at Napier in 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwean_cricket_team_in_New_Zealand_in_2011%E2%80%9312#Only_Test) when NZ dismissed Zimbabwe for 51 and 143 to end the match within three days.

  [^cite_note-145]

- Kane Williamson holds the record for most centuries by a New Zealander in Tests, with 33.

- Brendon McCullum holds the record for the highest Test innings by a New Zealander of 302 (vs India in 2014). He is currently the only triple centurion from New Zealand.

- Brendon McCullum holds the New Zealand Test record for the most innings of 200 or more, with 4.

- Brendon McCullum scored the fastest World Cup fifty (off 18 balls) for New Zealand in a Pool A Match of [2015 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Cricket_World_Cup) against England, beating his own 20-ball record set against Canada in World Cup (2007) earlier.

  [^cite_note-146]

- Martin Guptill holds the record for the highest One Day International innings by a New Zealander, with 237 not out against West Indies in the [2015 World Cup quarter-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Cricket_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Quarter-finals) in Wellington.

  [^cite_note-147]

- [Shane Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Bond) took an ODI hat-trick in the last over (innings bowling figures: 10–0–61–4) vs Australia at Hobart in January 2007.

  [^cite_note-148]

- [Tim Southee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Southee) took a [Twenty20 hat-trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Twenty20_International_records#Hat-tricks), taking 5–18 in the match against Pakistan.

  [^cite_note-149]

- [Colin Munro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Munro) scored the second fastest T20 International 50, off 14 balls, against Sri Lanka at [Eden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Park), Auckland on 10 January 2016.

- Chris Harris, Daniel Vettori, [Kyle Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Mills) and Chris Cairns are the only New Zealand cricketers to have taken 200 wickets in ODIs.

- [Ajaz Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajaz_Patel) took all 10 wickets in an innings, being the third international cricketer and first New Zealand cricketer to achieve this feat, after England's [Jim Laker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Laker) and India's [Anil Kumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anil_Kumble) .

  [^cite_note-150]

- In June 2022 against England, New Zealand scored the fifth-highest team total (553) and second-highest match aggregate (837) in a losing cause in test match history.
