**Perm**[^cite_note-13], originally known as **Yagoshikha** (Ягошиха; 1723–1781) and briefly as **Molotov** (Молотов; 1940–1957), is the [administrative centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre) of [Perm Krai](1765550.md) in the European part of [Russia](25391.md). It sits on the banks of the [Kama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_River) near the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains), covering an area of 799.68 square kilometres (308.76 square miles). With over one million residents[^cite_note-est.2020-14] Perm is the [15th-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Russia_by_population) in Russia and the 5th-largest in the [Volga Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Federal_District).

## History

Further information: [Great Perm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Perm)

Perm is located in the old Permiak area. Perm was first mentioned as the village of [Yagoshikha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yagoshikha) (Ягошиха) in 1647; however, the history of the modern city of Perm starts with the development of the [Ural region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(region\)) by [Tsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar) [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great). [Vasily Tatishchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Tatishchev), appointed by the Tsar as a chief manager of Ural factories, founded Perm together with another major centre of the Ural region, [Yekaterinburg](66524.md).

Modern Perm remains a major railway hub and one of the chief industrial centers of the Urals region. The city's diversified metallurgical and engineering industries produce equipment and machine tools for the petroleum and coal industries, as well as agricultural machinery. A major petroleum refinery uses oil transported by pipeline from the West Siberian oilfields, and the city's large chemical industry makes fertilizers and dyes. The city's institutions of higher education include the Perm A.M. Gorky State University, founded in 1916.

Perm grew considerably as industrialization proceeded in the Urals during the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) period, and in 1940 was named *Molotov* in honour of [Vyacheslav Molotov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Molotov). In 1957 the city was returned to its historical name.

### Etymology

The name *Perm* is of [Uralic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uralic_languages) etymology ([Komi-Permyak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komi-Permyak_language): Перем, *Perem*; [Komi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komi_language): Перым, *Perym*). Komi is a member of the [Permic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permic_languages) branch of the Uralic languages, which is also named for Perm. In [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_language) and [Vepsian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vepsian) *perämaa* means "far-away land"; similarly, in [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language) *perem* means "edge" or "verge". The [geologic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologic_period) of the [Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian) takes its name from the [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponym).

### Pre-industrial development

In 1723, a copper-smelting works was founded at the village of *Yagoshikha*.[^cite_note-ep-15] In 1781 it was granted city status[^cite_note-ep-15] and renamed *Perm*. Perm's position on the navigable Kama River, leading to the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga), and on the [Siberian Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_Route) across the Ural Mountains, helped it become an important trade and manufacturing centre. It also lay along the [Trans-Siberian Railway](56912.md).&#x20;

Perm was located on the route to [Siberia](29639.md) and served either as a stopover or a final destination for many exiles under Tsarist and Soviet rule.[^cite_note-ec78-16] According to [Mikhail Speransky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Speransky), in 1813, almost the entire population of Perm consisted of exiles.[^cite_note-ec79-17] Many [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) insurgents of the [November Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_Uprising) were exiled in Perm in the 1830s.[^cite_note-ec79-17]

Perm was a city where Poles were allowed to settle after serving their sentences, as they were not permitted to return to Polish territory.[^cite_note-ec78-16] Poles established a Catholic chapel in the 1830s, built a [Catholic church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception_Church,_Perm) in 1875, which also served [Lithuanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians) and other nationalities, founded a Poverty Relief Society in 1897, and helped establish a local museum and theatre.[^cite_note-18]

The city was devastated by a fire in 1842 and was subsequently rebuilt.[^cite_note-sgk-19] Perm became a major trade and industrial centre with a population of more than 20,000 people in the 1860s, with several [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy), [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper), and [steamboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat) producing factories, including one owned by a British entrepreneur. In 1870, an opera theatre was opened in the city, and in 1871 the first [phosphoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus) factory in Russia was built. In the 1880s, the city had over 20 industrial plants and over 60 craft workshops.[^cite_note-sgk-19] According to the 1897 census, the largest minorities were [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) (2.8%), [Jews](25955086.md) (1.8%) and Poles (1.6%).[^cite_note-20] Apart from Orthodox and Catholic churches, the city also had a Lutheran church, an [Old Believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Believers) church and a synagogue.[^cite_note-sgk-19] In 1916, [Perm State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_State_University)—a major educational institution in modern Russia—was opened.



Pokrovskaya Street (nowadays Lenin street) in central Perm around 1910

After the outbreak of the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), Perm became a prime target for both sides because of its [munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munitions) factories. It was heavily rumored from July–September 1918 that the Tsarina [Alexandra Feodorovna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Feodorovna_\(Alix_of_Hesse\)) and her four daughters were imprisoned at the perception and Berezine buildings. According to the file on the Tsar, the [Grand Duchess Anastasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Anastasia) would have attempted to flee. On 25 December 1918, the Siberian [White Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement) under [Anatoly Pepelyayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Pepelyayev) (who acknowledged the authority of the [Omsk](62593.md) Government of [Aleksandr Kolchak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Kolchak)), took Perm. On 1 July 1919, the city was retaken by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army).

### Soviet period

In the 1930s, Perm grew as a major industrial city with [aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation), [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding), and [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical) factories built during that period. During [World War II](32927.md), Perm was a vital center of [artillery](2508.md) production in the Soviet Union. During the [Cold War](325329.md), Perm became a [closed city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_city).[^cite_note-Bilbao-21]

### Modern city



[TGC-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGC-9?action=edit\&redlink=1)

The city is a major administrative, industrial, scientific, and cultural[^cite_note-Bilbao-21] centre. The leading industries include machinery, [defence](608296.md), [oil](23195.md) production (about 3% of Russian output), oil [refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refining), chemical and petrochemical, timber and wood processing and the food industry.

In 1996 a memorial was erected in the Yegoshikhinskoe cemetery to deceased inmates of the Gulag, forced settlers and victims of the [Great Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Terror).[^cite_note-22]

On 5 December, 2009 a [fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lame_Horse_fire) at the Lame Horse nightclub killed 156 people and injured over 100 more.[^cite_note-23]

On 20 September, 2021, [a mass shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_State_University_shooting) occurred at [Perm State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_State_University), resulting in six fatalities and 40 injuries.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Geography



The [Kama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_River) in Perm

The city is located on the bank of the [Kama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_River) upon hilly terrain. The Kama is the main [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the [Volga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River) and is one of the deepest and most picturesque rivers of Russia. This river is the waterway which grants the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains) access to the [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea), [Baltic Sea](3335.md), [Sea of Azov](78019.md), [Black Sea](3386.md), and [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). The Kama divides the city into two parts: the central part and the right bank part. The city stretches for 70 kilometers (43 mi) along the Kama and 40 kilometers (25 mi) across it. The city street grid parallels the Kama River, travelling generally east–west, while other main streets run perpendicularly to those following the river. The grid pattern accommodates the hills of the city where it crosses them.

Another distinguishing feature of the city's relief is the large number of small rivers and brooks. The largest of them are the [Mulyanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulyanka_River), the [Yegoshikha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yegoshikha_River), the [Motovilikha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motovilikha_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) (all are on the left bank of Kama River), and the [Gayva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gayva_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) (on the right bank).

### Climate

Perm has a [warm summer continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfb*).[^cite_note-27] Winters are long, snowy and quite cold. Summers are moderately warm with cool nights, although summers are shorter than winters. Due to its far inland location, there is a distinct lack of [seasonal lag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_lag) resulting in rapid cooling down of the warm weather as days get shorter. This results in September, October, and November being colder than May, April, and March, respectively.

## Administrative and municipal status

Perm is the [administrative centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre) of the [krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krai) and, within the [framework of administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Administrative_divisions), it also serves as the administrative center of [Permsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permsky_District), even though it is not a part of it.[^cite_note-Ref127-1] As an administrative division, it is, together with two [rural localities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia), incorporated separately as the **[city of krai significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance) of Perm**—an administrative unit with the status equal to that of the [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Perm_Krai).[^cite_note-Ref127-1] As a [municipal division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Municipal_divisions), the city of krai significance of Perm is incorporated as **Perm Urban Okrug**.[^cite_note-Ref843-8]

## Economy

Perm has the largest industrial output among cities in the Urals, ahead of [Yekaterinburg](66524.md), [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk) and [Ufa](321112.md), although Perm has a smaller population than these. Thirty-five per cent of [Perm Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_Oblast)'s industry is located in Perm.[^cite_note-city_econ-30] The largest industries in the city are electric power engineering, oil and gas refining, machine building, chemicals and petrochemicals, forestry processing, printing and food industry.[^cite_note-city_industrydept-31]

Several major industrial companies are located in Perm: [Perm refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_refinery); [Perm Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_Motors?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Aviadvigatel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviadvigatel), major suppliers of engines to the [Russian aircraft industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_aircraft_industry); rocket engine company [Proton-PM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton-PM), which as of 2011 was slated to produce the [RD-191 engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RD-191) for the upcoming [Angara rocket family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angara_rocket_family); electric engineering firms Morion JSC, Perm Scientific and Industrial Group, and Perm Electrical Engineering Plant; Russia's largest exporter of cables and wires, JSC KAMKABEL; and oil and natural gas companies such as [LUKoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUKoil)-Perm Ltd. and LUKoilPernefteprodukt Ltd.[^cite_note-city_econ-30]

Perm is a railway junction on the [Trans-Siberian Railway](56912.md) with lines radiating to [Central Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Russia), the north part of the [Urals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(region\)), and the far east of [Russia](25391.md). Perm has two major railway stations, the historical [Perm I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_I_railway_station?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the modern [Perm II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_II_railway_station). The [Kama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_River) is an important direct link between the European part of Russia to the sea ports on the [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea), [Baltic](3335.md), [Azov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Sea), [Black](3386.md), and [Caspian](19653787.md) seas.[^cite_note-Transport_in_Perm-32]

Perm is served by the international airport [Bolshoye Savino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshoye_Savino_Airport), which is located 16 km (9.9 mi) southwest of the city.[^cite_note-33]

Perm's public transit network includes [tram](30733.md), [bus](4146.md), and city-railway routes. The formerly important [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) service was discontinued in July 2019.

### Proposed metro system

The first plans for a *Perm Metro* system date back to the 1970s. A feasibility study was compiled in 1990, but [economic difficulties during the decade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Russian_financial_crisis) prevented its final planning and construction. The plans were revitalised in the early 2000s, but a lack of funding hampered the project and plans were once again put on hold. [Light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) has also been considered.[^cite_note-34]

## Culture



[Perm Opera and Ballet House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_Opera_and_Ballet_Theatre)

The [Perm Opera, and Ballet House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_Opera_and_Ballet_Theatre) is one of the best in Russia.[^cite_note-Пермяки_высадились_на_Манхэттене-35] There are many other theatres in Perm, including the [Drama Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_Academic_Theatre), the Puppet Theatre, the Theatre for Young Spectators, the Theatre "Stage Molot", and the *mystical* [At the Bridge Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At_the_Bridge_Theatre). Among the cities museums and galleries, the [Perm State Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_State_Art_Gallery?action=edit\&redlink=1) is recognised for its collections of art, including paintings from 15th- to 18th-century art movements, and wooden sculptures from the region. It is housed in a notable early 19th-century structure, once an orthodox cathedral. The spire of the museum towers over the rest of Perm, as it is situated on the Komsomolsky Prospect.[^cite_note-36] Perm is receiving attention from the development of the new Museum of Contemporary Art, [Perm Museum of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_Museum_of_Contemporary_Art) (PERMM) which officially opened in March 2009.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]



[Perm Museum of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_Museum_of_Contemporary_Art) (PERMM) in the building of the Perm River Terminal.

The RAV Vast [steel tongue drum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_tongue_drum) was invented in Perm by Andrey Remyannikov. This instrument is unique in the tongue drum and [handpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handpan) world because each note has multiple harmonic [overtones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtone) that resonate with other notes in the drum. The sound consequently has long sustain and [reverberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverberation).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

[The Legend of Perm Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Perm_Bear) or The Walking Bear is a sculpture depicting a walking [bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear), which is also shown on the city's coat of arms. It is situated in the central part of the city on Lenin Street, in front of the Organ Concert Hall and close to the building of Legislative Assembly of [Perm Krai](1765550.md). The author of the sculpture is [Vladimir Pavlenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Pavlenko_\(sculptor\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), a monumentalist sculptor from [Nizhny Tagil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Tagil), member of the Artists' Union of Russia and [UNESCO](21786641.md) International Association of Arts.[^cite_note-NR2-20060911-42][^cite_note-News-59-43]

The [Permyak Salty Ears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permyak_Salty_Ears) is a sculpture and one of the most visited tourist attractions in Perm.[^cite_note-44]

## Education

Perm is a scientific centre. Some of the scientific institutes are combined in the Perm Scientific Center of the Ural Branch of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences).

Perm is a home to several major [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) including [Perm State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_State_University),[^cite_note-45] Perm State Technical University,[^cite_note-46] Perm branch of state university Higher school of economics,[^cite_note-47] Perm State Teachers' Training University, Perm State Medical Academy,[^cite_note-48] Perm State Pharmaceutical Academy,[^cite_note-49] Perm State Agricultural Academy,[^cite_note-50] The Institute of Art and Culture, Perm State Choreographic School,[^cite_note-51] and others. There are also three military schools in Perm.
