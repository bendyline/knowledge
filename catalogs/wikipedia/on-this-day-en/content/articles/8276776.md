The [Philippines](23440.md) is the 5th largest [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian)-majority country on [Earth](9228.md) as of 2010,[^cite_note-2] with about 93% of the population being adherents.[^cite_note-top-1] As of 2019, it was the [third largest Catholic country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism_by_country#By_country) in the world and was one of the two predominantly [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) nations in [Asia](689.md).[^cite_note-3]

According to the [National Statistics Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority)'s national census for the year 2010, an estimated 90.1% of Filipinos were Christians; this consisted of 80.6% [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_Philippines), 4% [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md), 1.0% [Aglipayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iglesia_Filipina_Independiente), 2.7% [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism_in_the_Philippines) groups, and 3.4% other Christian groups including other [Protestant denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_the_Philippines) (Baptist, Pentecostal, [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_in_the_Philippines), Methodist, and [Seventh-day Adventist](28632.md)) as well as [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy_in_the_Philippines). Around 5.6% of the whole country was [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines); about 1-2% were [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_the_Philippines); 1.8% of the entire population adhered to other independent religions, while less than 0.1% (as of 2015)[^cite_note-atlantic-4] were [irreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism). With this, the Philippines has consistently demonstrated a low level of government restrictions on religion, ensuring that citizens are free to practice their faiths openly, with minimal interference from state authorities, which reflects the country's strong constitutional guarantee of religious freedom and diversity.[^cite_note-5] According to the 2020 census, at least 84% of the population is Christian; about 79% belong to the Catholic Church while about 5% belong to Protestantism and other denominations such as [Philippine Independent Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church), [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md), [Seventh-day Adventist Church](28632.md), [Apostolic Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Catholic_Church_\(Philippines\)), [United Church of Christ in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ_in_the_Philippines), [Members Church of God International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_Church_of_God_International) (MCGI) and [Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism).[^cite_note-PSA-PIF2020-6]

Many Filipinos in 2021 celebrated 500 years of Christian presence in the Philippines[^cite_note-7] with [Pope Francis](1687680.md) commemorating March 16, the day Magellan introduced [Catholicism](606848.md) with a mass on [Limasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limasawa), [Leyte](194750.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## History



Missionaries of the Sacred Heart with villagers in front of a Catholic church in the Philippines, circa pre-1920

Early Christian presence in the Malay archipelago and the Philippine Islands may be traced to [Arab Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Christian) traders from the Arabian Peninsula. They had trade contacts with early Malayan Rajahs and Datus that had ruled these various Islands. Early Arabians had heard the gospel from Peter the Apostle at Jerusalem (Acts 2\:11), as well as evangelized by Paul's ministry in Arabia (Galatians 1\:17) and also by the evangelistic ministry of St Thomas. Later, these Arab traders along with Persian Nestorians, stopped by the Philippines on their way to Southern China for trade purposes. However, no solid efforts were made to evangelize the native population. With the spread of Islam in Arabia, much of the Christian heritage of Arabia had ended and the Arab travelers focused more on spreading Islam to [Mindanao](20546.md),[^cite_note-12] through which they transmitted the knowledge of Jesus as a prophet to the Moro people.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

In 1521, the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) navigator and explorer [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan) under the service of [Spain](26667.md) came across the Philippines while searching for the [Spice Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_Islands). Ferdinand Magellan and his men landed in [Cebu Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_Island) in central Philippines.[^cite_note-chr-15]

At this time period, almost nothing was known to the West of the Philippines and so information on most pre-Hispanic societies in the islands date to the early period of Spanish contact. Most Philippine communities, with the exception of the Muslim sultanates in [Mindanao](20546.md) and the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago), were fairly small and lacking in complex centralised authority. This absence of centralised power meant that a minority of Spanish explorers were able to convert larger numbers of indigenous peoples than attempting such in larger, more organized, dominions such as the [Indianised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India) or [Theravada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theravada) Buddhist kingdoms in [mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia), the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula) and the [Indonesian Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Indonesia).



Bible used by a [Sangley](2298105.md#Social_classifications) Anglicans of the Philippines.

With his arrival in [Cebu](160278.md) on March 17, 1521, his first attempt was to colonize the islands and to Christianise its inhabitants. The story goes that Magellan met with [Rajah Humabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah_Humabon), ruler of the island of Cebu, who had an ill grandson. Magellan (or one of his men) was able to cure or help the young boy, and in gratitude Humabon allowed himself, his chief consort Humamay, and 800 of his subjects to be baptised *en masse*. In order to achieve this, Spain had three principal objectives in its policy towards the Philippines: the first was to secure Spanish control and acquisition of a share in the [spice trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade); use the islands in developing contact with [Japan](15573.md) and [China](5405.md) in order to further Christian missionaries’ efforts there; and lastly to spread their religion.[^cite_note-16]

After Magellan was killed by natives, the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_people) later sent [Miguel López de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_L%C3%B3pez_de_Legazpi). He arrived in Cebu from [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain) (now [Mexico](3966054.md)), where Spain introduced Christianity and colonisation in the Philippines took place.[^cite_note-17] He then established the first Permanent Spanish Settlement in Cebu in 1565. This settlement became the capital of the new Spanish colony, with Legazpi as its first governor. After Magellan, [Miguel López de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_L%C3%B3pez_de_Legazpi) conquered the Islamised [Kingdom of Maynila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Maynila) in 1570. The Spanish missionaries were able to spread Christianity in [Luzon](69375.md) and the [Visayas](72418.md), but the diverse array of ethno-linguistic groups in the highland areas of Luzon avoided Spanish annexation owing to their remote and difficult mountainous region. Sultanates in Mindanao retained the Islamic faith, which had been present in the southern Philippines since some time between the 10th and 12th century, had slowly spread north throughout the archipelago, particularly in coastal areas.[^cite_note-chr-15] This resistance to Western intrusion makes this story an important part of the nationalist history of the Philippines. Many historians have claimed that the Philippines peacefully accepted Spanish rule; the reality is that many insurgencies and rebellions continued on small scales in different places through the Hispanic colonial period.

## Important traditions

For most [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_people), the belief in God permeates many aspects of life. Christians celebrate important holidays in many different ways, the most important of which are [Christmas](6237.md), [Lent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lent) and [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week), [All Souls' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls'_Day), as well as many local fiestas honouring patron saints and especially the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary). Filipinos living and working in [Metro Manila](72340.md) and occasionally those from [the diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Filipino) often return to their respective home [provinces](165100.md) and towns to observe these holidays with their birth families, much like the practise in [Mainland China](55079.md) for [traditional holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_holidays). Filipino infants and individuals are more often than not expected to be baptised as Christians to affirm faith in Christ and membership in a specific denomination.[^cite_note-18]

### Christmas

Main article: [Christmas in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_in_the_Philippines)

[Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_in_the_Philippines) is the biggest holiday, and one of its most beloved rites is the *[Simbang Gabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simbang_Gabi)* or *[Misa de Gallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misa_de_Gallo)*, a [series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novena) of [Masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)) held before dawn in the nine days preceding [Christmas Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Day). Devotees attend each Mass (which is different from the otherwise [Advent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advent) liturgy of the day elsewhere) in anticipation of Christ's birth and to honour the Virgin Mary, along with the belief that attending the novena ensures fulfilment of a favour requested of God. After the service, worshippers eat or buy a breakfast of traditional delicacies that are sold in churchyards, the most common being *[puto bumbóng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puto_bumbong)* and *[bibingka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibingka)*.

### Lent

The second most important religious season is Lent, which commemorates Christ's [Passion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passion_\(Christianity\)) and [Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus), ending with [Easter](9325.md) which celebrates the [Resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_of_Christ). Beginning with [Ash Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Wednesday), Lent has a sombre mood that becomes more pronounced as [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week) (*Semana Santa*) arrives. [Holy Week in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_the_Philippines) is a period especially rich in centuries-old tradition, which have undertones from indigenous customs and beliefs that date back to the [pre-Christian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(900%E2%80%931521\)).

#### Customs

Practises include the continuous melodic recital of the *[Pasyón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasyon)*, a 17th-century [epic poem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poem) which narrates Biblical stories and the life of Christ, with a focus on the Passion narrative (hence its name). Adapted from the ancient Filipino art of [orally transmitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_tradition) poems through chant, the devotion is usually performed by groups of individuals, each member chanting in shifts to ensure complete, unbroken recitation of the text. Theatre troupes or towns meanwhile stage [Passion plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passion_play) called *Senákulo*, which are similar to its European predecessors in that there is no universal text, that actors and crew are often ordinary townsfolk, and that it depicts Biblical scenes related to [Salvation History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation_History) other than the Passion.

The *[Visita Iglesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visita_Iglesia)* is the praying of the [Stations of the Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stations_of_the_Cross) in several churches (often numbering seven) on either [Maundy Thursday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maundy_Thursday) or [Good Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday). [Processions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procession) are a staple throughout the week, the most important being on [Holy Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Wednesday), [Good Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday) (where the [burial of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burial_of_Christ) is reenacted with a town's *[Santo Entierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Entierro)* image) and the joyous *[Salubong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_the_Philippines#Easter_Sunday)* that precedes the first Mass on Easter Sunday.

[Fasting and abstinence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasting_and_abstinence_in_the_Catholic_Church) is undertaken throughout the season and traditional taboos are enforced on Good Friday, usually after 3\:00 p.m. [PHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Standard_Time) ([UTC+8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+8)) - the time Christ is said to have died - all through [Black Saturday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Saturday) until the [Easter Vigil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Vigil). [Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_the_Philippines) and [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_in_the_Philippines) limit broadcasting hours and air mostly inspirational programming alongside the days' religious services; [newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_the_Philippines) are also on hiatus, while shopping malls and most restaurants are closed to allow employees to return home. Popular holiday spots such as [Boracay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boracay) often dispense with these customs, while many people use the long holiday for overseas travel instead of observing the traditional rites.

### Other festivals



[Our Lady of Peñafrancia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Pe%C3%B1afrancia) in [Naga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga,_Camarines_Sur), [Camarines Sur](255883.md)

Other observances include [All Saints' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Saints'_Day) and [All Souls' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls'_Day) in November, which are taken as one season called *Undás* (traditionally known in English as [Allhallowtide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allhallowtide)). As with Christmas and Lent, most Filipinos also return home in the period (the third most important in the calendar), but with the main intent of visiting and cleaning ancestral tombs.

January itself has two important Christological feasts: the Feast of the [Translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_\(relic\)) of the [Black Nazarene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Nazarene) on January 9, where the image is returned to its shrine in [Quiapo Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiapo_Church) in a day-long procession of millions; and the Feast of the *[Santo Niño de Cebú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Ni%C3%B1o_de_Ceb%C3%BA)* (Holy [Child Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_Jesus)) every Third Sunday of January, with the largest celebrations being held in [Cebu City](285868.md).[^cite_note-chr-15]

In May, the *[Flores de Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores_de_Mayo)* (literally, "Flowers of May") is when small altars are bedecked with flowers in honour of the Virgin Mary. Communities also hold the *[Santacruzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santacruzan)*, which is part-procession honouring the [finding of the Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Cross) (on its [old Galician date](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invention_of_the_Cross#May_3)), and part-[fashion show](8312977.md) for a town's maidens.

In addition, most any place that has a [patron saint](68055.md) (often [barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay), towns, Catholic schools, and almost every church) holds a fiesta, where the saint's image is processed and feted with traditional foods, [funfairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funfair), and live entertainment on his/her feast day, which is often declared a holiday for the area. Examples of patronal fiestas are the [Nativity of St John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativity_of_St_John_the_Baptist) every June 24, where communities under his patronage would celebrate his summertime birth by splashing other people with water, and the [triduum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triduum) of feasts known as the [Obando Fertility Rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obando_Fertility_Rites) held in mid-May, where devotees dance for fertility in a custom that has ancient animist roots.
