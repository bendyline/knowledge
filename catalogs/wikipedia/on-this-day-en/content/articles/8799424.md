In the [Catholic Church](606848.md), a [bishop](4092.md) is an [ordained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_orders_in_the_Catholic_Church) [minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_\(Catholic_Church\)) who holds the fullness of the [sacrament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacraments_of_the_Catholic_Church) of holy orders and is responsible for teaching doctrine,[^cite_note-1] governing Catholics in his [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_jurisdiction),[^cite_note-2] [sanctifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctifying) the world[^cite_note-3] and representing the church.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Catholics trace the origins of the office of [bishop](4092.md) to the [apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament), who it is believed were endowed with a special [charism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charism) and office by the [Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Spirit_in_Christianity) at [Pentecost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecost).[^cite_note-6] Catholics believe this special charism and office has been transmitted through an [unbroken succession of bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_succession) by the [laying on of hands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laying_on_of_hands) in the sacrament of holy orders.[^cite_note-7] Bishops are members of a singular church-wide episcopal body by virtue of both their sacramental consecration and their remaining in hierarchical [communion with the Pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_communion) and other bishops.[^cite_note-cd-8]<sup>: Section 4 </sup>

The [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council)'s *[Decree concerning the Pastoral Office of Bishops in the Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decree_concerning_the_Pastoral_Office_of_Bishops_in_the_Church)*, promulgated on 28 October 1965, was devoted to outlining the Church's teaching on Bishops and their pastoral role.[^cite_note-cd-8]<sup>: Section 3 </sup>

## Canon law

Bishops are always men.[^cite_note-9] In addition, [canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_\(canon_law\)) 180 of the *[Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches](3547385.md)*[^cite_note-10] states that a candidate for the [Eastern](214903.md) episcopacy should:

1. demonstrate solid faith, good morals, piety, zeal for souls and prudence;

2. enjoy a good reputation;

3. not be bound by a matrimonial bond;

4. be at least thirty-five years old;

5. ordained a presbyter for at least five years;

6. possess a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) or [licentiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate) in some sacred science or at least be an expert in it.

Canon 378 § 2 of the [1983 *Code of Canon Law*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Code_of_Canon_Law) goes on to clarify that "The definitive judgement on the suitability of the person to be promoted rests with the Apostolic See."[^cite_note-11]

## Diocesan or eparchial bishops

See also: [Ordinary (Catholic Church)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_\(Catholic_Church\)) and [Diocesan bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocesan_bishop)

[Diocesan bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocesan_bishop) (known as eparchial bishops in the [Eastern Catholic Churches](214903.md)) are assigned to govern local regions within the Catholic Church known as [dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) in the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) and [eparchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eparchy) in the Eastern Churches. Bishops are collectively known as the [College of Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Bishops) and can hold such additional titles as [archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop), [cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(Catholicism\)), [patriarch](175409.md), or [pope](23056.md). As of 2020, there were approximately 5,600 living bishops total in the Latin and Eastern churches of the Catholic Church.[^cite_note-12] The traditional role of a bishop is to act as head of a diocese or eparchy. Dioceses vary considerably in geographical size and population. A wide variety of dioceses around the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) which received the Christian faith early are rather compact in size, while those in areas more recently evangelized, as in some parts of [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md), [South America](26769.md) and the [Far East](11672.md), tend to be much larger and more populous. Within his own diocese a Latin Church bishop may use pontifical vestments and regalia, but may not do so in another diocese without, at least, the presumed consent of the appropriate ordinary.[^cite_note-13]

The [appointment of bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appointment_of_Catholic_bishops) in the [Catholic Church](606848.md) is a complicated process. Outgoing [bishops](8799424.md), neighbouring bishops, the faithful, the [apostolic nuncio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuncio), various members of the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia), and the [pope](23056.md) all have a role in the selection. The exact process varies based upon a number of factors, including whether the bishop is from the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) or one of the [Eastern Catholic Churches](214903.md), the geographic location of the diocese, what office the candidate is being chosen to fill, and whether the candidate has previously been ordained to the [episcopate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_episcopate).

### Resignation at 75

Since the Second Vatican Council, diocesan bishops and their equivalents "who have become less capable of fulfilling their duties properly because of the increasing burden of age or some other serious reason" have been "earnestly requested to offer their resignation from office either at their own initiative or upon the invitation of the competent authority".[^cite_note-cd-8]<sup>: Section 21 </sup> The age of 75 was suggested for this, and [John Paul II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_II) had these provisions incorporated in the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church)'s new [1983 Code of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Code_of_Canon_Law).[^cite_note-14]

Article 401.1 of the 1983 Code of Canon Law states that "A diocesan Bishop who has completed his seventy-fifth year of age is requested to offer his resignation from office to the [Supreme Pontiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Pontiff), who, taking all the circumstances into account, will make provision accordingly".[^cite_note-15] On 15 February 2018, Pope Francis established the same rule for non-cardinal bishops serving in the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia), who had previously lost their positions automatically at 75.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Roles

A "diocesan bishop"[^cite_note-18] is entrusted with the care of a [local Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_Church) ([diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese)).[^cite_note-19] He is responsible for teaching, governing, and [sanctifying](8799424.md#Duties) the faithful of his diocese, sharing these duties with the priests and deacons who serve under him.[^cite_note-20] The Second Vatican Council's *Constitution on the Sacred Liturgy* described the diocesan bishop as "the high priest of his flock".[^cite_note-21]

To "teach, sanctify and govern"[^cite_note-22] means that he must (1) oversee preaching of the Gospel and Catholic education in all its forms; (2) oversee and provide for the administration of the sacraments; and (3) legislate, administer and act as judge for [canon-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_Law) matters within his diocese. He serves as the "chief [shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherd#In_religion)" (spiritual leader) of the diocese and has responsibility for the pastoral care of all Catholics living within his ecclesiastical and [ritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_Church) jurisdiction.[^cite_note-23] He is obliged to celebrate [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)) every Sunday and [Holy Day of Obligation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Day_of_Obligation) with the intention of praying for those in his care, assign clergy to their posts in various institutions and oversee finances.[^cite_note-24] A bishop is to have a special concern for priests, listening to them, using them as counsellors, ensuring that they are adequately provided for in every way, and defending their rights set forth in the *Code of Canon Law*.[^cite_note-25] Latin Catholic bishops also must make regular *[ad limina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinquennial_Visit_Ad_Limina)* visits to the Holy See every five years.[^cite_note-26]

Because of their function as teachers of the faith, it is customary in some English-speaking countries to add to the names of bishops the postnominal title of "D.D." ([Doctor of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Divinity)) and to refer to and address them as "Doctor".

Only a bishop has authority to confer the [sacrament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacraments_of_the_Catholic_Church) of holy orders. In the Latin Church the [minor orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_orders) were abolished after the Second Vatican Council. In Eastern Catholic Churches, a monastic [archimandrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimandrite) may [tonsure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonsure) and institute his subjects to minor orders; however, the tonsure and minor orders are not considered to be part of the sacrament of holy orders.[^cite_note-27]

The sacrament of [Confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation) is normally administered by a bishop in the Latin Church, but a bishop may delegate the administration to a priest. In the case of receiving an adult into full communion with the Catholic Church, the presiding priest will administer Confirmation.[^cite_note-28] In the Eastern Catholic Churches, Confirmation (called [Chrismation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrismation)) is normally administered by priests as it is given at the same time as [baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism). It is only within the power of the diocesan or eparchial bishop to bless [churches](6325.md) and [altars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar), although he may delegate another bishop, or even a priest, to perform the ceremony.[^cite_note-29]

On [Holy Thursday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maundy_Thursday), Latin Catholic bishops preside over the [Mass of the Chrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrism). In some cases this celebration takes place on the preceding [Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Wednesday).[^cite_note-30] Although Oil of the Sick for the sacrament of [Anointing of the Sick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anointing_of_the_Sick) is blessed at this Mass, it may also be blessed by any priest in case of necessity. Only a bishop may consecrate Chrism. In the Eastern Catholic Churches chrism is consecrated solely by heads of churches *[sui juris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_iuris)* (patriarchs and metropolitans) and diocesan bishops may not do so.

Only a bishop or other ordinary may grant *[nihil obstats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihil_obstat)* for theological books, certifying that they are free from doctrinal or moral error; this is an expression of the teaching authority and educational responsibility of the bishop.

Prior to the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council), it was also the prerogative of the bishop to consecrate the [paten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paten) and [chalice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalice_\(cup\)) that would be used during the Mass. One of the changes implemented since the council, is that a simple blessing is now said, and it may be given by any priest.

### Canonical authority

In both Western and Eastern Catholic churches, any priest can celebrate the [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(Catholic_Church\)) or [Divine Liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Liturgy). In order to offer Mass or Divine Liturgy publicly, however, a priest is required to have permission from the local Ordinary—authority for this permission may be given to pastors of parishes for a limited period, but for long-term permission recourse to the diocesan bishop is usually required. A [celebret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebret) may be issued to travelling priests so that they can demonstrate to pastors and bishops outside of their own diocese that they are in good standing. However, even if a priest does not possess such a document, he may celebrate the sacraments if the local bishop or pastor judges that the visiting priest is a person of good character.[^cite_note-31]

In the East an [antimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimension) signed by the bishop is kept on the altar partly as a reminder of whose altar it is and under whose [omophorion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omophorion) the priest at a local parish is serving.

In the Western Catholic church's view of bishops' powers, the "power which they exercise personally in the name of Christ, is proper, ordinary, and immediate, although its exercise is ultimately controlled by the supreme authority of the Church," as per *[Lumen Gentium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumen_gentium)* 27.[^cite_note-\:10-32]<sup>: 33 </sup>

For priests to validly celebrate the sacrament of [Penance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrament_of_Penance_\(Catholic_Church\)) they must have faculties (permission and authority) from the local bishop;[^cite_note-33] however, when the penitent is in danger of death, a priest has both the right and obligation to hear the confession no matter where he may be.[^cite_note-34]

To preside at matrimony ceremonies, Latin Church priests and deacons must have appropriate jurisdiction or delegation from a competent authority. In the Latin branch of the Catholic Church, the teaching is that it is the couple themselves who administer the graces of the sacrament; thus, although it is normally an ordained person who officiates at a marriage ceremony, a bishop may delegate a lay person to be present for the exchange of vows; this would be done only in extreme cases such as in mission territories. In the Eastern tradition, the clergy not only witness the exchange of vows but must impart a blessing for a valid marriage to have taken place.[^cite_note-35]

Unless a particular bishop has forbidden it, any bishop may preach throughout the Catholic Church,[^cite_note-36] and any priest or deacon may also preach anywhere (presuming the permission of local pastor) unless his faculty to preach has been restricted or removed.[^cite_note-37]

The [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral) of a diocese contains a special chair, called a *[cathedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedra)*, sometimes referred to as a [throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throne), set aside in the sanctuary for the exclusive use of its Ordinary; it symbolizes his spiritual and ecclesiastical authority.

## Additional titles, status and roles

Main article: [Catholic Church hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_hierarchy)

Bishops may fill additional roles in the Catholic Church, including the following:

### Bishop-elect

A priest who has been appointed as bishop or a diocesan bishop appointed as archbishop, but before [assuming office or installation](48795115.md).

### Titular bishop

A [titular bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_bishop) (or titular archbishop) is a bishop who is not bishop of a diocese; unless (since 1970) he is coadjutor or emeritus, he is assigned to a [titular see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see), which is usually the name of a city or area that used to be the seat of a diocese, but whose episcopal see (diocese) is no longer functioning as such. Titular bishops often serve as [auxiliary bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_bishop), as officials in the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia), in the Patriarchal Curias of Eastern Churches, as papal diplomatic envoys (notably [apostolic nuncios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_nuncio) or [apostolic delegates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_delegate)), or head certain missionary pre-diocesan jurisdictions (notably as [apostolic vicar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_vicar), which as of 2019 no longer gets a titular see). Since 1970, a coadjutor bishop (or archbishop) uses the title of the see he is assigned to, and a bishop (or archbishop) emeritus uses the title of his last residential see.

### Suffragan bishop

A [suffragan bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan_bishop) leads a diocese within an [ecclesiastical province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province) other than the principal diocese, the [metropolitan archdiocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop#Roman_Catholic).[^cite_note-metropolitan-38]

### Auxiliary bishop

An [auxiliary bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_bishop) is a full-time assistant to a diocesan bishop or archbishop. Auxiliaries are titular bishops without the right of succession, who assist the diocesan bishop or archbishop in a variety of ways and are usually appointed as [vicars general or episcopal vicars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicar_general) of the diocese in which they serve.[^cite_note-39]

### Coadjutor bishop

A [coadjutor bishop](1338403.md) is a bishop who is given almost equal authority to that of the diocesan bishop or archbishop; he has special faculties and the right to succeed the incumbent diocesan bishop or archbishop.[^cite_note-40] The appointment of coadjutors is seen as a means of providing for continuity of church leadership. Until recent times, there was the possibility of a coadjutor bishop not having the right of succession.

### Bishop-prelate

A bishop who holds an office that does not require episcopal ordination, notably either the Prelate of a personal prelature[^cite_note-41] or a territorial prelature.[^cite_note-42]

### Emeritus bishop&#x20;

When a diocesan bishop, archbishop, or auxiliary bishop retires, he is given the honorary title of "[emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus)" of the last see he served, i.e., archbishop emeritus, bishop emeritus, or auxiliary bishop emeritus of the see. "Emeritus" is not used for a titular see, but could be used for a bishop who has transferred to a nondiocesan appointment without actually being retired. For example, Cardinal [Luis Antonio Tagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Antonio_Tagle), who was promoted to be prefect for the [Congregation for the Evangelization of Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Evangelization_of_Peoples), became [Archbishop Emeritus of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Manila).[^cite_note-43]

Traditionally, bishops appointed ordinaries or auxiliaries served for life. When the rare resignation occurred, the bishop was assigned a titular see. The status of "emeritus" emerged after the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) when bishops were at first encouraged and then required to submit their resignations at the age of 75. On 31 October 1970, [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) decreed that "diocesan bishops or archbishops of the Latin rite who resign are no longer transferred to a titular church, but instead continue to be identified by the name of the see they have resigned."[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

### Cardinal

A [cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(Catholicism\)) is a bishop or archbishop appointed by the Pope to serve in the [College of Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Cardinals). Members of the college aged under 80 elect a new pope, who is in practice always one of their number, on the death or resignation of the incumbent. Cardinals also serve as papal advisors and hold positions of authority within the structure of the Catholic Church. Under canon law, a man appointed a cardinal must normally be a bishop, or accept consecration of it, but may seek papal permission to decline. Most cardinals are already archbishops of important archdioceses or patriarchates, others already serving as titular bishops in the Roman Curia. Recent popes have appointed a few priests, most of them renowned theologians, to the College of Cardinals, and these have been permitted to decline episcopal consecration. Examples include [Karl Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Josef_Becker) in 2012, [Ernest Simoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Simoni) in 2016, and [Timothy Radcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Radcliffe) in 2024.[^cite_note-47]

### Archbishop

Main article: [Archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop)

An archbishop is the head of an archdiocese, or a bishop assigned a titular see which is an archdiocese.

#### Metropolitan archbishop

A [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) is an archbishop with minor jurisdiction over an [ecclesiastical province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province); in practice this amounts to presiding at meetings and overseeing dioceses within the province.[^cite_note-48]

In Eastern Catholicism a metropolitan may also be the head of an [autocephalous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocephalous), *[sui juris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_juris)*, or [autonomous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_entity) church when the number of adherents of that tradition is small. In the Latin Church, metropolitans are always archbishops; in many Eastern churches, the title is "Metropolitan", with some of these churches using "archbishop" as a separate office.

### Pope

The [pope](23056.md) is the Bishop of [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Rome). The Catholic Church holds that the College of Bishops as a group is the successor of the College of Apostles. The Church also holds that uniquely among the [apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament), [Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter), the first Bishop of Rome, was granted a role of leadership and authority, giving the pope the right to govern the Church together with the bishops.[^cite_note-49] Hence, the Church holds that the Bishop of Rome, as successor of Peter, possesses the role, uniquely among bishops, of speaking for the whole Church, appointing other bishops, and managing the Church's central administration, the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia). Papal pronouncements which meet the requirements of the decree on [papal infallibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_infallibility) of the [First Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Vatican_Council) are considered infallible.

#### Pope emeritus

Upon [his resignation as pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resignation_of_Pope_Benedict_XVI) (Bishop of Rome) on 28 February 2013, [Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) became [Supreme Pontiff Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI#Pope_emeritus:_2013%E2%80%932022) (or, colloquially, Pope Emeritus). The sole holder of the title to date, he held it for nearly a decade, until [his death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_funeral_of_Pope_Benedict_XVI) at age 95 in 2022.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

### Patriarch

Further information: [Patriarch § Catholic Church](175409.md#Catholic_Church)

The title of patriarch in the Catholic Church is applied to either the patriarch of an Eastern Church *sui iuris* or to a minor Latin patriarch. The patriarch of an Eastern Church *sui iuris* heads an autonomous Church, is elected by the synod of that Church, and exercises authority within his patriarchal territory and on eparchies and parishes outside his territory. A minor Latin patriarch is an honorific title above archbishop given to some Latin dioceses for historical reasons.

### Catholicos

Some Eastern Catholic Churches style their heads as *catholicoi*, a historic title for the head of a Church. The [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church), the [Chaldean Catholic Church](698821.md), and the [Syro-Malankara Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malankara_Catholic_Church) all style their heads as such.

### Major archbishop

[Major archbishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_archbishop) are the heads of a [major archiepiscopal church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_archiepiscopal_church). The major archbishops' authority within their respective *sui juris* churches is equal to that of a patriarch, but they receive fewer ceremonial honors and their election must be confirmed by the Holy See.

### Primate

In the Catholic Church, a [primate](24672.md) is usually the bishop of the oldest diocese or the capital of a (present or former) [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation); the title is one of honor.

### Public office

Since the publication of the new [Code of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Code_of_Canon_Law) in 1983 by [Pope John Paul II](23805.md), all members of the Catholic clergy are forbidden to hold public office without the express permission of the [Holy See](13393.md).[^cite_note-52]

## Consecration of bishops

Main article: [Appointment of Catholic bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appointment_of_Catholic_bishops)

See also: [Consecrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecrator)

The appointment of bishops in the Catholic Church is a complicated process that requires the participation of several officials. In the Latin Church, the local synod, the papal nuncio (or apostolic delegate), various [dicasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery) of the Roman Curia, and the Pope all take a part; since the 1970s it has become common practice for the nuncio to solicit input from clergy and laity within the vacant diocese. In patriarchal and major archiepiscopal Eastern Churches, the permanent synod, the holy synod, and the patriarch or major archbishop also play a role in the selection of bishops.

## Apostolic succession and other churches

Main article: [Apostolic succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_succession)

The Catholic Church's view is that bishops form an unbroken succession to the [apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament),**[^cite_note-\:10-32]**<sup>: 33 </sup> a concept known as [apostolic succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_succession). Since 1896, when [Pope Leo XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII) issued the bull *[Apostolicae curae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolicae_curae)*, the [Catholic Church](606848.md) has not recognised [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) orders as valid, because of changes in the ordination rites that took place in the 16th century as well as divergence in the understanding of the theology of episcopacy and Eucharist. However, this view has since been complicated because [Old Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Catholic) bishops, whose orders are fully recognised as valid by Rome, have acted as co-consecrators in Anglican episcopal consecrations. According to the church historian Timothy Dufort, by 1969 all [Church of England](5955.md) bishops had acquired Old Catholic lines of [apostolic succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_succession) fully recognised by the Holy See.[^cite_note-53]

The Catholic Church does recognize, as [valid but illicit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valid_but_illicit), ordinations performed by some independent Catholic groups such as the [Old Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Catholic_Church) of the [Utrecht Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_Union) and the [Polish National Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_National_Catholic_Church), so long as those receiving the ordination are baptized males and a valid rite of episcopal consecration—expressing the proper functions and sacramental status of a bishop—is used. The Holy See also recognises as valid the ordinations of the [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox), [Old Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Catholic), [Oriental Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox) and [Assyrian Nestorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East) churches. Regarding the Churches of the East, the Second Vatican Council stated:

> To remove, then, all shadow of doubt, this holy Council solemnly declares that the Churches of the East, while remembering the necessary unity of the whole Church, have the power to govern themselves according to the disciplines proper to them, since these are better suited to the character of their faithful, and more for the good of their souls.[^cite_note-54]

## Dress and vestments

Main article: [Pontifical vestments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_vestments)

### Latin Church

A crosier, pectoral cross, episcopal ring, and jade box on top of a white cloth that covers a dark brown wooden table

Some of the insignia of a bishop's office *(clockwise from right)*: [crosier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crosier), [pectoral cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectoral_cross), and [episcopal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_ring).

The everyday dress of Latin Church bishops may consist of a black (or, in tropical countries, white) [cassock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassock) with [ amaranth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaranth_\(color\)) trim and purple [fascia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascia_\(vestment\)), along with a [pectoral cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectoral_cross) and [episcopal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_ring). The 1969 Instruction on the dress of prelates stated that the dress for ordinary use may instead be a simple cassock without coloured trim.[^cite_note-55] Since 1969, a black suit and clerical shirt, already customary in English-speaking countries, has become very common also in countries where previously it was unknown.

A Latin Church bishop's [choir dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choir_dress), which is worn when attending but not celebrating liturgical functions, consists of the purple [cassock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassock) with amaranth trim, [rochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochet), purple [zucchetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zucchetto), purple [biretta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biretta) with a tuft, and [pectoral cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectoral_cross). The [cappa magna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappa_magna) may be worn, but only within the bishop's own diocese and on especially solemn occasions.[^cite_note-56]

The [mitre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitre), [zucchetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zucchetto), and [stole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stole_\(vestment\)) are generally worn by bishops when presiding over liturgical functions. For liturgical functions other than the [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)) the bishop typically wears the [cope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cope). Within his own [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) and when celebrating solemnly elsewhere with the consent of the local [ordinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_\(Catholic_Church\)), he also uses the [crosier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crosier).[^cite_note-57] When celebrating [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)), a bishop, like a [priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priest_\(Catholic_Church\)), wears the [chasuble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chasuble). The [Caeremoniale Episcoporum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caeremoniale_Episcoporum) recommends, but does not impose, that in solemn celebrations a bishop should also wear a [dalmatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatic), which can always be white, beneath the chasuble, especially when administering the sacrament of [holy orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_orders), blessing an abbot or abbess, and dedicating a church or an altar.[^cite_note-58] The Caeremoniale Episcoporum no longer makes mention of [pontifical gloves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_gloves), [pontifical sandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_sandals), [liturgical stockings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical_stockings) (also known as [buskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buskins)), the [maniple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maniple_\(vestment\)), or the accoutrements that it once prescribed for the bishop's horse.

### Eastern Catholic

The everyday dress of Eastern Catholic bishops is often the same as their Latin Church counterparts: black clerical suit with pectoral cross or [panagia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagia).

#### Byzantine Rite Catholic

Traditionally, bishops are [monks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_monasticism) and so their everyday dress is the monastic habit with a panagia and, depending on rank, also a pectoral cross and a second panagia.

When attending liturgical functions at which he does not celebrate, a bishop may wear a [mantya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantya), panagia and an [engolpion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engolpion) if he is a [patriarch](175409.md) or [metropolitan bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop). He will also carry a pastoral staff in the form of a walking stick topped by a [pommel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilt#Pommel). No episcopal ring exists in the Byzantine Rite.

When participating in the [Divine Liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Liturgy), a bishop wears the [sakkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakkos) (Imperial [dalmatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatic)), [omophorion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omophorion), [epigonation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigonation) and Byzantine-style mitre which is based on the closed Imperial crown of the late [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and is made in the shape of a bulbous crown, completely enclosed, and the material is of [brocade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocade), [damask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damask) or [cloth of gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloth_of_gold). It may be embroidered, and richly decorated with jewels, with four [icons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icons) attached: [Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_in_Christianity), the [Theotokos](73513.md), [John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist) and the [Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_cross). These mitres are usually gold, but other [liturgical colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical_colours) may be used. The mitre is topped by a cross, made out of metal and standing upright. He also carries a [crosier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crosier) of that rite's style. Presiding over other services, he may wear fewer vestments, but also a mantya unless wearing a [sticharion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sticharion).
