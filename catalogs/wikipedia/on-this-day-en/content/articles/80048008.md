The **2025 Repco Bathurst 1000** was a [motor racing](1022.md) event for [Supercars](54162.md) held 9 to 12 October 2025.[^cite_note-1] It was Race 27 of the [2025 Supercars Championship](77547302.md) and took place at [Mount Panorama Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Panorama_Circuit) in [Bathurst, New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathurst,_New_South_Wales), Australia, featuring a single race of 1000 kilometres.

[Matthew Payne](70927866.md) and [Garth Tander](3210898.md) claimed victory for [Grove Racing](68325063.md), their very first win.[^cite_note-2] It was the first win for Payne, while for Tander it was his sixth victory, the first since [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Bathurst_1000) and first in a Ford. [James Golding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Golding_\(racing_driver\)) took the chequered flag first, however when there was five laps to go, Golding made contact with [Cooper Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Murray), and ended up getting a post race five second penalty relegated him to third.[^cite_note-31Penalty-3]

## Background

The event was the 68th running of the [Bathurst 1000](1072273.md), which was first held at the [Phillip Island Grand Prix Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Island_Grand_Prix_Circuit) in 1960 as a 500-mile race for Australian-made standard production sedans, and marked the 65th time that the race was held at Mount Panorama. It was the 29th running of the ["Australia 1000"](1072273.md) race, which was first held after the organisational split between the Australian Racing Drivers Club and [V8 Supercars Australia](54162.md) that saw two "Bathurst 1000" races contested in both 1997 and 1998.

Entering the weekend, [Broc Feeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broc_Feeney) of [Triple Eight Race Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Eight_Race_Engineering) was leading the season championship. This race also marked the last opportunity for drivers to qualify for the Final Series, a [new championship format](77547302.md#Format_changes) introduced at the beginning of the season. [Brodie Kostecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brodie_Kostecki) and [Todd Hazelwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Hazelwood) entered the event as the defending race winners and competed together with [Dick Johnson Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Johnson_Racing), having moved from [Erebus Motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erebus_Motorsport) at the end of 2024.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-Kostecki-5]

### Entry list

Twenty-seven cars were entered for the event - 16 sixth-generation Chevrolet Camaros and 11 seventh-generation Ford Mustangs. In addition to the twenty-four regular-season entries, three "wildcard" entrants joined the field – one each from [Matt Stone Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Stone_Racing), [Tickford Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tickford_Racing) and [Triple Eight Race Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Eight_Race_Engineering).[^cite_note-MSRWildCard-6][^cite_note-T8Wildcard-7][^cite_note-TickfordWildcard-8]

Seven drivers made their Bathurst 1000 debut: [Porsche Carrera Cup Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Carrera_Cup_Australia_Championship) champion [Harri Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harri_Jones), [2024 Super2 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Super2_Series) champion [Zach Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Bates) (son of eight time Bathurst 1000 starter [Rick Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Bates)), [2021 Super3 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Super2_Series) champion [Nash Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_Morris) (son of [2014 Bathurst 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Bathurst_1000) winner [Paul Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Morris_\(racing_driver\))) and fellow [Super2 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super2_Series) drivers [Lachlan Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachlan_Dalton), [Rylan Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rylan_Gray), Jarrod Hughes and [2023 Super3 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Super2_Series) champion Jobe Stewart.

## Broadcast

The event telecast was produced by Supercars Media and carried domestically by [Fox Sports Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports_Australia) (via Fox Sports 506 and [Kayo Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayo_Sports)), a [paid service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_television) which covered all sessions including support categories, and the [Seven Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Network) (via [free-to-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-to-air) channels 7HD and [7mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7mate), as well as [streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media) on [7plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7plus)), which covered select sessions from midday Friday onwards.[^cite_note-24] 2025 marked the last commentary appearance for [Neil Crompton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Crompton) at the Bathurst 1000, who had been involved in broadcasting the race (with breaks for his driving career) since [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_James_Hardie_1000).[^cite_note-25]
