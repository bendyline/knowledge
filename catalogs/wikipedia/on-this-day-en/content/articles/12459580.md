# Agriculture in Romania



[Sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower) field in Romania



Farmers working in [Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transylvania) in 2006

**Agriculture in Romania** is an important part of the [economy of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Romania), with almost 13.5 million hectares (ha) being used for agriculture.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Farming is a common occupation in the rural areas of the country, and the majority of the farms in Romania are small farms of less than 5 hectares.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

Romania is the biggest producer of [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower) seeds, [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey) and [plums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plums) in the [European Union](9317.md) as of 2023.[^cite_note-\:0-1] In 2022, Romania was the world's second producer of plums, and was among the world's top 20 biggest producers of sunflower seeds, [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed), [cherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley) and [soybean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean).[^cite_note-2]

Agriculture summed up about 4.3% of [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) in 2019, down from 12.6% in 2004.[^cite_note-3] In 2023, 23% of the Romanian labour force were employed in agriculture, the highest percentage in the European Union.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

Agricultural mechanization is relatively poor compared to [Western Europe](33800.md). In some regions of the country, farmers still use horse-drawn agriculture tools and rely on animal power. In 2009, while in Western Europe tractors were replaced after 3,000-4,000 hours of use, in Romania they sometimes lasted up to 12,000 hours.[^cite_note-sttract2009-09-11-4]

The main problems encountered by Romanian agriculturists are a lack of major investments in agriculture, due to difficulty in accessing available funds, fragmentation and erosion of soil, property-related lawsuits and obsolete technology. Several major companies have entered the Romanian market, including [Smithfield Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithfield_Foods), [Cargill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill), [Bunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunge_Limited), [Glencore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencore), [Lactalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactalis), and [Meggle AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meggle_AG). These companies have since invested hundreds of millions of euros in Romania.

## Production



Farmers at Tescani village in [Bacau County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacau_County), October 2017

According to the National Statistics Institute, in 2006 991,000 ha of sun-flower and 191,000 ha of soybean were cultivated. Cereal production in 2006 stood at 15.1 million tons, including 5.3 million tons of wheat and 8.6 million tons of maize.[^cite_note-stah2009-05-04-5] In 2007, a severe [drought](37335.md) destroyed over 60% of crops. Wheat production subsequently fell to 3 million tons, and prices fell 25%. Analysts claimed it was the worst harvest since 1940.[^cite_note-6]

Romania produced in 2018:

- 18.6 million tons of [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) (9th largest producer in the world);

- 10.1 million tons of [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) (16th largest producer in the world);

- 3 million tons of [sunflower seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower_seed) (4th largest producer in the world, only behind Ukraine, Russia and Argentina);

- 1.8 million tons of [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley);

- 1.6 million tons of [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed);

- 1 million tons of [cabbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage);

- 978 thousand tons of [sugar beets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet), which are used to produce [sugar](27712.md) and [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol);

## Romanian agricultural research

Agricultural research in Romania developed mainly through the Agronomical Research Institute (founded 1927), and post-World War II through its successor, the [Research Institute for Cereals and Industrial Crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Institute_for_Cereals_and_Industrial_Crops). Some of the main Romanian agricultural scientists include [Ion Ionescu de la Brad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Ionescu_de_la_Brad), [Gheorghe Ionescu-Sisești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Ionescu-Sise%C8%99ti), [Nichifor Ceapoiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nichifor_Ceapoiu?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Zoe Țapu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoe_%C8%9Aapu), [Alexandru-Viorel Vrânceanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru-Viorel_Vr%C3%A2nceanu?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Cristian Hera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Hera?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Nicolae N. Săulescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_N._S%C4%83ulescu), and others.[^cite_note-Rezgen-9][^cite_note-Incda-10]

## Genetically modified crops

[Genetically modified soybean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetically_modified_soybean) was legal to cultivate and sell until entry into the EU in 2007, whereupon they were banned. This resulted in an immediate withdrawal of 70% of the soybean hectares in 2008 and a [trade deficit](4810.md) of [€](9472.md)117.4m for purchase of replacement products. Farmer sentiment is very much in favour of relegalisation.[^cite_note-Hera-Popescu-2011-11]
