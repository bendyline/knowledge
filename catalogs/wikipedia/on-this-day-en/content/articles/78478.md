# Canyon

"Gorge" and "Cañon" redirect here. For other uses, see [Canyon (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyon_\(disambiguation\)), [Gorge (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorge_\(disambiguation\)), and [Canon (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_\(disambiguation\)).



[Grand Canyon](46989.md), [Arizona](21883824.md), at the confluence of the [Colorado River](180968.md) and [Little Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Colorado_River)

A **canyon** (from [Spanish](26825.md)&#x20;*&#x20;_[cañón](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ca%C3%B1%C3%B3n#Spanish)_*; archaic [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) spelling: *cañon*),[^cite_note-1] **gorge** or **chasm**, is a deep cleft between [escarpments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) or [cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff) resulting from [weathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weathering) and the [erosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) activity of a river over [geologic time scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologic_time_scale).[^cite_note-2] Rivers have a natural tendency to cut through underlying surfaces, eventually wearing away rock layers as [sediments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment) are removed downstream. A [river bed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_bed) will gradually reach a baseline elevation, which is the same elevation as the body of water into which the river drains. The processes of weathering and erosion will form canyons when the river's [headwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_source) and [estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary) are at significantly different elevations,[^cite_note-3] particularly through regions where softer rock layers are intermingled with harder layers more resistant to weathering.

A canyon may also refer to a rift between two mountain peaks, such as those in ranges including the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains), the [Alps](981.md), the [Himalayas](37966.md) or the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes). Usually, a river or stream carves out such splits between mountains. Examples of mountain-type canyons are [Provo Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provo_Canyon) in Utah or [Yosemite Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yosemite_Valley) in California's [Sierra Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada). Canyons within mountains, or gorges that have an opening on only one side, are called box canyons. [Slot canyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slot_canyon) are very narrow canyons that often have smooth walls.

Steep-sided valleys in the [seabed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabed) of the [continental slope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_slope) are referred to as [submarine canyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_canyon). Unlike canyons on land, submarine canyons are thought to be formed by [turbidity currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbidity_current) and [landslides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_landslide).

## Etymology



[Sumidero Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumidero_Canyon), [Mexico](3966054.md)

The word "*canyon"* is Spanish in origin (*cañón*,[^cite_note-4] pronounced [\[kaˈɲon\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)), with the same meaning. The word canyon is generally used in [North America](21139.md), while the words *gorge* and *[ravine](2014227.md)* (French in origin) are used in [Europe](9239.md) and [Oceania](22621.md), though *gorge* and *ravine* are also used in some parts of North America. In the United States, place names generally use *canyon* in the southwest (due to their proximity to [Spanish-speaking Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Spanish)) and *gorge* in the northeast (which is closer to [French Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canada)), with the rest of the country graduating between these two according to geography. In Canada, a *gorge* is usually narrow while a *ravine* is more open and often wooded. The military-derived word *[defile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defile_\(geography\))* is occasionally used in the United Kingdom. In South Africa, *kloof* (in [Krantzkloof Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krantzkloof_Nature_Reserve)) is used along with *canyon* (as in [Blyde River Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blyde_River_Canyon)) and *gorge* (in [Oribi Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oribi_Gorge)).[^cite_note-5]

## Formation



Kevo Canyon in [Utsjoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utsjoki), [Finland](10577.md)

Most canyons were formed by a process of long-time [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) from a [plateau](538005.md) or table-land level. The [cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff) form because harder [rock strata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_strata) that are [resistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_\(geology\)) to erosion and weathering remain exposed on the valley walls.

Canyons are much more common in arid areas than in wet areas because physical weathering has a more localized effect in arid zones. The wind and water from the river combine to erode and cut away less resistant materials such as [shales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale). The freezing and expansion of water also serves to help form canyons. Water seeps into cracks between the rocks and freezes, pushing the rocks apart and eventually causing large chunks to break off the canyon walls, in a process known as [frost wedging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost_wedging).[^cite_note-6] Canyon walls are often formed of resistant [sandstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) or [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite).



[Snake River Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_River_Canyon_\(Idaho\)), [Idaho](14607.md)

Sometimes large rivers run through canyons as the result of gradual geological uplift. These are called [entrenched rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrenched_river), because they are unable to easily alter their course. In the United States, the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_\(U.S.\)) in the [Southwest](179571.md) and the [Snake River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_River) in the [Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_United_States) are two examples of [tectonic uplift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_uplift).

Canyons often form in areas of [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) rock. As limestone is soluble to a certain extent, [cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave) systems form in the rock. When a cave system collapses, a canyon is left, as in the [Mendip Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendip_Hills) in [Somerset](51763.md) and [Yorkshire Dales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales) in [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire), England.

### Box canyon

For the Colorado canyon, see [Box Canyon, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_Canyon,_Colorado).

See also: [Box canyon (aviation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_canyon_\(aviation\))

A **box canyon** is a small canyon that is generally shorter and narrower than a river canyon, with steep walls on three sides, allowing access and egress only through the mouth of the canyon. Box canyons were frequently used in the [western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States) as convenient corrals, with their entrances fenced.[^cite_note-encarta1-7]

## Largest

The definition of "largest canyon" is imprecise, because a canyon can be large by its depth, its length, or the total area of the canyon system. Also, the inaccessibility of the major canyons in the [Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya) contributes to them not being regarded as candidates for the biggest canyon. The definition of "deepest canyon" is similarly imprecise, especially if one includes both mountain canyons (which do not have a well-defined rim elevation) and canyons cut through relatively flat [plateaus](538005.md) (which have a somewhat well-defined rim elevation).

The [Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarlung_Tsangpo_Grand_Canyon) (or Tsangpo Canyon), along the [Yarlung Tsangpo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarlung_Tsangpo) in [Tibet, China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region), is regarded by some as the deepest canyon on Earth at 5,500 metres (18,000 ft). It is slightly longer than [Grand Canyon](46989.md) in the United States.[^cite_note-8] Others consider the [Kali Gandaki Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Gandaki_Gorge) in midwest Nepal to be the deepest canyon, with a 6,400-metre (21,000 ft) difference between the level of the river and the peaks surrounding it.

Vying for the deepest canyon in the Americas is the [Cotahuasi Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotahuasi_Canyon) and [Colca Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colca_Canyon), in southern Peru. Both have been measured at over 3,500 metres (11,500 ft) deep.

[Grand Canyon](46989.md) of northern [Arizona](21883824.md) in the United States, with an average depth of 1,600 metres (5,200 ft) and a volume of 4.17 trillion cubic metres (147 trillion cubic feet),[^cite_note-9] is one of the world's largest canyons. It was among the 28 finalists of the [New 7 Wonders of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New7Wonders_of_Nature) worldwide poll. (Some referred to it as one of the [seven natural wonders of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonders_of_the_World#Seven_Natural_Wonders_of_the_World).)[^cite_note-10]

The largest canyon in Europe is [Tara River Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_River_Canyon), \~1,300 metres (4,300 ft) deep.

The largest canyon in Africa is the [Fish River Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_River_Canyon) in [Namibia](21292.md), \~550 metres (1,800 ft) deep.[^cite_note-birdfinder-11]

In August 2013, the discovery of [Greenland](12118.md)'s [Grand Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canyon_\(Greenland\)) was reported, based on the analysis of data from [Operation IceBridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_IceBridge). It is located under an ice sheet. At 750 kilometres (470 mi) long, it is believed to be the longest canyon in the world.[^cite_note-12]

Despite not being quite as deep or long as the Grand Canyon, the [Capertee Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capertee_Valley) in Australia is actually 1 km wider than the Grand Canyon, making it the widest canyon in the world.[^cite_note-MyUser_Dailytelegraph.com_March_28_2016c-13][^cite_note-MyUser_Abc.net_March_28_2016c-14]



Panoramic view of the [Capertee Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capertee_Valley) in Australia, the widest largest canyon in the world

## Cultural significance

Some canyons have notable cultural significance. Evidence of [archaic humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_humans) has been discovered in Africa's [Olduvai Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olduvai_Gorge). In the southwestern United States, canyons are important [archeologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archeology) because of the many [cliff-dwellings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff-dwelling) built in such areas, largely by the [ancient Pueblo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Pueblo_people) who were their first inhabitants.
