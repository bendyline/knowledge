The **Faroese People's Party – Radical Self-Government** ([Faroese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroese_language): *Hin føroyski fólkaflokkurin – radikalt sjálvstýri*) is a pro-[Faroese independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroese_independence) [conservative](6675.md)[^cite_note-Bergqvist1999-6] and [conservative-liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_liberalism)[^cite_note-Lansford2014-7] [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_on_the_Faroe_Islands) on the [Faroe Islands](17238607.md)[^cite_note-Vaclav_Brachtl-8] led by Beinir Johannesen. Founded in 1939 by defectors from the [Self-Government Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Government_Party_\(Faroe_Islands\)) and former members of the [Business Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Party_\(Faroe_Islands\)) (Vinnuflokkurin),[^cite_note-9] the party has traditionally supported greater autonomy for the Faroe Islands. Party leader [Hákun Djurhuus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A1kun_Djurhuus) served as [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_Faroe_Islands) from 1963 to 1967, as did [Jógvan Sundstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3gvan_Sundstein) from 1989 to 1991. In 1998, it adopted a policy that supports full independence from [Denmark](76972.md) as part of a coalition deal in which leader [Anfinn Kallsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anfinn_Kallsberg) became PM. Throughout the decades, it has formed coalition governments with all Faroese political parties except for [Framsókn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_\(Faroe_Islands\)), with its latest term in government lasting until 2022.[^cite_note-10]

The party was a member of the [European Conservatives and Reformists Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Conservatives_and_Reformists_Party) until 2022. The party is affiliated to the [International Democrat Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Democrat_Union).

## History



Former party logo

The party was founded in 1939 as *Vinnuflokkurin*.[^cite_note-Options_for_Independence-11] The party split from the [Self-Government Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Government_Party_\(Faroe_Islands\)) over [land reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform),[^cite_note-12] and maintained a policy of [economic liberalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalisation) and [social conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism), with the party's support based in the fishing industry and private business.[^cite_note-Options_for_Independence-11] The party's economic programme was one of exploitation of local resources to reduce dependence on Denmark, and success of the [Sjóvinnubankin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sj%C3%B3vinnubankin?action=edit\&redlink=1) was utilised by the party to demonstrate that the Faroes could be economically self-sustaining. The party was given its current name in 1940.[^cite_note-Options_for_Independence-11] In the [1943 Faroese election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1943_Faroese_general_election), the party won 12 out of 25 seats: one short of an overall majority.[^cite_note-13]

The People's Party entered a coalition government with the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Faroe_Islands\)) in 1990, breaking the cycle of centre-right and centre-left coalitions.[^cite_note-Love_146-14] The party withdrew in 1993, being replaced by left-wing parties. In the [1994 Faroese election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Faroese_parliamentary_election), the party lost over a quarter of its vote, remaining outside government. However, it did return in 1996, this time with the [Union Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Party_\(Faroe_Islands\)), the [Self-Government Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Government_Party_\(Faroe_Islands\)), and the [Labour Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Front_\(Faroe_Islands\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Love_146-14]

In the [election in 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Faroese_parliamentary_election), the party bounced back to its pre-1994 position, and entered into a cross-spectrum coalition with the Republican Party and the Self-Government Party,[^cite_note-15] under which the People's Party adopted a policy of seeking independence. The independence plan failed in 2001, after Denmark threatened to cut economic assistance earlier than anticipated. In the [following year's election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Faroese_parliamentary_election), the party remained on 21% of the vote, and stayed in a renewed coalition that also included the [Centre Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_Party_\(Faroe_Islands\)).[^cite_note-16]

When chairman [Anfinn Kallsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anfinn_Kallsberg) decided not to run for re-election, a new election was slated. There were two candidates, former minister of Fishery, [Jørgen Niclasen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B8rgen_Niclasen), and current minister of Industry, [Bjarni Djurholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjarni_Djurholm?action=edit\&redlink=1). The election on 2 August 2007 gave Jørgen Niclasen the majority of the votes, making him the new party chairman. In the [Danish parliamentary elections of 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Danish_parliamentary_election) the party received 20.5% of the Faroese vote (down from the 24.1% it had won in 2005) and lost the seat it had previously held in the Danish national [Folketing](429743.md). At the [2008 Faroese election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Faroese_parliamentary_election), the party won 20.1% of the popular vote and seven out of 33 seats.

In [early elections in 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Faroese_parliamentary_election), the party won eight seats. In 2013, [Janus Rein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janus_Rein?action=edit\&redlink=1), who was elected for Progress, joined the Peoples Party after being a member of the Løgting without any political membership for eleven months.[^cite_note-17] After this event, the Peoples Party has nine of the 33 members of the Løgting.

At the general election 2015, the party lost two seats, they got 18.9% of the votes and six members. Eight days after the election, [Annika Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annika_Olsen) who had received 961 personal votes, left the People's Party, which means that the party lost one member and now has five parliament members.[^cite_note-Annika_Olsen-18] On 4 February 2016 she became a member of the People's Party again.

Generally, the party is [liberal conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_conservatism).[^cite_note-Vaclav_Brachtl-8] In economics, the party is supportive of the [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism).[^cite_note-19]

The party supports Faroese independence from Denmark. It is one of two major parties (along with [Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_\(Faroe_Islands\))) whose primary concern was historically the constitutional issue, rather than economics.[^cite_note-20]
