**Antioquia** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[anˈtjokja\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/4d/ES-pe\_-\_Antioquia.ogg/ES-pe\_-\_Antioquia.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/4d/ES-pe_-_Antioquia.ogg/ES-pe_-_Antioquia.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is one of the 32 [departments of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia), located in the central northwestern part of Colombia with a narrow section that borders the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md). Most of its territory is [mountainous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountainous) with some [valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley), much of which is part of the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) mountain range. Antioquia has been part of many territorial divisions of former countries created within the present-day territory of Colombia. Before the adoption of the [Colombian Constitution of 1886](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1886), [Antioquia State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioquia_State) had a sovereign government.

The department covers an area of 63,612 km<sup>2</sup> (24,561 sq mi), and has a population of 6,994,792 (2023). Antioquia borders the [Córdoba department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba_department) and the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) to the north; [Chocó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choc%C3%B3_department) to the west; the departments of [Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%ADvar_department), [Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_department), and [Boyaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyac%C3%A1_department) to the east; and the departments of [Caldas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldas_Department) and [Risaralda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risaralda_Department) to the south.

[Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn) is Antioquia's capital and the second-largest city in the country. Other important towns are [Rionegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rionegro) located in the [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Antioquia), [Apartadó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartad%C3%B3) on the [Caribbean Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_region_of_Colombia), [Santa Fe de Antioquia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_de_Antioquia), the old capital on the [Cauca River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauca_River), and [Puerto Berrío](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Berr%C3%ADo) on the [Magdalena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_River).

## Geography



[Guatapé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatap%C3%A9) reservoir

Antioquia is the [sixth-largest department of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Colombian_departments_by_area). It is predominantly mountainous, crossed by the [Cordillera Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Colombia\)) and the [Cordillera Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Occidental_\(Colombia\)) of the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes). The [Cordillera Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Colombia\)) divides to form the [Aburrá valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aburr%C3%A1_Valley), in which the capital, [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn), is located. The [Cordillera Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Colombia\)) forms the plateaus of [Santa Rosa de Osos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Rosa_de_Osos) and [Rionegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rionegro,_Antioquia).

While 80% of the department's territory is mountainous, Antioquia also has lowlands in [Bajo Cauca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajo_Cauca), [Magdalena Medio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Medio_Antioquia), and eastern [Sonsón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons%C3%B3n), as well as coastline on the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md), in [Urabá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urab%C3%A1_Antioquia).[^cite_note-6] This area has a tropical climate and is of high strategic importance due to its location.

## Toponymy



[La Ceja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ceja,_Antioquia)

The basis for the name Antioquia is uncertain. A common explanation is that it was named after the [Hellenistic-founded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_Greece) city in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_\(region\)) of [Antioch on the Orontes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch_on_the_Orontes) ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Ἀντιόχεια *Antiocheia*, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic): *Antāqiyyah*, now officially [Antakya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antakya), [Turkey](11125639.md)). The region of Colombia known as the Coffee Zone has a strong influence from [Arab Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Jews), both demographically and culturally, and many towns and cities are named after cities in the Middle East. Moreover, ancient Antioch played a significant role in developing the communities of [early Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity) and thus was important to the Catholic Spaniard conquistadors.

There are many other cities in the Middle East named [Antiochia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochia_\(disambiguation\)), which were likewise named for the [Antiochid rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochus_\(disambiguation\)#The_Seleucid_Empire) during the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) (312–63 BC).

## History

### Native people of Antioquia

See also: [Columbian exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_exchange)

Before Spanish colonization, [Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_South_America) inhabited the Antioquia region. Some specialists believe they came from [Caribbean Sea](7822.md), while others say they came from the interior [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River).

Antioquia was primarily populated by the [Kalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalina_people) or "Mainland Caribs." Some scattered groups of [Muisca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca) were said to be present in the [Darién](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Province) region, a coastal region in the far north that is now in [Panama](22997.md), but no historical records refer to Muisca in Antioquia.

The Kalina in Antioquia comprised smaller groups called families. The most prominent were the Catía, [Nutabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutabe), and [Tahamí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taham%C3%AD_people), who inhabited central Antioquia. The [Quimbaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quimbaya) occupied southern Antioquia.

The Quimbaya, Kalina, and Muisca were the most prominent groups the [conquistadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador) encountered upon their arrival in Antioquia.

The Spaniards had a turbulent history of encounters with the Carib. Although the tribe was numerous and known for its warring culture, the Spaniards dominated or exterminated them in the process of [conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_\(military\)) and [colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization). Like all [indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), many died in [virgin soil epidemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_soil_epidemic) of diseases endemic to Eurasia.

Some surviving natives dispersed to evade the Spanish, and some committed suicide to escape [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery). Many survivors fled to what is now [Chocó department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choc%C3%B3_department). Barely 0.5% of Antioquia's population is Indigenous, but most residents have a significant Indigenous genetic component (26% on average).[^cite_note-7]

### Basque and Jewish influence in Antioquia

[Spain](26667.md)'s [Basque Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Country_\(greater_region\)) and [Jews](25955086.md) have influenced Antioquia and its language since the Spanish colonization. This began in 1499 during the [third voyage of Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages_of_Christopher_Columbus). The owner and master of the *[Santa María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_\(ship\))*, second in command to Columbus, was [Juan de la Cosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_la_Cosa), nicknamed *el Vizcaino* ("the [Biscayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscay)"). Hundreds of [Basques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basques) settled in the area, sponsored by the Spanish colonization companies.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

#### Dialect

Immigrants influenced the Spanish spoken in Antioquia in several ways:

- Immigrants from [Andalusia](2736.md) and the [Canary Islands](5717.md) brought their preference for *[seseo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seseo)*, a uniform pronunciation of "s", "z", and soft "c".

- However, those from [Extremadura](68204.md) favored an [apico-alveolar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_alveolar_fricative#Voiceless_apico-alveolar_sibilant) sound (part way to "sh").

- The [digraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digraph_\(orthography\)) "ll" (double L) is [affricative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affricate), like English "j".

- Certain words beginning with "r" are [prefixed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epenthesis) by "a" (with the "r" doubled to continue to indicate that it is trilled):&#x20;

  - *arrecostarse* instead of *recostarse*

  - *arrecoger* instead of *recoger*

  - *arrecordarse* instead of *recordarse*.

- Specific words have been adopted, such as *coscorria* (useless, inept) and *tap* (tap).

  [^cite_note-10]

#### Local character

It has been asserted since the mid-nineteenth century that Basque and Jewish origins have influenced the character of Antioquia. Specific evidence of cultural contribution is sparse, perhaps partly because Basque is not dominant in Spain.[^cite_note-11] However, American historian Everett Hagen sampled the Medellín telephone directory in 1957 and found that 15% of the surnames were of Basque origin, but 25% of the employers were. He concluded that Basque influence was important in explaining the increased industrial development of Antioquia in Colombia. The study reinforced notions of the character of social groups, though it has been criticized as stereotypical.

### Spaniards in Antioquia

The first Spaniard known to have visited the territory now known as Antioquia was [Rodrigo de Bastidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_de_Bastidas), who explored the area around the future site of [Darién](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Province) in 1500. Ten years later, [Alonso de Ojeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonso_de_Ojeda) founded [San Sebastián de Urabá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_de_Urab%C3%A1), 2 km from the present-day town of [Necoclí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necocl%C3%AD). It was later destroyed by the natives. The first Spanish military incursion into Antioquia, however, was not made until 1537. An expedition commanded by [Francisco César](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_C%C3%A9sar) traveled through the lands of chief Dabeiba, arriving at the [Cauca River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauca_River). They were said to have taken important treasures from the indigenous people's tombs. In response, the warriors of chief Nutibara harassed the Spaniards continually, and forced them to return to [Urabá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urab%C3%A1_Antioquia).

In 1541, the conquistador [Jorge Robledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Robledo_\(conquistador\)) departed from the site of the future (1542) Spanish town of Arma, a little below Aguadas in the North of Caldas, to lead an expedition north on the Cauca River.

Farther north, Robledo would found the city of [Santa Fe de Antioquia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_de_Antioquia), which in 1813 was declared the capital of the sovereign and independent state of Antioquia, and remained the seat of the governate until 1826, when Medellín was designated the capital.

### 16th to the 21st centuries

Due to its geographical isolation, being located among mountains, Antioquia suffered supply problems. Its topography did not allow for much agriculture, so the city became dependent upon trade, especially of gold and gin for the colonization of new land. Much of this trade was due to reforms passed after a 1785 visit from [Juan Antonio Mon y Velarde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Antonio_Mon_y_Velarde?action=edit\&redlink=1), an inspector of the Spanish Crown. The Antioquia became colonizers and traders.

The department was hard hit by the [Colombian conflict](1857628.md), with 30,000 people missing between 1997 and 2005.[^cite_note-12]

*[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* and Citi announced in the year 2013 that [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn), the capital of the department of Antioquia, is the winner of the City of the Year competition, a global program developed in partnership with the [Urban Land Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Land_Institute) to recognize the most innovative urban centers. Medellín was ranked above the other finalists, [Tel Aviv](31453.md) and [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The population of Antioquia is 6,994,792 (2023), of which more than half live in the metropolitan area of [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn). The racial composition is:[^cite_note-17]

- [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people) / [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) (88.6%)

- [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people) or [Afro-Colombian](7941778.md) (10.9%)

- [Indigenous](4053513.md) or [Amerindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerindian) (0.5%)

During the 16th and 18th centuries, Antioquia received many immigrants from Spain, especially [northern Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Spain).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Most Indigenous peoples died from the introduction of European diseases, and many of those who survived intermarried with early Spanish settlers, who were mostly men; later, Spanish women also began to immigrate. Thousands of Scottish and English who settled in Antioquia fought for the Colombian army during independence.[^cite_note-20] During the 19th and 20th centuries, immigrants (including Jews)[^cite_note-21] arrived from Italy, Germany, United Kingdom, France, Portugal, Lebanon, Israel, Palestine and Syria.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Many people from Antioquia are referred to as [Paisas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paisa_Region), people of mainly Spanish ancestry, a lot of them Basque.[^cite_note-25] There is a small Afro-Colombian and Zambo-Colombian (people of Indigenous and African descent) population originating in the majority of the [Urabá subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urab%C3%A1_Antioquia) and the neighboring departments of [Chocó](549667.md), [Córdoba](549670.md) and [Sucre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucre_Department).
