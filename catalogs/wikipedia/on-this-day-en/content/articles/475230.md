**Chelyabinsk Oblast**[^cite_note-7] is a [federal subject](701438.md) (an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)) of [Russia](25391.md) in the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains) region, on the border of [Europe](9239.md) and [Asia](689.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Its [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) is the [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia) of [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk).

## History

During the Middle Ages, [Bashkir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirs) tribes inhabited the Southern [Urals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(region\)); they formed part of the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde), [Nogai Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogai_Horde), and smaller Bashkir unions. The [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia) [incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Siberia) the area in the late 16th century. However, Russian colonization of the region only began in the 18th century, with the establishment of a system of fortresses and trade posts on the then-Russian border by the [Orenburg Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orenburg_Expedition?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1734. Many cities of Chelyabinsk Oblast, including the city of Chelyabinsk itself, trace their history back to those forts.

In 1743 the [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk) fortress became a center of the [Iset Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iset_Province?action=edit\&redlink=1), a constituent part of the [Orenburg Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orenburg_Governorate) (a direct successor of the Orenburg Expedition). The period from the 1750s to the 1770s saw the emergence of industrial enterprises in the Southern Urals when the first factory-centered towns like [Miass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miass), [Kyshtym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyshtym), and [Zlatoust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatoust) were founded. After the Southern Urals recovered from the [Pugachev's Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pugachev's_Rebellion) of 1773–1775, the territory of modern-day Chelyabinsk Oblast started to attract more people from the European part of Russia. By the mid-19th century Chelyabinsk was a major trade center in the Urals, and after the construction of the [Trans-Siberian Railway](56912.md) in the 1890s, it became an important transport hub that connected [Siberia](29639.md) to the rest of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).

In 1919, Chelyabinsk became the regional capital of the newly formed [Chelyabinsk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk_Governorate?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Socialist_Federative_Soviet_Republic), which combined eastern portions of the [Orenburg Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orenburg_Governorate) with [Kurgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan,_Kurgan_Oblast) of the [Tobolsk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobolsk_Governorate). At this time, the population of the new region has already exceeded one million people. In 1923, together with the [Perm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_Governorate), [Yekaterinburg Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg_Governorate?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Tyumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobolsk_Governorate) governorates, it merged into a single [Ural Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Oblast_\(1923%E2%80%931934\)) that lasted only ten years, until 1934. On 17 January 1934, Chelyabinsk Oblast was finally established. Its current boundaries were formed when [Kurgan Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan_Oblast) was detached from it in 1943.

### Soviet industrialization

During the 1930s the regional economy and industrial output grew as Chelyabinsk Oblast became a key focus of the [First Five-Year Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_five-year_plan_\(Soviet_Union\)). Key factories and enterprises that formed the core of the modern Chelyabinsk economy, including the [Magnitogorsk Iron and Steel Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitogorsk_Iron_and_Steel_Works), the [Chelyabinsk Tractor Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk_Tractor_Plant) and the [Chelyabinsk Metallurgical Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk_Metallurgical_Plant?action=edit\&redlink=1), originated at this time. The economy continued to grow after the outbreak of the [Great Patriotic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)) in 1941, as industries evacuated from the western parts of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) to the [Urals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(region\)), and to Chelyabinsk Oblast in particular. During the war, [Magnitogorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitogorsk) alone produced one third of all Soviet steel, while the city of Chelyabinsk became the main center of Soviet tank production, earning the nickname "Tankograd" (Tank City).

### Nuclear research

Chelyabinsk Oblast has been home to top-secret nuclear research since the 1940s. While there are no nuclear power stations in Chelyabinsk, a number of production reactors were located there starting with the early Cold War. A [serious nuclear accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyshtym_disaster) occurred in 1957 at the [Mayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayak) [nuclear fuel reprocessing plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fuel_reprocessing_plant), 150 km (93 mi) north-west of the city, which led to evacuations and fatalities throughout the oblast, although not in Chelyabinsk city. The province was [closed to all foreigners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_city) until 1992, with the sole exception of allowing a British medical team in following a two-train rail explosion in the mid-1980s.

[Sławomir Grünberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82awomir_Gr%C3%BCnberg) has made the documentary *Chelyabinsk: The Most Contaminated Spot on the Planet* (1994) about the unsafe dumping of [radioactive waste](37257.md) in the [Techa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techa_River) and in [Lake Karachay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Karachay).

### Recent history

On 4 July 1997, Chelyabinsk, alongside [Bryansk](476473.md), [Magadan](480997.md), [Saratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov_Oblast), and [Vologda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vologda_Oblast) signed a power-sharing agreement with the government of Russia, granting it autonomy.[^cite_note-12] The agreement would be abolished on 2 February 2002.[^cite_note-13]

On 15 February 2013, a [10,000 ton meteoroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk_meteorite) entered the Earth's atmosphere over Russia at about 09\:20 [YEKT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YEKT) (03\:20 UTC). It passed over the southern Ural region and [exploded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk_meteor) in a [meteor air burst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor_air_burst) over Chelyabinsk Oblast. About 1,500 people were reported injured, including 311 children. Health officials said 112 people had been hospitalized, mainly from injuries caused by glass from windows shattered by a shock wave; two were reported to be in serious condition. As many as 3,000 buildings in six cities across the region were damaged by the explosion and impacts. The meteor created a dazzling light as it air burst, bright enough to cast shadows during broad daylight in Chelyabinsk.

## Geography

Chelyabinsk Oblast is at the eastern end of the slope just under the Southern Urals, and the other part of the territory to the west is on the western slopes of the Southern Urals.

Chelyabinsk Oblast is situated in the Southern Urals, near [Kurgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan_Oblast) and [Sverdlovsk oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverdlovsk_oblast). Most of the Oblast is located at the eastern end of the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains), which form the continental [boundary between Europe and Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_between_Asia_and_Europe). This boundary is marked by a stone pillar. In the Sverdlovsk Oblast, north of Chelyabinsk, there is a famous pillar separating the two continents, which has "Europe" written on one side and "Asia" on the other. In Chelyabinsk Oblast, [Zlatoust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatoust) city, [Katav-Ivanovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katav-Ivanovsk), [Satka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satka), [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk), [Troitsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troitsk), and [Miass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miass) are in Europe. [Magnitogorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitogorsk) is located on both continents.[^cite_note-14]

The area of Chelyabinsk Oblast is 88,900 km<sup>2</sup> (34,300 sq mi).[^cite_note-\:0-15] The total length of its external border is 2,750 km (1,710 mi), and the Oblast measures 400 km (250 mi) from north to south and 490 km (300 mi) from west to east.

The highest point of Chelyabinsk Oblast, reaching 1,406 metres (4,613 ft) above sea level, is located in the [Nurgush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurgush), a 50 kilometres (31 mi) long mountain range rising near lake [Zyuratkul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zyuratkul).[^cite_note-16]

It also borders the country of [Kazakhstan](16642.md), specifically the [Kostanay Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostanay_Region).

### Relief

Chelyabinsk Oblast has a very diverse landscape, ranging from lowlands and hilly plains to mountain ranges with peaks exceeding 1,000 m, including [Nurgush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurgush) mountain (1406 m). The mountainous area has several [ski resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_resorts).

The [West Siberian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Siberian_Plain) is bounded on the west horizontal (elevation 190 m above sea level), which passes through the village of Bagaryak, Kunashak and continues through [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk) to the south. The lowlands are located in the northeast, and the elevation drops to 130 m in the eastern border region.

### Hydrology

Numerous rivers originate within the region, within the basins of the [Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_River), [Tobol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobol), and [Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_river) rivers. The region is home to 348 rivers longer than 10 km (6.2 mi) (totaling 10,235 km (6,360 mi) in length), 17 of which are over 100 km (62 mi) in length. Seven rivers, the [Miass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miass_River), [Uy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uy_River_\(Tobol_basin\)), [Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_River), [Ay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ay_River), Ufa, Uvelka, and Gumbeyka, pass through the area and are longer than 200 km (124 mi).



[Lake Itkul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Itkul)

Chelyabinsk Oblast is also home to more than 3,748 lakes, mostly located in the north and east and covering a total area of 2,125 km<sup>2</sup> (820 sq mi). Many of the lakes in this area, including [Lake Turgoyak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Turgoyak), [Zyuratkul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zyuratkul), and [Lake Itkul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Itkul), are famous for their clear waters and attract tourism. Some of the lakes in the eastern foothills have tectonic origins as water accumulated in tectonic failures (basins), resulting in very deep lakes that can reach 30–40 m (98–131 ft).

## Politics



Seat of the Oblast government in Chelyabinsk



Governor's residence

During the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) period, the highest authority in the Oblast was shared between three positions: the First Secretary of the Chelyabinsk [CPSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPSU) Committee (who held the most power), the Chairman of the Oblast [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_\(council\)) (legislative power), and the Chairman of the Oblast Executive Committee (executive power). Since the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991, the CPSU lost its monopoly on power.

Today, the Charter of Chelyabinsk Oblast governs the political structure of the region. The [Legislative Assembly of Chelyabinsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Chelyabinsk_Oblast) serves as the province's [regional parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_parliaments_of_Russia) and exercises legislative authority, with the power to pass laws, resolutions, and other legal acts and oversee their implementation and observance. The Oblast Government, led by the [Governor of Chelyabinsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Chelyabinsk_Oblast), is the highest executive body in the region, and includes territorial executive bodies such as district administrations, committees, and commissions that facilitate development and run the day-to-day matters of the province.

## Sights

### Taganay National Park

Taganay National Park is located northeast of the city of Zlatoust, Chelyabinsk Oblast. Taganay National Park is a popular tourist destination in the Urals. The park contains mountain ranges, alpine meadows, stone outcrops and a several kilometer stone river, forests, woodlands and mountain tundra, ancient mineral mines and mountain rivers flowing both to Europe and Asia. Taganay National Park was established on 5 March 1991, the first in the Urals.

### Gagarin Park

Gagarin Central Park is a 12-hectare (30-acre) recreational space in Chelyabinsk. The park is named after [Yuri Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin), a Soviet [cosmonaut](664.md) and the first person to enter space. The park contains forest walks, lakes, old quarries, and landscaped gardens. There is also a showground with rides.

### Monuments

There are several monuments in Chelyabinsk, many of which are on Kirovka street, a pedestrian street in the center of Chelyabinsk. The monuments include a monument to [Igor Kurchatov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Kurchatov), a nuclear scientist, which opened in 1986 to the 250th anniversary of Chelyabinsk; a monument to Orlenok, on the Aloe polye in Chelyabinsk, which opened on 29 October 1958 on the day of the fortieth anniversary of the [Komsomol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komsomol); the Sculpture of the Postman; the Memorial to Law and Order Soldiers; the Monument to Soldiers-Internationalists; and a sculpture of a firefighter.
