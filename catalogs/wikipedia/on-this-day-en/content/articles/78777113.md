**Kazem Gharibabadi** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): کاظم غریب‌آبادی; born 1974)[^cite_note-1] is an [Iranian](14653.md) diplomat and legal expert, who has served as his country's Deputy Foreign Minister for Legal and International Affairs since September 2024. He has previously held a number of senior roles in Iran's legal and diplomatic institutions, including as a key representative in discussions concerning [Iran's nuclear program](721807.md).[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Biography

### Education

Gharibabadi holds an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in diplomacy and international organizations and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in [public law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_law), both from [Allameh Tabataba'i University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allameh_Tabataba'i_University).[^cite_note-MinistryBio-5]

### Career (1996–2024)

From 1996 Gharibabadi held a number of positions relating to foreign affairs, including within the [Ministry of Defence](18379483.md) and the [Supreme National Security Council](6295948.md). He joined the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in 2005, when he was appointed Secretary of Iran's Nuclear Diplomacy Task Force and advisor to the foreign minister on nuclear affairs. Between 2009 and 2013 Gharibabadi served as Iran's Ambassador to the Netherlands, and as Iran's permanent representative to various international organizations based in [The Hague](30269.md). From 2014 he held several roles within the [High Council for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Council_for_Human_Rights),[^cite_note-MinistryBio-5] playing a key role in advocating for Iran's [human rights](13831.md) positions on the international stage.[^cite_note-\:0-2]

From 2015, Gharibabadi served as Secretary of the [Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_nuclear_deal) (JCPOA) Committee within the Supreme National Security Council. He remained active within Iran's nuclear diplomacy with his appointment as ambassador and permanent representative to various international organizations in [Vienna](55866.md), including the [International Atomic Energy Agency](14984.md) (IAEA), in July 2018.[^cite_note-MinistryBio-5][^cite_note-\:0-2] In early 2021 Gharibabadi accompanied foreign minister [Abbas Araghchi](21951274.md) to talks in Vienna aimed at reviving the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action. After these attempts stalled, Gharibabadi criticized the draft agreement negotiated by Araghchi in an interview with supreme leader [Ali Khamenei](385653.md)'s website.[^cite_note-Davar-6]

After leaving this position in October 2020 Gharibabadi held various high roles within the Iranian [Judiciary](66501437.md),[^cite_note-MinistryBio-5] including as Vice-President, and was appointed Secretary of the High Council for Human Rights. In these roles, he set out to defend the Iranian government's [crackdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahsa_Amini_protests#Casualties,_government_response) against the [Mahsa Amini protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahsa_Amini_protests) in 2022 from international criticism, including at meetings of the [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md).[^cite_note-Davar-6]

Gharibabadi appeared alongside [Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf](1421458.md) during the latter's presidential campaign ahead of the [2024 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Iranian_presidential_election), giving rise to rumors of Gharibabadi as a potential foreign minister under Ghalibaf.[^cite_note-Davar-6]

### Deputy Foreign Minister (since 2024)

Gharibabadi was appointed Deputy Foreign Minister for Legal and International Affairs in September 2024,[^cite_note-\:0-2] making him one of the top negotiators within Iran's nuclear diplomacy.[^cite_note-7] In December 2024, he met with Chinese Vice Foreign Minister [Ma Zhaoxu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Zhaoxu) in [Beijing](18603746.md) to discuss bilateral relations and Iran's nuclear program.[^cite_note-8] On 1 January 2025, he stated that Iran would hold a round of nuclear talks with [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain), [Germany](11867.md) and [France](5843419.md) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), Switzerland, on January 13 of that year.[^cite_note-9]

In April 2025, during [Iran–United States negotiations](79685207.md) held in [Oman](22316.md), Gharibabadi was accused on [online social media](2041117.md) of accidentally placing a gold-plated pen provided by the Omani administration in his pocket. The Iranian ambassador to Oman, Musa Farhang, denied the claim. Reports seen by *[Iran International](58555280.md)* suggested that the pen was valued at [US\$](18717338.md)14,000, that Gharibabadi's action had been recorded on video, and that he returned the pen.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Personal life

Gharibabadi is the son-in-law of [Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr](54308220.md), a former [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) general serving as secretary of the [Supreme National Security Council](6295948.md) since 2026.[^cite_note-Davar-6]

## Legal case

In late February 2026, a [universal jurisdiction](48640.md) case was filed in [Switzerland](26748.md) against Gharibabadi for [crimes against humanity](61099.md) during the [Iranian government crackdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahsa_Amini_protests#Casualties,_government_response) against the [Mahsa Amini protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahsa_Amini_protests). Lawyers [William Bourdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bourdon) and Philippine Vaganay called for Gharibabadi to be arrested if he visited Switzerland.[^cite_note-IranIntl_IR_deputy_FM_faces_CAH_complain-12]
