**Monero** ([/məˈnɛroʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); [abbreviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_4217#Cryptocurrencies): **XMR**) is a [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain)-based [cryptocurrency](36662188.md) which is [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_privacy_technologies), [untraceable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_tracing), [fungible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungibility), and [decentralized](49139.md).[^cite_note-3]

The protocol is [open source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_source) and based on [CryptoNote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CryptoNote) v2, a concept described in a 2013 [white paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_paper) authored by Nicolas van Saberhagen. Developers used this concept to design Monero, and deployed its [mainnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain#Testnet) in 2014. The Monero protocol includes various methods to obfuscate transaction details, though users can optionally share view keys for third-party auditing.[^cite_note-4] Transactions are validated through a [miner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_mining) network running RandomX, a [proof-of-work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof-of-work) algorithm. The algorithm issues new coins to miners and was designed to be resistant against [application-specific integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application-specific_integrated_circuit) (ASIC) mining.

Monero's privacy features have attracted [cypherpunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypherpunk) and users desiring privacy measures not provided in other cryptocurrencies. A 2022 study in *FSI Digital Investigations* concluded "For now, Monero is untraceable. However, it is probably only a matter of time and effort before it changes."[^cite_note-5] Due to its perceived untraceability Monero is gaining increased use in illicit activities such as [money laundering](19390.md), [darknet markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darknet_market), [ransomware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransomware), [cryptojacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptojacking), and other [organized crime](22625.md).

## Background

Monero's roots trace back to [CryptoNote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CryptoNote) v2, a [cryptocurrency](36662188.md) protocol first introduced in a [white paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_paper) published by the presumed pseudonymous Nicolas van Saberhagen in October 2013.[^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6] In the paper, the author described privacy and anonymity as "the most important aspects of electronic cash" and characterized [bitcoin](28249265.md)'s traceability as a "critical flaw".[^cite_note-Murphy-2021-7] A Bitcointalk forum user known as "*thankful_for_today*" implemented these ideas into a coin they called BitMonero. However, other forum users disagreed with *thankful_for_today'*&#x73; direction for BitMonero and decided to fork it in 2014, leading to the creation of Monero.[^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6] *Monero* translates to *coin* in [Esperanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanto).[^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6] The plural of monero ("moneroj") is likewise formed using [Esperanto grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanto_grammar#Nouns_and_adjectives). Both van Saberhagen and *thankful_for_today* remain anonymous.[^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6]

Monero has the third-largest community of developers, behind bitcoin and [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum).[^cite_note-Murphy-2021-7] The protocol's lead maintainer was previously South African developer Riccardo Spagni.[^cite_note-Melendez-2017-8] Much of the core development team chooses to remain anonymous.[^cite_note-Sigalos-2021-9]

## Privacy



[Ring signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_signatures) create ambiguity in blockchain analysis.

Monero's key features are those around privacy and anonymity.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6][^cite_note-Sigalos-2021-9] Even though it is a public and decentralized ledger, all transaction details are obfuscated.[^cite_note-explainer2-11] This contrasts to bitcoin, where all transaction details, user addresses, and wallet balances are public and transparent.[^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6][^cite_note-Sigalos-2021-9] These features have given Monero a loyal following among [crypto anarchists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto-anarchism), [cypherpunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypherpunk), and privacy advocates.[^cite_note-Murphy-2021-7]

The transaction outputs, or notes, of users sending Monero are obfuscated through [ring signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_signature), which groups a sender's outputs with other decoy outputs. Encryption of transaction amounts began in 2017 with the implementation of ring confidential transactions (RingCTs).[^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6][^cite_note-12] Developers also implemented a [zero-knowledge proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-knowledge_proof) method, "Bulletproofs", which guarantee a transaction occurred without revealing its value.[^cite_note-13] Monero recipients are protected through "stealth addresses", public keys generated by the sender that are untraceable to the receiver by a network observer.[^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6] These privacy features are enforced on the network by default.[^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6]

Monero uses Dandelion++, a protocol which obscures the [IP address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address) of devices producing transactions. This is done through a method of transaction broadcast propagation; new transactions are initially passed to one node on Monero's peer-to-peer network, and a repeated [probabilistic method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_method) is used to determine when the transaction should be sent to just one node or broadcast to many nodes in a process called flooding.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### Transaction tracing research

In April 2017, researchers highlighted three major threats to Monero users' privacy. The first relies on leveraging the ring signature size of zero, and ability to see the output amounts. The second, "Leveraging Output Merging", involves tracking transactions where two outputs belong to the same user, such as when they send funds to themselves ("churning"). Finally, "Temporal Analysis", shows that predicting the right output in a ring signature could potentially be easier than previously thought.[^cite_note-16] In 2018, researchers presented possible vulnerabilities in a paper titled "An Empirical Analysis of Traceability in the Monero Blockchain".[^cite_note-moser-17]

In September 2020, the United States [Internal Revenue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Service)'s [criminal investigation division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRS_Criminal_Investigation) (IRS-CI), posted a \$625,000 bounty for contractors who could develop tools to help trace Monero, other privacy-enhanced cryptocurrencies, the Bitcoin [Lightning Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_Network), or other "layer 2" protocol.[^cite_note-Franceschi-Bicchierai-2020-18][^cite_note-Murphy-2021-7] The contract was awarded to blockchain analysis groups [Chainalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chainalysis) and Integra FEC.[^cite_note-Murphy-2021-7]

In 2021, researchers presented a transaction-flooding attack against Monero’s transaction-graph privacy at the IEEE International Conference on Blockchain and Cryptocurrency. Under specific assumptions about transaction structure and fees, the "FloodXMR" attack modelled how an adversary who floods the blockchain with their own transactions could, over time, deanonymize a substantial fraction of new transaction inputs at relatively low cost.[^cite_note-19]

## Mining



Monero GUI running on a remote node

Monero uses a [proof-of-work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof-of-work) algorithm, RandomX, to validate transactions. The method was introduced in November 2019 to replace the former algorithm CryptoNightR.[^cite_note-r345-20] Both algorithms were designed to be resistant to ASIC mining, which is commonly used to mine other cryptocurrencies such as [bitcoin](28249265.md).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Monero can be mined somewhat efficiently on consumer-grade hardware such as [x86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86), [x86-64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86-64), [ARM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_architecture) and [GPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPU), a design decision which was based on Monero project's opposition to mining centralisation which ASIC mining creates,[^cite_note-vice-asic-23] but has also resulted in Monero's popularity among [malware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malware)-based non-consensual miners.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Use

Monero's privacy features have made it popular for illicit purposes.[^cite_note-Chainalysis-Use-26][^cite_note-explainer2-11][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

On-chain data from 2024 and 2025 shows that Monero’s transaction volumes remained significantly higher than in the 2020–2021 period, stabilizing at an elevated baseline that indicates sustained, regular demand rather than temporary speculation.[^cite_note-TRM-2025-Use-29] After a wave of [cryptocurrency exchange](3330391.md) delistings in 2024–2025, Monero trading migrated to [decentralized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized_exchange) and [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer) platforms, demonstrating resilient usage and ongoing demand.[^cite_note-TRM-2025-Use-29]

### Darknet markets

Monero's privacy features have made it the dominant cryptocurrency on [darknet markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darknet_market) (DNMs), where anonymity is critical for both buyers and sellers.[^cite_note-30] Unlike [Bitcoin](28249265.md), whose public [ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ledger) enables traceable [blockchain analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain_analysis), Monero obscures sender, receiver, and transaction amounts by default.[^cite_note-Chainalysis-glossary-31][^cite_note-32]

In August 2016, dark market [AlphaBay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaBay) permitted its vendors to start accepting Monero as an alternative to bitcoin.[^cite_note-WIREDFire2-6] The site was taken offline by law enforcement in 2017,[^cite_note-33] but it was relaunched in 2021 with Monero as the sole permitted currency.[^cite_note-WIRED-34]

A 2024 report covered by *[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_\(magazine\))* found that vendors of child sexual abuse material increasingly used Monero for laundering proceeds through instant exchangers, with blockchain analysis firm [Chainalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chainalysis) describing Monero as "the currency of choice" for this purpose.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

By 2025, nearly half of newly launched darknet markets operated exclusively with Monero, a sharp increase from earlier years, driven by law enforcement advances in tracing Bitcoin and [stablecoins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stablecoin).[^cite_note-TRM-2025-37] This trend has created a perception that markets relying solely on Bitcoin are less secure, pushing users toward Monero-only platforms that better protect against financial surveillance.[^cite_note-TRM-2025-37]

A prominent example was [White House Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Market), a darknet marketplace active from 2019 to 2021 that was used to traffic fentanyl and cocaine; the platform handled transactions in Monero and was referenced in multiple federal indictments.[^cite_note-DOJ-WHM-38] The market's closure did not halt the broader shift, as subsequent DNMs increasingly adopted Monero-only models that users deem more trustworthy because of the difficulty of tracing XMR payments.[^cite_note-TRM-2025-37]

The [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) and the [Treasury Department](53667.md) have identified Monero as a frequently used tool in darknet and ransomware-related money laundering, with sanctioned exchangers processing hundreds of millions of dollars in XMR.[^cite_note-39]

### Mining malware

See also: [Cryptojacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptojacking)

In late 2017, malware and antivirus service providers blocked Coinhive, a [JavaScript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JavaScript) implementation of a Monero miner that was embedded in websites and apps, in some cases by hackers. Coinhive wrote the script as an alternative to advertisements; a website or app could embed it, and use website visitors' [CPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU) to mine the cryptocurrency while the visitor is consuming the content of the webpage, with the site or app owner getting a percentage of the mined coins.[^cite_note-40] Some websites and apps did this without informing visitors, or in some cases using all possible system resources. As a result, the script was blocked by companies offering [ad blocking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_blocking) subscription lists, antivirus services, and antimalware services.[^cite_note-ArsCPU-41][^cite_note-ZDNetApps-42] Coinhive had been previously found hidden in *[Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_\(TV_network\))-*&#x6F;wned streaming platforms[^cite_note-43] and [Starbucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starbucks) Wi-Fi hotspots in Argentina.[^cite_note-Melendez-2017-8][^cite_note-44] Researchers in 2018 found similar malware that mined Monero and sent it to [Kim Il-sung University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Il-sung_University) in [North Korea](21255.md).[^cite_note-45]

### Ransomware



Ransomware deployed in 2021 by [REvil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/REvil). The hackers are demanding payment in Monero.

[^cite_note-46]

Monero is sometimes used by [ransomware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransomware) groups. According to [CNBC](223441.md), in the first half of 2018, Monero was used in 44% of cryptocurrency [ransomware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransomware) attacks.[^cite_note-cnbc-47]

The perpetrators of the 2017 [WannaCry ransomware attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WannaCry_ransomware_attack), which was attributed by the US government to North Korean threat actors,[^cite_note-48] attempted to exchange the ransom they collected in Bitcoin to Monero. *[Ars Technica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Technica)* and *[Fast Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Company)* reported that the exchange was successful,[^cite_note-Gallagher-2017-49][^cite_note-Melendez-2017-8] but [BBC News](1139893.md) reported that the service the criminals attempted to use, [ShapeShift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShapeShift), denied any such transfer.[^cite_note-50] [The Shadow Brokers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shadow_Brokers), who leaked the exploits which were subsequently used in WannaCry but are unlikely to have been involved in the attack, began accepting Monero as payment later in 2017.[^cite_note-Gallagher-2017-49]

In 2021, [CNBC](223441.md), the *[Financial Times](136566.md)*, and *[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek)* reported that demand for Monero was increasing following the recovery of a bitcoin ransom paid in the [Colonial Pipeline cyber attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Pipeline_cyber_attack).[^cite_note-Sigalos-2021-9][^cite_note-Murphy-2021-7][^cite_note-Browne-2021-51] The May 2021 hack forced the pipeline to pay a \$4.4M ransom in bitcoin, though a large portion was recovered by the United States federal government the following month.[^cite_note-Browne-2021-51] The group behind the attack, [DarkSide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DarkSide_\(hacking_group\)), normally requests payment in either bitcoin or Monero, but charge a 10–20% premium for payments made in bitcoin due to its increased traceability risk.[^cite_note-Murphy-2021-7] Ransomware group [REvil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/REvil) removed the option of paying ransom in bitcoin in 2021, demanding only Monero.[^cite_note-Murphy-2021-7] Ransomware negotiators, groups that help victims pay ransoms, have contacted Monero developers to understand the technology.[^cite_note-Murphy-2021-7] Despite this, CNBC reported that bitcoin was still the currency of choice demanded in most ransomware attacks, as insurers refuse to pay Monero ransom payments because of traceability concerns.[^cite_note-Sigalos-2021-9]

### Regulatory responses

The attribution of Monero to illicit markets has influenced some exchanges to forgo listing it. This has made it more difficult for users to exchange Monero for fiat currencies or other cryptocurrencies.[^cite_note-Sigalos-2021-9] Exchanges in South Korea and Australia have delisted Monero and other privacy coins due to regulatory pressure.[^cite_note-52]

In 2018, [Europol](525329.md) and its director [Rob Wainwright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Wainwright_\(civil_servant\)) wrote that the year would see criminals shift from using bitcoin to using Monero, as well as Ethereum, [Dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dash_\(cryptocurrency\)), and [Zcash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zcash).[^cite_note-Kottasová-2018-53] *[Bloomberg](3057255.md)* and [CNN](62028.md) reported that this demand for Monero was because authorities were becoming better at monitoring the Bitcoin blockchain.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-Kottasová-2018-53]

On 20 February 2024, the cryptocurrency exchange [Binance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binance) delisted Monero, citing regulatory compliance.[^cite_note-55]

The United States [Internal Revenue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Service) (IRS) has offered funding for contractors that can develop Monero tracing technologies.[^cite_note-Franceschi-Bicchierai-2020-18]
