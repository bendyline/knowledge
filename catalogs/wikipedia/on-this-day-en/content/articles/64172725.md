# Lawsuits involving Meta Platforms

&#x20;[Meta Platforms, Inc.](62420226.md) has been involved in many [lawsuits](113258.md) since its founding in 2004. Examples include:

- *[Young v. Facebook, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_v._Facebook,_Inc.)* (2011)

- *[Fraley v. Facebook, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraley_v._Facebook,_Inc.)* (2016)

- *[Force v. Facebook, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_v._Facebook,_Inc.)* (2019)

- *[FTC v. Meta Platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTC_v._Meta)* (ongoing)

| September 2004 | ConnectU.com lawsuit | Main article: [ConnectU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConnectU) [Divya Narendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divya_Narendra), [Cameron Winklevoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Winklevoss), and [Tyler Winklevoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Winklevoss), founders of the social network ConnectU, filed a lawsuit against Facebook in September 2004. The lawsuit alleged that Zuckerberg had broken an [oral contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_contract) to build the social-networking site, copied the idea,[^cite_note-globe-1][^cite_note-2] and used [source code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_code) that they provided to Zuckerberg to create competing site Facebook.[^cite_note-02138Mag-3][^cite_note-Crimson-4][^cite_note-Princetonian-5][^cite_note-Facebook_in_court_over_ownership-6] Facebook countersued in regards to Social Butterfly, a project put out by [The Winklevoss Chang Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Winklevoss_Chang_Group), an alleged partnership between [ConnectU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConnectU) and [i2hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I2hub). It named among the defendants [ConnectU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConnectU), [Cameron Winklevoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Winklevoss), [Tyler Winklevoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Winklevoss), [Divya Narendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divya_Narendra), and [Wayne Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Chang), founder of [i2hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I2hub).[^cite_note-justia1-7] The parties reached a settlement agreement in February 2008, for \$20 million in cash and 1,253,326 Facebook shares. On August 26, 2010, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that Facebook shares were trading at \$76 per share in the secondary market, putting the total settlement value now at close to \$120 million.[^cite_note-nytblog-8][^cite_note-venturebeat1-9] ConnectU filed another lawsuit against Facebook on March 11, 2008,[^cite_note-10] attempting to rescind the settlement, claiming that Facebook, in settlement negotiations, had overstated the value of stock it was granting the ConnectU founders as part of the settlement. ConnectU argued that Facebook represented itself as being worth \$15 billion at the time, due to the [post-money valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-money_valuation) arising from [Microsoft](19001.md)'s purchase in 2007 of a 1.6% stake in Facebook for US\$246 million. Facebook announced that valuation in a press release.[^cite_note-11] However, Facebook subsequently performed an internal valuation that estimated a company value of \$3.75 billion.[^cite_note-12] ConnectU then fired the law firm [Quinn Emanuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinn_Emanuel) that had represented it in settlement discussions. Quinn Emanuel filed a \$13 million [lien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lien) against the settlement proceeds and ConnectU sued for [malpractice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_malpractice).[^cite_note-13] On August 25, 2010, an arbitration panel ruled that Quinn Emanuel had "earned its full contingency fee". It also found that Quinn Emanuel committed no malpractice.[^cite_note-14] ConnectU's lawsuit against Facebook to quadruple its settlement remains ongoing. In January 2010, it was reported that [i2hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I2hub) founder [Wayne Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Chang) and The i2hub Organization launched a lawsuit against [ConnectU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConnectU) and its founders, [Cameron Winklevoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Winklevoss), [Tyler Winklevoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Winklevoss), and [Divya Narendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divya_Narendra), seeking 50% of the settlement. The complaint states "Through this litigation, Chang asserts his ownership interest in [The Winklevoss Chang Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Winklevoss_Chang_Group) and ConnectU, including the settlement proceeds."[^cite_note-cnet3-15] Lee Gesmer (of Gesmer Updegrove, LLP) posted the [detailed 33-page complaint](https://www.scribd.com/doc/25379721/Chang-v-Winklevoss-Complaint) online.[^cite_note-masslawblog1-16][^cite_note-gesmer1-17] On April 12, 2011, a three-judge panel of a federal appeals court in San Francisco ruled that the Winklevoss brothers cannot back out of a settlement they signed with the company in 2008. |
| 2006 | Aaron Greenspan and houseSYSTEM | **Aaron Greenspan**[^cite_note-18] created a web portal as an undergraduate called houseSYSTEM that launched in August 2003.[^cite_note-19] Two weeks after launching Harvard became aware that houseSYSTEM was asking for students' Harvard network credentials in violation of the school's policy, and ordered Greenspan to shut the site down immediately. Greenspan was required to turn over a list of students so the administration could reset their passwords, with the threat of disciplinary action if he failed to comply.[^cite_note-20] Seven months after the initial launch of houseSYSTEM Greenspan added a new social networking module called FaceNet to compete with Facebook, telling the Harvard Crimson, "It is possible for multiple sites to co-exist. \[We support] entrepreneurship, and we applaud Mark’s efforts. Competition is a very real part of entrepreneurship".[^cite_note-\:0-21] However, students who had tried both websites expressed doubts about Greenspan's website noting that "FaceNet isn’t as easy to use as thefacebook.com. It might be too little, too late. It will be hard to compete with thefacebook.com when thefacebook.com already has about 5,000 members and lots of momentum".[^cite_note-\:0-21] In 2006, two years after [Mark Zuckerberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Zuckerberg) launched thefacebook.com, Greenspan claimed in an open letter to Zuckerberg that Facebook used features that originally belonged to houseSYSTEM.[^cite_note-22] Regarding Greenspan's allegations, years later Zuckerberg was described in *[The New York Times](30680.md)* as "saying through a spokeswoman that he was not sure how to respond".[^cite_note-23] Greenspan later filed a Petition to Cancel the "Facebook" trademark at [USPTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USPTO).[^cite_note-24] Facebook, Inc. agreed to a formal settlement with Greenspan in late May 2009 and issued a press release, but the terms were not disclosed.[^cite_note-25] |
| November 17, 2009 | 2009 class action lawsuit | On November 17, 2009, Rebecca Swift, on behalf of herself and all others similarly situated, filed a class action lawsuit against [Zynga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zynga) and Facebook in the [United States District Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court) for the [Northern District of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_District_of_California) for violation of the [Unfair competition law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfair_competition_law) and the [Consumers Legal Remedies Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumers_Legal_Remedies_Act), and for [unjust enrichment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unjust_enrichment).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] |
