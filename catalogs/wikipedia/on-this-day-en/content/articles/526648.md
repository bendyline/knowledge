# Bronze medal

"Bronze Medal" redirects here. For other uses, see [Bronze Medal (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Medal_\(disambiguation\)).

Not to be confused with [Bronze star (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_star_\(disambiguation\)), [Bronze Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Award_\(disambiguation\)), or [Bronze Medallion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Medallion_\(disambiguation\)).



A bronze medal from the [1980 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics)



The bronze commemorative [Medal of John VIII Palaeologus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_John_VIII_Palaeologus) by [Pisanello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisanello) (1438), made in various metals, but usually in bronze

A **bronze medal** in sports and other similar areas involving competition is a [medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal) made of [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze) awarded to the third-place finisher of contests or competitions such as the [Olympic Games](22576.md), [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games), etc. The outright winner receives a [gold medal](526488.md) and the second place a [silver medal](526647.md). More generally, bronze is traditionally the most common metal used for all types of high-quality medals, including artistic ones. The practice of awarding [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze) third place [medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal) in the Olympic Games began at the [1904 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics) in [St. Louis](27687.md), Missouri, before which only first and second places were awarded.

## Olympic Games

Main article: [Olympic medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_medal)

[Minting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_\(coin\)) Olympic medals is the responsibility of the host city. From [1928](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics)–[1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics) the design was always the same: the obverse showed a generic design by [Florentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) artist [Giuseppe Cassioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Cassioli) with text giving the host city; the reverse showed another generic design of an Olympic champion. From [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics)–[2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics), Cassioli's design (or a slight reworking) remained on the obverse with a custom design by the host city on the reverse. Noting that Cassioli's design showed a Roman amphitheatre for what was originally a Greek game, a new obverse design was commissioned for the [Athens 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics) Games. [Winter Olympics](34069.md) medals have been of more varied design.

In a few tournament sports, such as [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo_at_the_Summer_Olympics), [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo_at_the_Summer_Olympics) and [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_Summer_Olympics), two bronze medals are awarded in each event – one for each eliminated semi-finalist or for the winners of the *[repechage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repechage)* brackets.[^cite_note-1]

## Psychological study

See also: [Silver medal § Rejection of silver medals](526647.md#Rejection_of_silver_medals)

In 1995, a study was carried out by [social psychologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_psychology) Victoria Medvec, Scott Madey and [Thomas Gilovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gilovich) on the effects of [counterfactual thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterfactual_thinking) on the Olympics. The study showed that athletes who won the bronze medal were significantly happier with their winning than those athletes who won the [silver medal](526647.md). The silver medalists were more frustrated because they had missed the [gold medal](526488.md), while the bronze medalists were simply happy to have received any honors at all (instead of no medal for fourth place). This is more pronounced in [knockout competitions](939680.md), where the bronze medals are achieved by winning a [third place playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_place_playoff), whereas silver medals are awarded after a defeat in the final.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] This psychological phenomenon was parodied in the [Jerry Seinfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Seinfeld) special *[I'm Telling You for the Last Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'm_Telling_You_for_the_Last_Time)*.
