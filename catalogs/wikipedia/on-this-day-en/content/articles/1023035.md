The **New Zealand men's national football team** ([Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language): *Tīma hoka a-motu o Aotearoa*) represents [New Zealand](4913064.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) competitions. The team is governed by the governing body for football in New Zealand, [New Zealand Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Football) (NZF), which is currently a member of [FIFA](11049.md) and the [Oceania Football Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation) (OFC). The team's official nickname is the **All Whites** ([Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language): *Ōmā* [\[ɔːmaː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/M%C4%81ori)).[^cite_note-3]

The team represented New Zealand at the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) tournaments in [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), [2010](168079.md) and [2026](36581929.md). It also participated in the [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), and [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup). New Zealand is a six-time [OFC Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OFC_Men's_Nations_Cup) champion. New Zealand was the only unbeaten country in the 2010 FIFA World Cup, drawing all three group stage games; nevertheless, they were eliminated in the group stage.

## History

### Early years



[New Zealand playing Australia in 1922.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_New_Zealand_v_Australia_soccer_match)

New Zealand's first international football match was played in [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin) at the old [Caledonian Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonian_Ground) on 23 July 1904 against a team representing New South Wales. New Zealand lost by the game's only goal, but drew with the same team 3–3 in a game at [Athletic Park, Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Park,_Wellington) seven days later.[^cite_note-4] The following year the team played a Wellington representative side on 10 June before embarking on a tour of Australia, during which they played eleven representative sides, including three "test matches" against New South Wales. Of these three matches they won one, lost one, and drew one.

A New Zealand national team did not play again until 1922, when New Zealand played three official full internationals against [Australia](4792249.md), played at [Carisbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carisbrook) in Dunedin, [Athletic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Park,_Wellington) in Wellington, and [Auckland Domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_Domain). The results were two 3–1 wins to New Zealand and a 1–1 draw in Wellington.[^cite_note-5] In 1927, [Canada](723688.md) became the second team to play in New Zealand as they played in four official matches with a win and a draw.[^cite_note-6]

New Zealand would become one of the founding members of the [Oceania Football Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation) in 1966, which was founded by [Charlie Dempsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Dempsey) and his Australian colleague [Jim Bayutti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Bayutti).[^cite_note-7]

### Success for Spain '82



New Zealand playing against [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_football_team) during the [1990 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(OFC\))

At the beginning of the 1980s the All Whites were on a run of consecutive victories until the [1980 Oceania Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Oceania_Cup) in New Caledonia. New Zealand ended up having a disastrous campaign, losing 1–3 and 0–4 to Tahiti and Fiji respectively. In the last round without a possible qualification for the final they beat the Solomon Islands 6–1.

The All Whites later improved when the team advanced to the final phase of the [qualifiers for the 1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification). With zero losses, the team's strength was highlighted by a 3–3 draw and a 1–0 victory against Australia, and a 13–0 victory against Fiji. For the final phase the All Whites, competed against [China PR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team), [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) and [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team). After a 5–0 victory against the Saudis, they competed in a play-off match against China, winning 2–1, eventually qualifying for the [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup) in Spain.

Up until the 1980s, the All Whites received criticism for having a high number of British players. [Of the 22-man squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_squads#New_Zealand) in their 1982 World Cup campaign, 11 members were born in the United Kingdom. This included the captain [Steve Sumner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Sumner) and striker [Steve Wooddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Wooddin), who had both played club football in England before immigrating. They lost all three games conceding 12 goals and scoring just 2. Over the following decades the composition of the national squad changed and "the face of football became increasingly Kiwi".[^cite_note-8]

Sent into Group 6, alongside [Brazil](149286.md), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) and [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team), New Zealand lost all three games, with Sumner and Woodin scoring the team's only goals, that happened in the 5–2 loss to Scotland.[^cite_note-1982Allwhites-9]&#x20;

### Consolidation in Oceania



New Zealand playing against Bahrain in the [2010 FIFA World Cup inter-confederation play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(inter-confederation_play-offs\)) at the [Westpac Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Regional_Stadium).

Since the 1990s, United States [college soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_soccer_in_the_United_States) has played a significant role in the development of New Zealand players. This influence began when former [Scotland international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) [Bobby Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Clark_\(footballer,_born_1945\)) returned to the US after his 1994–96 stint as New Zealand head coach to take the head coaching job at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Cardinal). Clark began recruiting in New Zealand, and former New Zealand national players [Ryan Nelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Nelsen) and [Simon Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Elliott) played for him at Stanford. The trend that Clark started has continued to the present; more than two dozen New Zealanders are now playing for [NCAA Division I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_I_\(NCAA\)) men's programmes in the US.[^cite_note-US_connection-10] A common next step in these players' career paths is a stint in [Major League Soccer](71802.md); [ESPN soccernet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPNsoccernet) journalist Brent Latham speculated in a March 2010 story that New Zealand's [2010 World Cup](168079.md) squad could have more MLS players than the [US squad](582488.md).[^cite_note-US_connection-10][^cite_note-11]

However, Latham's speculation did not prove true, as only one MLS player made the New Zealand squad for the World Cup. New Zealand formerly competed against [Australia](4792249.md) for top honours in the [OFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation). However, after Australia left to join the [AFC](715249.md) in 2006, New Zealand were left as the only seeded team in the OFC. New Zealand qualified for the 2010 World Cup, though exited the competition after the first round despite being the only team not to lose a game during the tournament. They drew 1–1 versus defending champions [Italy](362466.md), along with [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team) and a scoreless match against [Paraguay](804025.md) while eventual champions [Spain](609825.md) lost to [Switzerland](681862.md). New Zealand finished above Italy in their group as the Italians lost to Slovakia in their final group match and finished with two points compared to New Zealand's three.[^cite_note-12] &#x20;

### Horror in Honiara and World Cup misses

After a very positive cycle for the All Whites, the team competed for the [2012 OFC Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_OFC_Nations_Cup) as favourites to win the title winning the first two games by a small margin of victory (1–0 and 2–1), and a 1–1 draw against the [Solomonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands_national_football_team). In the next round, they faced New Caledonia in the semi-final, where they suffered 2–0 loss, with goals from [Bertrand Kaï](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Ka%C3%AF) in the 60th minute, and [Georges Gope-Fenepej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Gope-Fenepej) in the second minute of second-half stoppage time to seal the defeat known as the *Horror in Honiara*. [Ricki Herbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricki_Herbert) stepped down, but New Zealand would also be eliminated in the [intercontinental play-off for the 2014 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF%E2%80%93OFC_play-off\)) by [Mexico](808402.md) 9–3 on aggregate.



New Zealand playing against [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) in the [2017 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup).

In August 2014, [Anthony Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Hudson_\(football_manager\)) was appointed manager of the All Whites. Hudson's first game in charge of the national team was a 3–1 defeat away to [Uzbekistan](1032413.md) in September. As a result of the All Whites playing "just three matches" in the previous year, which was "the least of any country in world football",[^cite_note-13] and having "seven months without a match" the All Whites dropped to 161st in the [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-newshub.co.nz-15] The All Whites went on to win the [2016 OFC Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_OFC_Nations_Cup), winning four matches with the final being won via a penalty shootout after a 0–0 draw against Papua New Guinea, conceding only one goal, from a penalty, in the process. New Zealand's victory saw them crowned Oceania champions making New Zealand the most successful national team in the competition's history, having won the tournament five times, and also saw them qualify for the [2017 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) in Russia. The All Whites moved up to 88th in the FIFA World Rankings, the highest ranking in three years, on the back of the OFC Nations Cup victory that qualified them for the 2017 FIFA Confederations Cup.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

After a disappointing tournament at the [2017 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) where they finished bottom of their group which featured Russia, Mexico and Portugal, the national team fell 27 places to 122nd.[^cite_note-18] In September 2017, New Zealand won the [OFC Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_OFC_Nations_Cup_finals) against the Solomon Islands with an aggregate score of 8–3 to qualify for the inter-continental play-off qualifier against Peru, the fifth-ranked nation from South America's qualifiers.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] After holding Peru off in the first leg, they would go to lose 2–0 in the second leg to be eliminated from competition as Peru became the last team to qualify for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

After the All Whites' stoppage for almost two years, they returned to play friendlies (in 2021), obtaining positive results in their three (four counting against [Algeria A'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria_A'_national_football_team)) games played in that year. With the complications caused by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Oceania), the [2022 World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(OFC\)) were held in the host country itself, Qatar, where the Kiwis managed to win all the games, as well as breaking artillery records, when forward [Chris Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Wood_\(footballer,_born_1991\)) became the All Whites' top scorer, after scoring twice against [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji_national_football_team).



New Zealand playing [Australia](4792249.md) at home at [Eden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Park) in a match commemorating the rivalry.

With the continental victory, they qualified for the [inter-confederation play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONCACAF%E2%80%93OFC_play-off\)), where they disputed the vacancy against [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team). They started by conceding a goal in the 3rd minute of the game to [Joel Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Campbell), but New Zealand began to pressure the game, and in the 39th minute, Chris Wood scored after a poor kick by [Yeltsin Tejeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeltsin_Tejeda). However, his goal was disallowed when the [video assistant referee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee) (VAR) showed that [Matthew Garbett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Garbett) had fouled [Óscar Duarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Duarte_\(footballer,_born_1989\)) before the goal.[^cite_note-23] As the final whistle blew, the New Zealanders failed to qualify for the Cup, which was their third consecutive elimination in the inter-confederation play-offs.[^cite_note-24]

After the qualifiers, the All Whites played a home and away series against the [Socceroos](4792249.md) to mark the 100th anniversary of the first meeting between the two nations, which was first played in Dunedin in 1922.[^cite_note-25]

### Return to the international scene

In June 2023, with [Darren Bazeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Bazeley) already having his position as coach for the 2026 cycle, in a friendly against Qatar where the All Whites were winning during the first half, New Zealand defender [Michael Boxall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Boxall) claimed to have suffered a racist attack from the Qatari player [Yusuf Abdurisag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Abdurisag), and in protest by the New Zealand team players against the referee for not acting in this situation, they abandoned the match, not playing the second remaining time.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

Shortly afterwards, there was the return of the Soccer Ashes dispute against the Australians after the original urn was found again after almost 70 years of its disappearance.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The decisive title match was played in October in England, in which Australia consolidated its superiority after a solid 2–0 victory.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] New Zealand also competed in the [Oceania Nations Cup in Vanuatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_OFC_Men's_Nations_Cup), emerging as champions after a strong campaign in which they did not concede a single goal, and a part of the squad was then selected to compete for the "OlyWhites" at the [Olympic Games in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament), and these results enabled the team to break into the FIFA top 100 for the first time in seven years, reaching the 94th spot.

Following on this, they competed in qualifiers for the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md) in North America as favorites to advance thanks to the expansion of the tournament to 48 teams, which granted the OFC a direct qualifying spot. They secured their place at the World Cup by winning every match including emphatic victories and defeating [New Caledonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Caledonia_national_football_team) in the final in Auckland in March 2025, becoming the fifth team to qualify (and the second to do so via the qualifiers) and returning to the tournament after a 16-year absence.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

After an important victory against Chile in the [FIFA Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_Series_\(men's_matches\)#New_Zealand) in Auckland, they qualified for the 2026 World Cup, being placed in pot 4 for the group stage draw, and ultimately drawn into [Group G](81660197.md) with [Belgium](679716.md), [Egypt](1022953.md), and [Iran](272865.md).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] They played their first match against Iran, drawing 2–2 with two goals from [Elijah Just](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elijah_Just), followed by two assists from [Chris Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Wood_\(footballer,_born_1991\)), earning what would be their only point in the tournament, as in their subsequent matches they suffered heavy defeats against Egypt and Belgium, being eliminated last in the group stage. In the end, New Zealand managed to score two more goals in the tournament, but in total conceded 10 goals in their participation in the tournament.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

### Kit

&#x20;New Zealand's traditional home colours are white with a black trim, while its away kits are usually reversed, featuring black with a white trim. This reversal of the colour scheme by New Zealand's football team is due to the fact that black (the nation's traditional color in rugby) was traditionally reserved for referees by [FIFA](11049.md).

### Nickname

During the [qualification for the 1982 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC_and_OFC\)), the team appeared for the first time in an all white uniform against Taiwan in 1981. This led a commentator to dub them the "All Whites", a play on the traditional name "[All Blacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Blacks)" used for the national rugby team.[^cite_note-NZH-40] The name stuck, and was popularised in the song "Marching off to Spain" with its chant refrain "Kiwis! All Whites!"[^cite_note-NZH-40][^cite_note-41]

### Rivalries

Main article: [Australia–New Zealand soccer rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93New_Zealand_soccer_rivalry)

New Zealand's long time rivals are [Trans-Tasman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Tasman) neighbours [Australia](4792249.md).[^cite_note-42] The two teams' history dates back to 1922, where they first met in both their international debuts. The rivalry between the *Socceroos* (Australia) and the *All Whites* (New Zealand) is part of a [wider friendly rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia%E2%80%93New_Zealand_relations) between the geographical neighbours Australia and New Zealand, which applies not only to sport but to the culture of the two countries. The rivalry was intensified when Australia and New Zealand were both members of the [OFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation), regularly competing in [OFC Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OFC_Nations_Cup) finals and in [FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), where only one team from the OFC progressed to the [World Cup](11370.md).

Since Australia left the OFC to join the [AFC](715249.md) in 2006, competition between the two teams has been less frequent. However, the rivalry between the two teams is still strong, with the occasional match receiving much media and public attention.[^cite_note-43] The rivalry extends to club football, with New Zealand's only fully professional teams, the [Wellington Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Phoenix) and [Auckland FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_FC), playing in the Australian [A-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-League).

### Supporters



Panorama from the 'White Noise' zone during New Zealand v Peru – [2018 FIFA World Cup inter-confederation play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(inter-confederation_play-offs\)) at the [Sky Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Regional_Stadium).

The main [supporter group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporters'_groups) of the New Zealand national team are known as the **White Noise**.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] White Noise was formed in November 2007[^cite_note-48] with the supporters group of the [Wellington Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Phoenix_FC), ['Yellow Fever'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Phoenix_FC#Supporters), rebranding themselves when the national sides play.[^cite_note-49] Additionally, **The Flying Kiwis FC** founded by Matt Fejos, have been New Zealand's main supporter group for away fixtures. The Flying Kiwis have been synonymous with organizing fan friendly matches with supporters of opposing teams, most notably during the 2017 FIFA Confederations Cup where they organized a friendly match between supporters of the Russian national team.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

### Home stadium

New Zealand does not have a dedicated [national stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_stadium). Instead, the team plays at different venues throughout the country for exhibition or tournament purposes. In recent years, major international matches have usually been rotated around various large grounds, including the [Wellington Regional Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Regional_Stadium) and the [North Harbour Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Harbour_Stadium) in Auckland. International matches have also been played at the [Mount Smart Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Smart_Stadium) and [Eden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Park) in Auckland.
