**Chhattisgarh** ([/ˈtʃætɪsɡɑːr/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); Hindi: [\[ˈtʃʰət̪ːiːsgəɽʱ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu)) is a landlocked [state](375986.md) in [east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_India)-[central India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India).[^cite_note-14] It is the [ninth largest state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area), and with a population of roughly 30 million, the [seventeenth most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population).[^cite_note-15] It borders seven states – [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) to the north, [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md) to the northwest, [Maharashtra](20629.md) to the southwest, [Jharkhand](197225.md) to the northeast, [Odisha](250724.md) to the east, [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) and [Telangana](990267.md) to the south.[^cite_note-16] Formerly a part of [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), it was granted statehood on [1 November 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarh_Rajyotsava) with [Raipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raipur) as the designated state capital.[^cite_note-cgfinance.nic.in-17]

The [Sitabenga Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogimara_and_Sitabenga_Caves) in Chhattisgarh, one of the earliest examples of theatre architecture in India, are dated to the [Mauryan period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) of 3rd century BCE. The region was split between rivaling dynasties from the sixth to twelfth centuries, and parts of it were briefly under the [Chola dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty) in the 11th century. Eventually, most of Chhattisgarh was consolidated under the [Kingdom of Haihaiyavansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Haihaiyavansi), whose rule lasted for 700 years until they were brought under Maratha suzerainty in 1740. The [Bhonsles of Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhonsles_of_Nagpur) incorporated Chhattisgarh into the [Kingdom of Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Nagpur) in 1758 and ruled until 1845, when the region was annexed by the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company), and was later administered under the [Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) until 1947 as the [Chhattisgarh Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarh_Division) of the [Central Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces). Some areas constituting present-day Chhattisgarh were [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) that were later merged into Madhya Pradesh. The [States Reorganisation Act, 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act,_1956) placed Chhattisgarh in Madhya Pradesh, and it remained a part of that state for 44 years.

Chhattisgarh is one of the fastest-developing states in India.[^cite_note-18] Its [Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_domestic_product) is ₹5.09 lakh crore (US\$53 billion) (2023–24 est.),[^cite_note-Chhattisgarh_Budget_Analysis_2023-24-19] with a [per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita) GSDP of [₹](490783.md)152,348 (US\$1,600)[^cite_note-Chhattisgarh_Budget_Analysis_2023-24-19] (2023–24 est.). A resource-rich state, it has the third largest [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) reserves in the country and provides electricity, coal, and steel to the rest of the nation.[^cite_note-Ministry_of_Coal-20][^cite_note-21] It also has the third largest forest cover in the country after [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md) and [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh) with over 41.21% of the state covered by forests.

## Etymology

There are several theories as to the origin of the name *Chhattisgarh*, which in ancient times was known as [Dakshina Kosala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kosala) (South Kosala),[^cite_note-22] the native place of [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama)'s mother [Kausalya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kausalya). "Chhattisgarh" was popularised later during the time of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy) and was first used in an official document in 1795.[^cite_note-23] The [Bastar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastar_district) region was previously referred to as *Chakrakotya* and *Cakkarakoṭṭam*.[^cite_note-24]

The most popular theory claims that Chhattisgarh takes its name from the 36 ancient forts (from *chhattis* meaning thirty-six and *garh* meaning [fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort)) in the area.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] The old state had 36 demesnes (feudal territories): [Ratanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratanpur,_Chhattisgarh), Vijaypur, Kharound, Maro, Kautgarh, [Nawagarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawagarh), Sondhi, Aukhar, Padarbhatta, Semriya, Champa, Lafa, Chhuri, Kenda, Matin, Aparora, Pendra, Kurkuti-kandri, Raipur, Patan, Simaga, Singarpur, Lavan, Omera, Durg, Saradha, Sirasa, Menhadi, Khallari, Sirpur, Figeswar, Rajim, Singhangarh, Suvarmar, Tenganagarh and Akaltara.[^cite_note-27] However, most historians disagree with this theory as 36 forts have not been found and identified.

According to the opinion of Hiralal, it is said that at one time there were 36 strongholds in this area, that is why its name was Chhattisgarh. But even after the increase in the number of strongholds, there was no change in the name, Chhattisgarh is the [State of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_India) which has been given the status of 'Mahtari' (Mother). There are two regions in India which are named for special reasons – one was '[Magadha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadha_\(Mahajanapada\))' which became "[Bihar](442482.md)" due to the abundance of [Buddhism](3267529.md) viharas and the other was '[Dakshina Kosala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kosala)' which became "Chhattisgarh" due to the inclusion of thirty-six strongholds.

Another view, more popular with experts and historians, is that Chhattisgarh is the corrupted form of *Chedisgarh* meaning *Raj* or "[Empire of the Chedis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalachuris_of_Tripuri)". In ancient times, Chhattisgarh region had been part of the [Chedi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahameghavahana) of [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_\(historical_region\)), in modern [Odisha](250724.md). In the medieval period up to 1803, a major portion of present eastern Chhattisgarh was part of the [Sambalpur Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpur_State) of [Odisha](250724.md).

## History

See also: [Sirpur Group of Monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirpur_Group_of_Monuments) and [Malhar, Chhattisgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malhar,_Chhattisgarh)

### Ancient and medieval history

During post Vedic period the Chhattisgarh region south-east to [Daśārṇas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da%C5%9B%C4%81r%E1%B9%87a) was referred as [Pulinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulinda). Pulinda tribe were dominating tribe in this region.[^cite_note-28]

[Surguja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surguja) District of Chhattisgarh is notable for finding of Mauryan and Nanda period coins. Few gold and silver coins of the Nanda - Mauryan ages, picked up at Akaltara and Thathari of the adjacent district of [Bilaspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilaspur_district,_Chhattisgarh).[^cite_note-29] Another major discovery was [Sirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirpur,_Mahasamund) of Chhattisgarh.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] According to the Chinese traveler [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang), Ashoka erected Buddhist stupas in Shripura (modern-day [Sirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirpur,_Mahasamund)), the ancient capital of Dakshina Kosala.[^cite_note-32]

[Sitabenga Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogimara_and_Sitabenga_Caves) are one of the earliest examples of [theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_India) architecture in India located on [Ramgarh hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramgarh,_Chhattisgarh) of Chhattisgarh dated to Mauryan period of 3rd century BCE.[^cite_note-sitaoup-33]

[Jogimara Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogimara_and_Sitabenga_Caves) contain ancient Brahmi inscription and the oldest painting known in India. The inscription can be translated as either a love proclamation by a girl or a dancer-painter creating a cave theatre together.[^cite_note-35] In ancient times, this region was known as [Dakshina Kosala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshina_Kosala). This area is also mentioned in Ramayana and Mahabharata.One of the earliest statues of [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu) has been excavated from [Shunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunga_Empire) period site at [Malhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malhar,_Chhattisgarh).

The plains region of Chhattisgarh was formerly under the Mauryas, although they likely did not exercise much direct control in the region. After the collapse of the Mauryas, [Kharavela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharavela) of the [Mahameghavahana dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahameghavahana_dynasty), which was based in neighbouring [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_\(region\)), took over most of Dakshina Kosala. Later in the third century, the [Sathavahanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty) took over Dakshina Kosala, but this was contested and it returned to Megha rule. [Mahendra of Dakshina Kosala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahendra_of_Dakshina_Kosala), who is believed to be identified with a Megha monarch, was the ruler when [Samudragupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudragupta) carried out his [Dakshinapatha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshinapatha) conquests and conquered Dakshina Kosala, as recorded in Gupta inscriptions in the early 4th century CE. Afterwards the Guptas held control over Chhattisgarh through vassal rulers, and shared control with the contemporaneous [Vakatakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vakataka_dynasty). In the late 5th century CE, the Vakataka ruler Harisena recorded his conquest of the Dakshina Kosala region.

After the death of Vakataka ruler Harisena, the [Rajarsitulyakula dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajarsitulyakula) centred at [Arang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arang), former Gupta feudatories, took power and briefly ruled all of Dakshina Kosala. They were contemporaneous with the [Nala dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nala_dynasty), which was centered on the Bastar and Koraput regions.[^cite_note-36] Both these dynasties were succeeded by the [Sharabhpurias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharabhapuriya_dynasty) in the early 6th century, who were likely also former Gupta vassals who had their capital at present-day [Sirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirpur,_Mahasamund).[^cite_note-\:0-37] The [Panduvanshis of Mekala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panduvamshis_of_Mekala), centered in the northern Chhattisgarh plain, and the [Panduvamshis of Dakshina Kosala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panduvamshis_of_Dakshina_Kosala) both ruled parts of Chhattisgarh, but the chronology of these kingdoms is not certain. These kingdoms variously controlled the region from the 6th to 8th centuries CE.[^cite_note-Patnaik-38] There is some evidence that the [Somavanshis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somavamshi_dynasty), who later gained power in Kalinga, originated from the Panduvanshis of Dakshina Kosala and were driven out by the [Kalachuris of Tripuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalachuris_of_Tripuri) in the late 8th century.[^cite_note-\:0-37] The Kalachuris of Tripuri held on to the region for the next 200 years, splitting off their territories in Kosala in the late 10th century to be given to a vassal branch which also called itself Kalachuris.[^cite_note-mirashi-39]

The [Kalachuris of Ratnapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalachuris_of_Ratnapura), who were these vassals, became independent at the start of the 11th century to rule and fight off challenges to their authority by neighbouring kingdoms, most notably the [Eastern Gangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ganga_dynasty). The last known successor is from the late 13th century, after which the records become less available. By the early 14th century, it appears as if the dynasty split into two branches: one ruling from [Ratnapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratanpur,_Chhattisgarh) and another moving to [Raipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raipur). This is attested to by inscriptions of the king Vahara in the late 15th century, identified with a figure Bahar Sahai in local tradition at the end of the 18th century.[^cite_note-40] Vahara fought against the Afghans and shifted the capital to Kosgain from Ratnapur. These rulers are now identified as the [Haihaiyavanshis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Haihaiyavansi) and acknowledged the nominal overlordship of the Mughals when they arrived. In the late 14th century, Bastar was ruled by a dynasty which claimed descent from the brother of [Prataparudra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prataparudra), the last [Kakatiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakatiya_dynasty) king, Annamaraja.

Most of Chhattisgarh was consolidated under the [Haihaiyavanshi Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Haihaiyavansi), who ruled central Chhattisgarh and held smaller [kingdoms like Kanker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanker_State) under their authority.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] The Haihaiyavanshis continued to rule the region for 700 years until they were [invaded by the Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Ratanpur) in 1740 and came under their authority. Chhattisgarh was directly annexed to the Maratha [Nagpur Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur_Kingdom) in 1758 on the death of [Mohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohansingh_\(ruler\)), the last independent ruler of Chhattisgarh.[^cite_note-m-44][^cite_note-virginia-45]

### Modern history

See also: [Chhattisgarh Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarh_Division)

Chhattisgarh was under [Maratha Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) ([Bhonsles of Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhonsles_of_Nagpur)) from 1741 to 1845. It came under British rule from 1845 to 1947 as the [Chhattisgarh Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarh_Division) of the [Central Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces). Raipur gained prominence over the capital Ratanpur with the advent of the British in 1845. In 1905, the [Sambalpur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpur_district) was transferred to Odisha and the estates of Surguja were transferred from Bengal to Chhattisgarh.

The area constituting the new state merged into Madhya Pradesh on 1 November 1956, under the [States Reorganisation Act, 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act,_1956), and remained a part of that state for 44 years. Prior to that, the region was part of the [Central Provinces and Berar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces_and_Berar) (CP and Berar) under British rule. Some areas constituting the Chhattisgarh state were [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) under [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule), but were later on merged into Madhya Pradesh.[^cite_note-46]

### Separation of Chhattisgarh



Mantralaya in [Naya Raipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naya_Raipur)&#x20;

The demand for Chhattisgarh to be a separate state first rose in the 1920s, with similar demands appearing at regular intervals; however, a well-organised movement was never initiated. Several all-party platforms were created and usually resolved around petitions, public meetings, seminars, rallies and strikes.[^cite_note-Prithak_Chhattisgarh-47] The demand was raised by the Raipur Congress unit in 1924 and was also discussed in the Indian Congress at [Tripuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripuri,_Madhya_Pradesh). A discussion about forming a Regional Congress organisation for Chhattisgarh took place. In 1954, when the State Reorganisation Commission was set up, the demand was put forward but was rejected. In 1955, the demand was raised in the Nagpur assembly of [Madhya Bharat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Bharat).[^cite_note-Prithak_Chhattisgarh-47]

In the 1990s, the demand became more prominent, resulting in the formation of a statewide political forum known as the Chhattisgarh Rajya Nirman Manch. The forum was led by Chandulal Chadrakar and several successful region-wide strikes and rallies were organised under it, all of which were supported by major political parties, such as the [Indian National Congress](149333.md) and the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md).[^cite_note-Prithak_Chhattisgarh-47]

The new National Democratic Alliance government sent the Separate Chhattisgarh Bill for approval by the Madhya Pradesh Assembly, where it was unanimously approved and then submitted to the [Lok Sabha](275456.md). The bill was passed in the Lok Sabha and the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md), which allowed the creation of the state of Chhattisgarh. [K. R. Narayanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._R._Narayanan) gave his consent to the [Madhya Pradesh Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh_Reorganisation_Act) on 25 August 2000 and the government of India set 1 November 2000 as the day Chhattisgarh would be separated from Madhya Pradesh.[^cite_note-Prithak_Chhattisgarh-47] As such, Chhattisgarh was formed from Madhya Pradesh.[^cite_note-cgfinance.nic.in-17]

## Geography

The northern and southern parts of the state are hilly, while the central part is a fertile [plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain). The highest point in the state is the Gaurlata near Samri, Balrampur-Ramanujganj district.[^cite_note-48] Deciduous forests of the Eastern Highlands Forests cover roughly 44% of the state.[^cite_note-49] In the north lies the edge of the great [Indo-Gangetic plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_plain). The [Rihand River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihand_River), a tributary of the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_River), drains this area. The eastern end of the [Satpura Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satpura_Range) and the western edge of the [Chota Nagpur Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Plateau) form an east–west belt of hills that divide the [Mahanadi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi_River) basin from the Indo-Gangetic plain. The outline of Chhattisgarh is like a sea horse.

The central part of the state lies in the fertile upper basin of the [Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi_River) and its tributaries, of which [Shivnath River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivnath_River) is a major one running around 300 km long. This area has extensive rice cultivation. The upper Mahanadi basin is separated from the upper [Narmada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) basin to the west by the [Maikal Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maikal_Hill) (part of the Satpuras) and from the plains of Odisha to the east by ranges of hills. The southern part of the state lies on the [Deccan plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau), in the watershed of the [Godavari River](47988.md) and its tributary, the [Indravati River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indravati_River). The Mahanadi is the chief river of the state. The other main rivers are [Hasdeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasdeo_River) (a tributary of Mahanadi), [Rihand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihand), [Indravati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indravati), [Jonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonk_River), Arpa and [Shivnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivnath_River).[^cite_note-50]

### Forest



[Achanakmar-Amarkantak Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achanakmar-Amarkantak_Biosphere_Reserve)&#x20;

The state has the third largest forest by area in India. The state animal is the *van bhainsa*, or [wild Asian buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_water_buffalo). The state bird is the *pahari myna*, or [hill myna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_hill_myna). The state tree is the [Sal (Sarai)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorea_robusta) found in Bastar division.



*[Shorea robusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorea_robusta)*, the State Tree of Chhattisgarh

&#x20;Chhattisgarh has the 3rd largest forest cover in the country. The state is surrounded by the forests in [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md) (1st), [Odisha](250724.md) (4th), [Maharashtra](20629.md) (5th), [Jharkhand](197225.md) and [Telangana](990267.md) making it India's largest covered forests across state boundaries. There are multiple National Parks, Tiger Reserves across the state. [Achanakmar-Amarkantak Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achanakmar-Amarkantak_Biosphere_Reserve) is [UNESCO](21786641.md) recognised [Biosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere) with total area of 383,551 hectares (3,835.51 square kilometres; 1,480.90 square miles)

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Chhattisgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Chhattisgarh)

Chhattisgarh has a tropical [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate). It is hot and humid in the summer because of its proximity to the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer) and its dependence on the [monsoons](57630.md) for rains. Summer temperatures in Chhattisgarh can reach up to 49 °C (120 °F).[^cite_note-52] The monsoon season is from late June to October and is a welcome respite from the heat. Chhattisgarh receives an average of 1,292 millimetres (50.9 in) of rain. Winter is from November to January. Winters are pleasant with low temperatures and less humidity. Ambikapur, Mainpat, Pendra Road, Samri and Jashpur are some of the coldest places in the state.[^cite_note-53]

### Rail network



Bilaspur Junction Railway Station



[Raipur Junction railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raipur_Junction_railway_station)&#x20;

Almost the entire railway network spread over the state comes under the geographical jurisdiction of the [South East Central Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_Central_Railway) Zone of Indian Railways centred around [Bilaspur](2044758.md), which is the zonal headquarters of this zone. Almost 85% of tracks are electrified, the non-electrified route is the Maroda–Bhanupratappur line from the Durg–Bhanupratappur branch line, which is 120 km long. The main railway junctions are [Bilaspur Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilaspur_Junction), [Durg Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durg_Junction_railway_station), and Raipur, which is also a starting point of many long-distance trains. These three junctions are well-connected to the major cities of India and also these station comes under the top 50 booking stations in India.[^cite_note-rail-secr-54]

The state has the highest freight loading in the country, and one-sixth of the Indian Railway's revenue comes from Chhattisgarh. The length of the rail network in the state is 1,108 km, while a third track has been commissioned between Durg and Raigarh.[^cite_note-55] Construction of some new railway lines include Dalli–Rajhara–Jagdalpur rail line, Pendra Road–Gevra Road rail line, Raigarh–Mand Colliery to Bhupdeopur rail line and Barwadih–Chirmiri rail line.[^cite_note-56] Freight/goods trains provide services mostly to coal and iron ore industries in east–west corridor (Mumbai–Howrah route). There is a lack of passenger services to the north and south of Chhattisgarh.

### Air



Swami Vivekananda Airport

The air infrastructure in Chhattisgarh is gradually improving. [Swami Vivekananda Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swami_Vivekananda_Airport) in [Raipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raipur) is the primary airport (domestic) and is well connected to all major cities of India. Besides this, the smaller [Bilaspur Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilaspur_Airport), [Jagdalpur Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagdalpur_Airport) and [Ambikapur Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambikapur_Airport) are regionally connected with scheduled commercial services. A massive reduction in sales tax on aviation turbine fuel (ATF) from 25 to 4% in Chhattisgarh in 2003 contributed to a sharp rise in passenger flow. The passenger flow increased by 58% between 2011 and November 2012.[^cite_note-57]

## Administration

Each division is headed by a [Divisional Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisional_Commissioner_\(India\)), a senior [IAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) officer, appointed by the government. Each division comprises several districts under its administrative jurisdiction. The district administration is headed by a [Collector & District Magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_magistrate), an IAS officer. Each district is further divided into subdivisions, tehsils, and villages for revenue administration, while for development administration it is divided into blocks.[^cite_note-58]

### Agriculture

Agriculture is counted as the chief economic occupation of the state. According to a government estimate, the net sown area of the state is 4.828 million hectares and the gross sown area is 5.788 million hectares.[^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Chhattisgarh-61] Horticulture and animal husbandry also engage a major share of the total population of the state.[^cite_note-62] About 80% of the population of the state is rural and the main livelihood of the villagers is agriculture and agriculture-based small industry.

The majority of the farmers are still practicing the traditional methods of cultivation, resulting in low growth rates and productivity. The farmers have to be made aware of modern technologies suitable to their holdings. Providing adequate knowledge to the farmers is essential for a better implementation of the agricultural development plans and to improve productivity.[^cite_note-nhm.nic.in-63]

Considering this and a very limited irrigated area, the productivity of not only rice but also other crops is low, hence the farmers are unable to obtain economic benefits from agriculture and it has remained as subsistence agriculture till now.

#### Agricultural products

The main crops are rice, maize,[^cite_note-64] *kodo-kutki* and other small millets and pulses (*tuar*[^cite_note-65] and *kulthi*); oilseeds, such as groundnuts (peanuts), soybeans[^cite_note-66] and sunflowers are also grown. In the mid-1990s, most of Chhattisgarh was still a monocrop belt. Only one-fourth to one-fifth of the sown area was double-cropped. When a very substantial portion of the population is dependent on agriculture, a situation where nearly 80% of a state's area is covered only by one crop, immediate attention to turn them into double crop areas is needed. Also, very few cash crops are grown in Chhattisgarh, so there is a need to diversify the agricultural produce towards oilseeds and other cash crops. Chhattisgarh is also called the "rice bowl of central India".[^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Chhattisgarh-61]

#### Irrigation



Amritdhara chirimiri

In Chhattisgarh, rice, the main crop, is grown on about 77% of the net sown area. Only about 20% of the area is under irrigation; the rest depends on rain. Of the three agroclimatic zones, about 73% of the Chhattisgarh plains, 97% of the Bastar plateau, and 95% of the northern hills are rainfed. The irrigated area available for double cropping is only 87,000 ha in the Chhattisgarh plains and 2300 ha in Bastar plateau and northern hills. Due to this, the productivity of rice and other crops is low, hence the farmers are unable to obtain economic benefits from agriculture and it has remained as subsistence agriculture till now, though agriculture is the main occupation of more than 80% of the population.[^cite_note-nhm.nic.in-63]

In the Chhattisgarh region, about 22% of the net cropped area was under irrigation as compared to 36.5% in Madhya Pradesh in 1998–99, whereas the average national irrigation was about 40%. The irrigation is characterised by a high order of variability ranging from 1.6% in Bastar to 75.0% in Dhamtari. Based on an average growth trend in the irrigated area, about 0.43% of additional area is brought under irrigation every year as compared to 1.89% in Madhya Pradesh and 1.0% in the country as a whole. Thus, irrigation has been growing at a very low rate in Chhattisgarh and the pace of irrigation is so slow, that it would take about 122 years to reach the 75% level of net irrigated area in Chhattisgarh at the present rate of growth.[^cite_note-nhm.nic.in-63]

Chhattisgarh has a limited [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) system, with dams and [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) on some rivers. Average rainfall in the state is around 1400 mm and the entire state falls under the rice agroclimatic zone. The Large variation in the yearly rainfall directly affects the production of rice. Irrigation is the prime need of the state for its overall development and therefore the state government has given top priority to the development of irrigation.[^cite_note-Agriculture_in_Chhattisgarh-61]

A total of four major, 33 medium, and 2199 minor irrigation projects have been completed and five major, nine medium, and 312 minor projects are under construction, as of 31 March 2006.

### Industrial sector

#### Power sector

Chhattisgarh is one of the few states of India where the power sector is effectively developed. Based on the current production of surplus electric power, the position of the State is comfortable and profitable. The Chhattisgarh State Electricity Board (CSEB) is in a strong position to meet the electricity requirement of the state and is in good financial health. According to [Central Electricity Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Electricity_Authority_\(India\)) (CEA), Chhattisgarh provides electricity to several other states because of surplus production.[^cite_note-67]

In Chhattisgarh, [National Thermal Power Corporation Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Thermal_Power_Corporation_Limited) (NTPC) has [Sipat Thermal Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sipat_Thermal_Power_Station) with a capacity of 2,980 MW at Sipat, Bilaspur; [LARA Super Thermal Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LARA_Super_Thermal_Power_Station) with a nameplate capacity of 1600MW and [Korba Super Thermal Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korba_Super_Thermal_Power_Station) with a capacity of 2,600 MW at Korba, while CSEB's units have a thermal capacity of 1,780 MW and hydel capacity of 130 MW. Apart from NTPC and CSEB, there are several private generation units of large and small capacity. The state government has pursued a liberal policy with regard to captive generation which has resulted in a number of private companies coming up.[^cite_note-Power_Sector_in_Chhattisgarh-68]

The state has a potential of 61,000 MW of additional thermal power in terms of availability of coal for more than 100 years and more than 2,500 MW hydel capacity. To use this vast potential, substantial additions to the existing generation capacity are already underway.[^cite_note-Power_Sector_in_Chhattisgarh-68]

#### Steel sector

The steel industry is one of the biggest heavy industries of Chhattisgarh. [Bhilai Steel Plant, Bhilai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhilai_Steel_Plant) operated by [SAIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Authority_of_India_Limited), with a capacity of 5.4 million tonnes per year, is regarded as a significant growth indicator of the state. More than 100 steel rolling mills, 90 sponge iron plants, and ferro-alloy units are in Chhattisgarh. Along with Bhilai, today Jagdalpur, Raipur, Bilaspur, Korba and Raigarh have become the steel hub of Chhattisgarh. Today, Raipur and Jagdalpur has become the centre of the steel sector, the biggest market for steel in India.[^cite_note-Industries_in_Chhattisgarh-69]



#### Aluminium sector

The aluminium industry of Chhattisgarh was established by the erstwhile [Bharat Aluminium Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Aluminium_Company) (now [Vedanta Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanta_Resources)) in [Korba, Chhattisgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korba,_Chhattisgarh), which has a capacity of around 5,700,000 tonnes each year.[^cite_note-Industries_in_Chhattisgarh-69]

### Natural resources

**Gevra, Dipka, Kusmunda** open cast coal mines in [Korba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korba,_Chhattisgarh) are the largest in India and the biggest man-made structure visible in satellite images of India. Major coal companies are [SECL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Eastern_Coalfields), [Adani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adani_Group), [Jindal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jindal_Steel_and_Power) which operate multiple coal mines across northeast Chhattisgarh.

#### Central India Coalfields

The [Central India Coalfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India_coalfields?action=edit\&redlink=1) are spread over the districts of [Surguja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surguja_district), [Koriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koriya_district) (both in Chhattisgarh), [Shahdol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahdol_district) and [Umaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umaria_district) (both in Madhya Pradesh). The group covers an area of about 5,345 square kilometres (2,064 sq mi) with estimated reserves of 15,613.98 million tonnes. The deposits are at a depth of 0–1200 meters. Therefore, extraction is mainly amenable to underground mining except for a few blocks in the eastern part of these coalfields which have opencast potential.[^cite_note-70]

**Jhilimili Coalfield** located in [Surguja district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surguja_district) is spread over an area of 180 square kilometres (69 sq mi). Estimated total reserves are 215.31 million tonnes, out of which about half have been indicated to be Grade I.[^cite_note-71] According to the [Geological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_Survey_of_India), total reserves of non-coking coal (as of 1 January 2004) in Jhilimili Coalfield (up to a depth of 300m) was 267.10 million tonnes.[^cite_note-72]

The **Sonhat** is a large coal field representing one of the largest [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) reserves in [India](14533.md) having estimated reserves of 2.67 billion tonnes of [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal).[^cite_note-auto1-73]

**Bisrampur coal field** represents one of the largest [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) reserves in [India](14533.md) having estimated reserves of 1.61 billion tonnes of [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal).[^cite_note-auto1-73]

**Chirimiri Coalfield** is located in the valley of the [Hasdeo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasdeo_River), a tributary of the [Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi_River). Opened in 1930 with production starting in 1932, and has been owned by several companies and owners such as Chirimiri Colliery Company Pvt. Ltd., Dababhoy's New Chirimiri Ponri Hill Company (Private) Limited, United Collieries Limited, K.N. Dhady and Indra Singh & Sons (Private) Limited. These were nationalized in 1973. This coalfield is spread over 125 square kilometres (48 sq mi) of hilly country and includes both the sections – Kurasia and Chirimiri. Total reserves in the Chirimiri coalfield have been estimated to be around 312.11 million tonnes.[^cite_note-sharma-74][^cite_note-75] According to [Geological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_Survey_of_India) reserves of non-coking coal up to a depth of 300 m in Chirimiri Coalfield was 362.16 million tonnes.[^cite_note-76]

#### South Chhattisgarh coalfields

The [South Chhattisgarh Coalfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Chhattisgarh_coalfields?action=edit\&redlink=1) are made up of the Mand Raigarh, Korba, and Hasdo Arand coalfields. Of at least twelve seams in the Mand Valley, the Mand and Taraimar seams are important.[^cite_note-77]

**Mand Raigarh Coalfield** includes the areas earlier known as North Raigarh, South Raigarh, and Mand River Coalfields and is located in [Raigarh district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raigarh_district) and lies in the valley of the [Mand River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mand_River), a tributary of the [Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi_River). This coalfield is spread over an area of 520 square kilometres (200 sq mi). The field has a potential for mining power-grade coal, much of which can be extracted through open-cast mining. Gare block has been identified for captive mining by private companies.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

According to the [Geological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_Survey_of_India) total reserves (including proved, indicated, and inferred reserves) of non-coking coal in the Mand Raigarh Coalfield is 18,532.93 million tonnes. Out of this 13,868.20 million tonnes is up to a depth of 300 metres, 4569.51 million tonnes is at a depth of 300–600 metres and 95.22 million tonnes is at a depth of 600–1200 m.[^cite_note-80]

#### Mineral deposits

Chhattisgarh is rich in minerals. It produces 50% of the country's total cement production. Due to its proximity to the western States of Maharashtra and Gujarat, it has the highest producing [coal mines](208413.md) in India. It has the highest output of coal in the country with the second-highest reserves. It is third in iron ore production and first in tin production. [Limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [dolomite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomite_\(rock\)) and [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) are abundant. It is the only [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) ore-producing state in India. Other commercially extracted minerals include [corundum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corundum), [garnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garnet), [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz), [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), [alexandrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrite) and diamonds.

Rowghat iron ore deposits are located in the Antagarh Tahsil of [Kanker district](7111298.md) and contain the largest [iron ore](145352.md) deposits after the Bailadila Iron Ore Mine. Rowghat Mines' reserves have been assessed at 731.93 Mn tonnes. Bailadila has reserves assessed at 1.343 Bn tonnes.[^cite_note-chhattisgarhmines.gov.in-81] Iron ore deposits in Rowghat were discovered in 1899 and in 1949 [Geological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_Survey_of_India) investigated the area.[^cite_note-financialexpress.com-82] Rowghat deposit is 29 km (18 mi) NNW of Narayanpur, and about 140 km (87 mi) from [Jagdalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagdalpur). Fe content varies in the various blocks - A Block (62.58% Fe), B Block (50.29% Fe), C Block (57.00% Fe), D Block (60.00% Fe), E Block (52.93% Fe), and F Block (59.62% Fe).

## Human Development Indicators

### HDI

As of 2018, Chhattisgarh state had a [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) value of 0.613 (medium), ranking 31st in Indian states & union territories. The national average is 0.647 according to Global Data Lab.[^cite_note-ihdr-2001-84]

### Standard of living

The standard of living in Chhattisgarh is extremely imbalanced. The cities such as [Durg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durg), Raipur, Bhilai and Bilaspur have a medium to high standard of living, while the rural and forested areas lack even the basic resources and amenities. For example, Bhilai has a literacy rate of 86%, while [Bastar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastar_district) has a literacy rate of 54%.[^cite_note-85]

Raipur, the capital of Chhattisgarh, is one of the fastest-developing cities in India.[^cite_note-86] [Atal Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naya_Raipur) (Formerly *Naya Raipur*[^cite_note-87]) is the new planned city that is touted to become the financial hub of the [Central Indian region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_India). New world-class educational institutions and hospitals have already been established in the city.[^cite_note-88]

According to the NITI Aayog's Fiscal Health Index 2025, Chhattisgarh ranks second with a score of 55.2.[^cite_note-89]

### Education Index



School children in Chhattisgarh

Chhattisgarh has an Education Index of 0.526 according to the 2011 NHDR, which is higher than that of the states of Bihar, Jharkhand, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan. The Average Literacy rate in Chhattisgarh for Urban regions was 84.05 percent in which males were 90.58% literate while female literacy stood at 73.39%. Total literates in the urban region of Chhattisgarh were 4,370,966.

Among the marginalized groups, STs are at the bottom of the rankings, further emphasizing the lack of social development in the state. Bastar and Dantewada in south Chhattisgarh are the most illiterate districts and the dropout ratio is the highest among all the districts. The reason for this is the extreme poverty in rural areas.

[Ramakrishna Mission Asharama Narainpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramakrishna_Mission_Ashrama_Narainpur) serves the tribals in the abhjhmad jungle region of Chhattisgarh for their upliftment and education.[^cite_note-90]

As per census 2011, the State has a population of 25.5 million and six medical colleges (five Government and one private) with an intake capacity of 700 students and a doctor-patient ratio of 1\:17,000.[^cite_note-91] Under The NITI Aayog released Health Index report titled, "Healthy States, Progressive India", Chhattisgarh has an index of 52.02 Out of 100, which is better than states such as Madhya Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan, Odisha, Bihar, Assam and Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-92]

Despite different health-related schemes and programs, the health indicators such as the percentage of women with BMI<18.5, Under Five Mortality Rate and underweight children, are poor. This may be due to the difficulty in accessing the remote areas in the state. The prevalence of female malnutrition in Chhattisgarh is higher than the national average—half of the ST females are malnourished. The performance of SCs is a little better than the corresponding national and state average. The Under Five Mortality Rate among STs is significantly higher than the national average.

### Net state domestic product

Chhattisgarh is one of the emerging states with relatively high growth rates of net state domestic product (NSDP) (8.2% vs. 7.1% All India over 2002–2008) and per capita NSDP (6.2% vs. 5.4% All India over 2002–2008). The growth rates of the said parameters are above the national averages and thus it appears that Chhattisgarh is catching up with other states in this respect. However, the state still has very low levels of per capita income as compared to the other states.

### Urbanisation

Out of the total population of Chhattisgarh, 23.24% live in urban regions. The total population living in urban areas is 5,937,237, of which 3,035,469 are males and the remaining 2,901,768 are females.

Raipur, Durg, Bhilai Nagar, Bilaspur, Korba, Jagdalpur, Rajnandgaon, Ambikapur and Raigarh are some of the urban towns and cities in the region.[^cite_note-93]

### Sex ratio

There are more than 13 million males and 12.9 million females in Chhattisgarh, which constitutes 2.11% of the country's population. The [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sex_ratio) in the state is one of the most balanced in India with 991 females per 1,000 males, as is the child sex ratio with 964 females per 1,000 males (Census 2011)

### Fertility rate

Chhattisgarh has a fairly high fertility rate (2.4) as of 2017 compared to All India (2.2) and the replacement rate (2.1). It has a rural fertility rate of 2.6 and an urban fertility rate of 1.9

### SC and ST population

With the exception of the hilly states of the north-east, Chhattisgarh has one of highest shares of Scheduled Tribe (ST) populations within a state, accounting for about 10 percent of the STs in India. Scheduled Tribes make up 30.62% of the population. The tribals are an important part of the state population and mainly inhabit the dense forests of Bastar and other districts of south Chhattisgarh. The percentage increase in the population of the scheduled list of tribals during the 2001–2011 decade had been at the rate of 18.23%. The Scheduled Caste (SC) population of Chhattisgarh is 2,418,722 as per the 2001 census constituting 11.6 percent of the total population (20,833,803). The proportion of Scheduled Castes has increased from 11.6 percent in 2001 to 12.8% in 2011.

### Poverty



[Tendu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tendu_\(tree\)) Patta (Leaf) collection in Chhattisgarh, India.

The incidence of poverty in Chhattisgarh is very high. The estimated poverty ratio in 2004–05 based on uniform reference period consumption was around 50 percent, which is approximately double the all-India level. The incidence of poverty in the rural and urban areas is almost the same.

More than half of the rural STs and urban SCs are poor. In general, the proportion of poor SC and ST households in the state is higher than the state average and their community's respective national averages (except for rural SC households). Given that more than 50 percent of the state's population is ST and SC, the high incidence of income poverty among them is a matter of serious concern in the state.

This indicates that the good economic performance in recent years has not percolated to this socially deprived group, which is reflected in their poor performance in human development indicators.

### Access to drinking water

In terms of access to improved drinking water sources, at the aggregate level, Chhattisgarh fared better than the national average and the SCs of the state performed better than the corresponding national average. Scheduled Tribes are marginally below the state average, but still better than the STs at the all-India level.

The proportion of households with access to improved sources of drinking water in 2008–09 was 91%. This proportion was over 90% even in states like Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh. This was largely because these states had over 70% of their households accessing tube wells/hand pumps as sources of drinking water.

### Sanitation

Sanitation facilities in the state were abysmally low with only about 41 percent having toilet facilities before the [Swachh Bharat Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swachh_Bharat_Mission) was launched by the Government of India. The Urban areas of Chhattisgarh attained the title of open defecation free on 2 October 2017 and the rural areas have achieved a 90.31% sanitation coverage. What sets Chhattisgarh apart from other states of India is an approach to bring in behavioral change in order to get open defecation-free status. In Chhattisgarh, people don't get toilet incentives before the construction of toilets, so they have to construct the toilet with their own money, and only after using the toilet for 3 months are they entitled to the incentive amount.[^cite_note-94]

In 2020, it again won the title of the cleanest state with more than 100 [Urban Local Bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Local_Bodies), as announced by [Minister for Housing and Urban Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Housing_and_Urban_Affairs) [Hardeep Singh Puri](30909065.md) following the 'Swachh Survekshan 2020'.[^cite_note-95] In the Swachh Survekshan Awards-2023, Chhattisgarh secured the third rank in the 'Best Performing States' category.[^cite_note-96]

### Teledensity

Across states, it has been found that [teledensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teledensity) (telephone density) was below 10 percent in 2010 for Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand, reflecting a lack of access to telephones in these relatively poorer states. But due to development of new technology the teledensity in 2017 is 68.08 percent which shows improvement of telecom infrastructure. On the other hand, for states like Delhi and Himachal Pradesh and metropolitan cities like Kolkata, Mumbai, and Chennai, teledensity was over 100 percent in 2010 implying that individuals have more than one telephone connection.

### Road density

The total density of National Highways (NHs) in Chhattisgarh is at 23.4 km per 1,000 km<sup>2</sup> out of the total length of 3,168 km in the State, the Central Government has informed. Chhattisgarh Government had completed construction of 5,266 cement concrete (CC) roads having a total length of 1,530 km in various villages of the State as on 31 May 2016 under 'Mukhyamantri Gram Sadak Yojana'.[^cite_note-97]

Islam is the second-largest religion, concentrated in urban centres. Most Christians are tribals from the Surguija region.

The official languages of the state are [Modern Standard Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Hindi) and [Chhattisgarhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarhi_language), both of which are [Central Indo-Aryan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Indo-Aryan_languages) (also known as *Hindi languages*, forming a part of the [Hindi Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_Belt)). Chhattisgarhi is spoken and understood by the majority of people in Chhattisgarh and is the dominant language in the Chhattisgarh plain. Chhattisgarhi is called Khaltahi by tribals and Laria in [Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language). Chhattisgarhi is itself divided into many dialects, one of the most distinct being [Surgujia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgujia_dialect) from the Surguja region, which is sometimes considered its own language. Near the Uttar Pradesh border this dialect merges into [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language), while it merges with [Bagheli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagheli_language) near the Madhya Pradesh border. Surgujia also merges into [Sadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadri_language) in the northeast along the border with Jharkhand. Standard Hindi is spoken by many migrants from outside the state, and is a major language in the cities and industrial centres, while many whose language is actually Chhattisgarhi record their speech as "Hindi" in the census. [Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language) is widely spoken in eastern Chhattisgarh, especially near the Odisha border. Telugu and Marathi speaking minorities can be found along the Telangana and Maharashtra borders respectively. In the eastern Bastar region, [Halbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halbi_language) and [Bhatri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhatri_language) are major languages.

In addition, Chhattisgarh has several indigenous languages. [Kurukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurukh_language) and [Korwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korwa_language) are both spoken in the Surguja region. [Gondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondi_language) is a major language in southern Chhattisgarh: Bastar and the adjoining districts. Gondi has many dialects, such as [Muria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muria_language) in north Bastar, which transitions to [Madia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madiya_language) further south and Dorli, transitional between Gondi and [Koya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koya_language), along the borders of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. In the east of Bastar. Most Gonds in the north and east of Bastar, as well as the rest of the state, speak regional languages and have largely forgotten their original tongue.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

### Dance

Panthi, the folk dance of the Satnami community, has religious overtones. Panthi is performed on Maghi Purnima, the anniversary of the birth of [Guru Ghasidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghasidas). The dancers dance around a jaitkhamb set up for the occasion, to songs eulogising their spiritual head. The songs reflect a view of *[nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana)*, conveying the spirit of their guru's renunciation and the teachings of saint poets like [Kabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabir), Ramdas and Dadu. Dancers with bent torsos and swinging arms dance, carried away by their devotion. As the rhythm quickens, they perform acrobatics and form human pyramids.[^cite_note-110]

#### Pandavani

[Pandavani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandavani) is a folk ballad form performed predominantly in Chhattisgarh. It depicts the story of the Pandavas, the leading characters in the epic Mahabharata. The artists in the Pandavani narration consist of a lead artist and some supporting singers and musicians. There are two styles of narration in Pandavani, Vedamati, and Kapalik. In the Vedamati style, the lead artist narrates in a simple manner by sitting on the floor throughout the performance. The Kaplik style is livelier, where the narrator actually enacts the scenes and characters. Padma Shri, Padma Bhushan, and Padma Vibhushan [Teejan Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teejan_Bai) is most popular artist of Pandavani[^cite_note-111]

#### Raut Nacha

Raut Nacha, the folk dance of cowherds, is a traditional dance of Yaduvanshis (clan of [Yadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadu)) as symbol of worship to [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna) from the 4th day of Diwali (Goverdhan Puja) till the time of Dev Uthani Ekadashi (day of awakening of the gods after a brief rest) which is the 11th day after Diwali according to the Hindu calendar. The dance closely resembles Krishna's dance with the gopis (milkmaids).[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

In Bilaspur, the Raut Nach Mahotsav folk dance festival has been organised annually since 1978. Tens of hundreds of Rautt dancers from remote areas participate.[^cite_note-114]

#### Suwa Nacha

Soowa or Suwa tribal dance in Chhattisgarh is also known as Parrot Dance. It is a symbolic form of dancing related to worship. Dancers keep a parrot in a bamboo pot and form a circle around it. Then performers sing and dance, moving around it with clapping. This is one of the main dance forms of tribal women of Chhattisgarh.[^cite_note-115]

#### Karma

Tribal groups like Gonds, the Baigas and the Oraons in Chhattisgarh have the Karma dance as part of their culture. Both men and women arrange themselves in two rows and follow the rhythmic steps, directed by the singer group. The Karma tribal dance marks the end of the rainy season and the advent of spring season.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]
