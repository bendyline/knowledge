**Bukidnon** ([/buːˈkɪdnɒn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)), officially the **Province of Bukidnon** ([Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Lalawigan sa Bukidnon*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Lalawigan ng Bukidnon*; [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language): *Kapuoran sang Bukidnon*; [Binukid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukid_language) and [Higaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higaonon_language): *Probinsya ta Bukidnon*), is a landlocked [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the [Northern Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mindanao) [region](216786.md).[^cite_note-iaea-6] Its capital is the city of [Malaybalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaybalay) while [Valencia](2137518.md) is the largest city. The province borders, clockwise from the north, [Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Oriental), [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur), [Davao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Norte), [Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_province), [Lanao del Sur](297370.md), and [Lanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Norte). According to the 2020 census, the province is inhabited by 1,541,308 residents.[^cite_note-PSA20–10-7] The province is composed of 2 component cities and 20 municipalities. It is the third largest province in the country in terms of total area of jurisdiction behind [Palawan](240395.md) and [Isabela](271106.md) respectively.

The name "Bukidnon" means "highlander" or "mountain dweller." Occupying a wide [plateau](538005.md) in the north central part of the island of [Mindanao](20546.md), the province is considered to be the food basket of the region, being the major producer of [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize). Products from plantations in the province also include [pineapples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana) and [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane).

Situated within Bukidnon is [Mount Dulang-dulang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Dulang-dulang), the 2nd highest mountain in the country, with an elevation of 2,938 metres (9,639 ft) located in the [Kitanglad Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitanglad_Mountain_Range).[^cite_note-8] [Mount Kitanglad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kitanglad) (2,899 m), [Mount Kalatungan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kalatungan) (2,860 m), Mount Maagnaw (2,742 m), Mount Lumuluyaw (2,612 m), and Mount Tuminungan (2,400 m), the 4th, 5th, 8th, 17th, and 30th highest mountains in the country respectively, are also found in the province.[^cite_note-pinoymountaineer1-9]

Bukidnon was consecutively ranked 5th in the list of richest provinces in the Philippines for four straight years according to the [Commission on Audit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Audit_of_the_Philippines)'s 2018, 2019, 2020, and 2021 Annual Financial Reports which were posted in 2019, early to late 2021, and 2022, respectively.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## History

### Early history



One of the "tulugan" at Kaamulan Park, Malaybalay

According to oral history of the [Indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people) of Bukidnon, there were four main tribes in Central Mindanao: the Maranaos who dwell in Lanao del Sur, and the Maguindanao, Manobo and Talaandig tribes who respectively inhabit the eastern, southern, and north-central portions of [the original province of Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_\(historical_province\)). When the civil government divided central Mindanao into provinces at the turn of the 20th century, the groups included in the province of Bukidnon are the Talaandig and the Manobo, as well as other smaller [Lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad) tribes. The [Visayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayan_people), particularly the [Cebuanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_people) and the [Hiligaynons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people) from the [Northern Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mindanao) coastline and the southern [Visayas](72418.md), migrated into the province. The Visayans are still referred to by the Lumad as the *[dumagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayan_people)* ("sea people") to distinguish them from the original mountain tribes.[^cite_note-paredes-13] This was followed by various groups from [Luzon](69375.md), namely, the [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people), the [Igorots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot) and the [Ivatans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivatan_people), many of whom were merchants and wealthy entrepreneurs. All contributed massive acculturation among the Indigenous tribes. Most of those who moved to the mountains and forest continued to hold on their ancestors' cultural heritage. The wide variety of Filipino groups now thrives in the province and contributed immensely in the socioeconomic development.

### Spanish colonial era



Bukidnon Provincial Capitol, Malaybalay

Bukidnon became a part of [Misamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_\(province\)) in the latter part of 1850. The whole area was then called "Malaybalay" and the people were known as [Bukidnons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad#Bukidnon) (highlanders or mountain dwellers).

### American colonial era

The Philippine Commission, then headed by Commissioner [Dean C. Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Conant_Worcester), Secretary of Interior, proposed the separation of Bukidnon from Misamis Province. On August 20, 1907, Act No. 1693 created the province of [Agusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_\(province\)) with Bukidnon as its sub-province. About a month later, on September 23, the [rancheria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancheria) of [Malaybalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaybalay)[^cite_note-14] was designated as the sub-province's capital. Bukidnon became a regular province on March 10, 1917, by virtue of the creation of the [Department of Mindanao and Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mindanao_and_Sulu) under Act 2711.

### Japanese occupation

In 1942, invading Japanese troops entered Bukidnon. Mount Capistrano was a civilian evacuation area in the World War II. In 1945, the province was liberated from Japanese occupation by Filipino and American troops with the aid of Bukidnon-based Filipino guerrillas during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War).

### Martial law era

Main articles: [Marcos dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_dictatorship), [Human rights abuses of the Marcos dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship), and [Ilaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaga)

See also: [Jabidah massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_massacre) and [Moro conflict](4602307.md)

The 21-year administration of [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos), which included 14 years of one-man rule under Marcos, saw the rise of ethnic conflicts and the overall degradation of peace and order in Bukidnon and throughout Mindanao[^cite_note-FranciaIndiosBravos-15] - first in the form of conflicts between local Mindanaoan Muslims and Christian settlers which the Marcos administration had encouraged first to migrate and then to form militias,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-Mackerras_2003-17] and later in the form of Muslim secessionist movements arising from outrage after the 1986 [Jabidah massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_massacre).[^cite_note-Case-18][^cite_note-19] Additionally, an economic crisis in late 1969 led to social unrest throughout the country, and violent crackdowns on protests led to the radicalization of many students,[^cite_note-20] with some joining the [New People's Army](1262096.md), bringing the [Communist rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_rebellion_in_the_Philippines) to Mindanao.[^cite_note-MiclatinArguillas-21] In September 1972, Marcos was nearing the end of this last term allowed under the Philippines 1935 constitution when he placed the entirety of the Philippines under Martial Law, a period historically remembered for [its human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship),[^cite_note-McCoy199909202-22][^cite_note-Abinales\&Amoroso20052-23] particularly targeting political opponents, student activists, journalists, religious workers, farmers, and others who fought against the Marcos dictatorship.[^cite_note-24]

&#x20;Describing the specific sociopolitical situation in Bukindnon, the Philippines' [Bantayog ng mga Bayani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantayog_ng_mga_Bayani) memorial notes:&#x20;

> *Bukidnon was a land of great social divisions. Politics was rough, and bullets counted more than ballots. Peasants were oppressed by landlords, usurers and middlemen, and power was in the hands of a few. Conflicts simmered between the indigenous tribes, the settlers from the Visayas, and the ranchers and loggers who extracted the area’s rich natural resources.[^cite_note-BantayogProfileAlingal-25]*

Clergy in the Roman Catholic church were among the most prominent voices opposing the Marcos dictatorship in Bukidnon, most notably Bishop [Francisco Claver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Claver), who was Bishop of Malaybalay from 1969 to 1984, and Fr. Godofredo Alingal, S.J. of Kibawe, Bukidnon, who was assassinated in the Kibawe convent on April 13, 1981 after receiving numerous threats due to his advocacy for Bukidnon's poor.[^cite_note-BantayogProfileAlingal-25]

#### Deforestation during the Marcos era

Main article: [Deforestation in the Philippines § Deforestation during the martial law era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_the_Philippines#Deforestation_during_the_martial_law_era)

The Marcos era was a time of significant deforestation in Bukidnon and throughout the Philippines, with the forest cover of the Philippines shrinking until only 8% remained.[^cite_note-Dixon2010-26][^cite_note-Inoue2013-27][^cite_note-nhcp3-28] In Bukidnon, one of the major companies given Timber License Agreements (TLAs) to cut down trees during Martial Law was the Agricultural and Management Engineering Company (Ameco) owned by Juan Ponce Enrile.[^cite_note-nhcp3-28] Enrile was the government official Ferdinand Marcos put in place to approve Timber License Agreements during Martial Law.[^cite_note-nhcp3-28]

### Later 20th Century

On March 22, 1998, President [Fidel Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Ramos) signed the act (R.A. 8490) that converted Malaybalay to a city, making it the first component city of Bukidnon.

## Geography

Bukidnon is a landlocked [plateau](538005.md) in north-central Mindanao and the only one in Northern Mindanao. It is bounded on the north by [Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Oriental) and [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro); on the south by [Cotabato](297394.md) and [Davao City](489401.md); on the east by [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur) and [Davao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Norte); and west by [Lanao del Sur](297370.md). It lies between parallels 7°25' and 8°38' north latitude and meridians 124°03' and 125°16' east longitude. [Malaybalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaybalay), the capital city, is about 850 kilometers (530 mi) by air from [Manila](184334.md) and 91 kilometers (57 mi) by road from Cagayan de Oro.

It has two important landmarks, [Mount Kitanglad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kitanglad) and [Pulangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_River). Mount Kitanglad has a peak of 2,899 meters (9,511 ft) above sea level. Pulangi River, on the other hand, traverses through the northeastern and southern part of the province towards the [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao).

### Land area

The province's total land area is 10,498.59 square kilometres (4,053.53 sq mi),10,498.59 making it the largest in Mindanao in terms of land area. It accounts for 59 percent (59%) of [Northern Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mindanao). Thirty-eight percent (38%) is classified as alienable and disposable. The rest is timberland forest.

It also accounts for 80 percent (80%) or 34 million metric tons of the region's nonmetallic mineral deposits, which include high grade white and red clay, gold, chromite, copper, serpentine, manganese, quartz and limestone deposits can also be found in the province.

### Topography



Mangima Canyon at Maluko, [Manolo Fortich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manolo_Fortich). Also located in the town is a canyon near barangay Lunocan, dubbed as the "[Grand Canyon](46989.md) of the Philippines".

Much of Bukidnon is an extensive plateau, but the southern and eastern boundaries are mountainous. The province's average elevation is 915 meters (3,002 ft) above sea level. The slope gradient peaks at 2,899 meters (9,511 ft) of Mount Kitanglad, an extinct volcano occupying the central portion. Two other mountain bodies are found in its southern portion, Mount Kalatungan and Mount Tangkulan, which rise to 2,287 meters (7,503 ft) and 1,678 meters (5,505 ft), respectively. The rest of the province is composed of nearly level terraces, [alluvial plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial_plain), [canyons](78478.md) and gorges. The volcanic terraces and volcanic foot slopes that are ≥500 m above sea level are estimated to be about 221,600 hectares (548,000 acres).

Gently rolling grassland plateau are cut by deep and wide canyons of the [Cagayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_River_\(Mindanao\)), [Pulangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_River), and [Tagoloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagoloan_River) rivers and their tributaries, which cover a greater part of the province. The Bukidnon plateau is mainly of volcanic zone consisting of [pyroclastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_rock), [basaltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) and [andesitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesite) cones.



View from [Musuan Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musuan_Peak) of the Maapag Plain of central Bukidnon. The foothills of the [Kalatungan Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalatungan_Mountain_Range) is visible on the upper right.

The whole eastern and southern border adjoining the provinces of [Agusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur), [Davao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Norte), and [Cotabato](297394.md) are covered by lofty and densely forested [Pantaron Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantaron_Mountain_Range), also known as the Central Cordillera. The Central Cordillera is a mountain range of sedimentary, metamorphic and igneous rocks. About 49% of the land resource of the province is of rugged hills and mountains and 33% of undulating to rolling terrain.

At Mailag, 23 kilometers (14 mi) south of [Malaybalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaybalay), the plateau begins to descend and gradually merges into the lowlands of [Cotabato](297394.md) province.

### Climate

Two types of climate are roughly divided by the northern and southern areas of Bukidnon. The northern part is classified as belonging to Type III, that is, there is no pronounced rain period but relatively dry during the months of November to May. In the southern portion of the province, the climate is classified as Type IV with no dry season. The driest area is Baungon, while the wettest is the Calabugao plain. The climate is relatively cool and humid throughout the year.

The average annual rainfall is 2,800 millimeters (110 in). Just like in other parts of the country, rainfall is more pronounced from June to October compared to other months of the year. February to April are the drier months.

Temperature ranges vary with elevation. In areas lower than 500 meters (1,600 ft) above sea level (m.a.s.l.), the recorded temperature range is between 20 and 34 °C (68 and 93 °F). Areas with elevations greater than 500 meters (1,600 ft) above sea level would have temperatures ranging from 18 to 28 °C (64 to 82 °F).

Relative humidity also varies with elevation, with those above 500 m having relative humidity of about 80%, while areas lying below 500 meters (1,600 ft), 65-7 percent. Thus, the Malaybalay-Impasugong area and those around the volcanic cones approximate semi-temperate conditions and can support the cultivation of highland tropical crops.

Based on the records of climatological stations within and near the province, [lithology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithology) and land form, three agro-ecological zones are identified. One covers the mountainous eastern side (Central Cordillera), which is generally wet, with rainfall of about 2,340 to 4,000 millimeters (92 to 157 in) per annum. Another covers the high altitude volcanic plains, the Malaybalay-Impasug-ong area, and the foot slopes of Mount Kitanglad, and Mount Kalatungan. These areas have an annual rainfall in the range of 2,490 to 3,680 millimeters (98 to 145 in). The third zone covers the south-central and the north-western parts of the province, with elevations of less than 500 meters, relatively dry with mean annual rainfall in the range of 1,700 to 2,600 millimeters (67 to 102 in).

### Bodies of water



A waterfall found within the boundaries of the Kalatungan Mountain Range

#### Rivers

Bukidnon is home to the largest concentration of watersheds in Mindanao. It is endowed with six major river systems, namely: Pulangi, Tagoloan, Cagayan, Manupali, Muleta, and Bobonawan rivers. These rivers and their tributaries carved the landscape of the province, creating numerous canyons into the plateau.

The [Pulangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_River), considered the longest river in the province, is a tributary of the [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao). Its headwaters are found in the mountains of Kalabugao, [Impasugong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impasugong). It is the largest as well as the longest river found in the province. It covers the following cities and municipalities of the province: [Impasugong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impasugong), [Malaybalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaybalay), [Cabanglasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabanglasan,_Bukidnon), [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Bukidnon), [Valencia](2137518.md), [Maramag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maramag,_Bukidnon), [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon,_Bukidnon), [Don Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Carlos,_Bukidnon), [Kitaotao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitaotao,_Bukidnon), [Dangcagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangcagan,_Bukidnon), [Kibawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibawe,_Bukidnon) and [Damulog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damulog,_Bukidnon).

The [Tagoloan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagoloan_River) has its headwaters in the mountains of Can-ayan, Malaybalay. It traverses the province northwestward passing through Malaybalay, Impasugong, Sumilao, Manolo Fortich, Malitbog and finally empties into the sea at [Tagoloan, Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagoloan,_Misamis_Oriental).



The [Pulangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_River) winding through the Maapag Plain in [Valencia City](2137518.md)

The [Cagayan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_River_\(Mindanao\)) watershed is found mostly in the municipality of Talakag. Its headwaters are found in the Kitanglad Mountain Range in central Bukidnon. The river flows northward through the municipalities of [Talakag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talakag,_Bukidnon) and [Baungon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baungon,_Bukidnon). Its mouth lies at [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro) in [Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Oriental), where it is the main source of potable water.

The [Manupali River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manupali_River), a major tributary of the Pulangi River, starts in the mountains of [Lantapan, Bukidnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantapan,_Bukidnon), picking up tributaries along the way from the Kalatungan and Kitanglad Mountain Ranges. It forms part of the natural boundary of the [Valencia](2137518.md) and [Lantapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantapan,_Bukidnon). It flows eastward towards [Malaybalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaybalay), eventually joining the Pulangi River in Valencia.

The [Muleta River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muleta_River) is found in the southern portion of the province covering the municipalities of [Pangantucan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangantucan,_Bukidnon), [Don Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Carlos,_Bukidnon), [Kitaotao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitaotao,_Bukidnon), [Dangcagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangcagan,_Bukidnon), [Kibawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibawe,_Bukidnon), [Kadingilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadingilan,_Bukidnon) and [Damulog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damulog,_Bukidnon). It is another important tributary of the Pulangi River and flows southward. It will join the Pulangi River in the boundary of Bukidnon and [Cotabato](297394.md) province.



The Blue Water Cave in [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon,_Bukidnon)

The [Bobonawan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobonawan_River), found in the municipality of [Cabanglasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabanglasan,_Bukidnon), is another tributary of the Pulangi River. It covers most of the parts of the municipality, flowing southward towards Pulangi River.

#### Lakes

Aside from the relatively important river systems, various lakes also dot the landscape of the province. [Pinamaloy Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Pinamaloy), in [Don Carlos, Bukidnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Carlos,_Bukidnon), is the largest in the province covering about 50 hectares. It was named after Barangay Pinamaloy, where the lake is located. Another lake is found in Pigtauranan, [Pangantucan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangantucan,_Bukidnon) called the [Napalit Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Napalit). The lake covers an area of 36 hectares and is one of the tourist spots in Pangantucan, Bukidnon. There are 24 floating islets in the lake. The third significant inland body of water in the province is [Apo Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Apo) at Guinoyoran, [Valencia](2137518.md). It occupies an approximate area of 25 hectares. A man-made lake called [Pulangi Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_Lake) or Maramag Lake is found in [Maramag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maramag), Bukidnon, which was the result of the construction of the [Pulangi IV Hydroelectric Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_Dam) of the [National Power Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Power_Corporation) (NPC) in the course of the Pulangi River.

#### Springs and waterfalls

There are also numerous springs and waterfalls located in the province. Some of the waterfalls include the Alalum Falls, Dimadungawan Falls, Dila Falls, Gantungan Falls, Natigbasan Falls, Sagumata Falls, Magubo Falls, and Balisbisan Falls.

## Biodiversity

The Pantaron Mountain Range in Bukidnon is a biodiverse area that hosts endemic fauna. It is home to the critically endangered [Philippine eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_eagle), the vulnerable [Philippine deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_deer), the [Philippine flying lemur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_flying_lemur), and the [Mindanao gymnure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podogymnura_truei).[^cite_note-29] The Mount Kitanglad Mountain Range is home to the Philippine eagle, the [Mindanao pygmy fruit bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao_pygmy_fruit_bat), the Kitanglad shrew-mouse (*[Crunomys suncoides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanglad_shrew-mouse))*, and the gray-bellied mountain rat (*[Limonmys bryophilus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray-bellied_mountain_rat)*). *[Rafflesia schadenbergiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafflesia_schadenbergiana)* has also been found on Mount Kitanglad.[^cite_note-30]

Mount Musuan and Mount Kalatungan, along with [Mount Malindang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Malindang) in Misamis Occisdental, are also home to the Philippine eagle, the Mindanao wrinkled hornbill (*[Aceros leococephalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writhed_hornbill)*), and the Philippine tarsier (*[Tarsius syrichta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarsius_syrichta)*). The three mountains are home to more than 1,000 plant species, including 12 that are endangered, 221 that are endemic, 17 that are rare, and 187 that are economically or socioculturally important.[^cite_note-31]

Based on the 2024 census, Bukidnon has a total population of 1,601,902 residents.[^cite_note-PSA20–10-7]

In the 2000 census, males slightly edge the females with 546,234, accounting for about 52% of the province's total population while females, with 514,181, account about 48%. Based on age distribution, Bukidnon has a fairly young population, with ages 14 and below accounting 42.15% or 446, 952. The 15-34 age bracket account for 33.68% of the province's population or 357,112. Ages 55 and above barely accounts 6.5% of the total. The average population growth rate of the province is 2.05% (2.03% if exponential) from 2000 to 2010. Male-to-female ratio in the province stood at 1.06.

### Population density

The average population density for the province is 128 persons per square km. The cities/municipalities with the highest population densities are the following: [Don Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Carlos,_Bukidnon) (353/km<sup>2</sup>), [Kitaotao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitaotao,_Bukidnon) (250/km<sup>2</sup>), [Valencia](2137518.md) (244/km<sup>2</sup>), [Maramag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maramag,_Bukidnon) (213/km<sup>2</sup>) and [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon,_Bukidnon) (202/km<sup>2</sup>). The cities/municipalities with the lowest densities, on the other hand are: [Impasugong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impasugong) (29/km<sup>2</sup>), [Talakag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talakag,_Bukidnon) (58/km<sup>2</sup>), [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Bukidnon) (63/km<sup>2</sup>), [Malitbog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malitbog,_Bukidnon) (75/km<sup>2</sup>) and [Damulog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damulog,_Bukidnon) (83/km<sup>2</sup>).

### Ethnicity



Bukidnon's population by ethnic origin (2000)

According to ethnicity, majority of the people in Bukidnon are [Cebuanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_people) accounting for approximately 41% of the total population. The Bukidnon Lumads (Bukidnon, Higaonon, Manobo, Talaandig, etc.) account for about 24% of the total population of the province. The Maranaos form about 8% of the total population followed by the [Hiligaynon/Ilonggo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people) and [Boholano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boholano_people) groups with 12.7% and 7.37%, respectively, of the province's total population. The rest consists of [Iranuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranun_people), [Maguindanaons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people), [Bicolanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicolano_people), [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people), [Ivatans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivatan_people), [Karay-a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karay-a_language), [Kapampangans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people), [Pangasinans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_people), [Sambals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal_people), and [Warays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray_people).

Indigenous inhabitants of Bukidnon are the [Lumad peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad_peoples), including the Bukidnon, Higaonon, Manobo, and Talaandig. Their cultures and traditions are embodied in [oral folk literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_literature) of the province, which are classified into; *antoka* (riddles), *basahan* (proverbs or wise sayings), *kaliga* (ceremonial songs), *limbay* (lyric poem), *sala* (love song), *idangdang* (ballad), *ulaging* (epic), and *nanangon* (folktales). Religion is monotheistic. They believe in one [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God). *Magbabaya* (the ruler of all) has minor gods and goddesses under his command (Example: *Bulalakaw* watches rivers and lakes, *Tumpas Nanapiyaw* or *Itumbangol* watches the bases of the earth, night, and day).

Many of the province's inhabitants, however, are descendants of immigrants from [Cebu](160278.md) or elsewhere in Central Visayas.

*[Binaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binaki)*, a type of steamed corn cake wrapped with [corn husks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_husk) is believed to have originated in Bukidnon

Bukidnon is an [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) economy. It is a major producer of [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [sugar](27712.md), [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber), [pineapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), [banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana), [tomato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato), [flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flower), [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), and other fruits and vegetables. Almost all large firms operating in the province are into production or processing of these agricultural products. Recognized by the Food and Agriculture Organization as a foot-and-mouth disease-free province, it is also a major producer of [poultry](23197.md), [hogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hog_\(swine\)), [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat), and [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle).

The [Philippine Carabao Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Carabao_Center)'s outlet in [Central Mindanao University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Mindanao_University) makes dairy products from [water buffalos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabao). Due to being [landlocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlock), Bukidnon relies on the nearby cities of [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro) and [Davao](489401.md) for the supply of marine products. However, the province has its own supply of freshwater products such as [tilapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilapia), [carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carp), [catfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catfish), [mudfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channa_striata), [gourami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_gourami), [barb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_barb), [goby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goby), [climbing perch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabas_testudineus), [freshwater eels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anguillidae), [giant freshwater prawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrobrachium_rosenbergii) or [shrimps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caridea), native [freshwater snails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_snail) or [freshwater clams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_bivalve), and [freshwater crabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_crab), either via inland fishing or [fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fry_\(biology\)) production aquaculture in inland [fish farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_farming) and [fish hatcheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_hatcheries).



[Del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Monte_Foods) Pineapple fields in [Manolo Fortich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manolo_Fortich,_Bukidnon)

[Del Monte Philippines, Inc. (DMPI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Monte_Foods), Lapanday Diversified Products Corp. and Mt. Kitanglad Agri-Development Corporation are engaged in pineapple production. [Dole Philippines (Skyland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dole_Food_Company) and Mt. Kitanglad Agri-Ventures, Inc. are into banana production. DMPI is also engaged in cattle fattening. Bukidnon Sugar Milling Corporation (BUSCO) and Crystal Sugar Milling are into sugar milling and refining.

Phil-Agro Industrial Corporation is in starch production. Menzi Agricultural Development is in [cacao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean) production. Agaropyta Phils. Inc., Bukidnon Greens Inc., FP Obrero Farms and ARDEM, Inc. are in cut-flower production.

Food manufacturing giants, [San Miguel Foods Corp. (SMPFCI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_Pure_Foods_Company), Monterey Farms Corp., [Swift Foods, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_Pacific_Food) have intensified their contract breeding and growing operations in the province. Valencia Rubbertex, Inc., an 80-20 Japanese-Filipino joint venture produces rubber boots and rubber shoes for Japan.



[Robinsons Place Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinsons_Place_Valencia)

As one of the major anchors in crop production, Bukidnon is moving forward towards establishing its position as a principal trader of rice, corn, sugar, potato, tomato and many other commercial and industrial crops. As the second largest producer of corn in the country, it reached a total production of 481,370 metric tons. In year 2000, vast tracts of cornfields, rice paddles, and sugar plantations are distributed all over the province.

Bukidnon has already assumed its role as producer and supplier of fresh fruits and vegetables. These produce are either sold in domestic markets or exported to Japan and other neighboring countries. Fresh pineapples, banana, sugarcane and cut flowers grown over the years are among its exports. New agri-business industries are still growing such as [strawberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry) farming. Even export of rubber boots and shoes, an infant industry in the province is increasing tremendously.

A wide variety of resource-based handicrafts is extensively produced from [rattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattan), [bamboo](39029.md), and [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood). [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Bukidnon) is known for its rattan furniture. Bamboo baskets, wood wares and carvings, mats, and other handmade products are ideal souvenir items.

### Bukidnon Investment Grid

During the mid-1990s, the provincial government of Bukidnon, after careful studies and consultation, has adopted a strategic program called the Bukidnon Investment Grid or BIG. This program is aimed to confine all its investment promotion activities and projects to the strip of land three kilometers from both sides of the [Sayre Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayre_Highway) from [Damulog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damulog,_Bukidnon) to [Manolo Fortich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manolo_Fortich,_Bukidnon), and along the national/provincial road from [Kibawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibawe,_Bukidnon) to [Kadingilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadingilan,_Bukidnon); [Don Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Carlos,_Bukidnon) to Kadingilan; [Maramag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maramag,_Bukidnon) to [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon,_Bukidnon); Maramag to Kadingilan; Kadingilan to [Pangantucan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangantucan,_Bukidnon); [Valencia City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_City,_Bukidnon) to [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Bukidnon); [Malaybalay City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaybalay_City) to [Cabanglasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabanglasan,_Bukidnon); Malaybalay to [Lantapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantapan,_Bukidnon); [Manolo Fortich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manolo_Fortich,_Bukidnon) to [Libona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libona,_Bukidnon); Libona to [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro); [Talakag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talakag,_Bukidnon) to [Pangantucan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangantucan,_Bukidnon); and [Malitbog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malitbog,_Bukidnon) to [Tagoloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagoloan,_Misamis_Oriental) in [Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Oriental).

## Transportation

The province is very well accessible by road primarily from the cities of [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro) and [Davao](489401.md) with alternate road networks as well from the cities of [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan) (via [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur)), [Iligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iligan) (via [Talakag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talakag)), [Marawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marawi_City) (via Talakag), [Tagum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagum_City) (via [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Bukidnon)), and the province of [Cotabato](297394.md).



Sayre Highway in Malaybalay City

The entire province is bisected from north to south by the four-lane [Sayre Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayre_Highway) from [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro) to [Kabacan, Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabacan) via [Maramag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maramag) ([Bukidnon–Cotabato Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukidnon%E2%80%93Cotabato_Road)); and by the two-to-four lane [Bukidnon–Davao Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukidnon%E2%80%93Davao_Road) (colloquially known as "BuDa") from [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon,_Bukidnon) to [Davao City](489401.md). Both are components of National Route 10 (N10) of the [Philippine highway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_highway_network), and a spur of the Asian Highway 26 (AH26) of the [Asian highway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_highway_network). The Sayre Highway intersects with the BuDa Road in the barrio of Dologon in Maramag, where it changes to route N943 and continues on to [Dangcagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangcagan), [Damulog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damulog), and [Kabacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabacan).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] The planned [Central Mindanao Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Mindanao_Expressway?action=edit\&redlink=1) will allow drivers to avoid Sayre Highway.

The Sayre Highway (formerly "Route 3") was renamed in honor of [Francis Bowes Sayre, Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Bowes_Sayre,_Sr.), the U.S. Philippine High Commissioner who spearheaded its construction during the [American occupation of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_occupation_of_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-54]

There are several airstrips in the province being used by private firms. Commercial flights used to be flown from the [Malaybalay Airstrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaybalay_Airport), but it was closed down by the provincial government in the late 1990s. The airport where it used to be located was converted into a low-cost housing project. A proposed domestic airport site in the municipality of [Don Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Carlos,_Bukidnon) has already been on the talks since 2008 and in 2013, the Bukidnon Airport Development Project proposal was finalized.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Budget allocation was done in 2017[^cite_note-58] and construction started in 2018.[^cite_note-59] There are no seaports in Bukidnon because the province is [landlocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked). The nearest passenger seaport is in Cagayan de Oro.

## Festivals



A Kaamulan float

The province celebrates the [Kaamulan Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaamulan_Festival), an ethnic cultural festival held annually in Malaybalay, Bukidnon from the mid-February up to March 10, the founding date of the Bukidnon as a province in 1917. It is held to celebrate the culture and tradition of the seven ethnic tribal groups—*Bukidnon, Higaonon, Talaandig, Manobo, Matigsalug, Tigwahanon, and Umayamnon*—that originally inhabit the province. Kaamulan comes from the indigenous [Binukid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binukid_language) word *amul* meaning "to gather". Kaamulan is gathering for a purpose—a datuship ritual, a wedding ceremony, a thanksgiving festival during harvest time, a peace pact, or all of these together. The festival started in 1974 and is celebrated until now. It is the only authentic ethnic festival in the Philippines.[^cite_note-diplomats-60]

Further information: [Kaamulan Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaamulan_Festival)
