Bulgaria was represented at the [Eurovision Song Contest 2026](79992910.md) with the song "[Bangaranga](82538076.md)", written by [Anne Judith Wik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Judith_Wik), [Cristian Tarcea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoir), [Darina Yotova](31712238.md) and [Dimitris Kontopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitris_Kontopoulos), and performed by Yotova under her stage name Dara. The Bulgarian participating broadcaster, [Bulgarian National Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Television) (BNT), returned to the contest after a three-year hiatus and organised the national final *Natsionalnata selektsiya* in order to select its entry for the contest.

Bulgaria was drawn to compete in the second semi-final of the Eurovision Song Contest which took place on 14 May 2026. Performing during the show in position 1, "Bangaranga" was announced among the top 10 entries of the second semi-final and therefore qualified to compete in the final on 16 May. It was later revealed that Bulgaria placed first out of the 15 participating countries in the semi-final with 278 points. In the final, Bulgaria performed in position 12 and placed first out of the 25 participating countries, winning the contest with 516 points. This marked Bulgaria's first victory in the contest, with it becoming the twenty-eighth nation to record a win.

## Background

Main article: [Bulgaria in the Eurovision Song Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest)

Prior to the 2026 contest, [Bulgarian National Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Television) (BNT) had participated in the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md) representing Bulgaria fourteen times since its first entry in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005). Its best result in the contest was second, which it achieved in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2017) with the song "[Beautiful Mess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beautiful_Mess_\(Kristian_Kostov_song\))" performed by [Kristian Kostov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristian_Kostov). To this point, it had achieved another two top five placings at the contest: in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2007) when "Water" performed by [Elitsa Todorova and Stoyan Yankoulov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elitsa_and_Stoyan) placed fifth, and in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2016) when "[If Love Was a Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_Love_Was_a_Crime)" performed by [Poli Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poli_Genova) placed fourth. In [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022), Bulgaria failed to qualify for the final with the song "[Intention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intention_\(Intelligent_Music_Project_song\))" performed by [Intelligent Music Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_Music_Project).[^cite_note-1]

As part of its duties as participating broadcaster, BNT organises the selection of its entry in the Eurovision Song Contest and broadcasts the event in the country. The broadcaster has used various methods to select its entry in the past, including the national final *Bŭlgarskata pesen v „Evroviziya”* between [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005) and [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2013) (used only to select the song in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2010) and 2013, with the artists internally selected), as well as full internal selections between [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2016) and [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2018), in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021) and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022).[^cite_note-2] Bulgaria did not participate in the contest in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2014), [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2015), [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019), and between [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2025); its most recent absence was originally motivated by financial constraints.[^cite_note-3] On 31 October 2025, BNT announced it would return to the contest in 2026.[^cite_note-4] The following day, *[Fakti.bg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakti.bg?action=edit\&redlink=1)* reported that BNT would organise a national final to select its entry for the contest;[^cite_note-5] this was confirmed by the broadcaster on 18 November 2025, but it was not specified whether it would concern only the artist or both the artist and the song.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

## Before Eurovision

### *_Natsionalnata selektsiya_*

*_Natsionalnata selektsiya_* (Националната селекция) was the national final format developed by BNT in order to select its entry for the Eurovision Song Contest 2026. The event took place at BNT Studio 1 in [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia), on the 24 and 31 January, and 28 February 2026, to determine the artist and song respectively.[^cite_note-\:0-6] All shows, which was broadcast on [BNT 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNT_1), as well as online via the broadcaster's website *bnt.bg*, was hosted by [Georgi Lyubenov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgi_Lyubenov?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Boryana Gramatikova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boryana_Gramatikova?action=edit\&redlink=1), with Vladimira Ilieva serving as backstage host.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

#### Format

The selection of the Bulgarian Eurovision entry took place over two stages. The first stage was the artist selection, which consisted of two televised shows on 24 and 31 January 2026, and involved 15 competing acts performing their own previous songs. In the first show, all 15 artists competed and eight acts were selected to advance to the second show based on the combination of votes from the public and a five-member expert jury. Each juror awarded a score from 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest) to each artist, while the public vote awarded a set of points from 1 (lowest) to 15 (highest). Initially, seven artists that had the highest number of points were supposed to advance, but due to a tie between two acts, both were selected to proceed. In the second show, the eight remaining artists were voted upon by the combination of the public vote and five-member expert jury in order to select the winning artist. Each juror awarded a score from 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest) to each artist, while the public vote awarded a set of points from 1 (lowest) to 8 (highest); the artist that had the highest number of combined points was declared the winner. The second stage was the song selection, which took place on 28 February 2026, which featured the winning artist performing three candidate songs. The winning song was selected via the 50/50 combination of votes from a public vote and a ten-member expert jury. Each juror awarded a score from 1 (lowest) to 3 (highest) to each song, and a set of points from 1 to 3 was distributed based on ranking of the combined scores. The public vote awarded an additional set of points from 1 to 3, and the song with the highest number of combined points was declared the winner. Viewers were able to cast a vote via BNT's website during all shows.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-\:1-10]

#### Competing artists

On 18 November 2025, BNT signed an agreement with [Prophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophon) to jointly select the competing artists for the national final, with acts invited based on data provided by Prophon on the Top 40 ranking of the most played artists on digital platforms in Bulgaria over the preceding eight months.[^cite_note-\:0-6] The 15 competing acts were selected by a panel consisting of representatives of BNT and Prophon, and announced on 30 December 2025.[^cite_note-\:1-10]

#### Song selection

The song selection show took place on 28 February 2026, where the winning artist, [Dara](31712238.md), performed three candidate songs. ## At Eurovision



Dara during the second semi-final on 14 May 2026.



Dara and the Bulgarian delegation during the opening ceremony.

The [Eurovision Song Contest 2026](79992910.md) took place at the [Wiener Stadthalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Stadthalle) in [Vienna](55866.md), Austria, and consisted of two semi-finals held on the respective dates of 12 and 14 May and the final on 16 May 2026. All nations with the exceptions of the host country and the "[Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_\(Eurovision\))" (France, Germany, Italy and the United Kingdom) were required to qualify from one of two semi-finals in order to compete for the final; the top ten countries from each semi-final progressed to the final. On 12 January 2026, an allocation draw was held to determine which of the two semi-finals, as well as which half of the show, each country performed in; the [European Broadcasting Union](10380.md) (EBU) split up the competing countries into different pots based on voting patterns from previous contests, with countries with favourable voting histories put into the same pot.[^cite_note-22] Bulgaria was scheduled to open the second semi-final, preceding the entry from [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026).[^cite_note-23]

In Bulgaria, BNT broadcast all shows on [BNT 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNT_1), with live commentary from Elena Rosberg and Petko Kralev.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Semi-final

Keisha von Arnold and [Fredrik Rydman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredrik_Rydman) created the choreography and directed the staging.[^cite_note-26] Rydman had previously staged several entries, most notably [Sweden in 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2015), [Finland in 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023) and [Switzerland in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2024).[^cite_note-27] Supporting dancers in the performance were Iker Cederblom Herrera, Ellinea Siambalis, Lisa Högström, and Mateo Cordova Pomo.[^cite_note-28]

Bulgaria was allocated for the second semi-final, and later, was announced to perform in position one during the show, before the performance from [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026).[^cite_note-29] Dara was joined by Ellinea Afroditi Aina, Iker Cederblom Herrera, Lisa Högström and Mateo Rolando Cordova Pomo as backing dancers for the performance, while Victoria Halkiti provided backing vocals.[^cite_note-30] At the end of the show, the country was announced as a qualifier for the final, marking Bulgaria's first qualification for the final since [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021). It was later revealed that Bulgaria placed first out of the fifteen participating countries in the second semi-final with 278 points. In the jury vote, Bulgaria placed fifth with 94 points, whilst in the televote, Bulgaria placed first with 184 points.[^cite_note-31]

### Final

Following the semi-final, Bulgaria was drawn to in position 12 of the final, following the entry from [Czechia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026) and before the entry from [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026).[^cite_note-32] Dara took part in dress rehearsals on 15 and 16 May before the final, including the jury final where the professional juries cast their final votes before the live show on 16 May. Bulgaria placed 1st in the final, scoring 516 points; 312 points from the public televoting and 204 points from the juries. This marked Bulgaria's first victory in the Eurovision Song Contest, becoming the twenty-eighth country to win the event. This victory also breaks Alexander Rybak's 2009 record for the biggest point differential between a winner and runner-up in an edition.

### Voting

See also: [Voting at the Eurovision Song Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_at_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest)

Below is a breakdown of points awarded by and to Bulgaria in the second semi-final and in the final. Voting during the three shows involved each country awarding sets of points from 1-8, 10 and 12: one from their professional jury and the other from televoting.[^cite_note-votingBasel2025-33] The Bulgarian jury consisted of Kostadin Todorov Filipov, Krasimir Nikolov Gyulmezov, Stefan Mihaylov Dimitrov, [Joanna Nickolova Dragneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Zone_Project), who represented [Bulgaria in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2008), [Krisia Todorova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krisia_Todorova), who represented [Bulgaria in the Junior Eurovision Song Contest 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Junior_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2014), [Maria Ilieva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Ilieva), and Valeria Valerieva Voykova.[^cite_note-semiresults-34] In the first semi-final, Bulgaria placed 1st with 278 points, marking the country's first qualification to the final since [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021). The country placed first in the televote, obtaining 184 points, and fifth in the jury vote with 94 points. In the final, Bulgaria placed 1st overall with 516 points, placing 1st in both the jury vote and the televote. This marked Bulgaria's first win in the contest to date and the first winning entry since [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2017) to receive a unanimous first placing in both jury voting and televoting. Additionally, it broke the record for the largest winning margin over the runner-up, previously held by [Alexander Rybak's "Fairytale"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairytale_\(Alexander_Rybak_song\)) for Norway in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2009). The previous record of 169 points was surpassed with a new margin of 173 points. In the second-semi-final, Bulgaria scored twelve points in the jury vote from [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026) and twelve points in the televote from [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026) and the [Rest of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rest_of_the_World_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest). In the final, Bulgaria scored twelve points in the jury vote from Denmark, [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), Malta and [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), and twelve points in the televote from [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), Luxembourg, Denmark, [Israel](80498867.md), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026), Australia, the United Kingdom, Lithuania, Austria and the Rest of the World. Over the course of the contest, Bulgaria awarded its 12 points to Albania (televote) and Malta (jury) in the second semi-final, and to [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026) (televote) and Malta (jury) in the final.[^cite_note-semiresults-34][^cite_note-finalresults-35][^cite_note-36]

BNT appointed Vladimira Ilieva as its spokesperson to announce the Bulgarian jury's votes in the final.[^cite_note-37]

#### Detailed voting results

Each participating broadcaster assembles a seven-member jury panel consisting of music industry professionals who are citizens of the country they represent and two of which have to be between 18 and 25 years old. Each jury, and individual jury member, is required to meet a strict set of criteria regarding professional background, as well as diversity in gender and age. No member of a national jury was permitted to be related in any way to any of the competing acts in such a way that they cannot vote impartially and independently.[^cite_note-HowItWorks-38] The individual rankings of each jury member as well as the nation's televoting results were released shortly after the grand final.
