**Jaxon Smith-Njigba** ([/ɪnˈdʒɪɡbə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*in-JIG-bə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born February 14, 2002), also known by his initials **JSN**, is an American professional [football](18951490.md) [wide receiver](492476.md) for the [Seattle Seahawks](28390.md) of the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL). He played [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) for the [Ohio State Buckeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Buckeyes_football), setting school records for receptions in a single game (15, twice) and receiving yards in a season (1,606 in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Ohio_State_Buckeyes_football_team)). Smith-Njigba was selected 20th overall by the Seahawks in the first round of the [2023 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NFL_draft). He became a starter in his second season, amassing 1,130 receiving yards and earning his first [Pro Bowl](274117.md) selection. In the 2025 season, Smith-Njigba set a Seahawks franchise record with a league-leading 1,793 receiving yards on 119 receptions, earning him [Offensive Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_NFL_Offensive_Player_of_the_Year) (OPOY) honors. That same season, he helped the Seahawks win [Super Bowl LX](63727016.md).

## Early life

Smith-Njigba was born on February 14, 2002, in [Nacogdoches, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacogdoches,_Texas), to firefighter Maada and high school teacher Jami Smith, and grew up in [Rockwall, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwall,_Texas), attending [Rockwall High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwall_High_School).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Litman-3][^cite_note-4] He is of half white and [Sierra Leonean descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leonean_Americans); his paternal grandparents are from [Sierra Leone](27308.md).[^cite_note-5] He also has Nigerian heritage.[^cite_note-6] After Maada and Jami divorced, Maada began putting Jaxon and his older brother [Canaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan_Smith-Njigba) through daily sports workouts as a means of bonding with and passing the time with his sons, sometimes starting at 6 a.m. Although Maada initially only sought to "tire them out" so he could put them to bed, as they grew up, he found himself surprised at their skill.[^cite_note-Litman-3] [Canaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan_Smith-Njigba) is a professional baseball player who is currently a free agent.[^cite_note-7]

In his early years, Smith-Njigba only went by "Jaxon Smith." He appended "Njigba" to his surname upon reaching high school to honor his grandfather, John, after he died in 2014; John had changed his name to "Smith" upon immigrating to the United States in the 1970s in order to fit in.[^cite_note-Litman-3]

At Rockwall, Smith-Njigba led all high school football players in the [Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas%E2%80%93Fort_Worth_metroplex) with 97 [receptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reception_\(gridiron_football\)) for 1,828 yards and 20 [touchdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchdown) in his junior season.[^cite_note-8] As a senior, he caught 104 passes for 2,094 yards and 35 touchdowns and was named the recipient of both the [Landry Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landry_Award) and the Texas Gatorade Football Player of the Year award.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Smith-Njigba played in the 2019 [All-American Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-American_Bowl_\(high_school_football\)) and was named a high school All-American by *[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated)* and *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today_All-USA_high_school_football_team)*.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Smith-Njigba finished his high school career with 5,346 career receiving yards and 82 touchdowns in 44 games played.[^cite_note-13] A 5 star prospect and the #5 ranked wide receiver in the nation, Smith-Njigba received offers from many top [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) programs including [Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_Fighting_Irish_football), [Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_Sooners_football) and [Ole Miss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Miss_Rebels_football), before ultimately committing to [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## College career

Smith-Njigba played in seven games as a freshman and led Ohio State's freshmen with 10 receptions for 49 yards and one touchdown.[^cite_note-16]

He was named a starter at the slot receiver position for the Buckeyes going into his sophomore season.[^cite_note-17] On November 6, 2021, Smith-Njigba recorded 15 receptions (single game school record) for 240 yards (the second most receiving yards in a game in school history at the time) and one touchdown in a 26–17 win against [Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nebraska_Cornhuskers_football_team).[^cite_note-18] Later that season, he set both an Ohio State single game and [FBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Football_Bowl_Subdivision) Bowl record for receiving yards with 347 in Ohio State's win in the [2022 Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Rose_Bowl).[^cite_note-19] In that game, he also recorded 15 receptions (tying his own record set in the aforementioned Nebraska game) and passed [David Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Boston) for the single season receiving yards record at Ohio State with 1,606.[^cite_note-20]

Smith-Njigba was limited to just three games in 2022 due to a hamstring injury and his decision to not participate in the [College Football Playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Football_Playoff).[^cite_note-21] In addition, Smith-Njigba announced that he would forgo his senior year of college and enter the [2023 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NFL_draft).[^cite_note-22]

Smith-Njigba was selected by the [Seattle Seahawks](28390.md) in the first round (20th overall) of the 2023 NFL draft.[^cite_note-25]

### 2023 season

He caught his first regular-season NFL pass Week 1 against the [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) with a one-yard completion from [Geno Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geno_Smith).[^cite_note-26] Smith-Njigba started his first game in Week 7 of that year after starter [DK Metcalf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DK_Metcalf) was sidelined due to a rib injury;[^cite_note-27] during that game, he caught his first career touchdown pass on a 28-yard pass from Smith.[^cite_note-28] On October 29, Smith-Njigba caught the game winning touchdown pass from Smith on screen pass against the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns).[^cite_note-29] On December 18, Smith-Njigba caught the game winning touchdown pass from [Drew Lock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Lock) on *[Monday Night Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monday_Night_Football)* against the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles).[^cite_note-30] As a rookie, he appeared in all 17 games and made three starts. He finished with 63 receptions for 628 receiving yards and four receiving touchdowns.[^cite_note-31]

### 2024 season

In the 2024 season during the Week 9 game against the Los Angeles Rams, Smith-Njigba recorded a career high game with seven catches for 180 yards and two touchdowns.[^cite_note-32] He finished the 2024 season with 100 receptions for 1,130 yards and six touchdowns.[^cite_note-33] His 100 total receptions on the season tied [Tyler Lockett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Lockett) for the franchise record.[^cite_note-34] Smith-Njigba also made the [Pro Bowl](274117.md) for the first time in his career.[^cite_note-c294-35]

### 2025 season: Offensive Player of the Year and Super Bowl victory

In the 2025 season, Smith-Njigba was named NFC Offensive Player Of The Month after recording 24 catches with 417 yards and three touchdowns in October.[^cite_note-36] On November 23, 2025, Smith-Njigba broke the Seahawks single-season receiving yard record in a game against the [Tennessee Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Titans), surpassing [DK Metcalf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DK_Metcalf)’s previous mark of 1,303 yards.[^cite_note-37]

For the 2025 season, Smith-Njigba led the league in receiving yards with 1,793. [Puka Nacua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puka_Nacua) of the [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Los_Angeles_Rams_season) finished second with 1,715 yards and the next closest receiver would be [George Pickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pickens) of the [Dallas Cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Dallas_Cowboys_season), who was over 300 yards behind Smith-Njigba.[^cite_note-38] He had a receiving touchdown in the Divisional Round against the San Francisco 49ers.[^cite_note-39] In the 2025 [NFC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_Championship_Game) against the Rams, Smith-Njigba set a season-high in receptions with ten, to go with 153 yards and one touchdown, in the 31–27 victory, propelling Seattle to [Super Bowl LX](63727016.md) and the franchise’s first NFC title [in eleven years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Seattle_Seahawks_season).[^cite_note-40]

At season's end, Smith-Njigba was selected to the [Pro Bowl](274117.md), was unanimously named a [first-team All-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Pro) receiver and received the 2025 [AP Offensive Player of the Year award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_NFL_Offensive_Player_of_the_Year).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] He became just the second Seahawk to win Offensive Player of the Year after [Shaun Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaun_Alexander) in 2005. He beat out division rivals [Christian McCaffrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_McCaffrey) and [Puka Nacua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puka_Nacua), as well as [Bijan Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijan_Robinson) and [Drake Maye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_Maye) to win the award.[^cite_note-43] He won his first Super Bowl when the Seahawks defeated the New England Patriots 29–13 in Super Bowl LX.[^cite_note-44] He had four receptions for 27 yards in the Super Bowl.[^cite_note-45]

### 2026 season

On March 20, 2026, the Seahawks exercised the fifth-year option on Smith-Njigba's contract.[^cite_note-46] Three days later, Smith-Njigba and the Seahawks agreed on a four-year, \$168 million contract extension, making him the NFL's highest paid receiver and locking him with the team until 2031.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] On September 9, 2026, Smith-Njigba had his first touchdown of the season during the Seahawks 13–10 win over the Patriots.[^cite_note-49]

On September 20, 2026, versus the [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Cardinals), Smith-Njigba recorded a career high three touchdowns, including a career long 82-yard touchdown in Seattle's 31–7 victory.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] The following week on the road versus the Washington Commanders, Smith-Njigba recorded 10 catches for 128 yards and 2 touchdowns.

## Personal life

Smith-Njigba is a [Christian](5211.md).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] He wears [Sierra Leone](27308.md)'s flag on the back of his helmet to honor his paternal grandparents.[^cite_note-54]

Smith-Njigba has recreated soccer player [Michael Olise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Olise)'s trademark goal celebration after his first touchdown of the season in 2026.[^cite_note-55]
