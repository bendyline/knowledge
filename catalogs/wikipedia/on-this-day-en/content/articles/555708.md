**Halmahera**, formerly known as **Jilolo**, **Gilolo**, or **Jailolo**, is the largest [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island) in the [Maluku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands). It is part of the [North Maluku](453682.md) [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia) of [Indonesia](14579.md), and [Sofifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofifi), the capital of the province, is located on the west coast of the island.

Halmahera has a land area of 17,780 km<sup>2</sup> (6,860 sq mi).[^cite_note-1] It is the 7th largest island of Indonesia. It had a population of 162,728 in 1995; by 2010, it had increased to 449,938 for the island itself (excluding the tip which is considered part of the Joronga Islands, but including [Gebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebe) and Ju islands) and 667,161 for the island group (including all of South Halmahera and [Tidore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidore), but not [Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternate)).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Approximately half of the island's inhabitants are [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Indonesia) and half are [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Indonesia).

## History



A [Togutil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togutil_people) man of interior island of Halmahera.

Sparsely populated Halmahera's fortunes have long been closely tied to those of the smaller islands of [Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternate) and [Tidore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidore), both off its west coast. This island was the site of [Sultanate of Jailolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Jailolo), one of the four kingdoms of Maluku (*Maloko Kië Raha*, of [Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Ternate), [Tidore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Tidore), [Bacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bacan), and Jailolo) in the era before [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) [colonized](7299.md) the entire archipelago. The north of the island is also the base of [Loloda Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loloda_Kingdom?action=edit\&redlink=1), one of the earlier 13th century Moluccan kingdoms, but not recognized as one of the prominent four.[^cite_note-Handoko-4]

During [World War II](32927.md), Halmahera was the site of a [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) naval base at [Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kao,_Indonesia) Bay.

In 1999 and 2000, Halmahera was the site of violence that began as a purely ethnic dispute between residents of (mainly Christian) [Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kao,_Indonesia) and (entirely Muslim) [Malifut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_North_Maluku) districts and then took on a religious nature as it spread through much of the North Moluccas, called the [Maluku sectarian conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_sectarian_conflict). Thousands of people on Halmahera were killed in the fighting between religious militias. In June 2000, about five hundred people were killed when a [ferry](50771.md) carrying [refugees](45547.md) from the fighting on Halmahera sank off the northeast tip of [Sulawesi](28740.md) island. Conspiracy theories about this event abound. A memorial to this tragedy can be found in Duma village in [North Halmahera Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Halmahera_Regency).

Today, much transportation to the rest of Indonesia is through connections on the provincial capital, [Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternate) island although [Tobelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobelo), the largest town on Halmahera, also has direct ferry and cargo sea links to [Surabaya](212635.md) and [Manado](844858.md).

Particularly, since the inauguration of the first ever directly elected [bupati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bupati) (regent head), Tobelo is undergoing rapid development and is aiming at rivaling Ternate's historical dominance. As it is surrounded by flat land, Tobelo has the potential for expansion. Ternate is limited by its size, being a small island which can be driven around in forty-five minutes. Also, in 2010, the provincial government moved the provincial capital from Ternate City to [Sofifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofifi), a small village on the Halmahera coast opposite [Tidore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidore) island.

North Maluku Province consists of eight regencies and two municipalities (cities); five of the regencies and one municipality include a part of Halmahera island. The regencies are [North Halmahera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Halmahera), [West Halmahera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Halmahera), [East Halmahera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Halmahera_Regency), [Central Halmahera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Halmahera_Regency), [South Halmahera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Halmahera_Regency), [Morotai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morotai), [Sula Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sula_Islands) and [Taliabu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliabu), while the municipalities are [Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternate) and [Tidore Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidore). Only Ternate Municipality, and Morotai, the Sula Islands and Taliabu regencies do not include any part of Halmahera.

The coastal area of Halmahera is inhabited by the [Tobelo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobelo_people) (or the *O'Hoberera Manyawa* (literally "people that live outside of the forest")),[^cite_note-ppman-5] while a small semi-nomadic related population lives on the inner parts of Halmahera, the [Togutil people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togutil_people) (or the *O'Hongana Manyawa* (literally "people that live inside of the forest")) with an estimated 1,500–3,000 people (according to studies of ethnologists since 2001).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Somewhere between 300 and 500 people live in isolation in the forest in the [Aketajawe-Lolobata National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aketajawe-Lolobata_National_Park) where they obtain their means of subsistence.[^cite_note-8] Uncontacted members are being threatened by a massive Indonesian mining project to produce nickel for electric car batteries.[^cite_note-9]

## Geology



Topography of Halmahera

The volcanic island lies on an [island arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_arc) that includes the [Raja Ampat Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ampat_Islands), all uplifted by the northward migration of the [continent of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_\(continent\)) and subduction of the [Pacific Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Plate). [Dukono](8417114.md) is an active volcano at the north end of the island. [Mount Ibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ibu) is an active volcano on the island's northwest coast. [Ultramafic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultramafic) rocks and [ophiolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophiolites) form the bedrock over large areas of the eastern peninsula.[^cite_note-10]

## Ecology



*[Semioptera wallacii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semioptera_wallacii)* by [Richard Bowdler Sharpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bowdler_Sharpe) (1847–1909)

Main article: [Halmahera rain forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmahera_rain_forests)

The [flightless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flightless) [invisible rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisible_rail) is [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic) to the island.[^cite_note-IUCN-11] The recently discovered palm tree *[Jailoloa halmaherensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jailoloa_halmaherensis)* is also endemic to Halmahera.[^cite_note-12]

The naturalist [Alfred Russel Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Russel_Wallace) visited Halmahera, as described in his 1869 book *[The Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Malay_Archipelago)*. He considered the [standardwing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardwing) [bird of paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird-of-paradise), *Semioptera wallacii*, to be his greatest prize.[^cite_note-13] It was in February 1858, on the island of [Ternate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternate) (or perhaps while on Halmahera itself), between bouts of fever, that Wallace came to the idea of natural selection via the survival of the fittest. Wallace wrote his ideas during the next couple of days, and sent the historical letter to [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin).

## Mining

Halmahera is the site of several [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) projects.

[PT Weda Bay Nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PT_Weda_Bay_Nickel?action=edit\&redlink=1) operates a nickel and cobalt mining project in North and Central Halmahera Regencies, the [Weda Bay Industrial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weda_Bay_Industrial_Park). It is one of the world's largest nickel mining operations.[^cite_note-14] The Indonesian government has prohibited the export of unprocessed nickel ore since 2022.[^cite_note-15] As a result, the ore must first be processed on the island before being exported. A total of 11 (with three more in construction) power plants have already been built for this process. The park is likely to get even bigger. Several investors have already been found willing to invest.[^cite_note-16] The increased demand for nickel for batteries is one of the reasons for the growth.
