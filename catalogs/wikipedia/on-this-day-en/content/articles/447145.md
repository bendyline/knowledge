**Udmurtia**,[^cite_note-8] officially the **Udmurt Republic**,[^cite_note-9] is a [republic of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia) located in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe). It is administratively part of the [Volga Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Federal_District). Its [capital](181337.md) is the [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia) of [Izhevsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izhevsk).

It was established as the Udmurt (until 1931 — Votskaya) an [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) of the [Udmurts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurts), Udmurtia, within [Russia](25391.md) on 4 November 1920.[^cite_note-10]

## Name

The name *Udmurt* comes from *odo-mort* ('meadow people'), where the first part represents the ([Finno-Ugric) Permic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finno-Ugric_languages) root *od* or *odo* ('meadow, glade, turf, greenery'). This is supported by a document dated 1557, in which the [Udmurts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurt_people)[^cite_note-11] are referred to as *lugovye lyudi* ('meadow people'), alongside the traditional Russian name *otyaki*.[^cite_note-12] Udmurtia, and the Udmurts, are, still considered, [since 1960s](https://archive.org/details/decsy-einfuhrung-in-die-finnisch-ugrische-sprachwissenschaft-1965/page/20/mode/1up), as one of the [Finno-Ugric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finno-Ugric_languages) National Regions and Republics within Russia.[^cite_note-13]

The second part *murt* means 'person' (cf. [Komi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komi_language) *mort*, [Mari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_language) *mari*). It is probably an early borrowing from a [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian_languages) language *mertä* or *martiya* ('person, man'; Sanskrit: Manus or Manushya), which is thought to have been borrowed from the [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language) term *maryá-* ('man, mortal, one who is bound to die'. cf. [Old Indic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Indic) *márya* ('young warrior') and *marut* ('chariot warrior'), both connected specifically with horses and chariots.[^cite_note-14] The [Indo-Europeanists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Europeanists) [T. Gamkrelidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamaz_V._Gamkrelidze) and [V. Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Vsevolodovich_Ivanov) associate this word with horse-riding Altaic tribes in the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

On the other hand, in the Russian tradition, the name 'meadow people' refers to the inhabitants of the left bank of river in particular. Recently, the most relevant is the version of [V. V. Napolskikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Napolskikh) and S. K. Belykh. They suppose that ethnonym was borrowed either from [Indo-Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Iranian_languages) \**anta* 'outside, close, last, edge, limit, boundary' or Turkic-Altaic \**anda*/\**ant* 'oath (in fidelity), comrade, friend'.[^cite_note-17]

## History



Map of the Udmurt Republic.

On November 4, 1920, the Votyak Autonomous Oblast was formed.[^cite_note-1987USSR-1] On January 1, 1932, it was renamed [Udmurt Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurt_Autonomous_Oblast),[^cite_note-18] which was then reorganized into the [Udmurt ASSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurt_ASSR) on December 28, 1934.[^cite_note-1987USSR-1] During [World War II](32927.md), many industrial factories were evacuated from the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR) and western borderlands to Udmurtia.

On October 11, 1991, the Supreme Council of the Udmurt ASSR adopted a law according to which the Udmurt ASSR acquired a new name — the Udmurt Republic.[^cite_note-19]

## Geography

The republic is located to the west of the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains) and borders [Kirov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirov_Oblast), [Perm](1765550.md), [Bashkortostan](735530.md), and [Tatarstan](67135.md).[^cite_note-21]

Udmurtia is a republic in the Russian Federation, located in Central Russia between the branches of the rivers [Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_\(river\)) and its right tributary the [Vyatka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyatka_\(river\)).

The city of Izhevsk is the administrative, industrial, and cultural center of Udmurtia. Geographically, it is located not far from Moscow, the capital and largest city of the Russian Federation. The city has a well-developed transport system (including air, land, and water).

Udmurtia borders Kirov Oblast to the west and north, Perm Oblast to the east, and the Bashkortostan and Tatarstan Republics to the south.

### Climate

The republic has a moderate [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate), with warm summers and cold, snowy winters. Annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) averages 400–600 mm.

## Demographics

Population: 1,452,914 ([2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2021Census-22] 1,521,420 ([2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2010Census-23] 1,570,316 ([2002 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Russian_census));[^cite_note-PopCensus-24] 1,609,003 ([1989 Soviet census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Soviet_census)).[^cite_note-Census1989-25]

Although as of 2007 the population was declining, the decline was stabilizing and was more pronounced in urban areas. Out of the 19,667 births reported in 2007, 12,631 were in urban areas (11.86 per 1,000) and 7,036 were in rural areas (14.88 per 1,000). Birth rates for rural areas are 25% higher than that of urban areas. Of the total of 21,727 deaths, 14,366 were reported in urban areas (13.49 per 1,000) and 7,361 were in rural areas (15.56 per 1,000). Natural decline of the population was measured at −0.16% for urban areas and an insignificant −0.07% for rural areas (the average for Russia was −0.33% in 2007).[^cite_note-26]

### Ethnic groups

According to the [2021 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Census_\(2021\)),[^cite_note-census2021-29] [Russians](59510.md) make up 67.7% of the republic's population, while the ethnic [Udmurts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurt_people) make up only 24.1%. Other groups include [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) (5.5%), [Mari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_people) (0.5%), [Ukrainians](145144.md) (0.3%), and a host of smaller groups, each accounting for less than 0.5% of the republic's total population.

According to a 2012 survey,[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-37] 33.1% of the population of Udmurtia adheres to the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church), 5% are [unaffiliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondenominational_Christianity) generic [Christians](18337522.md), 2% are Eastern Orthodox Christian believers without belonging to any church or members of other [Eastern Orthodox churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_church), 4% are [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), 2% of the population adheres to the [Slavic native faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_native_faith) (Rodnovery) or to [Udmurt Vos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurt_Vos) (Udmurt native faith), 1% adheres to forms of [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), and 1% of the population are [Old Believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Believers). In addition, 29% of the population declares to be "[spiritual but not religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_but_not_religious)," 19% is [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism), and 3.9% follows other religions or did not give an answer to the question.[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-37]

The local Russian Orthodox Church is the Metropolitanate of Udmurtia, comprising the Eparchy of Izhevsk (founded 1927) under Bishop and Metropolitan Viktorin (Kostenkov) (2015), the Eparchy of Glazov (founded 1889) under Bishop Viktor (Sergeyev), and the Eparchy of Sarapul (founded 1868) under Bishop Anthony (Prostikhin) (2015).

#### Jews

Further information: [History of the Jews in Udmurtia and Tatarstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Udmurtia_and_Tatarstan)

Udmurt Jews are a special territorial group of the [Ashkenazi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews), which started to be formed in the residential areas of mixed Turkic-speaking ([Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars), [Kryashens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryashens), [Bashkirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirs), [Chuvash people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuvash_people)), Finno-Ugric-speaking ([Udmurts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurts), [Mari people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_people)) and Slavic-speaking ([Russians](59510.md)) population. The [Ashkenazi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) on the territory of the [Udmurt Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurt_Republic) first appeared in the 1830s.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-39][^cite_note-lechaim.ru-40][^cite_note-migdal.org.ua-41][^cite_note-42] The Udmurt Jewry had formed the local [variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(linguistics\)) on the base of the Yiddish of Udmurtia till the 1930s and features of Yiddish of migrants "joined" into it (in the 1930s and 1940s);[^cite_note-snioo.ru-43] as a result up to the 1970s and 1980s the Udmurt variety of Yiddish (*[Udmurtish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurtish)*) was divided into two linguistic subgroups: the central subgroup (with centers [Izhevsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izhevsk), [Sarapul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarapul), and [Votkinsk](417263.md)) and the southern subgroup (with centers [Kambarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kambarka), [Alnashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alnashi), [Agryz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agryz), and [Naberezhnye Chelny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naberezhnye_Chelny)).[^cite_note-snioo.ru-43] One of the characteristic features of the Udmurtish is a noticeable number of [Udmurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurt_language) and [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatar_language) loan words.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-files.literjewsudmurtia.webnode.ru-45]

## Culture

Further information: [Udmurt cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udmurt_cuisine) and [Music in Udmurtia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_in_Udmurtia)

Udmurt folklore is understood both in a broad sense (*kalyk oner*, *kalyk todon-valan*, *kalyk viz* - folk knowledge, folk wisdom), and in a narrower one (*kalyk kylos*, *kalyk kylburet* - folk poetry, oral poetry). In everyday life, folklore is not divided into genres, it is perceived in unity with material culture, with religious, legal, and ethical aspects. Popular terms-definitions have incorporated the ritual action (*syam*, *nerge*, *yilol*, *kiston*, *kuyaskon*, *syuan*, *madiskon*), symbolically figurative and magically forming words (*madkyl*, *vyzhykyl*, *tunkyl*, *kylbur*), musical and choreographic behavior (*krez*, *gur*, *shudon-serekyan*, *thatchan*, *ecton*).[^cite_note-46]
