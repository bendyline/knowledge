**Braniewo** ([\[braˈɲɛvɔ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish)) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Braunsberg in Ostpreußen*) is a [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town) in northern [Poland](22936.md), in [Warmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmia), in the [Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmian-Masurian_Voivodeship),[^cite_note-TERYT-2] with a population of 16,907 as of June 2021.[^cite_note-population-1] It is the capital of [Braniewo County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braniewo_County).

Founded in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), Braniewo is the oldest city in [Warmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmia), and currently the second largest (after [Olsztyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztyn)), and was its first capital until 1340. From the late medieval period, it prospered from international trade, and in the early modern period, it was a significant center of [higher education in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_in_Poland), called the "Athens of the North."[^cite_note-ciek-3] Additionally, since the 16th century Braniewo is a noted center for [brewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewing). The town features heritage sites in a variety of styles, including [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture), [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture), [Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) and [Gothic Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival_architecture). Braniewo hosts a garrison of the [Polish Armed Forces](40153898.md) and is a member of [Cittaslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cittaslow).[^cite_note-4]

## Location

Braniewo lies on the [Pasłęka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pas%C5%82%C4%99ka) River about 5 km (3 mi) from the [Vistula Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula_Lagoon), about 35 km (22 mi) northeast of [Elbląg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbl%C4%85g) and 55 km (34 mi) southwest of [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad) ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Królewiec*). The Polish border with Russia's [Kaliningrad Oblast](17311.md) lies 6 km (4 mi) north, and may be reached from Braniewo via [National road 54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_road_54_\(Poland\)).

## History

### Middle Ages



Remains of the [Braniewo Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braniewo_Castle)

According to the German geographer [Johann Friedrich Goldbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Friedrich_Goldbeck) (1748–1812), the town originally was named Brunsberg after [Bruno von Schauenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_von_Schauenburg) (1205–1281), bishop of [Olomouc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olomouc) in [Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravia), who accompanied King [Ottokar II of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottokar_II_of_Bohemia) in 1254 and 1267 when the latter participated in the crusade of the [Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Knights) against the [Old Prussians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Prussians).[^cite_note-5] It has also been suggested that the name *Braunsberg* might stem from *Brusebergue* ("camp of the Prussians"), but this notion is not documented.

In 1243, the settlement and the surrounding region of [Warmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmia) was given by the Teutonic Order to the newly created [Bishopric of Warmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Warmia), whose [bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishops_of_Warmia) built his cathedral in the town and made it his chief residence. The city was granted [town privileges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_privileges) based on those of [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck_law) in 1254, but in 1261 was destroyed and depopulated during the second of the [Prussian Uprisings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Uprisings). It was rebuilt in a new location in 1273 and settled by colonists from [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck). In 1284, it was given a new town charter, again based on that of Lübeck. However, the next bishop, Heinrich Fleming (1278–1300), transferred the chapter from Braunsberg to Frauenburg (now [Frombork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frombork)).

In 1296, a [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) abbey was built, and in 1342, a "new town" was added. As the most important trading and harbor city in Warmia, the town prospered as member of the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League), which it remained until 1608. The town maintained trade contacts with England, Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands, and Livonia.[^cite_note-ciek-3]

In 1440, the town was one of the founding members of the [Prussian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Confederation), which opposed Teutonic rule,[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] and upon the request of which King [Casimir IV Jagiellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_IV_Jagiellon) incorporated the territory to the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) in 1454. The town pledged allegiance to the Polish King and recognized his rule in March 1454 in [Kraków](16815.md).[^cite_note-8] After the subsequent [Thirteen Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Years'_War_\(1454%E2%80%931466\)), the Teutonic Knights renounced any claims to the town in the [Second Peace of Thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Peace_of_Thorn_\(1466\)) in 1466.[^cite_note-9] Administratively, it was part of the [Prince-Bishopric of Warmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Warmia) in the new autonomous province of [Royal Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Prussia), later on also in the [Greater Poland Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_Province,_Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland).

### Early modern era

After the secularization of the Teutonic Order in 1525, a large part of its residents converted to [Lutheran](23371382.md) [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism). Duke [Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert,_Duke_of_Prussia), who had been grand master of the Order, sought to unite Warmia with Ducal Prussia (a nearby vassal state of Poland), causing the Catholics of the town to swear allegiance to the king of Poland in return for aid against Protestant Prussia. In 1526 a Polish royal commission released Braunsberg burghers from the oath to the Polish king and handed the town back to [Prince-Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishop) [Mauritius Ferber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius_Ferber). However, just like the entire area of Warmia, Braunsberg swore allegiance to the [Prince-Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishop) of Warmia. Additionally, it had to denounce all Lutheran teachings and hand over Lutheran writings. Thereafter Warmia remained predominantly [Roman Catholic](606848.md).



The oldest known city map of Braniewo, from 1635

Braniewo was occupied by [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) for about three years during the [Livonian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livonian_War) in the 16th century. In Warmia, Lutheran teachings again were suppressed when Prince-Bishop [Stanislaus Hosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaus_Hosius) (1504–1579) brought in the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) and founded the [Collegium Hosianum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Hosianum) [school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_\(school\)). Among the students of the school were Polish Catholic Saint [Andrew Bobola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Bobola), Polish statesmen and high dignitaries [Mikołaj Zebrzydowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miko%C5%82aj_Zebrzydowski)[^cite_note-10] and [Piotr Gembicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Gembicki), Europe's most prominent 17th-century Latin poet [Maciej Kazimierz Sarbiewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maciej_Kazimierz_Sarbiewski),[^cite_note-11] missionary, explorer, mathematician, astronomer and sinologist [Jan Mikołaj Smogulecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Miko%C5%82aj_Smogulecki),[^cite_note-12] and Primate of Poland [Gabriel Podoski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Podoski).[^cite_note-13] Prominent [Hungarian](17106358.md) Renaissance poet [Bálint Balassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A1lint_Balassi) stayed in the town in 1590–1591.[^cite_note-14] A priestly seminary was added in 1564. [Pope Gregory XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XIII) later added a papal mission seminary for northern and eastern European countries. [Regina Protmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Protmann) (1552–1613), a native of Braunsberg (Braniewo), founded the [Saint Catherine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Alexandria) Order of Sisters in the town, recognized by the church in 1583. She was declared the [patroness saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patroness_saint) of Braniewo in 2002.[^cite_note-ciek-3] The Jesuit theologian [Antonius Possevinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonius_Possevinus) was instrumental in enlarging the Collegium Hosianum in the 1580s to counter the growing Protestant movement. The college's library, including the works of [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus), was looted by the Swedes in 1626 and is still housed in the [Uppsala University Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University_Library).[^cite_note-ciek-3]









Polish Catholic publications from 1716–1900

Local brewing traditions date back to the 16th century, with the local beer being called the "best beer in Warmia" since the turn of the 16th and 17th centuries.[^cite_note-mz-15]

Braunsberg was annexed by the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) in 1772 during the [First Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Partition_of_Poland) and made part of the newly formed province of [East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussia) the following year.

### 19th and 20th centuries



[Collegium Hosianum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Hosianum) in the 1840s

In 1812 [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) stopped in the town.[^cite_note-ciek-3] From 1834 to 1849, an astronomical and meteorological observatory operated at the college.[^cite_note-ciek-3] Braunsberg obtained its first railway connection with the rest of the kingdom via the [Prussian Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Eastern_Railway) in 1852. In the early 20th century, the town was the leading academic center of East Prussia next to [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nigsberg). In 1912 the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) college became the State Academy of Braunsberg (German: *Staatliche Akademie Braunsberg*). Prior to World War II, the population of Braunsberg had grown to more than 21,000, of whom 59 percent were listed as Catholic and 29 percent Protestant.

The Second World War turned much of the town into ruins. After three and a half years of savage warfare, Soviet forces began their assault on German land by attacking East Prussia on Jan. 13, 1945. Red Army formations reached the Vistula Lagoon north of Braunsberg on Jan. 26. In early February, German civilians began fleeing from Braunsberg across the ice of the frozen lagoon to the [Vistula Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula_Spit), from which many journeyed to either [Danzig](12099.md) (Gdańsk) or [Pillau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltiysk) (Baltiysk), and managed to board German ships that made the [perilous voyage westward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Hannibal). Braunsberg was captured by Soviet troops on March 20, 1945.

After capturing the town, Soviet soldiers executed 15 local nuns. The nuns were beautified by Pope Leo XIV on 31 May 2025.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

Heavy fighting and wanton destruction afterwards had left the town about [80 percent destroyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Polish_cities_and_towns_damaged_in_World_War_II), including much of its historic town center, largely consumed by fire. After the German surrender, sovereignty over the town was ceremoniously transferred to Polish authorities on July 7.[^cite_note-18]

The previous unilateral Soviet transfer of power to Poland was accepted according to the [Potsdam Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement), however, under preliminary terms. The town was partially repopulated by Polish settlers, many of whom came from areas of eastern Poland [annexed by the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_changes_of_Poland_immediately_after_World_War_II).

After the war, mineral water production began in the former brewery.[^cite_note-mz-15] Following reconstruction, the local brewery was reopened in 1965.[^cite_note-mz-15]

In 2001 the St. Catherine Church, built in 1346, destroyed in 1945, and rebuilt after 1979, was declared a Basilica Minor. This Gothic Hall church was built on a site which had held a previous wooden Church of St. Catherine since 1280. Prince-Bishop [Lucas Watzenrode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Watzenrode_the_Younger) of Warmia (1447–1512) had added extensively to the original building.
