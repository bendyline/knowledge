The **Royal Canadian Mounted Police** (**RCMP**; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Gendarmerie royale du Canada*, **GRC**) is the [national police service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Canada) of [Canada](5042916.md). The RCMP is an agency of the [Government of Canada](616255.md); it also provides police services under contract to 11 [provinces and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_and_territories) (all but [Ontario](22218.md) and [Quebec](7954867.md)), over 150 municipalities, and 600 Indigenous communities. The RCMP is commonly known as the **Mounties** in English (and colloquially in French as *la police montée*).

The Royal Canadian Mounted Police was established in 1920 with the amalgamation of the [Royal North-West Mounted Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_North-West_Mounted_Police) and the [Dominion Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Police). Sworn members of the RCMP have jurisdiction as [peace officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_officers) in all provinces and territories of Canada.[^cite_note-RCMPAct2-14] Under its federal mandate, the RCMP is responsible for enforcing federal legislation; investigating inter-provincial and international crime; border integrity;[^cite_note-15] overseeing [Canadian peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_peacekeeping) missions involving police;[^cite_note-16] countering terrorism both inside and outside the country; managing the [Canadian Firearms Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Firearms_Program), which licenses and registers firearms and their owners;[^cite_note-17] and for the Canadian Police College, which provides police training to Canadian and international police services.[^cite_note-18] Policing in Canada is considered to be a constitutional responsibility of provinces;[^cite_note-CanEncPiC2-19] however, the RCMP provides local police services under contract in all provinces and territories except [Ontario](22218.md) and [Quebec](7954867.md).[^cite_note-RCMPConPolOverview2-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Despite its name, the Royal Canadian Mounted Police are no longer an actual [mounted police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mounted_police) service, and horses are used only at ceremonial events and certain other occasions. Similarly, the familiar red serge, Stetson hat, and boots are only worn at these times.

The Government of Canada considers the RCMP to be an [unofficial national symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbols_of_Canada),[^cite_note-23] and in 2013, 87 per cent of Canadians interviewed by [Statistics Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Canada) said that the RCMP was important to their national identity.[^cite_note-WalrusRCMP-24]

## History

### Early history (1920–1970)

Further information: [North-West Mounted Police § history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Mounted_Police#history)



Several RCMP members involved in the hunt for [Albert Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Johnson_\(criminal\)), 1931.

The Royal Canadian Mounted Police was formed in 1920 by the amalgamation of two separate federal police services: the [Royal North-West Mounted Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_North-West_Mounted_Police) (RNWMP), which had been responsible for colonial policing in the [Canadian West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_West),[^cite_note-TCErnwmp-25] but by 1920 was becoming "rapidly obsolete;"[^cite_note-GlobeRCMPCP-26] and the [Dominion Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Police), which was responsible for federal law enforcement, intelligence, and parliamentary security.[^cite_note-27] The new police service inherited the [paramilitary](146730.md), frontline [policing-oriented culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_culture) that had governed the RNWMP, which had been modelled after the [Royal Irish Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Constabulary),[^cite_note-28] but much of the RCMP's local policing role had been superseded by [provincial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Canada#Provincial) and [municipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Canada#Municipal) police services.

In 1928, the federal government authorized the RCMP to enter into heavily subsidized contracts with provinces and municipalities, enabling the service to return to its roots in local policing. The federal government paid 60 per cent of the policing costs, while provinces and municipalities paid the remaining 40 per cent.[^cite_note-GlobeRCMPCP-26] By 1950, eight of the ten Canadian provinces had disbanded their provincial police services in favour of subsidized RCMP policing.[^cite_note-29]

As part of its national security and intelligence functions, the RCMP infiltrated ethnic or political groups considered to be dangerous to Canada. These included the [Communist Party of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Canada) (founded in 1921) and a variety of Indigenous, minority cultural, and nationalist groups.[^cite_note-30] The service was also deeply involved in immigration matters, and was responsible for deporting suspected radicals. The RCMP paid particular attention to nationalist and socialist [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Canadian) groups[^cite_note-31] and the [Chinese community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Canadian), which was targeted because of disproportionate links to [opium dens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_den). Historians estimate that Canada deported two per cent of its Chinese community between 1923 and 1932, largely under the provisions of the *Opium and Narcotics Drugs Act*.[^cite_note-32] The first Mountie to go undercover was [Frank Zaneth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Zaneth) who under the code name Operative Number 1 infiltrated various "radical" groups along with the Mafia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnuckle200739-33]

In 1932, RCMP members killed Albert Johnson, the [Mad Trapper of Rat River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_Trapper_of_Rat_River), after a shoot-out.[^cite_note-famous-34] Johnson had been the subject of a dispute with local Indigenous trappers—he had reportedly destroyed their traps, harassed them verbally, and on one occasion, pointed a firearm at them—and, when confronted with a search warrant, opened fire on RCMP officers, wounding one.[^cite_note-famous-34][^cite_note-35] Also in 1932, the Customs Preventive Service (CPS), a branch of the Department of National Revenue, was folded into the RCMP at the request of RCMP leadership.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

In 1935, the RCMP, acting as the provincial police service for [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan) (but against the wishes of the Saskatchewan government)[^cite_note-WalrusRCMP-24] and in collaboration with the [Regina Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Police_Service), attempted to arrest organizers of the [On-to-Ottawa Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-to-Ottawa_Trek) in the [Germantown neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina's_historic_buildings_and_precincts#Germantown_proper)'s market square by [kettling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettling) around 300 rally-goers, sparking the [Regina Riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Riot).[^cite_note-ReginaRiot-38] One city police officer and one protester were killed. The trek, which had been organized to call attention to conditions in [relief camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief_Camp_Workers'_Union), consequently failed to reach Ottawa, but nevertheless had political reverberations.[^cite_note-ReginaRiot-38] That same year, three RCMP members, acting under contract as provincial police officers, [were killed in Saskatchewan and Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Royal_Canadian_Mounted_Police_Killings) during an arrest and subsequent pursuit.[^cite_note-39]

During the interwar period, the RCMP employed [special constables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_constable) to assist with [strikebreaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikebreaking). For a brief period in the late 1930s, a volunteer militia group, the [Legion of Frontiersmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Frontiersmen), was affiliated with the RCMP.[^cite_note-40] Many members of the RCMP belonged to this organization, which was prepared to serve as an auxiliary police service.

In 1940, the RCMP [schooner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schooner) [*St. Roch*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Roch_\(ship\)) facilitated the first effective patrol of Canada's Arctic territory. It was the first vessel to navigate the [Northwest Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Passage) from west to east, taking two years, the first to navigate the passage in one season (from Halifax to Vancouver in 1944), the first to sail either way through the passage in one season, and the first to circumnavigate North America (1950).[^cite_note-41]

In 1941, two African-Canadian men from [Nova Scotia](21184.md) applied to join the RCMP. The commissioner at the time, [Stuart Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Wood_\(police_commissioner\)), allegedly allowed them to sit for entrance tests in the hopes that they could be definitively refused entry to the service as "their colour would raise the question of policy."[^cite_note-BEWHITE6AM-42] Both men ultimately passed the requisite tests, but neither was given an offer of employment.[^cite_note-BEWHITE6AM-42]

In the wake of the 1945 defection of [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) cipher clerk [Igor Gouzenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Gouzenko), who revealed that the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [was spying on Western nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_espionage), the RCMP separated its units responsible for domestic intelligence and counter-espionage from the [Criminal Investigation Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Investigation_Branch) to the new Special Branch, formed in 1950.[^cite_note-CBCrcmpHistory-43] The branch changed names twice: in 1962, to the Directorate of Security and Intelligence; and in 1970 to the Security Service.[^cite_note-CBCrcmpHistory-43] On April 1, 1949, [Newfoundland and Labrador](21980.md) joined in [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) with Canada, and the [Newfoundland Ranger Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_Ranger_Force) amalgamated with the RCMP.

In June 1953, the RCMP became a full member of the [International Criminal Police Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Police_Organization) (Interpol).[^cite_note-44] During the 1960s, the RCMP entered into an agreement with the [Canadian Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canadian_Press) to transmit images and fingerprints of criminals using the press organization's wirephoto equipment.[^cite_note-45] In 1969, the RCMP hired its first [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Canadians) police officer, Hartley Gosline.[^cite_note-BEWHITE6AM-42]

### Late 20th century



A member of the [Emergency Response Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Response_Team_\(RCMP\)) in training in 2010. The tactical unit was formed in 1977.

On July 4, 1973, during a visit to [Regina, Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina,_Saskatchewan), [Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_of_Canada) [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II) approved a new badge for the RCMP. The force subsequently presented the sovereign with a tapestry rendering of the new design.[^cite_note-46]

In 1978, the RCMP formed 31 part-time [emergency response teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Response_Team_\(RCMP\)) across the country to respond to serious incidents requiring a tactical police response.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

In 1986, in the wake of the [1985 Turkish embassy attack in Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Turkish_embassy_attack_in_Ottawa) and the bombing of [Air India Flight 182](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_India_Flight_182), the Canadian government directed the RCMP to form the [Special Emergency Response Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Emergency_Response_Team) (SERT), a full-time counter-terrorism unit.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

In the early 1990s, journalists at the [Canadian Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation)'s *[The Fifth Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_\(TV_series\))* opened an investigation into rumours that a senior RCMP officer in the [Criminal Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Intelligence_Service_Canada) (CISC) was on the payroll of a Montreal-based organized crime group, and in 1992, aired an episode identifying Inspector [Claude Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Savoie_\(policeman\)), then the assistant director of the CISC, as the leak, citing evidence that connected him to [Allan Ronald Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Ronald_Ross), an Irish-Canadian [drug lord](23002805.md), and [Sidney Leithman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Leithman), a prominent lawyer associated with Montreal's organized crime network.[^cite_note-51] Shortly after the episode aired, and minutes before being interviewed by detectives with the RCMP's professional standards unit, Savoie committed suicide in his Ottawa office.[^cite_note-52] One of Savoie's subordinates, Portuguese-Canadian constable [Jorge Leite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Leite), was [found guilty of corruption and breach of trust by a Portuguese court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Leite#Trial,_conviction_and_return_to_Canada) about his work with Savoie.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

In 1993, the SERT was disbanded and its counter-terrorism duties transferred to the [Canadian Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces), in the newly formed [Joint Task Force 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Task_Force_2) (JTF2). JTF2 inherited some equipment and the SERT's former training base near [Ottawa](22219.md).



RCMP security detail at the gates of [24 Sussex Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_Sussex_Drive), 2008. The Personal Protection Group was created in 1995.

In 1995 the Personal Protection Group (PPG) of the RCMP was created at the behest of [Jean Chrétien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chr%C3%A9tien) after the break-in by [André Dallaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Dallaire) at the prime minister's official Ottawa residence, [24 Sussex Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_Sussex_Drive).[^cite_note-ppggm-55] The PPG is a 180-member group responsible for VIP security details, chiefly the prime minister and the governor general.[^cite_note-Switched_on_-_Blue_Line-56]

In 1998, the RCMP, with the permission of the owners [AEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Energy_Company), bombed an oil well shed as part of a 'dirty tricks' campaign during a dispute between AEC and [Wiebo Ludwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiebo_Ludwig).[^cite_note-57]

#### RCMP Security Service (1950–1984)

Main article: [RCMP Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCMP_Security_Service)

Further information: [Canadian Security Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Security_Intelligence_Service) and [PROFUNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PROFUNC)

The [RCMP Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCMP_Security_Service) (RCMPSS) was a specialized political intelligence and counterintelligence branch with [national security](240468.md) responsibilities following revelations of illegal [covert operations](613453.md) relating to the [Quebec separatist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_separatist_movement).[^cite_note-ceroyco-58] As a result, the RCMPSS was replaced by the [Canadian Security Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Security_Intelligence_Service) (CSIS) in 1984, and is statutorily independent of the RCMP.

In the late 1970s, revelations surfaced that the RCMP Security Service had in the course of their intelligence duties engaged in crimes such as burning a barn and stealing documents from the separatist [Parti Québécois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parti_Qu%C3%A9b%C3%A9cois). This led to the [Royal Commission of Inquiry into Certain Activities of the RCMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_of_Inquiry_into_Certain_Activities_of_the_RCMP), better known as the "McDonald Commission", named for the presiding judge, Justice David Cargill McDonald. The commission recommended that the service's intelligence duties be removed in favour of the creation of a separate intelligence agency, CSIS. The RCMP and CSIS nonetheless continue to share responsibility for some law enforcement activities in the contemporary era, particularly in the anti-terrorism context.[^cite_note-59]

### 21st century



Memorial for the victims of the [Mayerthorpe Tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayerthorpe_Tragedy) in [Whitecourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitecourt), [Northern Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alberta)

Due to [9/11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11), the RCMP [Sky Marshals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_marshal#Canada), which is charged with security on passenger aircraft, was inaugurated in 2002.[^cite_note-60]

Four RCMP officers were fatally shot during the [Mayerthorpe tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayerthorpe_tragedy) in [Northern Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alberta) in March 2005. It was the single largest multiple killing of RCMP officers since the killing of three officers in Kamloops, British Columbia, by a mentally ill assailant in June 1962. Before that, the RCMP had not incurred such a loss since the [North-West Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Rebellion).[^cite_note-CBC-61] One result was that on 21 October 2011 Commissioner [William J. S. Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_J._S._Elliott) announced that RCMP officers would have the [C8 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C8_rifle) at their disposal, where in the past they had been limited to sidearms. One of the main conclusions from the fatality inquiry that led to this result was the fact that the officers who were involved in the events did not have the appropriate weapons to face someone with a semi-automatic rifle.[^cite_note-wjegm-62]

In 2006, the [United States Coast Guard](32223.md)'s Ninth District and the RCMP began a program called "Shiprider", in which 12 Mounties from the RCMP detachment at Windsor and 16 U.S. Coast Guard boarding officers from stations in Michigan ride in each other's vessels. The intent was to allow for seamless enforcement of the international border.[^cite_note-63]



An RCMP corporal and [U.S. Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard) member conduct a boarding as a part of Shiprider law enforcement operations. RCMP-U.S. Coast Guard Shiprider operations began in 2006.

On December 6, 2006, [RCMP Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCMP_Commissioner) [Giuliano Zaccardelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuliano_Zaccardelli) resigned after admitting that his earlier testimony about the [Maher Arar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maher_Arar) case was inaccurate. The RCMP's actions were scrutinized by the [Commission of Inquiry into the Actions of Canadian Officials in Relation to Maher Arar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_of_Inquiry_into_the_Actions_of_Canadian_Officials_in_Relation_to_Maher_Arar). In the aftermath of the Arar affair, the commission of inquiry recommended that the RCMP be subject to greater oversight from a review board with investigative and information-sharing capacities.[^cite_note-\:1-64] Following the commission of inquiry's recommendations, the [Harper government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41st_Canadian_Parliament) tabled amendments to the RCMP Act to create the [Civilian Review and Complaints Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_Review_and_Complaints_Commission_for_the_Royal_Canadian_Mounted_Police).[^cite_note-\:1-64]

In the wake of the 2007 [Robert Dziekański taser incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Dzieka%C5%84ski_taser_incident) at the [Vancouver International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_International_Airport), two officers were found guilty of perjury to the [Braidwood Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braidwood_Inquiry) and sentenced to jail for their actions. They appealed to the [Supreme Court of Canada](123468.md) but were unsuccessful.

In July 2007, two RCMP officers were shot and succumbed to their injuries in the [Spiritwood Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritwood_Incident) near [Mildred, Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mildred,_Saskatchewan).

By the end of 2007, the RCMP was named [Newsmaker of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsmaker_of_the_Year) by [The Canadian Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canadian_Press).[^cite_note-65]

#### 2010s

The RCMP mounted the [Queen's Life Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Life_Guard) in May 2012 during celebrations of [Queen Elizabeth II's Diamond Jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II's_Diamond_Jubilee).[^cite_note-66]

On June 3, 2013, the RCMP's A Division was renamed the "National Division" and tasked with handling corruption cases "at home and abroad".[^cite_note-67]



Local businesses express their gratitude for the RCMP after the suspect for the [Moncton shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moncton_shooting) was apprehended.

In June 2014, three RCMP officers were murdered during the [Moncton shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moncton_shooting).[^cite_note-68] A review from retired assistant commissioner Alphonse MacNeil in May 2015 issued 64 recommendations, while the RCMP was charged with violating the [Canada Labour Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Labour_Code) (CLC) for the slow roll-out of the C8 carbine, which had been recommended by the 2011 Elliott inquiry. The RCMP issued the first carbines in 2013, and with 12,000 members across the country had, as of May 2015, only purchased 2,200.[^cite_note-aacbc-69] At the CLC trial the Crown argued that the then newly-retired head of the RCMP [Bob Paulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Paulson_\(police_commissioner\)) had "played the odds" with officer safety and it proved fatal.[^cite_note-cbcodds-70] One result of the CLC trial was the conviction of the organization that had been led by Paulson for close to seven years.[^cite_note-cprcmp-71]

In October 2016, the RCMP issued an apology for harassment, discrimination, and sexual abuse of female officers and civilian members. Additionally, they set aside a \$100 million fund to compensate these victims. Over 20,000 current and past female employees who were employed after 1974 are eligible.[^cite_note-72]

In January 2019 of that year, the RCMP enforced an injunction against the Wet’suwet’en first nation, raiding the Unist’ot’en Camp and arresting 14 people. This sparked widespread protests and solidarity actions across Canada after reports surfaced of the use of violence by the RCMP.[^cite_note-73]

#### 2020s

In February 2020, The RCMP again enforced the injunction, leading to further arrests and escalating tensions. Rail blockades and other disruptions occurred across the country in support of the Wet’suwet’en. There was widespread condemnation across Canada of the surveillance tactics employed by the RCMP. During one protest, two journalists were arrested by the RCMP during the protests, prompting an investigation by the federal government.[^cite_note-74] The RCMP was further criticized when video footage of officers breaking into the homes of Wet'suwet'en community members and pointing weapons at peaceful protesters surfaced on youtube.[^cite_note-75] In audio recordings played in the BC supreme court, RCMP officers referred to First Nations opposed to gas pipelines as "orcs" and "ogres".[^cite_note-76]

On March 10, 2020, Chief [Allan Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Adam) of the [Athabasca Chipewyan First Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athabasca_Chipewyan_First_Nation) was arrested by two RCMP officers in [Fort McMurray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_McMurray), Alberta.[^cite_note-\:2-77][^cite_note-nytaa-78] After several minutes of Chief Adam yelling and posturing at officers, the officers tackled him and punched him in the head whilst struggling with him on the ground. Chief Adam was later charged with resisting arrest and assaulting a peace officer, but the charges were subsequently dropped.[^cite_note-79] After watching the video of the arrest, Prime Minister [Justin Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Trudeau) said, "\[w]e have all now seen the shocking video of Chief Adam's arrest and we must get to the bottom of this".[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-\:2-77][^cite_note-81] Following the revelation of Chief Adam's arrest—as well as several other recent instances in which RCMP officers had assaulted or killed Indigenous people[^cite_note-82]—RCMP Commissioner [Brenda Lucki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Lucki) stated, after initially demurring on the question, that [systemic racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_racism) exists in the RCMP: "I do know that systemic racism is part of every institution, the RCMP included", she said.[^cite_note-83] One day earlier, Trudeau had also stated that "\[s]ystemic racism is an issue right across the country, in all our institutions, including in all our police services, including in the RCMP."[^cite_note-84]

RCMP Constable Heidi Stevenson was killed while responding to the [Wortman killing spree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nova_Scotia_attacks) that left 23 dead in [Nova Scotia](21184.md) in April 2020. The political furor that followed engulfed Commissioner Brenda Lucki and her minister, [Public Safety Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Safety_Minister) [Bill Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Blair).[^cite_note-ctcbc-85] The RCMP was strongly criticized for its response to the attacks, the deadliest rampage in Canadian history,[^cite_note-AP-86] as well as their lack of transparency in the criminal investigation. [CBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_News)' television program *[The Fifth Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_\(TV_program\))* and online newspaper *[Halifax Examiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Examiner)* analyzed the timeline of events, and both observed a myriad of failures and shortcomings in the RCMP response.[^cite_note-TheFifthEstate-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] A criminologist criticized the RCMP's response as "a mess" and called for an overhaul in how the agency [responds to active shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_shooter_training) situations, after they had failed to properly respond to other such incidents in the past.[^cite_note-Global.Aftermath-90]

In the early 2020s, several governments, politicians, and scholars recommended terminating the RCMP's contract policing program.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-cox1-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Public Safety Minister [Marco Mendicino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Mendicino) was mandated to conduct a review of RCMP contract policing when he took office in 2022.[^cite_note-cox1-92]

In June 2021, Privacy Commissioner of Canada [Daniel Therrien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Therrien) found that the RCMP had broken Canadian privacy law through hundreds of illegal searches using [Clearview AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearview_AI).[^cite_note-clearview-95]

In February 2022, four men were arrested near [Coutts, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coutts,_Alberta), for their roles in an [alleged conspiracy to kill RCMP officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_alleged_plot_to_kill_Coutts_RCMP_officers) during the [Canada convoy protest](69876497.md).[^cite_note-\:22-96]



RCMP members leading the funeral procession during the [state funeral for Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Elizabeth_II) in [London](17867.md), 2022.

On September 19, 2022, the RCMP led the procession through London, England, following the [state funeral of Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Elizabeth_II) due to the long-standing special relationship with the Queen.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

In 2023, the Mass Casualty Commission recommended that the RCMP replace its Depot-based training regime with a more intensive university-style program and that the federal public safety minister review the RCMP's involvement in contract policing.[^cite_note-99] Later that year, the force established a new direct-entry program for federal policing candidates.[^cite_note-100] Those recruited for the program will be required to complete a shorter, more focused 14-week training curriculum in Ottawa before being posted to a federal policing position.[^cite_note-101] As of 2024, the implementation is suspended due to concerns raised by unions.[^cite_note-102]

In the early 2020s, the cities of [Surrey, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey,_British_Columbia), and [Grande Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Prairie), [Alberta](717.md), both established independent municipal police forces to replace the RCMP. In the wake of these decisions, and similar moves by the governments of [Alberta](717.md) and [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan) to establish supplementary provincial police services to support (and, according to some critics, eventually replace) the RCMP, Commissioner Mike Duheme indicated that the RCMP was learning how to better manage transitions to local policing from contract policing.[^cite_note-103] Similar transitions have been proposed, debated, or approved in some Alberta First Nations, rural [Manitoba](18926.md), and rural [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick).[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

In the 2020s, the RCMP played a leading role in Canada's counterterrorism efforts, notably through its [Integrated National Security Enforcement Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_National_Security_Enforcement_Teams) (INSETs), investigating and disrupting both religiously and ideologically motivated [violent extremism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_extremism).[^cite_note-106] Between 2022 and 2025, the RCMP arrested and charged multiple individuals in terrorism investigations, including a Toronto man arrested in December 2022 fundraising for Daesh ([Islamic State](9087364.md)),[^cite_note-107] a Montréal teenager arrested in August 2025 accused of planning an attack on behalf of the Islamic State,[^cite_note-108] and four individuals charged in July 2025 in connection with an anti-government [militia](20622.md) plot in Quebec.[^cite_note-109]

### Response to protests

Policing by the RCMP and other public and [private security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_security) has been central to disputes over [land claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_claims) and assertions of [sovereignty](28791.md) over land use by Indigenous peoples ([First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) and [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis)). Recent notable confrontations over rights to self-determination of land use precipitated around opposition to pipeline infrastructure through traditional and ancestral lands of Indigenous peoples. Recent scholarship highlights how the policing of [anti-pipeline movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-pipeline_movements?action=edit\&redlink=1) (e.g. against the [Coastal GasLink pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Canadian_pipeline_and_railway_protests) or the [Dakota Access pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Access_Pipeline_protests) in North Dakota, United States) can actively enforce the violation of Indigenous rights and perpetuate Canada's [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) dependency and the country's contribution to the [climate crisis](17987598.md).[^cite_note-110]



RCMP members and demonstrators in 2021 at the [Fairy Creek blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy_Creek_old-growth_logging_protests)

The [Canadian Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation) (CBC) gained access to internal police documents exposing "\[a]n RCMP national security unit monitored First Nations-led anti-pipeline activism for potential threats to the energy, transportation and banking sectors between 2021 and 2022."[^cite_note-111] This unit, named the [Community-Industry Response Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community-Industry_Response_Group?action=edit\&redlink=1) (C-IRG), was formed in 2016,[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] originally to secure the [Coastal GasLink pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_GasLink_pipeline) project, a pipeline carrying natural gas from northeastern BC to the BC coast transiting [Wetʼsuwetʼen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet%CA%BCsuwet%CA%BCen) and other First Nations territories, much of which remains [unceded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_title).

Surveillance by the RCMP and [CSIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Security_Intelligence_Service) also played a role in suppressing environmental activists.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-\:12-115] Internal RCMP accounting shows that the C-IRG unit spent almost \$50 million on policing pipeline, logging standoffs in BC in its first five years of operations.[^cite_note-\:02-116] This spending comprised approximately \$3.5 million, \$27.6 million, and \$18.7 million policing the [Trans Mountain Expansion pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_Mountain_pipeline#Protests), [CoastalGas Link pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_GasLink_pipeline#Protests), and [Fairy Creek logging blockage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy_Creek_old-growth_logging_protests), respectively.[^cite_note-\:02-116]

#### Policing targets

##### Land defence

###### Northern Gateway Pipeline

An access-to-information request revealed requests by the National Energy Board to the RCMP and CSIS to monitor and report on advocacy groups who opposed pipeline projects.[^cite_note-\:23-117] According to the records, a member of CSIS or RCMP allegedly infiltrated a community organizing meeting and wrote a report that was shared with [Enbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enbridge) — the company that owned the never-built [Northern Gateway pipeline project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enbridge_Northern_Gateway_Pipelines) — as well as other prominent oil and gas industry leaders part of [Natural Resources Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Resources_Canada). The [BC Civil Liberties Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Civil_Liberties_Association) launched two complaints regarding "improper surveillance" on advocacy groups, claiming the act unconstitutional and illegal to surveil such "peaceful democratic activities."[^cite_note-\:23-117]

###### Trans Mountain Pipeline

[Project Sitka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Sitka?action=edit\&redlink=1) was a coordinated intelligence effort to compile names of Indigenous and non-Indigenous activists who may use "unlawful tactics" in Indigenous resistance protests. The initiative was concluded in 2015 but was revived in 2016 after the Trans Mountain pipeline expansion's approval. The RCMP instructed officers to "provide any updates to Project Sitka's list of disruptive and volatile subjects from respective divisions."[^cite_note-\:12-115]

The RCMP spent around \$3.5 million policing protests around the [Trans Mountain Expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_Mountain_Expansion) project.[^cite_note-\:02-116]

Tiny House Warriors, a group of Indigenous activists who erected small houses along the pipeline's right of way,[^cite_note-118] were attacked by a group of masked assailants who destroyed ritual installations, attacked activists, and stole and drove one of the activists cars into their protest house.[^cite_note-119] A spokesperson for the Tiny House Warriors, Kanahus Manuel, whose car was used by assailants to attempt to demolish her house, told *[The Tyee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tyee)* that "there was not even any attempt by the RCMP to look for these guys."[^cite_note-120]

##### Coastal Gas Link pipeline

Further information: [2020 Canadian pipeline and railway protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Canadian_pipeline_and_railway_protests)

On December 14, 2018, a provincial court granted [TransCanada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TC_Energy) (now TC Energy) an injunction to proceed with construction of their [Coastal Gas Link pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_GasLink_pipeline) — a 670 km long pipeline that passes through the Wetʼsuwetʼen Nation. At the time, the [Unistʼotʼen Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unist%CA%BCot%CA%BCen_Camp) blockade was protesting the development. A new checkpoint was set up on land of the [Gitimʼten)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitim%CA%BCten\)?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-121] one of the five clans of the Wetʼsuwetʼen Nation, to continue blocking access to the construction site.



A banner displaying "RCMP off Wetʼsuwetʼen land" on a train in Vaughan, Ontario, at a demonstration in solidarity with Wetʼsuwetʼen hereditary chiefs in 2020

On January 7, 2018, the RCMP raided the Gitimʼten checkpoint, arresting 14 people. Prior to the raid, RCMP commanders had instructed and encouraged violence, "lethal overwatch" (a term to deploy lethal force), and that arrests were necessary to "sterilize the site."[^cite_note-122]

After the violent evictions, and surveillance recorded of Indigenous land defenders, the United Nations' Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination published a letter calling for more information on the ceasing of construction of the Trans Mountain Pipeline and the Coastal Gas Link Pipeline due to the related harms caused to the [Secwépemc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secw%C3%A9pemc) and [Wetʼsuwetʼen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet%CA%BCsuwet%CA%BCen) peoples.[^cite_note-123]

A report by Amnesty International detailed widespread "intimidation and harassment" of Wetʼsuwetʼen people by the RCMP when protecting the CoastalGas Link's construction.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125]

##### Global summits

Several global summits where protests erupted decrying global injustices suffered violent repression by RCMP and local police forces. The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) 3rd Summit of the Americas hosted in Quebec City from April 20 to 22, 2001 faced massive protests, referred to as the [Quebec City (or A20) protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Summit_of_the_Americas#Protests). Police liberally fired tear gas and rubber bullets and deployed water cannons to attack and disperse the crowd.[^cite_note-126] On November 13, 2003, the complaint's chairwoman Shirley Heafey argued that "RCMP members used excessive and unjustified force in releasing tear gas to move the protesters when a more measured response could have been attempted first."[^cite_note-127]



Kettled protesters in Toronto during the G20 protests

Other summits where the RCMP played a role in a controversial police response to protests include the [Security and Prosperity Partnership of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_and_Prosperity_Partnership_of_North_America#Trilateral_summit_meetings) (SPP) summit in Montebello, Quebec in August 2007, where masked protesters believed to be undercover police incited violence.[^cite_note-128] There was also what was then named the "largest-ever police spy operation" aimed at activists organizing the [2010 G20 Summit protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_G20_Toronto_summit_protests) in Toronto, Ontario.[^cite_note-\:32-129] It was revealed via Freedom of Information requests that "at least 12 undercover officers infiltrated groups" spanning Vancouver, southern Ontario, Toronto, Montreal, and Ottawa, in one of the largest-ever such operations internal to Canada.[^cite_note-\:32-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

### Role in the colonization of Canada

Main articles: [Colonial militia in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_militia_in_Canada) and [French and Indian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_Wars)

Further information: [Canada under British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_under_British_rule), [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas), and [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France)

As the federal police service of the [Government of Canada](616255.md), the RCMP has had an expansive and controversial role in the [colonization of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization_of_Canada). One of the RCMP's two preceding agencies—the Royal Northwest Mounted Police (RNWMP)—was established in 1873 by the government of John A. MacDonald, as a means of controlling the First Nation and Métis populations to advance settler colonialism from the West.[^cite_note-\:11-134] The RNWMP operated as a full-circle, independent police, prosecution, and prison justice system that would arrest, charge, and convict individuals.[^cite_note-135] Through this system they were successful in surveilling and restricting Indigenous people to reserves and regulating land use, often by criminalizing Indigenous individuals.[^cite_note-\:11-134] After the signing of the [Numbered Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbered_Treaties) between 1871 and 1899, however, the service generally failed to provide Indigenous communities with police services equal to those provided to non-Indigenous communities.[^cite_note-TCErnwmp-25]

This colonial foundation carrying onto the RCMP, has left lasting impacts for Indigenous people. Given the historical context, many Indigenous people feel distrust and resentment toward the law enforcement.[^cite_note-136] Additionally, Indigenous peoples are still overrepresented in the criminal justice system and report higher levels of policing and police-based violence, abuse and discrimination.[^cite_note-137]

American historian Andrew Graybill argued the RCMP historically resembled the [Texas Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Ranger_Division) in many ways: each protected the established order by confining and removing Indigenous peoples; tightly controlling the [mixed-blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-blood) peoples (the [African Americans](2154.md) in Texas and the [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis) in Canada); assisting the large-scale ranchers against the small-scale ranchers and farmers who fenced the land; and breaking the power of [labor unions](2474406.md) that tried to organize the workers of industrial corporations.[^cite_note-138]



A Mountie standing with an [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit) group in [Kinngait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinngait) to celebrate the establishment of [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut), 1999

From 1920 (1933, with respect to the *[Indian Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Act)*)[^cite_note-139] to 1996, RCMP officers served as [truant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truant) officers for [Indian residential schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_residential_schools), including through the transition of students from federal residential to provincial day schools after 1948,[^cite_note-140] assisting principals, staff, [Indian agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_agent_\(Canada\)), relatives, and members of the communities in bringing truant children to the schools,[^cite_note-report2-141] sometimes by force,[^cite_note-142] as per the *Indian Act*,[^cite_note-report2-141] and as was common for truant non-Indigenous children through the same period.[^cite_note-143] Marcel-Eugène LeBeuf stated in his report for the RCMP that records and oral histories indicate the force "was responding, in its most traditional police role, to a request to protect children"[^cite_note-144] and that [abuses within the residential school system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_genocide_of_Indigenous_peoples) were largely unreported to the RCMP at the time.[^cite_note-report2-141]

During the federal government's imposition of municipal-style elected councils on [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) people, the RCMP raided the government buildings of particularly resistant traditional hereditary chiefs' councils and oversaw the subsequent council elections—the [Six Nations of the Grand River Elected Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Nations_of_the_Grand_River_Elected_Council) was originally referred to as the "Mounties Council" as a result of the RCMP's involvement in its installation.[^cite_note-MountiesCouncil-145]

According to declassified documents released in 2025 after a 4-year investigation by CBC Indigenous,[^cite_note-146] starting as early as 1968 and lasting into the early 1980s, the RCMP performed covert surveillance on Indigenous organizations and specific people (extending beyond suspected radicals), dubbed the "Native extremism program", which included paid informants, [wiretapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiretapping) and video capture.[^cite_note-147] The RCMP issued a statement of regret, which was rejected by the [Assembly of First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_First_Nations).[^cite_note-148]

#### Role in land disputes

In 1995, the RCMP intervened in the [Gustafsen Lake standoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustafsen_Lake_standoff) between the armed Ts'peten Defenders, occupying what they claimed was unceded Indigenous land, and armed ranchers, who owned the land and had previously allowed Indigenous people to use part of it on the condition they not erect permanent structures. The RCMP's response included 400 tactical assault team members, five helicopters, two surveillance planes, and nine [Bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bison_and_Coyote_armoured_vehicles) [armoured personnel carriers](19278686.md) on loan from the [Canadian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Army)[^cite_note-Bison_APC-149] and sparked international controversy over the RCMP's use of unusually broad press exclusion zones.[^cite_note-150] One of the members of the Ts'peten Defenders was later granted political asylum in the United States after an Oregon judge found that the RCMP's reporting of the incident—marked by an RCMP member's off-hand comment to media that "smear campaigns are \[the RCMP's] specialty"—amounted to a "disinformation campaign."[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-Globe-152]

Between January 2019 and March 2020, the RCMP spent \$13 million policing and periodically enforcing [injunctions](39343.md) against Indigenous [protesters blocking the construction of a pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_GasLink_Pipeline#Protests) across what the protesters asserted was unceded Wet'suwet'en territory.[^cite_note-CBCWt-153] Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs Na'moks and Woos complained about the armed RCMP presence, as the police moved down the road, kilometre-by-kilometre, over days, dismantling fortified checkpoints and making arrests.[^cite_note-CBCWt-153] The RCMP's enforcement of a court injunction against the occupiers in 2020 sparked [international controversy and protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Canadian_pipeline_and_railway_protests). As of 2022, sporadic occupations and protests have continued at the site. There have also been attacks on infrastructure and work camps, allegedly by outside groups unaffiliated with Wet'suwet'en and other local people.[^cite_note-154]

### Women in the RCMP



A female Mountie during a [Remembrance Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remembrance_Day) ceremony, 2017

In the 1920s, Saskatchewan provincial pathologist [Frances Gertrude McGill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Gertrude_McGill) began providing forensic assistance to the RCMP in their investigations.[^cite_note-\:0-155] She helped establish the first RCMP forensic laboratory in 1937,[^cite_note-156] and later was its director for several years. In addition to her forensic work, McGill also provided training to new RCMP and police recruits in forensic detection methods.[^cite_note-\:0-155] Upon her retirement in 1946, McGill was appointed honorary surgeon to the RCMP and continued to act as a dedicated consultant for the service up until her death in 1959.[^cite_note-157]

On May 23, 1974, RCMP Commissioner [Maurice Nadon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Nadon) announced that the RCMP would accept applications from women as regular members of the service. Troop 17 was the first group of 32 women at [Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCMP_Academy,_Depot_Division) in Regina on September 16, 1974, for regular training.[^cite_note-158] This first all-female troop of 30 women graduated from Depot on March 3, 1975.[^cite_note-159]

After initially wearing different uniforms, female officers were finally issued the standard RCMP uniforms. Now all officers are identically attired, with two exceptions. The ceremonial dress uniform, or "walking-out order", for female members has a long, blue skirt and higher-heeled slip-on pumps plus a small black clutch purse (however, in 2012 the RCMP began to allow women to wear trousers and boots with all their formal uniforms).[^cite_note-160] The second exception is the official maternity uniform for pregnant female officers assigned to administrative duties.

## Organization

### National

The RCMP is organized under the authority of the *Royal Canadian Mounted Police Act* (*RCMP Act*), an act of the [Parliament of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada). Under sections 3 and 4 of the *RCMP Act*, the RCMP is a police service for Canada; namely, a federal police service.[^cite_note-165] However, section 20 of the *RCMP Act* provides that the RCMP may be used for law enforcement in provinces or municipalities if certain conditions are met.[^cite_note-166] As explained by Justice [Ivan Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Rand) of the Supreme Court of Canada, "what is set up is a police service for the whole of Canada to be used in the enforcement of the laws of the Dominion, but at the same time available for the enforcement of law generally in such provinces as may desire to employ its services."[^cite_note-167]

&#x20;Under section 5 of the *RCMP Act*,[^cite_note-168] the agency is headed by the [commissioner of the RCMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioner_of_the_RCMP), who, under the direction of the [minister of public safety and emergency preparedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Public_Safety_\(Canada\)), has the control and management of the service and all matters connected therewith. The RCMP is provided with a senior executive committee (SEC) which[^cite_note-169]

> is the senior decision-making forum established by the Commissioner for the development and approval of strategic, service-wide policies, under and consistent with the Commissioner's authority under section 5 of the RCMP Act. The role of \[the] SEC is to develop, promote, and communicate strategic priorities, strategic objectives, management strategies, and performance management for direction and accountability.

&#x20;The commissioner is assisted by deputy commissioners in charge of Contract and Indigenous Policing, Federal Policing, and Specialized Policing Services. The commanding officers of K Division and [E Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_Division) are also named deputy commissioners.[^cite_note-170]

#### Divisions

The RCMP divides the country into [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_division) for command purposes. In general, each division is coterminous with a province (for example, [C Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Royal_Canadian_Mounted_Police) in Quebec). The province of Ontario, however, is divided into two divisions: National Division ([National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(Canada\))) and O Division (rest of the province). There is one additional division, Depot Division, which comprises the RCMP Academy in [Regina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina,_Saskatchewan) and the Police Dog Service Training Centre[^cite_note-dogs-171] in [Innisfail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innisfail,_Alberta). ##### Detachments



An RCMP detachment at [Grise Fiord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grise_Fiord), Nunavut

A detachment is a section of the RCMP that polices a local area. Detachments vary greatly in size.

The largest RCMP detachment is in [Burnaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaby), British Columbia.[^cite_note-173] Previously, Surrey, British Columbia, once had the largest detachment with over a thousand employees. However, amid criticism over gang violence and growing debate during the 2018 civic election, the municipal [Surrey Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey_Police_Service) eventually assumed jurisdiction, ending Surrey's RCMP policing contract that had been in place since 1951.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175]

Conversely, detachments in small, isolated rural communities have as few as three officers. The RCMP formerly had many single-officer detachments in these areas,[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-SingleDetach-177] but in 2012 the RCMP announced that it was introducing a requirement that detachments should have at least three officers.[^cite_note-SingleDetach-177]

As of 2022, several large Indigenous communities do not have RCMP detachments and are instead served by detachments in much smaller non-Indigenous communities.[^cite_note-178]

### Personal Protection Group

Further information: [Monarchy of Canada § Federal residences and royal household](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Canada#Federal_residences_and_royal_household)



A member of the Personal Protection Group opens the door for then-Prince Charles (now King [Charles III](125248.md)), 2009

The Personal Protection Group (PPG) is a 180-member group responsible for security details for the [monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Canada), other members of the [royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Canada#Royal_family_and_house), and other VIPs.[^cite_note-Switched_on_-_Blue_Line-56] It was created after the 1995 [break-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Dallaire#The_break-in) at [24 Sussex Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_Sussex_Drive).[^cite_note-ppggm-55] There are three units within the PPG: The Governor General's Protection Detail and the Prime Minister's Protection Detail provide bodyguards for the safety of the [governor general of Canada](38152.md) and the [prime minister of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Canada), respectively, in Canada and abroad. These units are based in [Ottawa](22219.md), the former with operations at [Rideau Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rideau_Hall) (the monarch's and governor general's official residence in the capital) and the latter at 24 Sussex Drive (the prime minister's official residence) and [Harrington Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrington_Lake) (the prime minister's retreat), near [Chelsea, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea,_Quebec). The Very Important Persons Security Section provides security details to VIPs (including the [chief justice of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_justice_of_Canada), federal ministers other than the prime minister, and diplomats) and others under the direction of the minister of public safety.

### Regular members



An RCMP constable arresting an individual, August 2010

The term *regular member*, or RM, originates from the *RCMP Act* and refers to the 18,988 regular RCMP officers who are trained and sworn as [peace officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_officer), and include all the ranks from constable to commissioner. They are the police officers of the RCMP and are responsible for investigating crime and have the authority to make arrests. RMs operate in over 750 detachments, including 200 municipalities and more than 600 Indigenous communities. RMs are normally assigned to general policing duties at an RCMP detachment for a minimum of three years. These duties allow them to experience a broad range of assignments and experiences, such as responding to [emergency (9-1-1) calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_telephone_number), foot patrol, bicycle patrol, traffic enforcement, collecting evidence at crime scenes, testifying in court, apprehending criminals and plain clothes duties. Regular members also serve in over 150 different types of operational and administrative opportunities available within the RCMP, these include major crime investigations, emergency response, forensic identification, [forensic collision reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicular_accident_reconstruction), international peacekeeping, bike or marine patrol, explosives disposal, and police dog services. Also included are administrative roles including human resources, corporate planning, policy analysis, and public affairs.

#### Community constables (CC)

A designation introduced in 2014 as a replacement for the community safety officers and Indigenous community constables pilot programs.[^cite_note-ACC-185][^cite_note-CSO-186] Community constables are armed, paid members holding the rank of special constables, with peace officer power.[^cite_note-187] They are to provide a bridge between the local citizens and the RCMP using their local and cultural knowledge.[^cite_note-188] They are mostly focused on crime prevention, liaisons with the community, and providing resources in the event of a large-scale event.[^cite_note-189]

#### Reserve constables (R/Cst.)

A program reinstated in 2004 in British Columbia, it was later expanded to cover all of Canada to allow for retired, regular RCMP members and other provincially trained officers to provide extra manpower when shortages are identified.[^cite_note-RCMPNBVet-190] R/Cst. are appointed under Section 11 of the *Royal Canadian Mounted Police Act* as paid part-time, armed officers with the same powers as regular members.[^cite_note-191] However, they are not allowed to carry service-issued sidearms and [use of force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_of_force) options unless they are called upon to duty.[^cite_note-RCMPNBVet-190] They generally carry out community policing roles but may also be called upon if an emergency occurs.[^cite_note-RCMPNBVet-190][^cite_note-192]

#### Auxiliary constables (A/Cst.)

Volunteers within their community, appointed under provincial police acts.[^cite_note-auxil-8] [Auxiliary constables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_Constable#Royal_Canadian_Mounted_Police_Auxiliary) are not police officers and can not identify themselves as such. However, they are given peace officer powers when on duty with a regular member (RM). Their duties consist mainly of assisting RMs in routine events, for example, cordoning off crime scene areas, crowd control, participating in [community policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_watch), and assistance during situations where regular members might be overwhelmed with their duties (e.g., keeping watch of a backseat detainee while an RM interviews a victim). They are identified by the wording "RCMP Auxiliary" on cars, jackets, and shoulder flashes.

#### Special constables (S/Cst.)

Employees of the RCMP have varied duties depending on where they are deployed but are often given this designation because of the expertise they possess that needs to be applied in a certain area. For example, an Indigenous person might be appointed a [special constable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Constable#Canada) to assist regular members as they police an Indigenous community where English is not well understood, and where the special constable speaks the language well.

They still perform this role today in many isolated northern communities and the RCMP has 122 special constables who are active, and they are drawn almost entirely from the same Indigenous communities that they serve. From the early years of policing in northern Canada, and well into the 1950s, local Indigenous people were hired by the RCMP as special constables and were employed as guides to obtain and care for sled dog teams. Many of these former special constables still reside in the north to this day and are still involved in the regimental functions of the RCMP. Most pilots for RCMP aircraft, such as fixed-wing planes or helicopters, are special constables.

#### Livestock Investigators

Specialized Officers that conduct criminal investigations involving livestock such as [fraudulant branding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestock_branding) and [cattle rustling](1553326.md). As of August 2026 there were four RCMP Livestock Investigators across Canada.[^cite_note-193]

#### Civilian criminal investigators (CCI)

CCIs were implemented in 2021. They are civilian unarmed staff members, with limited peace officer status and are restricted from making physical arrests.[^cite_note-194] CCIs have backgrounds in computer science or financial markets and are involved in specialized investigations.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-184] They participate in interviews, the preparation of court documents, and the searching of scenes.

#### Public service employees

Also referred to as public servants, PSes, or PSEs, they provide much of the administrative support for the RCMP in the form of detachment clerks and other administrative support at the headquarters level. #### Municipal employees

Abbreviated as "ME" they are found in RCMP detachments where a contract exists with a municipality to provide front-line policing. ### Ranks

See also: [Police ranks of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_ranks_of_Canada)

The rank system of the RCMP is partly a result of their origin as a [paramilitary](146730.md) service. Upon its founding, the RCMP adopted the rank insignia of the [Canadian Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Militia) (which in turn came from the [British Army](4887.md)). As in the military, the RCMP also has a distinction between commissioned and non-commissioned officers.[^cite_note-197] The non-commissioned ranks are mostly based on military ranks (apart from constable). Non-commissioned officer ranks above staff sergeant resemble those that formerly existed in the Canadian Army, but have since been replaced by [warrant officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_officer).[^cite_note-198] The commissioned officer ranks, by contrast, use a set of non-military titles that are often used in Commonwealth police services. The number of higher ranks like chief superintendent and deputy commissioner have been added on and increased since the formation of the service, while the lower commissioned rank of sub-inspector has been dropped.

The inspector ranks and higher ranks are commissioned ranks and are appointed by the [Governor-in-Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-in-Council). Depending on the badges and dresses which are worn on the shoulder as slip-ons, on shoulder boards, or directly on the [epaulettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epaulette). The lower ranks are non-commissioned officers, and the insignia continues to be based on pre-1968 Canadian Army patterns. Since 1990, the non-commissioned officers' insignias & insignias have been embroidered on the epaulette slip-ons. Non-commissioned rank badges are worn on the right sleeve of the scarlet/blue tunic and blue jacket. Constables wear no rank insignia. There are also 122 special constables, as well as a varying number of reserve constables, auxiliary constables, and students who wear identifying insignia.

The [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath_star), or "pip", used in the insignia of commissioned officers represents the military Knight Grand Cross of the [Order of the Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Bath). The order's motto (*tria juncta in uno*, 'three joined in one', referring to the [holy trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_trinity)) is inscribed in a band in the middle of it. The three crowns inset in the centre not only represent the Christian Trinity but also the three former kingdoms that became the United Kingdom. The RCMP formerly had subaltern ([junior officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_officer)) ranks that were indicated by one "pip" for a sub-inspector (equivalent to an army second lieutenant) to three "pips" for an inspector (equivalent to an army captain).[^cite_note-204] A reorganization in 1960 changed the insignia to three "pips" for sub-inspectors[^cite_note-205] and a crown for inspectors,[^cite_note-206] making the latter a [field officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_officer) rank. The rank of sub-inspector was abolished in 1990, leaving the RCMP with no subaltern ranks.

A royal crown is used in the regimental cap badge and the insignia of senior commissioned officers. In 1955 [St. Edward's Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Edward's_Crown) replaced the [Tudor Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Crown). Although Queen Elizabeth II adopted the redesign of the heraldic crown in 1953, it took some time to design, approve, and manufacture the new insignia.

The crossed [Mameluke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mameluke) [sabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilij) and [baton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_\(military\)) is the insignia for [general officers](148537.md). In the RCMP it designates the commissioner (equivalent to an army general) and their subordinate deputy commissioners (equivalent to army lieutenant-generals). The assistant commissioners use the crown-over-three-pips insignia of an army [brigadier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier#British_tradition).

The brass shoulder title pin on the epaulettes was changed from "RCMP" to "GRC-RCMP" in 1968. (*GRC* stands for *Gendarmerie royale du Canada*, the RCMP's French-language title). This was due to a 1968 ruling stating that all statutes had to be published bilingually in both English and French. As a law enforcement agency, the RCMP had to use ranks and titles in both languages. This was later reinforced by the [*Official Languages Act* (1969)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Languages_Act_\(Canada\)) .

### Honorary positions and the role of the Royal Family.



Prince Charles (now King [Charles III](125248.md)) in [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia), wearing an RCMP lapel pin, May 2014

Several members of the [Canadian royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_royal_family) hold honorary titles in the RCMP. These roles are comparable to the colonel-in-chief and honorary colonel positions in the Canadian Army, serving as promoters of the service's identity, traditions, and history, as well as making occasional visits to operational units. The commissioner-in-chief of the RCMP receives information and updates on important activities and serve as an advisor to the force's commanding officer; although, they do not play an operational role with the service.[^cite_note-rcmproyalhist-207]

In addition to members of the Canadian Royal Family holding these positions, as federal law enforcement officers all members of the RCMP swear [allegiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oath_of_Allegiance_\(Canada\)) to the Monarch of Canada, currently King [Charles III](125248.md).[^cite_note-208]

#### Commissioner in chief

See also: [Colonel-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel-in-chief)

The commissioner-in-chief is the most senior honorary and ceremonial leadership position in the RCMP; it is held by Charles III, King of Canada, who was bestowed the role prior to his coronation in 2023.[^cite_note-\:6-209]

The role was established as a separate role for the Canadian monarch from that of honorary commissioner in 2012. The first holder was [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II), who was bestowed the title in celebration of [her diamond jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II).[^cite_note-\:7-210] The role was created to show and maintain the close link between the Canadian monarch and the RCMP. The role has no day-to-day operational function, but the commissioner-in-chief receives regular updates on the important activities of the RCMP, as well as promoting the Mounted Police both in Canada and abroad, and visiting RCMP events.[^cite_note-\:7-210] Upon appointment the commissioner-in-chief is presented with a Royal Canadian Mounted Police officer's sword, bearing the Canadian coat of arms and the royal cypher.[^cite_note-\:6-209]



Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II) with her RCMP Queen's Police Officer in [Toronto](64646.md), July 2010

Before the creation of commissioner in chief, the role of honorary commissioner was usually held by periodically by the Canadian monarchs and heirs apparent. Since 2023 the role of honorary commissioner has not been in use.[^cite_note-\:7-210]

#### Honorary deputy commissioner

Honorary deputy commissioners are honorary positions held by senior members of the Canadian Royal Family, whose role is also to show the connection of the Royal Family and the RCMP.

The current honorary deputy commissioners are [Anne, Princess Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne,_Princess_Royal), and [Prince Edward, Duke of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward,_Duke_of_Edinburgh).[^cite_note-\:7-210]

### Marine craft



An RCMP vessel near South Pender Island [British Columbia](3392.md), 2010

The RCMP polices [Canadian Internal Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Internal_Waters), including the [territorial sea](496983.md) and contiguous zone as well as the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) and [Saint Lawrence Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_Seaway); such operations are provided by the RCMP's Federal Services Directorate and include enforcing Canada's environment, fisheries, customs, and immigration laws. In provinces and municipalities where the RCMP performs contract policing, the service polices freshwater lakes and rivers.

To meet these challenges, the RCMP operates the Marine Division, with five [Robert Allan Ltd.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Allan_Ltd.)–designed high-speed [catamaran](195952.md) [patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_watercraft); *Inkster* and the *Commissioner*-class *Nadon*, *Higgitt*, *Lindsay* and *Simmonds*, based on all three coasts and manned by officers specially trained in maritime enforcement. *Inkster* is based in Prince Rupert, BC, *Simmonds* is stationed on Newfoundland's south coast, and the rest are on the Pacific Coast.[^cite_note-rcmp-grc.gc.ca-223] *Simmonds*' livery is unique, in that it sports the RCMP badge, but is otherwise painted with [Canadian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Coast_Guard) colours and the marking *Coast Guard Police*. The other four vessels are painted with blue and white RCMP colours.

The RCMP operates 377 smaller boats, defined as vessels less than 9.2 m (30 ft) long, at locations across Canada. This category ranges from [canoes](99960.md) and car toppers to rigid-hulled inflatables and stable, commercially built, inboard-outboard vessels. Individual detachments often have smaller high-speed [rigid-hulled inflatable boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid-hulled_inflatable_boat) and other purpose-built vessels for inland waters, some of which can be hauled by road to the nearest launching point.[^cite_note-rcmp-grc.gc.ca-223]

#### Past weapons and intervention options

##### Rifles

- Canadian Arsenals Limited (CAL) [C1A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C1_Rifle) – issued in [7.62mm NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7.62mm_NATO). Canadian variant of the [FN FAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN_FAL) and [L1A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L1A1) produced under licence by Canadian Arsenals Limited (CAL) (Long Branch). The RCMP's rifles were sourced from the testing batch of FALs received from [Fabrique Nationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrique_Nationale) and had been rebuilt by CAL to meet C1A1 standards. Used from 1961 to 1969.

- [Winchester Model 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_Model_70) Issued in [.308 Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.308_Winchester). Used from 1960–1973. This rifle was replaced by the Remington 700.

- [Lee–Enfield No. 4 Mk 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rifle_No_4) – issued in [.303 British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.303_British). World War II surplus rifles were used from 1947 to 1966. Replaced by CAL C1A1 and Winchester 70.

- [Short Magazine Lee Enfield (SMLE) No. 1 Mk III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Magazine_Lee-Enfield_Mk_III) – issued in [.303 British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.303_British). World War I surplus rifles used from 1919–1947.

  [^cite_note-calibremag.ca-233]

- [Lee-Enfield carbine (LEC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charger_Loading_Lee-Enfield) – issued in [.303 British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.303_British). Procured as military surplus from militia stores to replace the unsatisfactory Ross Rifle. Used from 1914 to 1920. This was the last general-issue rifle used by the NWMP. The RCMP that replaced it only issued rifles according to need.

- [Ross rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_rifle) – issued in .303 British. The Ross Mk I was issued from 1905 to 1907 and the improved Ross Mk II was in testing from 1909 to 1912.

  [^cite_note-calibremag.ca-233]

  &#x20;The Mk I design was accepted by the Canadian Militia in 1903. The NWMP looked at acquiring the Ross to replace the Winchester and Lee-Metford and ordered 1000. Production problems led to delays until 1904; the most glaring being that the finished product did not match their original specifications.

  [^cite_note-calibremag.ca-233]

  &#x20;The NWMP demanded their contract carbines use a different set of iron sights (which later became standard on the Mk II) which delayed production for a further year.

  [^cite_note-calibremag.ca-233]

  &#x20;The carbines received in 1905 were plagued with quality control problems that made them more fragile than the weapons they were to replace. After a constable suffered an eye injury in 1907 the Ross carbines were withdrawn.

  [^cite_note-calibremag.ca-233]

  &#x20;When the improved Ross Mk II rifles arrived in 1909 the wary NWMP decided to test-fire all of them fully before issuing them. A fire at the depot in Regina in 1911 destroyed almost all of the new rifles.

  [^cite_note-calibremag.ca-233]

  &#x20;The NWMP then gave up on the Ross.

- [Magazine Lee-Enfield (MLE) Mk.I rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charger_Loading_Lee-Enfield) – issued in .303 British; it was the first smokeless powder weapon in NWMP service. Loaned to the NWMP from the Victoria and Winnipeg militias to replace a stolen cache of M1876 Winchesters. The NWMP "forgot" to give them back later. Used from 1902 to 1920.

- [Lee-Metford carbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee%E2%80%93Metford) – issued in .303 British. The Metford rifling gave tighter groups when fired than the later Enfield, but the rifling wore out faster. Only 200 were procured. Used from 1895 to 1914. Replaced by the Lee-Enfield carbine.

- [Winchester Model 1876](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_Model_1876) saddle [carbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbine) – issued in [.45-75 Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.45-75_Winchester). Popular for its handiness and rate of fire, but it was too fragile for the rough handling and use it received in the field. Used from 1878 until 1914.

  [^cite_note-234]

  &#x20;and replaced by the Lee-Enfield Carbine.

- [Snider–Enfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snider%E2%80%93Enfield) Mark III cavalry carbine – issued in [.577 Snider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.577_Snider). Single-shot breach-loading conversion of an Enfield caplock muzzle-loader. Used from 1873 to 1878 and replaced by the Winchester Model 1876 lever-action rifle.

##### Pistols

Due to procurement problems with the Beaumont–Adams revolvers, constables sometimes carried their sidearms chambered in a standard service calibre.

- [Tranter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranter_\(revolver\)) revolver – chambered in .450 Adams, the standard service round. It was similar to the Beaumont-Adams revolver it was substituted for.

## Uniform

### Operational uniform



A Mountie in his operational uniform

RCMP officers on frontline police duties wear grey shirts with RCMP shoulder flashes, navy blue pants with gold trouser piping, [bulletproof vests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletproof_vests), and a [peaked cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaked_cap) with a solid gold band. High-ranking officers wear white shirts. A tie can be worn with a long-sleeved shirt for occasions such as testifying in court. In colder weather, members may wear heavier boots, winter coats, wool toques, or uniquely, muskrat fur caps.[^cite_note-242]

In 1990, Baltej Singh Dhillon became the RCMP's first [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) officer to be allowed to wear a [turban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turban) instead of the traditional [campaign hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_hat).[^cite_note-243] During the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Canada), Sikh, Muslim, and other bearded officers were initially assigned to administrative duties before being permitted to attend calls for service with low viral transmission risks after officer outcry.[^cite_note-SikhBeardException-244] The beards required as part of the Sikh practice of [kesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesh_\(Sikhism\)) and worn by some Muslim men prevented respirator masks from properly sealing around the mouth and nose, reducing their effectiveness.[^cite_note-SikhBeardException-244][^cite_note-245] As of 2019, all RCMP officers, regardless of religious belief, are allowed to wear full beards or braided hair below their collar.[^cite_note-MountieUniChange-246] Officers may also wear a ballcap in place of the traditional peaked cap.[^cite_note-MountieUniChange-246]

In 2025, the RCMP announced a transition to a blue uniform from the current grey uniform.[^cite_note-247]

### Dress uniform



An RCMP chief superintendent in dress uniform during COVID-19 Pandemic.

RCMP officers are equipped with a dress uniform, popularly known as the "blue serge", for performing certain formal duties, such as media relations or parliament testimony. It consists of a navy blue dress jacket with epaulets and brass buttons, a white shirt, a navy blue tie, navy blue pants with gold trouser piping, and a peaked cap with a solid gold band.[^cite_note-DressRegs-248] Shoulder flashes are not worn.

### Ceremonial uniform

Further information: [Red Serge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Serge)



Mounties marching in ceremonial uniform in Edmonton, 2012

For most formal and ceremonial duties, RCMP wears the internationally famous [Red Serge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Serge).[^cite_note-249] It has a high collared [scarlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_\(color\)) tunic, which was developed by the [North-West Mounted Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Mounted_Police) and coloured red to distinguish it from blue American military uniforms, [midnight blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_blue) [breeches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breeches) with yellow trouser piping, an [oxblood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxblood) [Sam Browne belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Browne_belt) with white sidearm [lanyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanyard) and matching [oxblood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxblood) [riding boots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riding_boots), brown felt [campaign hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_hat) with a "Montana crease" (pinched symmetrically at the four corners), and oxblood gloves.[^cite_note-DressRegs-248] Since 1990, identical ceremonial uniforms have been worn by both men and women.[^cite_note-250]

### Decorations

Members receive a clasp and service badge star for every five years of service.[^cite_note-MountieInsignia-251] The [King of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Canada) also awards the [Royal Canadian Mounted Police Long Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Mounted_Police_Long_Service_Medal) to members who have completed 20 years of service. A clasp is awarded for each successive 5 years to 40 years. Members also receive a service badge star for each five years of service, which is worn on the left sleeve. There are specialist insignia for positions such as first aid instructor and dog handler, a fingerprint insignia for forensic identification specialists, and pilot's wings worn by aviators. [Sharpshooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpshooter) badges for proficiency in pistol or rifle shooting are each awarded in two grades.[^cite_note-MountieInsignia-251] Sharpshooter badges and service badge stars are sewn onto the left sleeve of the [red serge](#Ceremonial_uniform).

### Tartan



RCMP tartan

The RCMP has since 1998 had its own distinctive [tartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartan). The creation of the tartan was the result of a committee created in the early 1990s to create a tartan by its 125th anniversary. Upon approval from Commissioner Phillip Murray, the tartan was registered with the [Scottish Tartans Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Tartans_Society) and presented to the agency by [Anne, Princess Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne,_Princess_Royal) during her [royal visit to Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_tours_of_Canada_by_the_Canadian_Royal_Family) in 1998. The tartan appeared for the first time by an RCMP pipe band at the [Royal Nova Scotia International Tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Nova_Scotia_International_Tattoo) in July and August 1998.[^cite_note-252]

Although the RCMP is a civilian police service, in 1921, following the service of many of its members during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), King [George V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_V) awarded the service the status of a regiment of [dragoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragoon), entitling it to display the [battle honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_honour) it had been awarded.

### Service in wartime

The RCMP predecessor, the North-West Mounted Police, were involved in several battles during the [North-West Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Resistance) in 1885.[^cite_note-255] During the Second Boer War, members of the NWMP were given [leaves of absence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaves_of_absence) to join the 2nd Battalion, [Canadian Mounted Rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Mounted_Rifles) (CMR) and [Strathcona's Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strathcona's_Horse). The service raised the Canadian Mounted Rifles, mostly from NWMP members, for service in South Africa. For the CMR's distinguished service there, King Edward VII honoured the NWMP by changing the name to the "Royal Northwest Mounted Police" (RNWMP) on June 24, 1904.

During the First World War, the Royal Northwest Mounted Police (RNWMP) conducted [border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border#Jurisdictional_borders) patrols, [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance) of enemy [aliens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_\(law\)), and enforcement of [national security](240468.md) regulations within Canada. However, RNWMP officers also served overseas. On August 6, 1914, a [squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(army\)) of volunteers from the RNWMP was formed to serve with the Canadian Light Horse in France. In 1918, two more squadrons were raised, A Squadron for service in France and [Flanders](10878.md) and B Squadron for service in the [Canadian Siberian Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Siberian_Expeditionary_Force).

In September 1939, at the outset of the Second World War, the Canadian Army had no military police. Five days after war was declared the Royal Canadian Mounted Police received permission to form a provost company of service volunteers. It was designated "No. 1 Provost Company (RCMP)", and became the [Canadian Provost Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Provost_Corps). Six months after war was declared its members were overseas in Europe and served throughout the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) as [military police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces_Military_Police).



A [U.S. Army](32087.md) unit in Afghanistan, with an RCMP element embedded with them (centre foreground), 2010.

RCMP members were embedded with several military units in Afghanistan during the [War in Afghanistan from 2001–14](19666611.md). The RCMP was a member agency of the [Afghan Threat Finance Cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Threat_Finance_Cell), a multi-agency intelligence organization formed in 2008.[^cite_note-KetkNbc2012-02-10-256]

## Public perception

Further information: [North-West Mounted Police in popular media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Mounted_Police_in_popular_media)

The Mounties have been immortalized as symbols of [Canadian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_culture) in numerous [Hollywood](418334.md) [Northwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_\(genre\)) movies and television series, which often feature the image of the Mountie as square-jawed, stoic, and polite, yet with a steely determination and physical toughness that sometimes appears superhuman. The RCMP's motto is the French phrase, *Maintiens le droit*, variously translated into English as "Defending the Law", "Maintain the right", and "Uphold the right".[^cite_note-badge-1][^cite_note-tipstaff-3][^cite_note-motto-4] The Hollywood saying that they 'always get their man' derives ultimately from the words "They fetch their man every time" occurring in a report made at [Fort Benton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Benton,_Montana) regarding an incident at [Fort Macleod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Macleod) in 1877, when two Mounties, notwithstanding the loss of their horses, managed to capture three whisky smugglers.[^cite_note-261]



An RCMP media relations member

### Depictions in media

In 1912, [Ralph Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Connor)'s *Corporal Cameron of the North-West Mounted Police: A Tale of the MacLeod Trail* appeared, and became an international best-selling novel. Mounties fiction became a popular genre in both [pulp magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_magazine) and book form. Among the best-selling authors who specialized in tales of the Mounted Police were [James Oliver Curwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Oliver_Curwood), [Laurie York Erskine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurie_York_Erskine), James B Hendryx, T Lund, Harwood Steele (the son of Sam Steele), and William Byron Mowery.



A [lobby card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobby_card) for *[Renfrew of the Royal Mounted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfrew_of_the_Royal_Mounted_\(1937_film\))*, a 1937 film that depicts a Mountie as its protagonist

In other media, a famous example is the [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_drama) and [television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_program), *[Sergeant Preston of the Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_Preston_of_the_Yukon).* [Dudley Do-Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Do-Right) (of *[The Rocky and Bullwinkle Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rocky_and_Bullwinkle_Show)*) is a 1960s example of the comic aspect of the Mountie myth, as is [Klondike Kat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klondike_Kat), from [Total Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Television). The [Broadway](725252.md) musical and Hollywood movie *[Rose-Marie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Marie_\(1936_film\))* is a 1930s example of its romantic side. A successful combination was a series of *[Renfrew of the Royal Mounted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfrew_of_the_Royal_Mounted)* boy's adventure novels written by Laurie York Erskine beginning in 1922 and running to 1941. In the 1930s Erskine narrated a *Sgt Renfrew of the Mounties* radio show and a series of films with actor-singer James Newill playing Renfrew were released between 1937 and 1940. In 1953 portions of the films were mixed with new sequences of Newill for a *Renfrew of the Mounted* television series.

Bruce Carruthers (1901–1953), a former Mounted Police corporal (1919–1923), served as an unofficial [technical advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_advisor) to Hollywood in many films with RCMP characters.[^cite_note-262] They included *Heart of the North* (1938), *Susannah of the Mounties* (1939), *Northern Pursuit* (1943), *Gene Autry and The Mounties* (1951), *The Wild North* (1952), and *The Pony Soldier* (1952).

### Merchandise and trademarks

There are products and merchandise that are made in the image of the RCMP, like Mountie statues or hats. Before 1995, the RCMP had little control over these products. The Royal Canadian Mounted Police received an international license on April 1, 1995, requiring those who use the RCMP to pay a licensing fee. Proceeds from the fees are used for community awareness programs.[^cite_note-264] Those that do not pay the licensing fee are legally unable to use the name of the RCMP or their correct uniforms, though a film such as *[Canadian Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Bacon)* used the name "Royal Mounted Canadian Police" and the character in the *[Dudley Do-Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Do-Right_\(film\))* film did not wear accurate insignia.

Through a master licensing agreement (MLA) with the RCMP, the [RCMP Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCMP_Foundation) is responsible for managing the commercial use of the RCMP name, image, and protected marks.[^cite_note-265] The foundation issues selected companies a royalty-based agreement allowing them to produce and market high-quality official RCMP merchandise. [Walt Disney Co. (Canada) Ltd.](37398.md) was contracted to aid in the initial set up of the licensing program,[^cite_note-266] but Disney never owned or controlled any of the RCMP's protected marks. Following the expiration of the Disney contract in 2000, all responsibilities and activities were taken over by the executive director and his staff, reporting to the foundation president and board of directors. In 2007, through a decree signed by Commissioner Beverley Busson, the operating name was changed to the "Royal Canadian Mounted Police Foundation".

### Public relations program



A [Musical Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_Ride) performance in [Essex, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex,_Ontario), 2016

RCMP community relationship-building programs include the [Musical Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_Ride). The Musical Ride is an equestrian showcase of RCMP riders, that performs across Canada each year from May to October.[^cite_note-267] The RCMP Sunset Ceremony ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Cérémonie du crépuscule*) has taken place every summer since 1989 at the Musical Ride Centre in [Ottawa](22219.md),[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269] with it in recent years featuring the [Ottawa Police Service Pipe Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Police_Service_Pipe_Band) and the [Governor General's Foot Guards Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_General's_Foot_Guards_Band).[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271] The RCMP National Ceremonial Troop is a unit that serves as dismounted version of the Musical Ride as well as a [drill team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drill_team). Individual divisions also have their ceremonial troops.



The northern facade of the [RCMP Heritage Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCMP_Heritage_Centre)

The [RCMP Heritage Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCMP_Heritage_Centre) is a multi-million-dollar museum designed by [Arthur Erickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Erickson) that opened in May 2007 in Regina, Saskatchewan, at the RCMP Academy, Depot Division. It replaced the old RCMP museum and is designed to celebrate the role of the service in Canada's history.

  [^cite_note-275]

Before 1994, the RCMP also operated the Royal Canadian Mounted Police Band ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *La Musique de la Gendarmerie royale du Canada*) was the RCMP's central musical ensemble. It was considered one of the best professional bands assembled in Canada.[^cite_note-RCMP-Band-276][^cite_note-Altissimo-277] Although it was an official regimental band, the members worked in the band as a secondary job. It is generally considered to have begun in 1938, though various police bands in the RCMP flourished at the time, leading the [Canadian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_government) approving the creation of a full-time central band in December 1958, with its headquarters in the capital of [Ottawa](22219.md).[^cite_note-278][^cite_note-Altissimo-277] Appearances made by the band included [Expo 86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_86) and the [Commonwealth Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meetings) in [Vancouver](32706.md), the [Calgary Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Winter_Olympics) in 1988, as well as the visits of [Soviet President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_President) [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev) and Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II) in 1990.[^cite_note-Altissimo-277][^cite_note-RCMP-Band-276]

It was dissolved in 1994 due to government budget cuts. In its 55-year existence, it operated as a voluntary regimental band, with its members working with it as a secondary job apart from their other duties in the RCMP. Members of the band wore the RCMP's [Red Serge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Serge) as part of their [full dress uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_dress_uniform) and adopted drill seen in [Canadian military bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_military_bands) and bands in the [British Army](4887.md). Its longest-serving director was Superintendent Edwin Joseph Lydall, who served from 1948 to 1968.[^cite_note-279]
