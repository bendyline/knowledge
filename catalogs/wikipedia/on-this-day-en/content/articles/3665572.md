As of November 2025, there have been 1,753 [tropical cyclones](8282374.md) of at least [tropical storm](255313.md) intensity, 972 at hurricane intensity, and 342 at major hurricane intensity recorded within the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) since [1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1851_Atlantic_hurricane_season), the first [Atlantic hurricane season](2930244.md) to be included in the official Atlantic [tropical cyclone record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_hurricane_reanalysis).[^cite_note-1] Though a majority of these cyclones have fallen within climatological averages, prevailing atmospheric conditions occasionally lead to anomalous tropical systems which at times reach extremes in statistical record-keeping including in duration and intensity.[^cite_note-Climatology-2] The scope of this list is limited to tropical cyclone records solely within the North Atlantic Ocean and is subdivided by their reason for notability.

## Tropical cyclogenesis

### Most active / least active Atlantic hurricane seasons

Most [Atlantic hurricane seasons](2930244.md) prior to the [weather satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_satellite) era include seven or fewer recorded [tropical storms](8282374.md) or hurricanes. As the usage of satellite data was not available until the mid-1960s, early storm counts are less reliable. Before the advent of the airplane or means of tracking storms, the ones recorded were storms that affected mainly populated areas. An undercount bias of zero to six tropical cyclones per year between 1851 and 1885 and zero to four per year between 1886 and 1910 has been estimated.[^cite_note-3]

With the advent of the satellite came better and more accurate weather tracking. The first satellites sent into space to monitor the weather were known as [Television Infrared Observation Satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_Infrared_Observation_Satellite) (TIROS). In 1961, [Hurricane Esther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Esther) was the first hurricane to be "discovered" through satellite readings.[^cite_note-4] Although this modern invention was now available, the systems were initially not fully active enough to provide daily images of the storms.[^cite_note-5] Data for the North Atlantic region remained sparse as late as 1964 due to a lack of complete satellite coverage.[^cite_note-6]

Due to the above factors, terms such as "ever" or "on record" should be interpreted to mean "since sometime between 1964 and 1978".

The most active Atlantic hurricane season on record in terms of total storms took place in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Atlantic_hurricane_season), with 30 documented. The storm count for the 2020 season also includes fourteen hurricanes, of which seven strengthened to major hurricane status. On the converse, the least active season on record in terms of total storms took place in [1914](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1914_Atlantic_hurricane_season). The 1914 season had just one tropical storm and no hurricanes.

### Earliest / latest formations for each category

Monochrome radar image of a hurricane. Rain, which the radar detects, is shown as white regions. Concentric circles denote distances from the radar site, located slightly offset from the center of the image.

[Radar](25676.md) image of [Hurricane Alice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Alice_\(December_1954\)) (1954–55), the only Atlantic tropical cyclone on record to span two calendar years at hurricane strength

Climatologically speaking, approximately 97 percent of [tropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclones) that form in the North Atlantic [develop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis) between June 1 and November 30 – dates which delimit the modern-day Atlantic hurricane season. Though the beginning of the annual hurricane season has historically remained the same, the official end of the hurricane season has shifted from its initial date of October 31. Regardless, on average once every few years a tropical cyclone develops [outside the limits of the season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_off-season_Atlantic_hurricanes);[^cite_note-Season-8] as of 2023 there have been 92 tropical cyclones in the off-season, with the most recent being an [unnamed subtropical storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Atlantic_hurricane_season#Unnamed_subtropical_storm) in January [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Atlantic_hurricane_season). The first tropical cyclone of the [1938 Atlantic hurricane season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_Atlantic_hurricane_season), which formed on January 3, became the earliest forming tropical storm and hurricane after [reanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_hurricane_reanalysis_project) concluded on the storm in December 2012.[^cite_note-Metadata-9] [Hurricane Able](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Able_\(1951\)) in [1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Atlantic_hurricane_season) was initially thought to be the earliest forming major hurricane;[^cite_note-11] however, following post-storm analysis, it was determined that Able only reached Category 1 strength, which made [Hurricane Alma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Alma_\(1966\)) of [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Atlantic_hurricane_season) the new record holder, as it became a major hurricane on June 8.[^cite_note-HURDAT-12] Though it developed within the bounds of the Atlantic hurricane season,[^cite_note-Season-8][^cite_note-HURDAT-12] [Hurricane Audrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Audrey) in [1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Atlantic_hurricane_season) was the earliest developing [Category 4 hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Category_4_Atlantic_hurricanes) on record after it reached the intensity on June 27.[^cite_note-Audrey-13] However, reanalysis[^cite_note-HURDAT-12] of 1956 to 1960 by NOAA downgraded Audrey to a Category 3, making Hurricane Dennis of 2005 the earliest Category 4 on record in the calendar year on July 8, 2005.[^cite_note-14] That mark stood until surpassed by [Hurricane Beryl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Beryl) on June 30, 2024. Beryl would also become the earliest Category 5 two days later, surpassing [Hurricane Emily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Emily_\(2005\)) of 2005.

Though the official end of the Atlantic hurricane season occurs on November 30, the dates of October 31 and November 15 have also historically marked the official end date for the hurricane season.[^cite_note-Season-8] December, the only month of the year after the hurricane season, has featured the cyclogenesis of fourteen tropical cyclones.[^cite_note-HURDAT-12] The second [Hurricane Alice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Alice_\(December_1954\)) in [1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Atlantic_hurricane_season) was the latest forming tropical storm and hurricane, reaching these intensities on December 30 and 31, respectively. Hurricane Alice and [Tropical Storm Zeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Zeta_\(2005\)) were the only two storms to exist in two calendar years – the former from 1954 to 1955 and the latter from 2005 to 2006.[^cite_note-AtlanticRecords-15] No storms have been recorded to exceed Category 1 hurricane intensity in December.[^cite_note-HURDAT-12] In [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Atlantic_hurricane_season), [Hurricane Lenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Lenny) reached Category 4 intensity on November 17 as it took an unusual west-to-east track across the Caribbean; its intensity made it the latest developing Category 4 hurricane, though this was well within the bounds of the hurricane season.[^cite_note-LateHurricanes-16] Based on reanalysis, the devastating [Cuba hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Cuba_hurricane) in [1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Atlantic_hurricane_season) reached Category 5 intensity on November 5, making it the latest in any Atlantic hurricane season to reach this intensity.[^cite_note-HURDAT-12][^cite_note-Metadata-9][^cite_note-19]

### Most tropical / subtropical storms formed in each month

The Atlantic hurricane season presently runs from June 1 through November 30 each year, with peak activity occurring between August and October. Specifically, the height of the season is in early to mid-September.[^cite_note-Season-8] Tropical systems that form outside of these months are referred to as ["off season"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_off-season_Atlantic_hurricanes), and account for roughly 3% of all storms that form in a given year.[^cite_note-Season-8] All of the records included below are for the most storms that formed in a given month, as the threshold for "fewest" is zero for expected months. Cases where "fewest storms" are unusual include the months when the hurricane season is at its peak.

## Intensity

### Most intense

Main article: [List of the most intense tropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_most_intense_tropical_cyclones)

Generally speaking, the intensity of a tropical cyclone is determined by either the storm's [maximum sustained winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_sustained_wind) or lowest [barometric pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barometric_pressure). The following table lists the most intense Atlantic hurricanes in terms of their lowest barometric pressure. In terms of wind speed, [Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Allen) from [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Atlantic_hurricane_season#Hurricane_Allen) and [Melissa](81393820.md) from [2025](65411671.md#Hurricane_Melissa) are tied for the strongest Atlantic tropical cyclones on record, with maximum sustained winds of 190 mph (305 km/h). For many years, it was thought that [Hurricane Camille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Camille) also attained this intensity, but this conclusion was changed in 2014. The original measurements of Camille are suspect since wind speed instrumentation used at the time would likely be damaged by winds of such intensity.[^cite_note-MostIntense-62] Nonetheless, their central pressures are low enough to rank them among the strongest recorded Atlantic hurricanes.[^cite_note-HURDAT-12] This is also why despite there being 2 hurricanes in the month of January, neither of them are ranked here as both of them had a higher pressure than [Unnamed Subtropical Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unnamed_subtropical_storm_\(2023\)) ([2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Atlantic_hurricane_season)).[^cite_note-63]

Owing to their intensity, the strongest Atlantic hurricanes have all attained Category 5 classification. [Hurricane Opal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Opal), the most intense Category 4 hurricane recorded, intensified to reach a minimum pressure of 916 mbar (hPa; 27.05 inHg),[^cite_note-OpalTCR-64] a pressure typical of Category 5 hurricanes.[^cite_note-SSHS-LSU-65] Nonetheless, the pressure remains too high to list Opal as one of the ten strongest Atlantic tropical cyclones.[^cite_note-HURDAT-12] Currently, [Hurricane Wilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Wilma) is the strongest Atlantic hurricane ever recorded, after reaching an intensity of 882 mbar (hPa; 26.05 inHg) in October 2005;[^cite_note-MostIntense-62] at the time, this also made Wilma the strongest tropical cyclone worldwide outside of the [West Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_typhoon),[^cite_note-EPAC_HURDAT-66][^cite_note-NIO-67][^cite_note-Gafilo-68][^cite_note-BOM-69][^cite_note-Wellington_BT-70] where seven tropical cyclones have been recorded to intensify to lower pressures.[^cite_note-WPACBT-71] However, this was later superseded by [Hurricane Patricia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Patricia) in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Pacific_hurricane_season) in the east Pacific, which had a pressure reading of 872 mbar. Preceding Wilma is [Hurricane Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Gilbert), which had also held the record for most intense Atlantic hurricane for 17 years.[^cite_note-MWRGilbert-72] The [1935 Labor Day hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Labor_Day_hurricane), with a pressure of 892 mbar (hPa; 26.34 inHg), is the third strongest Atlantic hurricane and the strongest documented tropical cyclone prior to 1950.[^cite_note-HURDAT-12] Since the measurements taken during Wilma and Gilbert were documented using [dropsonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dropsonde), this pressure remains the lowest measured over land.[^cite_note-DeanTCR-73] In 2025, [Hurricane Melissa](81393820.md) reached a pressure of 892 mbar (hPa; 26.34 inHg), tying the 1935 Labor Day hurricane as the third-most-intense Atlantic hurricane.[^cite_note-74]

2005's [Hurricane Rita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Rita) and 2024's [Hurricane Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Milton) are tied as the fifth strongest Atlantic hurricane in terms of barometric pressure. Rita is one of three tropical cyclones from 2005 on the list, with the others being Wilma and [Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) at first and ninth, respectively, while Milton is the only storm from the [2024 season](58609028.md) to be on the list.[^cite_note-HURDAT-12] However, with a barometric pressure of 895 mbar (hPa; 26.43 inHg), Rita and Milton are both the strongest tropical cyclone ever recorded in the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md).[^cite_note-RitaReport-75] In between Milton and Camille is [1980's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Atlantic_hurricane_season) [Hurricane Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Allen). Allen's pressure was measured at 899 mbar. [Hurricane Camille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Camille) is the eighth strongest hurricane on record. Camille is the only storm to have been moved down the list due to post-storm analysis. Camille was originally recognized as the fifth strongest hurricane on record, but was dropped to the seventh strongest at the time in 2014, with an estimated pressure at 905 mbars, tying it with Hurricanes [Mitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Mitch), and [Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dean). Camille then was recategorized with a new pressure of 900 mbars. Mitch and Dean share intensities for the tenth strongest Atlantic hurricane at 905 mbar (hPa; 26.73 inHg).[^cite_note-DeanTCR-73] [Hurricane Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Maria) is in eleventh place for most intense Atlantic tropical cyclone, with a pressure as low as 908 mbar (hPa; 26.81 inHg).[^cite_note-76] In addition, the most intense Atlantic hurricane outside of the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) and [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md) is [Hurricane Dorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dorian) of [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Atlantic_hurricane_season), with a pressure of 910 mbar (hPa; 26.9 inHg),[^cite_note-77] next most intense in the open ocean was [Hurricane Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irma) of [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Atlantic_hurricane_season), with a pressure of 914 mbar.

Many of the strongest recorded tropical cyclones weakened prior to their eventual [landfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landfall_\(meteorology\)) or demise. However, five of the storms remained intense enough at landfall to be considered some of the strongest landfalling hurricanes – five of the eleven hurricanes on the list constitute five of the top ten most intense Atlantic landfalls in recorded history. The [1935 Labor Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Labor_Day_hurricane) hurricane made landfall at peak intensity, making it the most intense Atlantic hurricane landfall. Hurricane Melissa made landfall near New Hope in [Westmoreland Parish](1439909.md), Jamaica with a pressure of 897 mbar (hPa; 26.49 inHg), making it the second most intense Atlantic hurricane landfall. Hurricane Camille made landfall in [Waveland, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waveland,_Mississippi) with a pressure of 900 mbar (hPa; 26.58 inHg), making it the third most intense Atlantic hurricane landfall.[^cite_note-mwr-78] Though it weakened slightly before its eventual landfall on the [Yucatán Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n_Peninsula), Hurricane Gilbert maintained a pressure of 900 mbar (hPa; 26.58 inHg) at landfall, making its landfall the third strongest, tied with Camille. Similarly, Hurricane Dean made landfall on the peninsula, though it did so at peak intensity and with a higher barometric pressure; its landfall marked the fifth strongest in Atlantic hurricane history.[^cite_note-DeanTCR-73]

- *Note: Dropsondes have only been GPS-based for use in eyewalls since 1997,[^cite_note-79] and the quantity of aircraft reconnaissance and surface observation stations has changed over time, such that values from storms in different periods may not be 100% consistent.*

## Worldwide cyclone records set by Atlantic storms

- **Costliest tropical cyclone**: [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) – [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Atlantic_hurricane_season) and [Hurricane Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Harvey) – [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Atlantic_hurricane_season) – US\$125 billion in damages

- **Fastest seafloor current produced by a tropical cyclone**: [Hurricane Ivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Ivan) – [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Atlantic_hurricane_season) – 2.25 m/s (5 mph)

  [^cite_note-103]

  [^cite_note-104]

- **Highest confirmed wave produced by a tropical cyclone**: [Hurricane Luis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Luis) – [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Atlantic_hurricane_season) – 98 feet (30 m)

  [^cite_note-105]

- **Highest forward speed of a tropical cyclone**: [Tropical Storm Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Atlantic_hurricane_season#Tropical_Storm_Six) – [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Atlantic_hurricane_season) – 69 mph (111 km/h)

- **Most tornadoes spawned by a tropical cyclone**: [Hurricane Ivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Ivan) – [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Atlantic_hurricane_season) – 120 confirmed tornadoes

  [^cite_note-106]

- **Smallest tropical cyclone on record**: [Tropical Storm Marco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Marco_\(2008\)) – [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Atlantic_hurricane_season) – gale-force winds extended 11.5 mi (18.5 km) from storm center

- **Smallest tropical cyclone eye on record**: [Hurricane Wilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Wilma) – [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Atlantic_hurricane_season) – diameter 2.3 miles (3.7 km)
