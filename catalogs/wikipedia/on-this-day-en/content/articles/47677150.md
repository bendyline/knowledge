# Immigrant health in Australia

&#x20; Immigration is the movement of an individual or group of peoples to a foreign country to live permanently.[^cite_note-1] Since 1788, when the first British settlers arrived in [Botany Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany_Bay), immigrants have travelled from across the world to establish a life in Australia.[^cite_note-2] The reason for people or groups of peoples moving to Australia varies. Such reasons can be due to seeking [work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employment) or even refuge from [third world countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World). The health of immigrants entering Australia varies depending on the individual's country of origin and the circumstance of which they came, as well as their state of travel to Australia. Immigrants are known to enter Australia both regularly and irregularly, and this can affect one's health immensely.[^cite_note-\:7-3] Once in Australia, immigrants are given the opportunity to access a high quality of healthcare services, however, the usage of these services can differ dependent on the culture and place of birth of the individual.[^cite_note-\:15-4] Australia has [strict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strict) health regulations that have to be met before one is allowed access into Australia and can determine if one is granted or denied such access. The [quarantine](25237.md) process of immigrants into Australia has been in place since 1830, starting at the [North Head Quarantine Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Head_Quarantine_Station) and continues all over Australia.[^cite_note-\:5-5]

## Australia's immigrants

### Europeans



Smallpox virus

In 1788, the first fleet of British immigrants established a colony in Australia. The arrival of the Europeans in the 1800s, saw those from Italy, [Greece](12108.md), [Poland](22936.md), [Malta](19137.md), [Russia](25391.md) and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_France) land on the shores of Australia.[^cite_note-\:0-6] Upon embarking, it is expected that the European immigrants would have carried [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_diseases) aboard the ships, and given the nature of the [vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_vessel), any disease would have spread quickly among the [passengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger). [Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Report) by Mark Stainforth suggest that many forms of bacteria and [viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus) caused high levels of illness and on-board deaths among those European immigrants travelling to Australia.[^cite_note-\:1-7]

### Chinese



Chinese immigrants upon arrival

According to the 1861 Colonial Census, Chinese-born persons made up 3.4% of the Australian population,[^cite_note-\:2-12] equating to approximately 38,258 Chinese in Australia after 1842 when the Chinese first settled in Australia. Some arrived in Australia hoping to escape civil disorder in China, although the majority of Chinese migrated to Australia after hearing about the gold rush.[^cite_note-\:2-12]

Within Australia, the average [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) is 81.7 years, while China-born individuals have an average of 74.7 years.[^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-\:3-13] The Queensland Health Multicultural Services suggests that [Ischaemic heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischaemic_heart_disease), cancer and [cerebrovascular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebrovascular_disease) are the major causes of death of China-born persons in Australia. Such cancers that have been identified include nasopharynx, [lung](18450.md), [intestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestine_cancer_\(disambiguation\)), [rectum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectum), [stomach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach) and [liver cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_cancer), all of which are prominent among Chinese immigrants.[^cite_note-\:2-12]

### Afghans

From the 1860s, ["Afghan" cameleers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_cameleers_in_Australia), who came mainly from [Afghanistan](737.md), but also [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) and other countries, settled in Australia, establishing work helping to open up [outback Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outback).[^cite_note-\:0-6]

Afghans who have migrated to Australia more recently face several disadvantages when accessing [health services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_services). Disadvantages include language barriers, a lack of translators in Australian health services, and the incompatibility of some Australian healthcare services and procedures with Islamic beliefs.[^cite_note-\:4-14] The migrants may experience a sense of alienation and lack of belonging as they are put in a situation that makes them feel like [outcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outcast_\(person\)) compared to the rest of Australia. However, the Queensland Health Multicultural Services have suggested that Afghan migrants use the health services just as much as Australian-born individuals.[^cite_note-\:4-14]

## Quarantine and health requirements

### Early years



North Head Quarantine Station

In the 1830s, the immigrants that arrived by boat on the shores of Sydney were suspected to be carrying [contagious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contagious_diseases). During this period, [influenza](19572217.md), [typhoid](31596.md), [scarlet fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_fever) and [whooping cough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whooping_cough) were all found on European ships arriving in Australia.[^cite_note-\:1-7] To contain such diseases, the ships were stopped at [North Head Quarantine Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Head_Quarantine_Station) where the authority would place the passengers and crew into quarantine.[^cite_note-\:0-6] The Australian Government (2015) suspects that each passenger would have spent on average, 40 days in [quarantine](25237.md) before being released into Australia, as Australian residents.[^cite_note-\:0-6] Each experience within North Head varied and was dependent on class. Some immigrants were exposed to further disease, disempowerment and in some cases death. In the beginning, [North Head Quarantine Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Head_Quarantine_Station) provided tents for those immigrants who came to Australia.[^cite_note-\:0-6] However, in 1837, those who appeared to be healthy were those who were granted [accommodation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwelling) in the tents, while those who were sick were required to stay on the ship.[^cite_note-\:0-6] The [Australian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_government) suggests that in the year of 1837, 295 healthy immigrants were kept ashore while those who were contagious or otherwise were kept on the ship. The North Head Quarantine Station's facilities continued to grow. However, between 1860 and 1870, the quarantine station took a turn for the worse when the world economy decelerated along with immigration. As a result, an outbreak of [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox) occurred due to insufficient maintenance of the [North Head Quarantine Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Head_Quarantine_Station).[^cite_note-\:0-6] It was only in 1909, when the [Commonwealth government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_government) took over North Head Quarantine, that the station attained a maximum volume of 1200 people.

### Government health requirements

In order to ensure Australia's health and safety, the Government is required to examine all immigrants prior to entering Australia. Those who seek either a permanent or temporary visa are part of this application. All candidates are checked for [Tuberculosis](30653.md), HIV and [Hepatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis), Yellow fever, [Polio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliomyelitis) and the [Ebola virus disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_disease) (EVD). In order to be allowed access into Australia, you must be cleared of all of these diseases and deemed to be no threat to Australian communities.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

## Australian health services

### Current health care system

The [Medicare](58499.md) system in Australia is a publicly funded system that is designed to provide heavily subsidised costs for all Australians, including those foreign born immigrants who either have a [citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship), a working visa, a permanent visa, or one who is married to an Australian citizen.[^cite_note-\:15-4]

### Health care difficulties for immigrants

See also: [Mental health and immigration detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health_and_immigration_detention)

For those who migrate to Australia, there are many disadvantages in the way of healthcare. The [Australian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Australia) provides well supported healthcare for immigrants, however, those immigrants who arrive in Australia are struck by culture and communication barriers as well as a lack of knowledge regarding the cost of health services and knowing their rights.[^cite_note-\:5-5]

#### Cultural barriers

[Cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture) barriers felt by immigrants include the Australians perceptions of health as well as their [behaviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavior) when sick.[^cite_note-\:5-5] Such differences are determined by ones [cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage). It is also prominent that some immigrants are concerned that doctors will not recognise or understand their specific cultural needs. An example of this includes the religious and cultural needs of women from the Middle East or Islamic faith, who may be fearful of male doctors examining them.[^cite_note-\:5-5] The [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_\(Australia\)) (2003) stated that "different cultural groups have specific expectations. For example, Jehovah's Witness followers will refuse to have blood transfusions, believing it is polluting."[^cite_note-\:5-5][^cite_note-16]

#### Communication barriers

For those immigrants coming to Australia from non-English speaking backgrounds, [communication barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication) can influence one's health experience, whether it be negative or positive.[^cite_note-\:5-5][^cite_note-\:4-14] The ABC (2003) stated that non-English speaking immigrants may say 'yes' in [stressful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_\(psychological\)) situation in order to avoid further need for communication with healthcare services.[^cite_note-\:5-5] This can have great impacts on the individual as they may not receive the help they need as a [result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Result), or they will choose not to contact health services as they are wary of the communication barriers and believe it is easier to avoid the situation. This could result in the individual's health deteriorating as a result of lack of attention.[^cite_note-\:5-5]

#### Patient rights

It has been identified that immigrants from non-English speaking backgrounds, tend to feel less empowered, resulting in the patients becoming reticent. Non-English speak immigrants tend not to ask questions. As a result, many individuals are discouraged from visiting the services again, which can lead to the [ill health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ill-health) of immigrants that are new to Australia.[^cite_note-\:5-5]

These factors can be reduced if new arrivals to Australia are aware of their rights.[^cite_note-\:5-5][^cite_note-\:6-17] All patients have the right to:

- Ask for a doctor of a particular sex.

- What privacy they wish for.

- An interpreter.

- Complain if they feel necessary.

Between 2007 and 2008, an Australian Charter of Healthcare Rights was established by the commission.[^cite_note-\:6-17] This charter provides a clear outline of the rights of those seeking healthcare, and is directed at patients, consumers, families, carers and service providers.[^cite_note-\:6-17] This charter can be applied in all health settings in Australia. These include, public hospitals,. general practices and other environments.

### Health studies

A study has been undertaken, comparing the [mental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health) of men and women in Australia, identifying the differences between Australian born and [foreign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_\(law\)) born individuals.[^cite_note-\:10-18] To achieve the following results, a diagnosis of [mental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health), current [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(clinical\)), [medical service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_service) use and use of [medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medications) have been studied.[^cite_note-\:10-18]

Three groups of individuals:[^cite_note-\:10-18]

1. Australian born

2. English speaking foreign born

3. Non-English speaking foreign born

The study has proven that both foreign born and Australian born groups of people access [mental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health) services at an equal rate. However, non-English speaking foreign born men have demonstrated an increased risk of [mental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health) issues and access health services less than Australian born men.[^cite_note-\:10-18] These results suggest that the government and health services need to be made aware of these particular health issues among non-English speaking foreign born men, in particular those men who unmarried, unemployed and live alone. The findings of this study identify the importance of [social support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_support) to prevent [mental health issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health).[^cite_note-\:10-18]

Whilst most immigrants and [refugees](45547.md) travel to Australia regularly, there are others who travel to Australia irregularly.[^cite_note-\:7-3][^cite_note-\:8-19] Those who seek refuge in Australia by irregular means travel by boat, most of which are intercepted by the [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) patrolling the [Australian border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Border_Force). From there, the Royal Australian Navy pass the refugees on to [Australian Customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Customs_and_Border_Protection_Service) for detention.[^cite_note-\:7-3] There are many risks associated with entering Australia via irregular means and considering the conditions of travel, the deterioration of persons on board is prevalent.[^cite_note-\:7-3][^cite_note-\:8-19]

Irregular immigrants are typically fleeing for their lives, so ascertaining a precise [medical history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_history) is difficult for those processing their arrival.[^cite_note-\:7-3][^cite_note-\:8-19] These refugees often present with [malnutrition](258979.md), [infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease) (which is often [respiratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_tract) or [intestinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestines).)[^cite_note-\:7-3] [HIV](14170.md), [tuberculosis](30653.md) and [hepatitis A and B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis) are also common,[^cite_note-\:7-3] as are dental issues.[^cite_note-\:7-3]

More than 20% of [asylum seekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum_seekers) in Australia appear to have suffered as a result of [torture](47702.md) prior to arriving in Australia.[^cite_note-\:8-19] Therefore, psychiatric conditions are prevalent in detention camps. [Anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety) and [stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_\(psychological\)) within the facilities has been emphasised by the lack of social support, unemployment and discrimination.[^cite_note-\:8-19] This psychological stress felt by detainees can be enhanced by the confined environment of the detention camps. Amongst the men and women, children have also been reported for suffering prolonged [psychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological) conditions. Whilst there are psychological impacts on the immigrants, there are also those who are affected by physical conditions such as [osteomyelitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteomyelitis) or [epilepsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epilepsy).[^cite_note-\:7-3][^cite_note-\:8-19]

Prolonged detention has also eventuated the development of [resignation syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resignation_syndrome) in hopeless children, as well as [psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosis). Suicide ideation is pervasive.

## Controversies

### Immunisation

It is stated that [vaccinations](32473.md) will not be given to those who arrive in Australia as [refugees](45547.md) or [asylum seekers](45547.md) due to the individual or group of people's country of origin and the different immunisation schedules that they uphold.[^cite_note-\:12-20] However, it has been suggested that refugees and asylum seekers should be vaccinated according to the Australian National Immunisation Program Schedule; unless [documentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentation) of prior immunisation is provided, catch-up vaccinations are required.[^cite_note-\:12-20] In this regard, it has been argued that it is necessary for immigrants to be immunised to ensure the health and safety of fellow [Australians](11197621.md). Nevertheless, there is a competing principle to afford every immigrant and [Australian citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_citizen) an equal opportunity to uphold their cultural beliefs.[^cite_note-\:12-20] In this regard, some have questioned the [Australian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_government)'s right to strip these refugees and [asylum seekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum_seekers) of their cultural beliefs and understandings in regards to their health.[^cite_note-\:11-15]

### Prolonged health issues

In 1992, [mandatory detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_detention) was introduced for refugees and [asylum seekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum_seekers) entering Australia irregularly.[^cite_note-\:13-21] However, the introduction of mandatory detention has triggered debate. The importance of protecting Australia's borders and integrity of Australia's immigration system was the initial reason for the policy,[^cite_note-\:13-21] while those who question the policy and Australian Government's decision argue whether it is good for both parties (the refugees and Australian citizens), stating that it is [inhumane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhumane) and ineffective as it is proven to cause further health issues for the refugees and asylum seekers.[^cite_note-22]

[Philip Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Flood), the former Secretary of the Department of Foreign Affairs, undertook an investigation into mandatory detention.[^cite_note-\:13-21] Several circumstances of self-harm, psychiatric problems and sexual, verbal and physical abuse of children were documented by Flood.[^cite_note-\:13-21] The final report identifies and expresses concerns in regards to the condition of which the refugees and asylum seekers are living, as well the department's management of long-term detention and the impact this can have on young children.[^cite_note-\:13-21]

This debate against mandatory detention identifies the negatives and prolonged health issues that can arise from long-term detention.[^cite_note-\:13-21] It has been recommended that once asylum seekers and refugees are cleared of the initial checks, such as those of [identity and health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_identity), that they are then released into community detention or granted a [bridging visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridging_visa) whilst waiting for their refugee status to be determined.[^cite_note-\:13-21] However, further studies prove that keeping such irregular immigrants in mandatory detention protects the Australian community whilst the legitimacy of the [refugees and asylum seekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees) is being assessed.[^cite_note-23]

### Use of force in immigration detention facilities

Those authorised officers within [immigration detention](1700791.md) centres are given the power to use force against [asylum seekers](45547.md) and refugees.[^cite_note-\:14-24] This is stated within the 2015 [bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(law\)) to maintain good order of immigration facilities. The [Australian Human Rights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Human_Rights_Commission) recognises the environment of particular detention facilities and force may be essential in specific circumstances. However, the Commission argues that the bill is deficient because:[^cite_note-\:14-24]

- The necessity and reasonableness of force should be assessed in every situation. The force used must be consistent with the [Crimes Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimes_Act) and the policies regarding the use of force in immigration detention facilities.

- The use and limits of force that is stated in the policies and procedures, should be included in the Act.

- The forceful movement of immigrants and force used upon children should be controlled and limited within immigration detention facilities.

It is evident that while force may be need in certain circumstances, the use of force must be appropriate and respect the immigrant's inherent right to be treated with [respect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respect).[^cite_note-\:14-24] Concerns have arisen in regards to 'authorised officers' [abusing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abuse) the rights of the detained immigrants and therefore [reinforcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement) the recommendation for the Bill to be altered and reworded by the [Australian Human Rights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Human_Rights_Commission) will be able to ensure that use of force used in immigration facilities is managed and acted out [appropriately](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appropriately?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:14-24]
