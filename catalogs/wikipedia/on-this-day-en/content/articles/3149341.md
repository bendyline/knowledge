**Ukrainian Railways**, or **PAT 'Ukrzaliznytsia (UZ)'** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): ПАТ "Укрзалізни́ця (УЗ)"), is a state-owned [joint-stock company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint-stock_company) administering [railway infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_infrastructure_manager) and [rail transport in Ukraine](6011024.md); a monopoly that controls the vast majority of the railroad transportation in the country.[^cite_note-6] Ukrainian Railways is [the world's sixth largest rail passenger transporter and world's seventh largest freight transporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_usage_statistics_by_country). As of 2020, the total length of the main [broad-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad-gauge_railway) ([1,520 mm (4 ft 11+27⁄32 in)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_ft_and_1520_mm_gauge_railways)) railroad network was 19,787 kilometres (12,295 mi),[^cite_note-UZ_stats_2020-7] making it the [13th largest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_rail_transport_network_size). Ukraine also has many stretches of [standard-gauge railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge_railway) (1,435 mm), and is currently working to expand these in order to improve its connections to the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-Moiseev-8]

In 2015, Ukrainian Railways was transformed through a merger of a state agency and a [state-owned enterprise](204768.md) into a public joint stock company owned by the state. Ukraine's State Administration of Railroad Transportation is subordinated to the [Ministry of Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Infrastructure_\(Ukraine\)),[^cite_note-9] administering the railways through the six territorial railway companies that immediately control and provide of all aspects of the railroad transportation and maintenance under the common Ukrzaliznytsia [brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_name). The general director of the administration is appointed by the [Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Ministers_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-10] The company employs more than 191,700 people throughout the country.[^cite_note-11]

During the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), Ukrainian Railways continued operating to evacuate and rescue millions of people from cities out of the country. The rail links between Ukraine and Russia have been blown up by the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md) to prevent their use by Russians, but the railways have continued operating within Ukraine and between Ukraine and [Poland](22936.md), [Hungary](13275.md), Republic of [Moldova](19260.md), and [Slovakia](26830.md). One long-abandoned cross-border rail link with Poland was quickly reconstructed, and others which had been used only for freight have been quickly opened for passenger use.[^cite_note-12] The rail service has evacuated[^cite_note-13] over two million people from Ukraine on special evacuation trains. After some of the Black Sea ports became unavailable for grain export, rail became an export route to the rest of Europe. Several rail sections in the North and South became unusable.[^cite_note-CNN2022march-14]

## Company structure

### Financial outlook



Headquarters of *Ukrainian Railways*, [Pecherskyi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecherskyi_District), [Kyiv](585629.md)

As of 2026, Ukrainian Railways (UZ) faces a critical financial crisis, induced by significant asset losses and necessitated military use amidst the ongoing conflict.[^cite_note-15] In the first quarter of 2026, the company reported a loss of 7.9 billion hryvnias.[^cite_note-16] Since January 2026, the company holds a *Restricted Default* (RD) [credit rating](1194146.md) from [Fitch Ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitch_Ratings), and a *Selective Default* (SD) from [S\&P Global Ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_Global_Ratings) due to the suspension of coupon payments on its debt obligations.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] UZ is currently in the process of restructuring negotiations to manage its significant debt. By the end of 2025, freight traffic had fallen by year-on-year 7.8 percent to 161.3 million tonnes, whilst revenue dropped by 19 percent.[^cite_note-\:1-19] By the end of the first quarter of 2026, freight volumes had dropped further year-on-year to 34.8 million tonnes.

&#x20;The railway serves a vital lifeline for the country, providing key services for passenger, freight and [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_railways) use.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] The company is one of Ukraine's largest employers, with over 169,000 personnel as of 2026. Nonetheless, a twenty-seven percent reduction in staff (62,000 people) has been observed since 2022.[^cite_note-22] In October 2022, it was reported that 263 railroad workers had been killed during the course of the war.[^cite_note-23] At the beginning of 2026, it was reported that 1,030 employees of UZ had been killed, with another 2,923 wounded as a result of strikes.[^cite_note-24]&#x20;



A destroyed train at [Lozova-Pasazhyrskyi railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozova-Pasazhyrskyi_railway_station)

UZ has faced significant asset degradation as a result of the conflict. Assets such as [rolling stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_stock), [railway stations](26161590.md) and [railway lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_lines) have faced increasing attacks, with incidents in 2025 exceeding the combined total of the previous two years.[^cite_note-25] In late June 2026, CEO Oleksandr Pertsovskyi remarked that a "simply insane" surge in the number of strikes had occurred, with over 100 locomotives falling under attack since the start of the year.[^cite_note-26] In early July 2026, it was further reported that over 200 locomotives had been destroyed or damaged since the start of the year.[^cite_note-27]

Further economic factors affecting the company include rising inflation in the country, as well as protectionist measures from the [European Union](9317.md), such as the [EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Carbon_Border_Adjustment_Mechanism).[^cite_note-28]



A destroyed [locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotive) in [Sumy Oblast](1567618.md), March 2026.

Against a difficult financial situation, UZ plans to consolidate its rolling stock by removing older equipment through a scrap metal scheme, as well as increase revenue from the relatively profitable freight sector. UZ plans to increase [tariffs](55551.md) on freight traffic by forty-five percent by January 2027, with a thirty percent rise to occur from August 2026.[^cite_note-\:1-19] The increase in tariffs has received significant backlash from affected parties, stating that the move would result in the further reduction and shutdown of industrial facilities, amidst a period of significant inflation for the [Ukrainian hryvnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hryvnia_sign), with industrial inflation as 40.2 percent as of April 2026.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] A letter from Ukrmetallurgprom states that increased tariffs would result in a decline in national [GDP](12594.md) of nearly 100 billion hryvnia, with prominent facilities such as [ArcelorMittal Kryvyi Rih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal_Kryvyi_Rih) having already partially shutdown due to a disruption in reliable industrial rail service.[^cite_note-31]

### Administrative structure

The railways are split into six territorial railway companies: [Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Railway), [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Railways), [Odesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_Railways), [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Railways_\(Ukraine\)), [South-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Railways) and [Cisdnieper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisdnieper_Railways). The subdivision is purely administrative as it doesn't correspond to the particular railway lines or branches. The names of regional railways are purely historic, inherited from the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) and [Austro-Hungarian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire) (for instance the 'South-Western Railway' actually operates the north-central part of Ukraine's rail network, while the 'Southern Railway' actually operates in the east of the country).[^cite_note-538855_ofitsiynih_dokumentah_ukrzaliznitsi-32]

#### Naming

With all operational lines constructed prior to Ukrainian Independence, the names of branches reflect the geography of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) and the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary). As a result, Southern Railways and Southwestern Railways are located in their respective directions in relation to [Russia](25391.md), but are located in the northeast and north of [Ukraine](31750.md). In 2024, it was reported that railway stations were being renamed to comply with Ukrainian [derussificaion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derussification) and [decommunisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization) laws, with the removal of names such as "[Paris Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Commune)".[^cite_note-40] In February 2025, the Commission of the [Ukrainian Institute of National Memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Institute_of_National_Memory) claimed that "Southwestern" and "Southern Railways" names were propaganda for Russian imperial policy and violate the [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Condemnation_and_Prohibition_of_Propaganda_of_Russian_Imperial_Policy_in_Ukraine_and_the_Decolonization_of_Toponymy).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

#### Enactment of the law "On Railway Transport of Ukraine"

The law seeks to harmonize Ukrainian rail-related legislation with European Union law. It sets out the basic requirements, responsibilities and rights of the infrastructure operator, the carrier and the owners of access tracks, rules for infrastructure management, basic requirements for railway rolling stock, and basic activities of railway rolling stock operators. The bill also provides for the creation of a system of public administration in the field of railway safety in accordance with the requirements of European Union legislation, the implementation of which is provided by the Association Agreement, which will increase transport safety in conditions of competition in the railway market. According to the Ministry of Infrastructure of Ukraine, the law, supported by European experts, serves as a starting point for real reforms in the industry.[^cite_note-\:0-43]

#### Structural reform of Ukrainian Railways JSC

The company is to be separated into verticals in accordance with modern practices. This involves the creation of separate freight, passenger, production and infrastructure operators within Ukrainian Railways by the end of 2021.[^cite_note-44] According to the Ministry of Infrastructure of Ukraine, the project will ensure the transparency of financial flows within the company, and improve the quality of management of each activity. This will allow Ukrainian Railways to prepare for the emergence of private competitors in the railway market.[^cite_note-\:0-43]

## Infrastructure

This section is an excerpt from [Rail transport in Ukraine § Infrastructure](6011024.md#Infrastructure).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rail_transport_in_Ukraine\&action=edit)]

Railways in Ukraine use various gauge types. The broad gauge (1,520 mm), a legacy of Imperial Russian and Soviet times, dominates the landscape, and [narrow-gauge railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge_railways_in_Ukraine) (750 mm) also exist, but plans are being made to construct, restore or switch to more standard-gauge railways (1,435 mm) for better train connections with the rest of Europe.[^cite_note-Rail_transport_in_Ukraine_Moiseev-45] About half of railways in Ukraine are electrified (some of which have been damaged during the ongoing [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) and temporarily switched to diesel trains).[^cite_note-Rail_transport_in_Ukraine_UZ_stats_2020-46]

The total length of Ukrainian rails of all gauges was 28,000 kilometres at the end of 1997.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEC202318-47] Around January 2022, the length of railways in Ukraine was 19,790 km in broad-gauge 1520 mm (excluding those located in temporarily occupied territory), about 350 km in standard-gauge 1435 mm, and about 400 km in narrow gauge 750 mm.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEC202318-47]

## Rolling stock



Ukrainian-made electric [DS3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DS3_\(locomotive\)) locomotive



Ukrainian-made [DPKr-3 Diesel Multiple Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPKr-3_Diesel_Multiple_Unit)

Ukrzaliznytsia has several repair factories capable of producing locomotives and railcars. In addition there is a separate [Kryukiv Railcar Engineering Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVBZ) and [Dnieper Railcar Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DniproVahonMash) that also produce railroad rolling stock for Ukrzaliznytsia and other companies for public transportation.

In November 2010, UZ agreed to buy 10 high-speed [HRCS2 multiple unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HRCS2_multiple_unit) interurban trainsets from [Hyundai Rotem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Rotem), with the prospect of a much larger order or joint venture for local production.[^cite_note-48] The first two trains would be delivered in February 2012, two more in April and another two in May, when they will start operating.[^cite_note-49] They will be rated as Inter City+ and will be connecting [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv-Pasazhyrskyi_railway_station) with [Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_railway_station), [Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donetsk_railway_station) and [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_railway_station), and at a later stage with [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_railway_station) and [Odesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_railway_station).[^cite_note-50]

In July 2011 UZ announced plans to buy 433 electric freight locomotives; 292 [2EL4s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2EL4?action=edit\&redlink=1) from [Transmashholding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmashholding), and 141 locomotives (including class [VL11M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VL11)/6) from [Elmavalmshenebeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmavalmshenebeli?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Tbilisi](42298.md)).[^cite_note-51] Due to the [2014 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) Transmashholding was put on a sanctions list.[^cite_note-52]

In December 2017, UZ and [GE Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Transportation) agreed a to strategic partnership for the implementation of a 7-year rolling stock renewal program including the delivery of 30 locomotives to Ukrzaliznytsia in September 2018 with 40% manufactured in Ukraine.[^cite_note-53]

### Rolling stock renewal

#### Passenger cars

The draft state budget for 2021 includes the purchasing of 100 new passenger cars from the [Kryukiv Railway Car Building Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryukiv_Railway_Car_Building_Works).[^cite_note-57] In 2021 Ukrzaliznytsia will also launch its first [disabled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability_in_Ukraine)-[friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easy_Access) trains with dedicated [wheelchair](21481546.md) space.[^cite_note-58]

#### Locomotives

In 2018 Ukrainian Railways purchased 30 [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric) [TE33AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TE33A) Trident freight diesel locomotives.[^cite_note-59] The 30 locomotives were planned to be the first stage of wider cooperation, including a 15-year partnership, and the replacement and modernization of additional Ukrainian rolling stock.[^cite_note-60] Later in 2020, Ukrainian Railways CEO Volodymyr Zhmak stated that he does not see the need for further GE diesel locomotives, as the company will focus on electric traction due to its higher efficiency.[^cite_note-61]

Following the appointment of Volodymyr Zhmak as CEO, ₴400 million was reallocated within the company for urgent locomotive repairs until the end of 2020.[^cite_note-cfts_org_ua-62] As a part of this measure, the [Lviv](49968.md) Locomotive Repair Plant has already received an order for the repair of 6 electric locomotives until the end of 2020.[^cite_note-63]

In 2021, ₴4.7 billion is planned to be spent on repairing the existing locomotive fleet.[^cite_note-cfts_org_ua-62]

As of 2020, [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom) is interested in the renewal of the Ukrainian Railways locomotive fleet through partial localization of production. Volodymyr Zhmak stated that Ukrainian Railways is initially interested in the purchase of 50 two-system electric freight locomotives.[^cite_note-64] The purchase of Alstom locomotives would be financed by the French government.[^cite_note-65]

## Classification of passenger trains (railway lines)



[Train station in Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_railway_station)

&#x20;On 27 April 2011 in accordance with order No. 504/2011 rail industry specialists developed a new classification system of passenger trains for Ukrainian Railways.[^cite_note-67]



[Chernihiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv) station [building and platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv%E2%80%93Ovruch_railway)

The development of a new classification system for passenger trains in Ukraine was made necessary by the need to inform customers (passengers) about the level of service quality they could expect to find in various passenger trains. The new system is based on the class of train and carriages.

Given the quality of the service provided UZ asked the following classes of passenger trains:

- Daytime passenger lines:&#x20;

  - **[Euro City (EC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroCity)** – Express (min. 90 km/h) daytime services on international routes which should offer a very high level of service and comfort. First and standard classes.

  - **[Inter City+ (IC+)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity-Express)** – Express (min. 90 km/h) daytime services on domestic routes which should offer a very high level of service and comfort. First and standard classes. These services are currently operated by [Hyundai Rotem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Rotem) [HRCS2 multiple unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HRCS2_multiple_unit) trains on routes between [Kyiv](585629.md) and [Kharkiv](2788558.md), [Lviv](49968.md), [Dnipro](989795.md), [Zaporizhzhia](1873579.md), [Pokrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokrovsk,_Ukraine), [Kryvyi Rih](624764.md), [Kostiantynivka](2879020.md), [Odesa](55545.md), [Ternopil](512798.md), [Kropyvnytskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kropyvnytskyi). Besides, there is one international express Kyiv - [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl) (Poland).

    [^cite_note-68]

  - **[Inter City (IC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail)** – (max. 70 km/h – 90 km/h) daytime services on domestic routes which should offer a heightened level of service and comfort. First, standard and economy classes. These services are currently operated by [Škoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0koda_Works) [UZ class 675](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CD_Class_471) trains on routes between [Kharkiv](2788558.md), [Dnipro](989795.md) and [Donetsk](562951.md).

  - **Regional Express (РЕ)** – (max. 70 km/h – 90 km/h) daytime services on domestic routes which should offer a standard level of service and comfort. First, standard and economy classes.

  - **[Regional train (РП)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail)** – (max. 70 km/h) daytime services on domestic routes which should offer a standard level of service and comfort. Standard and [economy classes.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_class)

- Nighttime passenger trains:&#x20;

  - **Euro Night (EN)** – Express (min. 90 km/h) nighttime services on international routes which should offer a very high level of service and comfort. [2 berth coupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeping_car) and 4 berth coupe classes.

  - **Night Express (НЕ)** (max. 70 km/h – 90 km/h) nighttime services on international and domestic routes which should offer a heightened level of service and comfort. [2 berth coupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeping_car), 4 berth coupe and [platskarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couchette_car) classes.

  - **Night fast (НШ)** – (max. 50 km/h – 70 km/h) nighttime services on international and domestic routes which should offer a heightened level of service and comfort. [2 berth coupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeping_car), 4 berth coupe and [platskarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couchette_car) classes.

  - **Night passenger (НП)** (max. 50 km/h) nighttime services on international and domestic routes which should offer a heightened level of service and comfort. [4 berth coupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeping_car) and [platskarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couchette_car) classes.

The advantages of the new classification system include full compliance with the classification of the [European Union](9317.md), compliance with Ukrainian and English names and abbreviations, linguistic and semantic consistency and clarity for customers in Ukraine and compatibility with existing and future tariff policy. The system is also not far displaced from the previous classification system used for passenger trains on the territory of Ukraine.

The R\&D branch of Ukrainian Railways is the Research and Design and Technological Institute of Railway Transport (NDKTI), which conducts scientific and technological research, development, engineering and design for infrastructure, rolling stock, security, telecommunications and so forth. It publishes a quarterly journal, *Railway Transport of Ukraine*, where the latest insights and results of experiments are shared within the community of engineers, scientists and railway workers of Ukraine.[^cite_note-69]

The [National Railway University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipropetrovsk_National_University_of_Rail_Transport) in [Dnipro](989795.md) currently has 10 faculties as well as a technical school, a business school and branches in [Odesa](55545.md) and [Lviv](49968.md), 450 professors and 39 separate fields of study related to railway transport.

## Social initiatives

### Iron Land

In June 2023, the Iron Land children’s area, created jointly by [UNICEF](19698110.md) and JSC ‘Ukrzaliznytsia’, was opened at Kyiv Central Railway Station. Covering an area of over 700 m², the space is designed for leisure, relaxation and the development of children and families travelling by rail[^cite_note-78].

Iron Land comprises sports and play areas, a library, a games room, a science and education area featuring railway exhibits, and a miniature model of the ‘Tarpan’ high-speed train. There are also waiting areas, baby care facilities and mobile device charging points for visitors. Entertainers, psychologists and medical staff are on hand at the venue.

Similar children’s areas are also in operation at the railway stations in Kharkiv and Lviv.[^cite_note-79]

### The ‘Granatomed’ project

In November 2025, Ukrzaliznytsia joined the ‘Granatomed’ charity project, launched by the ‘[Come Back Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Come_Back_Alive)’ Foundation. The project was aimed at raising funds to purchase automatic grenade launchers and mortars for units of the [Ukrainian Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Forces_of_Ukraine).

As part of the project, the partners, in collaboration with Gemini, have launched ‘Granatomed’ tea, with a pomegranate flavour and honey marmalade[^cite_note-80]. Thanks to the sale of tea and marmalade on long-distance trains between November 2025 and May 2026, nearly 24 million hryvnias were raised, which were used to purchase weapons for the [Ukrainian Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Forces_of_Ukraine).

In June 2026, the ‘Come Back Alive’ Foundation announced that it had handed over 10 mortars and 10 Ukrainian-made automatic grenade launchers, purchased as part of the project, to four brigades of the Ukrainian Defence Forces[^cite_note-81]. [Taras Chmut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Chmut), the head of the foundation, announced that the transfer of the weapons had been completed on 1 June 2026[^cite_note-82].
