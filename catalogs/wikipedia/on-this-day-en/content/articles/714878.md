**La Libertad** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[la liβeɾˈtað\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is a [department of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Peru). Located on the country's northwestern coast, it is the ninth smallest department in Peru, but is also its second-most populous after [Piura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Piura), and the second-most densely populated after [Lambayeque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Lambayeque). Its capital is the coastal city of [Trujillo](476649.md), the nation's third biggest city.

La Libertad is the site of many of [Pre-Columbian Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_Peru)'s significant cultures, such as the [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_culture), the [Wari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wari_culture), the [Chimú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimor) and, ultimately, the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire). It was ultimately conquered by the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) during the 16th century and, after a series of administrative changes, ultimately incorporated as the [Intendancy of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendancy_of_Trujillo) in 1784. It played a [significant role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Trujillo) in the [Peruvian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_War_of_Independence), after which it was created as one of the [foundational provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reglamento_Provisional) of the [Protectorate of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Peru). In 1825, it was given its current name.

Its territory was that of the intendancy, which originally included most of Peru's territory north and east of [Lima](85423.md), including that of the former [General Command of Maynas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Command_of_Maynas). Due to its large size, its territory gradually shrank from 1832 to 1874, with the creation of the departments of [Piura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Piura) (1837), [Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_\(Peruvian_department\)) (1832), [Cajamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Cajamarca) (1855) and [Lambayeque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Lambayeque) (1874), after which it virtually acquired its current borders.

It is the site of two important [heritage sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage_of_Peru) designated as [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) by [UNESCO](21786641.md): [Rio Abiseo National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Abiseo_National_Park) (designated in 1983) and [Chan Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chan) (designated in 1986). [Salaverry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaverry), its main port, is one of Peru's largest ports.

## Etymology

The department's name honours its [contribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Trujillo) to the [Peruvian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_War_of_Independence). Prior to 1825, it was known as the **Department of Trujillo**.[^cite_note-Alva-1]

## History

### Pre-Columbian period

As with most of the country's coastal and central region, the area was once the home to a number of [Pre-Columbian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_Peru), notably the [Chavín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chav%C3%ADn_culture), [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_culture), [Sican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sican_culture), and [Wari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wari_culture) peoples. The latter established [an empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wari_Empire) whose area would eventually be absorbed into the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire), the last pre-Columbian entity to exist prior to the arrival of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire).

Prior to the Spanish conquest, the long-extinct and very poorly known [Quingnam language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quingnam_language) was spoken in La Libertad.[^cite_note-\:3-2]

### Spanish period

Following the [Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_the_Inca_Empire), the area as a whole was incorporated into the [Intendancy of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendancy_of_Trujillo), a subdivision of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru). At the time, the area under its jurisdiction included present-day [Lambayeque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambayeque_Region), [Piura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piura_Region) and [Tumbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbes_Region), as well as [Guayaquil](215586.md) and [El Oro](696562.md) in Ecuador.

### Republican period

During the [Peruvian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_War_of_Independence), the intendancy was instrumental in assisting [José de San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_San_Mart%C3%ADn)'s military campaign against Spain. Following his landing at [Paracas Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracas_Bay) in September 1820, intendant [José Bernardo de Tagle y Portocarrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Bernardo_de_Tagle_y_Portocarrero) and the city mayor led a rebellion that culminated with the declaration of the [Independence of Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Trujillo) on December 29 of the same year.

On February 12, 1821, San Martín issued a [Provisional Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reglamento_Provisional), providing for the creation of the Department of Trujillo, among others.[^cite_note-O'Phelan-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] San Martín's regulations divided the department into seven provinces:[^cite_note-6]

While the city's name change was undone through another law on July 21, 1827, the department's name was not changed, and has since remained.[^cite_note-Alva-1]

## Geography

La Libertad is the only Peruvian region that includes all three natural regions of the nation: coast, [Sierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Peru#Andean_Mountain_Ranges) (highlands), and *selva* (rainforest).

Trujillo, the capital, has a strategic location, near where the Andes come closest to the coast. Seen from Trujillo, the Andes appears as a row of low-elevation hills. The Andean Plateau increases altitude sharply to the east, in the provinces of [Otuzco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otuzco_Province) and [Santiago de Chuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Chuco_Province). These two provinces comprise the Pacific hydrographic watershed, which give rise to the [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_River) and [Virú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vir%C3%BA_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) rivers, to the south, and [Chicama River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicama_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) to the north. [Pacasmayo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacasmayo_Province), located more to the north, is along the coast. To the east, [Sánchez Carrión Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1nchez_Carri%C3%B3n_Province) waterways drain into the [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) and thus belong to the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) watershed.

## Demographics

### Population

La Libertad region is the third-most populous in Peru; the census 2025 recorded 2,056,560 residents, representing around 6% of the Peruvian population. It was surpassed only by the Department of [Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Region) and [Piura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piura_Region). In 2012 its capital Trujillo is the [second-most populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_of_Peru) metropolitan area in Peru and the largest city in northern Peru. The largest population is concentrated in provincial capitals and districts, which comprise the majority urban population in La Libertad region. The migrant population to the region comes from border departments and immigrants from other countries.

## Archaeology



Chan Chan, the huge adobe town from Chimu time



Aia Paec, Moche god

The coastal strip set the stage for the rise of many pre-Columbian cultures, such as the pre-Ceramic [Huaca Prieta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_Prieta) civilization, which is more than 5,000 years old & the Cupisnique, which is more than 3,000 years old. From 200 A.C., the first one to expand beyond its cradle was the [Moche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moche_\(culture\)) or Mochica culture. It was basically an agriculture and/or a warrior culture, which built countless temples and palaces such as the *[Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca_del_Sol)* (Sun), *Luna* (Moon), *El Brujo* & *Cao Viejo*, and other [huacas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaca). The [Chimú culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chim%C3%BA_culture) emerged later and built its capital in [Chan Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Chan), the largest pre-Columbian city in South America, & huacas like *Esmeralda* & *Arco Iris* (Rainbow). At its [zenith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith), Chan Chan was home to 60,000 inhabitants who stubbornly resisted the expansion of the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire). These ancient cultures used irrigation canals and water reservoirs, which systems were increasingly better engineered and extensive over the years. The technological acumen of these sophisticated agricultural systems was carried into the Inca Empire, which surrounded the remnants of the prior cultures. The Spanish colonizers destroyed most of the agricultural works to more effectively establish political control and provide *de facto* slave labor from the displaced native agriculturalists.

The archaeological remains of Chan Chan, 6 km (3.7 mi) northeast of downtown Trujillo, are rather well-preserved despite being built out of [adobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe) (mud bricks), largely because 1) dearth of rainfall and consequent erosion, and 2) lack of significant re-use of its construction materials (adobes do not respond easily to removal and transport and are relatively cheap to make on-site in current methods of construction).

During the Late Moche phase (AD 600 to 850), one of the largest power centers of the region was the fortified site of [Cerro Chepén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Chep%C3%A9n?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the Jequetepeque Valley. It is located 3 km (1.9 mi) to the south of the site of [San José de Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jos%C3%A9_de_Moro).[^cite_note-11]

Cerro Chepén has a sophisticated system of fortifications, and a dominant position on top of the hill, with many monumental buildings. Recent excavations at the site revealed that there was much internal conflict there that affected local communities.[^cite_note-12]

## Culture

### Festivals and events

- [Spring Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Spring_Festival)

- [Marinera Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_Marinera_Festival)

- **[International Calzaferia El Porvenir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Calzaferia_El_Porvenir?action=edit\&redlink=1)**. It is a fair of footwear and in 2012 took place the 10th edition; it is held in *El Porvenir* city.

  [^cite_note-13]

- **[Regional Fair of the Pineapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Fair_of_the_Pineapple?action=edit\&redlink=1)**. It is held in the town of Poroto.

  [^cite_note-14]

- [Virgin of La Puerta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_of_La_Puerta). Patronal Feast; the celebration originated in 1664 when it placed the image of the Virgin at the entrance of Otuzco as precaution of the risk of a pirate raid. The main day is celebrated on December 15 every year and in 2012 the feast of Our Lady of the Gate was declared a National Cultural Heritage by the Peruvian government.

  [^cite_note-15]

- [Contradanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contradanza). Expression cultivated in Huamachuco city, in the villages Urpay, Shiracmaca and Culicanda. This dance in 2012 has been declared a National Cultural Heritage by the Peruvian government.

  [^cite_note-16]
