The **Diocese of Rochester** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Dioecesis Roffensis*) is a [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) of the [Catholic Church](606848.md) in the Upstate region of [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State) in the United States. The metropolitan for the diocese is the archbishop of [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_New_York). The [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral) [parish](23623.md) for the diocese is the [Cathedral of the Sacred Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Heart_Cathedral_\(Rochester,_New_York\)) in Rochester. The diocese comprises approximately 350,000 Catholics, 22 diocesan elementary schools, and seven independent parochial high schools. The bishop is [John Bonnici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_S._Bonnici).

## Territory

The Diocese of Rochester extends from [Lake Ontario](17947.md) through [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York), and the Finger Lakes region to part of the [Southern Tier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Tier) region near the [New York](8210131.md)-[Pennsylvania](23332.md) border.

## History

### Early history

During the Dutch and British rule of the [Province of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York) in the 17th and 18th centuries, Catholics were banned from the colony.[^cite_note-\:22-1] [Richard Coote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Coote,_1st_Earl_of_Bellomont), the first colonial governor, passed a law at the end of the 17th century that mandated a life sentence to any Catholic priest found in the colony. The penalty for harboring a Catholic was a £250 fine plus three days in the [pillory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillory).[^cite_note-2] In 1763, Catholic Bishop [Richard Challoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Challoner) of London stated that: "there is not much likelihood that Catholic priests will be permitted to enter these provinces."[^cite_note-\:22-1]

During the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), the new State of New York in 1777 approved a [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Constitution) that guaranteed freedom of worship for Catholics. This was soon followed by the same guarantee in the [US Bill of Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bill_of_Rights).

### 1784 to 1868

In 1784, [Pope Pius VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI) erected the [Apostolic Prefecture of United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Prefecture_of_the_United_States), including all of the new United States. In 1789, the same pope raised this prefecture to the [Diocese of Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Baltimore).[^cite_note-Mooney-3] In 1808, [Pope Pius VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII) erected the Diocese of New York, taking all of New York State from the Diocese of Baltimore.[^cite_note-\:2-4]

In Auburn, the first mass for non-native Catholics was held in a private residence in 1816.[^cite_note-5] The first church in the future [City of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York) was St. Patrick's, built in 1823.[^cite_note-6] In 1832, the first church was constructed in [Geneva, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva,_New_York), St. Francis de Sales.[^cite_note-7] [Pope Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX) erected the [Diocese of Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Buffalo) in 1847, including all of the present-day Diocese of Rochester.[^cite_note-\:2-4]

### 1868 to 1881



St. Patrick's Cathedral, Rochester, New York (1914)

The Diocese of Rochester was erected on March 3, 1868, by Pius IX. He transferred eight counties ([Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_County,_New_York), Livingston, Wayne, [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_County,_New_York), Seneca, [Cayuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayuga_County,_New_York), Yates, and [Tompkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tompkins_County,_New_York)) from the Diocese of Buffalo to the new diocese.[^cite_note-\:3-8] The pope appointed Monsignor [Bernard J. McQuaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_J._McQuaid), from the Diocese of New York, as the first bishop of Rochester.[^cite_note-\:3-8] The new diocese included approximately 54,500 Catholics, 35 parish churches and 29 mission churches.

When McQuaid first arrived in Rochester, the only parochial schools were operated by the five German-language parishes, educating a total of 2,000 students.[^cite_note-encyc2-9] In 1870, McQuaid opened St. Patrick [minor seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_seminary) for young men.[^cite_note-encyc2-9] It was renamed St. Andrew's in 1879.[^cite_note-10] In 1871, McQuaid announced his plan to create a system of tuition-free parochial schools in the diocese, staffed by the [Sisters of St. Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisters_of_St._Joseph).[^cite_note-11]

### 1881 to 1900



Bishop McQuaid (pre-1909)

In September 1893, [Saint Bernard's Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Bernard's_Seminary) opened in Rochester with 39 seminarians and eight faculty members. The faculty included [Edward Hanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Joseph_Hanna) as professor of [dogmatic theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogmatic_theology) and [Andrew Breen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Breen) as professor of Hebrew and Scripture.[^cite_note-12] McQuaid himself taught [homiletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homiletics) there.[^cite_note-13] By 1910, Saint Bernard's had an enrollment of 233 seminarians, second only to [St. Mary's Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary's_Seminary_and_University) in Baltimore.[^cite_note-14]

In 1896, [Pope Leo XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII) transferred four more southern counties ([Schuyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuyler_County,_New_York), Tioga, [Chemung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemung_County,_New_York), and Steuben) from the Diocese of Buffalo to the Diocese of Rochester, forming its current boundaries.[^cite_note-\:3-8]

### 1900 to 1933



St. Bernard's Seminary, Rochester, New York (2012)

In 1905, [Pope Pius X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_X) appointed [Thomas F. Hickey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Francis_Hickey_\(bishop\)) of Rochester as coadjutor bishop in the diocese to assist McQuaid. McQuaid died in 1909.[^cite_note-15] At that time, 53 of the diocese's 93 parishes had their own parochial school, with a total of 18,000 students.[^cite_note-directory-16] After McQuaid's death, Hickey became the second bishop of Rochester.

Hickey established a [catechetical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechetical) program for Catholic children enrolled in [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_\(government_funded\)). He supported the apostolate to [deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_impairment) persons, pioneered the work of [Catholic Charities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Charities) within the diocese, and helped the New York bishops establish an office to communicate with the [New York Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Legislature) about Catholic concerns.[^cite_note-17] Hickey led the creation of the [Aquinas Institute for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquinas_Institute) and [Nazareth Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazareth_Academy_\(Rochester,_New_York\)) for girls in Rochester, and the founding of [Nazareth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazareth_College_\(New_York\)) in Pittsford.[^cite_note-\:02-18] Hickey retired in 1928.

In 1929, [John Francis O'Hern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Francis_O'Hern) of Rochester was appointed the third bishop of the diocese by [Pope Pius XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI).[^cite_note-hierarchy-19] During his tenure, O'Hern worked toward establishing [ecumenical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical) ties with non-Catholics and promoting numerous associations of the [laity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laity).[^cite_note-diocese-20] He supported the [Community Chest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Chest_\(organization\)) and [American Red Cross](15487.md), and provided [chaplains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaplain) for Catholics students attending secular colleges in the diocese.[^cite_note-diocese-20] O'Hern died in 1933.

### 1933 to 1969



Bishop Sheen (1952)

[Pope Pius XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI) named Archbishop [Edward Mooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Aloysius_Mooney), formerly the Apostolic Delegate to [Japan](15573.md), as the next bishop of Rochester in 1933. During his tenure in Rochester, Mooney promoted the [Catholic Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Action) movement and the [Knights of Peter Claver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_of_Peter_Claver) as a means of outreach to the [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) community. He also took a deep interest in [Catholic social teaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_social_teaching) and labor relations.[^cite_note-21] In 1937, Pius XI appointed Mooney as the first archbishop of the [Archdiocese of Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Detroit).

To replace Mooney in Rochester, Pius IX in 1937 selected Bishop [James E. Kearney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._Kearney) of the [Diocese of Salt Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Salt_Lake_City). Kearney did much of the original planning of [McQuaid Jesuit High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McQuaid_Jesuit_High_School) in Brighton. Kearney resigned in 1966.[^cite_note-22] [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) then named Auxiliary Bishop [Fulton J. Sheen](256767.md) of New York as the next bishop of Rochester.[^cite_note-23] While serving in Rochester, he created the Sheen Ecumenical Housing Foundation.

In 1967, Sheen decided to give the St. Bridget's Parish building to the federal [Housing and Urban Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_and_Urban_Development) program. Sheen wanted to let the government use it for services for African-Americans. There was a protest in the community since Sheen had acted on his own accord. The pastor disagreed with Sheen's initiative, saying that "There is enough empty property around without taking down the church and the school." The deal eventually fell through.[^cite_note-24] Sheen resigned as bishop in 1969 to devote more time to his writings; [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) elevated him to a [titular archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_archbishop).

### 1969 to present



Nazareth University, Pittsford, New York (2012)

To replace Sheen in Rochester, Paul VI in 1969 selected Monsignor [Joseph Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Lloyd_Hogan) as bishop. After serving nine years as bishop, Hogan resigned in 1978.[^cite_note-25] [Pope John Paul I](23805.md)I then named [Matthew H. Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_H._Clark) as the next bishop of Rochester.[^cite_note-26] In August 1985, Brother John Walsh, vice principal of [Cardinal Mooney High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Mooney_High_School_\(New_York\)) in [Greece, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece,_New_York), was arrested on [kidnapping](169376.md) charges. Walsh had forced two boys he encountered on the street in Rochester into his car at gunpoint and tried to pay them for sex. He later released the boys unharmed.[^cite_note-27] Walsh pleaded guilty in January 1986 to coercion and unlawful imprisonment and was sentenced to six months in jail.[^cite_note-28] In 1986, Cardinal [Josef Ratzinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Ratzinger) ordered Clark to withdraw his [imprimatur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imprimatur), or church approval, from a [sex education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_education) manual written by a diocesan priest. Ratzinger said the manual was "defective" on church teachings.[^cite_note-29] That same year, Clark defended one of his priests, the theologian [Charles Curran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Curran_\(theologian\)), from criticism by Vatican officials for his stands on [birth control](18978770.md), abortion rights for women, [homosexuality](5488304.md), and divorce.[^cite_note-30] Gerard Guli of Holy Rosary Parish in Rochester was arrested in April 1989 on first-degree sexual abuse charges. He was accused of fondling the breasts of a nursing home patient who had severe [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease).[^cite_note-31] Guli pleaded guilty later that year and was sentenced to five years of [probation](406786.md).[^cite_note-32] Gull was later [laicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_of_clerical_state) at his own request.[^cite_note-33]

Eugene Emo was arrested in February 1996 on charges of sexually abusing a man with developmental disabilities in [Cohocton, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohocton,_New_York). The diocese had removed Emo from St. Januarius Parish in [Naples, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples,_New_York), in 1993 after he tried to cover the theft of parish funds by some boys and after a housekeeper found handcuffs and pictures of young men in his residence. The diocese sent Emo for treatment, then transferred him to a different parish.[^cite_note-34] Emo pleaded guilty to one felony count of first-degree [sexual assault](218407.md) and was sentenced to six months in jail and five years probation. By this time, the diocese had received several other complaints of sexual abuse by Emo.[^cite_note-35] In 1999, he violated his probation by having contact with a 16-year-old boy and was returned to prison.[^cite_note-36]

In 1998, the diocese removed [James Callan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Callan) as pastor of Corpus Christi Parish in Rochester. Callan had come into conflict with the diocese for opening communion to non-Catholics and blessing the unions of [LGBTQ](78174325.md) couples. He also allowed Mary Rammerman, a lay pastor, to raise the [chalice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalice) during mass. After the diocese transferred Callan and fired Rammerman, the congregation split from the diocese, forming Spiritus Christi Church. The diocese later removed Callan from his ministerial duties after he appeared at a service at the new church.[^cite_note-37]

In May 2002, two men sued the diocese, stating that they had been sexually abused by Robert O'Neil, pastor of St. Christopher Parish in [Chili, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili,_New_York). The plaintiffs said that O'Neil took them in the 1970s to his cottage in [Chaumont, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaumont,_New_York), where he plied them with alcohol and abused them. They later complained about O'Neil to auxiliary bishop [Dennis Hickey.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Walter_Hickey) The diocese sent O'Neil away for treatment, then reassigned him to pastoral work. A week before the lawsuit was filed in 2002, the diocese stripped O'Neil of his ministerial duties and banned him from diocesan housing.[^cite_note-38]

In 2003, Clark was criticized over his \$11 million renovation and liturgical re-alignment of [Sacred Heart Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Heart_Cathedral_\(Rochester\)) in Rochester.[^cite_note-39] Clark received some credit for clamping down on abusive priests;[^cite_note-40] in 2004, the diocese was deemed to be in "full compliance" with the US Conference of Catholic Bishops (USCCB) [Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_for_the_Protection_of_Children_and_Young_People).[^cite_note-41] Clark presided over the unpopular closing of many of Rochester's parochial schools and parishes, pledging to complete the "re-sizing" of the diocese prior to his retirement in 2012.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Dennis Sewar of Annunciation Parish in Rochester was arrested in July 2005 on charges of sexual abuse and endangering the welfare of a child.[^cite_note-44] The male accuser said that Sewar groped him numerous times between 1999 and 2001.[^cite_note-45] After a judge dismissed the more serious charges, Sewar pleaded guilty in August 2006 to attempted endangering the welfare of a child and was sentenced to one year of probation.[^cite_note-46]

The diocese revealed in June 2018 that it had paid \$1.6 million in compensation since 1950 to 20 individuals who had been sexually abused by diocesan clergy.[^cite_note-47] Most of the payments occurred after 2002, although some were decades old. In June 2019, a Rochester man sued the diocese alleging sexual abuse by Francis Vogt between 1969 and 1971. The plaintiff said that Vogt started abusing him when he was five-years-old and that the diocese shielded Vogt from potential prosecution.[^cite_note-grownnot-48]

The ninth bishop of Rochester was [Salvatore Matano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Ronald_Matano), formerly bishop of the [Diocese of Burlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Burlington). He was appointed by [Pope Francis](1687680.md) in 2013.[^cite_note-49] In September 2017, the diocese inaugurated its sesquicentennial anniversary, marked by a [Solemn Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solemn_Mass) at Sacred Heart Cathedral. The event marked a year-long celebration of the 150-year anniversary and the year of the Eucharist, which Matano proclaimed on the [Feast of Corpus Christi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_Corpus_Christi).

The diocese filed for bankruptcy protection in September 2019 in the wake of multiple sexual abuse lawsuits. As of July 2024, the bankruptcy court was still working on a settlement.[^cite_note-50] In January 2024, a jury awarded a \$95 million verdict against the diocese. The defendant had accused Foster P. Rogers, assistant pastor at St. Alphonsus Parish in Auburn, of sexually abusing him in 1979 when he was 15-years-old. Clark had removed Rogers from ministry in 2002 after the diocese transferred him to several parishes.[^cite_note-51]

[Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md) accepted Matano's resignation in 2026 and appointed [John S. Bonnici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_S._Bonnici) as the tenth bishop of Rochester. He was formerly an [auxiliary bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_bishop) of the [Archdiocese of New York](245234.md).[^cite_note-52]

### Statistics

The Diocese of Rochester grew as more Catholic immigrants moved to [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York), peaking in the 1960s. Since then, the Catholic population has stabilized while the number of priests and [religious sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_sister_\(Catholic\)) has fallen.[^cite_note-53]

- 1909 – 121,000 Catholics in 93 parishes, 36 missions and 53 parish schools with 18,000 pupils. The diocese had 164 priests and over 500 sisters.

- 1938 – 223,657 Catholics in 129 parishes, 36 missions and 72 parish schools serving 23,796 pupils. There were 289 active diocesan priests.

- 1966 – 361,790 Catholics in 155 parishes, 36 mission churches and 99 elementary parish schools serving 45,540 pupils. There were 371 active diocesan priests and 1,549 sisters.

- 1978 – 358,850 Catholics in 161 parishes, 29 mission churches and 75 schools serving 19,526 pupils. There were 311 active diocesan priests and 1,095 sisters.

- 1992 – 162 parishes and 58 elementary schools serving 11,992 pupils. There were 208 active diocesan priests and 842 sisters.

## Publishing

The Rochester Catholic Press Association, Inc. (RCPA) is the publishing arm of the Diocese of Rochester. The RCPA publishes the monthly *Catholic Courier* newspape&#x72;*,* the Spanish-language *El Mensajero Católico,* the *Official Directory of the Diocese of Rochester* and related digital media.

The *Courier* was founded in 1889 as *The Catholic Journal.* The diocese took over the newspaper during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression)*.* The paper became the *Catholic Courier* in 1989, its 100th anniversary.*[^cite_note-62]* The *Courier* has won state and national awards for journalistic excellence.[^cite_note-63]
