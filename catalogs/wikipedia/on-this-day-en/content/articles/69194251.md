The **2025 Virginia gubernatorial election** was held on November 4, 2025, to elect the [governor of Virginia](253093.md). [Democratic](5043544.md) former congresswoman [Abigail Spanberger](58910530.md) defeated [Republican](32070.md) lieutenant governor [Winsome Earle-Sears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winsome_Earle-Sears). Spanberger succeeded Republican incumbent [Glenn Youngkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Youngkin) who was [term-limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits_in_the_United_States) and ineligible to seek a second consecutive term.

Earle-Sears and Spanberger ran unopposed in their respective party primaries. In April 2025, they were confirmed as the major party nominees.[^cite_note-2] The central issues of the campaign were the economy and [affordability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_affordability_crisis), which included the federal layoffs from the [Trump administration](78298108.md). [Transgender rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_rights_in_the_United_States) and political rhetoric followed behind as secondary issues.[^cite_note-vg-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] [President Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Donald_Trump)'s approval and perceived performance on the economy, healthcare, immigration, and other issues were viewed as contributing factors for Earle-Sears's loss.[^cite_note-Trump-6]

Spanberger won the general election by 15.36%—the largest gubernatorial percentage margin since [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Virginia_gubernatorial_election).[^cite_note-Northern_Virginia-7] Democrats flipped all three statewide executive offices and [expanded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Virginia_House_of_Delegates_election) their majority in the [House of Delegates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_House_of_Delegates), creating a Democratic trifecta in Virginia's government for the first time since [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Virginia_elections).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Spanberger was elected the first [female governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_female_governors_in_the_United_States) of the state,[^cite_note-10] winning by the largest Democratic gubernatorial margin since [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Virginia_gubernatorial_election).[^cite_note-Failed-11] She was sworn in as the 75th governor of Virginia on January 17, 2026.[^cite_note-12]

## Background

In the [2021 gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Virginia_gubernatorial_election), Youngkin defeated former governor [Terry McAuliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_McAuliffe) by two points, after [Joe Biden](145422.md) won the state in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election_in_Virginia) by 10 points. The central issues of the election were the economy and education. In that election, Republicans flipped the three statewide executive offices and the [House of Delegates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_House_of_Delegates).[^cite_note-13] In the [2023 legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Virginia_elections), Democrats narrowly flipped the House of Delegates and maintained control of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Senate).[^cite_note-sweep2023-14] Youngkin, who had a 20% net approval, was ineligible to run for re-election to a second consecutive term due to [term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits_in_the_United_States) set in the [Virginia Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Constitution).[^cite_note-15]

In the 2024 presidential election, Republican former president Donald Trump was re-elected, which Democrats hoped would energize their voter base in the off-year elections. Trump's personal popularity and voters' position on the economy were viewed as crucial to which party would win the election.[^cite_note-16] In January 2025, President Trump and [Elon Musk](909036.md) introduced the [Department of Government Efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency).[^cite_note-17] DOGE laid off thousands of federal workers, mainly in [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia) and [Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads), where 140,000 federal workers resided.[^cite_note-18]

Due to Virginia's [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states) lean, the state was viewed as a likely gubernatorial pickup for Democrats while national circumstances were unfavorable for Republicans.[^cite_note-19] With the exception of [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Virginia_gubernatorial_election), Virginia has not elected a governor from the same party as the incumbent president since [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Virginia_gubernatorial_election).[^cite_note-20] The economy was described as the top issue for voters, while healthcare and education followed.[^cite_note-21] There were economic concerns over [tariffs](55551.md) and federal cuts from the [Trump administration](78298108.md).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

### Campaign

On September 4, 2024, Winsome Earle-Sears declared her candidacy for governor. She was criticized by attorney general [Jason Miyares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Miyares), who was viewed as a potential candidate, for making her announcement ahead of the 2024 elections.[^cite_note-28] After the 2024 elections, Miyares declined to run for governor, with Earle-Sears being described as the presumptive nominee.[^cite_note-29]

On February 27, former state senator [Amanda Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Chase) and former state delegate [Dave LaRock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_LaRock) entered the primary in opposition to Earle-Sears. They associated themselves with President Trump and criticized Earle-Sears's previous comments about him. Despite the newfound opposition, Earle-Sears was still viewed as the favorite in the primary.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Neither Chase nor LaRock was able to qualify for the primary ballot. Earle-Sears was subsequently announced as the Republican nominee on April 5.[^cite_note-is-24]

### Campaign

In 2020, Governor [Ralph Northam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Northam) encouraged [Abigail Spanberger](58910530.md) to eventually run for governor.[^cite_note-41] On November 13, 2023, she announced her candidacy and did not run for re-election in the House of Representatives in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections).[^cite_note-rg-42]

In December 2023, [Richmond mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Richmond,_Virginia) [Levar Stoney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levar_Stoney) declared his candidacy. Despite Stoney's candidacy, Spanberger was viewed as the favorite to win the primary due to his low statewide name recognition.[^cite_note-ls-43][^cite_note-sc-44] There was private pressure from Democrats for Stoney to withdraw from the race to allow Spanberger to win the nomination. In April 2024, Stoney withdrew from the race to "avoid a costly and damaging primary" and unsuccessfully ran for [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Virginia_lieutenant_gubernatorial_election#Democratic_primary).[^cite_note-ms-39]

In December 2024, U.S. representative [Bobby Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Scott_\(politician\)), explored the possibility of running for governor, despite doubt from internal sources over whether he would actually do so.[^cite_note-bs-45] In February 2025, Scott hinted he would not run for governor and ultimately did not file a candidacy.[^cite_note-nr-40] On April 3, Spanberger secured the Democratic nomination after no other candidacies were filed.[^cite_note-AbigailNomination-38]

This was the first gubernatorial election in the state's history in which both major party nominees were women.[^cite_note-55] President Trump instated [tariffs](78448911.md) on other nations including [Canada](5042916.md) and [China](5405.md). The tariffs were expected to increase prices on consumer goods from companies who relied on foreign imports. Spanberger disagreed with the tariffs and said they were "... a massive tax hike on Virginians — plain and simple."[^cite_note-56]

Virginia Democrats highlighted Earle-Sears's conservative positions on social issues and support for tariffs.[^cite_note-57] After the June primaries, the economy emerged as the main issue for both campaigns. Spanberger's campaign message was focused on affordability: she continued to oppose federal layoffs and tariffs from the Trump administration. Earle-Sears pledged to continue Youngkin's economic policies.[^cite_note-58] After Earle-Sears replaced her campaign manager, there were internal concerns from the [Virginia Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Virginia) over how her campaign was managed. By July, Spanberger significantly outraised Earle-Sears, while there was lack of Republican investment in the race compared to [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Virginia_gubernatorial_election).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

Earle-Sears's statements on federal workers throughout the campaign were criticized as "self-inflicted wounds" and supportive of President Trump's DOGE cuts.[^cite_note-61] Earle-Sears began to focus on [transgender policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_youth) in Northern Virginia's schools, which mirrored Youngkin's focus on [critical race theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_race_theory) in 2021. On August 21, Earle-Sears's speech at an [Arlington County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia) School Board meeting received protests. One protest sign stated, "Hey Winsome, if trans \[*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*] can’t share your bathroom, then Blacks can’t share my water fountain." The sign received media attention and condemnation from Spanberger and Earle-Sears.[^cite_note-62] Spanberger continued to campaign on affordability issues, which remained the top issue for voters. She campaigned in [Southwest Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Virginia) and attempted to win over rural voters who voted Trump in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Virginia).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] By October, Earle-Sears ran mainly on anti-transgender policies in schools. Earle-Sears ran attack ads against Spanberger which resembled the "[Kamala is for they/them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_is_for_they/them)" ad from the [2024 presidential election](59568546.md). She spent \$2 million on anti-transgender and [anti-woke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke) ads, which made up a significant portion of her campaign advertisements.[^cite_note-vg-3][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

On October 1, a [federal government shutdown](81225965.md) began, which further affected federal workers and became a central issue by the end of the campaign. According to several polls, the Republican Party was largely blamed for the shutdown. Both Spanberger and Earle-Sears opposed the shutdown, while Spanberger tied the shutdown to President Trump's policies on federal workers.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] Political violence became another central issue to the campaign after the [Charlie Kirk assassination](81040139.md), which Republicans hoped would energize low-turnout voters.[^cite_note-ts-69] The conservative *[National Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Review)* released text messages from [Jay Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Jones)—the Democratic nominee for [attorney general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Virginia_Attorney_General_election)—which referred to former Republican speaker [Todd Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Gilbert): "If those guys die before me...I will go to their funerals to piss on their graves." He also stated, "Three people, two bullets. Gilbert, hitler, and pol pot. Gilbert gets two bullets to the head. Spoiler: put Gilbert in the crew with the two worst people you know and he receives both bullets every time." He reportedly fantasized about the death of Gilbert's children.[^cite_note-70]

The text messages were criticized by President Trump, Glenn Youngkin, and other prominent Republicans officials who called for Jones to withdraw from the race. Spanberger condemned the text messages but did not call for him to withdraw from the race. Earle-Sears attempted to tie Jones to Spanberger and spent \$1 million on [attack ads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_ad), which criticized Spanberger's stance on Jones' text messages. Democrats largely defended Spanberger's response to the messages, while the messages themselves received significant media attention.[^cite_note-ts-69][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] At [James Madison University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison_University)'s football game, one fan shouted at Earle-Sears, "Go back to Haiti." The event received condemnation from the university, and the fan was banned from the university.[^cite_note-74]

Former president [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) appeared in two ads for Spanberger's campaign which highlighted abortion rights and economic issues. Obama further criticized President Trump's policies.[^cite_note-75] Other national figures campaigned for Spanberger, including [Pete Buttigieg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Buttigieg), [Andy Beshear](44388893.md), [Ruben Gallego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruben_Gallego), [Josh Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Shapiro), and [Wes Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wes_Moore).[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Obama and Spanberger held a high-profile rally in [Norfolk](57898.md) known as the "Virginia Votes Rally." Obama's speech focused on the economy, democracy, and Trump—most of Spanberger's advertisements continued to tie Earle-Sears to Trump.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] Trump did not campaign with nor officially endorse Earle-Sears; however, he did support her over Spanberger.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

### Debate

Both Spanberger and Earle-Sears initially declined to debate. In August, they agreed to meet on October 9 at [Norfolk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_State_University) for one televised debate.[^cite_note-v712-83]

Topics discussed during the debate included repeal of the car tax, political violence and rhetoric, the federal government shutdown, economic policies on data centers, affordability, transgender policies in schools, reproductive rights, immigration, education, and marijuana legalization.[^cite_note-84] Earle-Sears's debate style was described as "aggressive" as she frequently interrupted Spanberger. Spanberger sometimes "simply stood silent" and did not answer Earle-Sears's questions directed toward her. Earle-Sears made Jay Jones's messages and transgender policies a central part of her messaging.[^cite_note-85]

Spanberger condemned Jones's text messages but did not call for him to withdraw from the race. She declined to state if she would renew her endorsement of Jones. Earle-Sears pressured Spanberger to call for Jones to withdraw from the race.[^cite_note-86] Both candidates attacked each other on political violence and rhetoric. On education, Earle-Sears called for policies that would ban transgender students from [bathrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathroom_bill) and [sports](46676322.md) which do not conform to their birth sex; Spanberger called for local control over transgender policies in schools.[^cite_note-gd-87] Both Earle-Sears and Spanberger called for bipartisan support to end the shutdown.[^cite_note-88]

Earle-Sears, who was not endorsed by Trump, stated, "I will take all endorsements, including the president." According to CNN, Spanberger "went on offense" when she said Earle-Sears previously opposed same-sex marriage; Earle-Sears responded by saying, "That's not discrimination."[^cite_note-89]

#### Analysis

Political analyst David Richards said Spanberger won the debate: "Earle-Sears’ attempts to drag Spanberger into the Jay Jones scandal were unsuccessful. Overall, I’d give Spanberger a small edge because she seemed calmer and more professional." On their separate performances, Richards stated, "Spanberger held her own and came off as much more polished and capable, if not a little cold. Earle-Sears often came across as slightly unhinged."[^cite_note-gd-87]

When Earle-Sears was asked in an October interview about her debate response where she said, "That's not discrimination", Earle-Sears replied, "I was thinking that I was saying, 'I'm not discriminating.'"[^cite_note-90]

## Aftermath and analysis

Spanberger set a record for most votes received by a gubernatorial candidate in Virginia's history. Spanberger's sizable victory was attributed to concerns over affordability, Earle-Sears' tendency to change staff and failure to find a convincing message, Spanberger's fundraising advantage, a significant decline in turnout in heavily Republican Southwest Virginia, and outrage in Northern Virginia over the mass layoffs of federal workers as part of President Donald Trump's efforts to downsize the [federal government](195149.md).[^cite_note-244] Additionally, many furloughed federal workers were energized by the ongoing federal government shutdown. Discontentment with the [U.S. economy](32022.md) under Trump, which many Virginians blamed on [his tariff policies](78448911.md), also played a factor in motivating Spanberger voters.[^cite_note-245] The layoffs boosted Spanberger in [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia) which contributed to her large victory margin.[^cite_note-246]

Northern Virginia backed 88% of her statewide margin, where every locality in the region saw her margin increase significantly from [McAuliffe's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_McAuliffe) performance in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Virginia_gubernatorial_election). She could've won statewide without Northern Virginia by three points.[^cite_note-Northern_Virginia-7]

Spanberger did well with college-educated voters, winning voters with college degrees by 27 points (63%-36%). Many college campuses shifted leftward by 18 points on average due to young voters. [Virginia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Tech) shifted 34 points to the left, which was the largest shift of all the college campuses. Spanberger’s strongest independent city was [Charlottesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia), where the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia) is — her [alma mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_mater). The evangelical [Liberty University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_University) in [Lynchburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynchburg,_Virginia) shifted to the left by six points.[^cite_note-247] College-educated voters made up more of the electorate compared to [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Virginia_gubernatorial_election). Spanberger narrowly won non-college-educated voters (50%-49%) despite Youngkin's 19-point margin (59%-40%) with non-college-educated voters from 2021.[^cite_note-248]

This is the first time since [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Virginia_gubernatorial_election) that a Democrat carried [Spotsylvania County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotsylvania_County,_Virginia), a notable Republican stronghold, as well as [Waynesboro City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynesboro,_Virginia). [Stafford County](91245.md) also flipped for the first time since 1985, though Harris won Stafford in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Virginia). [Nelson County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_County,_Virginia) and [Caroline County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_County,_Virginia) also voted Democratic for the first time since [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Virginia_gubernatorial_election). [James City County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_City_County,_Virginia) voted Democratic for the first time since [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Virginia_gubernatorial_election), although it voted Democratic in 2020 and 2024.[^cite_note-249] Spanberger's closest victory was in [York County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_County,_Virginia), which voted Democratic for the first time since [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Virginia_gubernatorial_election).[^cite_note-250] Spanberger improved with Latino voters from [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Virginia).[^cite_note-251]
