# Government procurement

&#x20;**Government procurement** or **public procurement** is the purchase of goods, works ([construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction)), or services by the state, such as by a [government agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_agency) or a [state-owned enterprise](204768.md).[^cite_note-\:32-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] In 2019, public procurement accounted for approximately 12% of [GDP](12594.md) in [OECD](33853117.md) countries.[^cite_note-size-4][^cite_note-5] In 2021, the [World Bank Group](36752.md) estimated that public procurement made up about 15% of global GDP.[^cite_note-6] Therefore, government procurement accounts for a substantial part of the global economy.

Public procurement is based on the idea that governments should direct their society while giving the private sector the freedom to decide the best practices to produce the desired goods and services.[^cite_note-peters2-7] One benefit of public procurement is its ability to cultivate [innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovation) and [economic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth).[^cite_note-OECD2019-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The public sector picks the most capable nonprofit or for-profit organizations available to issue the desired good or service to the taxpayers. This produces competition within the private sector to gain these contracts that then reward the organizations that can supply more cost-effective and quality goods and services. Some contracts also have specific clauses to promote working with minority-led, women-owned businesses and/or [state-owned enterprises](204768.md).[^cite_note-11]

Competition is a key component of public procurement which affects the outcomes of the whole process.[^cite_note-12] There is a great amount of competition over public procurements because of the massive amount of money that flows through these systems; It is estimated that approximately eleven trillion USD is spent on public procurement worldwide every year.[^cite_note-13] To prevent [fraud](58790.md), [waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste), [corruption](66220.md), or local [protectionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectionism), the laws of most countries regulate government procurement to some extent. Laws usually require the procuring authority to issue public [tenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_for_bids) if the value of the procurement exceeds a certain threshold. Government procurement is also the subject of the [Agreement on Government Procurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_on_Government_Procurement) (GPA), a [plurilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurilateral) international treaty under the auspices of the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization).

## History

Public procurement occurred in Europe in various forms since at least the 18th century, even if it lacked regulations and strict procedures. Nonetheless, the need to regulate public procurement procedures and to constrain the powers of heads of administrative departments began to be deemed necessary as early as the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) (1789).[^cite_note-14] Noteworthy in Italy is the first regulation on public procurements, dating back to the time of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Napoleonic\)) of [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte) and dated May 1, 1807. This regulation contains many terms and principles typical of modern codes on public procurement.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Overview

### Need for government procurement

Government procurement is necessary because governments cannot produce all the inputs for the goods they provide themselves. Governments usually provide [public goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_good_\(economics\)), e.g. national defense or public infrastructure. Public goods are [non-rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivalry_\(economics\)) and non-excludable, which means that one individual's consumption does not diminish the quantity or quality of the commodity available to others, and individuals cannot be prevented from freely consuming the commodity, or ["free-riding"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-rider_problem). Consequently, private markets cannot provide public goods. Instead the government provides those goods and finances them by raising taxes from all citizens.[^cite_note-18]

In addition to public goods, governments often also provide [merit goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merit_good), such as education or health care. Merit goods are [private goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_good) which are rival and excludable and are therefore provided by private markets. Nevertheless, governments also provide merit goods because of reasons of [equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_\(economics\)) and fairness and because they have [positive externalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externality) for society as a whole.[^cite_note-19] In order to provide public and merit goods, the government has to buy input factors from private companies, e.g. police cars, school buildings, uniforms etc. This process is called government or public procurement.

### Scope and impact

Government procurement practice impacts on all [public works](323158.md), services and supply contracts entered into by a public authority, and the markets from which these are purchased. Public procurement regulations normally cover all works, services and supply contracts but there may be exceptions. These most notably cover [military acquisitions](22024319.md), which account for large parts of government expenditure, some aspects of health care,[^cite_note-Lord_Young_reforms-20] and low value procurement.[^cite_note-21]

&#x20;The GPA and EU procurement law allow for exceptions where public tendering would violate a country's essential security interests. Additionally, certain politically or economically sensitive sectors, such as public health, energy supply or public transport, may also be treated differently.[^cite_note-Prieß:_Global_Overview-22] Government procurement is linked to economic growth, protection and enhancement of [competitive market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competitive_market) conditions, policy achievement, and innovation promotion.[^cite_note-Pliatsidis-23] The United Kingdom's [Office of Fair Trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Fair_Trading), as it then was, commissioned a review addressing the impact of public sector procurement on competition, which reported in 2004. The review found that

> Competition effects from procurement can be both positive and negative. The public sector, by virtue of its overall demand in certain markets, may be in a position to protect and promote competition, for example by maintaining a competitive market structure through deliberately sourcing its requirements from a range of suppliers, by providing incentives to suppliers to invest and innovate, or by helping firms to overcome barriers to entry. It may, however, also restrict and distort competition, e.g. by adopting procurement practices that have the effect of restricting participation in public tenders and that might even discriminate against particular types of firms. Last but not least, the public sector may fail to contribute towards an improvement of competitive conditions where it could in principle do so.[^cite_note-24]

Contract types used in government procurement include [fixed-price contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-price_contract), [cost-plus contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost-plus_contract), [time-and-materials contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_and_materials) and [indefinite-quantity contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDIQ).[^cite_note-v996-25]

### Economics of public procurement

The *economics of public procurement* is an applied subfield of [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) that studies how procurement rules and the decisions of public buyers and suppliers determine what is bought and on what terms, whether it is delivered and how well, and how these decisions propagate from individual contracts to firms, industries and the wider economy.[^cite_note-DecarolisSrhoj2026-26] It draws on [auction theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction_theory), [contract theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_theory), [industrial organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_organization) and [political economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_economy), and takes the individual contract over its life cycle, rather than aggregate government spending, as its unit of analysis.[^cite_note-BosioEtAl2022-27]

The subfield analyses the behaviour of three actors whose choices interact across the procurement process.[^cite_note-BestEtAl2023-28]

1. Centralized procurement designer: a central authority that sets the rules of the system, such as the procurement law, the admissible procedures and the monetary thresholds that trigger them;

2. Decentralized public buyers: the contracting authorities, such as ministries, municipalities or public hospitals, that turn a need into a tender, run the competition and manage the resulting contract; and

3. Firms: the suppliers that decide whether to bid, how to bid, and how to perform once a contract is awarded.

These actors meet across five stages that together form a procurement cycle.[^cite_note-BosioEtAl2022-27]

1. Demand planning: deciding whether and what to buy;

2. Tender preparation: writing the specifications, dividing the work into lots and setting the award criteria;

3. Bidding and allocation: firms compete and a winner is selected;

4. Contract execution: the work is delivered, monitored and, where needed, renegotiated; and

5. Economic consequences: the effects that outlast delivery, on firms, markets and public value.

The stages are interrelated rather than self-contained, so a choice made at one commonly reshapes outcomes at the others: how a tender is written, for instance, shapes who competes for it and how well the contract is ultimately performed.

A central organising concept is the *public procurement wedge*, which arises across these stages. It is the gap between the value an efficient procurement process could deliver and the value procurement actually realises; rather than being measured directly, it surfaces as poorly chosen projects, weak competition, inflated prices, cost overruns, delays, poor quality and forgone firm growth, and can accumulate from one stage to the next.[^cite_note-DecarolisSrhoj2026-26]

The economics of public procurement is an active area of economic research.[^cite_note-CarrilEtAl2026-29][^cite_note-BosioEtAl2022-27][^cite_note-DecarolisSrhoj2026-26]

## Strategic purchasing

One of the consequences of the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) was an attempt to reduce public spending in order to control [public debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_debt). This trend affected government procurement for its significant share in public spending.[^cite_note-WagtBasYagafarovaVinczeStrandOrderud2014-30] Therefore, various purchasing strategies were implemented to increase quality and to decrease cost of government procurement. These strategies include [public e-procurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_eProcurement), centralized purchasing or [framework agreements](3680924.md).[^cite_note-WagtBasYagafarovaVinczeStrandOrderud2014-30] A United States federal memorandum issued in 2005 created an expectation that the procurement, finance and [IT](39206.md#Information_technology) functions within [federal government departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_executive_departments) would [work together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration) to generate savings through a process of [strategic sourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_sourcing).[^cite_note-31]

### Public e-procurement

Main article: [Public eProcurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_eProcurement)

[Public e-procurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_eProcurement) stands for replacing various phases of public procurement with electronic means. Purpose of using e-tools is reducing administrative costs by automation. E-procurement can also mitigate some [barriers to entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barriers_to_entry) for smaller suppliers, consequent increase of competition can reduce price of procurement.[^cite_note-32]

### Public procurement and innovation

The large buying power of the [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector) has led to the consideration of using public procurement as a stimulus to foster [innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovation). The activities of public procurement and innovation intersect in three specific areas: public procurement for innovation, public procurement of innovation, and innovative public procurement.[^cite_note-33] First, multiple studies have established that public procurement for innovation is a viable and efficient tool to stimulate innovation as a demand - side tool in the innovation policy mix.[^cite_note-34] Second, public procurement may also be used to innovate the public sector itself (public procurement of innovation), through the inclusion of "innovativeness" as a procurement goal (often as a secondary criterion).[^cite_note-35] Third, novel procurement approaches (such as [eProcurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-procurement) or [Public-Private Partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public%E2%80%93private_partnership)) may be introduced to innovate public procurement processes and entities.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### Centralized purchasing in public procurement

Centralized purchasing means awarding procurement contracts on behalf of one or more procuring entities. This method has been used to gain various benefits emerging from demand aggregation. Centralized procurement can be done by ordinary contracting authorities or established central purchasing body. Centralized procurement is regulated by local legislation. For instance, directives 2004/17/EC and 2004/18/EC are dealing with this issue in the EU. Commonly mentioned benefits of procurement centralization are as follows:

- Final unit price decrease – Higher procurement value coming from demand aggregation can increase buyers bargaining power and decrease final price. Moreover, higher value can attract more companies to bid in the tender, increased competition might lead to better price as well.

  [^cite_note-LigiKmeclLapimaa2019-38]

- Transaction costs reduction – Key objective of centralized procurement is preventing duplication of some procedures. Contracting units can reduce their transaction costs in cooperating with other entities. This aspect is often considered as most relevant argument for procurement centralization.

  [^cite_note-SkuhrovecPalansky2016-39]

- Knowledge sharing – Cooperation in purchasing can also result in sharing best procurement practices. Some central procurement bodies also perform research activities.

  [^cite_note-LigiKmeclLapimaa2019-38]

Other centralization aspects are often criticized. Discussed drawbacks are often connected to the decentralization theorem stated by American economist [Wallace E. Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_E._Oates) in 1972. The theorem claims that a decentralized system is more efficient, because of the [information asymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_asymmetry) between local and central government.[^cite_note-Pocarovska2018-40] Procurement centralization might also negatively impact supply side. Higher procured values might require higher capacity of supplying company and it might create [barrier to entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_to_entry) for small or medium companies. Consequently, it might lead to monopolizing public procurement market. Critics also mention that only some goods can be purchased centrally. Goods that are heterogeneous or they have many characteristics are not suitable for this strategy.[^cite_note-SkuhrovecPalansky2016-39]

### Thresholds

Under many jurisdictions, there are certain thresholds in value which oblige procurers to publish tender details and information on contracts awarded and expenditure incurred, and to follow specific procurement procedures. Greater transparency and regulatory compliance are incurred at higher levels of expenditure.[^cite_note-SkuhrovecPalansky2016-39] For example, the GPA applies to the letting of "any procurement contract with a value that reaches or exceeds the amounts ('thresholds') set in the Agreement".[^cite_note-41]

### Framework agreements

Main article: [Framework agreement](3680924.md)

A [framework agreement](3680924.md) is another method for aggregation of demand. It is a type of two-stage bid tendering procedure, that establishes incomplete contracts awards with one or more suppliers for given period of time.[^cite_note-Pocarovska2018-40] The discussed advantage is an administrative costs reduction again as tender procedure do not have to be duplicated for agreed period of time.[^cite_note-WagtBasYagafarovaVinczeStrandOrderud2014-30] On the other hand, the term "Winner's curse" is associated with framework agreement as there is a price uncertainty in time.[^cite_note-Pocarovska2018-40] All of these three procurement strategies are not mutually exclusive. So, framework agreements can be processed centrally through e-procurement.[^cite_note-Pocarovska2018-40]

## Challenges

### Corruption

A concern with public procurement is [corruption](3608404.md); companies have much to gain from bribing public officials to obtain these procurements. In societies where corruption is endemic and enforcement is low, public officials are incentivized to accept bribes due to either necessity or greed.[^cite_note-47] Academic research shows that discretion in procurement decisions is beneficial in countries with a high level of human capital, but is detrimental in low-human capital jurisdictions.[^cite_note-48]

Government procurement involves a high risk of corruption, collusion and unwanted favoritism.[^cite_note-Pliatsidis-23] An important role in this has the great size of financial turnover and the complexity of many procurement processes in which businesses interact very closely with politicians and civil servants. Often the personal interests of the public officials are not the same as the interests of the public. The vulnerability of public buyers to private subversion has led every country to restrict the discretion of procuring entities in what they buy and pay. But while the regulation of the private sector empowers public officials and enables them to extract bribes in exchange for regulatory relief, the regulation of government constrains public officials. Public procurement regulations reduce the discretion of buyers, typically with the intent of reducing corruption.[^cite_note-49]

### Fraud

Procurement [fraud](58790.md) can be defined as dishonestly obtaining an advantage, avoiding an obligation or causing a loss to public property or various means during procurement process by public servants, contractors or any other person involved in the procurement.[^cite_note-50] An example is a [kickback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickback_\(bribery\)), whereby a dishonest agent of the supplier pays a dishonest agent of the purchaser to select the supplier's bid, often at an inflated price. Other frauds in procurement include:

- Collusion among bidders to reduce competition.

- Providing bidders with advance "inside" information.

- Submission of false or inflated invoices for services and products that are not delivered or work that is never done. "Shadow vendors", shell companies that are set up and used for billing, may be used in such schemes.

- Intentional substitution of substandard materials without the customer's agreement.

- Use of "sole source" contracts without proper justification.

- Use of prequalification standards in specifications to unnecessarily exclude otherwise qualified contractors.

- Dividing requirements to qualify for small-purchase procedures to avoid scrutiny for contract review procedures of larger purchases.

[Integrity Pacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrity_pact) are one tool to prevent fraud and other irregular practices in procurement projects. The [G20](2206608.md) has recommended their use in their 2019 Compendium of Good Practices for Promoting Integrity and Transparency in Infrastructure Development.[^cite_note-51] A major [European Commission](9974.md) pilot project entitled *Integrity Pacts - Civil Control Mechanism for Safeguarding EU Funds* is seeking to evaluate the effectiveness of Integrity Pacts in reducing corruption in 17 EU-funded projects in 11 Member States with a total value of over EUR 920 million.[^cite_note-52] The [OECD](33853117.md) has published guidelines on how to detect and combat bid rigging.[^cite_note-53]

### Others

One issue of public procurement is the inability of governments to measure economic productivity, because as the size of public procurement systems substantially grows, so do their complexity and influence.[^cite_note-OECD2019-8][^cite_note-54] Public procurement is heavily embedded in all forms of public sector goods and services, from health care to road maintenance,[^cite_note-OECD2019-8] thus making it difficult for the government to monitor the impacts, positive or negative.[^cite_note-size-4] Monitoring public spending and its impact is important to reform public procurement, especially when pending economic instability calls for proactive responses.[^cite_note-OECD2019-8] In some cases, if a nation is extremely impoverished, it may not have the necessary funds or a large enough private sector to even procure companies to issue the goods or services to the people.[^cite_note-peters-55] Thus, omitting public procurement as a potential governing practice. [Cost-plus contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost-plus_contract) can incentivize cost overruns.[^cite_note-v996-25]

## Regulation by jurisdiction

### Albania

[Albania](738.md)'s Public Procurement Agency (*Agjencia e Prokurimit Publik*) is a central body with legal and public personality reporting to the [Prime Minister](259136.md), and financed by the State Budget.[^cite_note-56] Its activity is based on:&#x20;

- Law No. 9643/2006 "On public procurement", as amended

- Law No. 125/2013 "On concessions and public private partnership", as amended, which repealed the previous law "On concessions" (Law No. 9663, dated 18 December 2006),

  [^cite_note-57]

  &#x20;and

- Law No. 9874/2008 "On public [auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction)", as amended.

The main duties and competencies of the Public Procurement Agency are:

- preparation of project-proposals for public procurement regulations, public auctions and those in the field of concessions/public private partnerships, preparation of Standard Tender Documents and issuing the necessary instructions in order to assist the contracting authorities undertaking these procedures;

- verification of the implementation of public procurement, concessions and public auction procedures after the phase of contract signature and in case of infringements of the legal and sublegal provisions, penalizes with fines or proposes administrative measures;

- monitoring the progress of the public procurement system, and the implementation of measures and activities in order to achieve and maintain a completely transparent and efficient system of concessions/public private partnerships;

- preparation and publication of the Public Notices Bulletin;

- exclusion of economic operators from participation in public procurement, concessions or public auctions for a period of 1 to 3 years;

- promotion and organisation of training for central and local government officials involved in public procurement activities.

  [^cite_note-58]

The Public Procurement Commission (PPC in English, KPP in Albanian) is a quasi-judicial state body with responsibility for providing legal protection in relation to public procurement.[^cite_note-59] The US [Department of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Commerce) reports that businesses "occasionally complain about problems in the technical and financial criteria of contracts, resulting in biased and distorted competition" and that "improper implementation of \[Albania's] public procurement procedures" has also been noted as a problem.[^cite_note-60] Albania has [observer status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observer_status) with regard to the [Government Procurement Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Procurement_Agreement) and is negotiating accession.[^cite_note-parties-61]

### Algeria

### American Samoa

The Office of Procurement, based in [Tafuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafuna), is the central authority on procurement for the [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa) [Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_American_Samoa) (ASG), being responsible for the procurement of all construction, goods, and services including the management, control, warehousing, and sale of stores/inventory commodities contained in its warehouse.[^cite_note-63]

The Public Procurement Law repealed Law No. 7/96 of 16 February 1996 and Decree No. 40/05 of 8 June 2005. Public expenditure, the provision of services, the leasing and acquisition of goods, and public works contracts regulated through the Public Procurement Law.[^cite_note-64]

### Argentina

### Australia

### Bahrain

The Tender Board of the [Kingdom of Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bahrain), based in [Manama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manama,_Bahrain), regulates procurement and undertakes procurement activity for the kingdom's public bodies.[^cite_note-67] The Board was established in 2003 under the directives of His Majesty [King Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hamad_bin_Isa_Al_Khalifa).[^cite_note-68] Bahrain has [observer status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observer_status) with respect to the [Government Procurement Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Procurement_Agreement).[^cite_note-parties-61]

### Bangladesh

Main article: [Bangladesh Public Procurement Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Public_Procurement_Authority)

The [Cabinet Committee on Public Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Committee_on_Public_Purchase) ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): সরকারী ক্রয় সংক্রান্ত মন্ত্রিসভা কমিটি) is the [Bangladesh government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_government)'s highest decision-making body regarding public procurement.[^cite_note-69] The [Swiss Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_challenge_\(procurement\)) system is utilised in [Bangladesh](3454.md) to procure infrastructure development through [public-private partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public-private_partnerships).[^cite_note-70]

### Belize

[Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize) is a member of [CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM). Government procurement is subject to the Contractor-General Act, No. 6 of 1993, which received the [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Belize)'s assent on 5 January 1994. The Act provided for the establishment of the Office of the Contractor-General.[^cite_note-Rosebelize-71] The Contractor-General is an independent, non-political appointment made by the Governor-General acting on the recommendations of both Houses of the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Belize\)).[^cite_note-72]

### Brazil

Government procurement in [Brazil](3383.md) is regulated by a number of different laws with different procedures depending on the importance and the value of the procurement. The most important law about government procurement which contains basic rules of public procurements and administrative contracts was the Law nº 8.666, 21 June 1993, which contained rules for public tenders and for restricted tenders. This law was succeeded by the law [Lei 14.133/21](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_Ato2019-2022/2021/Lei/L14133.htm), 1st, April, 2021. There are different rules regulating procurement of public services, as Law nº 8.987, 13 February 1995 (Concession and Permission of Public Services); Law nº 12.462, 4 August 2011 (Differentiated Procurement – RDC in Portuguese) and Law nº 10.520, 17 July 2002, which deals with a reverse auction. In the internet field (e-procurement) there are executive orders (Decretos) which regulate public procurement, such as Decree nº 5.450, 31 May 2005 and Decree nº 7.982, 23 January 2013: the latter regulates procedures for specific situations of sharing acquisitions of goods or under portioned delivering.[^cite_note-73]

### Burkina Faso

[Burkina Faso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Burkina_Faso)'s *Autorite de Regulation de la Commande Publique* (ARCOP),[^cite_note-74] established in July 2008, is the regulatory oversight body which ensures fairness in government procurement processes. Its role is to monitor the execution of all government contracts and it may impose sanctions, initiate lawsuits, and [publish the names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_and_shame) of fraudulent or delinquent businesses.[^cite_note-bf1-75] Decree no. 2003-269/PRES/PM/MEF of May 2003 on the general regulations governing public procurement instituted a new public contracts code which extended the scope of public contracting and provided for improvements in transparency in procurement procedures.[^cite_note-bf-76] Appeals from bidders regarding the contract award process are heard by a Standing Committee on the Amicable Settlement of Disputes (CRAL).[^cite_note-bf-76] The [US Department of State](31975.md) has noted that Burkina Faso did not as of 2018 operate a "forced localization" policy and did not impose any "[offset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offset_agreement)" requirements (i.e. stipulations which require that foreign suppliers invest in local production or service facilities in order to be awarded a contract).[^cite_note-bf1-75]

### Canada

See also: [Canadian Commercial Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Commercial_Corporation)

Public procurement in Canada is regulated on various governmental levels (federal, [provincial](75763.md), and municipal). Most federal procurement is organized by the [Public Services and Procurement Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Services_and_Procurement_Canada) (PSPC) agency and is governed by a Code of Conduct of Procurement introduced in 2006,[^cite_note-better-77] in combination with principles laid out in the *[Federal Accountability Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Accountability_Act)* and in the *[Financial Administration Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Administration_Act)*. Public procurement is guided by the principles of fairness, transparency, openness, and non-discrimination and complies with all international agreements that Canada is a member of the WTO [Government Procurement Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_on_Government_Procurement) (GPA), [NAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Free_Trade_Agreement), [CETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Economic_and_Trade_Agreement), and various bilateral FTAs. Foreign suppliers from member nations of these agreements can bid on Canadian government procurements and are treated the same as domestic suppliers.[^cite_note-78] The principal statutory provisions regulating government procurement are:

- *Department of Public Works and Government Services Act* (1996)

- *Financial Administration Act* (1985) and the Government Contracts Regulations

  [^cite_note-79]

- *[Defence Production Act 1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Production_Act_1985?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (consolidated version published 2011)

  [^cite_note-80]

- *[Federal Accountability Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Accountability_Act)*, 2006 (FedAA):

  [^cite_note-81]

  &#x20;this law amended the Department of Public Works and Government Services Act 1996, creating the position of "Procurement Ombudsman" (called the "Procurement Auditor" when the proposal was announced,

  [^cite_note-better-77]

  &#x20;and in the original bill). The Procurement Ombudsman delivers an annual report on their work and findings to the [Minister of Public Works and Government Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Public_Works_and_Government_Services), who then tables the report in both Houses of Parliament.

  [^cite_note-82]

- The Directive on the Management of Procurement, which has been in operation since 13 May 2021.

  [^cite_note-83]

In general, bids must be solicited by the procuring department unless estimated expenditure does not exceed \$25,000, or \$100,000 "where the contract is for the acquisition of architectural, engineering and other services required in respect of the planning, design, preparation or supervision of the construction, repair, renovation or restoration of a work".[^cite_note-84] For contracts above \$25,000, tenders are published on the transparent Government Electronic Tendering Service (GETS). A non-competitive procurement process is only used in certain special circumstances. One such area of exception are security-related procurements. In that case the Defence Production Act applies, which allows using a special process and favouring domestic suppliers in acquiring defence supplies and conducting defence.[^cite_note-85] A political scandal regarding the procurement of developers for the [ArriveCAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArriveCAN) border control software arose in 2024.[^cite_note-86]

### China

During the period from 1949 to 1978, [Chinese](5405.md) public bodies acquired the goods and services they required in accordance with administratively directed transactions, whereas since the [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up) of 1978, "central planning has started to give way to market forces". Researchers Ping Wang and Xinglin Zhang suggest that for comparative law purposes, it only makes sense to speak of "government procurement" or "public procurement" after the implementation of the 1978 reforms.[^cite_note-87]

The *Government Procurement Law of the People's Republic of China*, adopted at the 28th Meeting of the Standing Committee of the [Ninth National People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_National_People's_Congress) on 29 June 2002, is the primary legislation in China.[^cite_note-cn-88] The law allows for "[buy local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_local)" clauses.[^cite_note-eesc-89]<sup>: 5 </sup> The *Implementing Regulations of the Government Procurement Law*, which supplement and clarify the Law, came into effect on 1 March 2015. Procedures and procurement methodologies are covered by these regulations, along with arrangements for handling queries and complaints, and supervision and inspection, and the *Implementing Regulations* also pay greater attention to transparency and public participation than did previous arrangements.[^cite_note-90] Regulations on military procurement are formulated separately by the [Central Military Commission](214345.md) (Article 86).[^cite_note-cn-88]

The finance departments within each [section of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministries_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) are responsible for the supervision of departmental public procurement (Article 13).[^cite_note-cn-88] Government purchasing of drugs for medical use is centralised and uses a strategy known as "Volume-based Procurement" (VBP).[^cite_note-91] On 26 December 2023, China prohibited the use of computer [CPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_processing_unit) made by US companies [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) and [AMD](2400.md) within government PCs and servers.[^cite_note-92] The government instead approved 18 processors made by Chinese companies [Loongson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loongson) and [Phytium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FeiTeng). China has observer status with regard to the Government Procurement Agreement and is negotiating accession:[^cite_note-parties-61] the country's accession application was submitted on 14 January 2008.[^cite_note-93]

### European Union

Main article: [Government procurement in the European Union](7278427.md)

Government procurement in the European Union accounts for more than EUR 2.2 trillion, or 14% of the EU GDP.[^cite_note-100] It has been regulated and harmonized by [community law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_law) since the 1970s in order to guarantee transparency and non-discrimination of EU companies in government procurement in all member states. EU laws apply only to tenders that exceed certain thresholds in value. These thresholds vary depending on the area the contract is for and if the procurement is done by a central government or by other public authorities (e.g. municipal government). National laws are applied for tenders below these threshold values.[^cite_note-Rules_&_procedures-101] Relevant EU Directives regarding government procurement currently in force are [Directive 2009/81](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?qid=1415011920358\&uri=CELEX\:32009L0081), [Directive 2014/24](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX\:32014L0024), and [Directive 2014/25](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX\:32014L0025). Title VII of the EU's *Financial Regulation* adopted in 2018 governs the procedures to be followed in acquiring goods and services for the EU's own needs.[^cite_note-102] There are five different procedures for public procurement:[^cite_note-Rules_&_procedures-101]

- Open procedure: any company is allowed to submit a tender

- Restricted procedure: only companies that have been preselected are allowed to submit a tender

- Negotiated procedure: there are direct negotiations with at least three companies

- Competitive dialogue: if it is not possible to define technical specifications at the beginning, a competitive dialogue with at least three companies is started after which tenders can be submitted. This procedure is applied for complex procurements.

- Electronic auctions: companies that pass a pre-evaluation process can bid in electronic auctions for public contracts.

The EU [Directive 2014/24](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX\:32014L0024) foresees two award criteria, namely the lowest price criterion or economically most advantageous offer criterion. In terms of contractual forms, the relationship between contracting authority and economic operator can be regulated by a public supply contract, framework agreement or dynamic purchasing system.[^cite_note-103] The European Commission is working on further improving efficiency and transparency in government procurement in the EU with its current public procurement strategy.[^cite_note-104]

#### Austria

Government procurement in [Austria](26964606.md) is regulated by the Federal Procurement Act 2017.[^cite_note-105] The Austrian Federal Procurement Agency (*[Bundesbeschaffungsagentur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesbeschaffungsagentur?action=edit\&redlink=1)* or BBG) centralises purchasing activity for Austrian federal agencies.[^cite_note-106] The BBG operates five centres of excellence or "competence centres", covering logistics and supply chain management, [total cost of ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_cost_of_ownership), quality assurance, sustainable procurement, and costing.[^cite_note-107]

For compliance, the Austrian Federal Procurement Office (*Bundesvergabeamt*) is a permanent body authorised to hear procurement compliance cases where the [federal government of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_Austria) is the contracting authority. The Federal Procurement Office is an example of decision-making body with both [first instance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_instance) and last instance ([final court of appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_court_of_appeal)) powers.[^cite_note-kokott-108]<sup>: Para 30 </sup> The Federal Procurement Office has the power to declare that a violation of procurement law has taken place but it cannot award [damages](8134.md): this role lies with the [Austrian civil courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Austria).[^cite_note-kokott-108]<sup>: Para 36 </sup>

After the Austrian State Printing House (*[Österreichische Staatsdruckerei GmbH](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96sterreichische%20Staatsdruckerei)*) was [privatised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_privatizations_by_country#Austria), Austria maintained a legal obligation for federal authorities to award contracts for [security printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_printing) for documents such as [passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport) and [driving licences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_licence) to the State Printing House without a competitive procurement process. The European Commission challenged this practice as not compliant with the public procurement directives and issued a [reasoned opinion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoned_opinion) to Austria in 2014. As of December 2015 the Austrian government had taken no action to address this matter and the Commission referred Austria to the [European Court of Justice](87187.md).[^cite_note-109]

#### Belgium

[Belgian](3343.md) legislation on public procurement is set out in the Act of 17 June 2016 implementing the 2014 EU procurement directives.[^cite_note-110] The Laws of 17 June 2016 on public procurement and on concession contracts, and the Law of 16 February 2017 on remedies, failed to meet the EU's transposition deadline (18 April 2016). [Royal Decrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_order_\(Belgium\)) issued on 18 April 2017 for general public procurement, 18 June 2017 for procurement in the water, energy, transport and postal services sectors, 22 June 2017 containing new rules on the performance of public works contracts and concession contracts for public works and 25 June 2017, for the award and performance of concession contracts, have augmented the earlier laws.[^cite_note-vbb-111]

Article 51 of the Royal Decree of 18 April 2017 includes a "revolving door mechanism", which targets the situation where a person previously working for a contracting authority is now being employed by an economic operator involved in a public procurement procedure established by that contracting authority. In this type of situation the person would be presumed to have a [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest) for a two-year period following the termination of his/her employment with the contracting authority.[^cite_note-vbb-111]

#### Bulgaria

Government procurement in Bulgaria is a critical area in corruption risk. Public procurement contracts have been awarded to a handful of companies amid widespread irregularities, procedure violations and tailor-made selection or award criteria.[^cite_note-112] The Bulgarian public procurement portal reported in September 2016 that since the beginning of 2016, "a total of 15,105 contracts were signed on the basis of public procurement orders".[^cite_note-113]

At the beginning of 2015, the [Bulgarian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_government) announced a 130-kilometer extension to the barbed wire [border fence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_border_barrier) along its border with [Turkey](11125639.md) in order to completely secure the land border. Prime Minister [Boyko Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyko_Borisov) described the extension as "absolutely necessary" in order to prevent persons from [illegally](7124412.md) entering the European Union member state.[^cite_note-114] The [Bulgarian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Parliament) authorised amendments to procurement legislation to allow continued construction of the fence without launching a public procurement procedure "because of the need to safeguard national security".[^cite_note-115]

#### Croatia

The first public procurement law in [Croatia](5573.md) based on the EU Procurement Directives was enacted in 2001, but a revised legal structure for public procurement was put in place with the Public Procurement Act of 2012,[^cite_note-HR-116] and this was superseded by the Public Procurement Act of 2016, effective 1 January 2017.[^cite_note-117] Public-private partnerships (PPPs) are governed by the Act on Public-Private Partnerships.[^cite_note-118] Two key Croatian institutions are the Public Procurement Office and the Public Procurement Supervisory Commission, established in 2001,[^cite_note-119] now (since 2013) the State Commission for Supervision of Public Procurement (DKOM).[^cite_note-HR-116]

The State Commission is an independent quasi-judicial body with nine members appointed by the [Croatian Parliament](398696.md) for a five-year term and accountable to the Croatian Parliament for its work. The High Administrative Court of the Republic of Croatia has jurisdiction over the State Commission in relation to disputes concerning procedure, but there is no right of appeal against Commission decisions.[^cite_note-120] Under [Croatian law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_law), procurement procedures must be carried out by authorised representatives of the contracting authority, of whom at least one must hold a valid procurement certificate.

#### Estonia

The [Estonian](28222445.md) [Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(Estonia\)) is responsible for public procurement policy, drafting the law, providing supervision and consultancy, and maintaining a central Public Procurement Register.[^cite_note-124] The current legislation is the Public Procurement Act of 2017, which came into effect on 1 September 2017,[^cite_note-125] and which operates in conjunction with the Public Information Act of 2000, which regulates the publication of "information concerning public procurements which are being organised or have been organised by the state or local governments".[^cite_note-126] Disputes are handled by the Public Procurements Appeal Committee.

#### Finland

In [Finland](10577.md) the following legislation applies to government procurement:

- Act on Public Contracts and Concession Contracts (Act no. 1397 of 2016, effective 1 January 2017)

  [^cite_note-129]

- Act on Public Contracts by Contracting Authorities in the Water, Energy, Transport and Postal Services Sectors (Act no. 1398 of 2016, also known as the 'Act on public contracts in special sectors')

- Act on Public Contracts in the Fields of Defence and Security.

  [^cite_note-130]

  [^cite_note-Finland-131]

A Government Decree on Public Contracts was also in force until 2017.[^cite_note-Finland-131] The [Ministry of Employment and the Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economic_Affairs_and_Employment_\(Finland\)) is responsible for the preparation of legislation concerning public procurement. The Finnish Competition and Consumer Authority (FCCA) oversees public procurement: section 139 of the Act on Public Procurement and Concession Contracts mandates the FCCA to supervise compliance with public contracts legislation and to provide 'administrative guidance' or if necessary to issue a caution to a non-compliant public authority.[^cite_note-132] The Market Court operates as a specialist court handling public procurement cases. #### France

In France, the Department of Legal Affairs (DAJ) of the [Ministry for the Economy and Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Ministry_for_the_Economy_and_Finance) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances*) is responsible for establishing regulations regarding public procurement (*la commande publique*). All currently relevant EU directives have been implemented into national law.[^cite_note-136] The *Union des Groupements d'Achat Public* (UGAP), based in [Champs-sur-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champs-sur-Marne) east of Paris,[^cite_note-137] operates as the only general public procurement agency in France.[^cite_note-138] Article III of [Decree](322949.md#France) 2016-247 of 3 March 2016 provides that the *Direction des Achats de l’Etat* (DAE: State Purchasing Directorate) has "exclusive competence in matters regarding the strategy for professionalisation of public procurement".[^cite_note-139]

#### Germany

In Germany, the [Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy](69466069.md) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Energie*, abbreviated BMWi) is responsible for defining laws and principles regarding public procurement. In 2016 Germany transposed the new EU Directives of 2014 into domestic law. Thereby, processes and contracts in public procurement have become easier and more flexible. The Act against Restraints of Competition – Part IV (German: *[Gesetz gegen Wettbewerbsbeschränkungen](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesetz%20gegen%20Wettbewerbsbeschr%C3%A4nkungen)*, abbreviated GWB) and the Ordinance on the Award of Public Contracts (German: [V*erordnung über die Vergabe öffentlicher Aufträge*](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vergabeverordnung), abbreviated VgV) regulate procurement above EU thresholds. Detailed procedures are specified in further regulations, e.g. the Procurement Regulation for Public Works (German abbreviation: [VOB](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vergabe-%20und%20Vertragsordnung%20f%C3%BCr%20Bauleistungen)), the Procurement Regulation for Public Supplies and Services ([VOL](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vergabe-%20und%20Vertragsordnung%20f%C3%BCr%20Leistungen)), and the Procurement Regulation for Professional Services ([VOF](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vergabeordnung%20f%C3%BCr%20freiberufliche%20Leistungen)).[^cite_note-140] For many contracts electronic procurement is made possible via an [online platform](https://www.evergabe-online.de/start.html?0).

For public procurement below the EU thresholds there are different regulations. At the federal level national budgetary law applies while the 16 federal German states and some municipalities have their own public procurement laws and regulations. This decentralized system reflects the political decentralization in Germany; however, sub-national level procurement regulations often take national regulations as examples and also ensure competition, non-discrimination, and transparency.[^cite_note-141] Germany's federal decree on contracts for workshops for the disabled (10 May 2005) requires German federal contracting authorities to set aside part of their budgets for contracts which can be awarded to [workshops for workers with disabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheltered_workshop).[^cite_note-bsocial-142]<sup>: 27 </sup>

#### Greece

Law 4412/2016 on public procurement and Law 4413/2016 on concessions are the main instruments of [Greek](12108.md) legislation governing public procurement. These two laws of 2016, along with earlier reforms introduced under Law 4281/2014 on public procurement law, have radically simplified the previously complex legal regime, repealing numerous previous laws.[^cite_note-143] The European Commission's profile for [Greece](12108.md) in its study of administrative capacity in the EU had described the public procurement system in the country as "singularly complex, ... being dispersed among as many as 400 laws, regulations, and presidential decrees".[^cite_note-profile-144] Public contract notices are published in the Central Electronic Registry for Public Procurement (KIMDIS).[^cite_note-145]

The Public Procurement Monitoring Unit (PPMU), established in 1997, part of the Centre of International and European Economic Law in [Thessaloniki](40471.md), provides Greek contracting authorities with "specialised and prompt legal advisory support on awarding public works and technical services contracts falling within the scope of EU Law on Public Procurement".[^cite_note-profile-144][^cite_note-146] The procurement impact of Greek laws on [construction company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_company) registration was [challenged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infringement_procedure) by the European Commission in 2014 because the legislation divided companies into bandings with a maximum and minimum budget range. This process meant that companies were not able to bid for work outside their financial banding, even though (for projects of a lower value) they had the economic and financial standing necessary to take on the work.[^cite_note-147]

#### Hungary

The [Hungarian](13275.md) Public Procurement Authority was established by Act XL of 1995[^cite_note-148] and the current Public Procurement Act (Act CXLIII of 2015) entered into force on 1 November 2015, implementing the 2014 EU procurement directives.[^cite_note-cxliii-149] The objectives of the 2015 legislation are:

- to secure transparency and public control of the effective use of public funds;

- to establish conditions of fair competition in public procurement;

- to enhance the access of local small and medium-sized enterprises to procurement procedures; and

- to promote environmental protection and the social considerations of the State.

  [^cite_note-cxliii-149]

Concession award procedures are also covered within the same legislation, and the fundamental principles set out in Act V of 2013 on the [Civil Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Code), the "ultimate instrument relating to the operation of civil persons and economic organizations", also apply to public procurement.[^cite_note-150] [Hungarian law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_law) requires public sector contracting authorities to pay suppliers within 30 days of delivery or service provision, and also allows suppliers to collect payments due to them directly from the contracting authority's [bank account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_account), where it is verified that the company has performed its contract obligations but the contracting authority has not paid within the deadline.[^cite_note-151]

#### Ireland

Government procurement in [Ireland](14560.md) is governed by the European Communities (Award of Public Authorities' Contracts) Regulations 2006[^cite_note-152] and the European Communities (Public Authorities' Contracts) (Review Procedures) Regulations 2010.[^cite_note-153] As of 2025, the [Minister for Public Expenditure, Infrastructure, Public Service Reform and Digitalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Public_Expenditure,_Infrastructure,_Public_Service_Reform_and_Digitalisation) with special responsibility for public procurement is [Emer Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emer_Higgins).

#### Italy

Public procurement in [Italy](14532.md) is primarily regulated by the Public Contracts Code (*[Codice dei contratti pubblici](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codice%20dei%20contratti%20pubblici)*), established under the legislative decree of 12 April 2006,[^cite_note-154] which is administered by the [Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Infrastructure_and_Transport_\(Italy\)) ([Italian](14708.md): Ministero delle infrastrutture e dei trasporti). The code was reformed in 2016 to implement the new EU directives of 2014 into domestic Italian law.[^cite_note-155] In addition to the code, guidelines from the [National Anti-Corruption Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Anti-Corruption_Authority_\(Italy\)) (Italian: *Autorità Nazionale AntiCorruzione*, abbreviated ANAC) and decrees from various ministries also apply to public procurement. Most public procurement on a national level is administered by the state-owned company [Consip S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consip) and larger [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) have their own agencies for public purchasing. From 1994, article 10(1bis) of Law No 109 on public works stated that multiple undertakings where there was a relationship of control (such as between a company and a subsidiary) could not take part in the same tendering procedure. However, because the "prohibition on simultaneous and competing participation in the same tendering procedure" did not allow undertakings the opportunity to demonstrate that the relationship did not impact on the tendering procedure, the European Court of Justice stated in *Assitur Srl v Camera di Commercio, Industria, Artigianato e Agricoltura di Milano* (May 2009) that this legislation did not comply with EU law.[^cite_note-156]

The *Autorità per la vigilanza sui lavori pubblici* (1994–2006) and *Autorità per la vigilanza sui contratti pubblici di lavori, servizi e forniture* (AVCP) (2006–2014) acted as supervisory authorities overseeing public works procurement, and later covering public procurement more generally. In 2014 this function was transferred to ANAC.[^cite_note-157] Decree No 34 of the President of the Republic of 25 January 2000 introduced earlier rules establishing a qualification system for persons who carry out public works.[^cite_note-158] The five regions with special autonomy ([Friuli-Venezia Giulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friuli-Venezia_Giulia), [Sardinia](29376.md), [Sicily](27619.md), [Trentino-Alto Adige](246766.md) and [Valle d'Aosta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aosta_Valley)) can also establish regional legislation regarding public procurement.[^cite_note-159] In case C-3/88, *Commission v Italian Republic*, the European Court of Justice ruled that arrangements made by the Italian government to restrict contracts for the provision of services to develop data processing systems for Italian public authorities to companies "in which all or a majority of the shares \[were] directly or indirectly in public or State ownership", and to include the supply of computing equipment within the services contract, the government had failed to comply with its obligations under Council Directive 77/62/EEC of 21 December 1976 coordinating procedures for the award of public supply contracts, and failed to comply with the principles of freedom of establishment and freedom to provide services in the [EEC Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EEC_Treaty).[^cite_note-160]

#### Latvia

Government procurement in [Latvia](17514.md) is regulated by the Public Procurement Law, effective 1 March 2017, and the Law on the Procurement of Public Service Providers, which came into effect on 1 April 2017. These laws transpose the EU procurement directives; one additional legal provision is that for supplier selection purposes, [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) tax debts are checked where tenderers are registered or permanently resident in Latvia. Public procurement opportunities are advertised on the Latvian *Elektronisko Iepirkumu Sistēma* (EIS) website, as well as in the [Official Journal of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Journal_of_the_European_Union) when above the threshold values.[^cite_note-161] The Procurement Monitoring Office within the [Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_institutions_in_Latvia#Ministry_of_Finance) oversees public procurement.[^cite_note-162] A deposit for filing a review application with the Procurement Monitoring Office must be paid, calculated as 0.5% of the estimated contract value, but no more than €15,000 for construction work contracts or €840 for supply and public service contracts.[^cite_note-163]

#### Luxembourg

In [Luxembourg](17515.md), the main policy body for public procurement is the Public Procurement Directorate within the Public Works Department of the [Ministry of Sustainable Development and Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Sustainable_Development_and_Infrastructure) (MDDI). This department is responsible for the regulatory framework, drafting relevant legislation and monitoring its implementation, and also for representing the Luxembourgish authorities in the field of public procurement. A Tender Commission with members drawn from contracting authorities, chambers of commerce and small business sectors undertakes a consultative role in relation to public procurement.[^cite_note-165]

Luxembourg's public sector eProcurement portal (*Portail des marchés publics*: <https://marches.public.lu/fr.html>) was recognised by the European Commission in 2008 as a good practice example working to facilitate SME access to public procurement opportunities.[^cite_note-166] The EU 2014 Directives on public procurement and utilities procurement were implemented by the Law of 8 April 2018 on public procurement, which was published in the Luxembourg official Gazette (*[Mémorial: Journal officiel du Grand-Duché de Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9morial)*) on 16 April 2018 and entered into force on 20 April 2018.[^cite_note-167] Procurement in the defence and security sector is covered by the Law of 26 December 2012.[^cite_note-168]

### India

See also: [Government e Marketplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_e_Marketplace) and [Corruption in India § Tendering processes and awarding contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_India#Tendering_processes_and_awarding_contracts)

The government procurement-related disciplines in [India](14533.md) are governed by various Public Procurement Orders and the General Financial Rule. Public Procurement Orders and the General Financial Rule are primarily taken care of by the Public Procurement Section of [Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Promotion_of_Industry_and_Internal_Trade_\(DPIIT\)), the [Ministry of Commerce and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Commerce_and_Industry_\(India\)) and the Department of Expenditure within the [Ministry of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(India\)) respectively. Emergency procurement powers are available to the [Indian armed forces](7171338.md), enabling them to meet "urgent operational requirements" without complying with standard procurement procedures.[^cite_note-210] Under [Indian law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_law), an [incumbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incumbent#Business_usage) contractor who has tendered for an extension contract has a right of first refusal ("ROFR") allowing that contractor to match the lowest bid submitted by a competing company. The case of *[National Highways Authority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highways_Authority_of_India) (NHAI) v Gwalior Jhansi Expressway Ltd.* (2018) confirmed that an incumbent contractor who does not submit a tender in accordance with the public body's [notice inviting tenders](36571608.md) loses the right of first refusal.[^cite_note-211]

The principles apply to all governmental procurement activity but the rules only apply to projects or purchases exceeding \$100,000 or construction projects valued over \$10 million.[^cite_note-sour-243]

Purchasers of certain common goods or services are required to use "All-of-Government contracts" (AoG) established by the Government Procurement Branch, overseen by the Procurement Functional Leader and managed by appointed procurement Centres of Expertise. Where a public agency wishes to opt out of the use of an AoG contract it must obtain the approval of approval the Procurement Functional Leader: if the agency and the Procurement Functional Leader fail to agree on an opt-out, the [State Services Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Services_Commissioner) will decide.[^cite_note-245]

Blavatnik School of Government

Blavatnik School of Government

New Zealand joined the Agreement on Government Procurement in 2015 after approval was granted on 29 October 2014.[^cite_note-monz-238] The second International Civil Service Effectiveness Index, published in April 2019 by the [Blavatnik School of Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blavatnik_School_of_Government) at the [University of Oxford](31797.md), ranked New Zealand as the top country for Government Procurement Effectiveness. The procurement indicator was a new addition to this index, not present in the previous 2017 index. The indicator covered both procurement systems and procurement practices. ### Pakistan

Government procurement in [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Pakistan) is overseen by the [Public Procurement Regulatory Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Procurement_Regulatory_Authority) (PPRA), an autonomous body based in [Islamabad](51153.md) which was established by the Public Procurement Regulatory Authority Ordinance of May 2002. ### Rwanda

In [Rwanda](25645.md), the public procurement process is managed on a daily basis by an autonomous organ, the Rwanda Public Procurement Authority (RPPA),[^cite_note-256] which operates under the Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning (MINECOFIN). Public procurement is regulated by the Law N°12/2007 of 27 March 2007 on public procurement[^cite_note-257] which was modified and complemented by the Law N°05/2013 of 13 February 2013.[^cite_note-258] The law is implemented by a Ministerial Order N°001/14/10/TC of 19 February 2014 establishing Regulations on Public Procurement, Standard Bidding Documents and Standard Contracts.[^cite_note-259]

Rwanda has a decentralized public procurement system whereby procuring entities (central government organs, local government entities, government projects, commissions, public institutions, parastatals, agencies or any other government entity charged by the Chief Budget Manager to manage public funds) have the power to conduct directly their public procurement process. The main mission of RPPA is (1) to process the establishment and improvement of public procurement legal framework, (2) provide public procurement legal advisory services, (3) conduct audit and monitoring of public procurement activities carried out by procuring entities (tender award and contract management) and (4) build the capacity of public officials involved in public procurement activities.[^cite_note-260]

The public procurement system in Rwanda is governed by 6 fundamental principles namely (1) transparency, (2) competition,(3) economy, (4) efficiency, (5) fairness and (6) accountability.[^cite_note-261] In the national system, bidders have the right to appeal against public procurement procedures they may think were not conducted appropriately. In that connection, the legal framework provides for the Independent Review Panels at National Level (National Independent Review Panel) and at District Level (Independent Review Panel at District Level).[^cite_note-262] The Independent Review Panels are composed of members from the Private Sector, Civil Society and the Public Sector, and the members from the Public Sector cannot form the majority of members of the Panel. The Independent Review Panel at National Level is under the supervision of the Minister of Finance and Economic Planning whereas the Independent Review Panel at District Level is under the supervision of the District Council. Rwanda introduced an e-procurement system in 2016. In order to make the procurement sector a profession in Rwanda, there is an Association of Procurement Professionals, which was established by the Law N°011/2016 of 2 May 2016.[^cite_note-263]

### Saudi Arabia

### Serbia

The [Serbian](29265.md) law on public procurement came into effect on 1 April 2013, replacing the previous legislation enacted in 2008. A particular concern for Serbia's [legislators](619753.md) was dealing with [corruption](42552633.md) in government procurement: the Law requires Serbia's Public Procurement Office, which oversees procurement, to draft a plan for combating corruption in public procurement procedures, and contracting authorities with an estimated annual value of public procurement in excess of one billion [dinars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinar) (8.9m Euros) to adopt an internal plan for preventing corruption.[^cite_note-266] The Public Procurement Office is based in [Belgrade](55904.md). The role of the Republic Commission for the Protection of Rights in Public Procurement Procedures, established in 2002, is to protect the rights of bidders during procurement exercises.[^cite_note-267]

The Regulation on Mandatory Elements of Tender Documents in Public Procurement Procedures and Way to Prove Fulfilment of Requirements[^cite_note-268] prescribes a model contract as a mandatory element of every set of tender documents, except when a negotiated procedure is being conducted or where a loan is being procured as a financial service.[^cite_note-269] In 2016, the EU funded a programme of support for "further improvement of Public Procurement system in Serbia", as part of the EU's pre-accession assistance programme.[^cite_note-270] There is no current target date for Serbia to [join the EU](10038148.md).[^cite_note-271]

### South Africa

See also: [Corruption in South Africa](18331902.md)

Section 217 of the [Constitution](441610.md) of the [Republic of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Africa), 1996 (Act 108 of 1996) provides the basis for government procurement:

(1) When an organ of state in the national, provincial or local sphere of Government, or any other institution identified in national legislation, contracts for goods or services, it must do so in accordance with a system which is fair, equitable, transparent, competitive and cost effective.

(2) Subsection (1) does not prevent the organs of state or institutions referred to in that subsection from implementing a procurement policy providing for –&#x20;

(a) categories of preference in the allocation of contracts; and

(b) the protection or advancement of persons, or categories of persons, disadvantaged by unfair discrimination.

(3) National legislation must prescribe a framework within which the policy referred to in subsection (2) must be implemented.

In Subsection (3), the previous words "may be implemented" were amended to "must be implemented" by section 6 of the Constitution's [Seventh Amendment Act of 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_South_Africa).[^cite_note-275] The [Public Finance Management Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Finance_Management_Act,_1999) of 1999 (PFMA) also refers to the duty of the Accounting Officer of a department to have and to maintain an appropriate procurement and supply system which is "fair, equitable, transparent, competitive and cost effective".[^cite_note-KwaZuluNatal-276]

### United States

Main article: [Government procurement in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_procurement_in_the_United_States)

Government procurement and government contracting by public authorities in the United States accounts for about US\$7 trillion annually;[^cite_note-Prieß:_Global_Overview-22] the central purchasing agency is the [General Services Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Services_Administration) (GSA). Contracts are not posted online, although two agencies have explored the possibility.[^cite_note-Halchin2013-298] In January 2014, the [Office of Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Inspector_General_\(United_States\)) at [NASA](18426568.md) released a report criticizing the agency's lack of strategic sourcing.[^cite_note-300] Because IT departments were spending autonomously, NASA spent \$25.7 million on similar purchases.[^cite_note-301] The National Institute of Governmental Purchasing and the [Federal Acquisition Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Acquisition_Institute?action=edit\&redlink=1) are active in procurement certification and training. ### World Bank Group

The [World Bank](36752.md) functions like a cooperative owned by its 189 member countries.[^cite_note-305] Its own procurement activities are handled at a corporate level,[^cite_note-306] and its Procurement Framework, approved in July 2015 and operational since July 2016, applies to projects and programmes which are supported with World Bank funds. The Procurement Framework has four key principles:

- Needs and risks of a project are analyzed through a Project Procurement Strategy for Development (PPSD). This analysis enables the borrower to have a strategy on how best to engage with bidders. The analysis ensures that procurement processes are fit for purpose, allow choice, and are appropriate to the size, value, and risk of the project.

- Value for Money is a core procurement principle in all procurements financed by the World Bank. This means the focus is on bids that provide the best overall value for money, taking into account quality, cost, and other factors as needed, rather than a focus on the lowest evaluated compliant bid.

- The approach to resolving procurement-related complaints has the capacity to promptly respond to any concerns during the procurement process. There is a standstill period -a pause between identifying who should win the contract and actually awarding them the contract- so that other bidders can voice any concerns before a contract is actually legally formed and awarded.

- The World Bank is more involved in contract management of procurements with high value and high risk to ensure the best possible outcomes and that problems are resolved quickly.
