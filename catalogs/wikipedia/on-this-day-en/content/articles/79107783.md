Since January 2025, several [protests](266137.md) broke out against United States president [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s [mass deportation of immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_in_the_second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump) following [his inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump) for his [second presidential term](78298108.md) on January 20, 2025. Large-scale protests have occurred throughout Trump's second term in [Alabama](303.md), [California](5407.md), [Arkansas](1930.md), [Georgia](48830.md), [Illinois](14849.md), [Indiana](21883857.md), [Massachusetts](1645518.md), [New York](8210131.md), [North Carolina](21650.md), [South Carolina](27956.md), [Texas](29810.md), [Minnesota](19590.md), and [Washington](13015878.md).

## Background

Main article: [Deportation in the second Trump administration](78314324.md)

On January 23, 2025, 3 days after returning to office in the [second presidential inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump), United States President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) implemented several campaign promises regarding stricter [immigration enforcement](15051.md), leading to an uptick in [United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) (ICE) operations across major metropolitan areas.

That same day, the [DHS](58236.md) authorized federal law enforcement personnel from numerous federal agencies to assist in carrying out Trump immigration policies. A memo from acting Homeland Security Secretary [Benjamine Huffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamine_Huffman) provided "the functions of an immigration officer" to several law enforcement agencies within the Justice Department, including the [Drug Enforcement Administration](146720.md), the [Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Alcohol,_Tobacco,_Firearms_and_Explosives), and the [U.S. Marshals Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marshals_Service). The memo, addressed to acting attorney general [James McHenry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_McHenry_\(lawyer\)), noted that FBI agents have a role for arrests related to immigration, known as [Title 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_8_of_the_United_States_Code) authority; this authority was now conferred onto other agencies.[^cite_note-\:0-24]

On January 23, high-profile ICE raids occurred in [Atlanta](3138.md), Boston, Denver, Miami, New York City, [Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Seattle](11388236.md), and Washington, D.C., detaining 538 illegal immigrants. The mayor of Newark claimed that ICE raided a local establishment, detaining illegal immigrants as well as citizens, including a veteran, without a warrant.[^cite_note-\:1-25][^cite_note-Snc-26][^cite_note-27] The White House said that "The Trump Administration arrested 538 illegal immigrant criminals, including a suspected terrorist, four members of the [Tren de Aragua](70859925.md) gang, and several illegals convicted of sex crimes against minors."[^cite_note-\:5-28]

On January 29, Trump ordered the preparation of the [Guantanamo Bay detention camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp) to house tens of thousands of migrants.[^cite_note-29]

## 2025

### Alabama

On January 29, 2025, an anti-mass deportation public demonstration took place in downtown [Albertville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertville,_Alabama). Many demonstrators chanted bilingual slogans such as "[nobody is illegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_one_is_illegal) on stolen land". Representative [Robert Aderholt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Aderholt), whose congressional district includes Albertville, characterized the demonstration as "deeply concerning".[^cite_note-30] Albertville is one of Alabama's most Hispanic cities with roughly a third of the population identifying as Hispanic or Latino in the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-31]

### California

#### February 2025

On February 2, a large anti-mass deportation protest began at [Olvera Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olvera_Street) in [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles). The event was organized in response to increased immigration enforcement activities by the [U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement) (ICE). Participants gradually amassed into a crowd of several thousand as the protest progressed, with many carrying [Mexican flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Mexico) and banners with pro-immigration messages such as "Nobody is illegal" and "Viva Mexico". Demonstrators marched to and temporarily occupied portions of the [Hollywood Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Freeway), leading to traffic disruptions in the downtown area. By approximately 4\:00 PM [PST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone), the LAPD Central Division confirmed that demonstrators voluntarily vacated the freeway and reassembled at City Hall. No arrests were reported.[^cite_note-32]

On the same date, an anti-mass deportation protest was held in [San Diego](28504.md), which attracted more than a thousand participants. The demonstration began at the [San Diego Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Convention_Center) and proceeded through the [Gaslamp Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaslamp_Quarter,_San_Diego) to the "Coming Together" sculpture at Park Boulevard. The sculpture was symbolically chosen for its representation of unity.[^cite_note-33]

On February 7, multiple high schools across Los Angeles staged walkouts and congregated at City Hall.[^cite_note-34]



Students from local high schools ([John Muir High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Muir_High_School), [Thurgood Marshall Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurgood_Marshall_Secondary_School), [Blair High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blair_High_School_\(Pasadena,_California\)), [Pasadena High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_High_School_\(California\)), and [Rose City High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_City_High_School?action=edit\&redlink=1)) picket [Pasadena City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_City_Hall)

#### June 2025

Main article: [June 2025 Los Angeles protests against mass deportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_Los_Angeles_protests_against_mass_deportation)

On June 6, protests began in Los Angeles following ICE arrests of at least 45 people.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] On June 7, as the protests turned into street riots, Trump announced that the National Guard would be deployed to quell them, authorising two thousand troops.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

On June 9, protests expanded to [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California) after reported raids throughout [Orange County](45330.md) including Santa Ana, [Huntington Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Beach,_California) and [Fountain Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_Valley,_California).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] The president authorized the deployment of an additional 2,000 National Guard members and the [Pentagon](7279897.md) activated 700 [Marines](17349325.md) to deploy to the city, who arrived the next day.

On July 8, an ICE car ran over protestors in downtown San Francisco, injuring at least 1.[^cite_note-43]

#### October 2025

On October 23, outside the [Coast Guard](32223.md) base in [Alameda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alameda,_California), a U-Haul began violently accelerating toward military and police barricades during an anti-deportation protest, causing authorities to open fire on the truck, injuring the driver and a bystander.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

### Colorado

On January 25, 2025, a march in [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora,_Colorado) was organized by the Metro Denver Sanctuary Coalition in protest against ICE raids.[^cite_note-46] Over 2,000 protesters[^cite_note-47] came out to support the cause and rally outside the state capitol building. This protest follows a series of intense ICE raids targeting neighborhoods and residential areas. There are also several volunteers,[^cite_note-48] like those from the Immigrant Legal Resource Center, that offer resources, such as legal services and literature on legal rights, to those at risk.

### Georgia

On February 1, 2025, around 1,000 protestors gathered on [Buford Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buford_Highway) in metro Atlanta, blocking the roadway before being contained by [Georgia State Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_State_Patrol) and [Chamblee Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamblee_Police_Department?action=edit\&redlink=1) officers.[^cite_note-ajc-buford--25-49]

### Massachusetts

## Criticisms of government responses

See also: [Targeting of political opponents and civil society under the second Trump administration § Compiling data on Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeting_of_political_opponents_and_civil_society_under_the_second_Trump_administration#Compiling_data_on_Americans), and [United States Department of Homeland Security § Criticism](58236.md#Criticism)

Several subpoenas were sent by the DHS to request personal information of social media accounts belonging to several users who had expressed views that were critical of ICE or were related to the protests against mass deportation.[^cite_note-76] Some of these subpoenas were withdrawn upon court filings to challenge them.[^cite_note-77]

In addition, facial recognition tools been deployed on activists, used to compile a database on arrests of activists involved.[^cite_note-78] Protesters expressed they felt this was used to intimidate them. This led to a lawsuit in Minnesota, filed against the administration over first amendment rights.[^cite_note-79]
