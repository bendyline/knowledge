**West Bengal**[^cite_note-14] is a state in the [eastern part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India) of India. It is situated along the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md). In 2011, its population was around 91 million in an area of 88,752 km<sup>2</sup> (34,267 sq mi), while the population estimate as of 2026 is 100.6 million.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] West Bengal is the [fourth-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population) and [thirteenth-largest state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area) in India, as well as the [eighth-most populous country subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first-level_administrative_divisions_by_population) of the world. As a part of the [Bengal region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_region) of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), it borders [Bangladesh](3454.md) in the east, and [Nepal](171166.md) and [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan) in the north. It also borders the Indian states of [Jharkhand](197225.md), [Odisha](250724.md), [Bihar](442482.md), [Sikkim](140372.md) and [Assam](186162.md). The state capital is [Kolkata](47905.md), the [third-largest metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_India), and [seventh largest city by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_India_by_population) in India. West Bengal includes the [Darjeeling Himalayan hill region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darjeeling_Himalayan_hill_region), the [Ganges delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_delta), the [Rarh region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarh_region), the coastal [Sundarbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans) and the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md). The state's main ethnic group are the [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis).&#x20;

The area's early history featured a succession of [Indian empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_monarchs), internal conflict, and a struggle between [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) and [Buddhism](3267529.md) for dominance. Ancient Bengal was the site of several major [Janapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janapadas), while the earliest cities date back to the [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period). The region was part of several ancient pan−Indian empires, including the [Vangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanga_kingdom), [Mauryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire), and the [Guptas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire). The citadel of [Gauḍa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gau%E1%B8%8Da_\(city\)) served as the capital of the [Gauda kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauda_kingdom), the [Pala Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire), and the [Sena Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_Empire). [Islam](6037917.md) was introduced through trade with the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate), but following the [Ghurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_Empire) conquests led by [Bakhtiyar Khalji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_bin_Bakhtiyar_Khalji) and the establishment of the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate), the Muslim faith spread across the entire Bengal region. During the [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate), the territory was a major [trading nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_nation) in the world, and was often referred by the Europeans as the "richest country to trade with". It was absorbed into the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) in 1576. Simultaneously, some parts of the region were ruled by several [Hindu states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_kingship), and [Baro-Bhuyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baro-Bhuyan) landlords, and part of it was briefly overrun by the [Suri Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suri_Empire). Following the death of Emperor [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb) in the early 1700s, the [proto-industrialised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-industrialization) [Mughal Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Bengal) became a semi-independent state under the [Nawabs of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawabs_of_Bengal), and therefore showed signs of the first [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution).[^cite_note-ray-17][^cite_note-sengupta-18] The region was later annexed into the [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency) by the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) after the [Battle of Buxar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Buxar) in 1764.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECampbellWatts1760-20] From 1772 to 1911, Calcutta was the capital of all of East India Company's territories and then the capital of the entirety of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) after the establishment of the [Viceroyalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty#British_Empire).[^cite_note-21] From 1912 to India's Independence in 1947, it was the capital of the [Bengal Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency).[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

The region was a hotbed of the [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) and has remained one of India's great artistic and intellectual centres.[^cite_note-Lochtefeld_2001_771-23] Following widespread [religious violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Action_Day), the [Bengal Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Bengal_Legislative_Council) and the [Bengal Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Legislative_Assembly_\(1937%E2%80%941947\)) voted on the [Partition of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Bengal_\(1947\)) in 1947 along religious lines into two independent [dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominions#India,_Pakistan_and_Bangladesh): West Bengal, a Hindu-majority Indian state, and [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal), a Muslim-majority province of [Pakistan](23235.md) which later became the independent Bangladesh. The state was also flooded with [Hindu refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengali_refugees) from East Bengal (present-day Bangladesh) in the decades following the 1947 [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India), transforming its landscape and shaping its politics.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The early and prolonged exposure to British administration resulted in an expansion of [Western education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture), culminating in developments in science, institutional education, and social reforms in the region, including what became known as the [Bengali Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Renaissance). Several [regional and pan−Indian empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Bengal) throughout [Bengal's history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bengal) have shaped its [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Bengal), [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_cuisine), and [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Bengal).&#x20;

Post-Indian independence, as a [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state), West Bengal's economy is based on [agricultural production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_production) and [small and medium-sized enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium-sized_enterprises).[^cite_note-undp_Bengal-26] The state's cultural heritage, besides varied folk traditions, ranges from stalwarts in literature including Nobel-laureate [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore) to scores of musicians, film-makers and artists. For several decades, the state underwent [political violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_violence) and [economic stagnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_stagnation) after the beginning of communist rule in 1977 before it rebounded.[^cite_note-WB1-27] In 2023–24, the [economy of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_West_Bengal) is the [sixth-largest state economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP) in India with a gross state domestic product (GSDP) of ₹17.19 lakh crore (US\$180 billion),[^cite_note-EcoSur-28] and has the country's [20th-highest GSDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP_per_capita) of ₹121,267 (US\$1,300)[^cite_note-29] as of 2020–21. Despite being one of the fastest-growing major economies, West Bengal has struggled to attract [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) due to adverse land acquisition policies, poor [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure), and [red tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_tape).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] It also has the [26th-highest ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_territories_by_Human_Development_Index) among Indian states in [human development index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_development_index), with the index value being lower than the Indian average.[^cite_note-snhdi-gdl-32][^cite_note-undp_Bengal-26] The state government debt of ₹6.47 lakh crore (US\$67 billion), or 37.67% of GSDP, has dropped from 40.65% since 2010–11.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-EcoSur-28] West Bengal has three [World Heritage sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_India) and ranks as the eighth-most visited [tourist destination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_West_Bengal) in India and third-most visited [state of India](375986.md) globally.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUNESCO2012-34][^cite_note-35]&#x20;

## Etymology

Main article: [Names of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_Bengal)

The origin of the name [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) (*Bangla* and *Bongo* in [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language)) is unknown. One theory suggests the word derives from "Bang", the name of a [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_peoples) tribe that settled the region around 1000 BCE.[^cite_note-36] The Bengali word *Bongo* might have been derived from the ancient [kingdom of *Vanga*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanga_kingdom) (or *Banga*). Although some early [Sanskrit literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_literature) mentions the name *Vanga*, the region's early history is obscure.[^cite_note-37]&#x20;

In 1947, at the end of [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) over the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), the [Bengal Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Bengal_Legislative_Council) and the [Bengal Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Legislative_Assembly_\(1937%E2%80%941947\)) voted on the [Partition of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Bengal_\(1947\)) along religious lines into two separate entities–West Bengal, which continued as an [Indian state](375986.md) and [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal), a province of [Pakistan](23235.md), which came to be known be as [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan) and later became the independent [Bangladesh](3454.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMukherjee1987230-13][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChakrabarty2004142-38]&#x20;

In 2011, the [Government of West Bengal](6629274.md) proposed a change in the official name of the state to Paschim Banga ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): পশ্চিমবঙ্গ *Pôshchimbônggô*).[^cite_note-39] This is the native name of the state, literally meaning "West Bengal" in [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language). Later on August 2016, the [West Bengal Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly) passed another resolution to change the name of West Bengal to "Bengal" in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and "Bangla" in Bengali. Despite the [Trinamool Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinamool_Congress) government's efforts to forge a consensus on the name change resolution, the [Indian National Congress](149333.md), the [Left Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Front_\(West_Bengal\)) and the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) opposed the resolution.[^cite_note-namec-40] However, the central government has turned down the proposal maintaining the state should have one single name for all languages instead of three and it should not be the same as that of any other territory (pointing out that the name 'Bangla' may create confusion with neighbouring [Bangladesh](3454.md)).[^cite_note-namec-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]&#x20;

## History

Main articles: [History of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bengal), [History of rulers of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rulers_of_Bengal), and [History of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_West_Bengal)

### Ancient and classical period

A map showing the extent of the Pala Empire

The [Pala Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire) was an imperial power during the [Late Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_India) on the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), which originated in the region of [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal).

[Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age) tools dating back 20,000 years have been excavated in the state, showing human occupation 8,000 years earlier than scholars had thought.[^cite_note-43] According to the Indian epic *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)*, the region was part of the Vanga kingdom.[^cite_note-44] Several [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic) realms were present in the Bengal region, including [Vanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanga), [Rarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarh_region), [Pundravardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pundravardhana) and the [Suhma kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suhma_kingdom). One of the earliest foreign references to Bengal is a mention by the [Ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) around 100 BCE of a land named [Gangaridai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangaridai) located at the mouths of the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges).[^cite_note-45] Bengal had overseas trade relations with [Suvarnabhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suvarnabhumi) (Burma, Lower [Thailand](30128.md), the Lower [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula) and [Sumatra](28679.md)).[^cite_note-46] According to the Sri Lankan chronicle *[Mahavamsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavamsa),* [Prince Vijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Vijaya) (c. 543 – c. 505 BCE), a Vanga kingdom prince, conquered [Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanka) (modern-day Sri Lanka) and named the country [Sinhala kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_kingdom).[^cite_note-47]&#x20;

The kingdom of [Magadha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadha_\(Mahajanapada\)) was formed in the 7th century BCE, consisting of the regions now comprising [Bihar](442482.md) and Bengal. It was one of the four main kingdoms of India at the time during the lives of [Mahavira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavira), the principal figure of [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) and [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha), founder of [Buddhism](3267529.md). It consisted of several [janapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janapada), or kingdoms.[^cite_note-48] Under [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka), the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) of Magadha in the 3rd century BCE extended over nearly all of [South Asia](21566765.md), including [Afghanistan](737.md) and parts of [Balochistan](8431743.md). From the 3rd to the 6th centuries CE, the kingdom of Magadha served as the seat of the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire).[^cite_note-49]&#x20;

Two kingdoms—Vanga or Samatata, and [Gauda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Gauda)—are said in some texts to have appeared after the end of the Gupta Empire although details of their ascendancy are uncertain.[^cite_note-50] The first recorded independent king of Bengal was [Shashanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashanka), who reigned in the early 7th century.[^cite_note-51] Shashanka is often recorded in Buddhist annals as an intolerant Hindu ruler noted for his persecution of the Buddhists. He murdered [Rajyavardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajyavardhana), the Buddhist king of [Thanesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanesar), and is noted for destroying the [Bodhi tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhi_tree) at [Bodh Gaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodh_Gaya), and replacing Buddha statues with [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) [lingams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingams).[^cite_note-52] After a period of anarchy,[^cite_note-53]<sup>: 36 </sup> the [Pala dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire) ruled the region for four-hundred years which began in the 8th century. A shorter reign of the Hindu [Sena dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_dynasty) followed.[^cite_note-54]&#x20;

[Rajendra Chola I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Chola_I) of the [Chola dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty) invaded some areas of Bengal between 1021 and 1023.[^cite_note-55]&#x20;

[Islam](6037917.md) was introduced through trade with the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate).[^cite_note-56] Following the [Ghurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_Empire) conquests led by [Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_bin_Bakhtiyar_Khalji) and the establishment of the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate), it spread across the entire Bengal region. [Mosques](19894.md), [madrasas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa) and [khanqahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanqah) were built throughout these stages. During the Islamic [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate), founded in 1352, Bengal was a major [trading nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_nation) in the world and was often referred by the Europeans as the richest country to trade with.[^cite_note-57] Later, in 1576, it was absorbed into the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire).[^cite_note-58]&#x20;

### Medieval and early modern periods

A coin of the King Shashanka showing the obverse and reverse sides

Coin of the King [Shashanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashanka), who created the first separate political entity in [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal), called the [Gauda kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauda_kingdom)

Firoz Minar a red stone tower at Gauda

[Firoz Minar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firoz_Minar) at [Gauḍa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gau%E1%B8%8Da_\(city\)) was built during the [Bengal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Sultanate).

Subsequent Muslim conquests further helped Islam to spread throughout the region.[^cite_note-59] It was ruled by dynasties of the Bengal Sultanate and feudal lords under the Delhi Sultanate for the next few hundred years. The Bengal Sultanate was interrupted for twenty years by a Hindu uprising under [Raja Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ganesha). In the 16th century, Mughal general [Islam Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_Khan_I) conquered Bengal. Administration by governors appointed by the court of the Mughal Empire gave way to semi-independence under the [Nawabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawab) of [Murshidabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murshidabad), who nominally respected the sovereignty of the Mughals in [Delhi](37756.md). Several independent Hindu states were established in Bengal during the Mughal period, including those of [Pratapaditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratapaditya) of [Jessore District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessore_District) and [Raja Sitaram Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Sitaram_Ray) of [Bardhaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardhaman). Following the death of Emperor [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb) and the Governor of Bengal, [Shaista Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaista_Khan), the [proto-industrialised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-industrialization) [Mughal Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Bengal) became a semi-independent state under the [Nawabs of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawabs_of_Bengal), and showed signs of the world's first [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution).[^cite_note-ray-17][^cite_note-sengupta-18] The [Koch dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_dynasty) in [northern Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bengal_division) flourished during the 16th and 17th centuries; it weathered the Mughals and survived until the advent of the British colonial era.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]&#x20;

### Colonial period



[Subhas Chandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhas_Chandra_Bose), a leading freedom fighter of India

An map of Bengal in 1880

An 1880 map of Bengal

Several European traders reached this area in the late 15th century. The [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) defeated [Siraj ud-Daulah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siraj_ud-Daulah), the last independent Nawab, in the [Battle of Plassey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Plassey) in 1757. The company gained the right to collect revenue in Bengal [subah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subah_\(province\)) (province) in 1765 with the signing of the treaty between the East India company and the Mughal emperor following the [Battle of Buxar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Buxar) in 1764.[^cite_note-62] The [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency) was established in 1765; it later incorporated all British-controlled territory north of the [Central Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces) (now [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md)), from the mouths of the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges) and the [Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra) to the [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya) and the [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region). The [Bengal famine of 1770](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_famine_of_1770) claimed millions of lives due to tax policies enacted by the British company.[^cite_note-63] [Calcutta](47905.md), the headquarters of the East India company, was named the capital of British-held territories in India in 1773.[^cite_note-64] The failed [Indian rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rebellion_of_1857) originated nearby Calcutta, and resulted in a transfer of authority to the [British Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_monarchy),[^cite_note-65] which was administered by the [Viceroy of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy_of_India).[^cite_note-66]&#x20;

The [Bengal Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Renaissance) and the [Brahmo Samaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmo_Samaj) socio-cultural reform movements significantly influenced the cultural and economic life of Bengal.[^cite_note-67] Between 1905 and 1911 an abortive attempt was made to [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Bengal_\(1905\)) the province of Bengal into two zones.[^cite_note-68] Years later, Bengal then suffered from the [Great Bengal famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_famine_of_1943) in 1943, which claimed three million lives during World War II.[^cite_note-69] Bengalis played a major role in the [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement), in which [revolutionary groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_movement_for_Indian_independence) such as [Anushilan Samiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anushilan_Samiti) and [Jugantar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugantar) were dominant.[^cite_note-Lochtefeld_2001_771-23] Armed attempts against the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) from Bengal reached a climax when news of [Subhas Chandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhas_Chandra_Bose) leading the [Indian National Army (INA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Army) against the British reached Bengal. The Indian National Army was subsequently routed by the British.[^cite_note-70]&#x20;

### Indian independence and afterwards

When India [gained independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Republic_of_India) in 1947, Bengal was partitioned along religious lines. The western part went to the [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India) and was named West Bengal. The eastern part went to the [Dominion of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Pakistan) as a province called [East Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal) (later renamed to East Pakistan in 1956), that became the independent nation of [Bangladesh](3454.md) in 1971.[^cite_note-71] In 1950, the Princely State of [Cooch Behar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooch_Behar_State) was merged with West Bengal.[^cite_note-Doldrums-72] Later in 1955, the former [French enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_India) of [Chandannagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandannagar), which had passed into Indian control after 1950, was integrated into West Bengal. Few portions of Bihar were also subsequently merged with West Bengal. Both West and East Bengal experienced large influxes of refugees during and after the [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947. Refugee resettlement and related issues continued to play a significant role in the politics and socio-economic condition of the state.[^cite_note-Doldrums-72]&#x20;

During the 1970s and 1980s, severe power shortages, strikes and a violent [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist)–[Maoist](46636.md) movement by groups known as the [Naxalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxalites) damaged much of the infrastructure, leading to a period of [economic stagnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_stagnation) and [deindustrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deindustrialisation).[^cite_note-WB1-27] The [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) of 1971 resulted in an influx of millions of refugees to West Bengal, causing a further significant strain on its infrastructure.[^cite_note-73] The [1974 smallpox epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_smallpox_epidemic_of_India) killed thousands. West Bengal politics underwent a major change when the [Left Front (LF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Front_\(West_Bengal\)) won the [1977 assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly_election), defeating the incumbent [Indian National Congress](149333.md). The Left Front, led by the [Communist Party of India (Marxist)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_India_\(Marxist\)), governed the state next for more than three decades.[^cite_note-longcommu-74]&#x20;

The state's economic recovery gathered momentum after the [central government](553883.md) introduced [economic liberalisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalisation_in_India) in the mid-1990s. This was aided by the advent of [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology_in_India) and [IT-enabled services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing). Beginning in the mid-2000s, armed insurgents conducted minor terrorist attacks in some parts of the state.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] Clashes with the administration took place at several controversial locations over the issue of [industrial land acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Nano_Singur_controversy).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] This became a decisive reason behind the defeat of the ruling Left Front government in the [2011 assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly_election).[^cite_note-79] Although the economy was severely damaged during the years of unrest in the LF rule, the state has steadily managed to revive its economy.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] It has shown improvement regarding *[bandhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandh)* ([strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_action))[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] and educational infrastructure.[^cite_note-86] Significant strides have been made in reducing unemployment,[^cite_note-87] though the state suffers from substandard healthcare services,[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] a lack of socio-economic development,[^cite_note-90] poor infrastructure,[^cite_note-91] unemployment (persistent) and civil violence.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] In 2006, the state's healthcare system was severely criticised in the aftermath of the [West Bengal blood test kit scam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal_blood_test_kit_scam).[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]&#x20;

## Geography

Main articles: [Geography of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_West_Bengal) and [Climate of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_West_Bengal)



Many areas remain flooded during the heavy rains brought by [monsoons](57630.md).

West Bengal is on the [eastern bottleneck of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siliguri_Corridor), stretching from the [Himalayas](37966.md) in the north to the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) in the south. The state has a total area of 88,752 square kilometres (34,267 sq mi).[^cite_note-2011_pp_tableA2-96] The [Darjeeling Himalayan hill region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darjeeling_Himalayan_hill_region) in the northern extreme of the state is a part of the [eastern Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Himalayas) mountain range. In this region is [Sandakphu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakphu), which, at 3,636 m (11,929 ft), is the highest peak of the state.[^cite_note-97] The narrow [Terai region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terai_region) separates the hills from the [North Bengal plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bengal_plains), which in turn transitions into the [Ganges delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_delta) towards the south. The [Rarh region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rarh_region) intervenes between the Ganges delta in the east and the [western plateau and high lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_plateau_and_high_lands). A small coastal region is in the extreme south, while the [Sundarbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans) [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) forests form a geographical landmark at the [Ganges delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges_Delta).[^cite_note-98]&#x20;

The main river in West Bengal is the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges), which divides into two branches. One branch enters Bangladesh as the *[Padma](1538264.md)*, or *Pôdda*, while the other flows through West Bengal as the [Bhagirathi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagirathi_River) and [Hooghly River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooghly_River). The [Farakka barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farakka_barrage) over the Ganges feeds the Hooghly branch of the river by a [feeder canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farakka_Feeder_Canal). Its [water flow management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharing_the_water_of_the_Ganges) has been a source of lingering dispute between India and Bangladesh.[^cite_note-99] The [Teesta](1365385.md), [Torsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsa_River), [Jaldhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaldhaka_River) and [Mahananda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahananda_River) rivers are in the northern hilly region. The western plateau region has rivers like the [Damodar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damodar_River), [Ajay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajay_River) and [Kangsabati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangsabati_River). The Ganges delta and the Sundarbans area have numerous rivers and creeks. [Pollution of the Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution_of_the_Ganges) from waste dumped indiscriminately into the river is a major problem.[^cite_note-100] Damodar, a tributary of the Ganges and once known as the "Sorrow of Bengal" (due to its frequent floods), has several dams under the [Damodar Valley Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damodar_Valley_Project). At least nine districts in the state suffer from [arsenic contamination of groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenic_contamination_of_groundwater), and as of 2017, an estimated 1.04 crore people were affected by arsenic poisoning.[^cite_note-101]&#x20;

West Bengal's climate varies from [tropical savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) in the [southern portions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bengal) to [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) in the [north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bengal_division). The main weather seasons are [summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer), the rainy season, a short [autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn) and [winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter). While the summer in the delta region is noted for excessive humidity, the western highlands experience a dry summer like [northern India](1552939.md). The highest daytime temperatures range from 38 to 45 °C (100 to 113 °F).[^cite_note-webindia-102] At night, a cool southerly breeze carries moisture from the Bay of Bengal. In early summer, brief [squalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squall) and thunderstorms known as *[Kalbaisakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nor%27westers)*, or Nor'westers, often occur.[^cite_note-103] West Bengal receives the [Bay of Bengal branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_India#Monsoon) of the [Indian Ocean monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_monsoon) that moves in a southeast to northwest direction. Monsoons bring rain to the whole state from June to September. Heavy rainfall of above 250 centimetres (98 in) is observed in the [Darjeeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darjeeling_district), [Jalpaiguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalpaiguri_district), and [Cooch Behar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooch_Behar_district). During the arrival of the monsoons, low pressure in the Bay of Bengal region often leads to the formation of storms in the [coastal areas](#Coastal_plain). Winter (December–January) is mild over the plains with average minimum temperatures of 15 °C (59 °F).[^cite_note-webindia-102] A cold and dry northern wind blows in the winter, substantially lowering the humidity level. The Darjeeling Himalayan hill region experiences a harsh winter, with occasional snowfall.[^cite_note-104]&#x20;

### Flora and fauna

Photograph of a Bengal Tiger

A [Royal Bengal tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bengal_tiger)

Photograph of a Sal tree

[Sal trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sal_tree) in the [Arabari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabari) forest in West [Midnapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnapore)

The "India State of Forest Report 2017", recorded forest area in the state as 16,847 km<sup>2</sup> (6,505 sq mi),[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] while in 2013, forest area was 16,805 km<sup>2</sup> (6,488 sq mi), which was 18.93% of the state's geographical area, compared to the then national average of 21.23%.[^cite_note-107] [Reserves, protected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_forests_and_protected_forests_of_India) and unclassed forests constitute 59.4%, 31.8% and 8.9%, respectively, of forested areas, as of 2009.[^cite_note-fsiwbforest-108] Part of the world's largest [mangrove forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove_forest), the [Sundarbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarban) in southern West Bengal.[^cite_note-109]&#x20;

From a [phytogeographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytogeographic) viewpoint, the southern part of West Bengal can be divided into two regions: the [Gangetic plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangetic_plain) and the [littoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral) mangrove forests of the Sundarbans.[^cite_note-Mukherji-110] The [alluvial soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvium) of the Gangetic plain, combined with favourable rainfall, makes this region especially fertile.[^cite_note-Mukherji-110] Much of the vegetation of the western part of the state has similar species composition with the plants of the [Chota Nagpur plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chota_Nagpur_Plateau) in the adjoining state of [Jharkhand](197225.md).[^cite_note-Mukherji-110] The predominant commercial tree species is *Shorea robusta*, commonly known as the [sal tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sal_tree). The coastal region of [Purba Medinipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purba_Medinipur) exhibits coastal vegetation where the predominant tree is the *[Casuarina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casuarina)*. A notable tree from the Sundarbans is the ubiquitous *sundari* (*[Heritiera fomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritiera_fomes)*), from which the forest gets its name.[^cite_note-111]&#x20;

The distribution of vegetation in northern West Bengal is dictated by elevation and [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)). For example, the foothills of the Himalayas, the *[Dooars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dooars)*, are densely wooded with sal and other tropical evergreen trees.[^cite_note-India123-112] Above an elevation of 1,000 metres (3,300 ft), the forest becomes predominantly subtropical. In Darjeeling, which is above 1,500 metres (4,900 ft), [temperate forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_forest) trees like [oaks](39696.md), [conifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifer) and [rhododendrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhododendron) predominate.[^cite_note-India123-112]&#x20;

3.26% of the geographical area of West Bengal is protected land, comprising fifteen [wildlife sanctuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_wildlife_sanctuaries_of_India) and five [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_India)—[Sundarbans National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_National_Park), [Buxa Tiger Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buxa_Tiger_Reserve), [Gorumara National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorumara_National_Park), [Neora Valley National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neora_Valley_National_Park) and [Singalila National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singalila_National_Park).[^cite_note-fsiwbforest-108] Extant wildlife includes [Indian rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rhinoceros), [Indian elephant](5323507.md), [deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer), [leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard), [gaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaur), [tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger) and [crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodile), as well as many bird species. [Migratory birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_migration) come to the state during the winter.[^cite_note-113] The high-altitude forests of Singalila National Park shelter [barking deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barking_deer), [red panda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_panda), [chinkara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinkara), [takin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takin), [serow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serow), [pangolin](51124.md), [minivet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minivet) and [kalij pheasants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalij_pheasant). The Sundarbans are noted for a reserve project devoted to conserving the endangered [Bengal tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_tiger), although the forest hosts many other endangered species such as the [Gangetic dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangetic_dolphin), river [terrapin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrapin) and [estuarine crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_crocodile).[^cite_note-hdrchap10-114] The mangrove forest also acts as a natural fish nursery, supporting [coastal fishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_fish) along the Bay of Bengal.[^cite_note-hdrchap10-114] Recognising its special conservation value, the Sundarbans area has been declared a [Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Network_of_Biosphere_Reserves).[^cite_note-fsiwbforest-108]&#x20;

According to the provisional results of the [2011 national census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India), West Bengal is the [fourth-most-populous state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population) in India with a population of 91,347,736 (7.55% of India's population).[^cite_note-2011_pp_tableA2-96] The state's 2001–2011 decennial population growth rate was 13.93%,[^cite_note-2011_pp_tableA2-96] lower than the 1991–2001 growth rate of 17.8%[^cite_note-2011_pp_tableA2-96] and lower than the national rate of 17.64%.[^cite_note-indiastatesprov2011-116] The [gender ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) is 947 females per 1,000 males.[^cite_note-indiastatesprov2011-116] As of 2011, West Bengal had a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 1,029 inhabitants per square kilometre (2,670/sq mi) making it the second-most densely populated state in India, after [Bihar](442482.md).[^cite_note-indiastatesprov2011-116]&#x20;

The [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) is 77.08%, higher than the national rate of 74.04%.[^cite_note-117] Data from 2010 to 2014 showed the [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) in the state was 70.2 years, higher than the national value of 67.9.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] The proportion of people living [below poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Below_Poverty_Line) in 2013 was 19.98%, a decline from 31.8% a decade prior.[^cite_note-120] [Scheduled castes and tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_castes_and_tribes) form 28.6% and 5.8% of the population, respectively, in rural areas and 19.9% and 1.5%, respectively, in urban areas.[^cite_note-hdrchap1-121]&#x20;

In September 2017, West Bengal achieved 100% electrification, after some remote villages in the Sunderbans became the last to be electrified.[^cite_note-122]&#x20;

As of September 2017, of 125 towns and cities in Bengal, 76 have achieved [open defecation free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_defecation_free) (ODF) status. All towns in the districts of: [Nadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_district), [North 24 Parganas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_24_Parganas_district), [Hooghly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooghly_district), [Bardhaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardhaman_district) and [East Medinipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purba_Medinipur_district) are ODF zones, with Nadia becoming the first ODF district in the state in April 2015.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]&#x20;

A 2013 study conducted in three [districts of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_West_Bengal) found that accessing private health services to treat illness had a disastrous impact on households. This indicates the importance of the public provision of health services to mitigate poverty and the impact of illness on poor households.[^cite_note-125]&#x20;

The latest Sample Registration System (SRS) statistical report shows that West Bengal has the lowest [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate) among Indian states. West Bengal's [total fertility rate (TFR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) was 1.6, lower than neighbouring Bihar's 3.4, which is the highest in the entire country. Bengal's TFR of 1.6 roughly equals that of [Canada](5042916.md).[^cite_note-126]&#x20;

[Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_people), consisting of [Bengali Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Hindus), [Bengali Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Muslims), [Bengali Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Christians) and a few [Bengali Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Buddhists), comprise the majority of the population.[^cite_note-127] [Marwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwari_people), [Maithili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithils) and [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_people) speakers are scattered throughout the state; various indigenous ethnic Buddhist communities such as the [Sherpas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherpa_\(people\)), [Bhutias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutia), [Lepchas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepcha_people), [Tamangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamang_people), [Yolmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyolmo_people) and ethnic [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) can be found in the Darjeeling Himalayan hill region. Native [Khortha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khortha_language) speakers are found in [Malda district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malda_district).[^cite_note-128]&#x20;

[Surjapuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjapuri_language), a language considered to be a mix of Maithili and Bengali, is spoken across northern parts of the state.[^cite_note-129] The Darjeeling Hills are mainly inhabited by various [Gorkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurkha) communities who overwhelmingly speak [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language) (also known as Gorkhali), although there are some who retain their ancestral languages like [Lepcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepcha_language). West Bengal is also home to indigenous tribal [Adivasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adivasi) such as: [Santhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhal_people), [Munda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munda_people), [Oraon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oraon_people), [Bhumij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumij_people), [Lodha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodha), [Kol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kol_\(people\)) and [Toto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toto_people).&#x20;

There are a small number of [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_communities_in_Kolkata) primarily in the state capital, including but not limited to [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese), [Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_people), [Maharashtrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtrians), [Odias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_people), [Malayalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalis), [Gujaratis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_people), [Anglo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Indian_people), [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_India), [Jews](25955086.md), [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis) and [Parsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsi_people).[^cite_note-130] India's sole [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown) is in [eastern Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Kolkata).[^cite_note-131]&#x20;

West Bengal is religiously diverse, with regional cultural and religious specificities. Although [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_West_Bengal) are the predominant community, the state has a large minority [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_West_Bengal) population. [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_India), [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Buddhism_in_India) and others form a minuscule part of the population. As of [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) is the most common religion, with adherents representing 70.54% of the total state population.[^cite_note-134] [Islam](6037917.md), the second-most common religion, has 27.01% adherents of the total state population.[^cite_note-135] Three of West Bengal's districts: [Murshidabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murshidabad_district), [Malda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malda_district) and [Uttar Dinajpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Dinajpur_district), are Muslim-majority. [Sikhism](27964.md), [Christianity](5211.md), [Buddhism](3267529.md) and other religions make up the remainder.[^cite_note-136] Buddhism remains a widely followed religion in the [Himalayan region](37966.md) of the [Darjeeling hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darjeeling_district); almost the entirety of West Bengal's Buddhist population is from this region.[^cite_note-137] Christianity is mainly found among the tea garden tribes at tea plantations scattered throughout the [Dooars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dooars) of Darjeeling, [Jalpaiguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalpaiguri_district) and [Alipurduar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alipurduar_district) districts.&#x20;

The Hindu population of West Bengal is 64,385,546 while the Muslim population is 24,654,825, according to the 2011 census.[^cite_note-138]&#x20;

## Government and politics

Main articles: [Government of West Bengal](6629274.md) and [Politics of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_West_Bengal)

West Bengal is governed through a [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) of [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy), a feature the state shares with other Indian states. [Universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) is granted to its residents. There are three branches in the state's government namely [legislature](410238.md), [executive](553883.md) and [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_India). The [West Bengal Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly), consists of elected members and special office bearers such as the [Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_speakers_of_the_West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly) and [Deputy Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Speaker_of_the_West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly), who are elected by the members. Assembly meetings are presided over by the Speaker, or the Deputy Speaker in the Speaker's absence. The state's judiciary is composed of the [Calcutta High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_High_Court) and a system of [lower courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_courts_of_India). [Executive authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) is vested in the Council of Ministers headed by the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_West_Bengal) although the titular head of government is the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_West_Bengal).&#x20;

The Governor is the [Head of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_State) appointed by the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India). The leader of the party or coalition with a majority in the Legislative Assembly is appointed as the Chief Minister by the Governor. The [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_West_Bengal_ministries) is appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister. The Council of Ministers reports to the Legislative Assembly. The Assembly is [unicameral](204304.md) with 295 [members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly), or MLAs,[^cite_note-139] including one nominated from the [Anglo-Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Indian) community. Terms of office run for five years unless the Assembly is dissolved before the completion of the term. Auxiliary authorities known as *[panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_raj_in_India)*, for which local body elections are regularly held, govern local affairs. The state contributes 42 seats to the [Lok Sabha](275456.md)[^cite_note-140] and 16 seats to the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md) of the [Indian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Parliament).[^cite_note-141]&#x20;

[Politics in West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_in_West_Bengal) is contested by political parties such as the [Trinamool Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinamool_Congress) (TMC), [Bharatiya Janata Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party,_West_Bengal) (BJP), [Indian National Congress](149333.md) (INC), and the [Left Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Front_\(West_Bengal\)) alliance (led by the [Communist Party of India (Marxist)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_India_\(Marxist\)) or CPI(M)). Following the election in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_West_Bengal_state_assembly_election), the Trinamool Congress and Indian National Congress coalition under [Mamata Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamata_Banerjee) of the Trinamool Congress was elected to power with 225 seats in the legislature.[^cite_note-142]&#x20;

Prior to this, West Bengal was ruled by the Left Front for 34 years (1977–2011), making it the world's longest-running democratically elected [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) government.[^cite_note-longcommu-74] Banerjee was re-elected twice as Chief Minister in the [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly_election) election and [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_West_Bengal_Legislative_Assembly_election) election with 211 and 215 seats respectively, an absolute majority by the Trinamool Congress.[^cite_note-143] In the [2026](77457015.md) election, the Bharatiya Janata Party formed the government for the first time, winning 207 seats, with [Suvendu Adhikari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suvendu_Adhikari) becoming the Chief Minister. The state has one autonomous region, the [Gorkhaland Territorial Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkhaland_Territorial_Administration).[^cite_note-144]&#x20;

In August 2026, the West Bengal government approved the state's first-ever [anti-terrorism squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Terrorism_Squad) and a new anti-narcotics task force.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]&#x20;

The Grand Hotel in Kolkata

The [Grand Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Hotel_\(Kolkata\)) in [Kolkata](47905.md). Tourism, especially from [Bangladesh](3454.md), is an important part of West Bengal's economy.

Brown jute sticks stacked in groups with small green saplings of rice in the foreground

Freshly sown saplings of rice in a [paddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice); in the background are stacks of [jute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jute) sticks.

As of 2015, West Bengal has the sixth-highest [Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP) in India. GSDP at current prices (base 2004–2005) has increased from Rs 2,086.56 billion in 2004–05 to Rs 8,00,868 crores in 2014–2015,[^cite_note-156] reaching Rs 10,21,000 crores in 2017–18.[^cite_note-157] GSDP per cent growth at current prices varied from a 10.3% low in 2010–2011 to a 17.11% high in 2013–2014. The growth rate was 13.35% in 2014–2015.[^cite_note-niti.gov.in-158] The state's per capita income has lagged the all India average for over two decades. As of 2014–2015, per capita Net State Domestic Product (NSDP) at current prices was Rs 78,903.[^cite_note-niti.gov.in-158] Per-capita NSDP growth rate at current prices varied from a 9.4% low in 2010–2011 to a 16.15% high in 2013–2014. The growth rate was 12.62% in 2014–2015.[^cite_note-159]&#x20;

In 2015–2016, the percentage share of [Gross Value Added](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_value_added) (GVA) of factor cost to measure economic activity at the constant price (base year 2011–2012) for each sector was as follows—[Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_India)-[Forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry_in_India) and [Fishery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_in_India)–14.84%, Industry–18.51% and Services–66.65%. A observation was drawn that there has been a slow but steady decline in the percentage share of industry and agriculture over the years.[^cite_note-160] Agriculture is the leading economic sector in West Bengal. [Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) is the state's principal food crop. Its other top crops are [potato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato), [jute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jute), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) and [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat).[^cite_note-ibef2011-161]<sup>: 14 </sup> [Tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea) is produced commercially in [northern districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bengal_division); the region is well known for [Darjeeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darjeeling_tea) and other high-quality teas.[^cite_note-ibef2011-161]<sup>: 14 </sup>&#x20;

State industries are localised in the Kolkata region, the mineral-rich western highlands, and the [Haldia Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldia_Port) region.[^cite_note-wbidcindinf-162] The Durgapur-Asansol colliery belt is home to a number of [steel plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_and_steel_industry_in_India).[^cite_note-wbidcindinf-162] Important [manufacturing industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_India) include–engineering products, electronics, electrical equipment, cables, steel, leather, textiles, jewellery, [frigates](11464.md), automobiles, railway coaches and [wagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon). The Durgapur centre has established several industries in the areas of tea, sugar, [chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical) and [fertilisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertiliser). Natural resources like tea and jute in nearby areas have made West Bengal a major centre for the jute and tea industries.[^cite_note-163]&#x20;

Years after independence, West Bengal is dependent on the [central government](553883.md) for assistance to meet its demands for food; food production remained stagnant, and the [Indian green revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_green_revolution) bypassed the state. However, there has been a significant increase in food production since the 1980s and the state now has a surplus of grains.[^cite_note-hdrchap1-121] The state's share of total industrial output in India was 9.8% in 1980–1981, declining to 5% by 1997–1998. In contrast, the service sector has grown at a rate higher than the national rate.[^cite_note-hdrchap1-121] The state's total financial debt stood at ₹1,918,350 million (US\$20 billion) as of 2011.[^cite_note-164]&#x20;

In the period 2004–2010, the average GSDP growth rate was 13.9% (calculated in [Indian rupee](490783.md) terms) lower than 15.5%, the average for all states of the country.[^cite_note-ibef2011-161]<sup>: 4 </sup>&#x20;

The economy of West Bengal has witnessed many surprising changes in direction. The agricultural sector in particular rose to 8.33% in 2010–11 before tumbling to −4.01% in 2012–13.[^cite_note-165] Many major industries such as the [Uttarpara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttarpara) [Hindustan Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Motors) car manufacturing unit, the jute industry, and the [Haldia Petrochemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldia_Petrochemicals) unit experienced shutdowns in 2014. In the same year, plans for a 300 billion [Jindal Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jindal_Steel) project was mothballed. The state's tea industry has also witnessed shutdowns for financial and [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_West_Bengal) reasons.[^cite_note-166] Also its [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_West_Bengal) industry was negatively impacted in 2017 because of the [Gorkhaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkhaland) agitation.[^cite_note-167]&#x20;

However, over the years due to effective changes in the stance towards industrialisation, ease of doing business has improved in West Bengal.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] Steps are being taken to remedy this situation by promoting West Bengal as an investment destination. A [leather complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Leather_Complex) has been built in Kolkata. Smart cities are being planned close to Kolkata, and major roadway projects are in the offing to revive the economy.[^cite_note-171] West Bengal was only able to attract 2% of the [Foreign direct investment (FDI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) in the 2000s.[^cite_note-172]&#x20;

In 2024–2025, West Bengal's NSDP grew by 9.86% to Rs 16,32,000 crores with an improvement over the 8.94% growth recorded in previous [fiscal year](5752099.md) (FY).[^cite_note-bizstd_2026-173] In the annual [state budget](4360909.md) allocation for this FY, West Bengal's government allocated Rs 22,620 crores to Agriculture and Allied activities of which Rs 5,800 crores was allocated for farmers' financial support and Rs 1,200 crores was allocated for [crop insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_insurance); Rs 47,470 crores was allocated for Education, Sports, Arts, and Culture of which Rs 18,029 crores was allocated to aid private [secondary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education) and Rs 7,732 crores was allocated to aid private [primary schools](25058.md). The state's industrial sector grew by 7.3% which exceeded that FY's national average of 6.2%.[^cite_note-ibef_2026-174]&#x20;

In March 2025, Haldia Petrochemicals was exploring to open a [polycarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate) production plant by investing Rs 8,500 crores. In July that year, the state government announced that it attracted Rs 1,33,000 crores from [private investors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_investor) and created 1,08,000 jobs across [iron and steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_and_steel_industry_in_India), [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_India) (oil and gas), and [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) sectors. The state also organised its eighth global summit in which the large industries received an investment of Rs 23,94,595 crores.[^cite_note-ibef_2026-174]&#x20;

As of 2025–2026, West Bengal is India's sixth-largest economy with a projected GSDP of Rs 20,30,000 crores. The current GVA is as follows—Agriculture–21%, Industry–24%, and Services–55%.[^cite_note-orf_2026-175]&#x20;

For the FY 2026–27, the GSDP was projected to be Rs 21,48,224 crores with an year-on-year growth of 8% meanwhile the state debt was projected to be reaching Rs 8,15,000 crores.[^cite_note-prs_2026-176][^cite_note-IndEx_2026-177]&#x20;

### Literature

Main articles: [Bengali literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_literature) and [History of Bengali literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bengali_literature)

A portrait of Swami Vivekanada

[Swami Vivekananda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swami_Vivekananda) was a key figure in introducing [Vedanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanta) and [Yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga) to [Europe](9239.md) and the [US](3434750.md),

[^cite_note-179]

The [Bengali language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) boasts a rich literary heritage it shares with neighbouring [Bangladesh](3454.md). West Bengal has a long tradition of [folk literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore_of_India), evidenced by the *[Charyapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charyapada)*, a collection of [Buddhist mystic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism) songs dating back to the 10th and 11th centuries; *[Mangalkavya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangalkavya)*, a collection of Hindu narrative poetry composed around the 13th century; *[Shreekrishna Kirtana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shreekrishna_Kirtana)*, a pastoral [Vaishnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnava) drama in verse composed by [Boru Chandidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boru_Chandidas); *[Thakurmar Jhuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thakurmar_Jhuli)*, a collection of Bengali folk and fairy tales compiled by [Dakshinaranjan Mitra Majumder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshinaranjan_Mitra_Majumder); and stories of [Gopal Bhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopal_Bhar), a court [jester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jester) in medieval Bengal.&#x20;

In the 19th and 20th centuries, [Bengali literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_literature) was modernised in the works of authors such as [Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankim_Chandra_Chattopadhyay), whose works marked a departure from the traditional verse-oriented writings prevalent in that period;[^cite_note-180] [Michael Madhusudan Dutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Madhusudan_Dutt), a pioneer in [Bengali drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_theatre) who introduced the use of [blank verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blank_verse);[^cite_note-181] and [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore), who reshaped [Bengali literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_literature) and [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Bengal). [Indian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_art) saw the introduction of [Contextual Modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contextual_Modernism) in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.[^cite_note-182] Other notable figures include [Kazi Nazrul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazi_Nazrul_Islam), whose compositions form the [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde_music) genre of *[Nazrul Sangeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazrul_Sangeet)*,[^cite_note-183] [Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarat_Chandra_Chattopadhyay), whose works on contemporary social practices in Bengal are widely acclaimed,[^cite_note-184] and [Manik Bandyopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manik_Bandyopadhyay), who is considered one of the leading lights of modern Bengali fiction.[^cite_note-185]&#x20;

In modern times, [Jibanananda Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jibanananda_Das) has been acknowledged as "the premier poet of the post-Tagore era in India".[^cite_note-186] Other writers include–[Bibhutibhushan Bandopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibhutibhushan_Bandopadhyay), best known for his work *[Pather Panchali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pather_Panchali)*, which was later adapted into a [Bengali film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_West_Bengal) of the same name by [Satyajit Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satyajit_Ray); [Tarashankar Bandopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarashankar_Bandopadhyay), well known for his portrayal of the lower strata of society;[^cite_note-187] and [Ashapurna Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashapurna_Devi), [Shirshendu Mukhopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirshendu_Mukhopadhyay), [Saradindu Bandopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saradindu_Bandopadhyay), [Buddhadeb Guha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhadeb_Guha), [Mahashweta Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahashweta_Devi), [Samaresh Majumdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaresh_Majumdar), [Sanjeev Chattopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjeev_Chattopadhyay), [Shakti Chattopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakti_Chattopadhyay), [Buddhadeb Basu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhadeb_Basu),[^cite_note-188] [Joy Goswami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Goswami) and [Sunil Gangopadhyay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunil_Gangopadhyay).[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190]&#x20;

### Music and dance

Main article: [Music of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_West_Bengal)



[Baul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baul) singers at [Basanta-Utsab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basanta-Utsab), [Shantiniketan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantiniketan)

A notable music tradition is the Baul music, practised by the [Bauls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baul), a sect of mystic [minstrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minstrel).[^cite_note-191] Other folk music forms include [Gombhira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombhira) and [Bhawaiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhawaiya). [Folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_folk_music) in West Bengal is often accompanied by the [ektara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ektara), a one-stringed instrument. [Shyama Sangeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shyama_Sangeet) is a genre of devotional songs, praising the [Hindu goddess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_deities) [Kali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali); *[Kirtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirtan)* refers to the devotional group songs performed in dedication to the Hindu god [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna).[^cite_note-192] [Rabindra Sangeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindra_Sangeet), songs composed and written by Rabindranath Tagore, and [Nazrul Geeti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazrul_Geeti) (by Kazi Nazrul Islam) are popular. Also just as other states in [northern India](1552939.md), West Bengal has a heritage in [Hindustani classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_classical_music).&#x20;

Other significant songwriters and their music include–[Dwijendralal Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwijendralal_Ray) with his most notable song being "[Dhana Dhanya Pushpa Bhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhana_Dhanya_Pushpa_Bhara)", [Atul Prasad Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atul_Prasad_Sen), [Rajanikanta Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajanikanta_Sen), and *adhunik* or modern music from [films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_West_Bengal) and other composers.[^cite_note-193] From the early 1990s, [new genres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rock#Rock_scenes) of music have emerged, including what has been called Bengali *Jeebonmukhi Gaan* (a modern genre based on [realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_\(arts\))). Bengali dance forms draw from folk traditions, especially with those of the [tribal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adivasi), as well as the broader [Indian dance traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_dance). [Chhau dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhau) of [Purulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purulia_district) is an uncommon form of masked dance.[^cite_note-194]&#x20;

### Films

Main article: [Cinema of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_West_Bengal)

[West Bengali films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_West_Bengal) are shot mostly in studios in the Kolkata neighbourhood of [Tollygunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tollygunge); the name "Tollywood" (similar to [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles) and [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood)) is derived from that name. The Bengali film industry is well known for its [art films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_film), and has produced acclaimed directors like [Satyajit Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satyajit_Ray), who is widely regarded as one of the greatest filmmakers of the 20th century;[^cite_note-195] [Mrinal Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrinal_Sen), whose films were known for their artistic depiction of social reality; [Tapan Sinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapan_Sinha),[^cite_note-196] and [Ritwik Ghatak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritwik_Ghatak).&#x20;

Some contemporary directors include veterans such as [Buddhadeb Dasgupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhadeb_Dasgupta), [Tarun Majumdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarun_Majumdar), [Goutam Ghose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goutam_Ghose), [Aparna Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aparna_Sen), [Rituparno Ghosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rituparno_Ghosh), and a newer pool of directors such as [Kaushik Ganguly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaushik_Ganguly) and [Srijit Mukherji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srijit_Mukherji).[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199]&#x20;

[Uttam Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttam_Kumar) was the most popular [lead actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading_actor) in both Tollywood as well as Bollywood for decades, and his romantic pairing with actress [Suchitra Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suchitra_Sen) in films attained legendary status.[^cite_note-200] [Soumitra Chatterjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soumitra_Chatterjee), who acted in many Satyajit Ray-films, and [Prosenjit Chatterjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosenjit_Chatterjee) are among other popular lead male actors. As of 2020, Bengali films have won India's annual [National Film Award for Best Feature Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_Award_for_Best_Feature_Film) twenty-two times in sixty seven years, the highest among all [Indian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India).&#x20;

### Fine arts

Panchura Temple made from terracotta

[Panchchura Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shyam_Ray_Temple) in [Bishnupur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnupur_\(West_Bengal\)), one of the older examples of the [terracotta arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_terracotta) of India.

There are significant examples of [fine arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_art) in Bengal from earlier times, including the terracotta art of Hindu temples and the [Kalighat paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalighat_painting). Bengal has been the vanguard of [modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism) in fine arts. [Abanindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abanindranath_Tagore), called the *father of modern Indian art*, started the [Bengal School of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_School_of_Art), which had one of its goals as to promote the development of styles of art outside the European realist tradition that had been taught in [art colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_school) under the [British colonial administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj). This movement had many adherents which include but not limited to– [Gaganendranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaganendranath_Tagore), [Ramkinkar Baij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramkinkar_Baij), [Jamini Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamini_Roy) and Rabindranath Tagore. After [Indian Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Independence_Act_1947), important groups such as the [Calcutta Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_Group) and the Society of Contemporary Artists were formed in West Bengal, and it later had a significant impact on the [art scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_art) in India.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202]&#x20;

### Reformist heritage

The capital, Kolkata, was the workplace of several [social reformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_social_reformers_of_India), including [Raja Ram Mohan Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Ram_Mohan_Roy), [Iswar Chandra Vidyasagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iswar_Chandra_Vidyasagar) and [Swami Vivekananda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swami_Vivekananda). Their social reforms eventually led to a cultural atmosphere that made it possible for practices like [sati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sati_\(practice\)), [dowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowry#India), and [caste-based discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste_system_in_India), or [untouchability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untouchability), to be abolished.[^cite_note-Bengali-speaking-203] The region was also home to several religious teachers, such as [Chaitanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitanya_Mahaprabhu), [Ramakrishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramakrishna), [Prabhupada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._C._Bhaktivedanta_Swami_Prabhupada) and [Paramahansa Yogananda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramahansa_Yogananda).[^cite_note-Bengali-speaking-203]&#x20;

### Cuisine

Main article: [Cuisine of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_West_Bengal)







Assorted food eaten in West Bengal: *[Patisapta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patisapta)*, a kind of [pitha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitha); *shorshe [ilish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilish)* (*hilsha* with [mustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_\(condiment\)) sauce); and [rasgullas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasgulla) in sugar syrup

[Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) are traditional favourite foods, leading to a popular saying in [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), "*machhe bhate bangali*", which translates to "fish and rice make a Bengali".[^cite_note-204] Bengal's vast repertoire of fish-based dishes includes *[hilsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilsa)* preparations, a favourite among Bengalis; *[pabda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ompok_pabda)*, *[chingdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prawn)*, [*lotte*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_duck), *rui* ([rohu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohu)), *[katla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catla)*, *[bata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labeo_bata)*, and *shutki* ([dried fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dried_fish)). There are numerous ways of cooking a fish depending upon its texture, size, fat content and bones.[^cite_note-205] Most of the people also consume [eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg), [chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken), [mutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_and_mutton), and [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp). *[Panta bhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panta_bhat)* (rice soaked overnight in water) with [onions](51258.md) and [green chillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper) is a traditional dish consumed in rural areas.[^cite_note-206]&#x20;

Common spices found in a Bengali kitchen include [posto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppy_seed), [cumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumin), *ajmoda* ([radhuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psammogeton_involucratus)), [bay leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_leaf), [mustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_seed), [ginger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginger), [garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic), [green chillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper) and [turmeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmeric).[^cite_note-207] [Sweets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_sweets) occupy an important place in the diet of Bengalis and at their social ceremonies. Bengalis make distinctive [sweetmeats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confectionery) from milk products, including *[Rôshogolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasgulla)*, *[Chômchôm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chomchom)*, *[Kalojam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_jamun)* and several kinds of *[sondesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandesh_\(confectionery\))*. [Pitha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitha), a kind of sweet cake, bread, or [dim sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dim_sum), are specialties of the winter season. Sweets such as *narkel-naru* ([coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut) [laddu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laddu)), *til-naru* ([sesame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame) laddu), *moa* (particularly [Jaynagarer Moa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaynagarer_Moa)) and *[payesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kheer)* are prepared during Bengali festivals such as [Lakshmi puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmi_Puja).[^cite_note-208] Popular [street foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_food) include [Aloor Chop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloo_chap), [Beguni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beguni), [Kati roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kati_roll), [Kolkata biryani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_biryani), and [phuchka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuchka).[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210]&#x20;

### Clothing



[Jamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamdani) Sari of [Bangladesh](3454.md) is very popular in West Bengal.

Bengali women commonly wear the *[sari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari)*, often distinctly designed according to local cultural customs. In urban areas, many women and men wear [western attire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Western_fashion). Among men, western dress has a greater acceptance. Particularly on cultural occasions such as festivals and events, men also wear traditional costumes such as the *[panjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurta)* with *[dhuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhoti)* while women wear *[salwar kameez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salwar_kameez)* or *sari*.[^cite_note-211]&#x20;

West Bengal produces several varieties of [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) and [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk) *saris* in the country. [Handlooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loom) are a popular way for the state's rural population to earn a living through [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving). Every district has weaving clusters, which are home to [artisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisan) communities, each specialising in specific varieties of handloom weaving. Notable handloom saris include *[Tant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tant_sari)*, *[Jamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamdani)*, *[Garad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garad_Saree)*, *[Korial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korial_Saree)*, *[Baluchari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchari_sari)*, *[Tussar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tussar_silk)*, *[Shantipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantipuri_sari)* and *[Murshidabad silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murshidabad_silk)*.[^cite_note-212]&#x20;

### Festivals

Main article: [List of festivals in West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_festivals_in_West_Bengal)









Festivals of West Bengal: [Durga Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja), [Rath Yatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rath_Yatra), Goddess [Saraswati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswati) dressed in a yellow [sari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari) on [Saraswati Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswati_Puja) and [Karam festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karam_festival) in [Jhargram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhargram)

[Durga Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja) is the largest, most popular and widely celebrated festival in West Bengal.[^cite_note-213] The five-day-long colourful [Hindu festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Hindu_festivals) features fiery celebrations across the state. [Pandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandal) are erected in various [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City), [towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town), and [villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village) throughout West Bengal. The city of Kolkata transforms at the time of Durga Puja. It is decked up in [lighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighting) decorations and thousands of colourful pandals are set up where [effigies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effigy) of the goddess [Durga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga) and her four children namely goddess [Lakshmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmi), [Saraswati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswati), god [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha), and [Kartikeya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartikeya) are displayed and worshipped. The idols of all the 5 deities are brought in from [Kumortuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumortuli), where idol-makers work throughout the year to fashion its clay models. Since independence in 1947, Durga Puja has slowly changed into more of a glamorous carnival-like festival than a religious one. Today people of diverse religious and ethnic backgrounds partake in the festivities.[^cite_note-214] On [Vijayadashami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami), the last day of the festival, the effigies are paraded through the streets with riotous pageantry before being immersed into the rivers.[^cite_note-215]&#x20;

[Rath Yatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rath_Yatra) is a Hindu festival which celebrates [Jagannath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagannath), a form of [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna). It is celebrated with much fanfare in Kolkata as well as in rural Bengal. Images of Jagannath are set upon a chariot and pulled through the streets.[^cite_note-Betts-2013-216]&#x20;

Other major festivals of West Bengal include–[Poila Baishakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poila_Baishakh), the Bengali new year; [Dolyatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolyatra) or [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi), the festival of lights; [Poush Parbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobanno), [Kali Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Puja), [Nabadwip Shakta Rash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakta_Rash) (Raas Yatra), [Saraswati Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswati_Puja), [Deepavali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwali), [Lakshmi Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmi_Puja), [Janmashtami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janmashtami), [Jagaddhatri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagaddhatri) Puja, [Vishwakarma Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishwakarma_Puja), [Bhai Phonta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhai_Phonta), [Rakhi Bandhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raksha_Bandhan), [Kalpataru Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpataru_Day), [Shivratri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivratri), [Ganesh Chathurthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesh_Chathurthi), [Maghotsav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghotsab_of_Brahma_Samaj), [Karam festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karam_festival), [Kartik Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartik_\(month\)), [Akshay Tritiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akshay_Tritiya), [Guru Purnima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Purnima), [Annapurna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annapurna) Puja, [Charak Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charak_Puja), [Gajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajan_\(festival\)), [Buddha Purnima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha_Purnima), [Christmas](6237.md), [Eid ul-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_ul-Fitr), [Eid ul-Adha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_ul-Adha) and [Muharram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muharram). Some other important cultural events (or holidays) include [Rabindra Jayanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindra_Jayanti), [Kolkata Book Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Book_Fair), [Kolkata Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Film_Festival), and [Nazrul Jayanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazrul_Jayanti).[^cite_note-Betts-2013-216]&#x20;

[Eid al-Fitr](154944.md) is the most important [Muslim festival](2035419.md) in West Bengal. They celebrate the end of [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan) with prayers, alms-giving, shopping, gift-giving, and feasting.[^cite_note-Chakrabarti-2013-217]&#x20;

Christmas, called *Bôŗodin* (Great day) is perhaps the next major festival celebrated in Kolkata, after Durga Puja. Although Hinduism is the major religion in the state, people show significant passion to the festival. Just like Durga Puja, Christmas in Kolkata is an occasion when all communities and people of every religion take part. Large masses of people go to parks, gardens, museums, parties, [fairs](504805.md), churches and other places to celebrate the day. A lot of Hindus go to Hindu temples and the festival is celebrated there too with specific rituals.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219] The state tourism department organises a gala Christmas Festival every year in [Park Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Street,_Kolkata).[^cite_note-220] The whole of Park Street is hung with colourful lights, and food stalls sell cakes, chocolates, [Chinese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine) ([Indo-chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Chinese_cuisine) food), [momo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momo_\(food\)), and various other items. The state invites musical groups from [Darjeeling](186729.md) and other [North East India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_India) states to perform [choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choir) [recitals](236918.md), [carols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_\(music\)), and [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) numbers.[^cite_note-221]&#x20;

[Buddha Purnima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesak), which marks the birth of [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buddha), is one of the most important Hindu/[Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) festivals and is celebrated with much gusto in the Darjeeling hills. On this day, processions begin at the various [Buddhist monasteries](45856.md), or *gumpas*, and congregate at the [Chowrasta (Darjeeling)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowrasta_\(Darjeeling\)) Mall. The [Lamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lama) chant mantras and sound their [bugles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugle) meanwhile students as well as people from every community carry the holy books or *pustaks* on their heads. Besides Buddha Purnima, [Dashain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashain), or [Dusshera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusshera), Holi, Diwali, [Losar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Losar), Namsoong or the Lepcha New Year, and [Losoong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Losoong_Festival) are the other major festivals of the Darjeeling Himalayan region.[^cite_note-Chakrabarti-2013-217]&#x20;

Each year between July and August or [Shravana (month)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravana_\(month\)) at [Tarakeswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarakeswar) Yatra, nearly 3 million devotees come from various parts of India, bringing holy water of Ganga with them in order to offer it to the Hindu god, [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva).[^cite_note-statesman_2026-222]&#x20;

[Poush Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poush_Mela) is a popular [winter festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_festival) of [Shantiniketan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantiniketan), with performances of folk music, Baul songs, dance, and theatre taking place throughout the town.[^cite_note-Chakrabarti-2013-217]&#x20;

[Gangasagar Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangasagar_Mela) coincides with the [Makar Sankranti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makar_Sankranti), and hundreds of thousands of Hindu pilgrims congregate where the river Ganges meets the sea to bathe during this fervent festival.[^cite_note-Betts-2013-216]&#x20;

## Transport

See also: [Transport in West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_West_Bengal) and [List of airports in West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_West_Bengal)



[Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhas_Chandra_Bose_International_Airport) is a hub for flights to and from [Northeast India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India), [Bangladesh](3454.md), [East Asia](19605700.md), [Nepal](171166.md) and [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan)



[Durgapur Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durgapur_Expressway)



An [SBSTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bengal_State_Transport_Corporation) bus in Karunamoyee, [East Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Kolkata)



[Kolkata Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Metro), India's first metro rail system

As of 2011, the total length of surface roads in West Bengal was over 92,023 kilometres (57,180 miles);[^cite_note-ibef2011-161]<sup>: 18 </sup> [national highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_highways_of_India) comprise 2,578 km (1,602 mi)[^cite_note-223] and [state highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_highways_in_India) 2,393 km (1,487 mi).[^cite_note-ibef2011-161]<sup>: 18 </sup> In 2006, the road density of the state was 103.69 kilometres per square kilometre (166.87 miles per square mile), higher than the national average of 74.7 km/km<sup>2</sup> (120.2 mi/sq mi).[^cite_note-224]&#x20;

As of 2011, the total railway route length was around 4,481 km (2,784 mi).[^cite_note-ibef2011-161]<sup>: 20 </sup> [Kolkata](47905.md) is the headquarters of three zones of the [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways)—[Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Railway_\(India\)) and [South Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Eastern_Railway_\(India\)) and the [Kolkata Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Metro), which is the newly formed 17th zone of the Indian Railways.[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226] The [Northeast Frontier Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Frontier_Railway) (NFR) serves the [northern parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bengal_division) of the state. The Kolkata metro is the country's first [underground railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_railway_system).[^cite_note-227] The [Darjeeling Himalayan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darjeeling_Himalayan_Railway), part of NFR, is a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-228]&#x20;

[Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhas_Chandra_Bose_International_Airport) at [Dum Dum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dum_Dum), Kolkata, is the state's largest airport. [Bagdogra Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagdogra_Airport) near [Siliguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siliguri) is a [customs airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_airport) that offers international service to [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan) and [Thailand](30128.md), besides regular domestic service. [Kazi Nazrul Islam Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazi_Nazrul_Islam_Airport), India's first private sector airport, serves the twin cities of [Asansol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asansol)-[Durgapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durgapur) at [Andal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal,_Paschim_Bardhaman), [Paschim Bardhaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paschim_Bardhaman).[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230]&#x20;

Kolkata is a major [river port](1341936.md) in [eastern India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India). The [Kolkata Port Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Port_Trust) manages the Kolkata and the [Haldia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldia) docks.[^cite_note-231] There is passenger service to [Port Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Blair) on the [Andaman and Nicobar Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_and_Nicobar_Islands). [Cargo ship](786056.md) service operates to other [ports in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ports_in_India) and abroad, operated by the [Shipping Corporation of India (SCI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipping_Corporation_of_India). [Ferries](50771.md) are a principal mode of transport in the southern part of the state, especially in the [Sundarbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundarbans_district) area. Kolkata is the only city in India to have [trams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Kolkata) as a mode of transport; these are operated by the [Calcutta Tramways Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_Tramways_Company).[^cite_note-232]&#x20;

Several government-owned organisations operate bus services in the state, including: the [Calcutta State Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_State_Transport_Corporation), the [North Bengal State Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bengal_State_Transport_Corporation), the [South Bengal State Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bengal_State_Transport_Corporation), the [West Bengal Surface Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal_Surface_Transport_Corporation) and the [Calcutta Tramways Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_Tramways_Company).[^cite_note-233] There are also private bus companies. The railway system is a nationalised service without any private investment.[^cite_note-234] Hired forms of transport include metered taxis and [auto rickshaws](593765.md), which often ply specific routes in cities. In most of the state, [cycle rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_rickshaw) and in Kolkata, [hand-pulled rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulled_rickshaw#India) and [electric rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_rickshaw) are used for short-distance travel.[^cite_note-235]&#x20;

## Education

Main articles: [Education in West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_West_Bengal), [Universities and colleges of West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_and_colleges_of_West_Bengal), [List of institutions of higher education in West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_institutions_of_higher_education_in_West_Bengal), and [List of schools in West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_West_Bengal)



[University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta), the oldest [public university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university) of India



The front entrance to the academic block of the [National University of Juridical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal_National_University_of_Juridical_Sciences), [Kolkata](47905.md)



Prajna Bhavan, housing the School of Mathematical Sciences and School of RKMVU

West Bengal schools are run by the [state government](6629274.md) or by [private organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privately_held_company), including religious institutions. Instruction is mainly in English or [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), though [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) is also used, especially in Central Kolkata. [Secondary schools](554992.md) are affiliated with the [Council for the Indian School Certificate Examinations (CISCE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_the_Indian_School_Certificate_Examinations), the [Central Board for Secondary Education (CBSE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education), the [National Institute of Open School (NIOS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Open_Schooling), [West Bengal Board of Secondary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal_Board_of_Secondary_Education), or the [West Bengal Board of Madrasah Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal_Board_of_Madrasah_Education).[^cite_note-236]&#x20;

As of 2016, 85% of children within the 6-17 years age group attend school (86% do so in [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) and 84% in [rural areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_area)). School attendance is almost universal among the 6–14 years age group, which drops to 70% within the 15–17 years age group. There is a [gender disparity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_differences_in_humans) in school attendance in the 6–14 years age group, by which more girls than boys are attending school. In West Bengal, 71% of women aged 15–49 years and 81% of men aged 15–49 years are literate. Only 14% of women aged 15–49 years in the state have completed 12 or more years of schooling, compared with 22% of men. 22% of women and 14% of men aged 15–49 years have never attended school.[^cite_note-237]&#x20;

West Bengal has eighteen universities.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241] Kolkata has played a pioneering role in the development of the modern [education system in India](293121.md). It was the gateway to the revolution of [European education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_education) during the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj).[^cite_note-242] [William Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jones_\(philologist\)) established the [Asiatic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_Society) in 1794 to promote [oriental studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_studies). Various reformers and [educationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educationalist) such as [Ram Mohan Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Mohan_Roy), [David Hare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hare_\(philanthropist\)), [Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishwar_Chandra_Vidyasagar), [Alexander Duff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Duff_\(missionary\)) and [William Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Carey_\(missionary\)) played leading roles in setting up modern schools and colleges in the city.[^cite_note-Chakrabarti-2013-217]&#x20;

The [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta), the oldest and one of the most prestigious [public universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university) in India, has 136 affiliated colleges. [Fort William College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_William_College) was established in 1810. The [Hindu College (now Presidency University)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_University,_Kolkata) was established in 1817. The [Lady Brabourne College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Brabourne_College) was established in 1939. The [Scottish Church College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Church_College), the oldest Christian [liberal arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts_education) college in [South Asia](21566765.md), started in 1830. The [Vidyasagar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidyasagar_College) was established in 1872 and was the first purely Indian-run [private college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university) in [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-243] In 1855, the Hindu College was renamed to the Presidency College.[^cite_note-244] The state government granted it [university status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Grants_Commission_\(India\)) in 2010 and it was renamed as [Presidency University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_University,_Kolkata). [Kazi Nazrul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazi_Nazrul_University) was established in 2012. The University of Calcutta and [Jadavpur University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadavpur_University) are prestigious technical universities.[^cite_note-245] [Visva-Bharati University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visva-Bharati_University) at [Santiniketan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiniketan) is a central university and an [institution of national importance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutes_of_National_Importance).[^cite_note-246]&#x20;

## Media

In 2005, West Bengal had a total of 505 published newspapers,[^cite_note-rniindia-256] of which 389 were in Bengali.[^cite_note-rniindia-256] *[Ananda Bazar Patrika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ananda_Bazar_Patrika)*, a daily published in [Kolkata](47905.md) with 1,277,801 daily copies, has the largest circulation for a single-edition, regional language [newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_India) in India.[^cite_note-rniindia-256] Other major Bengali newspapers are: *[Bartaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartaman)*, [*Ei Samay*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ei_Samay_Sangbadpatra), *[Sangbad Pratidin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangbad_Pratidin)*, *[Aajkaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aajkaal)* and *[Uttarbanga Sambad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttarbanga_Sambad)*. Major English language newspapers include *[The Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Telegraph_\(Kolkata\))*, *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India)*, *[Hindustan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Times)*, *[The Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hindu)*, *[The Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Statesman_\(India\))*, *[The Indian Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Indian_Express)* and *[Asian Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Age)*. Some important financial dailies such as *[The Economic Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economic_Times)*, *[Financial Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Financial_Express_\(India\))*, *[Business Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Line)* and *[Business Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Standard)* are widely circulated. Vernacular newspapers such as those in Hindi, Nepali, [Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language), Odia, Urdu and Punjabi also exist.[^cite_note-257]&#x20;

[DD Bangla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DD_Bangla) is the state-owned television broadcaster. [Multi system operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi_system_operator) provide a mix of Bengali, Nepali, Hindi, English and international channels via [cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television). [Bengali 24-hour television news channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:24-hour_television_news_channels) include [ABP Ananda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABP_Ananda), [News18 Bangla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News18_Bangla), [Republic Bangla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Bangla), [Kolkata TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_TV), [News Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Time), [Zee 24 Ghanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zee_24_Ghanta), TV9 Bangla, [Calcutta News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_News_\(TV_channel\)) and [Channel 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_10_\(India\)).[^cite_note-258][^cite_note-calcuttaweb.com-259] [All India Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio) is a public radio station.[^cite_note-calcuttaweb.com-259] Private [FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_modulation) stations are available only in cities like Kolkata, [Siliguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siliguri), and [Asansol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asansol).[^cite_note-calcuttaweb.com-259] [Vodafone Idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone_Idea), [Airtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharti_Airtel), [BSNL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSNL), [Jio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jio) are all of the available [cellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_phone) phone providers. Broadband Internet is available in select towns and cities, and is provided by the state-run BSNL and by other private companies. [Dial-up access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dial-up_access) is also provided throughout the state by BSNL and other providers.[^cite_note-260]&#x20;

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_West_Bengal)



[Salt Lake Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_Stadium) (Vivekananda Yuva Bharati Krirangan), Kolkata



[Netaji Indoor Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Indoor_Stadium), Kolkata

Netaji Indoor Stadium was completed in January 1975 and was constructed specifically to host the [1975 World Table Tennis Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_World_Table_Tennis_Championships), which were held at the venue that year.[^cite_note-261] The stadium has also hosted other major events, including the state funeral service of [Mother Teresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Teresa) on 13 September 1997.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263]&#x20;

[Cricket](25675557.md) and [association football](10568.md) are popular. West Bengal, unlike most other states of India, is noted for its passion and patronage of football.[^cite_note-dineo_foot-264][^cite_note-bose_foot-265][^cite_note-das_foot-266] [Kolkata](47905.md) is one of the major centres for football in India[^cite_note-267] and houses top national clubs such as [Mohun Bagan Super Giant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohun_Bagan_Super_Giant), [East Bengal Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bengal_Club) and [Mohammedan Sporting Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammedan_Sporting_Club_\(Kolkata\)).[^cite_note-268]&#x20;

West Bengal has several large stadiums. [Eden Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Gardens) was one of only two 100,000-seat cricket stadiums in the world;[^cite_note-269] renovations before the [2011 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup) reduced the capacity to 66,000.[^cite_note-270] The stadium is the home to various cricket teams such as the [Kolkata Knight Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Knight_Riders), the [Bengal cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_cricket_team) and the [East Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Zone_cricket_team). The [1987 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Cricket_World_Cup) final was hosted in Eden Gardens. The [Calcutta Cricket and Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_Cricket_and_Football_Club) is the second-oldest cricket club in the world.[^cite_note-271]&#x20;

[Vivekananda Yuba Bharati Krirangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_Stadium) (VYBK), is a multipurpose stadium in Kolkata, with a current capacity of 85,000. It is the largest [stadium in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stadiums_in_India) by seating capacity.[^cite_note-272] Before its renovation in 2011, it was the second-largest football stadium in the world, having a seating capacity of 120,000. It has hosted many national and international sporting events like the 1987 [SAF Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Federation_Games), and the 2011 [FIFA](11049.md) friendly football match between [Argentina](454699.md) and [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela_national_football_team), which feature [Lionel Messi](2150841.md).[^cite_note-273] In 2008, the [German](11867.md) goalkeeper, [Oliver Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Kahn) played his farewell match on this ground.[^cite_note-274] The stadium hosted the final match of the [2017 FIFA U-17 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_U-17_World_Cup).&#x20;

Notable sports persons from West Bengal include former [Indian national cricket team captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_national_cricket_captains) [Sourav Ganguly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sourav_Ganguly), [Pankaj Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankaj_Roy), [Olympic tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_Summer_Olympics) [bronze medallist](526648.md) [Leander Paes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leander_Paes) and chess [grandmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Grandmaster) [Dibyendu Barua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibyendu_Barua).[^cite_note-dineo_foot-264][^cite_note-bose_foot-265][^cite_note-das_foot-266]&#x20;
