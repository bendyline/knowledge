The **2025 AFL Women's season** was the tenth season of the [AFL Women's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Women's) (AFLW) competition, the highest-level senior [women's Australian rules football](3827044.md) competition in Australia. The season featured 18 clubs and ran from 14 August to 29 November, comprising a twelve-round home-and-away season followed by a four-week finals series featuring the top eight clubs.

[North Melbourne](22106.md#AFL_Women's_team) won the [premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AFL_Women's_premiers), defeating [Brisbane](5026.md#AFL_Women's_team) by 40 points in the [2025 AFL Women's Grand Final](81690463.md); it was North Melbourne's second (consecutive and overall) AFL Women's premiership. North Melbourne won the [minor premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_AFL_Women's_minor_premiers) by finishing atop the home-and-away ladder with a perfect 12–0 win–loss record and won all three of its finals, completing a [perfect season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_season). North Melbourne's [Ash Riddell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Riddell) won the [AFL Women's best and fairest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Women's_best_and_fairest) award as the league's best and fairest player, while [Port Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Adelaide_Football_Club_\(AFL_Women's\))'s [Indy Tahau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indy_Tahau) won the [AFL Women's leading goalkicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Women's_leading_goalkicker) award as the league's leading goalkicker.

## Background

Players contest the football

[Greater Western Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Western_Sydney_Giants#AFL_Women's_team) and [Essendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essendon_Football_Club#AFL_Women's_team) players contest the football in round 1

In September 2024, [Australian Football League](4732785.md) (AFL) chief executive officer [Andrew Dillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Dillon_\(sports_administrator\)) announced that the 2025 season would have an earlier start date than previous seasons to accommodate an extra home-and-away match,[^cite_note-1] and in November, AFL executive general manager [Laura Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Kane) announced that a twelve-match home-and-away season would be played over twelve weeks in 2025, abandoning the strategy of a compressed fixture trialled in 2024 where eleven matches were played over ten weeks.[^cite_note-2]

## Awards

### Major awards

See also: [2025 AFL Women's best and fairest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_AFL_Women's_best_and_fairest), [2025 AFL Women's All-Australian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_AFL_Women's_All-Australian_team), and [2025 AFL Women's Rising Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_AFL_Women's_Rising_Star)

- The [AFL Women's best and fairest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Women's_best_and_fairest) was awarded to [North Melbourne](22106.md#AFL_Women's_team) midfielder [Ash Riddell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Riddell).

  [^cite_note-83]

- The [AFLPA AFLW most valuable player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Players_Association_awards#Most_valuable_player) was awarded to North Melbourne's [Jasmine Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_Garner); the best captain award was also won by Garner, the most courageous player award was won by [Kiara Bowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiara_Bowers) of [Fremantle](312661.md#AFL_Women's_team) and best first-year player was won by [Zippy Fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zippy_Fish) of [Sydney](29210.md#Women's_team).

  [^cite_note-84]

- The [AFLCA AFLW champion player of the year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Coaches_Association_awards#AFLW_champion_player_of_the_year) was awarded to North Melbourne's Ash Riddell.

  [^cite_note-85]

- The [AFL Women's All-Australian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Women's_All-Australian_team) was announced on 24 November; North Melbourne's Jasmine Garner was named captain and Melbourne forward [Kate Hore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Hore) was named vice-captain.

  [^cite_note-86]

  &#x20;Garner's selection was the eighth of her career, tying the league record with teammate [Emma Kearney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Kearney_\(footballer\)).

- The [AFL Women's leading goalkicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Women's_leading_goalkicker) was awarded to [Port Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Adelaide_Football_Club_\(AFL_Women's\))'s [Indy Tahau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indy_Tahau), who scored 25 goals in the home-and-away season.

  [^cite_note-87]

- The [AFL Women's Rising Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Women's_Rising_Star) was awarded to Sydney's Zippy Fish.

  [^cite_note-88]

- The Goal of the Year was awarded to Sydney's [Montana Ham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_Ham).

  [^cite_note-MGOTY-89]

- The Mark of the Year was awarded to [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Football_Club#AFL_Women's_team)'s [Sarah Hosking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Hosking).

  [^cite_note-MGOTY-89]
