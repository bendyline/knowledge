**Shenzhen**[^cite_note-8] is a [prefecture-level city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture-level_city) in [Guangdong](65109.md), [China](5405.md). A [special economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zones_of_China), it is located on the east bank of the [Pearl River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_\(China\)) estuary on the central coast of Guangdong, bordering [Hong Kong](13404.md) to the south, [Dongguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguan) to the north, [Huizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huizhou) to the northeast, and [Macau](19068.md) to the southwest. With a population of 18.2 million in 2025,[^cite_note-9] Shenzhen is the [third-most-populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_China_by_population) by urban population in China after [Shanghai](27643.md) and [Beijing](18603746.md).[^cite_note-10] The [Port of Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Shenzhen) is the [world's fourth-busiest container port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_container_ports). Shenzhen has established itself as a premier global metropolis with a GDP that has surpassed [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Singapore](27318.md) in recent years.[^cite_note-11]

Shenzhen was developed from a largely rural village area into a city in 1979. In the early 1980s, the [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up) introduced by [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) resulted in the city becoming the first special economic zone of China due to its close proximity to Hong Kong, attracting [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) and migrants searching for opportunities. In thirty years, the city's economy and population boomed and has since emerged as a hub for technology, international trade, and finance. As of 2026, Shenzhen has solidified its status as a premier global trading hub, though recent rankings continue to place it alongside other heavyweights like Singapore to rank as the No. 1 mainland Chinese city for foreign trade.&#x20;

Shenzhen is home to the [Shenzhen Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Stock_Exchange), one of the [largest stock exchanges in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stock_exchanges) by market capitalization, and the [Guangdong Free-Trade Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Free-Trade_Zone). Shenzhen is ranked as an Alpha− ([global first-tier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city)) city by the [GaWC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network). Its nominal GDP has surpassed not only Hong Kong, but another neighboring city, [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), and it is now a city with one of the ten largest economies in the world. Shenzhen also has [the second-largest number of skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_with_the_most_skyscrapers), [fifth-highest number of billionaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_number_of_billionaires), and the seventh-most [*Fortune* Global 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500) headquarters of any city in the world. It is also the [eighth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index)-most competitive and largest financial center in the world, and the world's [18th largest city by scientific research output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_scientific_output), and has [several higher education institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Guangdong), including [Shenzhen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_University) and [SUSTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_University_of_Science_and_Technology). [Shenzhen railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_railway_station) was the last stop on the mainland Chinese section of the [Kowloon–Canton Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon%E2%80%93Canton_Railway).

The city is a global [technology hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_hub). In the media Shenzhen is sometimes called *China's [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley)*.[^cite_note-\:0-12][^cite_note-\:1-13] The city's entrepreneurial, innovative, and competitive-based culture has resulted in the city being home to numerous small manufacturers and software companies. Several of these firms have become large technology corporations, such as [Huawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huawei), [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent), [DJI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI), and [Oppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppo). As an important international city, Shenzhen hosts numerous national and international events every year, such as the [2011 Summer Universiade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Summer_Universiade) and the China Hi-Tech Fair. Shenzhen is home to the main operations of [BYD Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYD_Company), and is the largest automobile manufacturing city in China. A large portion of Shenzhen's population are [migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_in_China) from all over China, and the city's population structure skews younger than most cities in China.

## Toponymy

The earliest recorded mention of the name *Shenzhen* is in 1410, during the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), but a market town was not established in the area until the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty).[^cite_note-14] Shenzhen literally means "deep [ditch](232403.md)" in the local vernacular; the words [圳](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%9C%B3) *zhen* and [涌](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%B6%8C) *yong* are used colloquially to refer to irrigation ditches.[^cite_note-szmc4-15][^cite_note-szmc23-16] The name was chosen for the city in 1978 by [Xi Zhongxun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_Zhongxun), the father of future Chinese paramount leader [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) and the [Party Secretary of Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Secretary_of_Guangdong) at the time.[^cite_note-17]

## History

### Prehistory to early republic era



[Nantou Historic Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantou_\(historic_town\))

The oldest evidence of humans in the area on which Shenzhen was established dates back during the mid-[Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) period.[^cite_note-szmc-18][^cite_note-ShenzhenEveningNews-2018-19] Humans have inhabited the area throughout the past 6,700 years. Historic counties were first established in the area 1,700 years ago. The historic towns of [Nantou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantou_\(historic_town\)) and [Dapeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapeng_New_District), were built on the area that is now Shenzhen over 600 years ago.[^cite_note-szmc3-20]

In 214 BC, when Emperor [Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang) unified China under the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty), the area was submitted to the jurisdiction of the established [Nanhai Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanhai_Commandery), one of the three [commanderies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandery_\(China\)) that were set up in [Lingnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingnan), and was assimilated into [Zhongyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongyuan) culture.[^cite_note-szmc2-21] In 331 AD, the [Eastern Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(266%E2%80%93420\)) administration split up Nanhai and established a new [Dongguan Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguan_Commandery?action=edit\&redlink=1) (东官郡).[^cite_note-ShenzhenHistory2-22] The seat of both the commandery and Bao'an County, one of its six counties, was located around the modern town of Nantou. In 590, the [Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty) administration merged the region back into Nanhai. In 757, the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) administration renamed the county Dongguan, and moved its seat to what is now [Dongguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguan) city, although a military garrison remained.[^cite_note-szmc2-21]

During the 12th century, [Nantou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantou_\(historic_town\)) and the surrounding area became an important trade hub for salt and spices in the [South China Sea](74209.md).[^cite_note-szmc2-21][^cite_note-autogenerated1-23] The area then became known for producing pearls during the 13th century. In the 1362 era, Chinese sailors of a fleet would go to a [Mazu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazu) temple in [Chiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiwan) (in present-day [Nanshan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshan_District,_Shenzhen)) to pray as they go to [Nanyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanyang_\(region\)). The [Battle of Tunmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tunmen), when the Ming empire won a naval battle against invading [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire), was fought south of Nantou at Shenzhen.[^cite_note-24]

To prevent *[wokou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wokou)* pirates from attacking Shenzhen, its residents were resettled northward.[^cite_note-szmc2-21] As a result, [Bao'an County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bao'an_County) lost two-thirds of its territory to the neighboring [Dongguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguan) and was incorporated into Dongguan in 1669. The mid-19th century [Opium Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_Wars) resulted in China ceding [Hong Kong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Island) and the [Kowloon Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Peninsula) to Britain. On 21 April 1898 China leased the [New Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories) to Britain for 99 years and in the following year Xin'an was occupied by British forces for roughly six months.[^cite_note-Sohu-2019-25] From the 3,076 square kilometres (1,188 mi<sup>2</sup>) of territory that Xin'an held before the treaties, 1,055.61 square kilometres (407.57 mi<sup>2</sup>) of the county was ceded to Britain.[^cite_note-ShenzhenHistory2-22]



The opening of [Shenzhen (Shumchun) station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_railway_station), October 1911

In response to the [Wuchang Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang_Uprising) in 1911, Xin'an residents rebelled against the local Qing administration and successfully overthrew them.[^cite_note-BaoanDaily-2012-26] In the same year the Chinese section of the [Kowloon–Canton Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon%E2%80%93Canton_Railway) (KCR) was opened to the public. The last stop on the Chinese side was [Shenzhen railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_railway_station), helping the town's economy and opened Shenzhen up to the world.[^cite_note-Sohu-2019-25][^cite_note-27] In 1913, the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) administration renamed Xin'an County back to Bao'an County to prevent confusion from [another county of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xin'an_County) in [Henan](166408.md).[^cite_note-szmc2-21]

During the [Canton–Hong Kong strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton%E2%80%93Hong_Kong_strike) in 1925, the [All-China Federation of Trade Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-China_Federation_of_Trade_Unions) set up a reception station for strike workers in Hong Kong in Shenzhen.[^cite_note-28] Strike workers were also given pickets and armored vehicles by the strike committee to create a blockade around Hong Kong. In 1931 [Chen Jitang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Jitang) and his family established several casinos in Shenzhen, the largest of which being Shumchun Casino.[^cite_note-29]

During [World War II](32927.md), the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) occupied Shenzhen and [Nantou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantou_\(historic_town\)),[^cite_note-szmc2-21] forcing the [Bao'an County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bao'an_County) government to relocate to the neighboring Dongguan County.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] In 1941, the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) tried to cross into Hong Kong through the [Lo Wu Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo_Wu_Bridge) in Shenzhen, though the bridge was blown up by British forces, which prevented the Japanese from entering Hong Kong that way.[^cite_note-32]

### 1950s to 1980

In 1953, four years after the founding of the [People's Republic of China](5405.md), the Bao'an County government decided to move to Shenzhen, since the town was closer to the KCR and had a larger economy than Nantou.[^cite_note-szmc2-21] From the 1950s to the end of the 1970s, Shenzhen and the rest of Bao'an County oversaw [a huge influx of refugees trying to escape to Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee_wave_from_the_People's_Republic_of_China_to_British_Hong_Kong) from the upheavals that were occurring in [mainland China](55079.md), and a range from 100,000[^cite_note-tao2-33] to 560,000[^cite_note-tao1-34] refugees resided in the county.

In January 1978, a Central Inspection Team sent by the [State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) investigated and established the issue of creating a foreign trade port in Bao'an County.[^cite_note-HuizhouMuniCommittee-2015-35] In May, the investigation team wrote the "Hong Kong and Macao Economic Investigation Report" and proposed to turn Bao'an County and [Zhuhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuhai) into commodity export bases. In August 1978, the [Huiyang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huiyang_District) Committee reported to the Provincial Committee on the "Report on the Request for the Change of Bao'an County to Shenzhen". On 18 October, the Standing Committee of the [Guangdong](65109.md) Provincial Party Committee decided to change Bao'an County into Bao'an City and to turn it into a medium-level [prefecture-level city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture-level_city) with a foreign trade base. The Huiyang District Committee and the Bao'an County Committee, however, defended the change to rename Bao'an County to Shenzhen, claiming that people in the world know more about Shenzhen and its port than they know about Bao'an County.

On 23 January 1979, the [Guangdong](65109.md) provincial administration and the district of [Huiyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huiyang_District) announced their proposal to rename Bao'an County to Shenzhen and was approved and put into effect by the [State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) on 5 March of that year.[^cite_note-HuizhouMuniCommittee-2015-35] Also, the city would establish six districts: [Luohu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luohu_District), Nantou, Songgang, [Longhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhua_District,_Shenzhen), [Longgang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longgang_District,_Shenzhen) and Kuiyong. On 31 January 1979, the [Central Committee of the Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) approved a plan to establish the [Shekou Industrial Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekou) in Shenzhen with the purpose "to lead domestic, overseas, and diversified operations, industrial and commercial integration, and trading" based on the systems of that of Hong Kong and [Macau](19068.md).[^cite_note-1月31日，中央决定成立招商局蛇口工业区-36] The Shekou Industrial Zone project was led by Hong Kong-based [China Merchants Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Merchants_Group) under [Yuan Geng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_Geng)'s leadership and was to become the first export processing industrial zone in mainland China.

At the beginning of April 1979, the Standing Committee of [Guangdong](65109.md) Province discussed and proposed to the [Central Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee) to set up a "trade cooperation zone" in Shenzhen, [Zhuhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuhai), and [Shantou](268030.md).[^cite_note-HuizhouMuniCommittee-2015-35] In the same month, the Central Working Conference decided on the "Regulations on Vigorously Developing Foreign Trade to Increase Foreign Exchange Income" and agreed to pilot the first [special economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zones_of_China) (SEZ) in Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Shantou, and [Xiamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen).[^cite_note-37] In November, Shenzhen was elevated to the status of prefecture-level city at the regional level by the Guangdong provincial administration.[^cite_note-BaoanDaily-2012-26] Hundreds of small villages nearby, such as Yumin Cun, were incorporated into Shenzhen.[^cite_note-\:Chatwin-38]<sup>: 60 </sup>

### Special Economic Zone (1980s–present)



Billboards of high-rise construction in Shenzhen, 1982

In 1980, Shenzhen had a population of 30,000.[^cite_note-39]<sup>: 29 </sup> In May 1980 the Central Committee designated Shenzhen as the first SEZ in China,[^cite_note-szmc2-21][^cite_note-Newsweek_IS_Fish-40] which was promoted by then-[paramount leader](262766.md) [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) as part of China's [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up).[^cite_note-41] Its objective is to be an experimental ground for the practice of [market capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalism) within a community guided by the ideals of [socialism with Chinese characteristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_market_economy).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-Economist-44] On 26 August, the [Standing Committee of the National People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_of_the_National_People's_Congress) (NPC) approved the "Regulations of the Guangdong Special Economic Zone."[^cite_note-45]

In March 1981 Shenzhen was promoted to a [sub-provincial division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-provincial_division).[^cite_note-szmc-18][^cite_note-szmc2-21] There were plans for Shenzhen to develop its currency, but the plans were shelved due to the risk and the disagreement that a country should not be operating with two currencies.[^cite_note-46] To enforce law and order in the city, the Shenzhen government erected barbed wire and checkpoints between the land borders of the main sections of the SEZ and the SEZ outskirts, as well as the rest of China, in 1983, which was known as the second line border.[^cite_note-Southern_Metropolis_Daily-2014-47][^cite_note-exg1-48] Much of Shenzhen's urban development in the 1980s focused in the area around the old border crossing and the market town.[^cite_note-\:Chatwin-38]<sup>: 61 </sup> Its [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) was typical for the 1980s.[^cite_note-\:04-49]<sup>: 157 </sup>



[Futian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futian,_Shenzhen) [CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) in the spring of 1998 from [Lianhuashan Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianhuashan_Park)



Futian CBD from [Lianhuashan Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianhuashan_Park) in 2018

In December 1990, under the authority of the [China Securities Regulatory Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Securities_Regulatory_Commission), the [Shenzhen Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Stock_Exchange) was established to provide a platform for centralized securities trading.[^cite_note-50] In February 1992, the Standing Committee of the NPC granted the government of Shenzhen the power to make local laws and regulations.[^cite_note-szmc23-16] In 1996 and early 1997, the [Shenzhen Guesthouse Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Guesthouse_Hotel) in Shenzhen was home to the [Provisional Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Legislative_Council) and Provisional Executive Council of Hong Kong in preparation for the [handover of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handover_of_Hong_Kong) in 1997.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] By 2001, as a result of Shenzhen's increasing economic prospects, increasing numbers of migrants from mainland China chose to go to Shenzhen and stay there instead of trying to illegally cross into Hong Kong.[^cite_note-53] There were 9,000 captured border-crossers in 2000, while the same figure was 16,000 in 1991. Around the same time, Shenzhen hosted the second Senior Officials' Meeting of [APEC China 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APEC_China_2001) on 26 May 2001 in its southern manufacturing center and port.[^cite_note-54] In May 2008, the State Council approved the Shenzhen SEZ to promote Shenzhen's administrative management system, economic system, social field, independent innovation system and mechanism, system and mechanism for opening up and regional cooperation, and resource conservation and environmental friendliness.[^cite_note-55]

On 1 July 2010 the State Council dissolved the "second line", and expanded the Shenzhen SEZ to include all districts, a five-fold increase over its pre-expansion size.[^cite_note-exg-56] On 26 August 2010, on the 30th anniversary of the establishment of the Shenzhen SEZ, the State Council approved the "Overall Development Plan for [Qianhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianhai) Shenzhen-Hong Kong Modern Service Industry Cooperation Zone."[^cite_note-国务院批复前海深港现代服务业合作区发展规划-57] In August 2011, the city hosted the [26th Universiade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Summer_Universiade), an international multi-sport event organized for university athletes.[^cite_note-58] In April 2015, the Shekou Industrial Zone and the Qianhai Zone were integrated within the newly established [Guangdong Free-Trade Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Free-Trade_Zone).[^cite_note-59]

On 18 August 2019 the [central government](23239.md) in [Beijing](18603746.md) unveiled reform plans covering economic, social, and political sectors of Shenzhen,[^cite_note-60] labeling Shenzhen a pilot demonstration zone for socialism with Chinese characteristics.[^cite_note-\:02-61]<sup>: 58 </sup>

Shenzhen roughly follows the administrative boundaries of the historical [Bao'an County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bao'an_County). The southern portion of Bao'an County became part of [British Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong) after the [Opium Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_Wars), while the village of Shenzhen was on the border. [Shenzhen railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_railway_station) was the last stop on the mainland Chinese section of the [Kowloon–Canton Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon%E2%80%93Canton_Railway), and Shenzhen's economy grew and it became a city by 1979.

Shenzhen is located within the [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta), bordering [Hong Kong](13404.md) to the south, [Huizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huizhou) to the north and northeast, [Dongguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguan) to the north and northwest, [Lingdingyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingdingyang) and [Pearl River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_\(China\)) to the west, and [Mirs Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirs_Bay) to the east, and roughly 100 kilometres (62 mi) southeast of the provincial capital, [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou). As of the end of 2017, the resident population of Shenzhen was 12,528,300, of which the registered population was 4,472,200, the actual administrative population was over 20 million.[^cite_note-citypopulation.de-62] It makes up part of the Pearl River Delta built-up area with 44,738,513 inhabitants, spread over 9 municipalities (including [Macau](19068.md)). The city is elongated measuring 81.4 kilometers from east to west while the shortest section from north to south is 10.8 kilometers.

Over 160 rivers or channels flow through Shenzhen. There are 24 reservoirs within the city limits with a total capacity of 525 million tonnes.[^cite_note-63] Notable rivers in Shenzhen include the [Shenzhen River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_River), Maozhou River and Longgang River.[^cite_note-64]

### Climate



The urban area of Shenzhen in 2005

Although Shenzhen is situated about a degree south of the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer), due to the Siberian [anticyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticyclone) it has a warm, [monsoon](57630.md)-influenced, [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cwa*). Winters are mild and relatively dry, due in part to the influence of the [South China Sea](74209.md), and frost is very rare; it begins dry but becomes progressively more humid and overcast. However, fog is most frequent in winter and spring, with 106 days per year reporting some fog. Early spring is the cloudiest time of year, and rainfall begins to dramatically increase in April; the rainy season lasts until late September to early October. With monthly percent possible sunshine ranging from 27 percent in March to 53 percent in October, the city receives 1,853 hours of bright sunshine annually.

The monsoon reaches its peak intensity in the summer months, when the city also experiences very humid and hot conditions. Despite this, extreme heat is rare, there are only 2.4 days of 35 °C (95 °F)+ temperatures.[^cite_note-SZMB_via_21CMA-65] The region is prone to torrential rain as well, with 9.7 days that have 50 millimetres (2.0 inches) or more of rain, and 2.2 days of at least 100 mm (3.94 in).[^cite_note-SZMB_via_21CMA-65] The latter portion of autumn is dry. The annual precipitation averages at around 1,933 mm (76 in), some of which is delivered in [typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon) that strike from the east during summer and early autumn. Extreme temperatures have ranged from 0.2 °C (32 °F) on 11 February 1957 to 38.7 °C (102 °F) on 10 July 1980.[^cite_note-Mherrera-66]

Like all [governing institutions in mainland China](23239.md), Shenzhen has a parallel party-government system,[^cite_note-PoliticalSystem-72] in which the [secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Secretary_of_Shenzhen) of the [CCP Shenzhen Municipal Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Municipal_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) outranks the [Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Shenzhen).[^cite_note-73] The CCP committee acts as the top policy-formulation body, and is typically composed of 12 members (including the secretary).[^cite_note-74]

Despite being a [sub-provincial city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-provincial_division), Shenzhen as a SEZ still wields a lot of autonomy from the central government.[^cite_note-szmc-18][^cite_note-szmc2-21] In addition to being promoted to a sub-provincial city, the [National People's Congress](38878740.md) (NPC) in 1981 granted legislative powers to Shenzhen and other Special Economic Zones, giving the city the privilege to make its own laws and regulations.[^cite_note-75] The [Standing Committee of the NPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_of_the_National_People's_Congress) also granted Shenzhen voted and passed the "Decision on Authorizing the [Shenzhen Municipal People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Municipal_People's_Congress) and its Standing Committee and the [Shenzhen Municipal People's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Municipal_People's_Government) to respectively formulate laws and regulations for implementation in the Shenzhen Special Economic Zone" in order to give fully strengthen Shenzhen's legislative powers without interference from the central government.[^cite_note-76]

### Corruption

There were several cases of high-ranking Shenzhen officials who were arrested on charges relating to corruption. In December 2002, the Shenzhen People's Intermediate Court sentenced Zhao Yucun, former Commissioner of Shenzhen Customs, to life imprisonment for taking bribes of 9 million [RMB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMB).[^cite_note-77] In November 2003, the Guangzhou People's Intermediate Court charged former Shenzhen Deputy Mayor Wang Ju with bribery and abuse of power and sentenced him to 20 years in prison.[^cite_note-78] In June 2005, the Shenzhen People's Intermediate Court charged sentenced Luohu District Public Security Director An Huijun to 15 years in prison for accepting bribes.[^cite_note-79] In May 2011, the Zhengzhou Intermediate Court sentenced former mayor [Xu Zongheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Zongheng) to the [death penalty with a two-year reprieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_penalty_with_reprieve) for accepting bribes up to US\$5.4 million.[^cite_note-80]

Shenzhen was originally [Bao'an County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bao'an_County). On 5 March 1979, the [State Council of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) dissolved the county and set up the city of Shenzhen in its place initially with six districts: Luohu (罗湖), Nantou (南头), Songgang (松岗), Longhua (龙华), Longgang (龙岗), and Kuiyong (葵涌), with the seat based in Luohu. In October 1981, Bao'an County was re-established, with its region now based outside Shenzhen. In June 1983, the districts were dissolved and re-established instead as five management areas (管理区): [Shekou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekou) (蛇口; south-west Shenzhen), Nantou (南头; west Shenzhen), Shangbu (上步; central Shenzhen), Luohu (罗湖; east-central Shenzhen), and Shatoujiao (沙头角; far-east Shenzhen).[^cite_note-ShezhenHistory-2011-84] To enforce law and order in the city, the Shenzhen government erected a border known as the second line ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 二线关), which consisted of barbed wire and checkpoints between the city and the rest of China.[^cite_note-Southern_Metropolis_Daily-2014-47][^cite_note-exg1-48] Initially, the border control was relatively strict, requiring non-Shenzhen citizens to obtain special permissions for entering. Over the years, border controls have gradually weakened, and permission requirement has been abandoned.

In January 1990, the city merged Shekou Management Area and Nantou Management Area to form the [Nanshan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshan_District,_Shenzhen), renamed Shangbu Management Area to the [Futian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futian,_Shenzhen), and merged Luohu Management Area and Shatoujiao Management Area to form the [Luohu District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luohu_District). In December 1992, Bao'an County was dissolved again, with its area taken by Shenzhen and split into two new districts: [Bao'an District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bao'an_District) and [Longgang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longgang_District,_Shenzhen), though economic privileges within special economics zones did not pertain to them as they were outside the second line border. At this point, Shenzhen has five districts: Luohu, Futian, Nanshan, Bao'an, and Longgang. In March 1998, Shenzhen's government created the [Yantian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantian_District) from the eastern portions of the Luohu District (the original area of the Shatoujiao Management District), and within the second line border.[^cite_note-ShezhenHistory-2011-84] Yantian, Luohu, Futian, and Nanshan together as the special economic districts within the second line border are referred to as *guannei* (关内; 'within the border') while districts that are outside the second line and do not have special economic privileges such as Bao'an and Longgang are referred to as *guanwai* (关外; 'outside the border').[^cite_note-Zaidian-2016-85] The Shenzhen government later established two new districts as part of the *guanwai*: [Guangming New District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangming_District) in August 2007 and [Pingshan New District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingshan_District,_Shenzhen) in June 2009.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

On 1 July 2010, the second line border was dissolved, and the Shenzhen SEZ was expanded to cover the entire city. Therefore, the four *guanwai* districts Bao'an District, Longgang District, Guangming New District, and Pingshan New District, would be given special economic privileges like the *guannei* districts.[^cite_note-exg-56] The area of the Shenzhen SEZ also increased from 396 square kilometres (153 sq mi) to 1,953 square kilometres (754 sq mi).[^cite_note-88] Since June 2015, the existing unused border structures have been demolished and are being transformed into urban greenspaces and parks.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] On 15 January 2018, the State Council approved the removal of the barbed wire fence set up to mark the boundary of the SEZ.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

In early 2011, the provincial government of Guangdong approved the establishment of the Shenzhen-Shantou Special Cooperation Zone in the city and SEZ of [Shantou](268030.md), [Guangdong](65109.md) that will last until 2040 with the purpose of economic development. The zone would be managed by Shenzhen and another Cantonese city, [Shanwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanwei).[^cite_note-94] The zone is under the jurisdiction of Shenzhen instead of Shantou, with residents living there considered to be permanent residents of Shenzhen.[^cite_note-95]

The Shenzhen government later established two new districts on 27 October 2011, [Longhua New District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhua_District,_Shenzhen) and [Dapeng New District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapeng_New_District).[^cite_note-96] With approval of the State Council, Shenzhen re-organized Longhua New District as Longhua District and Pingshan New District as Pingshan District on 11 October 2016 and Guangming New District as Guangming District on 24 May 2018, therefore becoming their own jurisdictions.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

## Economy



[Shenzhen Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Stock_Exchange)

Shenzhen was the first of the [Special Economic Zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zones_of_China) (SEZ) to be established by then paramount leader [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping).[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-Newsweek_IS_Fish-40] Back to Apr 1979, Shenzhen was reformed as a Special Export Zone to create a favorable investment environment and introduce advanced technology and management experience, later renamed to [Special Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zones_of_China) in May 1980.[^cite_note-100] As of 2022, Shenzhen has a [nominal GDP](12594.md) of 3.24 trillion [RMB](66958.md) (HK\$2.87 trillion), which surpassed neighboring [Hong Kong](13404.md)'s GDP of HK\$2.11 trillion and [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou)'s GDP of 2.88 trillion RMB (HK\$2.68 trillion),[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] making the economic output of Shenzhen the [3rd largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_top_Chinese_cities_by_GDP) out of Chinese cities,[^cite_note-hm-104] trailing behind [Shanghai](27643.md) and [Beijing](18603746.md).[^cite_note-municipalities-105] In addition, Shenzhen's [GDP growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) between 2016 and 2017 of 8.8% surpassed that of Hong Kong and [Singapore](27318.md), with 3.7% and 2.5% respectively.[^cite_note-106] With a [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization) of [US\$](18717338.md)2.5 trillion as of 30 November 2018, the [Shenzhen Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Stock_Exchange) (SZSE) is the [8th largest exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stock_exchanges) in the world.[^cite_note-107]

In the 2021 [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index), Shenzhen was ranked as having the 8th most competitive and largest financial center in the world and 6th in Asia & [Oceania](22621.md) region (after Shanghai, Hong Kong, Singapore, Beijing, and Tokyo).[^cite_note-GFCI-108] As of 2020, Shenzhen is ranked as an Alpha- ([global first-tier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city)) city by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network).[^cite_note-109] According to *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*, Shenzhen has the fifth-highest number of billionaires of any city in the world.[^cite_note-110] Shenzhen's [nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_GDP) is projected to be among the world top 10 largest cities in 2035 (together with [Beijing](18603746.md), [Shanghai](27643.md) and [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) in China) according to a study by Oxford Economics[^cite_note-111] and its nominal GDP per capita will reach above US\$57,000 (ranking first in mainland China) in 2030, which is comparable to Tokyo and Seoul.[^cite_note-112]

Shenzhen is part of the [21st Century Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Maritime_Silk_Road) that runs from the Chinese coast south to the tip of India via the [Suez Canal](29323.md) to the Mediterranean, there to the Upper Adriatic region to the northern Italian hub of [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) with its rail connections to [Central Europe](5188.md) and the [North Sea](21179.md).[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

As of August 2023, Shenzhen has the seventh-most [*Fortune* Global 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500) headquarters of any city in the world and the third-most in China after (Beijing and Shanghai) within its city limits.[^cite_note-118]

## Industry

Shenzhen's industry is described by its Municipal Bureau of Statistics to be upheld by its four-pillar industries: [high tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics), and [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_industry).[^cite_note-2015统计公报2-122]

### High Tech

Shenzhen is a large hub of the Chinese and global technology industry and home to a large startup ecosystem. As of 2020 the city was ranked as the 4th [Fintech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fintech) powerhouse in the world.[^cite_note-123] Shenzhen is primarily known for its high-tech industry, which has a value of 585.491 billion RMB (US\$82.9 billion) in 2015, a 13 percent increase compared to last year.[^cite_note-2015统计公报2-122] Out of the nominal GDP of 1,750.299 billion RMB in 2015 the high-tech industry generated 33.4 percent. Shenzhen is home to a number of prominent tech firms, such as [Huawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huawei),[^cite_note-124] [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent),[^cite_note-125] [DJI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI),[^cite_note-126] and [ZTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZTE).[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] Other tech firms include personal computer manufacturer [Hasee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasee),[^cite_note-NYT-129] [Hytera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hytera),[^cite_note-130] and [OnePlus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OnePlus).[^cite_note-131] Huaqiangbei is a wholesale hub for consumer high-tech products like smartphones and gadgets.

Shenzhen annually holds the China International High-tech Achievements Fair, which showcases high-tech products and provides for dialogue and investment for high-tech.[^cite_note-132] As a result, Shenzhen is dubbed by media outlets as "China's [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley)"[^cite_note-Kremkau-2017-133][^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-\:0-12] or the "Silicon Valley of Hardware" for the world.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] Shenzhen hosts [BYD Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYD_Company)[^cite_note-137] and has become a manufacturer of automobiles; it produced 2.9 million automobiles in 2024, overtaking Guangzhou to become the largest car manufacturing city in China.[^cite_note-138]

### Financial services

Shenzhen is home to a number of large financial institutions, such as [China Merchants Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Merchants_Bank)[^cite_note-139] and [Ping An Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping_An_Insurance)[^cite_note-140] and its subsidiary [Ping An Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping_An_Bank).[^cite_note-141] Since the city's establishment as a SEZ, a number of foreign banks had established offices in the city, including [Citibank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank), [HSBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSBC), [Standard Chartered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chartered), and [Bank of East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_East_Asia).[^cite_note-142] In total, the financial industry accounts for 14.5% of the city's nominal GDP in 2015 (254.282 billion RMB), which was a 15.9% increase over the previous year.[^cite_note-2015统计公报2-122] By the end of 2016, the total assets of the financial industry amounted to 12.7 trillion RMB (banking industry assets were 7.85 trillion RMB, security companies assets were 1.25 trillion RMB, and insurance industry assets were 3.6 trillion RMB), making Shenzhen's financial industry the third largest in China.[^cite_note-2016年深圳金融业发展基本情况-143]

Shenzhen is one of the world's [top ten financial centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index) as of 2019, jumping five places to ninth place as determined by "variety of areas of competitiveness, including business environment, human capital, infrastructure, financial sector development and reputation."[^cite_note-144]

### Container port



[Yantian International Container Terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantian_International_Container_Terminals) in the [Yantian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantian_District), one of the [container terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Container_port) of the [Port of Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Shenzhen)

[SF Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SF_Express) and [China International Marine Containers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_International_Marine_Containers) (CIMC) have their headquarters in Shenzhen.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] The [Port of Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Shenzhen) comprises the [Yantian International Container Terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantian_International_Container_Terminals), the [Chiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiwan) Container Terminals, the container terminals of the [Shekou Industrial Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekou_Industrial_Zone), the [China Merchants Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Merchants_Port), and [Shenzhen Haixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Haixing?action=edit\&redlink=1). The Port of Shenzhen is therefore capable of handling a record volume of [containerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containerization). With rising trade increased cargo shipments in 2005, the container port was ranked as the [world's fourth-busiest container port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_container_ports).[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-ShenzhenPort-2016-149]



One of the production floors in Foxconn factory at Shenzhen in the past

High port traffic levels combined with a high urban population make Shenzhen a large *port megacity*.[^cite_note-150] The logistics industry accounts for around 10.1 percent (178.27 billion RMB) of the city's nominal GDP in 2015, which was an increase of 9.4 percent.[^cite_note-2015统计公报2-122] Shenzhen Port's first foreign trade [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain) cargo release platform was launched in 2021.[^cite_note-blockchain-151] Also in 2021, a month-long COVID-related slowdown in processing shipping containers at the port caused a shock to global supply chains.[^cite_note-152]

### Cultural industry

Shenzhen had prioritized the cultural industry in according to the 13th Five-Year Plan, establishing the Shenzhen Fashion Creative Industry Association (深圳市时尚文化创意协会) and planning the 4.6 square-kilometer Dalang Fashion Valley (大浪时尚创意城).[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-龙华新区创建全国时尚服饰产业知名品牌示范区纪-154] On 7 December 2008, [UNESCO](21786641.md) approved Shenzhen's entrance into the [Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network), and awarded the Shenzhen the title of "United Nations Design Capital".[^cite_note-設計之都-155] Altogether, the cultural industry in turn contributes to 5.8 percent (102.116 billion RMB) of Shenzhen's economy in 2015.

Shenzhen-based [video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games_in_China) developer [Game Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Science) released *[Black Myth: Wukong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Myth:_Wukong)*, which achieved significant commercial success on its 20 August 2024 release.[^cite_note-\:03-156] The game is set against the backdrop of the classic novel, *[Journey to the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_to_the_West)*.[^cite_note-\:03-156]

### Real estate

In addition to the four pillar industries that were listed by the municipal government, Shenzhen also has a relatively notable real-estate industry.[^cite_note-157] The real-estate industry altogether contributes to 9.2 percent (162.777 billion RMB) of Shenzhen's economy in 2015, which was an increase of 16.8 percent compared to the previous year.[^cite_note-2015统计公报2-122] Real estate developers such as the [Evergrande Group](16207770.md),[^cite_note-158] [Vanke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanke),[^cite_note-159] and [China Resources Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Resources_Land)[^cite_note-160] are headquartered within the city.

Select-service chains including [Holiday Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_Inn), and [Courtyard by Marriott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtyard_by_Marriott) all have presence in the city amongst local chains, offering both urban hotels in key business districts and beach resorts in [Dameisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dameisha), Xiaomeisha and Jinshawan (Golden Bay). Notably, the [St. Regis Hotels & Resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Regis_Hotels_&_Resorts) occupies the top portion of the [KK100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK100) skyscraper.

Shenzhen's tourism industry is recently expanding under the "13th Five-Year Plan for Tourism Development of Shenzhen" as promoted under the Shenzhen local government.[^cite_note-Shenzhen.gov-2019a-165] In this plan, the tourist industry plans to exceed 200 billion RMB and receive 150 million domestic and foreign tourists by 2020.[^cite_note-Shenzhen.gov-2019b-166] In 2023 an increasing numbers of Hong Kong residents began visiting Shenzhen during weekends, which in turn caused Hong Kong service establishments to face a decline in business.[^cite_note-185]

### Retail

Retail is an important pillar of Shenzhen's [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy). Out of the added value of Shenzhen's tertiary sector of 1.42 trillion RMB (US\$201 billion) in 2018, retail contributed 43% (616.89 billion RMB) of this amount, a 7.6 percent increase compared to last year (601.62 billion RMB).[^cite_note-186] In addition, 10.9% of Shenzhen's [FDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment) is directed towards the wholesale and retail sector.



[COCO Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COCO_Park)

[Huaqiang North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaqiangbei) (华强北) is one of Shenzhen's notable retail areas, being known for having one of the largest [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics) markets in the world.[^cite_note-187] [Luohu Commercial City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luohu_Commercial_City), a commercial complex located adjacent to [Shenzhen Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_railway_station), is noted for having a variety of products that ranges from electronics and [counterfeit goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterfeit_consumer_goods) to tailored suits and curtains.[^cite_note-Springer-2018-182] In addition to Huaqiang North and Luohu Commercial City, Shenzhen has numerous [shopping malls](235723.md) and commercial areas, including [COCO Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COCO_Park) and its branches COCO City and [Longgang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longgang_District,_Shenzhen) COCO Park,[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189] Uniworld (壹方天地),[^cite_note-190] Uniwalk (壹方场),[^cite_note-191] and [Coastal City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_City) (海岸城).[^cite_note-192] Shenzhen is also home to drugstore chain [China Nepstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Nepstar).[^cite_note-193]

"Smart retail", which uses technologies such as [artificial intelligence](1164.md) and [big data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_data) in production, circulation, and sales of consumer goods, has been growing popular within enterprises in Shenzhen.[^cite_note-194] Businesses in Shenzhen are encouraged to use the Internet to develop the consumer market, and new retail projects focus on incorporating technology into the business. In addition, the Shenzhen administration is setting up a new retail industry development fund to promote the use of "smart retail," with the intention of stimulating the economy of Shenzhen and to turn the city into a "new retail" hub.

proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_proper_by_population) in China.[^cite_note-202] Shenzhen is part of the [Pearl River Delta Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Delta) (covering cities such as [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), [Dongguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguan), Before Shenzhen's establishment as a SEZ in 1980, the area was composed mainly of [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_people) and [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_people) people.[^cite_note-204] When the SEZ was established, the city attracted migrants from all around Guangdong, including Hakka, Cantonese, and [Teochew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_people), as well as migrants from [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_China) and [Central Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_China) provinces such as [Hunan](166409.md), [Guangxi](47836950.md), [Jiangxi](173817.md), [Sichuan](65185.md), and [Henan](166408.md).[^cite_note-Netease-2020-205] Most of these migrants live in urban villages called *[chengzhongcun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_village_\(China\))* (城中村; 'village in the city') such as [Baishizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baishizhou) in the [Nanshan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshan_District,_Shenzhen).[^cite_note-206] Shenzhen also has a notable [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_China) minority based in the Nanshan District and the [Futian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futian,_Shenzhen) originating from migrants moving to Shenzhen to work for South Korean companies that had branched out into the city when China had started [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up).[^cite_note-Donghai-207][^cite_note-2ndhometown-208]

Due to Shenzhen's population overshooting the 14.8 million population target for 2016 to 2020, the Shenzhen justice bureau on 25 May 2021 announced it would make it harder to earn a hukou to live in the city.[^cite_note-Msn-2021-198] In regard to the registered population (hukou), Shenzhen experienced an increase of 2.178 million (58.9 percent) registered residents in the city from 2015 to 2020.[^cite_note-Netease-2020-205] The city's permanent population increased by 7,136,088 (68.46 percent) from 2010 to 2020, for an average annual growth rate of 5.35%.[^cite_note-ShenzhenStats-2021-196]

### Religion

According to the Department of Religious Affairs of the Shenzhen Municipal People's Government, the two main religions present in Shenzhen are [Buddhism](3267529.md) and [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism). Every district also has [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) and [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) churches, as well as [mosques](19894.md).[^cite_note-209] Shenzhen also hosts the headquarters of the [Holy Confucian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Confucian_Church), established in 2009.[^cite_note-210]

### Languages

Prior to the establishment of [Special Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Economic_Zone), the indigenous local communities could be divided into [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese) and [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese) speakers,[^cite_note-211] which were two cultural and linguistic sub-ethnic groups vernacular to Guangdong province. Two Cantonese varieties were spoken locally. One was a fairly standard version, known as [standard Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Cantonese). The other, spoken by several villages south of Fuhua Road, was called [Weitou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weitou_dialect).[^cite_note-212] Two or three Hong Kong villages south of the Shenzhen River also speak this dialect. This is consistent with the area settled by people who accompanied the Southern Song court to the south in the late 13th century.[^cite_note-213]

The influx of migrants from other parts of the country has drastically altered the city's linguistic landscape, as Shenzhen has undergone a [language shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_shift) towards [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese), which was both promoted by the [Chinese Central Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Central_Government) as a national [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) and natively spoken by most of the out-of-province immigrants and their descendants.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215] However, in recent years multilingualism has been on the rise as descendants of immigrants of out-of-province Mandarin native speakers have begun to assimilate into the local culture through friends, television and other media.[^cite_note-He-2014-216] Despite the ubiquity of Mandarin Chinese, according to the [*SCMP*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Morning_Post), some Shenzhen residents, Cantonese and non-Cantonese alike, have attempted to revive the Cantonese language as part of Shenzhen's culture.[^cite_note-He-2014-216]

Shenzhen also has several historical buildings based in [traditional Chinese architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_architecture). There are [Hakka walled villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_walled_village) located in the city such as Crane Lake and Gangeng in the [Longgang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longgang_District,_Shenzhen).[^cite_note-Macpherson-2020-229][^cite_note-230] Like typical Hakka walled villages, the architecture of Crane Lake and Gangeng are based around large thick grey walls, narrow alleyways, and courtyards.[^cite_note-Macpherson-2020-229] [Nantou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantou_\(historic_town\)) (or Xin'an) is a historic town located in the present-day [Nanshan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshan_District,_Shenzhen) and has some buildings that date back to the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) such as the Guandi Temple, though most of these traditional buildings have been replaced by modern ones.[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233] [Chiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiwan), located in the Nanshan District, also has several historical buildings, such as the tomb to the last emperor of the [Southern Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Song), [Zhao Bing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Bing) and Tianhou Temple which was built by [Zheng He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He) as an offering to [Mazu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazu) to protect the [Ming treasure fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_treasure_fleet).[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235] There are also several historical forts that had defended the coastline located within the city, such as Dapeng Fortress and Chiwan Left Fort.[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237]

In 2023, the [Marisfrolg Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marisfrolg_Pavilion), featuring an insect exoskeleton design, was completed and became a new landmark in Longhua District.

## Education and research

Main article: [Education in Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Shenzhen)



[Shenzhen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_University)

Before the 1980s, Shenzhen's education system was based on [primary](25058.md) and limited [secondary schooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education). No residents were admitted to a university.[^cite_note-Xiao-1998-238] Since Shenzhen's establishment as a SEZ in the 1980s, migrants poured into the city, and jobs requiring a university education grew.&#x20;



[Southern University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_University_of_Science_and_Technology)

In the mid-1980s, as upper secondary education became popular, there was a need for [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education) institutions in the city.[^cite_note-Xiao-1998-238] Opened in 1983, Shenzhen Normal School, later upgraded to Shenzhen Normal College, trained students to become primary school teachers. Approved by the [State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) in the same year, [Shenzhen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_University) became Shenzhen's first comprehensive full-time higher educational institution.[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240] In 1999, the Shenzhen Municipal Government set up the Shenzhen Virtual University Park in the Science and Technology Park, where teachers from China's top universities taught graduate students.[^cite_note-241] In 2011, the [Southern University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_University_of_Science_and_Technology) was established[^cite_note-szjy-242] followed in 2018 by the [Shenzhen Technology University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Technology_University).[^cite_note-243] Other universities have established campuses in the city, including Tsinghua University, Peking University, the [Chinese University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_University_of_Hong_Kong,_Shenzhen), the [Harbin Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin_Institute_of_Technology_\(Shenzhen\)), and [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_MSU-BIT_University).[^cite_note-244]

As of 2025, Shenzhen also has the [18th largest scientific research output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_scientific_output) of any city in the world.[^cite_note-245]



[Shenzhen Middle School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Middle_School)

Quality primary education in Shenzhen depends on parent's ability to get their kids [hukou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukou). In Shenzhen, this depends on the birth, education and marriage of the parents.[^cite_note-\:20-246][^cite_note-247] In 2020, 33% of Shenzhen residents had hukou.[^cite_note-\:19-248][^cite_note-249] The nine-year compulsory education in Shenzhen is free.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251] For secondary education, parents try to get their children into *Shenzhen's four famous schools*: [Shenzhen Middle School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Middle_School), [Shenzhen Experimental School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Experimental_School), [Shenzhen Foreign Languages School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Foreign_Languages_School), and Shenzhen High School.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-Qianboom-253] These schools have an approximately 10% admission rate for hukou students and 2% for students applying without hukou.[^cite_note-Qianboom-253]

As of 2024, Shenzhen has 14 higher educational institutions, 554 general secondary schools, 359 primary schools, and 1,973 [preschools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool).[^cite_note-2015统计公报3-254] According to Laurie Chen of the *[South China Morning Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Morning_Post)*, Shenzhen, which had 15 million people as of 2019, had not built as many primary and secondary schools for its populace as it should have, compared to similarly developed cities in China.[^cite_note-Cheneyestop-255] Laurie Chen cited the acceptance rate of Shenzhen secondary schools in 2018: 35,000 slots were available for almost 80,000 applicants. She also cited how [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) had 961 primary schools while Shenzhen had only 344 primary schools, as well as how Guangzhou's count of primary school teachers exceeded that of Shenzhen's by 17,000; Chen argued that Guangzhou and Shenzhen have similar populations. In response Shenzhen schools began increasing salaries for prospective teachers.[^cite_note-Cheneyestop-255]

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Shenzhen)

Shenzhen is the second largest transportation hub in [Guangdong](65109.md) and [South China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China), trailing behind the provincial capital of [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou).[^cite_note-256] Shenzhen has a developed extensive public transportation system, covering [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit), buses and [taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab),[^cite_note-transport2016-257] most of which can be accessed by either using a [Shenzhen Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Tong) card or using QR codes generated by [WeChat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WeChat) mini programs.[^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260] Shenzhen is noted for being the first major city worldwide to only use electric buses and taxis.[^cite_note-261] As of 2023, 70% of cars in Shenzhen were electric vehicles.[^cite_note-\:023-262]<sup>: 104 </sup>

### Air



[Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Bao'an_International_Airport) Terminal 3

&#x20;Regarding air transport, Shenzhen is served by its own [Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Bao'an_International_Airport) and the neighboring [Hong Kong International Airport](248457.md) (HKIA). Located 35 kilometres (22 miles) from the center of the city, Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport serves as the main hub for passenger airlines [Shenzhen Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Airlines)[^cite_note-263] and [Donghai Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donghai_Airlines)[^cite_note-264] and a main hub for cargo airlines [Jade Cargo International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade_Cargo_International),[^cite_note-265] [SF Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SF_Airlines),[^cite_note-266] and [UPS Airlines](9704675.md).[^cite_note-autogenerated22-267] Together, Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport handled 49,348,950 passengers, 355,907 aircraft, and 1,218,502.2 cargo in 2018, making it the [5th busiest airport in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_China) in terms of passenger traffic and the 4th busiest airport in the country in terms of aircraft and cargo traffic.[^cite_note-268] In addition to flying through Bao'an International Airport, ticketed passengers can also take ferries from the [Shekou Cruise Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekou_Cruise_Centre) and the [Fuyong Ferry Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuyong_Ferry_Terminal) to the [Skypier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skypier) at Hong Kong International Airport.[^cite_note-269] There are also coach bus services connecting Shenzhen with HKIA.[^cite_note-270]



The [Shenzhen Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Metro) is the sixth [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system in mainland China and second such system in Guangdong.

### Metro

The [Shenzhen Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Metro) serves as the city's rapid transit system. The system in 2022 reaches 419 kilometres (260 miles) of route operating on 12 lines with 290 stations. By 2030 the network is planned to be 8 express and 24 non-express lines totalling 1142 kilometres of trackage.[^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273] The average daily metro ridership in 2021 is 5.99 million passengers. The metro also operates a [tram system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Tram) in the [Longhua District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhua_District,_Shenzhen).[^cite_note-274]

Due to its proximity to Hong Kong, Shenzhen has the largest number of entry and exit ports, the largest number of entry and exit personnel, and the largest traffic volume in China.[^cite_note-szjt-308] Shenzhen is busiest in China when it comes to border crossings, with people entering and exiting the country through the city and Hong Kong reaching 239 million in 2015.[^cite_note-309] In the same year, a total of 15.5 million vehicles crossed the border in Shenzhen, a 0.4% increase of last year. Border crossing ports include the [Shenzhen Bay Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Bay_Port), [Futian Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futian_Port), [Huanggang Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanggang_Port), [Man Kam To Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Kam_To_Control_Point), and [Luohu Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luohu_Port).

Multiple ports on the part of the coastline of Shenzhen constitute the Shenzhen Port. In 2019, Shenzhen had 211 international container routes, and the container throughput of the entire Shenzhen port reached nearly 25.77 million boxes in 2019, ranking fourth in the world. Yantian Port is the busiest port among Shenzhen ports and the main foreign trade channel in South China in the mid-term.[^cite_note-310]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Shenzhen)



A light show in the [Futian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futian,_Shenzhen), May 2019

As Shenzhen is located in [Guangdong](65109.md), the city historically had a Cantonese culture before its transition to a SEZ.[^cite_note-311] The competitive culture that the city promotes among the youth have also used the term "[Shenzhen speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_speed)", which resulted from the fast construction of the tallest building in Shenzhen. The term also describes a period of constant competition, quick changes, and high-efficiency.[^cite_note-312]

In 2003 the municipal government announced plans to turned Shenzhen into a cultural city by promoting design, animation, and library construction.[^cite_note-313] The municipal government also intends to develop the city's cultural industry in accordance to the [13th Five-Year Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Five-Year_Plan), establishing the [Shenzhen Fashion Creative Industry Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Fashion_Creative_Industry_Association?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the 4.6-square-kilometre (1.8 sq mi) [Dalang Fashion Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalang_Fashion_Valley?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-314][^cite_note-龙华新区创建全国时尚服饰产业知名品牌示范区纪-154]

Shenzhen's cultural industry specializes in being one of the largest [handicraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicraft) manufacturers in China,[^cite_note-315] and is also an industry center for [oil painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_painting) in bases such as [Dafen Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafen_Village).[^cite_note-316] Shenzhen also hosts the Shenzhen International Cultural Fair which specializes as an expo for the world's cultural industries, with the first expo being in November 2004.[^cite_note-317][^cite_note-318] As a result of these developments, Shenzhen was awarded by [UNESCO](21786641.md) the title of "United Nations Design Capital" and was accepted entry into the [Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) on 7 December 2008.[^cite_note-設計之都-155]



[He Xiangning Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_Xiangning_Art_Museum)

As part of turning Shenzhen into a cultural city, the municipal government established the "Library City" (图书馆之城) concept in 2003.[^cite_note-319] The plan would create a library network within the city through library construction, service improvement, and create a comfortable reading environment. By the end of 2015, Shenzhen has 620 [public libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_library), including 3 city-level public libraries, 8 district-level public libraries, and 609 grassroots libraries. Notable libraries include the [Shenzhen Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Library) and the Shenzhen Children's Library.[^cite_note-320] Shenzhen also has bookstores, with the most notable being [Shenzhen Book City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Book_City) in the [Futian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futian,_Shenzhen).[^cite_note-321] With an operating area of 42,000 square metres (450,000 ft<sup>2</sup>), it claimed to be the largest bookstore of Asia at the time of its opening. Shenzhen has a number of museums and art galleries,[^cite_note-322][^cite_note-323] such as the [Shenzhen Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Museum),[^cite_note-\:Chatwin-38]<sup>: 111 </sup> the Shenzhen Art Museum, the Shekou Maritime Museum, the Longgang Museum of Hakka Culture, [Shenzhen Museum of Contemporary Art and Urban Planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Museum_of_Contemporary_Art_and_Urban_Planning), and the [He Xiangning Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_Xiangning_Art_Museum). Shenzhen also has a few theaters, notably the [Shenzhen Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Concert_Hall), the Shenzhen Grand Theater, and the Shenzhen Poly Theater.[^cite_note-324]



[Shenzhen Middle Schools Summer Uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_school_uniform)

Shenzhen has a citywide standardized [school uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_school_uniform) for primary and secondary schools. The uniforms feature a blue and white color scheme, with the colors reversed for boys and girls. This well-known uniform set is a representative of Chinese student uniforms. It is often worn by internet celebrities and anime characters.[^cite_note-325]

## Food



A [Cantonese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_cuisine) restaurant in the [Luohu District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luohu_District)

The main cuisine of Shenzhen is [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_cuisine).[^cite_note-326] Due to the recent growth of migrants to the city, Shenzhen also hosts a diverse array of cuisines, including [Teochew cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_cuisine), [Hakka cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_cuisine), [Sichuan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_cuisine), [Shanghai cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_cuisine), and [Hunan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_cuisine).[^cite_note-Lanyon-2014-327]

The [Yantian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantian_District) is known for its Teochew-based and Hakka-based seafood, with restaurants lined up along the coastline.

[Street food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_food) such as [Xinjiang cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_cuisine), and [black sesame soups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_sesame_soup), can be found in Xijie Street and the [urban village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_village_\(China\)) of [Baishizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baishizhou).[^cite_note-Lanyon-2014-327]

[Shekou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekou) area in [Nanshsan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshan,_Shenzhen) is the first opening up area in China, which has a lot of local restaurants that opened for many years, that you can eat Desserts, Chicken Pot, Goose, Sea food, also western food.[^cite_note-328]

In regard to food chains, the first [McDonald's](2480627.md) restaurant in mainland China opened for business in Shenzhen [Luohu District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luohu,_Shenzhen) on 8 October 1990 providing the city American fast food.[^cite_note-329] Now, one can find McDonald's everywhere in the city.

Shenzhen also has its own [tea culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_tea_culture).[^cite_note-330] Shenzhen is home to the [Hey Tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hey_Tea) chain of tea shops, which provides a variety of cheese and fruit teas.[^cite_note-331][^cite_note-332]

In 2020, Shenzhen passed legislation banning the consumption of cats, dogs, and wildlife, becoming the first city in China to enact a law that mandatorily prohibits the eating of cats and dogs.[^cite_note-333]

## Nightlife



[COCO Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COCO_Park) at night

Shenzhen has a prominent [nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife) culture, with most of the activity centered in the entertainment complexes of [COCO Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COCO_Park) and [Shekou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekou),[^cite_note-Lanyon-2014-327] with the former being referred by the *[South China Morning Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_Morning_Post)* (*SCMP*) as "Shenzhen's answer to [Lan Kwai Fong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lan_Kwai_Fong)".[^cite_note-BenKwok-334][^cite_note-335] There are many bars and clubs in the city, mostly unregulated, that stay open till the morning. [Tunnel raves](43488.md), referred by the *SCMP* as "a Shenzhen nightlife staple", have earned a reputation in the world, though they are often cracked down by police. Police has also cracked down on [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution) and [pornography](5710507.md), which were elements of nightlife entertainment in Shenzhen, with one of the most prominent operations being centered in Shazui (沙嘴村) in the [Futian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futian,_Shenzhen) in the mid-2000s, resulting in closures of entertainment businesses and a decrease of foreign tourists in that area of the city.[^cite_note-336]

## Environment

### Parks and beaches

Main article: [List of parks in Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parks_in_Shenzhen)



View of the [China Resources Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Resources_Headquarters) from [Shenzhen Bay Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Bay_Park)

Shenzhen has an extensive three-level public park system that was established in 2006, which categorizes parks as natural parks, urban parks, and community parks. By 2019, the city had 1,090 parks covering about 39,320 hectares, including 33 natural parks, 152 urban parks and 905 community parks. According to state-owned news outlet [Xinhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhua_News_Agency), Shenzhen plans to build and renovate over 40 parks per year, bringing the number of parks in the city to 1,500 by 2035.[^cite_note-351][^cite_note-352] [Lianhuashan Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianhuashan_Park) is located on the territory of 150 hectares in the [Futian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futian,_Shenzhen). At the top of its Lotus Hill is a six-metre bronze statue of [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping).[^cite_note-\:Chatwin-38]<sup>: 111 </sup> Wutongshan National Park is spread around the [mountain of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wutong_Mountain) in the Luohu District. From the observation deck, there is a view of the Shenzhen skyline as well as Hong Kong and the surrounding bay, and on the next peak there is a transmission tower of a local television station.[^cite_note-353]

[Shenzhen Bay Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Bay_Park), located along the city's coastline along [Shenzhen Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Bay,_China), opened in 2011, which included the nearby Mangrove Park. There are several thematic recreation areas and attractions, and along the 9-kilometer-long coastal strip there is an embankment.[^cite_note-354] The Mangrove Ecopark was established in 2000 in the Futian District and at that time was the smallest national park in China. A large group of birds migrate to the ecopark in the [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) on an area of 20.6 hectares in a 9-kilometer coastal zone of the Shenzhen Bay.[^cite_note-355]



[Dameisha Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dameisha_Beach)

Shenzhen Bay Park is connected to the Dashahe Park (大沙河公园, 'big sand river'), located in Nanshan District, it follows the Dashahe River.[^cite_note-356] Other notable parks in Shenzhen include the Shenzhen Garden Flower Exposition Center, [Shenzhen Safari Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Safari_Park),[^cite_note-357] Xili Lake Resort,[^cite_note-358] and Yangtai Mountain Fountain Park. Shenzhen also has several beaches: [Dameisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dameisha_Beach) (大梅沙; 'big mesa') and [Xiaomeisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaomeisha?action=edit\&redlink=1) (小梅沙; 'small mesa') in the [Yantian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantian_District), and Jinshawan (金沙湾; 'golden sands bay'), Nan'ao (南澳; 'southern inlet'), and [Xichong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xichong,_Shenzhen) (西冲) in [Dapeng Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapeng_Peninsula) (in the vicinity of [Dapeng New District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapeng_New_District), which is administered by the [Longgang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longgang_District,_Shenzhen)).[^cite_note-Karacs-2017-181]

### Pollution

See also: [Pollution in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution_in_China)

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Shenzhen achieved an average [air quality index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_quality_index) (AQI) score of 44.8 μg/m<sup>3</sup> and daily AQI score of 19 μg/m<sup>3</sup>. Out of ten Chinese mega-cities, Shenzhen recorded the lowest in average PM<sub>2.5</sub> concentration (22.5 μg/m<sup>3</sup>), average PM<sub>10</sub> concentration (37.7 μg/m<sup>3</sup>), average carbon monoxide concentration (0.6 μg/m<sup>3</sup>), and average nitrogen dioxide concentration (21.9 μg/m<sup>3</sup>).[^cite_note-359] Swiss environmental technology company [IQAir](31884673.md) attributed most of the pollution in Shenzhen to stem from the engineering industry, continued use of coal, and traffic.[^cite_note-360]

In 2014, Shenzhen experienced severe water pollution in the city's rivers and waterways, with 173 of the 310 rivers considered to be in "critical" condition and four rivers: the Maozhou, Guanlan, Longgang and Pingshan Rivers, to be the most polluted out of all rivers in the Pearl River Delta. The pollutants in the river consisted mainly of ammonia, phosphorus, and nitrogen. In response, the city conducted a campaign to restore the city's rivers by building more water pipes and sewage treatment plants.[^cite_note-SCMorningPost-2014-361][^cite_note-Shenzhen.gov-2021b-362]

### Environmental protection



A [BYD](1461760.md) electric taxi in Shenzhen

From 2000 to 2014, Shenzhen spent 30 billion RMB to restore the city's rivers from water pollution, which some were considered at the time to be the most polluted in the Pearl River Delta. The city had constructed 33 sewage treatment plants and laid almost 4,300 kilometres (2,700 mi) of sewage pipes.[^cite_note-SCMorningPost-2014-361] By 2020, the city laid an additional 3,274 kilometres (2,034 mi) of water pipelines and completed 13,793 pipeline renovation projects in urban villages and housing estates.[^cite_note-Shenzhen.gov-2021b-362]

In 2009, Shenzhen was chosen as one of thirteen cities to pilot a national new-energy vehicle program. In 2017, Shenzhen offered 3.3 billion RMB in subsidies in electric buses and the construction of charging facilities. In mid-2018, the city made major headlines for being the first city to roll an all-electric public bus fleet. In the same year, more than half of the city's taxi fleet are electric, with the goal to turn the fleet all-electric.[^cite_note-363][^cite_note-364][^cite_note-365][^cite_note-366] By early 2019, Shenzhen rolled out an all-electric taxi fleet, with 99% of taxis now electric-powered.[^cite_note-367]

In late 2019, Shenzhen launched a garbage classification program in which waste is to be sorted in four categories: recyclables, kitchen waste, hazardous waste, and other waste.[^cite_note-368] Residents who follow the guidelines will be given cash while those who do not would be fined by the government.[^cite_note-369][^cite_note-370]

## Relations with Hong Kong



The [Shenzhen Bay Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Bay_Bridge) forms part of the Shenzhen Bay Port crossing, connecting [Dongjiaotou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongjiaotou) in Shenzhen with Ngau Hom Shek in Hong Kong.

The area encompassed by Shenzhen and [Hong Kong](13404.md) formerly belonged to [Bao'an County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bao'an_County) in imperial times. After the [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) defeat in the [Second Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Opium_War), the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) seized [Hong Kong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Island) and was leased the [New Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Territories), forming the modern-day boundaries between Hong Kong and Shenzhen (the successor to Bao'an).[^cite_note-szmc2-21] The two cities are separated by two bays: [Shenzhen Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Bay,_China) and [Mirs Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirs_Bay), and a river: [Sham Chun River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_Chun_River). According to then-[Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_Hong_Kong) member [Leung Chun-ying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leung_Chun-ying), the two cities' close relationship can be due to the close distance between the two, similar economic systems, differences in wages and price levels, and that the two cities have different systems compared to other Chinese cities, with Hong Kong embracing the [one country, two systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_country,_two_systems) principle while Shenzhen is a [SEZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zones_of_China).[^cite_note-380]

From the establishment of Shenzhen as a SEZ in 1980 to 2007, Hong Kong has been Shenzhen's largest trade partner, with exports to Hong Kong accounted for 46.6% of Shenzhen's total exports.[^cite_note-381] In 2015, the total import and export volume of Shenzhen Port to Hong Kong was 1.1 trillion RMB.[^cite_note-382] Both cities had established the [Qianhai Shenzhen-Hong Kong Modern Service Industries Cooperation Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianhai) within the [Nanshan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanshan_District,_Shenzhen) which is a [free-trade zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-trade_zone) that mirrors the economic policies of both cities and to bring Hong Kong closer to mainland China.[^cite_note-nzcta.co.nz3-163][^cite_note-383][^cite_note-SCMorningPost-2021-384] [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent) estimated that by 2020, Qianhai is expected to create a total output value of 150 billion RMB, with an output of 10 billion RMB per square kilometer.[^cite_note-385] As of 23 February 2021, Qianhai has a total of 11,325 firms from Hong Kong.[^cite_note-SCMorningPost-2021-384]

As of September 2016, there are nine crossing points on the boundary between Shenzhen and Hong Kong, among which six are land connections. From west to east these include the [Shenzhen Bay Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Bay_Port), [Futian Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futian_Port), [Huanggang Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanggang_Port), [Man Kam To Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Kam_To_Control_Point), [Luohu Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luohu_Port) and [Shatoujiao Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatoujiao_Port). On either sides of each of these ports of entry are road and/or rail transportation.[^cite_note-386][^cite_note-HKG_Monthly_Digest_of_Statistics-387]
