**Mohammed Ali Ahmed al-Haddad** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): محمد علي أحمد الحداد; 1967 – 23 December 2025) was a Libyan field marshal who served as the [Chief of the General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_General_Staff) of the [Libyan Army](35021571.md) from 2020 until his death in [a plane crash](81917460.md) in 2025. The highest-ranking Libyan officer in the [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)) (GNU), al-Haddad played a key role in efforts to reunify Libya's military. He also expanded military relations with other countries, both regionally and internationally.

## Early life and education

Al-Haddad was born in [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata) in 1967. He graduated from a military college in [Tripoli](31232.md) in 1987.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:8-2]

## Military career

Al-Haddad held many positions in the Libyan Army before the first [Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_civil_war_\(2011\)) broke out in 2011. In February 2011, al-Haddad defected from [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md)'s regime and joined the rebel forces after the civil war began, fighting against Gaddafi's forces in the [battle of Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Misrata_\(2011\)).[^cite_note-\:6-3] In 2015, he was given a senior role in the Tripoli military region.[^cite_note-\:8-2] He was also a senior officer of the [Halbous Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/111th_Brigade_Majhfal). Al-Haddad was appointed commander of the Central Military Region by the [Libyan Presidential Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Council_\(Libya\)) on 4 June 2017, while he held the rank of [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel). In September, he was ordered to secure [Tawergha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawergha) and facilitate the return of displaced residents.[^cite_note-\:7-4]

In August 2018, he was appointed to oversee ceasefire talks in [Tripoli](31232.md) regarding the second [Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_civil_war_\(2014%E2%80%932020\)) after [clashes broke out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tripoli_\(2018\)) in the city's south. He oversaw the army's withdrawal from areas affected by the clashes, and returned captured military bases to the forces who occupied them before fighting broke out. On 1 September, he was abducted after a military gathering, but was recovered alive in [Karzaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karzaz,_Libya?action=edit\&redlink=1) after negotiations.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:7-4] In 2019, he played a role in confronting [Libyan National Army](35457665.md) forces in Tripoli during the [Western Libya campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Libya_campaign).[^cite_note-\:6-3]

### Chief of the General Staff (2020–2025)

Following his promotion to lieutenant general, al-Haddad was appointed chief of staff of the [Libyan Army](35021571.md) by prime minister [Fayez al-Sarraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayez_al-Sarraj) in August 2020, officially taking office on 17 September.[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] He became the highest-ranked officer of the internationally recognized Government of National Unity military.[^cite_note-8]

From the time of his inauguration on, he vowed to end division in the military and build an army that could defend the nation's sovereignty.[^cite_note-\:4-9] He led [United Nations](31769.md)-backed talks to unify the army under the "5+5 Joint Military Committee". The military is divided under the larger context of the [Libyan crisis](44791215.md), with a [Tobruk](405871.md)-based [rival government](71621370.md) led by [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md) controlling the military in eastern Libya.[^cite_note-\:3-10][^cite_note-\:2-11][^cite_note-\:1-5] As part of these efforts, he met with his eastern counterpart, [Abdulrazek al-Nadoori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulrazek_al-Nadoori), several times in Tripoli, [Cairo](6293.md), and Tunisia in 2022 to discuss unification.[^cite_note-12] These talks continued in 2023, with meetings between the two being held in [Paris](22989.md).[^cite_note-\:5-13] He also helped rebuild forces in western Libya and integrated local militias into the military.[^cite_note-14]

During his tenure, al-Haddad aimed to expand Libya's international military relations, especially Turkey, making visits to [Ankara](802.md) in 2020, 2024, and 2025.[^cite_note-\:8-2] He visited a Turkish frigate stationed off Libya in August 2021, and later met with Turkish defense minister [Hulusi Akar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulusi_Akar) in Tripoli to widen cooperation between the two states.[^cite_note-\:4-9] He traveled to [Moscow](19004.md) that same year.[^cite_note-\:5-13] In March 2023, he authorized a military pact with Italy on training Libyan special troops. He met with [United States Africa Command](9347800.md) commanders in July 2024 to discuss bolstering border security and unifying the Libyan military. He participated in talks with foreign leaders, including in Cairo and [Rome](25458.md), to gather support for Libya's unification and the creation of a combined European and American force to defend the country's borders.[^cite_note-\:1-5]

## Death

Main article: [Harmony Jets Flight 185](81917460.md)

On 23 December 2025, al-Haddad traveled to [Ankara](802.md), where he was given a formal military ceremony upon arrival. He then held talks with Turkish chief of staff [Selçuk Bayraktaroğlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sel%C3%A7uk_Bayraktaro%C4%9Flu).[^cite_note-15]

Later that day, a [Dassault Falcon 50](3098320.md) jet carrying al-Haddad and seven others crashed near [Kesikkavak](34139920.md), Turkey, shortly after taking off from Ankara, killing all occupants.[^cite_note-\:2-11][^cite_note-16] Libyan prime minister [Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_Dbeibeh) confirmed al-Haddad's death, saying "This grave loss is a great loss for the nation, for the military institution, and for all the people."[^cite_note-17] Three days of [national mourning](2111211.md) were declared by the [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)).[^cite_note-\:3-10] [Libyan National Army](35457665.md) commander [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md) also expressed condolences over his death.[^cite_note-18] Al-Haddad's deputy, [Salaheddine al-Namroush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaheddine_al-Namroush?action=edit\&redlink=1), was temporarily appointed to take his role.[^cite_note-19]

On 27 December 2025, al-Haddad was posthumously promoted to field marshal by the president of the Libyan [Presidential Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Council_\(Libya\)), [Mohamed al-Menfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_al-Menfi).[^cite_note-20] His remains and that of the other victims were repatriated to Libya, and al-Haddad was buried in his native Misrata following official ceremonies in Ankara and Tripoli.[^cite_note-21]
