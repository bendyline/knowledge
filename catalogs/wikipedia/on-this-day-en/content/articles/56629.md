**Bissau** ([/bɪˈsaʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); Portuguese pronunciation: [\[biˈsaw\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese)) is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) and largest city of [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md). As of 2015, it had a population of 492,004.[^cite_note-internetworldstats.com-1] Bissau is located on the [Geba River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geba_River) estuary, off the Atlantic Ocean, and is Guinea-Bissau's major port, as well as the country's administrative and military center.

## Etymology

The term "Bissau" may have come from the name of a clan, N'nssassun, in its plural form Bôssassun.[^cite_note-Nanque-2] Intchassu (Bôssassu) was the name given to the nephew of King Mecau—the first sovereign of the Bissau—son of his sister Pungenhum. Bôssassu formed a clan of the [Papel peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papel_people).[^cite_note-3]

## History

See also: [Timeline of Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Bissau)

From well before the arrival of Europeans to the early 20th century, the island of Bissau was governed as a kingdom inhabited by the [Papel people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papel_people). According to oral tradition, the kingdom was founded by Mecau, the son of the king of [Quinara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinara) ([Guinala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinala)), who moved to the area with his pregnant sister, six wives, and subjects of his father's kingdom. The kingdom was composed of seven clans, descended from the sister and six wives. The Bossassun clan, which descends from the sister, inherited the throne.[^cite_note-Nanque-2] The Kingdom of Bissau was highly stratified.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodney196673-4] The king's coronation involved the practice of binding and beating the king, as the king should know what punishment felt like before administering it, as well as the presentation of a spear, the royal badge of office.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodney196666-5] When the Portuguese began to trade there in the 16th century, the king of Bissau was among the most supportive monarchs of the region. In 1680 Bissau even helped the Portuguese in a conflict with the Papels of Cacheu.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodney1966304-6]

The city was founded in 1687 as a Portuguese trading post.[^cite_note-Europa2001-7] During this same period French activities in the area were increasing. Although the king of Bissau Bacompolco refused them permission to build a fort, he did grant them a trading factory, from which they shipped thousands of slaves, among other things. In response the Portuguese [Conselho Ultramarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conselho_Ultramarino?action=edit\&redlink=1) established the captaincy-general of Bissau, and by 1696 the town had [a fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortaleza_de_S%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_da_Amura), a church, and a hospital.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendyLobban201354-8] It was the main emporium for trade on and south of the [Geba river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geba_river),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodney1966289-9] and was rivaling if not eclipsing [Cacheu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacheu) in importance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodney1966305-10]

Bacompulco died in 1696. King Incinhate emerged from the ensuing succession dispute despite tacit Portuguese opposition, and relations rapidly deteriorated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodney1966313-11] When Captain-General Pinheiro tried to enforce Portugal's monopoly in defiance of the Papel policy of free trade, Incinhate surrounded the incomplete fort and threatened to massacre the inhabitants. Pinheiro later died in Papel custody.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodney1966316-12] Unable to enforce a trading monopoly or collect duties from foreign shipping, the Portuguese soon abandoned the fort.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERodney1966322-13] They returned in 1753 but, faced with determined Papel resistance, were unable to build a new fort and left two years later.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendyLobban201354-8]

The [fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortaleza_de_S%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_da_Amura) was rebuilt by the [Grão Pará and Maranhão Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A3o_Par%C3%A1_and_Maranh%C3%A3o_Company) in 1775 to better project Portuguese power and store more slaves for shipment to Brazil.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendyLobban201354-8] Real control of the area, however, remained in the hands of the Papel kings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendyLobban2013299-14] In 1869, as part of an effort to more efficiently govern the territory, Bissau was raised to the status of commune.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendyLobban201355-15]

The decades on either side of the turn of the 20th century saw fierce resistance on the part of the Papels to colonial 'pacification campaigns.' In 1915, after 30 years of war, the Portuguese under the command of Officer [Teixeira Pinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Teixeira_Pinto) and warlord [Abdul Injai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Injai) defeated the Kingdom of Bissau and permanently incorporated it into [Portuguese Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Guinea).[^cite_note-\:3-16]<sup>: 9 </sup> In 1941 the capital was transferred from [Bolama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolama_\(town\)) to Bissau.[^cite_note-Europa2001-7] 1959 saw the bloody repression of a dockworkers' strike, a key event that pushed the nationalists towards armed resistance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendyLobban201355-15]

After the declaration of independence by the anti-colonial guerrillas of [PAIGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Party_for_the_Independence_of_Guinea_and_Cape_Verde) in 1973, the capital of the rebel territories was declared to be [Madina do Boe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madina_do_Boe), while Bissau remained the colonial capital. The city was attacked in 1968 and 1971 by nationalist forces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendyLobban201356-17] When Portugal granted independence, following the [military coup of 25 April](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution) in Lisbon, Bissau became the capital of the newly independent state.

Bissau was the scene of intense fighting during the beginning and end of the [Guinea-Bissau Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea-Bissau_Civil_War) in 1998 and 1999.[^cite_note-18] Much of the infrastructure was destroyed and most of the population fled. The city rebounded after peace returned, holding more than 25% of the country's population during the 2009 census and witnessing the erection of many new and rehabilitated buildings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendyLobban201355-15]

In 2023, the Turkish electric company [Karpowership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karpowership) cut power to the city, due to an unpaid bill exceeding \$15 million. Power delivery stopped early on the morning of 17 October, and was resumed late the following day, after Guinea-Bissau had made a payment of \$6 million.[^cite_note-19]

## Geography and climate



View of downtown Bissau in the 1960s as the capital of Portuguese Guinea

Bissau is located on the [Geba River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geba_River) estuary, off the Atlantic Ocean. The land surrounding the city is extremely low-lying, and the river is accessible to ocean-going vessels despite its modest discharge for about 80 kilometres (50 mi) beyond the city.

Bissau has a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Aw*), not quite wet enough to qualify as a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) (*Am*) but much wetter than most climates of its type.

Almost no rain falls from November to May, but during the remaining five months of the year, the city receives around 2,000 millimetres (79 in) of rain.

At the 1979 census, Bissau had a population of 109,214. By the 2015 census, Bissau had a population of 492,004.[^cite_note-internetworldstats.com-1]

## Economy

Bissau is the country's largest city, major port, and educational, administrative, industrial and military center. [Peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), [hardwoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardwood), [copra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copra), [palm oil](57561.md), [milk products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_products), and [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber) are the chief products. Bissau is also the main city of the fishing and agricultural industry in the country.[^cite_note-23]

## Transportation

### Air

Bissau is served by [Osvaldo Vieira International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osvaldo_Vieira_International_Airport), the country's sole international airport, which currently offers flights from six different airlines.

### Highways

The main highway connecting Bissau to the rest of the nation and the continent is the [Trans–West African Coastal Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans%E2%80%93West_African_Coastal_Highway). There are also many smaller national highways that connect to other big cities such as [Bafatá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bafat%C3%A1) and [Gabu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabu_\(town\)).

## Education

The main secondary school institutions in Bissau are the [National Lyceum Kwame N'Krumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lyceum_Kwame_N'Krumah)[^cite_note-24] and the Bethel-Bissau Adventist School.[^cite_note-25] The main higher education institutions in the city are the [Amílcar Cabral University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidade_Am%C3%ADlcar_Cabral),[^cite_note-26] the [Catholic University of Guinea Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Guinea_Bissau?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the [Jean Piaget University of Guinea-Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Piaget_University_of_Guinea-Bissau?action=edit\&redlink=1).

The city of Bissau still has two international schools:[^cite_note-27]

- Escola Portuguesa da Guiné-Bissau

- Escola Portuguesa Passo a Passo

## Culture

Attractions include the Portuguese-built [Fortaleza de São José da Amura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortaleza_de_S%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_da_Amura) barracks from the 18th century, containing [Amílcar Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%ADlcar_Cabral)'s [mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum), the Pidjiguiti Memorial to the dockers killed in the Bissau dockers' strike on 3 August 1959, the Guinea-Bissau National Arts Institute, Bissau New Stadium and local beaches.

Many buildings in the city were ruined during the [Guinea-Bissau Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea-Bissau_Civil_War) (1998–1999), including the Guinea-Bissau Presidential Palace and the Bissau French Cultural Centre (now rebuilt), and the city center is still underdeveloped. Because of the large population of Muslims in Bissau, [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan) is also an important celebration.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

## Sports

Football is the most popular sport in the country, as well as in the city. Many teams are based in the city, such as: [UD Internacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UD_Internacional), [SC de Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_de_Bissau), [SC Portos de Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Portos_de_Bissau), [Sport Bissau e Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Bissau_e_Benfica), and [FC Cuntum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Cuntum). Stadiums that are located in the city are [Estádio Lino Correia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Lino_Correia) and [Estádio 24 de Setembro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_24_de_Setembro).

## Religion

The main religions are [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) (50%), then Christian (34%) and [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) (7.9%).[^cite_note-30]

### Places of worship

Among the [places of worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Places_of_worship), [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) mosques are predominant.[^cite_note-31] There are also some Christian churches and temples such as the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Bissau) ([Catholic Church](606848.md)), [Evangelical Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Churches), and the [Universal Church of the Kingdom of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Church_of_the_Kingdom_of_God).
