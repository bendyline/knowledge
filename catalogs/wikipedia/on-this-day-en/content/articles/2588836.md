# List of Category 5 Atlantic hurricanes



[Hurricane Melissa](81393820.md) before making landfall on Jamaica on October 28, 2025. It is the most recent hurricane of this strength to form.

A **Category 5 Atlantic hurricane** is a [tropical cyclone](8282374.md) that reaches Category 5 intensity on the [Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffir%E2%80%93Simpson_hurricane_wind_scale), within the Atlantic Ocean to the north of the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator). They are among the strongest tropical cyclones that can form on Earth, having 1-minute sustained wind speeds of at least 137 [knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_\(unit\)) (254 [km/h](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilometres_per_hour); 158 [mph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_per_hour); 70 [m/s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_per_second)). The United States [National Hurricane Center](255317.md) (NHC) currently estimates that 11 tropical cyclones between 1851 (the first [Atlantic hurricane season](2930244.md) to be included in the official Atlantic [tropical cyclone record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_hurricane_reanalysis)) and 1959 peaked as Category 5 hurricanes. However, because technologies such as [satellite monitoring](1184557.md) were not available until the 1960s, some cyclones may have remained undetected. Since 1960, 33 Atlantic hurricanes have reached Category 5.

## Background



Tracks of all known Category 5 Atlantic hurricanes between 1851 and 2025. This map does not reflect the downgrade of [Edith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Edith_\(1971\)) to Category 4.

Within the Atlantic Ocean to the north of the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator), hurricanes are officially monitored by the United States's [National Hurricane Center](255317.md) (NHC), however, other meteorological services, such as [Météo-France](4732627.md), the United Kingdom's [Met Office](319671.md) and [Environment Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_Canada) also monitor the basin. Within the region, a Category 5 hurricane is a [tropical cyclone](8282374.md) which reaches Category 5 status on the [Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffir%E2%80%93Simpson_hurricane_wind_scale), that is, a tropical cyclone that has 1-minute mean maximum sustained wind speeds of 137 knots (254 km/h; 158 mph; 70 m/s) or greater at 10 metres (32.8 ft) above ground.[^cite_note-SSHWS-1]

No Category 5 hurricanes were observed officially before 1924. It can be presumed that earlier storms reached Category 5 strength over open waters, but the strongest winds were not measured. Although the [anemometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemometer), a device used for measuring wind speed, was invented in 1846, during major hurricane strikes, the instruments were often blown away or damaged, leaving the hurricane's peak intensity unrecorded. For example, as the [Great Beaufort Hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Beaufort_Hurricane) of 1879 struck North Carolina, the anemometer cups were blown away when indicating 138 mph (222 km/h).[^cite_note-Hudgins-2]

As of May 2018, a [reanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_hurricane_reanalysis) of weather data was ongoing by researchers who may upgrade or downgrade Atlantic hurricanes.[^cite_note-reanalysis-3] For example, the [1825 Santa Ana hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1825_Santa_Ana_hurricane) is suspected to have reached Category 5 strength.[^cite_note-4] Furthermore, [paleotempestological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleotempestology) research aims to identify past major hurricanes by comparing sedimentary evidence of recent and past hurricane strikes. For example, a "giant hurricane" significantly more powerful than [Hurricane Hattie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hattie) (Category 5) has been identified in [Belizean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize) sediment, having struck the region sometime [before 1500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-1600_Atlantic_hurricane_seasons).[^cite_note-Mccloskey2009-5]

## Records



Track of an [October Category 5 hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Cuba_hurricane) that hit [Cuba](5042481.md), [Florida](18933066.md), and [The Bahamas](3451.md) in [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Atlantic_hurricane_season). It was the first hurricane to be officially recognized as a Category 5 on the [modern-day Saffir–Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale](255313.md).

Officially, the decade with the most Category 5 hurricanes is the 2000s, with eight Category 5 hurricanes having occurred: Isabel (2003), Ivan (2004), Emily (2005), Katrina (2005), Rita (2005), Wilma (2005), Dean (2007), and Felix (2007). The previous decades with the most Category 5 hurricanes were the 1930s and 1960s, with six occurring between 1930 and 1939.[^cite_note-HURDAT-6] The most Category 5 hurricanes recorded in a single season is four, in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Atlantic_hurricane_season), followed by [2025](65411671.md), which recorded three. No other seasons had more than two Category 5 hurricanes. The most consecutive years to feature at least one Category 5 hurricane each is four, both from [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Atlantic_hurricane_season) to [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Atlantic_hurricane_season) and from [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Atlantic_hurricane_season) to [2025](65411671.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]



A collage of all Category 5 Atlantic hurricanes since 1980.

Ten Atlantic hurricanes—[Camille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Camille), [Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Allen), [Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Andrew), [Isabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Isabel), [Ivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Ivan), [Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dean), [Felix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Felix), [Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irma), [Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Maria), and [Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Milton)—reached Category 5 intensity on more than one occasion; that is, by reaching Category 5 intensity, weakening to a Category 4 status or lower, and then becoming a Category 5 hurricane again. Camille, Andrew, Dean, Felix, Irma, and Maria each attained Category 5 status twice during their lifespans. Allen, Isabel, Ivan, and Milton reached Category 5 intensity on three occasions. The [1932 Cuba hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Cuba_hurricane) holds the record for the most time spent as a Category 5 hurricane (although it took place before satellite or aircraft reconnaissance, so this record may be somewhat suspect). [Irma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irma) holds the record for the longest continuous span as a Category 5 storm in the satellite era.[^cite_note-HURDAT-6][^cite_note-Andrew_reanalysis-9]

Of the 44 Category 5 Atlantic hurricanes on record, 2 have been recorded in July, 8 in August, 25 in September, 8 in October, and 1 in November. There have been no officially recorded June or off-season Category 5 hurricanes.[^cite_note-HURDAT-6]

July and August Category 5 hurricanes reached their high intensities in either the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), Caribbean, or the southwestern Atlantic.[^cite_note-HURDAT-6][^cite_note-climatology-10]

September sees the most Category 5 hurricanes, with over half of the total. This coincides with the climatological peak of the Atlantic hurricane season, which occurs in early September.[^cite_note-September-11] September Category 5s reached their strengths in any of the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md), Caribbean, and open Atlantic. These places are where September tropical cyclones are likely to form.[^cite_note-climatology-10] Many of these hurricanes are either [Cape Verde hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde_hurricane), which develop their strength due to a long track over warm waters,[^cite_note-CapeVerde-12] or else intensify over the warm [Loop Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loop_Current) in the Gulf of Mexico.

All but two of the Category 5 hurricanes in October and November (the exceptions being [Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Michael) and [Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Milton)) reached their intensities in the western Caribbean, a region that Atlantic hurricanes strongly gravitate toward late in the season.[^cite_note-climatology-10] This is due to the climatology of the area, which sometimes has a high-altitude [anticyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticyclone) that promotes rapid intensification late in the season, as well as warm waters.

### Other systems

The [1947 Fort Lauderdale hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Fort_Lauderdale_hurricane) and Hurricanes [Dog (1950)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dog_\(1950\)), [Easy (1951)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Easy_\(1951\)), [Cleo (1958)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Cleo_\(1958\)), [Donna (1960)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Donna), [Ethel (1960)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Ethel_\(1960\)), [Carla (1961)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Carla) and [Edith (1971)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Edith_\(1971\)) were all originally estimated to have Category 5 sustained wind speeds. However, later systematic studies by the [Atlantic hurricane reanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_hurricane_reanalysis) project found that the wind speeds associated with these systems were overestimated and downgraded them to Category 4 (Category 3 in the case of Ethel). Separately, [Hurricane Iota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Iota) (2020) was operationally considered to be a Category 5 hurricane, with estimated 1-minute sustained wind speeds of 160 mph (260 km/h).[^cite_note-Iota_TCR-73] However, during their routine post-analysis best track process after the season (rather than the historical reanalysis project), the NHC downgraded Iota to a Category 4 hurricane after determining that there was a high bias in wind speeds derived from the [Stepped Frequency Microwave Radiometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_radiometer) instrument.[^cite_note-Iota_TCR-73]

## Landfalls



[Hurricane Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Michael) as it was making landfall as a Category 5 hurricane in 2018

Nearly all Atlantic Category 5 hurricanes have made [landfall](5016402.md) at some location while a tropical or subtropical cyclone. This is primarily because of their proximity to land in the Caribbean and Gulf of Mexico, where the usual synoptic weather patterns carry them towards land, as opposed to the westward, oceanic mean track of Eastern Pacific hurricanes.[^cite_note-meanTrack-74] 19 of the storms made landfall at least once while at Category 5 intensity;[^cite_note-HURDAT-6] 2007 and 2017 are the only years in which two storms made landfall at this intensity.[^cite_note-HURDAT-6] All but six landfalling systems (the [1944 Great Atlantic hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_Great_Atlantic_hurricane), [Carol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Carol_\(1953\)), [Esther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Esther), [Mitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Mitch), [Isabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Isabel), and [Erin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Erin_\(2025\))) did so at major hurricane strength.

Many of these systems made landfall shortly after weakening from a Category 5 hurricane. This weakening can be caused by dry air near land, shallower waters due to [shelving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf), interaction with land, [eyewall replacement cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyewall_replacement_cycle), increased vertical wind shear, or cooler waters near shore.[^cite_note-Katrina_TCR-49]

The only Atlantic Category 5 hurricanes that did not make landfall while a tropical or subtropical cyclone were [Hurricane Lorenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Lorenzo_\(2019\)), which still brought hurricane-force winds to the [Azores](3226.md); [Hurricane Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Lee_\(2023\)), which still made landfall as an [extratropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_cyclone) in [Nova Scotia](21184.md); and [Hurricane Humberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Humberto_\(2025\)), which still caused increased surf along the [East Coast of the United States](89126.md) and minor impacts to [Bermuda](3460.md). These three storms are therefore not included in the table below.
