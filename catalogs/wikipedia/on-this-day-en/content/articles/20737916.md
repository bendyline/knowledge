**Bhumjaithai Party** (**BJT**; [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): พรรคภูมิใจไทย; [RTGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_General_System_of_Transcription): *Phak Phumchaithai*, lit. 'Thai Proud Party') is a major [conservative](6675.md) [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism) [political party in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party_in_Thailand). It was founded on 5 November 2008, in anticipation of the 2 December 2008 [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Thailand) ruling that dissolved its *de facto* predecessor, the [Neutral Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Democratic_Party) (PMT), along with the [People's Power Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Power_Party_\(Thailand\)) (PPP) and the [Thai Nation Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Nation_Party). After those dissolutions, former members of the [PMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Democratic_Party) and the [Friends of Newin Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_Newin_Group) (KPW) faction of the PPP switched to this party. It currently heads a majority government.

Bhumjaithai has a [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism) platform, with some policies drawn from [Thaksin Shinawatra](360815.md)'s populist [Thai Rak Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Rak_Thai) party and its second incarnation, the PPP.[^cite_note-13] The party has a strong base in [Buriram Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buriram_Province).

## History



The first logo of the Bhumjaithai Party (2008–2025)

On 15 December 2008, the party endorsed the [Democrat Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrat_Party_\(Thailand\)), forming a six-party coalition government under Prime Minister [Abhisit Vejjajiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhisit_Vejjajiva). The party's "*de facto*" leader and power broker behind joining the Democrat-led coalition is [Newin Chidchob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newin_Chidchob). Due to his role as an executive of the PPP predecessor party, the [Thai Rak Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Rak_Thai) party, which was dissolved in 2007, he became ineligible to be a party member for five years. It is alleged that army commander and co-leader of the [2006 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Thai_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), general [Anupong Paochinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anupong_Paochinda), coerced the MPs of the [Friends of Newin Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_Newin_Group) faction in the PPP to endorse a Democrat Party-led coalition. This secured enough parliamentary votes to allow Abhisit to be elected prime minister. Bhumjaithai was the second largest coalition partner in the [Abhisit government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhisit_cabinet), supplying the ministers of [Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_\(Thailand\)), [Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Commerce_\(Thailand\)), and [Interior](20545894.md) ([Chavarat Charnvirakul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavarat_Charnvirakul)), and four deputy ministers.

For the [general election on 3 July 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Thai_general_election), Bhumjaithai forged an alliance with coalition partner [Chartthaipattana Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartthaipattana_Party).[^cite_note-14] The party aimed at winning as many as 111 seats in the new parliament.[^cite_note-15] During the campaign, a main BJT canvasser, Suban Chiraphanwanit, was shot dead.[^cite_note-16] Eventually, BJT won 34 of the 500 seats in the House of Representatives, which observers have interpreted as disappointing.[^cite_note-17] Subsequently, the party's [Matchima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Democratic_Party)-faction, led by [Somsak Thepsuthin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somsak_Thepsuthin), tried to join the Pheu-Thai-led coalition government of Prime Minister-designate [Yingluck Shinawatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yingluck_Shinawatra), despite the party's ruling out cooperation with Pheu Thai before the elections. The Pheu Thai Party rejected the participation of BJT members.[^cite_note-18]

On 14 October 2012, Anuthin Charnvirakul was elected the new leader of the Bhumjaithai Party during the party's general assembly to elect a new 11-member executive committee. He replaced his father [Chavarat Charnvirakul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavarat_Charnvirakul), who had resigned. Also elected were Saksayam Chidchob as the new secretary-general, Nathee Ratchakijprakarn as treasurer, Sora-ath Klinprathum as an executive committee member and party advisory chairman, and Supamas Issarapakdi as party spokeswoman.[^cite_note-19]

### 2025

See also: [2025 Thai political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Thai_political_crisis)

On 18 June 2025 the Bhumjaithai Party announced its withdrawal from Thailand's ruling coalition government led by the [Pheu Thai Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheu_Thai_Party). This decision followed the leak of a phone conversation between Prime Minister [Paetongtarn Shinawatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paetongtarn_Shinawatra) and former Cambodian Prime Minister [Hun Sen](393779.md), which raised concerns about national sovereignty amid ongoing border tensions between Thailand and Cambodia.[^cite_note-20] In a statement by the Bhumjaithai party, all party ministers have submitted their resignations to the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paetongtarn_Shinawatra), effective 19 June.[^cite_note-21]

## Ideology

Bhumjaithai Party combines elements of [conservatism](6675.md), [populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism), and [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism) in its policy platform. The party's approach to governance is characterised by welfare-oriented developmental policies while maintaining traditional conservative values, particularly regarding the [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Thailand).[^cite_note-bloomberg-20230518-5]
