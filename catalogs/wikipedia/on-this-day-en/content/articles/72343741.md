**Betssy Betzabet Chávez Chino** (born 3 June 1989) is a Peruvian attorney and politician, who served as [prime minister of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Council_of_Ministers_of_Peru) from 26 November until 7 December 2022, when she resigned.

She previously was [Minister of Labour and Employment Promotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Labour_and_Promotion_of_Employment_\(Peru\)), from October 2021 until May 2022, when a censure measure was approved against her.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:1-2] She later held the position of [Minister of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(Peru\)), before being appointed prime minister.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Chávez was a member of Congress from July 2021 and was suspended in March 2023.

## Early life and pre-political career

Chávez was born on 3 June 1989 in [Ciudad Nueva District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Nueva_District), [Tacna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacna), [Peru](170691.md).

### Education

Chavez studied law at [Jorge Basadre Grohmann National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Basadre_Grohmann_National_University), and was a student leader, holding positions in the student government. She graduated from law school in 2016, and holds a master's degree, with focus on Constitutional Law, from [José Carlos Mariátegui University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Carlos_Mari%C3%A1tegui_University?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

### Career

Chávez worked as a lawyer in the Regional Government of Tacna (2020), a technician and assistant in the [Congress of the Republic](1571007.md) (2017–2020) and head of practices at the Jorge Basadre Grohmann University School of Law.[^cite_note-7]

## Political career

Chávez said that her interest in politics arose from her father's work as a social leader for families that settled in the northern cone of Tacna.

In 2013, she was a candidate for the regional council of Tacna, for [Alliance for Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Progress_\(Peru\)) but she was not elected.

### Congresswoman

She was elected congresswoman in the [2021 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Peruvian_general_election) with 8,472 votes; she represented Tacna, for the [Free Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Peru) party. She took her oath as congresswoman on 27 July that year. She is also part of the [Democratic Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Peru) parliamentary group.[^cite_note-8]

### Ministries

#### Work and Employment Promotion

On 6 October 2021 she was appointed and sworn in by President Pedro Castillo, as Minister of Labour and Promotion of Employment of Peru.[^cite_note-\:1-2]

In April 2022, various media outlets denounced that the minister had authorized a strike by air traffic controllers, which caused the cancellation of internal and international flights on the occasion of Holy Week. After that, she was questioned by the Congress of the Republic of Peru on 13 May. On the 19th of that month, in Congress, various congressmen presented a motion of censure against Minister Chávez. The following 26, Congress approved the censorship with 71 votes in favor due to *her inability to manage and negligence in her performance.*[^cite_note-9]

#### Culture

On 5 August of the same year, she was appointed and inaugurated by President Castillo, as Minister of Culture of Peru. In statements to the press, the new minister mentioned that having been censured as Minister of Labour does not prevent her from assuming the portfolio of Culture. Minister [Diana Miloslavich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Miloslavich) said at the time: *"Her censorship does not mean that she does not do an excellent job",* and Minister Alejandro Salas stated: *"She is a great professional... and, if at any moment (the president) considers that she should be premier"*. She held that position until the following November 25, being replaced by Silvana Robles Araujo.[^cite_note-10]

### President of the Council of Ministers



[Castillo](49637220.md) swearing in Chávez as Prime Minister

On 25 November 2022 she was appointed and sworn in by President [Pedro Castillo](49637220.md) as [President of the Council of Ministers of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Council_of_Ministers_of_Peru); after the acceptance of the resignation of Prime Minister [Aníbal Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C3%ADbal_Torres).[^cite_note-\:0-1] Chávez was the fifth Prime Minister to be appointed by President [Pedro Castillo](49637220.md). Her appointment was seen by political observers as evidence of ongoing political instability.[^cite_note-11] After two weeks in office, she announced her resignation.[^cite_note-12] On 7 December 2022 she resigned from office following President Castillo's [attempted self-coup](72438093.md). On 20 June 2023 the Peruvian National Police arrested her for alleged crimes of rebellion and conspiracy against the state. The Supreme Court ordered her to be detained for 18 months. The charges against Chávez stem from ousted former President Pedro Castillo's attempt to dissolve Congress and rule by decree in December 2022.[^cite_note-13]

Chávez, then Minister of Culture, through her [TikTok](56822861.md) account, described the Peruvian Attorney General [Patricia Benavides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Benavides) as a "coup plotter" and referred to her as "Blanca Nélida Colán 2.0", a reference to a prosecutor aligned with [Fujimorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujimorism) who failed to prosecute [Vladimiro Montesinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimiro_Montesinos).[^cite_note-14] This compounded problems after the initiation of a preliminary investigation against her, related to the favoring of her relatives in public office.[^cite_note-15]

## Post-premiership

On 22 March 2023 Chávez was suspended by congress.[^cite_note-16]

On 3 November 2025, Chávez sought asylum in the Mexican embassy amid an investigation against her on charges of rebellion relating to her role in President Castillo's self-coup. After her asylum request was granted, Peru severed [diplomatic relations with Mexico](39326409.md).[^cite_note-17] On 27 November, Chávez was sentenced to 11.5 years' imprisonment by the [Supreme Court](8801058.md) on charges of conspiracy to commit a rebellion over the self-coup.[^cite_note-18]
