On June 14, 2026, a [PAC P-750 XSTOL](2566403.md) skydiving aircraft crashed in [Butler, Missouri](122519.md), United States, killing all 12 occupants. The aircraft was a private plane operated by Skydive Kansas City, which departed from [Butler Memorial Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler_Memorial_Airport). The plane crashed near a highway. The accident was the deadliest skydiving plane crash in the United States since the September 1995 crash of a [Beechcraft Queen Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_Queen_Air) near [West Point, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Point,_Virginia).[^cite_note-CNNDeadliest-1][^cite_note-2]

## Background

### Aircraft

The aircraft involved was N221BN, a fixed-wing [PAC P-750 XSTOL](2566403.md) manufactured in 2010 by Pacific Aerospace in [New Zealand](4913064.md) and operated by Skyhi Aero LLC on behalf of Skydive Kansas City. The aircraft was powered by a single [Pratt & Whitney Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada) engine.[^cite_note-NBC-3] [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) records show that the aircraft's current registration certificate had been issued in January 2023 and was set to expire in January 2030.[^cite_note-KSHB-4]

### Passengers and crew

Eleven skydivers and a pilot were on board the aircraft.[^cite_note-KSHB-4] Nine of the skydivers were experienced, while two others were going to participate in tandem jumps.[^cite_note-CNNDeadliest-1] Among the skydivers were a [North Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kansas_City,_Missouri) music teacher, a father, and a [Vietnam War](32611.md) veteran.[^cite_note-5]

## Accident



The aircraft seconds before crashing

While climbing, at around 98 feet (30 m) of altitude, the aircraft [stalled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stall_\(fluid_dynamics\)), then made a sharp left turn and impacted the ground in a nose down position.[^cite_note-NBC-3][^cite_note-6] A witness said the aircraft completely shattered upon impact and the skydivers didn't have time to jump.[^cite_note-7] First responders received a 911 call shortly before 11\:30 a.m. that a skydiving plane crashed just after takeoff. The plane crashed on airport property and was destroyed by a post-crash fire.[^cite_note-KSHB-4]

## Investigation

The [National Transportation Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board) (NTSB) and the FAA confirmed they are investigating the crash.[^cite_note-8] The NTSB said weight, balance, or [engine failure on takeoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine_failure_on_takeoff) might have caused the crash.[^cite_note-9]
