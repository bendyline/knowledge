On 24 June 2026, doublet[^cite_note-a-3][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] large [strike-slip earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike-slip_earthquakes) affected northwestern and central [Venezuela](32374.md). The [epicenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicenter) of the first earthquake was in [Veroes Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veroes_Municipality), west of [San Felipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Felipe_\(Venezuela\)), the capital city of [Yaracuy](556302.md). This earthquake, which measured [M<sub>w</sub>](6207163.md#Mw) 7.2, occurred at 18\:04 [VET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Venezuela) and was classified as a [foreshock](1160429.md). It was followed by a M<sub>w</sub> 7.5 mainshock 30 seconds later centered off the coast of [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira_\(state\)), west of [Catia La Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catia_La_Mar). The two earthquakes caused widespread damage across the country, particularly in the capital [Caracas](55289.md) and especially the state of [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira_\(state\)), where 80% of buildings collapsed.[^cite_note-orinoco-8] As of August 2026, at least 6,500 fatalities were officially confirmed, about 226,700 were injured or hospitalized, and tens of thousands still remain missing.[^cite_note-missing-5]

The [United States Geological Survey](23814944.md) (USGS) [Prompt Assessment of Global Earthquakes for Response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAGER) (PAGER) system predicted the death toll could be significantly higher than the initial reports, potentially exceeding 10,000. The [mainshock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainshock) was the strongest in Venezuela since the [1900 San Narciso earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_San_Narciso_earthquake).

## Tectonic setting



[Tectonic setting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonic_setting) of [Venezuela](32374.md), between the [Caribbean plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_plate), the [North Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Andes_plate), and the [South American plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_plate)

Northern Venezuela lies within a broad, [transpressional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transpression) boundary zone between the [Caribbean plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_plate) and the [South American plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_plate) rather than along a single fault. Its principal active structure is the [Boconó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocon%C3%B3_Fault)–[San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Fault)–[El Pilar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pilar_Fault_System) fault system, with slip transferred farther east through the [Los Bajos Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Bajos_Fault?action=edit\&redlink=1) and onto the [Warm Springs Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm_Springs_Fault?action=edit\&redlink=1) near [Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad).[^cite_note-Audemard06-12] It is a compressional and right-lateral [strike-slip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)#Strike-slip_faults) system that extends for more than 1,300 km (810 mi) from the Venezuelan Andes to Trinidad[^cite_note-Schubert84-13] and accommodates much of the dextral motion between the Caribbean and South American plates at about 10 mm (0.39 in) per year.[^cite_note-Audemard06-12]

The 24 June 2026 [doublet earthquake](26402849.md) occurred within this plate-boundary zone near the San Sebastián fault system. The USGS located the M<sub>w</sub> 7.2 foreshock near [Yumare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yumare) and the M<sub>w</sub> 7.5 mainshock in the Caribbean Sea, northwest of Caracas.[^cite_note-anss-14][^cite_note-anss2-15] The USGS described the mainshock as shallow strike-slip faulting near the complex plate boundary along the San Sebastián fault system.[^cite_note-anss2-15] This is consistent with the regional tectonic setting of east–west-trending right-lateral faults in northern Venezuela.[^cite_note-Audemard06-12] The Boconó and San Sebastián faults were previously identified as potential sources of future large earthquakes. The Boconó Fault, which last ruptured in 1812, has accumulated a slip deficit that could be released as a magnitude 7.0–7.6 earthquake. Machel Higgins, a geophysicist at [Florida International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_International_University), said the San Sebastián Fault was capable of producing a magnitude 7.1 earthquake.[^cite_note-16]



Segments of the [Boconó Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocon%C3%B3_Fault) and [San Sebastián Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Fault) systems

The San Sebastián Fault is mostly offshore along north-central Venezuela, north of the [Venezuelan Coastal Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_Coastal_Range). A short onshore segment near [Simón Bolívar International Airport](8277069.md) is also known as the Bruscas Fault. Farther east, the San Sebastián and El Pilar faults are separated by the [Cariaco pull-apart basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cariaco_Basin). The offshore [San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_Fault) and [El Pilar faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pilar_Fault_System) comprise 400 km (250 mi) of the total length of the plate boundary from longitudes −68° to −64°.[^cite_note-ColónA-17]

The same plate-boundary system has been associated with several large historical earthquakes in northern Venezuela, including events in [1641](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1641_Caracas_earthquake), [1766](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1766_Southeastern_Caribbean_earthquake), [1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1812_Caracas_earthquake), 1900, and [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Caracas_earthquake). A 2015 marine seismic study attributed the M<sub>w</sub> 7.6 [1900 San Narciso earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_San_Narciso_earthquake) to a rupture on the eastern San Sebastián Fault. The 1900 rupture source may have been east of [Maiquetía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiquet%C3%ADa) and along the northern margin of the Cariaco Basin, between the ruptures of the 1812 Caracas and [1853 Cumaná earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1853_Cuman%C3%A1_earthquake).[^cite_note-ColónA-17]

## Earthquakes

USGS ShakeMaps of the M7.2 and M7.5 events





The first shock was an M<sub>w</sub> 7.2 earthquake east-northeast of [Yumare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yumare) at 22\:04 UTC and a depth of 10 km (6.2 mi). It was caused by right-lateral strike-slip faulting on an east–west trending fault.[^cite_note-anss-14] The [GFZ Helmholtz Centre for Geosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GFZ_Helmholtz_Centre_for_Geosciences) (GFZ) catalogued the foreshock at M<sub>w</sub> 7.3 and a depth of 12 km (7.5 mi).[^cite_note-gfz2026mhpf-18]

Thirty seconds later, a M<sub>ww</sub> 7.5 shock struck about 100 km (62 mi) east of the first event at a depth of 10 km (6.2 mi). The epicenter of the first earthquake is in the [Veroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veroes_Municipality) municipality in the state of [Yaracuy](556302.md). The second quake was centered off the coast of [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira) state near [Catia La Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catia_La_Mar). This earthquake was caused by either right-lateral strike-slip faulting on an east–west trending fault or left-lateral strike-slip faulting on a north–south striking fault. The USGS said that it likely ruptured along the San Sebastián Fault, with slip confined to a 175-by-20-kilometre (109-by-12-mile) area.[^cite_note-anss2-15] The GFZ reported this earthquake as M<sub>w</sub> 7.4 and placed the epicenter northwest of Caracas. The GFZ's focal mechanism indicated right-lateral strike-slip faulting on an east–west trending fault.[^cite_note-gfz2026mhpg-19] This mainshock is the largest in Venezuela since 1900, when a magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck northeast of Caracas.[^cite_note-20]

The [GEOSCOPE Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEOSCOPE_Observatory) only reported one M<sub>w</sub> 7.8 earthquake with an epicenter area close to that of the USGS event epicenters, lasting for about two minutes.[^cite_note-Skapski-2] The focal mechanism solution provided by the agency indicated right-lateral strike-slip faulting that initiated at a depth of 12 km (7.5 mi).[^cite_note-Geoscope-21]

Italy's [National Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Geophysics_and_Volcanology) (INGV) published an earthquake finite fault model describing slip along the fault based on analysing surface displacement data from ESA's [Sentinel-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel-1) Earth-observation satellite. According to the model, the rupture involved an east–west trending, north-dipping fault. Because ground deformation data collected for both earthquakes were indistinguishable, the INGV's model combined them into a single event measuring M<sub>w</sub> 7.6 containing two episodes of high energy release. Slip occurred along a 210 by 30 km (130 by 19 mi) section of the fault extending from near Morón to northeast of Caracas. The rupture initiated near Morón, which corresponded with the first earthquake; this event produced up to 2.5 m (8 ft 2 in) of slip at a depth of 20 km (12 mi). The INGV estimated that rupture propagated towards Caracas at a velocity of 3–3.5 km (1.9–2.2 mi) per second. A second episode of energy release occurred 30 to 40 seconds later, corresponding to the second earthquake and representing the greatest energy release phase. The second event was associated with a maximum slip of 3.6 m (12 ft) offshore, north of Catia La Mar, at a depth of 11.1 km (6.9 mi).[^cite_note-ingvA-22][^cite_note-INGV2-23]

USGS fault slip distribution and surface projection





The USGS modelled two fault segments with a combined dimension of about 230 by 40 km (143 by 25 mi) and combined magnitude of 7.54, representing both events. These segments dipped 78 degrees south. Most of the rupture occurred west of the hypocenter of the first event, which ruptured a \~40 km (25 mi) east–northeast onshore portion of the fault. An east–west trending, offshore, 180 km (110 mi) fault rupture represented the second earthquake. Slip on the longer east–west segment was greater; more than 1 m (3 ft 3 in) of slip occurred for most of its length, peaking at 1.8232 m (5 ft 11.78 in) in a region west of the fault. Around the hypocenter, the slip also peaked at 1.5811 m (5 ft 2.25 in). The zone of greatest slip (>1 m (3 ft 3 in)) occurred at depth from west of Veroes Municipality to Simón Bolívar International Airport, a distance of about 200 km (120 mi). The earthquake sequence lasted about 70 seconds.[^cite_note-USGS-mainshock_finite_fault-1]

Seismologists at [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University) modelled one M<sub>w</sub> 7.61 earthquake at a depth of 10 km (6.2 mi) rupturing in an asymmetric bilateral way across a 210 by 40 km (130 by 25 mi) area. The earthquake propagated mainly eastwards with slip concentrating at shallow depths along the fault. Most of the slip occurred at 60–130 km (37–81 mi) east of the epicenter, peaking at 4.49 m (14.7 ft) some 70 km (43 mi) away; the model also indicated a significant vertical slip component in addition to the strike-slip motion. The greatest energy release occurred some 20–80 seconds after the rupture initiated, but continued for 90 seconds.[^cite_note-Yuhe1-24]

Six aftershocks were felt in [Caracas](55289.md) within two hours of the mainshock.[^cite_note-elpais-25] According to [Fundación Venezolana de Investigaciones Sismológicas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundaci%C3%B3n_Venezolana_de_Investigaciones_Sismol%C3%B3gicas?action=edit\&redlink=1), 1,115 aftershocks were recorded from the time of the earthquakes to 8 July. These aftershocks mainly occurred in Yaracuy, Carabobo, Aragua, Miranda and La Guaira.[^cite_note-26] The largest aftershock occurred at 22\:54 UTC on 24 June and measured mb 5.0.[^cite_note-anss3-27]

Shaking was also felt in [Bogotá](211271.md),[^cite_note-28] northeastern Colombia, and [northern Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Brazil), including in [Manaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaus), [Belém](409573.md) and [Macapá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macap%C3%A1), triggering evacuations.[^cite_note-ap-29] Tremors were also felt in the [ABC islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_islands) ([Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba), [Bonaire](108737.md) and [Curaçao](65205.md)),[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] in parts of the [Dominican Republic](8060.md), including [Santo Domingo](57638.md),[^cite_note-32] and in [Puerto Rico](23041.md).[^cite_note-33] In Trinidad and Tobago, coastal uplift of up to 20 ft (6.1 m) was observed in Galfa Point, along the southwestern peninsula of Trinidad.[^cite_note-34]

A [tsunami advisory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsunami_advisory) was issued for the Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico and the [Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Islands) following the earthquakes, but was later rescinded.[^cite_note-ap-29][^cite_note-35] A 4 cm (1.6 in) high tsunami struck [Isla de Mona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_de_Mona) and [Yabucoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yabucoa,_Puerto_Rico), Puerto Rico, while a 2 cm (0.79 in) high surge was detected at [Fort-de-France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort-de-France), [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique).[^cite_note-36]

According to official government figures released on 24 August, at least 6,509 people died and 226,696 were injured or hospitalized.[^cite_note-38] Tens of thousands were displaced; more than 16,300 people who lost their homes,[^cite_note-EFE4a-39] and another 28,300 whose homes were uninhabitable.[^cite_note-Singer2a-40] On 4 August, José Alejandro Terán, the governor of La Guaira, reported that 7,265 people died and 1,579 others were missing, though the national government did not reflect this.[^cite_note-laguaria7265-41][^cite_note-laguairadeadandmissing-42] No official death toll update have been released by the government since late August,[^cite_note-43] even though bodies continue to be recovered in La Guaira as recent as end-September.[^cite_note-44]

Many people remained missing a month later,[^cite_note-Maturana27a-45] although the exact number remains debated.[^cite_note-46] A crowdsourced database, *Venezuela Reporta*, created by a [Miami](53846.md)-based Venezuelan,[^cite_note-Saltman2a-47] estimated that 41,300 people were missing, nearly all of them in La Guaira, while international sources and the United Nations estimated that the total number of missing persons still buried beneath collapsed buildings on 18 July could range between 40,000 and 50,000.[^cite_note-france3a-48][^cite_note-larazonmissing-10] The website *Venezuela Looks for You* said 18,100 people were missing as of 7 July.[^cite_note-vlfy18k-9] On another tracking persons website, *Desaparecidos Terremoto Venezuela*, approximately 29,000 people were reported missing as of 3 August.[^cite_note-france3a-48] United Nations under-secretary-general for humanitarian affairs and emergency relief coordinator [Tom Fletcher](41593401.md) also said that more than 50,000 people remained missing on 27 June.[^cite_note-ST27a-11] No official missing figures were put out by the Venezuelan government, with National Assembly president Jorge Rodríguez saying it was to avoid speculation.[^cite_note-49] An initial figure of 157 was reported by the government on 25 June, but that number was never updated.[^cite_note-Maturana27a-45] More than 100 Venezuelans who arrived in La Guaira aboard a [deportation flight](78314324.md) by the [United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) went missing after their hotel collapsed.[^cite_note-theguardian30a-50] According to *[El País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pa%C3%ADs)*, at least 32 deportees were killed.[^cite_note-Colomé3a-51] By late August, only 30% of collapsed buildings in La Guaira had been searched, with many bodies still yet to have been recovered.[^cite_note-laguairadeadandmissing-42]

More than two hundred foreigners were identified among the dead, including 2 Brazilians,[^cite_note-52] 4 Chileans,[^cite_note-53] 9 Chinese,[^cite_note-lavanguardia26a-54][^cite_note-55] 24 Colombians,[^cite_note-56] a Cuban,[^cite_note-57] a Dominican,[^cite_note-58] 3 Ecuadorians,[^cite_note-59] 16 Italian-Venezuelans,[^cite_note-60] and a Uruguayan.[^cite_note-61] Portugal also confirmed that 100 citizens died, including at least 59 with Venezuelan citizenship, while 59 others were missing.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] Forty-five French citizens died and another 50 were missing.[^cite_note-64] In addition to 40 deaths, Spanish foreign minister [José Manuel Albares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Albares) also said 138 citizens were missing.[^cite_note-65] The Argentinian government said 6 citizens had died and 3 were missing.[^cite_note-66] A spokesperson for the [Trump administration](78298108.md) said 3 Americans were among the dead and 12 were missing.[^cite_note-67] Peru's foreign ministry confirmed 3 Peruvian citizens had died and 7 were missing.[^cite_note-68]

Initial casualty reports remained scarce, particularly in more remote areas. This is believed to be due to disrupted communications or a possible [media blackout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_blackout) in Venezuela.[^cite_note-BBC_Live-69][^cite_note-BNO-70] Since 24 June is a national holiday in Venezuela, commemorating the 1821 [Battle of Carabobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Carabobo), many people were at home instead of at work when the earthquakes struck.[^cite_note-71]

### Casualty estimates

According to the USGS PAGER service, "high casualties and extensive damage are probable and the disaster is likely widespread".[^cite_note-CNA25a-72] For the M<sub>w</sub> 7.5 earthquake, PAGER estimated a 18% probability of 1,000 to 10,000 deaths, a 43% probability of 10,000 to 100,000 deaths, and a 38% probability of more than 100,000 deaths.[^cite_note-anss2-15] For the M<sub>w</sub> 7.2 foreshock, PAGER estimated a 33% probability of 1,000 to 10,000 deaths, a 41% probability of 10,000 to 100,000 deaths, and a 17% probability of more than 100,000 deaths.[^cite_note-anss-14][^cite_note-74] Based on an assessment of seismic modelling, satellite imagery and population data, the [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme) estimated \$4.7–8.7 billion in housing and economic damage, or about 6% of the nation's [gross domestic product](12594.md). This estimate did not include damage from infrastructure loss, long-term economic disruption, and reconstruction. They also estimated that 1.7 million buildings, including many in the worst-affected areas, were exposed to strong shaking.[^cite_note-UNDP1-75] Upon anticipating a large number of deaths, the United Nations supplied 10,000 body bags.[^cite_note-76]

Following discrepancies between government updates of the number of casualties, the [Venezuelan Education-Action Program on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PROVEA) (PROVEA) called for independent verification and government transparency. According to PROVEA, a 27 June update placed the death toll at 1,430, and 24 hours later, updated that number with only 20 additional deaths. Another earlier update lowered the number of injured from 3,238 to 3,150.[^cite_note-elnacional29c-77] PROVEA added that the government figures "raise more doubts than they provide answers." On the contrary, sociologist David Smilde at [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University) does not believe the government would underreport these figures, since there was already a large international response and the government could use them to appeal for more aid. Another sociologist and think tank founder, Rafael Uzcátegui, said the government was already rigged with inefficiency and favoritism, so managing a crisis is challenging.[^cite_note-Saltman2a-47]

On 29 June, when the death toll stood at 1,450, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* said it may be a "substantial undercount", and it could take weeks to realize the true extent of destruction. Architectural engineering professor Emily So expected the toll to rise significantly after more bodies are removed from the rubble. This was based on interpreting the number of missing and widespread damage to infrastructure. Ilan Kelman, a professor of disasters and health at [University College London](52029.md), said there was a possibility the true number of fatalities may never be known. Additional deaths from injuries were also expected due to limitations with Venezuela's strained healthcare infrastructure. Contributing to the delayed response is a lack of machinery and medical supplies.[^cite_note-Sands29-78] Tens of thousands of people remained missing, according to an unofficial missing persons tracking website. These figures are not independently verified and may contain people who were separated from their families. However, according to Linda Hornisberger of REDOG, a Swiss search-and-rescue group, while communication may be difficult, it is not to the extent that making contact is impossible. Hornisberger suspected that most of the missing were already dead. *The New York Times* believed that it may take considerable time for a finalized death toll to be published, reminiscent of [Hurricane Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Maria) and the [2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami) where governments spent a year updating figures.[^cite_note-79]

### Identification and funeral preparations

As hospital morgues were operating beyond capacity, many bodies were later taken to the La Guaira port which was converted into a makeshift mortuary for relatives—some of whom were still hospitalized for their injuries—to identify the dead. To delay the decomposition process, some bodies were coated in lime. There, a makeshift tent also served as an authorization center releasing death certificates and cremation approvals. Numerous private funeral homes began offering free funeral car services and cremations.[^cite_note-80] Without proper record-keeping and identification protocols, relatives had to search in improvised morgues and [refrigerator trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigerator_truck) in unhygienic conditions by themselves. Some families reported lost or misplaced bodies after they were identified which prevented burial or funeral processions.[^cite_note-81] A forensic pathologist for the La Guaira port facility estimated that about 400 bodies are processed every day, many of them mangled beyond recognition. Due to the number of bodies, refrigerator trucks were overwhelmed and remaining bodies were laid outdoors and left to decompose. Some bodies had decomposed so much that they could only be identified by relatives through tattoos, dental records or clothes.[^cite_note-Saltman2a-47]

By 6 July, more than 2,200 bodies had been claimed by relatives and buried; another 200 remained unclaimed. Unclaimed bodies were buried in a cemetery at Catia La Mar, each marked by a cross, with identification plaques and forensic files for families to identify them in the future. La Guaira governor José Alejandro Terán said forensic experts from [Servicio Nacional de Medicina y Ciencias Forenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servicio_Nacional_de_Medicina_y_Ciencias_Forenses?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Cuerpo de Investigaciones Científicas, Penales y Criminalísticas were responsible for identifying the dead. Terán dispelled rumors on social media of mass graves for the dead.[^cite_note-León6a-82] The burial site was in a mountainous area of the state, where bodies were placed in large trenches. An official in-charge of the cemetery said they had the capacity to hold 2,000 to 3,000 coffins.[^cite_note-Martín6a-83]

### Recovering bodies

Many volunteers and firefighter continued to look for the dead under collapsed apartment blocks after most rescue operations ended by burrowing tunnels through the concrete slabs. One volunteer at the site of the OPP 26 building collapse managed to find 12 bodies.[^cite_note-84] Some said they were unable to recover their dead relatives from the rubble, and even after requesting for government assistance, did not get a response. One said to remove the bodies of his mother and brother, the latter's limbs had to be cut off. A firefighter in La Guaira said on July 7 that many bodies still buried beneath collapsed buildings would have decomposed so heavily that they would often disintegrate if personnel attempted to recover them.[^cite_note-pbsrecovery-85] Some private crane owners were charging people up to US\$3,000 a day to rent their equipment so bodies could be extracted.[^cite_note-86]

## Damage

[Telesur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telesur) reporting from [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_Parish), [Distrito Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_District_\(Venezuela\))

The earthquake collapsed nearly 800 buildings, of which 189 were completely leveled.[^cite_note-elnacional29b-97] Satellite imagery suggests that approximately 590,000 buildings were damaged across the country overall.[^cite_note-98] Buildings collapsed in [Caracas](55289.md) and the states of [Trujillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo_\(state\)), [Carabobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabobo), [Aragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragua), [Miranda](556284.md), and [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira_\(state\)).[^cite_note-elpais-25][^cite_note-99] The [United Nations](31769.md) estimated that the earthquake caused US\$4.7–8.7 billion worth of damage, roughly 4–8% of Venezuela's [GDP](12594.md), based on losses to housing and economic assets, adding that the true cost could be between 1.5 and 3 times that estimate.[^cite_note-100]

The [United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_for_Disaster_Risk_Reduction) estimated that both earthquakes caused US\$37 billion in direct damages. About US\$24 billion in damage were to residential, commercial, educational, health, institutional and industrial buildings, or 3% of the total number of buildings exposed to shaking. Another US\$13 billion in damage represented water and sanitation, telecommunications, roads, railways, energy, ports, airports, oil and gas infrastructure, or 4%. The estimate did not include indirect losses such as due to supply chain disruptions, social, environmental or economic effects, and reconstruction.[^cite_note-101] [Verisk Analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verisk_Analytics) estimated the economic loss to be in excess of US\$10 billion.[^cite_note-102]

The United Nations Development Programme estimated that 1.7 million buildings were exposed to Mercalli intensity VI (*Strong*) or higher intensities of shaking; 452,000 experienced VII (*Very strong*); 60,000 experienced VIII (*Severe*), and 5,000 experienced IX (*Violent*).[^cite_note-elnacional29d-103] About 38 hospitals across the country were also damaged.[^cite_note-2_July_2026-104]

### Caracas



A collapsed building in Caracas

Interior Minister [Diosdado Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Cabello) said that the [Los Palos Grandes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Palos_Grandes) and [Altamira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altamira,_Caracas) municipalities were the worst-affected parts of Caracas.[^cite_note-Buschschlüter1-105] At least four collapsed buildings in Caracas were residential high-rises. At the Petunia Residences in Los Palos Grandes, 14 floors partially collapsed, leaving only 6 floors intact.[^cite_note-Singer26a-106] Three buildings collapsed in Altamira,[^cite_note-PhillipsGuardian1-107] including a 22-storey building that totally collapsed.[^cite_note-CNA24b-108] Another 30 buildings in the district were heavily damaged.[^cite_note-Singer26a-106] In an unspecified area in southeastern Caracas, almost all high-rise buildings were heavily damaged or destroyed, with many of them collapsing.[^cite_note-elpais-25]

On 9 July, Carmen Meléndez, the mayor of Capital District, said 69 people died; 47 deaths occurred in San Bernardino parish of [Libertador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertador_Municipality,_Caracas).[^cite_note-libertador-109] In the eastern Caracas municipality of [Chacao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chacao_Municipality), the mayor, [Gustavo Duque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Duque?action=edit\&redlink=1), said at least 58 people were killed and 26 people were rescued. Additionally, 100 buildings sustained minor to severe structural damage.[^cite_note-chacao-110] Two buildings in the municipality also collapsed,[^cite_note-111] with many people trapped under rubble.[^cite_note-AP_Photos-112] At least three people were killed after two structures collapsed in [Baruta Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruta_Municipality), and several were killed in a building collapse in Pinto Salinas.[^cite_note-baruta-113]

Buildings housing the French embassy and the headquarters of the [Venezuelan Red Cross Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_Red_Cross_Society) were severely damaged.[^cite_note-f24-114] The collapse of the Rita building in [San Bernardino Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_Parish) resulted in at least seven deaths.[^cite_note-115] In [La Pastora Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Pastora_Parish), several homes, public infrastructure and religious buildings were damaged. A fallen cornice from one of these damaged buildings killed four people and injured several more. The Caracas Fire Department said at least 174 people where injured. They also estimated that more than 200 homes were damaged to varying degrees including partial collapses.[^cite_note-116]

### La Guaira



Venezuelan rescue teams at destroyed buildings in [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira)

In La Guaira, the worst-affected state, north of Caracas, 80% of buildings collapsed,[^cite_note-orinoco-8] with officials reporting 1,400 destroyed structures.[^cite_note-Silva28a-117] The government said nearly 200 buildings in the state collapsed, but satellite analysis indicated a greater number.[^cite_note-ST3a-118] According to [Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus_Programme) satellite imagery analyzed by [NASA](18426568.md), more than half the buildings in [Caraballeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraballeda), [Macuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macuto,_Vargas), [Naiguatá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naiguat%C3%A1), La Guaira and [Catia la Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catia_la_Mar) had a damage probability of at least 75%.[^cite_note-elnacional29d-103] That approximated to almost 59,000 buildings damaged according to their analysis.[^cite_note-119] Some of the rubble were also further damaged by fires.[^cite_note-120]

The main airport serving the capital, [Simón Bolívar International Airport](8277069.md), was heavily damaged.[^cite_note-CNA25a-72] All flights departing from or arriving there were canceled.[^cite_note-elpais-25] An independent database said more than 41,300 people were missing in the state alone.[^cite_note-larazonmissing-10] Venezuelan rescue teams reported that hundreds, possibly thousands of people still remained buried beneath collapsed structures as of 26 June.[^cite_note-121] Images on social media showed dozens of buildings collapsed completely or partially. Those that remained standing sustained collapsed walls and shattered columns. Telecommunication services in the city were disabled.[^cite_note-diariolasamericas24a-122] Video from a helicopter also showed many buildings flattened in the Playa Grande, Tanaguarenas and Los Corales neighbourhoods.[^cite_note-ST26c-123] In Los Corales, the Coral Park, Vallarta, Coral Mar and Coral Beach buildings were destroyed.[^cite_note-elnacional29c-77]

The Microsoft initiative, AI for Good Lab, said AI-based damage assessment tools estimated that nearly a third of Catia La Mar's 30,000 buildings were damaged. The heavy damage in Venezuela was attributed to substandard construction and collapse of older buildings. After the 1967 Caracas earthquake, the government revised its [seismic codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismic_code), but such enforcements were never prioritized. According to Juan Carlos Vielma, a civil engineer, after the [Vargas landslides of 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vargas_tragedy), then-president [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez)'s administration rushed to build housing for those displaced. Engineers also highlighted the construction of [soft story buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_story_building) where there are fewer structural elements on the ground floor in favor of more open spaces or garages; such buildings are vulnerable to [pancake collapses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_collapse).[^cite_note-Burke27a-124]

The state-run Hotel Santuario La Llanada, partially collapsed, trapping and likely killing most of its inhabitants including many repatriated citizens. Satellite imagery showed one section of the hotel reduced to rubble while another still intact.[^cite_note-125] The Eduard's Hotel completely collapsed, trapping staff and families of some baseball players from [Guerreros de Lara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerreros_de_Lara?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Delfines de La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delfines_de_La_Guaira?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-126] The relatives of [Gorkys Hernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkys_Hern%C3%A1ndez), [Eliézer Alfonzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli%C3%A9zer_Alfonzo), and his son [Eliézer Alfonzo Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli%C3%A9zer_Alfonzo_Jr.) were later found dead in the hotel ruins.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] Further from the coast, in [Carayaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carayaca), a mixed-use residential building collapsed, killing 11 people.[^cite_note-elnacional28a-129] An administration building of the [Military Academy of the Bolivarian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Academy_of_the_Bolivarian_Navy) and another office collapsed, trapping several people.[^cite_note-130] In Catia La Mar, a children's hospital collapsed, killing 16 children and injuring 35 others.[^cite_note-131] During search and rescue operations, rescuers found that some buildings had styrofoam and concrete making up the walls.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

### Elsewhere



The [Valencia Cathedral's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Cathedral_\(Venezuela\)) clock tower following the earthquakes.

Closer to the epicenter, in the state of [Carabobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabobo), 19 people were killed, 67 were injured, 568 houses were destroyed and 787 others were damaged in [Naguanagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naguanagua_Municipality), [Juan José Mora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Jos%C3%A9_Mora_Municipality), [Puerto Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Cabello), and [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Municipality,_Carabobo). Fifteen people died in Juan José Mora, one person died in Puerto Cabello, another died in Naguanagua, and 34 people were injured in [Mariara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariara).[^cite_note-carabobo-134][^cite_note-135] At least 40 people were treated for injuries in Puerto Cabello's Adolfo Prince Lara and Dr. Molina Sierra Social Security hospitals. More than 25 homes were destroyed in the Morón and Urama parishes, while some buildings in Puerto Cabello were damaged.[^cite_note-136]

In [Yaracuy](556302.md) state, where epicenter of the earthquakes are, 13 homes were heavily damaged in [San Felipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Felipe,_Yaracuy), two buildings under construction collapsed in [Cocorote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocorote), and cracks appeared in one home in [Independencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independencia_Municipality,_Yaracuy).[^cite_note-137] In Veroes, 25 homes collapsed and 35 others were badly damaged; in total, at least 2,686 homes were damaged out of an inspected 3,000.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

At least 18 people were killed in [Aragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragua); 16 in Santiago Mariño, and 1 each in Bolívar and Tovar municipalities.[^cite_note-aragua-140] The collapse of one apartment in the Bosque Lindo housing complex of Santiago Mariño caused multiple deaths. Inspections by engineers later revealed that the three towers that did not collapse were unsafe for residents. The earthquake also displaced more than 700 people in the state.[^cite_note-141]

[Falcón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falc%C3%B3n) governor [Víctor Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Clark?action=edit\&redlink=1) said 32 people were treated for injuries and 15 people were trapped under collapsed buildings.[^cite_note-Cano25a-142] The collapse of the La Mar Suites in [Tucacas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucacas), Falcón left 12 people dead and 33 injured.[^cite_note-falcon-143] In Boca de Aroa parish, the earthquakes damaged 87 homes, affecting 252 residents. Eight housing complexes and two buildings were also damaged.[^cite_note-144]

### Other damage

An aftershock on 26 June caused additional damage to buildings in Caracas[^cite_note-145] as well as the collapse of a bridge connecting the parish of [Caraballeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraballeda) to the rest of La Guaira, disrupting relief efforts.[^cite_note-146] On 2 July, one death occurred outside a hospital in [Zulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulia) due to a falling façade. The hospital was already damaged by the earthquakes the week prior.[^cite_note-147] Eight days after the earthquakes, a school in La Pastora Parish partially collapsed; one person was injured and four homes were evacuated due to damage from the collapse.[^cite_note-148]

## Search and rescue



USMC Gen. Kevin Jarrard surveys damage at [Simón Bolívar Airport](8277069.md) in [Maiquetía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiquet%C3%ADa), [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira_\(state\))



Members of the National Civil Protection System of Panama and the Panamanian Red Cross, preparing to travel to Venezuela.

In Caracas, rescue personnel and volunteers began searching for survivors under the debris of collapsed buildings.[^cite_note-PhillipsGuardian1-107] Staff at the Hospital de Clínicas in Caracas were requested to "double up on the night shift" to respond to the injured.[^cite_note-ST24b-149] Acting president [Delcy Rodríguez](44199228.md) warned that the death count was likely to rise, as operations that focused on searching through rubble and debris had not been completed yet. She specifically mentioned [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira_\(state\)), which was described as a "disaster zone", because the number of deaths from that state had not been verified or added to the total.[^cite_note-150] By 1 July, more than 6,400 people were rescued. At the time of the earthquakes, about 30,000 people were in Caraballeda and Catia la Mar; 13,400 of them managed to rescue themselves from the rubble.[^cite_note-eldiario1a-151]

Volunteers, many of them residents of La Guaira, began their own rescue efforts to retrieve their neighbours from collapsed apartment buildings. Almost 24 hours after the earthquakes, they dug through the wreckage with their hands as the city faced a shortage of heavy equipment and machinery, with very limited government assistance. Due to this, civilians from cities across the country collaborated and donated food, clothes and resources for the affected, as well as created donation accounts for potential international donors. The high number of injured people began to overwhelm the José María Vargas Hospital, and some casualties had to be treated outdoors. A military supply line was also established outside the city's stadium providing aid. The [Venezuelan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_Armed_Forces) said field hospitals would be deployed with the capability to support emergency surgeries.[^cite_note-ST26a-152] Other hospitals across the affected area, such as in Morón, were also overwhelmed by the injured or were short of supplies.[^cite_note-ST26b-153] A week after the earthquakes, there were 11 international field hospitals established and healthcare personnel from 33 countries, according to Rodríguez.[^cite_note-154]



The second contingent of the 48 [Costa Rica](5551.md) Urban Search and Rescue (USAR) specialists from the [Costa Rica](5551.md) Fire Dept. en route to assist with post earthquake operations in [Venezuela](32374.md).&#x20;

On 26 June, rescue convoys from Mexico, El Salvador and the Dominican Republic arrived in Venezuela.[^cite_note-Janetsky26b-155] Firefighters, the military and police civil protection were only present in limited areas one day after the earthquake. Dozens of people from nearby Valencia journeyed through the night, making their way to La Guaira with aid.[^cite_note-Sequera26a-156] Residents from Caracas also brought their own supplies to the city along the [Caracas–La Guaira highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas-La_Guaira_highway) to aid in the initial rescue efforts.[^cite_note-ST26b-153] President of the National Assembly [Jorge Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Rodr%C3%ADguez_G%C3%B3mez) requested that citizens mobilize their supplies through authorities instead of delivering them to La Guaira to prevent road congestions.[^cite_note-ST27b-157]

On 27 June, Delcy Rodríguez said over 14,000 military personnel and more than 100 pieces of machinery were deployed to La Guaira for peacekeeping efforts.[^cite_note-AP28a-158][^cite_note-Benezra27a-159] They were joined alongside more than 3,600 rescuers from abroad.[^cite_note-160] However, the search and rescue operations were first carried out by civilians volunteers and relatives of those trapped in the rubble before official and international assistance.[^cite_note-CNA29a-161] The sheer number of non-government response and volunteers arriving from elsewhere worsened the chaos in the state.[^cite_note-162] The government later restricted access to the city on the night of 26 June, only giving access to authorized personnel. Interior Minister [Diosdado Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Cabello) defended the restriction to clear roadways for ambulances and for health reasons but did not elaborate on the latter. A volunteer registration center was also established at the [Poliedro de Caracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliedro_de_Caracas) while civilians who want to contribute to the relief were encouraged to do so via aid collection centers.[^cite_note-163] By 4 July, there were nearly 27,000 registrations.[^cite_note-EFE4a-39]

Many Venezuelans, frustrated, claimed the government's response was limited and insufficient; what they saw as the government's attempt to create the impression of an efficient response caused further discontent. At one rubble site, government officials often took selfies before leaving without participating in the recovery effort.[^cite_note-AP28a-158] Eighty members of a Swiss rescue team said their search dogs found several survivors beneath a rubble site but were unable to rescue them in time.[^cite_note-ST29a-164] Outrage among civilians were directed at authorities who were managing traffic and making street patrols rather than assisting them in the rescue. In one instance, President Rodríguez disrupted a foreign rescue team from working to thank them.[^cite_note-Martín30a-165]



Venezuelan citizens search through the rubble of destroyed buildings in La Guaira

More than 90 hours after the earthquakes, rescue operations were still challenged by insufficient specialized manpower and some equipment breaking down.[^cite_note-elnacional29c-77] According to Leonardo Malvasida, a heavy equipment operator, he and 11 others arrived from [Lara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lara_\(state\)) to assist in rescue efforts but found no equipment prepared. He added that many qualified operators were in La Guaira, but there were no machinery for use. Those that were available were either privately owned and their owners restricted its use, or were inoperable due to lack of fuel or mechanical faults. Machinery began to arrive on 26 and 27 June, but they were insufficient for the scale of destruction. The official response when asked for additional equipment was to wait until current teams have completed their rescue efforts.[^cite_note-166]

Some international rescue teams had ended rescue operations by early July. Teams from Spain and the Netherlands ended their operations as they were unable to find survivors and added that the chance of discovering anyone alive was very slim.[^cite_note-167] Rescuers from Colombia and some United States rescue teams also said they were ending operations for similar reasons.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] In some areas where signs of life were detected, rescuers spent many hours digging through the rubble only to find no survivors.[^cite_note-ST3a-118] By the tenth day, most rescue operations had ceased and people began to recover the dead.[^cite_note-170]

Some survivors, including children,[^cite_note-171] spent up to eight days beneath the rubble before they were rescued. One person was Hernán Gil, a guard at the Galerias Playa Grande mall which collapsed, but the concrete booth he was working in shielded him from any injuries.[^cite_note-BBC_2_July-172][^cite_note-Guardian_2_July-173] He was rescued after eight days, after rescue workers from seven countries worked for more than three days to free him, as the tunnel rescuers had excavated suffered multiple collapses. He became a symbol of hope for Venezuela.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] The rescue team was awarded the [Hero of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_Venezuela?action=edit\&redlink=1) medal by the Venezuelan government.[^cite_note-176] As of 13 July, more than 2,400 foreign rescuers, 31,800 government personnel, and 30,500 volunteers remained actively deployed.[^cite_note-177] On 23 July, almost a month after the earthquakes, local personnel found two people alive beneath the rubble of a collapsed building.[^cite_note-178]

## Aftermath



Buildings in the "Hugo Chávez" housing development affected by the earthquake

Eight hospitals in the greater Caracas area and twelve private health facilities were activated for triage by the health ministry. Many hospitals were strained by the high influx of injured, and were already facing manpower, supply, and equipment shortages before the disaster.[^cite_note-Benezra27a-159] The World Health Organization said three hospitals sustained heavy damage and another six were damaged or running at partial capacity following an inspection. Its spokesperson, Christian Lindmeier, said in its early inquiries revealed disorganized delivery services and patient management, increasing surgical queues, and overcrowding. He also added that several midwifery professionals went missing in La Guaira, leading to a "critical gap in obstetric care."[^cite_note-179] Four hospitals in Caracas were found with damage; two were heavily damaged and patients were relocated to other facilities, including a field hospital established by the Indian rescue team.[^cite_note-180]

Injured patients who cannot be treated in La Guaira were transported to Caracas's Pérez Carreño Hospital. Some victims died on the way to the hospital. The hospital's morgue was so overwhelmed with bodies that many were taken to other facilities at Bello Monte and Llanito.[^cite_note-Benezra27a-159] The initial influx of patients were those with [crush injuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crush_injuries) and broken bones. Over the days, the type of injuries physicians increasingly encountered were permanent loss of limbs, kidney failure and [crush syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crush_syndrome). Some also arrived seriously dehydrated and with [rhabdomyolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhabdomyolysis). At the Ricardo Baquero González Hospital, Caracas, all emergency supplies were depleted within four hours of the earthquakes, so residents began donating their own supplies. At one health facility in La Guaira, patients were treated in its parking lot as there was no more space indoors. Meanwhile, some private health centers in Caracas began providing free treatment.[^cite_note-Itriago27a-181] Commercial buildings in La Guaira were transformed into makeshift health centers. A McDonald's in Caraballeda was converted into a field hospital with a triage area, pharmacy, storage unit, and specialized in psychological and veterinary needs, with 30 doctors supporting these care. A bus terminal in Catia La Mar treated about 4,000 patients.[^cite_note-ST4b-182]



USMC personnel and Venezuelan citizens conduct search and rescue operations at a collapsed building in [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira)

Aid workers expressed concerns about potential disease outbreaks in the worse-affected areas due to a lack of clean water and poor sanitary conditions, compounded by the existing lack of health specialists. Leading trauma specialist at José Gregorio Hernández Hospital in Caracas, Eugenio Cova, said patients may introduce infections. Veronique Durroux of the United Nations said there could be an outbreak of [vector-borne diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector-borne_disease) given the hot climate and poor waste control.[^cite_note-183] Doctors were increasingly wary as a rising number of people had to be treated for diarrhoea, dysentery, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting.[^cite_note-ST4b-182] Doctors began reporting a rise in skin diseases and diarrhea in Catia La Mar about two weeks after the earthquakes. There was also a surge in requests for medications treating chronic health problems like diabetes and high blood pressure. They linked these occurrences to the poor hygiene conditions and lack of clean water.[^cite_note-184]

Relatives began posting pictures of missing people on social media in the hope that they would be found and later started creating websites independently to report the missing, found and deceased. The Associated Press reported that Venezuelans were sharing details of their missing relatives on digital flyers on platforms such as [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp), [Facebook](7529378.md), [Instagram](31591547.md) and [X](9988187.md). However, social media restrictions in the country by the government made the search for missing people challenging. On 26 June, the United Nations appealed to ease those restrictions, and X was subsequently accessible.[^cite_note-Janetsky26a-185] Three days later, some users later reported that X was inaccessible.[^cite_note-186]

Over 14,600 displaced people across the country were settled into 87 makeshift camps. In La Guaira, there were more than 8,600 people living in 26 shelters. Authorities also created 39 makeshift shelters in Caracas with the capacity for 11,100 people, though only about 5,000 people were living there. Another 1,060 people were distributed across 22 sites in Miranda.[^cite_note-187]

Many displaced residents of La Guaira moved into in schools, sports complexes, stadiums, and other public buildings that became shelters. There, some reported fights and other conflicts among the crowd. In the José María Vargas Sports Complex, more than 1,700 residents brought their tents and mattresses into the game courts. The República de Panamá School also became a shelter for over 350 people, where a single classroom could house three families. Many of these shelter residents spend their day returning to the site of collapsed buildings to search for missing relatives or salvage their belongings. A member of the non-profit, Maniapure Foundation, warned of overcrowding in these facilities, and that aid should be distributed fairly among these shelters. [Plan International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_International) member Geraldine Gómez said some shelters lack basic hygiene facilities or amenities for women and children.[^cite_note-188]

In Caracas, many displaced people slept on the streets while they await approval from engineers who had to ensure their residential buildings were safe for occupants. Along México Avenue, more than 270 families camped outside the Andrés Bello High School because their apartment was severely damaged. At the Continente residential complex, engineers determined that its basement sustained heavy damage and needed repairs before its occupants could move back inside. Other buildings in [La Candelaria Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Candelaria_Parish) were declared unsafe. In La Hoyada, about 160 households sought refuge at Plaza Darío Vivas after their buildings, built under the [Great Mission Housing Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mission_Housing_Venezuela) scheme, were damaged.[^cite_note-189]

Reports of looting in La Guaira began in the immediate aftermath. One video on social media also showed people ransacking the remains of a collapsed store. Residents said people were looting supermarkets, a pharmacy, other shops, and among the rubble. People were also stealing fuel from cars and impersonating rescue workers to their advantage. Some soldiers and police officers were also caught in the act by residents.[^cite_note-190] Multiple videos and accusations were made online about security forces looting homes. In another video, a police officer was confronted by people for stealing a stash of cash which they managed to remove.[^cite_note-191] Four officers of the [Cuerpo de Investigaciones Científicas, Penales y Criminalísticas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuerpo_de_Investigaciones_Cient%C3%ADficas,_Penales_y_Criminal%C3%ADsticas) were arrested and discharged of duties after they were caught stealing, and were expected to be tried, according to its director, [Douglas Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Rico).[^cite_note-192]

## Response

Acting president [Delcy Rodríguez](44199228.md) speaking about the earthquakes along with [Jorge Rodríguez](8767262.md) and [Diosdado Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Cabello).





Collection centers in [Naguanagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naguanagua_Municipality) and [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_M%C3%A9rida).

Immediately after the earthquake, Venezuelan acting president [Delcy Rodríguez](44199228.md) designated the [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira) area a disaster zone.[^cite_note-eluniverso24a-193] A [state of emergency](298608.md) was declared in Venezuela by acting president Rodríguez.[^cite_note-194] Interior Minister [Diosdado Cabello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Cabello) said that "several" states were impacted.[^cite_note-Buschschlüter1-105] The government ordered the gas supply to be turned off in order to avoid explosions in collapsed buildings in Caracas.[^cite_note-195] Services on the [Caracas Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_Metro) were suspended and schools were to remain closed for the coming days.[^cite_note-196] Rodríguez also said she would appeal for funds from multilateral organizations to support search and rescue operations.[^cite_note-ST24b-149] Rodríguez initiated the creation of US\$200 million in funds from the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md).[^cite_note-197] The [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Venezuela\)) announced that classes would be canceled for several days and that some school buildings would be used as shelters and donation centers.[^cite_note-WSLS-198] The [Caracas Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracas_Stock_Exchange) was closed and was to be re-purposed to help rescue efforts.[^cite_note-f24-114]

On 29 June, Rodríguez announced an inspection committee to evaluate damage incurred to homes and other infrastructure. The committee would comprise public institutions, engineering organizations and universities. These inspections would determine if the building is safe for occupation based on a "traffic light system" of red, yellow and green.[^cite_note-elnacional29b-97] A similar system was also announced by mayor Duque of Chacao, Caracas.[^cite_note-elnacional29a-199] Rodríguez also announced plans for a rehousing project for those made homeless. The project's chair, [Jorge Rodríguez](8767262.md), would work with the Ministry of Habitat and Housing, and local administrations in La Guaira, Aragua, Miranda, Carabobo and Falcón states.[^cite_note-elnacional29b-97] Rodríguez said they were in talks with experts and international organizations offering immediate housing, and that assured those affected that they would be housed by the end of the year.[^cite_note-200]

Nationwide, citizens organized themselves in several cities to create collection centers to gather supplies. In [Falcón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falc%C3%B3n) state, more than 20 supply collection centers were established to assist those affected in the Silva municipality, including the headquarters of the National College of Journalists in [Paraguaná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguan%C3%A1_Peninsula); the Comprehensive Defense Operational Zone (ZODI) established safe-conduct passes to transport donations.[^cite_note-201] Reports later emerged of officials blocking, preventing, or forbidding aid or the organization of collection centers in the affected areas, by requiring permits from the population and direct collaboration with state authorities. In [Altamira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altamira,_Caracas), Caracas, police seized the supplies and demanded that they be transported in official state vehicles. In Guanarito, [Portuguesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguesa_\(state\)), Civil Protection teams demanded that citizens hand over supplies and said that only they could run a collection center. At [Obispos Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obispos_Municipality) in Barinas, the national police shut down a collection center.[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203] The mayors of [Charallave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charallave) in Miranda and [Campo Elías Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_El%C3%ADas_Municipality) in Mérida also prohibited collection centers started by civilians with the former saying that donations can only be delivered to the headquarters of the [United Socialist Party of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Socialist_Party_of_Venezuela).[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205]

Political opponents of the ruling party criticized these actions, accusing Rodríguez and her administration of politicizing the disaster response. Opposition leaders Heidy Loicett and María Oropeza called these actions "political persecution" and an attempt to control humanitarian aid and improve the government's public image. Former academic at [Andrés Bello Catholic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Bello_Catholic_University), Benigno Alarcón, said past Venezuelan leaders have attempted to capitalize on disasters including [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez). Cynthia Arnson, an international relations analyst at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), said these earthquakes could expose the government's inability to provide basic needs and handle the aftermath, which could delay the country's [democratic transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Venezuela).[^cite_note-206] The [International Rescue Committee](196452.md) also said the response was not adequate given the scale of destruction. In addition, international journalists reported that the government forces were blocking rescuers in their attempts to aid in the affected areas,[^cite_note-207] including and even impeding international rescue teams from entering the country.[^cite_note-Las_Americas_equipo_Alemania-208] In response, Rodríguez said such criticisms were distasteful and were "narratives manufactured in propaganda laboratories.”[^cite_note-209]
