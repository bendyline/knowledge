The **Swedish Security Service** ([Swedish](26689.md): *Säkerhetspolisen* [\[ˈsɛ̂ːkɛrheːtspʊˌliːsɛn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swedish), **Säpo** [\[ˈsɛ̌ːpʊ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swedish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/80/Sv-S%C3%84PO.ogg/Sv-S%C3%84PO.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/80/Sv-S%C3%84PO.ogg/Sv-S%C3%84PO.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, lit. 'The security-police', formerly *Rikspolisstyrelsens säkerhetsavdelning*, **RPS/Säk**, lit. 'Security Department of the Realm Police Board'[^cite_note-3] until 1989) is a [Swedish](5058739.md) [government agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_agencies_in_Sweden) organized under the [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Sweden\)). It operates as a [security agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_agency) responsible for [counter-espionage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-espionage), [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism), as well as the protection of [dignitaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodyguard) and the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Sweden). The Swedish Security Service is also tasked with investigating crimes against [national security](240468.md) and terrorist crimes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_20138-4][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'Yearbook'9-5][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESFS_1984\:387§ 3-6] Its main mission, however, is to prevent crimes, not to investigate them. Crime prevention is to a large extent based on information acquired via contacts with the [regular police force](47359232.md), other authorities and organisations, foreign intelligence and security services, and with the use of various [intelligence gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_assessment) activities, including [interrogations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interrogation), [wiretapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiretapping), [covert listening devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covert_listening_device), and hidden surveillance cameras.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESOU_2012\:44114,_118–123-7][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE2014/15\:JuU2-8]

The Service was, in its present form, founded in 1989, as part of the [National Police Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_Board) and became an autonomous police agency on 1 January 2015.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'History'-9][^cite_note-FOOTNOTE2013/14\:JuU1-10] National headquarters are located at Bolstomtavägen[^cite_note-11] in south-east [Solna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solna_Municipality) since 2014, drawing together personnel from five different locations into a single 30,000 m<sup>2</sup> (320,000 sq ft) HQ facility.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETörnmalm2010-12][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESkanska_2015-13]

## History



Lt Col [Adlercreutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Adlercreutz), credited with the formation of the General Security Service in 1938

The origins of the Swedish Security Service is often linked to the establishment of a special police bureau (*Polisbyrån*) during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) in 1914, which reported directly to the [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff_\(Sweden\)), predecessor of the Office for the [Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Commander_of_the_Swedish_Armed_Forces).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENationalencyklopedin_1989-14][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_20147,_9-15] The bureau's main mission was protecting national security (e.g. counter-espionage), and its first chief was Captain [Erik af Edholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_af_Edholm).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_20147,_9-15] Operations shut down after the end of the war in 1918, although some intelligence activities carried on at the Stockholm police, managed by a small group of approximately ten police officers led by Chief Superintendent [Eric Hallgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Hallgren), who later was to become the first chief of the General Security Service (*Allmänna säkerhetstjänsten*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENationalencyklopedin_1989-14][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_20147,_10,_15-16] Operations were mainly focused on monitoring [communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) from the start of the war until the early 1930s when the service also began to focus on [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazis).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_20149–12-17]

In 1932, operations were transferred to the newly formed State Police (*statspolisen*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENationalencyklopedin_1989-14] The group of officers working at the State Police did not have the means to monitor phone calls or to intercept and open mail. This, and the general lack of staff and financial resources worried the chief of Sweden's military intelligence, Lieutenant-Colonel [Carlos Adlercreutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Adlercreutz), who felt the country needed a more powerful security agency if Europe once again ended up in war. Thus, in 1938 the General Security Service was formed, following an initiative by Adlercreutz and [Ernst Leche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Leche) at the [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Sweden\)), among others.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_201415–16-18] The entire organisation and its activities were top-secret. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) the agency monitored about 25,000 phone calls and intercepted over 200,000 letters every week.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_201416–18-19] In 1946, following a [post-war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_World_War_II_in_Europe) parliamentary evaluation, operations were significantly reduced and once again organized under the State Police, mainly tasked with counter-espionage.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENationalencyklopedin_1989-14][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_20147,_19-20] In 1965, the Swedish police was nationalized, and all work was organized under the [National Police Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_Board) in the Department of Security (*Rikspolisstyrelsens säkerhetsavdelning*, abbreviated RPS/SÄK).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_20145-21][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrahn2013-22]



Stig Wennerström, convicted Soviet spy, c. 1960

The period between 1939 and 1945 was marked by extensive foreign intelligence activity in Sweden, resulting in the arrest of numerous spies and enemy agents. Some of the most notorious post-war spies are [Fritiof Enbom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritiof_Enbom), [Hilding Andersson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilding_Andersson?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Stig Wennerström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stig_Wennerstr%C3%B6m_\(spy\)) and [Stig Bergling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stig_Bergling). In all of these cases the spying was done on behalf of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and the spies were convicted to [lifetime of penal labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_labor), the supreme penalty under civilian law. All were, however, pardoned after roughly ten years.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENationalencyklopedin_1989-14][^cite_note-23]

In the early 1970s, Sweden was rocked by a number of terrorist acts perpetrated by [Croatian separatists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_National_Resistance). Some of the most significant cases were the [1971 Yugoslavian embassy attack in Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Yugoslav_Embassy_shooting) and the hijacking of [Scandinavian Airlines System Flight 130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_Airlines_System_Flight_130) a year later.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEForsberg2003Bilaga_1-24] The inception of the first Terrorist Act in 1973 was an immediate policy upshot of this, which among other things gave the police the right to deport people affiliated with terrorist organizations without delay. These incidents also led to internal changes within the Department of Security, which received more resources.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_20145,_35,_40-25][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHansén200747–48,_175,_178-26] On 28 February 1986, [Prime Minister](195846.md) [Olof Palme was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Olof_Palme) by an unknown gunman. The department was not widely criticized, partly because Palme himself had declined protection on the night of the murder.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHansén2007163-27] It nevertheless sparked the resignation of the [National Police Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_Commissioner_\(Sweden\)) [Nils Erik Åhmansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nils_Erik_%C3%85hmansson) and the head of the department, [Sune Sandström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sune_Sandstr%C3%B6m), following the revelation of the [Ebbe Carlsson affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebbe_Carlsson_affair) in 1988.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIsaksson2007-28]

The Swedish Security Service was established on 1 October 1989, on the recommendations put forward by a Government committee tasked with evaluating the Department of Security following the assassination of Palme.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEForsberg200318-29] The new agency was—although still formally a part of the National Police Board—more independent, with its own Director-General and political oversight also increased.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_201312-30][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_201447-31][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHansén200789–90,_178-32] Furthermore, the Service took over the formal responsibility for all close protection tasks, which was previously shared with the National Police Board and the Stockholm County Police.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_201447-31][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHansén200787-33] On 10 September 2003, [Minister for Foreign Affairs](462050.md) [Anna Lindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Lindh) was assassinated by [Mijailo Mijailović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mijailo_Mijailovi%C4%87), who was arrested two weeks later. The Government reviewed its procedures in the wake of the Lindh killing,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESOU_2004\:108-34] which led to the doubling of the number of close-protection officers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'Livvakter'-35] On 1 January 2015, the police reorganized again into a unified agency, with the Swedish Security Service becoming a fully independent agency.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Ministry_of_Justice_2015-36]

## Areas of responsibility

The Swedish Security Service is responsible for five core areas of national security:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESFS_1984\:387§ 3-6][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENationalencyklopedin_1989-14]

### Counter-espionage

Prevention and detection of [espionage](10501.md) and other illegal intelligence activities directed at Sweden, its [national interests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_interests) abroad, and foreign interests and individuals in Sweden.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'Yearbook'9-5]

### Counter-subversion

Countering illegal [subversive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subversion) activities aimed at influencing political decisions or restricting constitutional rights and freedoms, including violence, threats and harassment directed at elected representatives, public officials and journalists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'Yearbook'9-5]

### Counter-terrorism

Prevention and detection of [terrorism](30636.md), including acts directed at Sweden or foreign interests in Sweden, as well as financing and support of terrorist organisations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'Yearbook'9-5]

### Dignitary protection

Provision of security and [close protection officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_protection_officer) for state visits, senior public officials, members of the [Royal Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Royal_Family) and foreign diplomatic representatives.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'Yearbook'9-5][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESFS_2014\:1103§ 4-37]

As of 2014, the Service employed 130 close protection officers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'Yearbook'9-5]

### Protective security

Provision of advice, analysis and oversight to government agencies and companies important to national security, including [background checks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Background_checks).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'Yearbook'9-5]

## Organisation

The Swedish Security Service became a separate agency 1 January 2015, and is directly organized under the Ministry of Justice. Similar to other government agencies in Sweden, it is essentially autonomous. Under the [1974 Instrument of Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Instrument_of_Government), neither the Government nor individual ministers have the right to influence how an agency decide in a particular case or on the application of legislation. This also applies to the Security Service, which instead is governed by general policy instruments.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeckmanOlssonWockelberg200319–20-38][^cite_note-A-39] What sets the Security Service apart from other agencies is that most directives guiding the Service are classified on the grounds of national security, along with the bulk of the reports it produces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_201312-30] The Service is led by a Director-General, who is titled *Head of the Swedish Security Service*. Operations are led by a Chief Operating Officer, reporting directly the Head of the Security Service. He is in turn assisted by a Deputy Chief Operating Officer and an Office for Operations. The Service is organized into four departments and a secretariat, each led by a Head of Department.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwedish_Security_Service_2015,_'Organisation'-40]

Department of Intelligence Collection

Responsible for security intelligence work, primarily aimed at providing the Service with data for decisions regarding security measures.

Department of Security Measures

Deals with threat mitigation and risk reduction measures. Areas of responsibility include [close protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodyguard), investigations, [information security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_security), [physical security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_security) and background checks.

## Reorganization

Sweden's Foreign Minister, [Maria Malmer Stenergard](72039473.md) announced the cteation of a formal civilian Inteligance agency termed the Sveriges utrikes underrättelsetjänst or UND focused on foreign Intelligence.[^cite_note-NDS_UND_2026-42] The UND will absorb responcibility for Covert Human Intelligence [HUMINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HUMINT) and will work alongside MUST on military Intelligence and [Säpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A4po) on domestic Intelligence.[^cite_note-NDS_UND_2026-42]

### Offices

The Service has a regional presence and operates from several locations; from its headquarters in [Solna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solna) and from six regional units with offices in [Umeå](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ume%C3%A5), [Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala), [Örebro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96rebro), [Norrköping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norrk%C3%B6ping), [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg) and [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6). The Service has approximately 1,100 employees, of which about 10 percent are stationed at the regional offices. ## In popular culture
