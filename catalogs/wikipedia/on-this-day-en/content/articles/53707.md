**Gujarat** ([Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language): *Gujarāt*, pronounced [\[ˈɡudʒəɾaːt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Gujarati) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d5/Gujarat.ogg/Gujarat.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d5/Gujarat.ogg/Gujarat.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a state along the [western coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_India) of India. Its coastline of about 2,340 km (1,450 mi) is the longest in the country, most of which lies on the [Kathiawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathiawar) peninsula. Gujarat is the [fifth-largest Indian state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area), covering some 196,024 km<sup>2</sup> (75,685 mi<sup>2</sup>); and the [ninth-most populous state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population), with a population of 60.4 million in 2011. It is bordered by [Rajasthan](26291.md) to the northeast, [Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadra_and_Nagar_Haveli_and_Daman_and_Diu) to the south, [Maharashtra](20629.md) to the southeast, [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md) to the east, and the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) and the [Pakistani](23235.md) province of [Sindh](28972.md) to the west. Gujarat's capital city is [Gandhinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhinagar), while its largest city is [Ahmedabad](206337.md).[^cite_note-13] The [Gujaratis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_people) are indigenous to the state, and their language, [Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language), is the state's official language.

Gujarat state [encompasses 23 sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indus_Valley_civilisation_sites#List_of_Indus_Valley_sites_discovered) of the ancient [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation) (more than any other state). The most important sites are [Lothal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothal) (the world's first dry dock), [Dholavira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dholavira) (the fifth largest site), and [Gola Dhoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gola_Dhoro) (where five uncommon seals were found). Lothal is believed to have been one of the world's first [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_ports).[^cite_note-kulke-14] [Gujarat's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Sultanate) coastal cities, chiefly [Bharuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharuch) and [Khambhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khambhat),[^cite_note-15] served as ports and trading centres during the [Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) and [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) empires, and during the succession of royal [Saka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka) dynasties in the [Western Satraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Satraps) era.[^cite_note-Hinduism_and_modernity-16][^cite_note-books.google.com-17] Along with [Bihar](442482.md), [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram) and [Nagaland](408677.md), Gujarat is one of the four Indian states to [prohibit the sale of alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_prohibition_in_India).[^cite_note-18] The [Gir National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gir_National_Park) in Gujarat is home to the only wild population of the [Asiatic lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_lion) in the world.[^cite_note-19]

Gujarat is regarded as one of the most industrialised states and has a low [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_unemployment_rate).[^cite_note-Gujarat_info1-20] The [economy of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Gujarat) is the [fourth-largest in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP), with a gross state domestic product (GSDP) of [₹](490783.md)33.24 trillion (US\$350 billion) and has the country's [ninth-highest GSDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP_per_capita) of ₹371,000 (US\$3,900).[^cite_note-Gujarat_GSDP-21] Gujarat has the [highest exports of all states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_exports), accounting for around one-third of national exports.[^cite_note-22] It ranks [21st among Indian states and union territories in human development index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_Human_Development_Index).[^cite_note-snhdi-gdl-23] The world's largest data center is being developed by [Reliance Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Industries) in [Jamnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamnagar).[^cite_note-24] The state has one of the lowest crime rates, with Ahmedabad, [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara) and [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat) crowned among the five safest cities in India.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Etymology

Parts of modern [Rajasthan](26291.md) and Gujarat were known as the *Gurjarat* or *[Gurjaradesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjaradesha)* region for centuries before the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) period.[^cite_note-27] It is generally believed that the region derived its name from the [Gurjars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjars).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

## History

Main article: [History of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Gujarat)

### Ancient history

Gujarat was one of the main central areas of the Indus Valley Civilisation, which was spread across modern-day northern India and Pakistan.[^cite_note-30] It contains ancient metropolitan cities from the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation) such as [Lothal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothal), [Dholavira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dholavira) and [Gola Dhoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gola_Dhoro).[^cite_note-31] The ancient city of Lothal was where India's first port was established.[^cite_note-kulke-14] The ancient city of Dholavira is one of the largest and most prominent archaeological sites in India, belonging to the Indus Valley Civilisation. The most recent discovery was Gola Dhoro. Altogether, about fifty Indus Valley settlement ruins have been discovered in Gujarat.[^cite_note-mapsofindia.com-32]

The ancient history of Gujarat was enriched by the commercial activities of its inhabitants. There is clear historical evidence of trade and commerce ties with [Egypt](8087628.md), [Bahrain](18933277.md) and [Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer) in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) during the time period of 1000 to 750 BCE.[^cite_note-mapsofindia.com-32][^cite_note-34] There was a succession of various polities such as the [Mauryan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_dynasty), [Satavahana dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty), [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire), [Gurjara-Pratihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjara-Pratihara) Empire, as well as regional ones such as the [Western Satraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Satraps), the [Kingdom of Valabhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Valabhi), the Kingdom of Gujarat, the [Sultanate of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Gujarat), and finally the [Kingdom of Baroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Baroda).

The early history of Gujarat includes the imperial grandeur of [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya) who conquered a number of earlier states in what is now Gujarat. Pushyagupta, a [Vaishya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishya), was appointed the governor of [Saurashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurashtra_\(region\)) by the Mauryan regime. He ruled Girinagar (modern-day [Junagadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junagadh)) (322 BCE to 294 BCE) and built a dam on the Sudarshan lake. Emperor [Ashoka the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka), the grandson of Chandragupta Maurya, not only ordered his edicts engraved in the rock at Junagadh, but also asked Governor Tusherpha to cut canals from the lake where an earlier Indian governor had built a dam. Between the decline of Mauryan power and Saurashtra coming under the sway of the [Samprati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samprati) Mauryas of [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain), there was an [Indo-Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Greek) defeat in Gujarat of [Demetrius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_I_of_Bactria). In 16th-century manuscripts, there is an apocryphal story of a merchant of King [Gondophares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondophares) landing in Gujarat with [Apostle Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostle_Thomas). The incident of the cup-bearer torn apart by a lion might indicate that the port city described is in Gujarat.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

For nearly 300 years from the start of the 1st century CE, [Saka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka) rulers played a prominent part in Gujarat's history. The weather-beaten rock at [Junagadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junagadh) gives a glimpse of the ruler [Rudradaman I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudradaman_I) (100 CE) of the Saka satraps known as [Western Satraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Satraps), or Kshatraps. Mahakshatrap Rudradaman I founded the [Kardamaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kardamaka) dynasty which ruled from [Anupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anupa) on the banks of the [Narmada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada) up to the [Aparanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aparanta) region bordering Punjab. In Gujarat, several battles were fought between the Indian dynasties such as the [Satavahana dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty) and the Western Satraps. The greatest and the mightiest ruler of the Satavahana dynasty was [Gautamiputra Satakarni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautamiputra_Satakarni) who defeated the Western Satraps and conquered some parts of Gujarat in the 2nd century CE.[^cite_note-37]

The Kshatrapa dynasty was replaced by the [Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) with the conquest of Gujarat by [Chandragupta Vikramaditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Vikramaditya). Vikramaditya's successor [Skandagupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skandagupta) left an inscription (450 CE) on a rock at Junagadh which gives details of the governor's repairs to the embankment surrounding Sudarshan lake after it was damaged by floods. The [Anarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarta) and [Saurashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurashtra_\(region\)) regions were both parts of the Gupta empire. Towards the middle of the 5th century, the Gupta empire went into decline. Senapati Bhatarka, the general of the Guptas, took advantage of the situation and in 470 set up the [Kingdom of Valabhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Valabhi). He shifted his capital from Giringer to [Valabhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valabhi), near [Bhavnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavnagar), on Saurashtra's east coast. The [Maitrakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitraka) of Vallabhi became very powerful with their rule prevailing over large parts of Gujarat and adjoining [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa). A university was set up by the Maitrakas, which came to be known far and wide for its scholastic pursuits and was compared with the noted [Nalanda University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalanda_University). It was during the rule of Dhruvasena Maitrak that Chinese philosopher-traveler [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang)/[I Tsing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Tsing) visited in 640 along the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road).[^cite_note-38]

Gujarat was known to the [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks) and was familiar with other Western centres of civilisation through the end of the European [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). The oldest written record of Gujarat's 2,000-year maritime history is documented in a Greek book titled *[The Periplus of the Erythraean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea): Travel and Trade in the Indian Ocean by a Merchant of the First Century*.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### Medieval history



[Sun Temple of Modhera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Temple,_Modhera), with [stepwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepwell) surrounding the "[kunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_tank)" (tank), was built by [Bhima I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhima_I), King of Gujarat in 1026. It is one of the finest examples of [stepwell architecture of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_stepwells_in_Gujarat).

In the early 8th century, the [Arabs](2185.md) of the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) established an empire in the name of the rising religion of [Islam](6037917.md), which stretched from [Spain](26667.md) in the west to [Afghanistan](737.md) and modern-day [Pakistan](23235.md) in the east. Al-Junaid, the successor of [Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Qasim), finally subdued the Hindu resistance within [Sindh](28972.md) and established a secure base. The Arab rulers tried to expand their empire southeast, which culminated in the [Caliphate campaigns in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate_campaigns_in_India) fought in 730; they were defeated and expelled west of the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus) river, probably by a coalition of the Indian rulers [Nagabhata I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagabhata_I) of the [Gurjara-Pratihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjara-Pratihara) dynasty, [Vikramaditya II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikramaditya_II) of the [Chalukya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalukya) dynasty and [Bappa Rawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bappa_Rawal) of the [Guhila dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guhila_dynasty). After this victory, the Arab invaders were driven out of Gujarat. General [Pulakeshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avanijanashraya_Pulakeshin), a Chalukya prince of [Lata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lata_\(region\)), received the title *Avanijanashraya* (refuge of the people of the earth) and honorific of "Repeller of the unrepellable" by the Chalukya emperor Vikramaditya II for his victory at the battle at [Navsari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navsari), where the Arab troops suffered a crushing defeat.[^cite_note-41]

In the late 8th century, the Kannauj Triangle period started. The three major Indian dynasties – the northwestern Indian Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty, the southern Indian [Rashtrakuta dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_dynasty) and the eastern Indian [Pala Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pala_Empire) – dominated India from the 8th to 10th centuries. During this period the northern part of Gujarat was ruled by the northern Indian Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty and the southern part of Gujarat was ruled by the southern Indian Rashtrakuta dynasty.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-42] However, the earliest epigraphical records of the Gurjars of [Broach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharuch) attest that the royal bloodline of the Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty of [Dadda I, II and III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjaras_of_Lata) (650–750) ruled south Gujarat.[^cite_note-43] Southern Gujarat was ruled by the Indian Rashtrakuta dynasty until it was captured by the Indian ruler [Tailapa II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailapa_II) of the [Western Chalukya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Chalukya_Empire).[^cite_note-Gajrani_32-44]

[Zoroastrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrians_in_Iran) from [Greater Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Iran) migrated to the western borders of India (Gujarat and [Sindh](28972.md)) during the 8th or 10th century,[^cite_note-Hodivala_1920_88-45] to avoid [persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Zoroastrians) by Muslim invaders who were in the process of conquering Iran. The descendants of those Zoroastrian refugees came to be known as the [Parsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsi).[^cite_note-boy148-46][^cite_note-khan17-47][^cite_note-jack-48][^cite_note-bleek212-49]

Subsequently, [Lāṭa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lata_\(region\)) in southern Gujarat was ruled by the [Rashtrakuta dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_dynasty) until it was captured by the [Western Chalukya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Chalukyas) ruler [Tailapa II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tailapa_II).[^cite_note-Gajrani_32-44][^cite_note-Wink1991-50]

The Chaulukya dynasty[^cite_note-horace_glossary_punjab_nwfp-51] ruled the Kingdom of Gujarat from 960 to 1243. Gujarat was a major center of Indian Ocean trade, and their capital at [Anhilwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhilwara) ([Patan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patan,_Gujarat)) was one of the largest cities in India, with a population estimated at 100,000 in the year 1000. Under the rule of [Subhatvarman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhatavarman) (1194–1209 CE), a Parmara ruler in central India, Jainism faced hardships as a result of the ruler's animosity towards the religion.[^cite_note-dp-52] Subhatavarman attacked Gujarat and plundered large number of Jain temples in Dabhoi and Cambay in 11th century.[^cite_note-53] Subhatavarman, during his campaigns in Lata, destroyed numerous Jain temples.[^cite_note-dp-52] After 1243, the Solankis lost control of Gujarat to their feudatories, of whom the [Vaghela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaghela_dynasty) chiefs of [Dholka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dholka) came to rule the Kingdom of Gujarat. In 1292, the Vaghelas became tributaries of the [Yadava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seuna_Yadavas_of_Devagiri) dynasty of [Devagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulatabad,_Maharashtra) in the [Deccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau). Karandev of the Vaghela dynasty was the last Hindu ruler of Gujarat. He was defeated and overthrown by the superior forces of [Alauddin Khalji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alauddin_Khalji) from Delhi in 1297. With his defeat, Gujarat became part of the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate), and the Rajput hold over Gujarat would never be restored.

Fragments of printed cotton from Gujarat have been discovered in Egypt, providing evidence for medieval trade in the western Indian Ocean. These fragments represent the Indian cotton traded in Egypt during the [Fatimid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate), [Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) and [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate_\(Cairo\)) periods, from the 10th to 16th centuries. Similar cotton was also traded as far east as Indonesia.[^cite_note-Barnes2017-54]

#### Islamic conquests (1197–1614)



The [Mughal Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Emperor) Akbar triumphantly enters [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat).

After the [Ghoris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghoris) had assumed a position of Muslim supremacy over North India, [Qutbuddin Aibak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutbuddin_Aibak) attempted to conquer Gujarat and annexe it to his empire in 1197, but failed in his ambitions.[^cite_note-Handbuch_der_Orientalistik-55] An independent Muslim community continued to flourish in Gujarat for the next hundred years, championed by Arab merchants settling along the western coast. From 1297 to 1300, [Alauddin Khalji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alauddin_Khalji), the Turko-Afghan [Sultan of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Delhi), destroyed the Hindu metropolis of [Anhilwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhilwara) and incorporated Gujarat into the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate). After [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) sacked [Delhi](37756.md) at the end of the 14th century, weakening the Sultanate, Gujarat's Muslim [Khatri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatri) governor Zafar Khan Muzaffar ([Muzaffar Shah I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffar_Shah_I)) asserted his independence, and his son, Sultan [Ahmed Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Shah_of_Gujarat) (ruled 1411–1442), established [Ahmedabad](206337.md) as the capital. [Khambhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khambhat) eclipsed Bharuch as Gujarat's most important trade port. Gujarat's relations with [Egypt](8087628.md), which was then the premier Arab power in the Middle East, remained friendly over the next century and the [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_people) scholar, *Badruddin-ad-Damamimi*, spent several years in Gujarat in the shade of the Sultan before proceeding to the [Bahmani Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahmani_Sultanate) on the Deccan Plateau.[^cite_note-wink143-56][^cite_note-57]

[Shah e Alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_e_Alam), a famous Sufi saint of the [Chishti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chishti) order who was the descendant of Makhdoom [Jahaniyan Jahangasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahaniyan_Jahangasht) from [Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhara), soon arrived in a group that included Arab theologian *Ibn Suwaid*, several [Sayyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid) Sufi members of the [Aydarus family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_al-Aydarus) of [Tarim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarim,_Yemen) in [Yemen](350939.md),[^cite_note-Sufiorders-58] Iberian court interpreter *Ali al-Andalusi* from [Granada](38264.md),[^cite_note-59] and the Arab jurist *Bahraq* from [Hadramaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadramaut) who was appointed a tutor of the prince.[^cite_note-60] Among the illustrious names who arrived during the reign of [Mahmud Begada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Begada) was the philosopher *Haibatullah Shah Mir* from [Shiraz](39622.md), and the scholar intellectual *Abu Fazl Ghazaruni* from [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia)[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] who tutored and adopted [Abu'l-Fazl ibn Mubarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu'l-Fazl_ibn_Mubarak), author of the *[Akbarnama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbarnama)*.[^cite_note-63] Later, a close alliance between the [Ottoman](22278.md) [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) and Gujarati sultans to effectively safeguard [Jeddah](277718.md) and the [Red Sea](26191.md) trade from Portuguese [imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism), encouraged the existence of powerful [Rumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumi) elites within the kingdom who took the post of [viziers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viziers) in Gujarat keen to maintain ties with the Ottoman state.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

[Humayun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayun) also briefly occupied the province in 1536, but fled due to the threat [Bahadur Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahadur_Shah_of_Gujarat), the Gujarat king, imposed.[^cite_note-69] The [Sultanate of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Gujarat) remained independent until 1572, when the Mughal emperor [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) conquered it and annexed it to the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire).[^cite_note-sen2-70]

The [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat) port (the only Indian port facing west) then became the principal port of India during Mughal rule, gaining widespread international repute. The city of Surat, famous for its exports of silk and [diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamonds), had reached a par with contemporary [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) and [Beijing](18603746.md), great mercantile cities of Europe and Asia,[^cite_note-71] and earned the distinguished title, *Bab al-Makkah* (Gate of Mecca).[^cite_note-Hinduism_and_modernity-16][^cite_note-books.google.com-17]

Drawn by the religious renaissance taking place under Akbar, [Mohammed Ghaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Ghaus) moved to Gujarat and established spiritual centers for the [Shattari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shattari) Sufi order from Iran, founding the [Ek Toda Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ek_Toda_Mosque) and producing such devotees as [Wajihuddin Alvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wajihuddin_Alvi) of Ahmedabad whose many successors moved to [Bijapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijapur) during the height of the [Adil Shahi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adil_Shahi_dynasty).[^cite_note-72] At the same time, Zoroastrian high priest [Azar Kayvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azar_Kayvan) who was a native of [Fars](448049.md), immigrated to Gujarat founding the Zoroastrian school of [illuminationists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminationism) which attracted key Shi'ite Muslim admirers of the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid) philosophical revival from [Isfahan](223149.md).

Early 14th-century [Maghrebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebis) adventurer, [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta), who famously visited India with his entourage, recalls in his memoirs about Cambay, one of the great emporia of the Indian Ocean that indeed:

> [Cambay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambay) is one of the most beautiful cities as regards the artistic architecture of its houses and the construction of its mosques. The reason is that the majority of its inhabitants are foreign merchants, who continually build their beautiful houses and wonderful mosques – an achievement in which they endeavor to surpass each other.



16th-century Portuguese illustration from the [Códice Casanatense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3dice_Casanatense), depicting inhabitants of Gujarat

Many of these "foreign merchants" were transient visitors, men of [South Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Arabia) and [Persian Gulf](24761.md) ports, who migrated in and out of Cambay with the rhythm of the monsoons. But others were men with Arab or Persian patronyms whose families had settled in the town generations, even centuries earlier, intermarrying with Gujarati women, and assimilating everyday customs of the Hindu hinterland.[^cite_note-73]

The [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery) heralded the dawn of pioneer Portuguese and Spanish long-distance travel in search of alternative [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_routes) to "[the East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indies)", moved by the trade of [gold](12240.md), [silver](27119.md) and [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spices). In 1497, Portuguese explorer [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama) is said to have discovered the [Europe-to-India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_the_sea_route_to_India) sea route which changed the course of history, thanks to [Kutchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutchi_people) sailor Kanji Malam, who showed him the route from the East African coasts of [Mozambique](19301.md) sailing onwards to [Calicut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calicut) off the [Malabar coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_coast) in India.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] Later, the Gujarat Sultanate allied with the [Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks) and [Egyptian Mamluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluks) naval fleets led by governor-generals [Malik Ayyaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Ayyaz) and [Amir Husain Al-Kurdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Husain_Al-Kurdi), vanquished the Portuguese in the 1508 [Battle of Chaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chaul) resulting in the first Portuguese defeat at sea in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md).[^cite_note-77]

To 16th-century European observers, Gujarat was a fabulously wealthy country. The customs revenue of Gujarat alone in the early 1570s was nearly three times the total revenue of the whole [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) in Asia in 1586–87, when it was at its height.[^cite_note-78] Indeed, when the British arrived on the coast of Gujarat, houses in [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat) already had windows of [Venetian glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_glass) imported from [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) through the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).[^cite_note-79] In 1514, the Portuguese explorer [Duarte Barbosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duarte_Barbosa) described the cosmopolitan atmosphere of [Rander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rander) known otherwise as *City of Mosques* in Surat province, which gained the fame and reputation of illustrious Islamic scholars, Sufi-saints, merchants and intellectuals from all over the world:[^cite_note-80]

> &#x20;Ranel (Rander) is a good town of the [Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors), built of very pretty houses and squares. It is a rich and agreeable place ... the Moors of the town trade with [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca), [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal), Tawasery (Tannasserim), [Pegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegu), [Martaban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martaban), and [Sumatra](28679.md) in all sort of spices, drugs, silks, musk, benzoin and porcelain. They possess very large and fine ships and those who wish Chinese articles will find them there very completely. The Moors of this place are white and well dressed and very rich they have pretty wives, and in the furniture of these houses have china vases of many kinds, kept in glass cupboards well arranged. Their women are not secluded like other Moors, but go about the city in the day time, attending to their business with their faces uncovered as in other parts.

The conquest of the Kingdom of Gujarat marked a significant event of Akbar's reign. Being the major trade gateway and departure harbour of pilgrim ships to Mecca, it gave the Mughal Empire free access to the Arabian sea and control over the rich commerce that passed through its ports. The territory and income of the empire were vastly increased.[^cite_note-81]

#### The Sultanate of Gujarat and the merchants



A [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrian) [Agiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agiary) in [Udvada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udvada), Gujarat

For the best part of two centuries, the independent [Khatri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatri) [Sultanate of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Gujarat) was the cynosure of its neighbours on account of its wealth and prosperity, which had long made the Gujarati merchant a familiar figure in the ports of the Indian Ocean.[^cite_note-wink143-56][^cite_note-Mehta1991-82] Gujaratis, including Hindus and Muslims as well as the enterprising [Parsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsi) class of [Zoroastrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrians), had been specialising in the organisation of overseas trade for many centuries, and had moved into various branches of commerce such as [commodity trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_trade), [brokerage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokerage), [money-changing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money-changing), [money-lending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money-lending) and [banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking).[^cite_note-83]

By the 17th century, [Chavuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_\(Gujarat\)) and [Baghdadi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdadi_Jews) had assimilated into the social world of the Surat province, later on their descendants would give rise to the [Sassoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sassoon_\(treasurer\)) of Bombay and the [Ezras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezra_Mir) of Calcutta, and other influential Indian-Jewish figures who went on to play a philanthropical role in the commercial development of 19th-century British Crown Colony of [Shanghai](27643.md).[^cite_note-84] Spearheaded by [Khoja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoja_\(community\)), [Bohra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustaali), [Bhatiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhatia_caste) [shahbandars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahbandar) and Moorish [nakhudas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhuda) who dominated sea navigation and shipping, Gujarat's transactions with the outside world had created the legacy of an international transoceanic empire which had a vast commercial network of permanent agents stationed at all the great port cities across the [Indian Ocean](14580.md). These networks extended to the [Philippines](23440.md) in the east, [East Africa](260987.md) in the west, and via maritime and the inland caravan route to [Russia](25391.md) in the north.[^cite_note-85]

[Tomé Pires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A9_Pires), a Portuguese official at [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_City), wrote of conditions during the reigns of Mahmud I and Mozaffar II:

> [Cambay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambay) stretches out two arms; with her right arm she reaches toward [Aden](38050.md) and with the other towards Malacca[^cite_note-86]

He also described Gujarat's active trade with [Goa](40010153.md), the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) and the [Malabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_region). His contemporary, [Duarte Barbosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duarte_Barbosa), describing Gujarat's maritime trade, recorded the import of horses from the Middle East and elephants from Malabar, and lists exports which included muslins, chintzes and silks, carnelian, ginger and other spices, aromatics, opium, indigo and other substances for dyeing, cereals and legumes.[^cite_note-87] [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) was the destination for many of these commodities, and they were partly paid for in [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horses) and [pearls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearls) taken from [Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormuz_Island).[^cite_note-88] The latter item, in particular, led Sultan [Sikandar Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikandar_Lodi) of [Delhi](37756.md), according to Ali-Muhammad Khan, author of the Mirat-i-Ahmadi, to complain that the

> support of the throne of Delhi is wheat and barley but the foundation of the realm of Gujarat is coral and pearls[^cite_note-89]

Hence, the sultans of Gujarat possessed ample means to sustain lavish patronage of religion and the arts, to build madrasas and ḵānaqāhs, and to provide douceurs for the literati, mainly poets and historians, whose presence and praise enhanced the fame of the dynasty.[^cite_note-iranicaonline.org-90]



[Jama Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jama_Mosque,_Ahmedabad), Ahmedabad

Even at the time of [Tomé Pires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A9_Pires)' travel to the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies) in the early 16th century, Gujarati merchants had earned an international reputation for their commercial acumen and this encouraged the visit of merchants from [Cairo](6293.md), [Armenia](10918072.md), [Abyssinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire), [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan), [Shiraz](39622.md), [Turkestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkestan) and [Guilans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilan_Province) from Aden and Hormuz.[^cite_note-91] Pires noted in his *Suma Orientale*:[^cite_note-92]

> &#x20;These \[people] are \[like] Italians in their knowledge of and dealings in merchandise ... they are men who understand merchandise; they are so properly steeped in the sound and harmony of it, that the *Gujaratees* say that any offence connected with merchandise is pardonable. There are *Gujaratees* settled everywhere. They work some for some and others for others. They are diligent, quick men in trade. They do their accounts with fingers like ours and with our very writings.

#### Gujarat in the Mughal Empire

Gujarat was one of the twelve original [subahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subah) (imperial top-level provinces) established by [Mughal Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Emperor) (*Badshah*) [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar), with a seat at Ahmedabad, bordering on [Thatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatta) (Sindh), [Ajmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmer), [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) and later Ahmadnagar subahs.

[Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb), the sixth Mughal Emperor, was born in [Dahod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahod), Gujarat. He was the third son and sixth child of [Shah Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jahan) and [Mumtaz Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumtaz_Mahal).[^cite_note-93] At the time of his birth, his father, Shah Jahan, was then the Subahdar (governor) of Gujarat, and his grandfather, [Jehangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehangir), was the Mughal Emperor. Before he became emperor, Aurangzeb was made Subahdar of Gujarat subah as part of his training and was stationed at Ahmedabad.

Aurangzeb had great love for his place of birth. In 1704, he wrote a letter to his eldest son, [Muhammad Azam Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Azam_Shah), asking him to be kind and considerate to the people of Dahod as it was his birthplace. Muhammad Azam was then the Subedar (governor) of Gujarat.[^cite_note-DNA_May2012-94]

In his letter, Aurangzeb wrote:[^cite_note-DNA_May2012-94]

> &#x20;My son of exalted rank, the town of Dahod, one of the dependencies of Gujarat, is the birthplace of this sinner. Please consider a regard for the inhabitants of that town as incumbent on you.

### Maratha Empire

When the cracks had started to develop in the edifice of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) in the mid-17th century, the [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy) were consolidating their power in the west, [Chatrapati Shivaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatrapati_Shivaji), the great Maratha ruler, attacked Surat in southern Gujarat twice first in 1664 and again in 1672.[^cite_note-95] These attacks marked the entry of the Marathas into Gujarat. However, before the Maratha had made inroads into Gujarat, the Europeans had made their presence felt, led by the Portuguese, and followed by the Dutch and the English.

The [Peshwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) had established sovereignty over parts of Gujarat and collected taxes and tributes through their representatives. [Damaji Rao Gaekwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damaji_Rao_Gaekwad) and [Kadam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadam_\(clan\)) Bande divided the Peshwa territory between them,[^cite_note-96] with Damaji establishing the sway of [Gaekwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaekwad_dynasty) over Gujarat and making [Baroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroda) (present day [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara) in southern Gujarat) his capital. The ensuing internecine war among the Marathas was fully exploited by the British, who interfered in the affairs of both Gaekwads and the Peshwas.

In [Saurashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurashtra_\(region\)), as elsewhere, the Marathas were met with resistance.[^cite_note-cpc-97] The decline of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) helped form larger peripheral states in Saurashtra, including [Junagadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junagadh_State), [Jamnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawanagar_State), [Bhavnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavnagar_State) and a few others, which largely resisted the Maratha incursions.[^cite_note-cpc-97]

### European colonialism (1614–1947)



Territory ceded by the [Sultanate of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Sultanate) to Portugal in 1534 according to the [Treaty of Bassein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Bassein_\(1534\)).

In the 1600s, the Dutch, French, English and [Portuguese](23033.md) all established bases along the western coast of the region. Portugal was the first European power to arrive in Gujarat, and after the [Battle of Diu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Diu), acquired several enclaves along the Gujarati coast, including [Daman and Diu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daman_and_Diu) as well as [Dadra and Nagar Haveli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadra_and_Nagar_Haveli). These enclaves were administered by [Portuguese India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India) under a single union territory for over 450 years, only to be later incorporated into the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_India) on 19 December 1961 by military conquest.

The [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) established a [factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_\(trading_post\)) in [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat) in 1614 following the commercial treaty made with Mughal Emperor Nuruddin Salim [Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahangir), which formed their first base in India, but it was eclipsed by [Bombay](19189.md) after the English received it from Portugal in 1668 as part of the marriage treaty of [Charles II of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) and [Catherine of Braganza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Braganza), daughter of [King John IV of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_John_IV_of_Portugal). The state was an early point of contact with the west, and the first British commercial [outpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outpost_\(military\)) in India was in Gujarat.[^cite_note-wingsbirds.com-98]

17th-century French explorer [François Pyrard de Laval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Pyrard_de_Laval), who is remembered for his 10-year sojourn in South Asia, bears witness in his account that the Gujaratis were always prepared to learn workmanship from the Portuguese, and in turn imparted skills to the Portuguese:[^cite_note-99]

> &#x20;I have never seen men of wit so fine and polished as are these Indians: they have nothing barbarous or savage about them, as we are apt to suppose. They are unwilling indeed to adopt the manners and customs of the *Portuguese*; yet do they regularly learn their manufactures and workmanship, being all very curious and desirous of learning. In fact, the *Portuguese* take and learn more from them than they from the *Portuguese*.

Later in the 17th century, Gujarat came under control of the Hindu [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) that arose, defeating the Muslim Mughals who had dominated the politics of India. Most notably, from 1705 to 1716, [Senapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senapati) [Khanderao Dabhade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanderao_Dabhade) led the Maratha Empire forces in Baroda. [Pilaji Gaekwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilaji_Gaekwad), first ruler of [Gaekwad dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaekwad_dynasty), established the control over Baroda and other parts of Gujarat.

The [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) wrested control of much of Gujarat from the Marathas during the [Second Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Maratha_War) in 1802–1803. Many local rulers, notably the Maratha Gaekwad Maharajas of Baroda ([Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara)), made a separate peace with the British and acknowledged British sovereignty in return for retaining local self-rule.

An epidemic in 1812 killed half the population of Gujarat.[^cite_note-100]

Gujarat was placed under the political authority of the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency), with the exception of [Baroda State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroda_State), which had a direct relationship with the [Governor-General of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_India). From 1818 to 1947, most of present-day Gujarat, including [Kathiawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathiawar), Kutch and northern and eastern Gujarat were divided into hundreds of [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_states), but several districts in central and southern Gujarat, namely [Ahmedabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_district), Broach ([Bharuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharuch_district)), Kaira ([Kheda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kheda_district)), [Panchmahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchmahal_district) and [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat_district), were governed directly by British officials. In 1819, [Sahajanand Swami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaminarayan) established the first [Swaminarayan Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaminarayan_Temple,_Ahmedabad) in [Kalupur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalupur), [Ahmedabad](206337.md).

### Post-independence (1947–present)



Gujarat (including Saurashtra and Kutch) in Bombay state pre-bifurcation in 1960



[Gujarat High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_High_Court) in Ahmedabad



[Gandhinagar Capital railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhinagar_Capital_railway_station)

Initially there was confusion over whether Junagadh would join India or Pakistan. This was resolved in 1947 with a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite) for full union with India following the next year.[^cite_note-101]

After [Indian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_India) and the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947, the new Indian government grouped the former princely states of Gujarat into three larger units; [Saurashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurashtra_\(region\)), which included the former princely states on the [Kathiawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathiawad) peninsula, [Kutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutch), and [Bombay state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_state), which included the former British districts of Bombay Presidency together with most of [Baroda State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroda_State) and the other former princely states of eastern Gujarat. Bombay state was enlarged to include Kutch, Saurashtra (Kathiawar) and parts of [Hyderabad state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad_state) and [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md) in central India. The new state had a mostly Gujarati-speaking north and a Marathi-speaking south. Agitation by Gujarati nationalists, the [Mahagujarat Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahagujarat_Movement), and Marathi nationalists, the [Samyukta Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samyukta_Maharashtra_Samiti), for their own states led to the split of Bombay state on linguistic lines; on 1 May 1960, it became the new states of Gujarat and [Maharashtra](20629.md). In [1969 riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Gujarat_riots), at least 660 died and properties worth millions were destroyed.[^cite_note-Yasmeen2007-102][^cite_note-LaurentChris2012-103]

The first capital of Gujarat was [Ahmedabad](206337.md). The capital of Gujarat was moved to [Gandhinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhinagar) in 1970. *[Nav Nirman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nav_Nirman) Andolan* was a socio-political movement of 1974. It was a students' and [middle class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class) people's movement against economic crisis and corruption in public life. This was the first and last successful agitation after the Independence of India that ousted an elected government.[^cite_note-it-104][^cite_note-avk-105][^cite_note-bs-106]

Gujarat has emerged as an important industrial hub in India. In Western India [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat) was among the strongest industrial clusters in the 1970s. Between 1971 and 1981 [diamond cutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_cutting) was established as industry in Surat. At the same time the production of [artificial silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_silk) and a substantial [petrochemical industry](2293201.md) became a fixture in Surat.[^cite_note-107]

The [Morvi dam failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morvi_dam_failure), in 1979, resulted in the death of thousands of people and large economic loss.[^cite_note-expi-108] In the 1980s, a [reservation policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservation_in_India) was introduced in the country, which led to anti-reservation protests in 1981 and 1985. The protests witnessed violent clashes between people belonging to various [castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_caste_system).[^cite_note-109]

The [2001 Gujarat earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Gujarat_earthquake) was located about 9 km south-southwest of the village of [Chobari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chobari) in the [Bhachau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhachau) taluka of [Kutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutch) District. This magnitude 7.7 shock killed around 20,000 people (including at least 18 in South-eastern Pakistan), injured another 167,000 and destroyed nearly 400,000 homes.[^cite_note-110]

In February 2002, the [Godhra train burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godhra_train_burning) led to statewide [riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Gujarat_violence), resulting in the deaths of 1,044 people – 790 Muslims and 254 Hindus, and hundreds missing still unaccounted for.[^cite_note-Riots_toll-111] The [Swaminarayan Akshardham in Gandhinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaminarayan_Akshardham_\(Gandhinagar\)) was [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akshardham_Temple_attack) by two terrorists in September 2002, killing 32 people and injuring more than 80 others. [National Security Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Guard) intervened to end the siege killing both terrorists.[^cite_note-akshar-112] On 26 July 2008 a [series of 17 bomb blasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Ahmedabad_serial_blasts) rocked Ahmedabad, killing and injuring several people.[^cite_note-113]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Gujarat)

See also: [Climate of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Gujarat) and [List of rivers of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Gujarat)



Physical map of Gujarat



[Sardar Sarovar Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Sarovar_Dam), Gujarat

Gujarat borders the [Tharparkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tharparkar_District), [Badin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badin_District) and [Thatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatta_District) districts of [Pakistan](23235.md)'s [Sindh](28972.md) province to the northwest, is bounded by the Arabian Sea to the southwest, the state of [Rajasthan](26291.md) to the northeast, Madhya Pradesh to the east, and by Maharashtra, the [Union Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Territory) of [Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadra_and_Nagar_Haveli_and_Daman_and_Diu) to the south. Historically, the north was known as [Anarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarta), the Kathiawar peninsula, "Saurastra", and the south as "Lata".[^cite_note-114] Gujarat was also known as Pratichya and Varuna.[^cite_note-115] The Arabian Sea makes up the state's western coast. The capital, Gandhinagar is a [planned city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_city). Gujarat has an area of 75,686 sq mi (196,030 km<sup>2</sup>) with the longest coastline (24% of Indian sea coast) 1,600 km (990 mi), dotted with 41 ports: one major, 11 intermediate and 29 minor.

The [Narmada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) is the largest river in Gujarat followed by the [Tapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapti_River). The [Sabarmati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_River) has the longest course through the state. The Sardar Sarovar Project is built on Narmada, one of the major rivers of [peninsular India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_India) where it is one of only three major rivers that run from east to west – the others being the Tapi and the [Mahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahi_River). It is about 1,312 km (815 mi) long. Several riverfront embankments have been built on the Sabarmati River.

The eastern borders have fringes of low mountains of India, the [Aravalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravalli_Range), [Sahyadri (Western Ghats)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats), [Vindhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vindhya_Range) and [Saputara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saputara). Apart from this the Gir hills, Barda, Jessore and [Chotila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chotila) together make up a large minority of Gujarat. [Girnar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girnar) is the tallest peak and [Saputara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saputara) is the only hill-station (hilltop resort) in the state.

### Flora and fauna

Main articles: [Fauna of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_Gujarat) and [List of national parks and wildlife sanctuaries of Gujarat, India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_and_wildlife_sanctuaries_of_Gujarat,_India)

#### Prehistoric fauna



Indroda [Dinosaur](8311.md) and Fossil Park, [Gandhinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhinagar)

In the early 1980s, palaeontologists found [dinosaur egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_egg) hatcheries and [fossils](10958.md) of at least 13 species in [Balasinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balasinor). The most important find was that of a carnivorous [abelisaurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelisauridae) dinosaur named *[Rajasurus narmadensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasaurus)* which lived in the [Late Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cretaceous) period.[^cite_note-bbc_jurassic-116][^cite_note-Smithsonian-117] A notable discovery in the village of Dholi Dungri was that of *[Sanajeh indicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanajeh_indicus)*, a primitive [madtsoiid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madtsoiidae) snake that likely preyed on [sauropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropod) dinosaur hatchlings and embryos.[^cite_note-Smithsonian-117][^cite_note-118]

#### Extant species

As of 2023, Gujarat has 15,016.64 km<sup>2</sup> of forest cover, which is about 7.65% of its geographical area. The total recorded forest area in the state is 21,870.35 km<sup>2</sup> (11.14%).[^cite_note-TOI2025Forest-119] Among the districts, [the Dangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dangs) has the largest area under forest cover. Gujarat has four national parks and 21 sanctuaries. It is the only home of Asiatic lions and, outside [Africa](5334607.md), is the only present natural habitat of lions.[^cite_note-Singh2011-120] [Gir Forest National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gir_Forest_National_Park) in the southwest part of the state covers part of the lions' habitat. [Indian leopards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_leopard) are also found in the state. They are spread across the large plains of Saurashtra and the mountains of South Gujarat. Other national parks include [Vansda National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vansda_National_Park), [Blackbuck National Park, Velavadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackbuck_National_Park,_Velavadar), and [Narara Marine National Park, Gulf of Kutchh, Jamnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_National_Park,_Gulf_of_Kutch). Wildlife sanctuaries include [Wild Ass Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Ass_Wildlife_Sanctuary), [Nal Sarovar Bird Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nal_Sarovar_Bird_Sanctuary), [Porbandar Bird Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porbandar_Bird_Sanctuary), [Kutch Desert Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutch_Desert_Wildlife_Sanctuary), [Kutch Bustard Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutch_Bustard_Sanctuary), [Narayan Sarovar Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayan_Sarovar_Sanctuary), [Jessore Sloth Bear Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessore_Sloth_Bear_Sanctuary), Anjal, [Balaram-Ambaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaram_Ambaji_Wildlife_Sanctuary), [Barda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barda_Wildlife_Sanctuary), [Jambughoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jambughoda_Wildlife_Sanctuary), [Khavda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khavda), Paniya, [Purna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purna_Wildlife_Sanctuary), Rampura, [Ratan Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratan_Mahal_Wildlife_Sanctuary), and Surpaneshwar.

In February 2019, a [Bengal tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_tiger) claimed to be from [Ratapani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratapani_Tiger_Reserve) in Madhya Pradesh was spotted in the area of [Lunavada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunavada) in [Mahisagar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahisagar_district), in the eastern part of the state,[^cite_note-HT_02-2019-121][^cite_note-DTE_02-2019-122] before being found dead later that month, likely from starvation.[^cite_note-TOI_03-2019-123]

### Social groups

Main article: [Gujarati people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_people)

While Gujarati speakers constitute a majority of Gujarat's population, the metropolitan areas of Ahmedabad, [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara) and [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat) are cosmopolitan, with numerous other ethnic and language groups. [Marwaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwari_people) compose large minorities of economic migrants; smaller communities of people from the other states of India have also migrated to Gujarat for employment. [Luso-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luso-Indian), [Anglo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Indian), [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) and [Parsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsi) also live in the areas.[^cite_note-126] [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_people) presence is traditionally important here following the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947.[^cite_note-127] The [Koli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koli_people) forms the largest caste-cluster, comprising 24% of the total population of the state.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

[Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language) is the official language of the state of Gujarat. An [Indo-Aryan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages), it is spoken natively by 86% of the state's population, or 52 million people (as of 2011). Hindi is the second-most frequently spoken language in the state, being spoken by more than 6% of the population. Marathi also is spoken in urban areas.[^cite_note-language-130] 1.9% speak Sindhi, 1.5% Marathi, 1.4% Bhili, 0.79% Urdu, 0.30% Odia. In Kutch region people speak the [Kutchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutchi_language) and [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_language) languages and [Memoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memoni) is spoken in Kathiawar by Sindhi Memons. Almost 88% of the [Gujarati Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_Muslims) speak Gujarati as their mother tongue and the remaining 12% speak [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu).

In rural areas among the tribals, various Bhil dialects are spoken by approximately 1.37% of the population. In the northeast part of the state [Bhili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhili_language) is spoken, in the central part of the state Bhili, [Bhilali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhilali_language), and [Vasava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasavi_language) are spoken, while in the southeast part of the state [Dangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhanki_language), [Varli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varli_language), [Chodri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chodri_language), and [Dhodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhodia-Kukna_language) (which are related to Marathi) are spoken.

According to 2011 census, the religious makeup in Gujarat was 88.57% [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu), 9.67% [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), 0.96% [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain), 0.52% [Christian](18337522.md), 0.10% [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh), 0.05% [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) and 0.03% others. [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_Muslims) are the biggest minority in the state accounting for 9.7% of the population. Gujarat has the third-largest population of Jains in India, following [Maharashtra](20629.md) and [Rajasthan](26291.md), almost all of whom live in urban areas like Vadodara, Ahmedabad and Surat.[^cite_note-Religion-133]

## Governance and administration

Main articles: [Politics of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Gujarat), [Government of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Gujarat), [Gujarat Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Legislative_Assembly), [Districts of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Gujarat), [Cities of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Gujarat), and [Talukas of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talukas_of_Gujarat)

See also: [Chief Ministers of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Ministers_of_Gujarat) and [Gujarat Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Council_of_Ministers)



Legislative Assembly and seat of Gujarat government located in [Gandhinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhinagar), the capital of Gujarat State.

Gujarat is governed by a [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Legislative_Assembly) of 182 members. Members of the Legislative Assembly are elected on the basis of adult suffrage from one of 182 constituencies, of which 13 are reserved for [scheduled castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Tribes) and 27 for [scheduled tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_tribe). The term of office for a member of the Legislative Assembly is five years. The Legislative Assembly elects a [speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Legislative_Assembly_\(India\)) who presides over the meetings of the legislature. A [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Gujarat) is appointed by the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India), and is to address the state legislature after every general election and the commencement of each year's first session of the Legislative Assembly. The leader of the majority party or coalition in the legislature (Chief Minister) or his or her designee acts as the Leader of the Legislative Assembly. The Governor is the constitutional head of the state, while the [Gujarat Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Council_of_Ministers), headed by the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Gujarat), exercises executive authority and functions as the de facto government.[^cite_note-135]

After the [independence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_India) in 1947, the [Indian National Congress](149333.md) (INC) ruled the [Bombay State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_State) (which included present-day Gujarat and Maharashtra). Congress continued to govern Gujarat after the state's creation in 1960.

During and after India's [State of Emergency of 1975–1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_\(India\)), public support for the INC eroded, but it continued to hold government until 1995 with the brief rule of nine months by [Janata Morcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janata_Morcha). In the 1995 Assembly elections, the Congress lost to the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP) led by [Keshubhai Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshubhai_Patel) who became the Chief Minister. His government lasted only two years. The fall of that government was provoked by a split in the BJP led by [Shankersinh Vaghela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shankersinh_Vaghela). BJP again won election in 1998 with clear majority. In 2001, following the loss of two assembly seats in by-elections, Keshubhai Patel resigned and yielded power to [Narendra Modi](444222.md). BJP retained a majority in the 2002 election, and Narendra Modi remained as Chief Minister. On 1 June 2007, Narendra Modi became the longest serving Chief Minister of Gujarat.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] BJP retained the power in subsequent elections in 2007 and 2012 and Narendra Modi continued as the chief minister. After Narendra Modi became the prime minister of India in 2014, [Anandiben Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anandiben_Patel) became the first female chief minister of the state. [Vijay Rupani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Rupani) took over as chief minister and Nitin Patel as deputy chief minister on 7 August 2016 after Anandiben Patel resigned earlier on 3 August. [Bhupendrabhai Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhupendrabhai_Patel) became chief minister in September 2021 after the resignation of Vijay Rupani.

The incumbent [chief secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_secretary_\(India\)) of Gujarat is Raj Kumar[^cite_note-139] and [director general of police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_general_of_police) (DGP) is KLN Rao.[^cite_note-140]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Gujarat)



[Mundra Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundra_Port), Kutch

Gujarat is the most industrialised state in India and has the highest industrial output when compared to other states in the country.[^cite_note-148] It has the highest exports of any Indian state, accounting for 30.7% of all Indian exports in 2024–2025.[^cite_note-149] It leads in diverse industrial sectors such as chemicals, petrochemicals, dairy, drugs and pharmaceuticals, cement and ceramics, gems and jewellery, textiles and engineering.[^cite_note-150] It has the highest electricity production capacity[^cite_note-151] and maritime port cargo volume[^cite_note-152] among all states in India. Major agricultural produce of the state includes cotton, [groundnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut) (peanuts), [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_\(fruit\)), [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane), milk and milk products. Industrial products include cement and [petrol](23639.md).[^cite_note-153] Gujarat is ranked number one in the [pharmaceutical industry in India](3373039.md), with a 33% share in drug manufacturing and 28% share in drug exports. The state has 130 USFDA certified drug manufacturing facilities. Ahmedabad and Vadodara are considered as pharmaceutical hubs, as there are many big and small pharmaceutical companies established in these cities.[^cite_note-154]

Gujarat has the longest coastline in India (1600 km), and its ports (both private and public sector) handle around 40% of India's ocean cargo, with [Mundra Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundra_Port) located in [Gulf of Kutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Kutch) being the largest port of India by cargo handled (144 million tons) due to its favourable location on the westernmost part of India and closeness to global shipping lanes. Gujarat also contributes around 20% share in India's industrial production and merchandise exports. According to a 2009 report on [economic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_freedom) by the [Cato Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Institute), Gujarat is the most free state in India (the second one being [Tamil Nadu](29918.md)).[^cite_note-155] [Reliance Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Industries) operates the oil refinery at [Jamnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamnagar), which is the world's largest grass-roots refinery at a single location. The world's largest shipbreaking yard is in Gujarat near Bhavnagar at [Alang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alang). India's only Liquid Chemical Port Terminal at [Dahej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahej), developed by Gujarat Chemical Port Terminal Co Ltd. Gujarat has two of the three liquefied natural gas (LNG) terminals in the country (Dahej and Hazira). Two more LNG terminals are proposed, at [Pipavav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipavav) and [Mundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundra).

According to most recent data, Gujarat is considered the fourth richest state in India.[^cite_note-156]

Gujarat has 85% village connectivity with all‐weather roads.[^cite_note-157] Nearly 100% of Gujarat's 18,000 villages have been connected to the electrical grid for 24-hour power to households and eight hours of power to farms, through the [Jyotigram Yojana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyotigram_Yojana).[^cite_note-158] As of 2015, Gujarat ranks [first nationwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_India_by_installed_power_capacity) in gas-based thermal electricity generation with a national market share of over 8%, and second nationwide in nuclear electricity generation with national market share of over 1%.[^cite_note-159]

The state registered 12.8% agricultural growth in the last five years against the national average of 2%.[^cite_note-160]

Gujarat records highest decadal agricultural growth rate of 10.97%. Over 20% of the *S\&P CNX 500* conglomerates have corporate offices in Gujarat.[^cite_note-161] As per RBI report, in year 2006–07, 26% of total bank finance in India was in Gujarat.

According to a 2012 survey report of the Chandigarh Labour Bureau, Gujarat had the lowest unemployment rate of 1% against the national average of 3.8%.[^cite_note-162]

[Legatum Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legatum_Institute)'s Global Prosperity Index 2012 recognised Gujarat as one of the two highest-scoring among all states of India on matters of social capital.[^cite_note-163] The state ranks 15th alongside Germany in a list of 142 nations worldwide: higher than several developed nations.[^cite_note-164]

### Industrial growth

Gujarat's major cities include Ahmedabad, [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat), [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara), [Rajkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajkot), [Jamnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamnagar) and [Bhavnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavnagar). In 2010, *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*' list of the world's fastest growing cities included Ahmedabad at number 3 after [Chengdu](170375.md) and [Chongqing](171752.md) from China.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-07_October_2010-167] The state is rich in [calcite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcite), [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese), [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite), [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [agate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agate), [feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar), and [quartz sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz_sand), and successful mining of these minerals is done in their specified areas. Jamnagar is the hub for manufacturing [brass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass) parts. Gujarat produces about 98% of India's required amount of soda ash, and gives the country about 78% of the national requirement of salt. It is one of India's most prosperous states, having a per-capita GDP significantly above India's average.[Mehsana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehsana), [Kalol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalol_\(Panchmahal\)), Khambhat, and [Ankleshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankleshwar) are today known for their oil and natural gas production. Dhuvaran has a thermal power station, which uses coal, oil, and gas. Also, on the Gulf of Khambhat, 50 km (31 mi) southeast of Bhavnagar, is the Alang Ship Recycling Yard (the world's largest). [MG Motor India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MG_Motor_India) manufactures its cars at Halol near Vadodara, The world's least expensive car, the [Tata Nano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Nano), was manufactured in [Sanand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanand) near Ahmedabad. Surat, a city by the [Gulf of Khambhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Khambhat), is a hub of the global diamond trade. In 2003, 92% of the world's diamonds were cut and polished in Surat.[^cite_note-time.com-168] The diamond industry employs 500,000 people in Gujarat.[^cite_note-169]

At an investor's summit entitled "Vibrant Gujarat Global Investor Summit", arranged between 11 and 13 January 2015, at Mahatma Mandir, Gandhinagar, the state government signed 21000 Memoranda of Understanding for Special Economic Zones worth a total of ₹ 2.5 million crores ([short scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_and_short_scales)).[^cite_note-170] However, most of the investment was from domestic industry.[^cite_note-171] In the fourth Vibrant Gujarat Global Investors' Summit held at Science City, Ahmedabad, in January 2009, there were 600 foreign delegates. In all, 8668 MOUs worth ₹ 12500 billion were signed, estimated to create 2.5 million new job opportunities in the state.[^cite_note-in.rediff.com-172] In 2011, Vibrant Gujarat Global Investors' Summit MOUs worth ₹ 21 trillion (US\$ 463 billion) were signed.

Gujarat is a state with surplus electricity.[^cite_note-173] The [Kakrapar Atomic Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakrapar_Atomic_Power_Station) is a nuclear power station run by NPCIL that lies in the proximity of the city of Surat. According to the official sources, against demand of 40,793 million units during the nine months since April 2010, Gujarat produced 43,848 million units. Gujarat sold surplus power to 12 states: Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Delhi, Haryana, Karnataka, Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, Madhya Pradesh, and West Bengal.[^cite_note-174]

### Agriculture



Traditional farming

The total geographical area of Gujarat is 19,602,400 hectares, of which crops take up 10,630,700 hectares.[^cite_note-180] The three main sources of growth in Gujarat's agriculture are from cotton production, the rapid growth of high-value foods such as livestock, fruits and vegetables, and from wheat production, which saw an annual average growth rate of 28% between 2000 and 2008 (According to the [International Food Policy Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Food_Policy_Research_Institute)).[^cite_note-181] Other major produce includes bajra, groundnut, cotton, rice, maize, wheat, mustard, sesame, pigeon pea, green gram, sugarcane, mango, banana, sapota, lime, guava, tomato, potato, onion, cumin, garlic, isabgol and fennel. Whilst, in recent times, Gujarat has seen a high average annual growth of 9% in the agricultural sector, the rest of India has an annual growth rate of around 3%. This success was lauded by former President of India, APJ Abdul Kalam.[^cite_note-182]

The strengths of Gujarat's agricultural success have been attributed to diversified crops and cropping pattern; climatic diversity (eight climatic zones for agriculture); the existence of four agricultural universities in the state, which promote research in agricultural efficiency and sustainability;[^cite_note-183] co-operatives; adoption of hi-tech agriculture such as tissue culture, green houses and shed-net houses; agriculture export zones; strong marketing infrastructure, which includes cold storage, processing units, logistic hubs and consultancy facilities.[^cite_note-184]

Gujarat is the main producer of tobacco, cotton, and groundnuts in India. Other major food crops produced are rice, wheat, *jowar, bajra*, maize, *tur*, and *gram*. The state has an agricultural economy; the total crop area amounts to more than one-half of the total land area.[^cite_note-185]

Animal husbandry and dairying have played vital roles in the rural economy of Gujarat. Dairy farming, primarily concerned with milk production, functions on a co-operative basis and has more than a million members. Gujarat is the largest producer of milk in India. The [Amul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amul) milk co-operative federation is well known all over India, and it is Asia's biggest dairy.[^cite_note-186] Among the livestock raised are, buffaloes and other cattle, sheep, and goats. As per the results of livestock census 1997, there were 20.97 million head of livestock in Gujarat State. In the estimates of the survey of major livestock products, during the year 2002–03, Gujarat produced 6.09 million tonnes of milk, 385 million eggs and 2.71 million kg of wool. Gujarat also contributes inputs to the textiles, oil, and soap industries.

The adoption of cooperatives in Gujarat is widely attributed to much of the success in the agricultural sector, particularly sugar and dairy [cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative). Cooperative farming has been a component of India's strategy for agricultural development since 1951. While the success of these was mixed throughout the country, their positive impact on the states of Maharashtra and Gujarat have been the most significant. In 1995 alone, the two states had more registered cooperatives than any other region in the country. Out of these, the agricultural cooperatives have received much attention. Many have focused on subsidies and credit to farmers and rather than collective gathering, they have focused on facilitating collective processing and marketing of produce. However, while they have led to increased productivity, their effect on equity in the region has been questioned, because membership in agricultural cooperatives has tended to favour landowners while limiting the entry of landless agricultural labourers.[^cite_note-187] An example of co-operative success in Gujarat can be illustrated through dairy co-operatives, with the particular example of [Amul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amul) (Anand Milk Union Limited).



Amul plant at [Anand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand,_Gujarat)

Amul was formed as a dairy cooperative in 1946,[^cite_note-188] in the city of Anand, Gujarat. The cooperative, Gujarat Cooperative Milk Marketing Federation Ltd. (GCMMF), is jointly owned by around 2.6 million milk producers in Gujarat. Amul has been seen as one of the best examples of cooperative achievement and success in a developing economy and the Amul pattern of growth has been taken as a model for rural development, particularly in the agricultural sector of developing economies. The company stirred the White Revolution of India (also known as [Operation Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Flood)), the world's biggest dairy development program, and made the milk-deficient nation of India the largest milk producer in the world, in 2010.[^cite_note-189] The "Amul Model" aims to stop the exploitation by middlemen and encourage freedom of movement since the farmers are in control of procurement, processing and packaging of the milk and milk products.[^cite_note-190] The company is worth 2.5 billion US dollars (as of 2012).[^cite_note-191]

70% of Gujarat's area is classified as semi-arid to arid climatically, thus the demand on water from various economic activities puts a strain on the supply.[^cite_note-192] Of the total gross irrigated area, 16–17% is irrigated by government-owned canals and 83–84% by privately owned tube wells and other wells extracting groundwater, which is the predominant source of irrigation and water supply to the agricultural areas. As a result, Gujarat has faced problems with [groundwater depletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overdrafting), especially after demand for water increased in the 1960s. As access to electricity in rural areas increased, submersible electric pumps became more popular in the 1980s and 1990s. However, the Gujarat Electricity Board switched to flat tariff rates linked to the horsepower of pumps, which increased tubewell irrigation again and decreased the use of electric pumps. By the 1990s, groundwater abstraction rates exceeded groundwater recharge rate in many districts, while only 37.5% of all districts has "safe" recharge rates. Groundwater maintenance and preventing unnecessary loss of the available water supplies is now an issue faced by the state.[^cite_note-193] The Sardar Sarovar Project, a debated dam project in the Narmada valley consisting of a network of canals, has significantly increased irrigation in the region. However, its impact on communities who were displaced is still a contested issue. In 2012 Gujarat began an experiment to reduce water loss due to evaporation in canals and to increase sustainability in the area, by constructing solar panels over the canals. In a one megawatt (MW) solar power project set up at Chandrasan, Gujarat uses solar panels fixed over a 750-metre stretch of an irrigation canal. Unlike many solar power projects, this one does not take up large amounts of land since the panels are constructed over the canals, and not on additional land. This results in lower upfront costs since land does not need to be acquired, cleared or modified to set up the panels. The Chandrasan project is projected to save 9 million litres of water per year.[^cite_note-194]

The Government of Gujarat, to improve [soil management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_management) and introduce farmers to new technology, started on a project which involved giving every farmer a Soil Health Card. This acts like a ration card, providing permanent identification for the status of cultivated land, as well as farmers' names, account numbers, survey numbers, [soil fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_fertility) status and general fertiliser dose. Samples of land from each village are taken and analysed by the [Gujarat Narmada Valley Fertiliser Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Narmada_Valley_Fertilisers), State Fertiliser Corporation and [Indian Farmers Fertilisers Co-operative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Farmers_Fertiliser_Cooperative_Limited). 1,200,000 [soil test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_test) data from the villages was collected as of 2008, from farmer's field villages have gone into a database. Assistance and advice for this project was given by local agricultural universities and crop and soil-specific data was added to the database. This allows the soil test data to be interpreted and recommendations or adjustments made in terms of fertiliser requirements, which are also added to the database.[^cite_note-195]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Gujarati cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_cuisine)



Gujarati thali

Gujarati food is primarily vegetarian. The typical Gujarati *[thali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thali)* consists of *[rotli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti)* or *[bhakhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhakri) or thepala or rotlo*, *[dal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dal)* or *[kadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadhi)*, *[khichdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khichdi)*, Bhat and *[shak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry)*. [Athanu (Indian pickle)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_pickle#India) and *[chhundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhundo)* are used as [condiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condiment). The four major regions of Gujarat all bring their own styles to Gujarati food. Many Gujarati dishes are distinctively sweet, salty, and spicy at the same time. In the [Saurashtra region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurashtra_\(region\)) & Kutch region *[chhash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chass)* (buttermilk) is believed to be a must-have in their daily food.

### Music

Main article: [Music of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Gujarat)

Gujarati folk music, known as *Sugam Sangeet*, is a hereditary profession of the [Barot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barot_\(caste\)) community. [Gadhvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadhvi) and [Charan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charan) communities have contributed heavily in modern times. The omnipresent instruments in Gujarati folk music include wind instruments, such as *turi*, *bungal*, and *pava*, [string instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_instrument), such as the *ravan hattho*, *ektaro*, and *jantar* and percussion instruments, such as the *manjira* and *zanz* pot drum.[^cite_note-209]

Surat is especially well known for the strong string which is made by applying glass powder on the row thread to provide it a cutting edge.[^cite_note-211]

### Diffusion of culture

Due to close proximity to the Arabian Sea, Gujarat has developed a mercantile ethos which maintained a cultural tradition of [seafaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafaring), long-distance trade, and overseas contacts with the outside world since ancient times, and the diffusion of culture through Gujarati [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora) was a logical outcome of such a tradition. During the pre-modern period, various European sources have observed that these merchants formed diaspora communities outside of Gujarat, and in many parts of the world, such as the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), Middle East, [Horn of Africa](62142.md), Hong Kong, [Indonesia](14579.md), and [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-212] long before the internal rise of the [Maratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha) dynasty, and the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) colonial occupation.[^cite_note-213]

Early 1st-century Western historians such as [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) and [Dio Cassius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dio_Cassius) are testament to Gujarati people's role in the spread of Buddhism in the Mediterranean, when it was recorded that the *[sramana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shramana)* monk [Zarmanochegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarmanochegas) (Ζαρμανοχηγὰς) of [Barygaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barygaza) met [Nicholas of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_of_Damascus) in [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch) while Augustus ruled the Roman Empire, and shortly thereafter proceeded to [Athens](1216.md) where died by [setting himself on fire](916669.md) to demonstrate his faith.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215] A tomb to the *sramana*, was still visible in the time of [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch),[^cite_note-216] which bore the mention "ΖΑΡΜΑΝΟΧΗΓΑΣ ΙΝΔΟΣ ΑΠΟ ΒΑΡΓΟΣΗΣ" ("The *sramana* master from Barygaza in India").[^cite_note-217]

The progenitor of the [Sinhala language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language) is believed to have been [Prince Vijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Vijaya), son of King Simhabahu, who ruled Simhapura (modern-day Sihor near Bhavnagar).[^cite_note-218] Prince Vijaya was banished by his father for his lawlessness and set forth with a band of adventurers. This tradition was followed by other Gujaratis. For example, in the [Ajanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajanta_Caves) frescoes, a Gujarati prince is shown entering [Sri Lanka](26750.md).[^cite_note-ReferenceB-219]

Many Indians migrated to [Indonesia](14579.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md), most of them Gujaratis. King Aji Saka, who is said to have come to Java in Indonesia in year 1 of the Saka calendar, is believed by some to have been a king of Gujarat.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-220] The first Indian settlements in the Philippines and Java Island of Indonesia are believed to have been established with the coming of Prince Dhruvavijaya of Gujarat, with 5000 traders.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-220] Some stories propose a Brahmin named Tritresta was the first to bring Gujarati migrants with him to Java, so some scholars equate him with Aji Saka.[^cite_note-221] A Gujarati ship has been depicted in a sculpture at Borabudur, [Java](69336.md).[^cite_note-ReferenceB-219]

### Museums and memorials

Gujarat has a variety of museums on different genres that are run by the state's Department of Museums located at the principal state museum, [Baroda Museum & Picture Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroda_Museum_&_Picture_Gallery) in [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara),[^cite_note-224] which is also the location of the [Maharaja Fateh Singh Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_Fateh_Singh_Museum). The [Kirti Mandir, Porbandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirti_Mandir,_Porbandar), [Sabarmati Ashram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Ashram), and [Kaba Gandhi No Delo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaba_Gandhi_No_Delo) are museums related to Mahatma Gandhi, the former being the place of his birth and the latter two where he lived in his lifetime. Kaba Gandhi No Delo in [Rajkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajkot) exhibits part of a rare collection of photographs relating to the life of Mahatma Gandhi. Sabarmati Ashram is the place where Gandhi initiated the [Dandi March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dandi_March). On 12 March 1930 he vowed that he would not return to the Ashram until India won independence.[^cite_note-225]

The Maharaja Fateh Singh Museum is housed within [Lakshmi Vilas Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmi_Vilas_Palace), the residence of the erstwhile [Maharajas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja), located in [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara).[^cite_note-226]

The [Calico Museum of Textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calico_Museum_of_Textiles) is managed by the Sarabhai Foundation and is one of the most popular tourist spots in Ahmedabad.[^cite_note-227]

The Lakhota Museum at Jamnagar is a palace transformed into museum, which was residence of the Jadeja Rajputs. The collection of the museum includes artefacts spanning from 9th to 18th centuries, pottery from medieval villages nearby and the skeleton of a whale.

Other well-known museums in the state include the [Kutch Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutch_Museum) in [Bhuj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhuj), which is the oldest museum in Gujarat founded in 1877, the [Watson Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_Museum) of human history and culture in [Rajkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajkot),[^cite_note-228] [Gujarat Science City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_Science_City) and [Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel National Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel_National_Memorial) in Ahmedabad. In October 2018, the world's tallest statue commemorating the independence leader Sardar Patel was unveiled. At 182 metres tall the [Statue of Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Unity) is the newest tourist attraction with over 30,000 visitors every day.[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230]

### Religious sites

Religious sites play a major part in the tourism of Gujarat. [Somnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath) is the first of the twelve [Jyotirlingas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyotirlinga), and is mentioned in the [Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda). The [Dwarkadhish Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarkadhish_Temple), [Radha Damodar Temple, Junagadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radha_Damodar_Temple,_Junagadh) and [Dakor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakor) are holy pilgrimage sites with temples dedicated to [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna). The [Sun Temple, Modhera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Temple,_Modhera) is a ticketed monument, handled by the [Archaeological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Survey_of_India).[^cite_note-231] Other religious sites in state include [Ambaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambaji), Dakor, [Shamlaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamlaji), [Chotila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chotila), [Becharaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Becharaji), [Mahudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahudi), [Shankheshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shankheshwar) etc. The [Shatrunjaya Mahatirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palitana_temples) of [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) on Mount [Shatrunjaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatrunjaya), [Palitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palitana) are considered the holiest of all [pilgrimage places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirtha_\(Jainism\)) by the [Svetambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svetambara) and [Digambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digambara) [Jain community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_community).[^cite_note-232] Palitana is the world's only mountain with more than 900 temples.[^cite_note-233] [Girnar Mahatirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girnar) is another major pilgrimage destination for Jains.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-Ginas-235][^cite_note-gs-236]

### Fairs



Tarnetar Fair, [Tarnetar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnetar)&#x20;



A man in traditional costumes during [Tarnetar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnetar) fair

A five-day festival is held during [Maha Shivaratri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Shivaratri) at the fort of [Girnar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girnar), Junagadh, known as the Bhavanth Mahadev Fair (Gujarati: ભવનાથ નો મેળો). The Kutch Festival or Rann Festival (Gujarati: કચ્છ or રણ ઉત્સવ) is a festival celebrated at Kutch during [Mahashivratri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahashivratri). The *Modhra Dance Festival* is a festival for classical dance, arranged by the [Government of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Gujarat)'s Cultural Department, to promote tourism in state and to keep traditions and culture alive.[^cite_note-237]

The Ambaji Fair is held in the Hindu month of Bhadrapad (around August–September) at Ambaji, during a time which is particularly suitable for farmers, when the busy monsoon season is about to end. The Bhadrapad fair is held at Ambaji which is in the Danta Taluka of Banaskantha district, near the Gujarat-Rajasthan border. The walk from the bus station to the temple is less than one kilometre, under a roofed walkway. Direct buses are available from many places, including Mount Abu (45 km away), Palanpur (65 km away), Ahmedabad and Idar. The Bhadrapad fair is held in the centre of the Ambaji village just outside the temple premises. The village is visited by the largest number of sanghas (pilgrim groups) during the fair. Many of them go there on foot, which is particularly enriching as it happens immediately after the monsoon, when the landscape is rich with greenery, streams are full of sparkling water and the air is fresh. About 1.5 million devotees are known to attend this fair each year from all over the world. Not only Hindus, but some devout Jains and Parsis also attend the functions, while some Muslims attend the fair for trade.

The [Tarnetar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnetar) Fair is held during the first week of *Bhadrapad*, (September–October according to [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar)), and mostly serves as a place to find a suitable bride for tribal people from Gujarat. The region is believed to be the place where [Arjuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjuna) took up the difficult task of piercing the eye of a fish, rotating at the end of a pole, by looking at its reflection in the pond water, to marry [Draupadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draupadi).[^cite_note-238] Other fairs in Gujarat include Dang Durbar, Shamlaji Fair, Chitra Vichitra Fair, Dhrang Fair and Vautha Fair.

The Government of Gujarat has [banned alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_India) since 1960.[^cite_note-articles.economictimes.indiatimes.com-239] In 2024, the Gujarat police seized around 82 lakh bottles of Indian-made foreign liquor, amounting to ₹144 crore.[^cite_note-240] Gujarat government collected the *Best State Award* for 'Citizen Security' by IBN7 Diamond States on 24 December 2012.[^cite_note-241]

Main pages: [Railway stations in Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Railway_stations_in_Gujarat) and [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways)

Indian Railways is planning a dedicated rail freight route Delhi–Mumbai passing through the state of Gujarat.

[India's first high speed rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_India) is set to run from [Ahmedabad](206337.md), the largest city in Gujarat to [Mumbai](19189.md), the [financial hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_centre) of India, with a top speed of 320 km/h (200 mph).

67.56 km (41.98 mi) long four corridors of [Ahmedabad Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad_Metro), a [metro rail system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) for Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar is currently operational. The construction started on 14 March 2015.[^cite_note-DeshGuj32015-245][^cite_note-gujarat-246] Two corridors of [Surat Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat_Metro) with a combined length of 40.35 km (25.07 mi) are under construction since 18 January 2021.[^cite_note-\:0-247] The project is expected to be completed by December 2026.[^cite_note-Ops_from_Dec_2026-248]

### Research

School of Business Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.K._School_of_Business_Management) is ranked sixth in financial management. [K. S. School of Business Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._S._School_of_Business_Management) is also an MBA college in [Gujarat University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat_University) providing a five-year integrated MBA course. Shanti Business School in Ahmedabad is a business school offering a post-graduate diploma in management through a corporate citizenship initiative.

The Institute of Seismological Research (ISR) was established by the Science and Technology Department, Government of Gujarat, in 2003 and is registered as a society. ISR campus is at Raisan, Gandhinagar, on the banks of Sabarmati river. Aims and objectives include assigning optimum seismic factors for buildings in different regions and long-term assessment of potential. The ISR is the only institute in India fully dedicated to seismological research and is planned to be developed into a premier international institute in few years time.

## Organised crime

This section is an excerpt from [Organised crime in India § Organised crime in Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organised_crime_in_India#Organised_crime_in_Gujarat).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Organised_crime_in_India\&action=edit)]

The Gujarati Underworld holds ties with [United Arab Emirates](19042592.md), [South Africa](17416221.md), and [East Africa](260987.md) Mafia. Activities include [Bootlegging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rum-running), [Maritime Smuggling](64062444.md), and Illegal Mining[^cite_note-Organised_crime_in_India_TOI_SandMafia_2025-276][^cite_note-277]. Despite Gujarat’s strict prohibition laws, organized crime networks drive a pervasive bootlegging industry through inter-state smuggling.[^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279] The devastating human toll of this illegal trade was highlighted by the [2022 Gujarat alcohol poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Gujarat_alcohol_poisoning), in which at least 42 people died and more than 97 were hospitalized after consuming spurious liquor made with stolen toxic methyl alcohol in the Botad district. Operatives linked to the Gujarat underworld have engaged in maritime smuggling and bootlegging networks, facilitating the illicit transit of contraband—such as narcotics and illegal liquor—between the coastal state of Gujarat and East African hubs including [Tanzania](30118.md).[^cite_note-280] [Abdul Latif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Latif_\(gangster\)) was an Indian gangster and bootlegger based in Gujarat state of India, and was one of the most notorious criminals from the state. He was also an associate of [Dawood Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawood_Ibrahim), and wanted in the 1993 Mumbai blasts case. D-Company used South Africa as an operational base to conspire with local associates, attempt recruitment, and orchestrate targeted assassinations linked to the 2002 Gujarat riots in India.[^cite_note-281]
