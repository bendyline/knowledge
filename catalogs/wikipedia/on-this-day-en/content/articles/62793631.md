**Zealand Pharma A/S** is a [Danish](76972.md) [biotechnology](4502.md) research company, which designs and develops [peptide-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptide) medicines, mainly focusing on [metabolic diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_diseases) like [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes) and [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity).[^cite_note-\:02-1] The company's head office is situated in [Søborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8borg) near [Copenhagen](5166.md), and it has close to 200 employees.[^cite_note-2] In 2018, they opened a subsidiary in [the US](3434750.md).[^cite_note-\:03-3] Zealand Pharma forms part of the Danish-[Swedish](5058739.md) life science cluster [Medicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicon_Valley).[^cite_note-4]

## History

Zealand Pharma A/S was founded by Bjarne Due Larsen, Lars Hellerung Christiansen, Leif Helth Jensen, Dan Buxbom, and Florian Schönharting in 1997 as Peptide Probe Technologies ApS, a biopharmaceutical company focused on the design and development of peptide-based medicine.[^cite_note-ZPhistory-5][^cite_note-Medwatch-6][^cite_note-CVR-7]

In 1998, the company changed its name to Zealand Pharma ApS following the addition of several new members, including Eva Steiness and former Lundbeck personnel. Eva Steiness was appointed CEO when the company was restructured into a stock corporation, Zealand Pharma A/S, in the spring of 1999.[^cite_note-ZPhistory-5][^cite_note-CVR-7]

In 2005, Zealand Pharma tried to be listed on the [stock exchange](28168.md), but had to resign for want of interest from investors. In 2010, they finally succeeded in being listed, and the stocks are now traded at the [Copenhagen Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Stock_Exchange) (NASDAQ OMX København).[^cite_note-8]

On January 15, 2015, Britt Meelby Jensen acquired the position as [CEO](52234.md) from David Solomon, who had led the company during the years 2008–2014.[^cite_note-9] In February 2019, Jensen left her position, and in April 2019, Emmanuel Dulac was chosen and inaugurated as [CEO](52234.md). Dulac has worked both European and American companies within the [pharmaceutical industry](560876.md).[^cite_note-10]

In September 2019, the Dutch family foundation Van Herk Investments injected 560 million [Danish Kroner](236323.md) (approx. 64 million [pound sterling](270673.md)) into Zealand Pharma, which means the foundation owns a fifth of the company's shares.[^cite_note-11] In October 2019, Zealand Pharma bought up the [Canadian](5042916.md) biotechnology company Encycle Therapeutics.[^cite_note-12]

On March 30, 2022, Dr. Adam Steensberg assumed the position of Chief Executive Officer.[^cite_note-13]

## Product development

Zealand Pharma does not promote and sell products in the market themselves, however, they form partnerships with several international [pharmaceutical companies](560876.md), who are responsible for placing the products on the market.[^cite_note-14]

Zealand Pharma is mostly known and recognized for the [diabetes medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-diabetic_medication) [Lyxumia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyxumia), which has been developed in collaboration with the [French](5843419.md) [pharmaceutical company](560876.md) [Sanofi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanofi). Since 2014, Zealand Pharma has collaborated with the coronary care unit at [Rigshospitalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigshospitalet) in [Copenhagen](5166.md) developing the medication Danegaptid, which should serve the purpose of preventing injuries in the [tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_\(biology\)) of the heart, frequently seen among [angioplasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angioplasty) patients.[^cite_note-15] Furthermore, they are developing several [orphan drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orphan_drug) with government assistance.[^cite_note-16]

Because of the acquisition of the Canadian Encycle Therapeutics, Zealand Pharma now also possesses the right to develop and sell the [pre-clinical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preclinical_development) candidate medication ET3764, which is also being developed for the treatment of diseases of the [alimentary tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_tract).[^cite_note-17]
