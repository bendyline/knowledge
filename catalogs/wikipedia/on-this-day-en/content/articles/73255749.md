The **2026 World Baseball Classic** was an international professional [baseball](3850.md) tournament between 20 national baseball teams, and the sixth iteration of the [World Baseball Classic](1877416.md) (WBC). It ran from March 5 to 17, 2026.[^cite_note-2] The pool-play rounds were played in [LoanDepot Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LoanDepot_Park) in [Miami, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami,_Florida); [Daikin Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daikin_Park) in [Houston, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston,_Texas); [Hiram Bithorn Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Bithorn_Stadium) in [San Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico), Puerto Rico; and the [Tokyo Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Dome) in [Tokyo](30057.md), Japan. Two quarterfinals were played in Houston, while the remaining knockout stage games were played in Miami.[^cite_note-MLB-3]

[Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_national_baseball_team) was the defending champion, defeating the [United States](4119270.md) in the [2023 WBC championship game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic_championship),[^cite_note-4] but was eliminated by the eventual champion [Venezuela](4264306.md) in the quarterfinals. Japan's failure to reach the semifinals was its worst-ever finish in tournament history.[^cite_note-5] The [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_baseball_team) and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_baseball_team), the tournament's last undefeated teams, were eliminated by the United States and Venezuela, respectively, in the semifinals, meaning that for the first time since [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Baseball_Classic), no team finished the WBC with an undefeated record. The Dominican Republic and Venezuela, the top two teams from the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBSC_Americas) (alongside Olympic hosts United States), qualified for the [2028 Olympic baseball tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_at_the_2028_Summer_Olympics), to be held in [Los Angeles](18110.md).[^cite_note-OLYQual-6][^cite_note-7]

[Venezuela](4264306.md) won the World Baseball Classic for the first time, taking a 3–2 lead in the [championship game](82034770.md) with a [run batted in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_batted_in) double by [Eugenio Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Su%C3%A1rez) in the top of the ninth inning. [Maikel García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maikel_Garc%C3%ADa) was named the [most valuable player](235000.md) of the tournament.

16 teams qualified for the 2026 WBC tournament by virtue of having placed in the top four of their respective pools in the [2023 World Baseball Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic) tournament.

Four additional teams qualified through the 2026 WBC qualifying tournament.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-MLB-3] The qualifying tournament included 8 teams, downsized from the 12 teams that had competed in the [2023 WBC qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic_qualification). The first pool took place in [Taipei](57648.md), and included [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_national_baseball_team), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_baseball_team), [Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_national_baseball_team), and [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_baseball_team); the second pool took place in [Tucson, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson,_Arizona), and included [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia_national_baseball_team), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_baseball_team), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_national_baseball_team), and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_national_baseball_team).[^cite_note-9] The Taipei pool ran from February 21–25, 2025, and the Tucson pool ran from March 2–6, 2025.[^cite_note-10]

Colombia, Nicaragua, Brazil, and Chinese Taipei all qualified for the 2026 WBC. Brazil qualified for the first time since [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Baseball_Classic). China failed to qualify for the first time in the World Baseball Classic, as it was eliminated after it lost all three of its games in the qualifiers.

### Pools

The pools and schedule were announced on August 21, 2024. Each pool contained an unspecified winner of a qualifier.[^cite_note-12] The pool assignments of the teams that advanced from the qualifiers (pot 5) were announced on April 9, 2025.[^cite_note-ps-13] The pool assignments prioritized the hosts ([Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_national_baseball_team), [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico_national_baseball_team), and the [United States](4119270.md)). The remaining pool assignments were made based on [WBSC World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBSC_World_Rankings), competitive balance, and commercial and geographic interest.

## Pool stage

In each of the four pools, the top two teams qualified for the knockout stage. The teams finishing third and fourth achieved automatic qualification to the next WBC, while the team finishing last would be required to enter the qualifying phase for the next WBC.

The games were played at the dates and locations shown below.[^cite_note-ps-13]

## Controversies and storylines

### Netflix deal in Japan

In Japan, [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) acquired the exclusive rights to the tournament for an estimated 15 billion [yen](34392.md).[^cite_note-\:0-56] Previously, the World Baseball Classic had been split between [free-to-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-to-air) (FTA) television and the [pay television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay_television) channel [J Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_Sports), with Japan's games usually being the only ones broadcast by FTA channels.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-\:0-56] Netflix subcontracted production of the broadcasts to [Nippon Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Television).[^cite_note-\:1-60]

The Netflix agreement faced a mixed reaction among fans and critics, as it marked the first time that Japan's WBC games would not be available on FTA television.[^cite_note-61] Bars raised concerns that they would not be able to stream the tournament at their establishments due to Netflix's [terms of service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terms_of_service) prohibiting commercial use, with some venues electing to do so discreetly in order to evade detection and legal complaints. As another workaround, some [karaoke parlors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaoke_box) enabled the ability for patrons to sign into the Netflix app in their private booths, so that they could watch the tournament with their friends.[^cite_note-\:2-62] Netflix announced that it would offer a one-month subscription for 500 yen (approximately US\$3.34) during March as a promotional offer for the tournament, and also organized public viewing parties in collaboration with venues such as [Aeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeon_\(company\)) and pub chain The HUB.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-\:2-62]

Some critics argued that negative reception to the deal was a form of [Galápagos syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_syndrome), noting that streaming was more prevalent among younger audiences than traditional television, the fact that non-Japan games would be available on a cheaper, over-the-top platform, and that Netflix was a well-known outlet internationally.[^cite_note-66] Others believed that the lack of WBC coverage on television would reduce [fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_fatigue) from the [excessive media coverage](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A7%E8%B0%B7%E3%83%8F%E3%83%A9%E3%82%B9%E3%83%A1%E3%83%B3%E3%83%88) of [Shohei Ohtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shohei_Ohtani) by Japanese terrestrial networks.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

According to the Japanese newspaper [Nikkei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nikkei), the number of Netflix [mobile app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_app) downloads grew about five times higher than in March of the prior year.[^cite_note-71]

Free coverage was also available via radio, with [Nippon Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Broadcasting_System) holding rights to all Japan's games, and [Nippon Cultural Broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Cultural_Broadcasting) broadcasting one quarterfinal, one semifinal, and the final, regardless of whether Japan played or not, streaming service [Radiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiko) showing both radio stations audios.[^cite_note-72] As part of its subcontract to produce the telecasts, Nippon Television also received the rights to produce a series of [specials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_special) following the tournament.[^cite_note-\:1-60]

### Baseball's "Dream Team" and later disappointment

The success of the [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_World_Baseball_Classic) and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Baseball_Classic) tournaments resulted in more MLB players interested in playing for their national teams. For the 2026 tournament, the United States had significantly better players, especially pitchers, than previous editions. Some have compared the 2026 United States team to [the Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_\(comics\)) or the [1992 United States men's Olympic basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_men's_Olympic_basketball_team), known as the "Dream Team".[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

But from February, support for the American team started to sour; first, it was announced that American pitcher [Tarik Skubal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarik_Skubal) had decided to pitch only a single game before returning to [spring training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_training) with the [Detroit Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Tigers). A few hours later, that game was announced as a [group pool stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_stage) game against Great Britain. The decision was met with controversy, with fans calling Skubal a "quitter" and wondering if it would be better to pick another pitcher in his place.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

Skubal blamed the calendar, saying, "If this tournament were in the middle of season, like if it was [\[Olympic\] hockey](75053831.md), I'd had no problem playing those games".[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

Later in the tournament, fan scrutiny increased in part due to a statement by [Bryce Harper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryce_Harper) comparing the tournament with the Olympics. This sparked discussion about American players' commitment to a team, which had also been questioned in prior WBC editions.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

The peak of debate went after the loss over Italy during group pool play. Prior to the U.S.–Italy game, U.S. manager [Mark DeRosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_DeRosa) erroneously mentioned in an interview with [MLB Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Network) that Team USA had qualified for the quarterfinals, though no spot had been clinched at the time. DeRosa also allowed players to celebrate the early qualification, contributing to a loss against Italy that left the U.S. team's fate in jeopardy.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

The U.S. ended up qualifying for the quarterfinals after Mexico lost to the same Italian team. Following this, a win against Canada and a controversial win over the Dominican Republic guaranteed a spot in the championship game.

After the loss in the final to Venezuela, fans called for DeRosa to step down from the national team, even if he wanted to manage Team USA for the next WBC.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

### Insurance rule debate and Puerto Rico roster issues

> Could we participate with 10 horrible (players) for fear that there will be some kind of retaliation? Or does the dignity of Puerto Rico have some kind of value or price?

— Jose Quiles, President of Puerto Rican Baseball Federation

[^cite_note-Del_Valle-91]

If players are on Major League Baseball 40-man rosters, they must have an insurance policy that compensates teams (in case a player is injured during the tournament)[^cite_note-92] or else have a written [waiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waiver) allowing the player to play. The reasons to refuse a player are various, but often related to age, extensive injury history or recent injuries.[^cite_note-Rosenthal-93][^cite_note-94] Most of WBC and MLB-related insurances are issued by National Financial Partners, an [Aon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aon_\(company\)) company.[^cite_note-Rosenthal-93][^cite_note-95]

This rule has long been a point of contention for fans, teams, and organizers, because it often affects the performance of teams and makes teams heavily dependent on knowing the eligibility of a player beforehand. For example, in 2023, [Clayton Kershaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayton_Kershaw) was not allowed to play for Team USA due to an insurance refusal, but was allowed to play in 2026 because he is now technically classified as a [free agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_agent) due to his retirement following the [2025 MLB season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_MLB_season).[^cite_note-Rosenthal-93][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

The rule reached another boiling point in 2026 due to widespread insurance refusal among players, such as [Jose Altuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Altuve) and [Miguel Rojas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Rojas_\(baseball\)) of Venezuela.[^cite_note-98]









Puerto Rico's [Francisco Lindor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Lindor) (top left), [Carlos Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Correa) (top right), [Víctor Caratini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Caratini) (bottom left) and [José Berríos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Berr%C3%ADos) were all prevented from playing in the tournament for insurance reasons.

[^cite_note-Rosenthal-93]

Team Puerto Rico suffered from the refusal of several potential players, such as [Francisco Lindor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Lindor) and [Carlos Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Correa). The impact on the team prompted fans to boycott the tournament, and the federation, alongside the local government, considered withdrawing from the tournament completely due to the insurance issues with the players.[^cite_note-Rosenthal-93][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] The situation quickly escalated to WBC and MLB leaderships, resulting in an emergency meeting with Puerto Rico on the afternoon of January 31. After the meeting, it was reported that [World Baseball Classic Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Classic_Inc.) (WBCI), MLB, and the [Major League Baseball Players Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_Players_Association) (MLBPA) were negotiating with insurers to change some decisions.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] On February 1, another meeting was held between MLB and Puerto Rican federation. Reports indicated that the Puerto Rican federation and local businesspeople were working to secure backup insurance in case the original insurance re-refused the players, but the three players asked by Puerto Rico were unable to be selected due to the insurance rules.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-Del_Valle-91]

The president of [WBSC Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBSC_Americas) and Venezuelan Baseball Federation, Aracelis León, also voiced her displeasure at the insurance issues on February 1, asking WBCI and MLBPA to fix the situation.[^cite_note-104] Lindor was ruled out for the Classic on February 11, due to having surgery on his hand for a [hamate bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamate_bone) injury.[^cite_note-105]

### Visa denials for Cuban staff

The [Baseball Federation of Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Federation_of_Cuba) announced on February 25, 2026, that American officials had denied visas to eight members of its traveling party, including the organization's president and general secretary due to the ongoing [2026 Cuban crisis](82348646.md). The visa denials were tied to the tightening of immigration policies, which currently require specialized licensing for Cuban team officials to enter the U.S. All Cuban players and coaches were cleared to enter the country due to visa exemptions for athletes and coaches participating in big sporting tournaments, Olympic qualifiers, and events supported by Major League Baseball.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

### Non-usage of ABS

As was the case in recent WBCs, the 2026 tournament uses [the regular season MLB rules of the prior year's season](77630006.md). This meant that the [Automated Ball-Strike System (ABS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_Ball-Strike_System) challenge rules would not be in play for the 2026 WBC.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

Following the opening game of the tournament between [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_national_baseball_team) and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_baseball_team), umpire Omar Peralta was heavily criticized by Taiwanese fans and media for missing key calls, leading Taiwanese media to describe his [strike-zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_zone) as an "[amoeba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoeba)". Chinese Taipei ended up losing 0–3 against Australia, in one of the most critical games of Pool C, which caused the controversy to deepen and raised calls for an early adoption of ABS.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

On March 4, 2026, [Ronald Acuña Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Acu%C3%B1a_Jr.) mistakenly tried to use the ABS challenge system, in [Venezuela](4264306.md)'s pre-tournament exhibition game against the [Houston Astros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Astros).[^cite_note-114]

On March 15, 2026, the [Dominican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic_national_baseball_team) lost to the [United States](4119270.md) in the semifinal on a controversial strike during a full count where had it been reviewed and called correctly, would have allowed [Geraldo Perdomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldo_Perdomo) to walk as it was a ball. This led to further calls to implement ABS during the tournament.[^cite_note-115]

### Taiwanese Premier Cho Jung-tai's visit

See also: [Japan–Taiwan relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93Taiwan_relations)

In March 2026, Taiwanese Premier [Cho Jung-tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cho_Jung-tai) made a personal trip to [Tokyo](30057.md) to watch the 2026 World Baseball Classic, marking the first time the Taiwanese Premier visited Japan since diplomatic relations were severed in 1972. Chinese [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](7453497.md) spokesperson [Guo Jiakun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guo_Jiakun) criticized the visit as having "evil designs" and warned "Japan's indulgence in provocation ... will inevitably come at a cost". The Japanese government defended the trip as a personal affair, while Cho said he paid for the trip himself.[^cite_note-116] Cho claimed that his out-of-pocket expenses included purchasing WBC tickets from Taiwan's [Chinese Professional Baseball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Professional_Baseball_League) (CPBL). Since tickets for Taiwan's matches had sold out early, this further triggered controversy regarding the CPBL's potential involvement in the illegal resale of tickets.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]
