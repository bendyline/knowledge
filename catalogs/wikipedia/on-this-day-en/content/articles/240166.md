[Israel](9282173.md)'s [political system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_system) is based on [proportional representation](23298.md) and allows for a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system) with numerous parties represented in the 120-seat [Knesset](74656.md).

A typical Knesset includes many factions represented. This is because of the low [election threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_threshold) required for a seat – 1 percent of the vote from 1949 to 1992, 1.5 percent from 1992 to 2003, 2 percent from 2003 to 2014, and 3.25 percent since 2015. In the [2015 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Israeli_legislative_election), for instance, ten parties or alliances cleared the threshold, and five of them won at least ten seats. The low threshold, in combination with the nationwide [party-list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_proportional_representation) system, makes it all but impossible for a single party to win the 61 seats needed for a [majority government](465640.md). No party has ever won a majority of seats in an election, the most being 56, won by the [Alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alignment_\(political_party\)) grouping in the [1969 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Israeli_legislative_election) (the Alignment had briefly held a majority of seats before the elections, following its formation in January 1969).

As a result, while only four parties (or their antecedents) have ever led governments, all Israeli governments, as of 2024, have been [coalitions](6036.md) comprising two or more parties.
