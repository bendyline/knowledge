[Donald Trump](4848272.md) assumed office as the 47th [president of the United States](24113.md) on [January 20, 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump). The president has the legal authority to nominate members of his [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States) to the [United States Senate](24909346.md) for confirmation under the [Appointments Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appointments_Clause) of the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution).

All permanent members of the [Cabinet of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States) as heads of executive departments require the [advice and consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent) of the [United States Senate](24909346.md) following appointment by the president before taking office. The vice presidency is exceptional in that the position requires an election to office pursuant to the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution). The president may also designate heads of other agencies and non-Senate-confirmed members of the [Executive Office of the President](44029.md) as cabinet-level members of the cabinet. The cabinet meets with the president in the [Cabinet Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Room_\(White_House\)), a room adjacent to the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office).

As the Republican Party controls the Senate, it was expected that all of Trump's designees would be confirmed with little contest.[^cite_note-1] However, some nominees were met with criticism by a few Senate Republicans.[^cite_note-dhm-nyt-24-2]

Trump's cabinet choices were described by *[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider)* and [Reuters](18998750.md) as valuing personal loyalty over relevant experience,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] and for having a range of conflicting ideologies and "eclectic personalities".[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] It was also described as the wealthiest administration in modern history, with more than 13 billionaires chosen to take government posts.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Trump officials and [Elon Musk](909036.md) threatened to fund primary challengers in upcoming elections against Republican senators who did not vote for Trump's nominees.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] He nominated or appointed 23 former [Fox News](11121.md) employees to his administration.[^cite_note-11]

On November 12, 2024, [President-elect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President-elect_of_the_United_States) Trump announced that his administration would establish a [Department of Government Efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency) (DOGE).[^cite_note-12] Despite the name, DOGE is not a [federal executive department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_executive_departments), since official departments require [congressional](31756.md) approval, but a component of the [Executive Office of the President](44029.md).[^cite_note-DOGE-executive-order-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Elon Musk, a then [senior advisor to the President](29024072.md), played a key role in DOGE's operations before leaving the government in May 2025, as his special employee government contract had ended. Shortly after Musk left the government, Trump and Musk exchanged a [public feud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump%E2%80%93Musk_feud) over the nearly \$3 trillion deficit projected to result from the [One Big Beautiful Bill.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Big_Beautiful_Bill_Act)[^cite_note-Peoples_&_Barrow_2024-16][^cite_note-17]

### Vice President

Main articles: [2024 Republican Party vice presidential candidate selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_Party_vice_presidential_candidate_selection) and [Office of the Vice President of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Vice_President_of_the_United_States)

See also: [Vice presidency of JD Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_presidency_of_JD_Vance)

The vice president is the only cabinet member to be elected to the position. The vice president does not require Senate confirmation, and does not serve at the pleasure of the president. There were [dozens of potential running mates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_Party_vice_presidential_candidate_selection#Media_speculation_on_possible_candidates) for Trump who received media speculation. Trump's eventual pick of Senator [JD Vance](53396477.md) (R-OH) was officially announced on July 15, 2024, and confirmed [by acclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acclamation#Voting) via [parliamentary procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_procedure) amongst delegates to the [2024 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_National_Convention) on July 15, 2024.

[United States senator](24909346.md) **[JD Vance](53396477.md)** (R-OH) was elected [Vice President of the United States](32759.md) on November 5, 2024. He received 312 electoral votes while the [governor of Minnesota](214877.md), [Tim Walz](2216593.md), received 226. The [formal certification of the results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_Electoral_College_vote_count) took place on January 6, 2025. He [assumed office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump) on January 20, 2025. Vance is the [third-youngest vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_vice_presidents_of_the_United_States_by_age) in [U.S. history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States).

Bondi was fired on April 2, 2026, and replaced by [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) in the interim.[^cite_note-46]

### White House chief of staff

The [White House chief of staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Chief_of_Staff) has traditionally been the highest-ranking staff employee of the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Office). The responsibilities of the chief of staff are both managerial and advisory over the president's official business. The chief of staff is appointed by and serves at the pleasure of the [president](24113.md); it does not require [Senate confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent). On November 7, 2024, Trump announced [Susie Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susie_Wiles) from [Florida](18933066.md) as his choice for his chief of staff, having served as his [2024 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2024_presidential_campaign) co-chair. Wiles is the first woman to hold the position.[^cite_note-wiles-94]

## Acting Cabinet officials

Because cabinet members must be confirmed by the Senate, acting officials are typically appointed for the period before the Senate votes, in accordance with the [Federal Vacancies Reform Act of 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Vacancies_Reform_Act_of_1998). For cabinet positions, only people who already actively hold a position confirmed by the Senate at the end of the previous administration are eligible, and they retain that position while they are detailed to the acting position.[^cite_note-\:1-95] Normally, a senior employee of the same executive agency who is equivalent to a GS-15 or above on [the federal pay scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Schedule_\(US_civil_service_pay_scale\)) would also be eligible, but this is believed to be unconstitutional in the case of secretaries of the [federal executive departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_executive_departments), although this has not been tested in court.[^cite_note-\:2-96]
