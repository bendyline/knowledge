# Humanoid robot



Valkyrie (R5), a humanoid robot

[^cite_note-1]

&#x20;from [NASA](18426568.md)

A **humanoid robot** is a [robot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot) resembling the [human body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_body) in shape. The design may be aimed at functional purposes, such as interacting with human tools and environments and working alongside humans, for experimental purposes, such as the study of [bipedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipedal) locomotion,[^cite_note-2] or for other purposes.

In general, humanoid robots are characterized by their [anthropomorphic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropomorphism), which includes a torso, a head, two arms, and two legs. Some humanoid robots may have a more limited range of body replicas, comprising only a subset of the above-mentioned components.

The term [*android*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(robot\)) can refer to humanoid robots designed to closely resemble the [human physique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_physique). The term *[gynoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gynoid)* is sometimes used to refer to robots with a feminine appearance.

## Applications

### Medical and research

Humanoid robots are a valuable resource in the world of medicine and biotechnology, as well as other fields of research such as [biomechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomechanics) and cognitive science.[^cite_note-os-3] Humanoid robots are being used to develop complex prosthetics for individuals with physical disabilities such as missing limbs.[^cite_note-w2-4] The WABIAN-2 is a new medical humanoid robot created to help patients in the rehabilitation of their lower limbs.[^cite_note-w2-4]

Although the initial aim of humanoid research was to build better [orthosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthosis) and [prosthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosthesis) for human beings, knowledge has been transferred between both disciplines. A few examples are powered leg prosthesis for the neuromuscularly impaired, ankle-foot orthosis, biological realistic leg prosthesis, and forearm prosthesis.

Humanoid robots can be used as test subjects for the practice and development of personalized healthcare aids, essentially performing as robotic nurses for demographics such as the elderly.[^cite_note-w2-4] Humanoids are also suitable for some procedurally-based vocations, such as reception-desk administrators and automotive manufacturing line workers. In essence, since they can use tools and operate equipment and vehicles designed for the human form, humanoids could theoretically perform any task a human being can, so long as they have the proper [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software). However, the complexity of doing so is immense.

While [robot-assisted surgery](2629669.md) previously relied on specialized hardware, remote-controlled surgeries using general-purpose humanoid robots were experimentally conducted in 2026, demonstrating new possibilities.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

### Entertainment



Humanoid robots in a staged boxing match during a robotics exhibition.

Humanoid robots have long appeared in entertainment and fiction, from mythological figures such as [Prometheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus) to mechanical characters in early automata and science fiction films.[^cite_note-os-3] In contemporary theme parks, humanoid machines are widely used as sophisticated [animatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animatronics) that mimic human motion, facial expressions and speech. Research groups at [Walt Disney Imagineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Imagineering) have developed "stuntronics", free-flying humanoid stunt doubles that use onboard sensing and control to perform aerial flips and poses during live shows, allowing characters such as superheroes to execute stunts that would be dangerous for human performers.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Outside of theme parks, humanoid robots increasingly appear in live sports-style events and competitions. In 2025 the Chinese robotics company Unitree live-streamed what it billed as the first boxing tournament between humanoid robots, with four G1 robots taking part in a small-ring event that combined remote control with short autonomous sequences.[^cite_note-9] The same year, Beijing hosted the inaugural [World Humanoid Robot Games](80751665.md), where more than 500 robots from 280 teams competed in events such as soccer, running and boxing and performed choreographed routines including hip-hop and martial arts during the opening ceremony.[^cite_note-10] WHRG also advertises the capabilities of various manufacturers to investors and potential customers. Exhibition matches and tournaments with humanoid robot football teams have also been promoted as trials for future multi-sport robot events.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Humanoid robots even performed as dancers during the opening ceremony of the [2026 Asian Beach Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Asian_Beach_Games) in [Sanya](1638437.md), China.

Humanoid robots have also been used as performers in concerts and televised shows. At the 2025 VOYAGEX music festival in Changchun, the PNDbotics humanoid Adam appeared on stage as a keytar player as part of a live band performance.[^cite_note-13] Earlier that year, a Chinese Spring Festival television gala featured a choreographed routine in which sixteen Unitree H1 humanoid robots danced alongside human performers, presented as a demonstration of the country's progress in humanoid robotics.[^cite_note-14] These and similar appearances at music events and public festivals have been described as ways to familiarise audiences with humanoid robots while showcasing advances in dynamic control, balance and human–robot interaction.[^cite_note-15] Another example is the 2026 China Media Group Spring Festival Gala.[^cite_note-16]

In July 2026, EngineAI debuted [Ultimate Robot Knock-out Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Robot_Knock-out_Legend) (URKL), a professional combat league for human-sized humanoid robots, with a cash prize pool of 1.4 million dollars and a ten kilogram pure gold belt to be given to the champion. The league issued EngineAI standardized T800 robots to all participating teams who competed by developing algorithms and strategies to help their robot beat their opponents. During the opening matches, a T800 landed a [tornado kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_kick) that resulted in an opponent's head being decapitated.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] According to Zhao Tongyang, EngineAI's CEO, the competition was not solely for entertainment as data from the matches is used to further develop and improve the future generation of humanoids for commercial and industrial applications.[^cite_note-21]

Various humanoid robots and their possible applications in daily life, including service and companion roles, are featured in the independent documentary film *[Plug & Pray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug_&_Pray)*, released in 2010.[^cite_note-os-3]

### Demonstrative



[Ameca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ameca_\(robot\)) generation 1 pictured in the lab at [Engineered Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineered_Arts)

Though many real-world applications for humanoid robots are unexplored, their primary use is to demonstrate up-and-coming technologies.[^cite_note-fr-22] Modern examples of humanoid robots, such as the Honda Asimo, are revealed to the public in order to demonstrate new technological advancements in motor skills, such as walking, climbing, and playing an instrument.[^cite_note-fr-22] Other humanoid robots have been developed for household purposes, however excel only in single purpose skills and are far from autonomous.[^cite_note-fr-22] Humanoid robots, especially those with [artificial intelligence](1164.md) (AI) algorithms, could be useful for future dangerous and/or distant [space exploration](28431.md) missions, without having the need to turn back around again and return to [Earth](9228.md) once the mission is completed.

## Sensors

A [sensor](235757.md) is a device that measures some attribute of the world. Being one of the three primitives of robotics (besides planning and control), sensing plays an important role in [robotic paradigms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotic_paradigms).

Sensors can be classified according to the physical process with which they work or according to the type of measurement information that they give as output. In this case, the second approach was used.[^cite_note-23]

### Proprioceptive

[Proprioceptive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprioceptive) sensors sense the position, orientation, and speed of the humanoid's body and joints, along with other internal values.[^cite_note-24]

In human beings, the otoliths and semi-circular canals (in the inner ear) are used to maintain balance and orientation.[^cite_note-25] Additionally, humans use their own proprioceptive sensors (e.g. touch, muscle extension, limb position) to help with their orientation. Humanoid robots use [accelerometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerometer) to measure the acceleration, from which velocity can be calculated by integration;[^cite_note-26] [tilt sensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilt_sensor) to measure inclination; force sensors placed in robot's hands and feet to measure contact force with environment;[^cite_note-27] position sensors that indicate the actual position of the robot (from which the velocity can be calculated by derivation);[^cite_note-28] and even speed sensors.

### Exteroceptive



An artificial hand holding a [lightbulb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightbulb)

Arrays of [tactels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactile_sensor) can be used to provide data on what has been touched. The [Shadow Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Hand) uses an array of 34 tactels arranged beneath its [polyurethane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurethane) skin on each finger tip.[^cite_note-29] Tactile sensors also provide information about forces and torques transferred between the robot and other objects.

*[Computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision)* refers to the processing of data from any modality which uses light to produce an image. In humanoid robots it is used to [recognize objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_recognition) and determine their properties. Vision sensors work most similarly to the eyes of human beings. Most humanoid robots use [charge-coupled device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge-coupled_device) cameras as vision sensors.

Sound sensors allow humanoid robots to hear speech and environmental sounds, akin to the ears of the human being. [Microphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphone) are usually used for the robots to convey speech.

## Actuators

See also: [Humanoid hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanoid_hand)

[Actuators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuator) are the motors responsible for motion in the robot.[^cite_note-sr-30] The most popular example of a humanoid robot using hydraulic actuators was the original [Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_\(robot\)) robot made by [Boston Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Dynamics) (retired in 2024).

Humanoid robots are constructed in such a way that they mimic the human body. They use actuators that perform like [muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle) and [joints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint), though with a different structure.[^cite_note-sr-30] The actuators of humanoid robots can be either electric, [pneumatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumatic), or [hydraulic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic).[^cite_note-mh-31][^cite_note-ba-32] It is ideal for these actuators to have high power, low mass, and small dimensions.[^cite_note-ba-32]

### Electric

Electric actuators are the most popular types of actuators in humanoid robots.[^cite_note-mh-31] These actuators are smaller in size, and a single electric actuator may not produce enough power for a human-sized joint.[^cite_note-mh-31] Therefore, it is common to use multiple electric actuators for a single joint in a humanoid robot.[^cite_note-mh-31] An example of a humanoid robot using electric actuators is [HRP-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HRP-2).[^cite_note-ba-32] By 2026, the industry transitioned toward integrated joint modules that combine high-torque brushless motors with [strain wave gearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strain_wave_gearing) for increased power density.

### Hydraulic

Hydraulic actuators produce higher power than electric actuators and pneumatic actuators, and they have the ability to control the torque they produce better than other types of actuators.[^cite_note-ba-32] However, they can become very bulky in size.[^cite_note-mh-31][^cite_note-ba-32] One solution to counter the size issue is [electro-hydrostatic actuators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-hydraulic_actuator) (EHA).[^cite_note-ba-32] The most popular example of a humanoid robot using hydraulic actuators is the [ATLAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_\(robot\)) robot made by [Boston Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Dynamics).[^cite_note-ba-32][^cite_note-33]

### Pneumatic

Pneumatic actuators operate on the basis of [gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas) [compressibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressibility).[^cite_note-mh-31][^cite_note-ba-32] As they are inflated, they expand along the axis, and as they deflate, they contract. If one end is fixed, the other will move in a linear [trajectory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajectory). A popular example of a pneumatic actuator is the [Mac Kibben muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumatic_artificial_muscles).[^cite_note-ba-32]

## Planning and control

Planning in robots is the process of planning out motions and trajectories for the robot to carry out.[^cite_note-pc-34] Control is the actual execution of these planned motions and trajectories.[^cite_note-pc-34] In humanoid robots, the planning must carry out biped motions, meaning that robots should plan motions similar to a human.[^cite_note-th-35] Since one of the main uses of humanoid robots is to interact with humans, it is important for the planning and control mechanisms of humanoid robots to work in a variety of terrain and environments.[^cite_note-th-35]

The question of walking biped robots stabilization on the surface is of great importance.[^cite_note-ms-36] Maintenance of the robot's gravity center over the center of bearing area for providing a stable position can be chosen as a goal of control.[^cite_note-ms-36]

To maintain dynamic balance during the [walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk), a robot needs information about contact force and its current and desired motion.[^cite_note-th-35] The solution to this problem relies on a major concept, the [Zero Moment Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_Moment_Point) (ZMP).[^cite_note-th-35]

Another characteristic of humanoid robots is that they move, gather information (using sensors) on the "real world", and interact with it.[^cite_note-ue-37] They do not stay still like factory manipulators and other robots that work in highly structured environments.[^cite_note-ue-37] To allow humanoids to move in complex environments, planning and control must focus on self-collision detection, [path planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_planning) and [obstacle avoidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstacle_avoidance).[^cite_note-ue-37][^cite_note-38]

Humanoid robots do not yet have some features of the human body.[^cite_note-hr-39] They include structures with variable flexibility, which provide safety (to the robot itself and to the people), and redundancy of movements, i.e. more [degrees of freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrees_of_freedom_\(engineering\)) and therefore wide task availability.[^cite_note-hr-39] Although these characteristics are desirable to humanoid robots, they will bring more complexity and new problems to planning and control.[^cite_note-40] The field of whole-body control deals with these issues and addresses the proper coordination of numerous degrees of freedom, e.g. to realize several control tasks simultaneously while following a given order of priority.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

Some humanoid robots feature only the upper body, which is typically mounted to a wheeled base,[^cite_note-43] drastically reducing the risk of falling common to [bipedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipedal) units.[^cite_note-44]

## In science fiction

A common theme for the depiction of humanoid robots in science fiction pertains to how they can help humans in society or serve as threats to humanity.[^cite_note-sf-138] This theme essentially questions whether artificial intelligence is a force of good or bad for mankind.[^cite_note-sf-138] Humanoid robots that are depicted as good for society and benefit humans include [Commander Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_\(Star_Trek\)) in *[Star Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek)* and [C-3PO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-3PO) in *[Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars)*.[^cite_note-sf-138] Opposite portrayals where humanoid robots are shown as terrifying and threatening to humans include the T-800 in *[Terminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_\(character\))* and [Megatron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megatron) in *[Transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformers).*[^cite_note-sf-138] An Indian [Tamil-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) film, *[Enthiran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enthiran)*, showed the benefits and disadvantages of a humanoid robot, [Chitti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitti_\(character\)).[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

Another prominent theme found in science fiction regarding humanoid robots focuses on personhood. Certain films, particularly *[Blade Runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner)* and *[Blade Runner 2049](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner_2049)*, explore whether or not a constructed, synthetic being should be considered a person.[^cite_note-141] In the films, androids called "[replicants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replicant)" are created indistinguishably from human beings, yet they are shunned and do not possess the same rights as humans. This theme incites audience sympathy while also sparking unease at the idea of humanoid robots mimicking humans too closely.[^cite_note-142]

The term *[gynoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gynoid)* was first used by [Isaac Asimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Asimov) in a 1979 editorial, as a theoretical female equivalent of the word *android*.[^cite_note-143]

## Criticism

Humanoid robots are considered inefficient for performing many kinds of work as they share the same general flaws present in the human form and can be outperformed by other kinds of robots.[^cite_note-144] Some have suggested that forcing robots to perform labor could be akin to [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery),[^cite_note-145] but others argue that there is no reason to think AI is conscious.[^cite_note-146] [Sex robots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_robot) may present ethical issues[^cite_note-147] and robots designed to look like humans can evoke feelings of unease or revulsion in humans due to the [uncanny valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncanny_valley) effect.[^cite_note-148]
