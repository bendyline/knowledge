**David Szalay** ([/ˈsɒlɔɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*SOL-oy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born January 1974) is a British and Hungarian writer. His novels *[All That Man Is](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_That_Man_Is)* and [*Turbulence*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbulence_\(Szalay_novel\)) have unique narrative structures with intertwined short stories. *All That Man Is* was shortlisted for the [2016 Man Booker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Man_Booker_Prize) and won the [Gordon Burn Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Burn_Prize) that year. Szalay's sixth novel, *[Flesh](79950367.md)*, won the [2025 Booker Prize](80569020.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Education and early life

Szalay was born in Montreal in 1974, into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Canada) family,[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] to a Canadian mother and a Hungarian father. His family then moved to [Beirut](37428.md). They were forced to leave Lebanon after the onset of the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War). They next moved to London, where he attended [Sussex House School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_House_School).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-Clark-2018-8] Szalay read English at [Brasenose College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasenose_College,_Oxford).[^cite_note-Unitedagents-9][^cite_note-10] After graduating, he worked sales jobs in London. Szalay moved to [Brussels](3708.md), then to [Pécs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9cs), Hungary, to pursue his ambition of becoming a writer.[^cite_note-Clark-2018-8]

## Career

Szalay has written a number of radio dramas for the [BBC](19344654.md).[^cite_note-Unitedagents-9] His 2018 short-story collection *Turbulence* originated in a series of 15-minute programmes for [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4). Its 12 stories follow different people on flights around the world, exploring the [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization) of family and friendship in the 21st century.[^cite_note-Cummins-2025-11] He won the [Betty Trask Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Trask_Prize_and_Awards) for his first novel, *London and the South-East*, along with the [Geoffrey Faber Memorial Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Faber_Memorial_Prize). Since then, he has written three other novels: *Innocent* (2009),[^cite_note-innocent-12] *Spring* (2011),[^cite_note-spring-13] and *[Flesh](79950367.md)* (2025).[^cite_note-14]

Szalay's linked collection of short stories *[All That Man Is](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_That_Man_Is)*[^cite_note-allthatmanis-15] was shortlisted for the [Man Booker Prize](4446.md) and won the [Gordon Burn Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Burn_Prize) in 2016.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] *[The Spectator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spectator)* wrote, "nobody captures the super-sadness of modern Europe as well as Szalay".[^cite_note-18] *[The Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Observer)* questioned its structure and whether it qualifies as a novel in the traditional sense: "does it in any sense work, as [Jonathan Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Cape) wants us to believe, as a novel? Yes, there's a thematic consistency that makes this more than a collection, and Szalay even throws in the odd narrative link (the 73-year-old, it transpires, is the 17-year-old's granddad). But still, a novel? I don't think so."[^cite_note-Skidelsky-2016-19]

Szalay was included in *[The Daily Telegraph](23797577.md)*'s 2010 list of the top 20 British writers under 40,[^cite_note-20] as well as the 2013 edition of the [*Granta* Best of Young British Novelists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granta#Granta_Best_of_Young_British_Novelists).[^cite_note-21] In 2025, his novel *Flesh* won the Booker Prize.[^cite_note-22] Szalay is the first Hungarian–British author to receive the award.[^cite_note-Muvija-2025-23][^cite_note-24] He said he "wanted to write a book that stretched between Hungary and London and involved a character who was not quite at home in either place."[^cite_note-Muvija-2025-23] A film adaptation of *Flesh* was announced in November 2025, with House Productions as the producer.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

### Bibliography

The categorization of Szalay's books as novels has sparked debate, particularly in the cases of *All That Man Is*[^cite_note-Skidelsky-2016-19] and *Turbulence*.[^cite_note-27] *All That Man Is* comprises nine distinct stories that weave a larger thematic picture. *Turbulence* is a collection of twelve loosely connected stories about different people. *Flesh*, while episodic, more closely resembles a conventional novel centred on a single protagonist.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]
