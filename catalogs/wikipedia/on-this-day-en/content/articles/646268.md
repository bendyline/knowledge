In Russia, the **oblasts** are 46 administrative territories; they are one type of [federal subject](701438.md), the highest-level administrative division of Russian territory.[^cite_note-Hea23-1]<sup>: 43 </sup>

## Overview

Oblasts are constituent [political entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity) in a [federal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_state) with representation in the [Federation Council](1268578.md), and serve as a first-level [administrative division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division). Each oblast features a [state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_government) holding authority over a defined geographic territory, with a [state legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislature), the *Oblast Duma*, that is [democratically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) elected. The [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_\(Russia\)) is the highest [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) position of the state government in an oblast and is elected by the people. Oblasts are divided into *[raions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion#Russian_Federation)* (districts), [cities of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance) (district-equivalent [independent cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city)), and [autonomous okrugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_okrug), which are legally federal subjects equal to an oblast but are administratively subservient to one. Two oblasts have autonomous okrugs: [Arkhangelsk Oblast](472779.md) ([Nenets Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenets_Autonomous_Okrug)) and [Tyumen Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyumen_Oblast) ([Khanty-Mansi Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanty-Mansi_Autonomous_Okrug) and [Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug](482044.md)).

The term *[oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)* can be translated into [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) as "[province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province)" or "[region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region)", and there are currently 46 oblasts, the most common type of the 85 federal subjects in Russia.[^cite_note-Hea23-1] The majority of oblasts are named after their [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre#Russia), the official term for a [capital city](181337.md) in an oblast, which is generally the largest city. Exceptions to this include [Leningrad Oblast](480845.md) and [Moscow Oblast](372315.md), which have no official capital, and [Sakhalin Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin_Oblast), which is named after a geographic location. Leningrad Oblast and [Sverdlovsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sverdlovsk_Oblast) retain the previous names of [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) and [Yekaterinburg](66524.md), respectively. Oblasts are typically areas that are predominantly populated by ethnic [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_people) and native [Russian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) speakers, and are mostly located in [European Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Russia). The largest oblast by geographic size is [Tyumen Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyumen_Oblast) at 1,435,200 km<sup>2</sup> (excluding autonomous okrugs [Irkutsk Oblast](79477.md) is the largest at 767,900 km<sup>2</sup>) and the smallest is [Kaliningrad Oblast](17311.md) at 15,100 km<sup>2</sup>. The most populous oblast is [Moscow Oblast](372315.md) at 7,095,120 and the least populous is [Magadan Oblast](480997.md) at 156,996.

[Krais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krais_of_Russia), another type of federal subject, are legally identical to oblasts. The difference between a political entity with the name "oblast" and one named "[krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krai)" is purely traditional.

## History

Further information: [oblasts of the Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblasts_of_the_Russian_Empire) and [oblasts of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblasts_of_the_Soviet_Union)

In the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), oblasts were a third-level administrative division, organized in 1849 and few in number, dividing the larger *[guberniyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_\(Russia\))* (governorates) within the first-level [krais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krais_of_the_Russian_Empire). Following the numerous administration reforms during the [Soviet era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union), the number of oblasts gradually increased as they became the primary top-level administrative division of the [Soviet Socialist Republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union) (SSRs), the constituent political entities of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). These oblasts held very little [autonomy](191145.md) or power, but when the [Soviet Union dissolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) into [sovereign states](23604120.md) along the lines of the SSRs, they became the first-level administrative divisions. The oblasts of the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic), which transitioned into the Russian Federation, became the first-level administrative divisions of the new country and received greater [devolved power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution).

### Russo-Ukrainian War

During the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Russia [annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts) the [Donetsk](494889.md), [Kherson](1273370.md), [Luhansk](494904.md) and [Zaporizhzhia](494936.md) regions of Ukraine.[^cite_note-2] While Donetsk and Luhansk were incorporated as republics, having their respective separate constitutions adopted since 2014 back when they were two breakaway states of Ukraine, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia were annexed *de jure*, but not *de facto*, as regular oblasts. Including these, the Kremlin claims a total of 48 oblasts. The four regions remain internationally recognized as part of Ukraine and are only partially occupied by Russia and its control of the territory is not totally assured.[^cite_note-Hea23-1]<sup>: 5–6, 16 </sup>
