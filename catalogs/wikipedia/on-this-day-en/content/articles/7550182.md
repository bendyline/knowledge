The **Falkland Islands** ([/ˈfɔː(l)klənd, ˈfɒlk-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-8] Spanish: *Islas Malvinas* [\[ˈislas malˈβinas\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)), commonly referred to as the **Falklands**, is an [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) in the [South Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Ocean) on the [Patagonian Shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagonian_Shelf). The principal islands are about 300 mi (500 km) east of South America's southern [Patagonian](65433.md) coast and 752 mi (1,210 km) from [Cape Dubouzet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Dubouzet) at the northern tip of the [Antarctic Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_Peninsula), at a latitude of about 52°S. The archipelago, with an area of 4,700 sq mi (12,000 km<sup>2</sup>), comprises [East Falkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Falkland), [West Falkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Falkland), and 776 smaller islands. As a [British Overseas Territory](207114.md), the Falklands have internal [self-governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance), while the United Kingdom takes responsibility for their defence and foreign affairs. The capital and largest settlement is [Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley,_Falkland_Islands) on East Falkland.

The islands are believed to have been uninhabited prior to European discovery in the 17th century. Controversy exists over the Falklands' discovery and subsequent colonisation by Europeans. At various times, the islands have had French, British, Spanish, and Argentine settlements. Britain [reasserted its rule in 1833](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reassertion_of_British_sovereignty_over_the_Falkland_Islands_\(1833\)), but [Argentina](18951905.md) maintains its claim to the islands. In April 1982, Argentine military forces [invaded the islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_invasion_of_the_Falkland_Islands). British administration was restored two months later at the end of the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War). In a [2013 sovereignty referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Falkland_Islands_sovereignty_referendum), almost all of the votes cast were in favour of remaining a UK overseas territory. The territory's sovereignty status is part of an ongoing [dispute between Argentina and the UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_sovereignty_dispute).

The 2021 census recorded a population of 3,662 inhabitants,[^cite_note-census2021-4] around 30% of which are temporary residents, on short-term work visas or working at the [RAF Mount Pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Mount_Pleasant) military base. English is the official and predominant language. Under the [British Nationality (Falkland Islands) Act 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Nationality_\(Falkland_Islands\)_Act_1983), Falkland Islanders are [British citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_citizens).

The islands lie at the boundary of the [subantarctic oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) and [tundra climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra_climate) zones, and both major islands have mountain ranges reaching 2,300 ft (700 m). They are home to large bird populations, although many no longer breed on the main islands owing to predation by [introduced species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduced_species). Major economic activities include fishing, tourism, and [sheep farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep_farming), with an emphasis on high-quality wool exports. [Oil exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_exploration), licensed by the [Falkland Islands Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_Government), remains controversial as a result of maritime disputes with Argentina.

## Etymology

See also: [List of Falkland Islands placenames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Falkland_Islands_placenames)

The name *Falkland Islands* comes from [Falkland Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Sound), the [strait](58646.md) that separates the two main islands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJones200973-9] The name *Falkland* was applied to the channel by [John Strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Strong_\(mariner\)), captain of an English expedition that landed on the islands in 1690. Strong named the strait in honour of [Anthony Cary, 5th Viscount Falkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Cary,_5th_Viscount_Falkland), the [Treasurer of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer_of_the_Navy) who sponsored his journey.[^cite_note-10] The Viscount's title originates from the town of [Falkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland,_Fife), Scotland—the town's name probably comes from a [Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic) term referring to an *enclosure* (*lann*),[^cite_note-12] but it could less plausibly be from the Anglo-Saxon term *folkland* (land held by [folk-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_law#Folk-right)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoom2006129-13] The name *Falklands* was not applied to the islands until 1765, when [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) captain [John Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Byron) of the [Royal Navy](26061.md) claimed them for King [George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III) as "Falkland's Islands".[^cite_note-14] The term *Falklands* is a standard abbreviation used to refer to the islands.

The common Spanish name for the archipelago, *Islas Malvinas*, derives from the French *Îles Malouines*—the name given to the islands by [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France) explorer [Louis-Antoine de Bougainville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis-Antoine_de_Bougainville) in 1764.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHince2001121-15] Bougainville, who founded the islands' first settlement, named the area after the port of [Saint-Malo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Malo) (the point of departure for his ships and colonists).[^cite_note-16] The port, located in the [Brittany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany) region of western France, was named after [St. Malo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malo_\(saint\)) (or Maclou), the Christian [evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism) who founded the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBalmaceda2011Chapter_36-17]

In 1965, at the 20th session[^cite_note-18] of the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md), the [Fourth Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Fourth_Committee) determined that, in all languages other than Spanish, all UN documentation would designate the territory as *Falkland Islands (*Malvinas*)*. In Spanish, the territory was designated as **Islas Malvinas* (Falkland Islands)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEForeign_Office196180-19] The nomenclature used by the United Nations for statistical processing purposes is *Falkland Islands (*Malvinas*)*.[^cite_note-std_name-20]

## History

Main articles: [History of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Falkland_Islands) and [Timeline of the history of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_history_of_the_Falkland_Islands)

Although [Fuegians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuegians) from [Patagonia](65433.md) may have visited the Falkland Islands in prehistoric times,[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] the islands were uninhabited when Europeans first explored them.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarafano2005367-23] European claims of discovery date back to the 16th century, but no consensus exists on whether early explorers sighted the Falklands or other islands in the South Atlantic.[^cite_note-M_White-24][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoebel1971xiv–xv-25][^cite_note-27] The first undisputed landing on the islands is attributed to English captain John Strong, who, en route to [Peru](170691.md) and [Chile](5489.md)'s littoral in 1690, explored the Falkland Sound and noted the islands' water and game.[^cite_note-28]

The Falklands remained uninhabited until the 1764 establishment of [Port Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Louis,_Falkland_Islands) on [East Falkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Falkland) by French captain [Louis Antoine de Bougainville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Antoine_de_Bougainville) and the 1765 foundation of [Port Egmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Egmont) on [Saunders Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunders_Island,_Falkland_Islands) by British captain [John Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Byron); the latter settlement being expanded by British captain [John MacBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_MacBride_\(Royal_Navy_officer\)) a year later.[^cite_note-31] Whether or not the settlements were aware of each other's existence is debated by historians.[^cite_note-32] In 1766, [France](5843419.md) surrendered its claim on the Falklands to [Spain](26667.md), which renamed the French colony [Puerto Soledad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Soledad) the following year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESegal1991240-33] Problems began when Spain detected and [captured Port Egmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Port_Egmont) in 1770. [War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_Crisis_\(1770\)) was narrowly avoided by its restitution to Britain in 1771.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibran199826-34]



[Gauchos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauchos) drinking [mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mate_\(drink\)) at a salting plant in [Valle Esperanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Place), located in the centre of [East Falkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Falkland) in the 1850s.

The British and Spanish settlements coexisted in the archipelago until 1774, when Britain's new economic and strategic considerations led it to withdraw the garrison from the islands, leaving a plaque claiming the Falklands for King George III.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibran199826–27-35] Spain's [Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) became the only formal presence in the territory. [West Falkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Falkland) was left abandoned, and Puerto Soledad became a penal colony.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibran199827-36] Amid the [British invasions of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_invasions_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) during the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) in Europe, the islands' governor evacuated the archipelago in 1806; Spain's remaining colonial garrison followed suit in 1811, except for [gauchos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaucho) and fishermen who remained voluntarily.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibran199827-36]

Thereafter, the archipelago was visited only by fishing ships; its political status was undisputed until 1820, when Colonel [David Jewett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Jewett), an American [privateer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privateer) working for the [United Provinces of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata), informed anchored ships about [Buenos Aires](255919.md)' 1816 claim to Spain's territories in the South Atlantic.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-39] Since the islands had no permanent inhabitants, in 1823 Buenos Aires granted German-born merchant [Luis Vernet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Vernet) permission to conduct fishing activities and exploit feral cattle in the archipelago.[^cite_note-42] Vernet settled at the ruins of Puerto Soledad in 1826, and accumulated resources on the islands until the venture was secure enough to bring settlers and form a permanent colony.[^cite_note-43] Buenos Aires named Vernet military and civil commander of the islands in 1829,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPascoePepper2008540–46-44] and he attempted to regulate sealing to stop the activities of foreign whalers and sealers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibran199827-36] Vernet's venture lasted until a dispute related to fishing and hunting rights led to [a raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_Expedition) by the American warship [USS *Lexington*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Lexington_\(1825\)) in 1831,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPascoePepper2008541–44-45][^cite_note-46] when [United States Navy](20518076.md) commander [Silas Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silas_Duncan) declared the dissolution of the island's government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPeterson1964106-47]

Depiction of a Falklands [corral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corral), shepherds and sheep in 1849 (painting by Royal Navy Admiral [Edward Fanshawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Fanshawe))

Buenos Aires attempted to gain influence over the settlement by installing a garrison in October 1832, which mutinied within a month and was followed the next year by the arrival of British forces, who [reasserted Britain's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reassertion_of_British_sovereignty_over_the_Falkland_Islands_\(1833\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraham-Yooll200250-48] The [Argentine Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Confederation) (headed by Buenos Aires Governor [Juan Manuel de Rosas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_de_Rosas)) protested against Britain's actions,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReginaldElliot198325–26-49][^cite_note-52] and Argentine governments have continued since then to register official protests against Britain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGustafson198834–35-53][^cite_note-55] The British troops departed after completing their mission, leaving the area without formal government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraham-Yooll200251–52-56] Vernet's deputy, the Scotsman [Matthew Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Brisbane), returned to the islands that year to restore the business, but his efforts ended after, amid unrest at Port Louis, gaucho [Antonio Rivero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Rivero) led a group of dissatisfied individuals to murder Brisbane and the settlement's senior leaders; survivors hid in a cave on a nearby island until the British returned and restored order.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGraham-Yooll200251–52-56] In the late 1830s, an appeal was made to the Colonial Office in London by businessmen seeing potential profit, for organised settlement of the islands.[^cite_note-57] In 1840, the Falklands became a [Crown colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_colony) and Scottish settlers subsequently established an official pastoral community.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAldrichConnell1998201-58] Four years later, nearly everyone relocated to Port Jackson, considered a better location for the government, and merchant [Samuel Lafone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Lafone) began a venture to encourage British colonisation.[^cite_note-59]

[Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley,_Falkland_Islands), as Port Jackson was soon renamed, officially became the seat of government in 1845.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReginaldElliot19839-60] Early in its history, Stanley had a negative reputation due to cargo-shipping losses; only in emergencies would ships rounding [Cape Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Horn) stop at the port.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBernhardson2011Stanley_and_Vicinity:_History-61] Nevertheless, the Falklands' geographic location proved ideal for ship repairs and the "Wrecking Trade", the business of selling and buying shipwrecks and their cargoes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStrange198772–74-62] Aside from this trade, commercial interest in the archipelago was minimal due to the low-value hides of the feral cattle roaming the pastures. Economic growth began only after the [Falkland Islands Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_Company), which bought out Lafone's failing enterprise in 1851,[^cite_note-64] successfully introduced [Cheviot sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheviot_sheep) for wool farming, spurring other farms to follow suit.[^cite_note-Bernhardson_2011_loc=Stanley_and_Vicinity:_History-65] The high cost of importing materials, combined with the shortage of labour and consequent high wages, meant the ship repair trade became uncompetitive. After 1870 it declined as the replacement of sail ships by [steamships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamship) was accelerated by the low cost of coal in South America; by 1914, with the opening of the [Panama Canal](24849.md), the trade effectively ended.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStrange198772–73-66] In 1881, the Falkland Islands became financially independent of Britain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReginaldElliot19839-60] For more than a century, the Falkland Islands Company dominated the trade and employment of the archipelago; in addition, it owned most housing in Stanley, which greatly benefited from the wool trade with the UK.[^cite_note-Bernhardson_2011_loc=Stanley_and_Vicinity:_History-65]

Two battling ships, with one sinking

Depiction of the sinking of the *Scharnhorst* during the 1914 [Battle of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Falkland_Islands) (painting by [William Lionel Wyllie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lionel_Wyllie))

In the first half of the 20th century, the Falklands served an important role in Britain's territorial claims to [subantarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subantarctic) islands and a section of Antarctica. The Falklands governed these territories as the [Falkland Islands Dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_Dependencies) starting in 1908 and retained them until their dissolution in 1985.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDay2013129–30-67] The Falklands also played a minor role in the two world wars as a military base aiding control of the South Atlantic. In the [Battle of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Falkland_Islands) in December 1914 during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), a Royal Navy fleet defeated an [Imperial German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) squadron. In the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), following the December 1939 [Battle of the River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_River_Plate), the battle-damaged [HMS *Exeter*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Exeter_\(68\)) steamed to the Falklands for repairs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarafano2005367-23] In 1942, a battalion en route to India was redeployed to the Falklands as a garrison amid fears of a Japanese seizure of the archipelago.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaddelseyCarroll2014Prologue-68] After the war ended, the Falklands economy was affected by declining wool prices and the political uncertainty resulting from the revived sovereignty dispute between the United Kingdom and Argentina.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBernhardson2011Stanley_and_Vicinity:_History-61]

Simmering tensions between the UK and Argentina increased during the second half of the century, when Argentine President [Juan Perón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Per%C3%B3n) asserted sovereignty over the archipelago.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZepeda2005102-69] The sovereignty dispute intensified during the 1960s, shortly after the United Nations passed a resolution on [decolonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation) which Argentina interpreted as favourable to its position.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELaver2001125-70] In 1965, the UN General Assembly passed [Resolution 2065](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_2065), calling for both states to conduct bilateral negotiations to reach a peaceful settlement of the dispute.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELaver2001125-70] From 1966 until 1968, the UK confidentially discussed with Argentina the transfer of the Falklands, assuming its judgement would be accepted by the islanders.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas199124-71] An agreement on trade ties between the archipelago and the mainland was reached in 1971 and, consequently, Argentina built a temporary airfield at Stanley in 1972.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReginaldElliot19839-60] Nonetheless, Falklander dissent, as expressed by their strong lobby in the [UK Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Parliament), and tensions between the UK and [Argentina](18951905.md) effectively limited sovereignty negotiations until 1977.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas199124–27-72]

Concerned at the expense of maintaining the Falkland Islands in an era of budget cuts, the UK again considered transferring sovereignty to Argentina in the early [Thatcher government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_Margaret_Thatcher).[^cite_note-Guardian-73] Substantive sovereignty talks again ended by 1981, and the dispute escalated with passing time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas199128–31-74] In April 1982 the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War) began when Argentine military forces [invaded the Falklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_invasion_of_the_Falkland_Islands) and other [British territories in the South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Georgia_and_the_South_Sandwich_Islands), briefly [occupying them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Falkland_Islands) until a UK [expeditionary force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_warfare) retook the territories in June.[^cite_note-75] After the war the UK expanded its military presence, building [RAF Mount Pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Mount_Pleasant) and increasing the size of its garrison.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibran1998130–35-76] The war also left some 117 minefields containing nearly 20,000 mines of various types, including anti-vehicle and anti-personnel mines.[^cite_note-BBC_Mines-77] Due to the large number of [deminer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deminer) casualties, initial attempts to clear the mines ceased in 1983.[^cite_note-BBC_Mines-77][^cite_note-79] Landmine clearance work restarted in 2009, in accordance with the UK's obligations under the [Ottawa Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Treaty), and [Sapper Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapper_Hill) Corral was cleared of mines in 2012, allowing access to an important historical landmark for the first time in 30 years.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Demining was completed in October 2020.[^cite_note-PenguinNews-82]

Based on [Lord Shackleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Shackleton)'s recommendations, the Falklands diversified from a sheep-based monoculture into an economy of tourism and, with the establishment of the Falklands [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone), fisheries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECawkell2001147-83][^cite_note-84] The road network was also made more extensive, and the construction of [RAF Mount Pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Mount_Pleasant) allowed access to [long haul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_haul) flights.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECawkell2001147-83] [Oil exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_exploration) also began in the 2010s, with indications of possible commercially exploitable deposits in the Falklands basin.[^cite_note-85] Argentina and the UK re-established diplomatic relations in 1990, but neither has agreed on the terms of future sovereignty discussions.[^cite_note-86]

## Government

Main article: [Politics of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Falkland_Islands)

Large, rambling house with greenhouse and white fence

[Government House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_House,_Falkland_Islands) in [Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley,_Falkland_Islands) is the Governor's [official residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_residence).

The Falkland Islands are a self-governing [British Overseas Territory](207114.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECahill2010"Falkland_Islands"-87] Under the [2009 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_Constitution), the islands have full internal self-government; the UK is responsible for foreign affairs, retaining the power "to protect UK interests and to ensure the overall good governance of the territory".[^cite_note-Constitution-88] The [Monarch of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch_of_the_United_Kingdom) is the head of state, and executive authority is exercised on the monarch's behalf by the [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_the_Falkland_Islands), who appoints the islands' [chief executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Executive_of_the_Falkland_Islands) on the advice of members of the [Legislative Assembly](4504398.md).[^cite_note-constitutiontext-89] Both the governor and the chief executive serve as the [head of government](37570.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckman2012394-90]

Governor [Colin Martin-Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Martin-Reynolds) was appointed in July 2025[^cite_note-91] and Chief Executive [Andrea Clausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Clausen_\(politician\)) took up the post on 1 April 2025. Dr. Clausen is the first woman and the first Falkland Islander to become Chief Executive of the Government. The UK minister responsible for the Falkland Islands since 2024, [Stephen Doughty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Doughty), administers British foreign policy regarding the islands.[^cite_note-92]

The governor acts on the advice of the islands' [Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_the_Falkland_Islands), composed of the chief executive, the [Director of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_Finance_of_the_Falkland_Islands) and three elected members of the Legislative Assembly (with the governor as chairman).[^cite_note-constitutiontext-89] The Legislative Assembly, a [unicameral legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameral_legislature), consists of the chief executive, the director of finance and eight members (five from Stanley and three from [Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_\(Falkland_Islands\))) elected to four-year terms by [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage).[^cite_note-constitutiontext-89] All politicians in the Falkland Islands are [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_\(politician\)); no political parties exist on the islands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECentral_Intelligence_Agency2011"Falkland_Islands_(Malvinas) –_Government"-93] Since the [2013 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Falkland_Islands_general_election), members of the Legislative Assembly have received a salary and are expected to work full-time and give up all previously held jobs or business interests.[^cite_note-94]

As a territory of the United Kingdom, the Falklands were part of the [overseas countries and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_member_state_territories_and_the_European_Union) of the European Union until [2020](41688778.md).[^cite_note-95] The islands' judicial system, overseen by the [Foreign and Commonwealth Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Office), is largely based on [English law](85441.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESainato2010157–158-96] and the constitution binds the territory to the principles of the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights).[^cite_note-Constitution-88] Residents have the right of appeal to the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) and the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom).[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] Law enforcement is the responsibility of the [Royal Falkland Islands Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Falkland_Islands_Police) (RFIP).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESainato2010157–158-96]

### Defence

Main article: [Military of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Falkland_Islands)

Defence of the islands is provided by the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECentral_Intelligence_Agency2011"Falkland_Islands_(Malvinas) –_Transportation"-99] A British military garrison is stationed on the islands, and the Falkland Islands government funds the [Falkland Islands Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_Defence_Force), an additional [platoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platoon_\(military_unit\)) to [company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_\(military_unit\))-sized [light infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_infantry).[^cite_note-timesfidf-100] The Falklands claim an [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) (EEZ) extending 200 nmi (370 km) from its coastal baselines, based on the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](31940.md); this zone overlaps with the EEZ of Argentina.[^cite_note-101]

### Sovereignty dispute

Main article: [Falkland Islands sovereignty dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_sovereignty_dispute)

The UK and Argentina both assert sovereignty over the Falkland Islands. The UK bases its position on its continuous administration of the islands since 1833 and the islanders' "right to [self-determination](29269.md) as set out in the [UN Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Charter)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELansford20121528-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] Argentina claims that, when it [achieved independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Declaration_of_Independence) in 1816, it acquired the Falklands from Spain.[^cite_note-RIS-105][^cite_note-auto1-106][^cite_note-auto-107] The [incident of 1833](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reassertion_of_British_sovereignty_over_the_Falkland_Islands_\(1833\)) is particularly contentious; Argentina considers it proof of "Britain's usurpation" whereas the UK discounts it as a mere reassertion of its claim.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGustafson198826–27-108][^cite_note-111]

In 2009, the British prime minister, [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown), had a meeting with the Argentine president, [Cristina Fernández de Kirchner](1644780.md), and said that there would be no further talks over the sovereignty of the Falklands.[^cite_note-112] In March 2013, the Falkland Islands held a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Falkland_Islands_sovereignty_referendum) on its political status: 99.8% of votes cast favoured remaining a British overseas territory.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] Argentina does not recognise the Falkland Islanders as a partner in negotiations.[^cite_note-RIS-105][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Falkland_Islands)

Topographic image

Map of the Falkland Islands

The Falkland Islands have a land area of 4,700 sq mi (12,000 km<sup>2</sup>) and a coastline estimated at 800 mi (1,300 km).[^cite_note-117] The archipelago consists of two main islands, West Falkland and East Falkland, and 776 smaller islands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESainato2010157-118] The islands are predominantly mountainous and hilly,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECentral_Intelligence_Agency2011"Falkland_Islands_(Malvinas) –_Geography"-119] with the major exception being the depressed plains of [Lafonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafonia) (a peninsula forming the southern part of East Falkland).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETrewby200279-120] The Falklands consist of [continental crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_crust) fragments resulting from the break-up of [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana) and the opening of the South Atlantic that began 130 million years ago. The islands are located in the [South Atlantic Ocean](698.md), on the [Patagonian Shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagonian_Shelf), about 300 mi (480 km) east of Patagonia in southern Argentina.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlügel200966-121]

The Falklands' approximate location is latitude 51°40′– 53°00′ S and longitude 57°40′– 62°00′ W.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuo2007112-122] The archipelago's two main islands are separated by the [Falkland Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Sound),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHemmerle2005318-123] and its deep coastal indentations form [natural harbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_harbour).[^cite_note-124] East Falkland houses Stanley (the capital and largest settlement),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuo2007112-122] the UK military base at RAF Mount Pleasant, and the archipelago's highest point: [Mount Usborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Usborne), at 2,313 ft (705 m).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHemmerle2005318-123] Outside of these significant settlements is the area colloquially known as "Camp", which is derived from the Spanish term for countryside (*Campo*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHince2001"Camp"-125]

The [climate of the islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_the_Falkland_Islands) is cold, windy, and humid [maritime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlügel200966-121] Variability of daily weather is typical throughout the archipelago.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibran199816-126] Rainfall is common over half of the year, averaging 610 mm (24 in) in Stanley, and sporadic light snowfall occurs nearly all year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECentral_Intelligence_Agency2011"Falkland_Islands_(Malvinas) –_Geography"-119] The temperature has historically stayed between 21.1 and −11.1 °C (70.0 and 12.0 °F) in Stanley, with mean monthly temperatures varying from 9 °C (48 °F) in January and February (summer) to −1 °C (30 °F) in July (winter).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibran199816-126] Strong [westerly winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerlies) and cloudy skies are common.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECentral_Intelligence_Agency2011"Falkland_Islands_(Malvinas) –_Geography"-119] Although numerous storms are recorded each month, conditions are normally calm.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibran199816-126]

## Biodiversity

Main article: [Wildlife of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_the_Falkland_Islands)

Large group of short, squat penguins on barren shore

Colony of [southern rockhopper penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_rockhopper_penguin) on [Saunders Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunders_Island,_Falkland_Islands)

The Falkland Islands are [biogeographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeographically) part of the [Antarctic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_ecozone),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJónsdóttir200784–86-127] with strong connections to the flora and fauna of [Patagonia](65433.md) in mainland South America.[^cite_note-epk-128] Land birds make up most of the Falklands' [avifauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avifauna). The only [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) bird species on the Falkland Islands are the flightless [Falkland steamer duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_steamer_duck) and [Cobb's wren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobb's_wren).[^cite_note-Cobb-129][^cite_note-epk-128][^cite_note-Steamer-130] Sixty-three species breed on the islands, including 14 endemic [subspecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspecies).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClarkDingwall1985131-131]

Abundant [arthropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod) diversity occurs on the islands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClarkDingwall1985132-132] The Falklands' flora consists of 163 native [vascular plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant) species.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClarkDingwall1985129-133] More than 400 species of [lichens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen) and [lichen-dwelling fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichenicolous_fungus) have been recorded.[^cite_note-Fryday_et_al._2019-134] The islands' only native terrestrial mammal, the [warrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrah), was hunted to extinction by European settlers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHince2001370-135]

The islands are frequented by [marine mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_mammal), such as the [southern elephant seal](20598100.md) and the [South American fur seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_fur_seal), and various types of [cetaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetacea); offshore islands house the rare [striated caracara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striated_caracara). There are also five different penguin species and a few of the largest [albatross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albatross) colonies on the planet.[^cite_note-Science_and_Diplomacy.-136] Endemic fish around the islands are primarily from the genus *[Galaxias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxias)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEClarkDingwall1985132-132] The Falklands are treeless and have a wind-resistant vegetation predominantly composed of a variety of [dwarf shrubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subshrub).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJónsdóttir200785-137]

Virtually the entire land area of the islands is used as pasture for sheep.[^cite_note-CIA-138] Introduced species include [reindeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer), hares, rabbits, [Patagonian foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_gray_fox), [brown rats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_rat), and cats.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBell2007544-139] Several of these species have harmed native flora and fauna, so the government has tried to contain, remove or exterminate foxes, rabbits and rats. Endemic land animals have been the most affected by introduced species, and several bird species have been extirpated from the larger islands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBell2007542–545-140] The extent of [human impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_impact_on_the_environment) on the Falklands is unclear, since there is little long-term data on habitat change.[^cite_note-epk-128]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Falkland_Islands)

See also: [Falkland Islands oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_oil) and [Transport in the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_the_Falkland_Islands)

Aerial photograph of small seaside city

[Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley,_Falkland_Islands), now officially a city, is the financial centre of the Falkland Islands' economy.

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoyle2001171-141]

As of 2023, the economy of the Falkland Islands is ranked the 221st largest out of 229 in the world by GDP ([PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity)),[^cite_note-142] but ranks 10th worldwide by [GDP (PPP) per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_\(PPP\)_per_capita).[^cite_note-143] The unemployment rate was 1% in 2016, and inflation was calculated at 1.4% in 2014.[^cite_note-CIA-138] Based on 2010 data, the islands have a high [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) of 0.874[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvakov201347-7] and a moderate [Gini coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gini_coefficient) for [income inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality) of 34.17.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvakov201354-144] The local currency is the [Falkland Islands pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_pound), which is [pegged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_exchange-rate_system) to the British [pound sterling](270673.md).[^cite_note-145]

Economic development was advanced by [ship resupplying](213654.md) and sheep farming for high-quality wool.[^cite_note-146] The main sheep breeds in the Falkland Islands are [Polwarth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polwarth_\(sheep\)) and [Corriedale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corriedale).[^cite_note-147] During the 1980s, although ranch under-investment and the use of [synthetic fibres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fibre) damaged the sheep-farming sector, the government secured a major revenue stream by the establishment of an [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) and the sale of fishing licences to "anybody wishing to fish within this zone".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoyle2001170-148] Since the end of the Falklands War in 1982, the islands' economic activity increasingly focused on oil field [exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon_exploration) and tourism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHemmerle2005319-149] All large settlements are now connected by road and, since 2008, a ferry links West and East Falkland.[^cite_note-britannica.com-150] The islands' major exports include wool, hides, venison, fish and squid; its main imports include fuel, [building materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_material) and clothing.[^cite_note-CIA-138]

The port settlement of Stanley has regained the islands' economic focus, with an increase in population as workers migrate from Camp.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoyle2001170–171-151] Fear of dependence on fishing licences and threats from [overfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfishing), [illegal fishing](6346579.md) and fish [market price fluctuations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_trend) led to increased interest in oil drilling as an alternative source of revenue. As of 2001, exploration efforts had yet to find "exploitable reserves".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoyle2001171-141] By 2023, oil exploration was still proceeding off the shelf of the islands with a deepwater project led by [Rockhopper Exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockhopper_Exploration).[^cite_note-152] Development projects in education and sports have been funded by the Falklands government, without aid from the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoyle2001170-148]

In December 2025, it was indicated that, after prolonged consideration, Rockhopper Exploration and Navitas Petroleum had taken the final investment decisions for the development of Phase 1 of the [Sea Lion Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Lion_Field) and sanctioned the project. Phase 1, aims to produce 170 million barrels (27 million m<sup>3</sup>) at a peak production of approximately 50,000 barrels per day (8 million litres per day). The first oil from Phase 1 was planned for 2028.[^cite_note-153] Argentina immediately rejected these plans with the Argentine foreign ministry stating that Argentina would: “deepen its action plan to adopt all additional measures, in accordance with international law, that it deems necessary to safeguard its sovereign rights and interests.”[^cite_note-154]

The [primary sector of the economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector_of_the_economy) accounts for most of the Falkland Islands' gross domestic product, with the fishing industry alone contributing between 50% and 60% of annual GDP; agriculture also contributes significantly to GDP and employs about a tenth of the population.[^cite_note-155] A little over a quarter of the workforce serves the Falkland Islands government, making it the archipelago's largest employer.[^cite_note-FI_Guardian-156] Tourism, part of the service economy, has been spurred by increased interest in [Antarctic exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Antarctica) and the creation of direct air links with the United Kingdom and South America.[^cite_note-157] Tourists, mostly [cruise ship](314855.md) passengers, are attracted by the archipelago's wildlife and environment, as well as activities such as fishing and [wreck diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreck_diving); the majority find accommodation in Stanley.[^cite_note-158] The main international airport, located at [RAF Mount Pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Mount_Pleasant) on East Falkland, provides flights to [RAF Brize Norton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Brize_Norton) in the UK and mainland South America.[^cite_note-britannica.com-150] [Port Stanley Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Stanley_Airport) provides internal flights.[^cite_note-159] Despite [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Falkland_Islands) restrictions causing suspensions of flights from [Santiago](51572.md) and [São Paulo](390875.md) and prohibiting cruise ship tourism, the economy of the islands remained stable and healthy.[^cite_note-160]

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Falkland_Islands)

See also: [Falkland Islanders](13820413.md) and [Religion in the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Falkland_Islands)

Photograph of a building

[Christ Church Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church_Cathedral_\(Falkland_Islands\)), the local parish church of the [Anglican Communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Communion). Most Falklanders identify as Christian.

The Falkland Islands population is homogeneous, mostly descended from Scottish and Welsh immigrants who settled in the territory after 1833,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELaver20019-161] and also from English and [French people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), [Gibraltarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltarians), Scandinavians, and South Americans. The 2016 census indicated that 43% of residents were born in the archipelago, with foreign-born residents assimilated into local culture. The legal term for the right of residence is "belonging to the islands".[^cite_note-2006Census-162][^cite_note-Census_2016-163] In 1983, full [British citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_citizenship) was given to Falkland Islanders under the [British Nationality (Falkland Islands) Act 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Nationality_\(Falkland_Islands\)_Act_1983).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELaver20019-161]

A significant population decline affected the archipelago in the 20th century, with many young islanders moving overseas in search of education, a modern lifestyle, and better job opportunities,[^cite_note-164] particularly to the British city of [Southampton](7920751.md), which came to be known in the islands as "Stanley North".[^cite_note-165] In recent years, the islands' population decline has reduced, thanks to immigrants from the United Kingdom, [Saint Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Helena), and Chile.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinahan2013139-166] In the 2012 census, a majority of residents listed their nationality as [Falkland Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islander) (59 per cent), followed by [British](19097669.md) (29 per cent), Saint Helenian (9.8 per cent), and [Chilean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chileans_in_the_Falkland_Islands) (5.4 per cent).[^cite_note-census2012-167] A small number of [Argentines](28820502.md) also live on the islands.[^cite_note-mercopress-168]

The Falkland Islands [have a very low population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_American_countries_by_population).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoyle2006181-169] According to the 2012 census, the average daily population of the Falklands was 2,932, excluding military personnel serving in the archipelago and their dependents.[^cite_note-170] A 2012 report counted 1,300 uniformed personnel and 50 [British Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Ministry_of_Defence) civil servants present in the Falklands.[^cite_note-FI_Guardian-156] Stanley (with 2,121 residents) is the most populous location on the archipelago, followed by [Mount Pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Mount_Pleasant) (369 residents, primarily air-base contractors) and Camp (351 residents).[^cite_note-census2012-167] The islands' age distribution is skewed towards working age (20–60). Males outnumber females (53 to 47 per cent), and this discrepancy is most prominent in the 20–60 age group.[^cite_note-2006Census-162]

In the 2012 census, most islanders identified themselves as Christian (66 per cent), followed by those with no religious affiliation (32 per cent). The remaining 2 per cent identified as adherents of other religions, including the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith),[^cite_note-Adherents2001-171] [Buddhism](3267529.md),[^cite_note-172] and [Islam](6037917.md).[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-census2012-167] The main Christian denominations are [Anglicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) and other [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), and [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism).[^cite_note-174]

## Education

Further information: [Education in the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Falkland_Islands)

Education in the Falkland Islands follows [England's system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_England) and is free and compulsory for residents aged between 5 and 16 years.[^cite_note-EDU-175] Primary education is available at Stanley, RAF Mount Pleasant (for children of service personnel) and a number of rural settlements. Secondary education is only available in Stanley, which offers boarding facilities and 12 subjects to [General Certificate of Secondary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Certificate_of_Secondary_Education) (GCSE) level. Students aged 16 or older may study at colleges in England for their [GCE Advanced Level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCE_Advanced_Level) or vocational qualifications. The Falkland Islands government pays for older students to attend institutions of higher education, usually in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-EDU-175]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Falkland_Islands)

Falklands culture is based on the [cultural traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_Kingdom) of its British settlers but has also been influenced by [Hispanic South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinahan2013139-166] Falklanders still use some terms and place names from the former Gaucho inhabitants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWagstaff200121-176] The Falklands' predominant and official language is English, with the foremost dialect being [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English); nonetheless, some inhabitants also speak Spanish.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinahan2013139-166] According to naturalist [Will Wagstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Wagstaff), "the Falkland Islands are a very social place, and stopping for a chat is a way of life".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWagstaff200121-176]

The islands have one weekly newspaper, *[The Penguin News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Penguin_News)*,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWagstaff200166-177] and television and radio broadcasts generally feature programming from the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinahan2013139-166] Wagstaff describes the local cuisine as "very British in character with much use made of the home-grown vegetables, local lamb, mutton, beef, and fish". Common between meals are "homemade cakes and biscuits with tea or coffee".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWagstaff200163–64-178] Social activities are, according to Wagstaff, "typical of that of a small British town with a variety of clubs and organisations covering many aspects of community life".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWagstaff200165-179]

### Sport

Main article: [Sport in the Falkland Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_the_Falkland_Islands)

Despite its small size, the Falkland Islands competes in the [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games) and [Island Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Games).[^cite_note-insidethegames.biz_2021_g927-180] The [Falkland Islands national cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_national_cricket_team) is a member of the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council).[^cite_note-ICC_2024-181]
