**Juba International Airport** ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **JUB**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **HJJJ**) is a multi-use international airport serving [Juba](1514879.md), the capital city of [South Sudan](32350676.md). The airport is located 5 km (3.1 mi) northeast of the city's [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district), on the western banks of the [White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile).[^cite_note-3] The city and airport are located in South Sudan's [Central Equatoria State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Equatoria).

It is one of the two international airports in South Sudan, the other being [Malakal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malakal_Airport). Juba Airport handles international and local airlines, cargo air traffic and chartered commercial flights. It is also used by the South Sudanese military and by the United Nations agencies such as [UNMISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNMISS), [UNHAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHAS) and the [World Food Programme](33582.md), as well as the [ICRC](5023832.md) and many NGOs for relief flights for the country.

## History

The first airfield in Juba was cleared in 1929.[^cite_note-jubainthemaking-4] The Shell Company constructed the first runway in 1931. It was surfaced with crushed [laterite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laterite). In February 1931, Imperial Airways opened the first 2,670 miles of the weekly Croydon to [Tanganyika Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_\(territory\)) (now part of Tanzania) portion of the Cape to Cairo air-route, and established a mooring place near [Rejaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejaf) to the south of Juba, for Imperial Airways' Calcutta flying-boats, which carried passengers between [Khartoum](17320.md) and [Kisumu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisumu).[^cite_note-jubainthemaking-4] Labourers had been settling on the land that has since become the Juba airport's present location and, in 1934, when the Juba aerodrome was expanded and cleared, these residents were relocated.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] By 1976, the runway had been expanded to 2,400 by 45 meters and paved with asphalt. Access roads leading to the terminal buildings were unsurfaced and "almost impassable during the rainy season."[^cite_note-colquhoun-7] The airport's "very old radio beacon" was located a short distance from the airport, which was also equipped with a weak VHF radio for pilot-to-ground communication. Field lighting was not present.[^cite_note-colquhoun-7]

In early February 1977, the airport was the site of an [unsuccessful coup attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Sudan_Juba_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt), when [ex-Anyanya forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anyanya) attempted to take the airport.[^cite_note-8]

### Airport expansion and country's independence



Juba Airport Terminal under construction.

As of May 2011, Juba International Airport was undergoing improvements and expansion. The work on the airport included expansion of the passenger and cargo terminal buildings, resurfacing of the runway and installation of runway lights to facilitate night operations.[^cite_note-9]

As of July 2011, the day of the country's independence, Juba International Airport had a new runway light system commissioned with simple approach lights for Runway 13/31, runway edge lights, taxiway lights for Exit Delta, Apron edge lights, illuminated windsocks, ATC tower rotating beacon as well as [PAPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_approach_path_indicator) for both approaches.[^cite_note-10]

In 2016, Juba International Airport was ranked the second worst airport in the world in a survey conducted by The Guide to Sleeping in Airports. It was the worst airport in 2017[^cite_note-11] and the fourth worst in 2019.[^cite_note-12] During this time the terminal consisted of two adjacent tents to facilitate customs and immigration, arrivals and departures. A new, smaller terminal was built on the site of the original terminal by the Chinese. The new terminal was inaugurated on 29 October 2018.[^cite_note-13]

## Facilities

The airport resides at an elevation of 461 metres (1,513 ft) above [mean sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_sea_level). It has one [runway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway) designated 13/31 with an [asphalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asphalt_concrete) surface measuring 3,100 m (10,171 ft) long and 45 m (148 ft) wide.[^cite_note-WAD-1]

## Accidents and incidents

- On 12 August 1990, [Lockheed L-100-30 Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-100_Hercules) N911SJ of [Southern Air Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Air_Transport) took off on a flight to Nairobi. Early in climbout, number 4 engine started to lose power, then engines 1 and 2 developed overspeed and pitch lock issues. On the way back to Juba, engine 3 lost power. The aircraft successfully landed, but overran the runway, colliding with containers and was a write off. The crew of five survived.

- On 19 December 2013, a [Nova Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Airways) [Boeing](18933266.md) 737-500 registration ST-NVG suffered nose-gear collapse resulting in major damage when landing at Juba. This was the same day that many people were being evacuated from Juba because of the [South Sudanese Civil War](41382446.md). The Nova Airways aircraft blocked the runway for several hours, delaying the evacuation. The aircraft was repaired and returned to service.

- On 10 December 2019, Ethiopian Airlines Flight 357, a [Bombardier Dash 8 Q400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada_Dash_8) registration ET-AQC excursed from the runway during takeoff. The aircraft was substantially damaged. All 21 people on board survived.

- On 27 April 2026, a [Cessna 208 Caravan](364573.md) operated by CityLink Aviation crashed south-west of [Juba](1514879.md) killing all 15 occupants.
