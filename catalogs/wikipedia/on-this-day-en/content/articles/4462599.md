The **Tarlac River** is a river in [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon), [Philippines](23440.md), it is the longest tributary of the [Agno River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River) with a total length of 95.2 km (59.2 mi) covering a [drainage area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) of 1,900 square kilometres (730 sq mi) traversing the provinces of [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac) and [Pangasinan](1691800.md).[^cite_note-pagasa-1] The river originates around the vicinity of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo) and empties itself into the [Agno River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno_River) at [Poponto Swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poponto_Swamp) located between the boundaries of Tarlac and Pangasinan. It was formerly the site of traditional balsa or bamboo [raft riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_rapids_ride), until the river was heavily [silted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siltation) by sticky [lahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar) or mud flow brought by [the eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_eruption_of_Mount_Pinatubo) of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo) on June 15, 1991, filling the river with over 10 feet (3.0 m) of lahar. Its main [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) is the O'Donnel River in Santa Lucia, [Capas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capas), Tarlac.

The Tarlac River is dammed at Barangay Tibag in [Tarlac City](2144455.md) where its water is used for [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) and distributed to the northern and central regions of Tarlac, the rest of the river is now a bed of sand. The river was once a good source of fish and water used for irrigation, the irrigation system now takes just about "all" of the remaining water and distributes it to [barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) in Tarlac City such as Matatalaib and Maliwalo. The [river bed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_bed) is also being used as a source of sand and stones that can be used as construction materials.

## Ninoy Aquino and Agana bridges

On August 28, 2026, a portion of the Ninoy Aquino bridge collapsed at nighttime due to the effects of rains and wind brought by the [Habagat](57630.md). Due to the bridge already showing signs of instability hours prior to collapse, no motorists were traversing the bridge at the time of its collapse.[^cite_note-2]

A day later on August 29, 2026, a portion of the Agana Bridge also collapsed in the evening while a Toyota Avanza with 5 occupants was traversing it. Prior to the collapse, a truck ban for the bridge was ordered by the DPWH as a measure to prevent further instability. As of August 29, 4 people were still missing while 1 person had been rescued from the Tarlac River, with search and rescue operations still underway.[^cite_note-3]

In response to the collapse of the Ninoy Aquino Bridge, [DPWH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPWH) Secretary [Vince Dizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Dizon) alongside local officials visited the site and discovered the formation of a sinkhole near the bridge. Light vehicles were diverted to the nearby Agana bridge before its collapse on the same day, while heavy vehicles and trucks were diverted to [Gerona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerona,_Tarlac) following safety risks posed by rains. Dizon ordered the construction of a temporary steel structure to reconnect both sides of the Ninoy Aquino bridge.[^cite_note-4]
