# Replenishment oiler

"Oiler (ship)" redirects here. For ships used principally for commercial oil transport, see [Oil tanker](15923603.md).



[INS *Deepak*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Deepak) (left) conducting replenishment at sea with [aircraft carrier](2219.md) [INS *Vikrant*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Vikrant_\(2013\)).



Fleet replenishment oiler [USNS *Patuxent*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USNS_Patuxent)

A **replenishment oiler** or **replenishment tanker** is a naval [auxiliary ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_ship) with [fuel tanks](1071636.md) and dry [cargo holds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_hold) which can supply both fuel and dry stores during [underway replenishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underway_replenishment) (UNREP) at sea. Many countries have used replenishment oilers.

The [United States Navy's hull classification symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Navy_hull_classification_symbol) for this type of ship was 'AOR' (Auxiliary Oil Replenishment). Replenishment oilers are slower and carry fewer dry stores than the US Navy's modern [fast combat support ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_combat_support_ship), which carry the classification 'AOE'. In 2020 the US Navy began to develop a new type of ship, the 'AOL' or [light replenishment oiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_oilers#Light_Replenishment_Oilers_\(T-AOL\)); construction of the first is planned for 2026.[^cite_note-USNI_20230428-1]

## History



[RFA *Tiderace*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFA_Tiderace_\(A137\)) performing a dual repenishment of [Royal Navy](26061.md) frigates [HMS *Lancaster*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Lancaster_\(F229\)) and [HMS *Westminster*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Westminster_\(F237\)).

&#x20;The development of the "oiler" paralleled the change from coal- to oil-fired boilers in warships. Prior to the adoption of oil fired machinery, navies could extend the range of their ships either by maintaining [coaling stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaling_station) or for warships to raft together with [colliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collier_\(ship\)) and for coal to be manhandled aboard. Though arguments related to fuel security were made against such a change, the ease with which liquid fuel could be transferred led in part to its adoption by navies worldwide.



The replenishment oiler [HMAS *Sirius*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAS_Sirius) (right) providing fuel to the amphibious warfare ship [USS *Juneau*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Juneau_\(LPD-10\)) while both are underway

One of the first generation of "blue-water" navy oiler support vessels was the British RFA *Kharki*, active 1911 in the run-up to the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War). Such vessels heralded the transition from coal to oil as the fuel of [warships](263960.md) and removed the need to rely on, and operate within range of coaling stations. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the United States Navy's dramatically enlarged fleets, especially those in the [Pacific Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean_theater_of_World_War_II), required massive quantities of black oil, [diesel oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_oil), [avgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avgas), and other fuels and lubricants to support American land, sea, and air operations against remote, widely dispersed Japanese forces. Those supply demands resulted in U.S. Navy personnel refining many established practices for oilers and creating new procedures for replenishing warships while [underway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underway_replenishment) and for transporting highly combustible materials with increased effectiveness through hostile waters and over vast ocean distances.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Characteristics



Replenishment oiler [USS *Wabash*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wabash_\(AOR-5\)) at work

For all but the largest navies, replenishment oilers are typically one of the largest ships in the fleet. Such ships are designed to carry large amounts of fuel and dry stores for the support of naval operations far away from port. Replenishment oilers are also equipped with more extensive medical and dental facilities than smaller ships can provide.

Such ships are equipped with multiple refueling gantries to refuel and resupply multiple ships at a time. The process of refueling and supplying ships at sea is called [underway replenishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underway_replenishment). Furthermore, such ships often are designed with helicopter decks and hangars. This allows the operation of rotary-wing aircraft, which allows the resupply of ships by helicopter. This process is called [vertical replenishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_replenishment). These ships, when operating in concert with surface groups, can act as an aviation maintenance platform where helicopters receive more extensive maintenance than can be provided by the smaller hangars of the escorting ships.

Their size, additional facilities, and ability to support the operation of other vessels, means that replenishment oilers have been used as [command ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_ship), with some ships, such as the French [*Durance* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durance-class_tanker), this capability being built into the vessels from the start.

## Armament



[PNS Nasr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Nasr) armed with [Phalanx CIWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanx_CIWS).

Because the replenishment oiler is not a combat unit, but rather a support vessel, such ships are often lightly armed, usually with self-defense systems (such as the [Phalanx CIWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanx_CIWS) close-in weapons systems), small arms, machine guns and/or light automatic cannons. They may also carry [man-portable air-defense systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man-portable_air-defense_systems) for additional air defense capability.

## United States Navy oilers

Further information: [List of United States Navy oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_Navy_oilers)



[USNS *Big Horn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USNS_Big_Horn), an underway replenishment oiler.



US sailors aboard [USS *Makin Island*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Makin_Island_\(LHD-8\)) prepare to receive replenishment from [USNS *Yukon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USNS_Yukon_\(T-AO-202\))



Underway replenishment of [PNS Tippu Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNS_Tippu_Sultan_\(D-185\)) from [USNS Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USNS_Supply).

In the [United States Navy](20518076.md), an **Oiler** is a Combat Logistics ship that replenishes other ships with fuel and in some cases food, mail, ammunition and other necessities while at sea, in a process called [Underway Replenishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underway_replenishment) or UNREP.[^cite_note-5] Up through the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) Navy oilers used commercial tanker hulls, with the addition of UNREP gear, defensive guns, and military electronic and damage-control equipment; since the 1950s however they have been built from the keel up as specialized naval auxiliaries. They were previously classified as **Fleet Oilers**[^cite_note-6] in the 20th century; under the current [MSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Sealift_Command) operation their full classification is listed as **Fleet Replenishment Oilers**.[^cite_note-ffrep-7] Since the 1960s the classification **Transport Oiler** (AOT) has applied to tankers which ship petroleum products to depots around the world, but do not engage in UNREP.

The first fleet oilers[^cite_note-fffo-8] were identified by the hull designation AO, which is still in use.[^cite_note-ffrep-7] Large, fast multifunction oilers which also provide ammunition and dry stores are identified as [Fast Combat Support Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Combat_Support_Ship) (AOE),[^cite_note-ffcss-9] and mid-size ones Replenishment Oilers (AOR). The AOR designation is no longer in use. All of these oilers provide the combined services of the AO, AE, AFS and AK.

The style "**USNS**" and prefix "**T**" identify a ship as being operated by a civilian crew under the [Military Sealift Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Sealift_Command) (known as the [Military Sea Transportation Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Sea_Transportation_Service) until 1970).

### Current classes

There are three classes of vessels currently in commissioned service:

- [*Henry J. Kaiser* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_J._Kaiser-class_replenishment_oiler), T-AO-187-T-AO-204, in service 1986

- [*Supply* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply-class_fast_combat_support_ship), T-AOE, in service 1990

- [*John Lewis* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lewis-class_replenishment_oiler), T-AO-205-T-AO-221, in service 2022
