**Pau Cubarsí Paredes** (born 22 January 2007) is a Spanish professional [footballer](10568.md) who plays as a [centre-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-back) for [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) club [Barcelona](68187.md) and the [Spain national team](609825.md). He is considered one of the best young defenders in the world.

Formerly a [Girona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girona_FC) youth player, Cubarsí transitioned into being a [La Masia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Masia) talent, and he later made his senior debut for Barcelona on 18 January 2024, just days before his 17th birthday. He later won La Liga in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_FC_Barcelona_season) and [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_FC_Barcelona_season) with the club.

A former Spain youth international, Cubarsí represented his country at every youth level from [under-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-15_football_team) to [under-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-23_football_team) level. In March 2024, he made his senior debut for Spain in a friendly against [Colombia](680175.md) at the age of 17. Cubarsí went on to become a key member of the squad that won the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), playing every minute of Spain's campaign and being named the tournament's [Best Young Player](3482503.md#FIFA_Young_Player_Award).

## Early life and education

Pau Cubarsí Paredes[^cite_note-uefa-2] was born on 22 January 2007 in [Bescanó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bescan%C3%B3), Girona, Catalonia,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-uefa-2] to Robert Cubarsí Vilaró and Gloria Paredes Parra. He has an older sister, Irene, who is a physiotherapist.[^cite_note-4] He grew up in the small village of Estanyol in Bescanó,[^cite_note-5] where four generations of his father's family had operated the family [carpentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpentry) business, Fusteria Cubarsí, since 1905.[^cite_note-6] Cubarsí is a great-great-grand-nephew of the painter [Ginés Parra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gin%C3%A9s_Parra?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-7]

Cubarsí's first language is [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language). While playing for Spain at the [Olympic Games in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics), he said that he rarely gave interviews because he was not fluent in [Spanish](26825.md), having only ever lived in [Catalonia](6822.md) and used his native language.[^cite_note-8]

When attending high school, he trained in the morning and attended classes from 3 p.m. Teachers from Lleó XIII school[^cite_note-10] would go to [La Masia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Masia) (football academy in Barcelona) and give classes until eight in the evening. If he missed the class owing to games, he would ask for notes, or just pay extra attention the following day.[^cite_note-11] After graduation from high school, he sat university entrance exams and was due to begin a university degree in [business administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_administration) and management in September 2025. He planned to leave his residence at La Masia, where he had lived through his teenage years, and share an apartment with his sister in [Barcelona](4443.md).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

## Club career

### Early career

Cubarsí began his career with [Girona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girona_FC), before moving to [Barcelona](68187.md) in 2018.[^cite_note-md-14] Having progressed through the academy, he became the third-youngest Barcelona player to debut in the [UEFA Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Youth_League), behind [Lamine Yamal](71691936.md) and [Ilaix Moriba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilaix_Moriba), when he started in an eventual 1–1 draw with Czech side [Viktoria Plzeň](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Viktoria_Plze%C5%88).[^cite_note-md-14]

### Barcelona

In April 2023, Cubarsí trained with the first team for the first time, having been called up by manager [Xavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavi_\(footballer,_born_1980\)).[^cite_note-15] He signed his first professional contract with the club three months later, on 8 July.[^cite_note-16] He was included in Barcelona's pre-season squad ahead of the 2023–24 season.[^cite_note-17]

On 18 January 2024, Cubarsí made his first-team debut against [Unionistas de Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unionistas_de_Salamanca_CF) in the [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Copa_del_Rey), being subbed on in the 46th minute.[^cite_note-18] His first start came in the next match against [Real Betis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Betis) in La Liga, a day before his 17th birthday.[^cite_note-19] Two months later, on 12 March, he was named Player of the Match on his [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) debut in a 3–1 victory over [Napoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSC_Napoli) in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_phase) second leg, in which he became the youngest player to debut at 17 years and 50 days in the competition's knockout phase, breaking former [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) defender [David Alaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Alaba)'s previous record of 17 years and 258 days.[^cite_note-\:0-20][^cite_note-\:2-21] On 10 April, Cubarsí has become the youngest defender to start in the quarter-finals of the Champions League aged only 17 years and 79 days breaking the previous record held by [CSKA Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFC_CSKA_Moscow)'s [Georgi Shchennikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgi_Shchennikov).[^cite_note-22] On 9 May, [Barcelona](68187.md) announced that they had reached an agreement with Cubarsí for an extension until 30 June 2029, which has a 500 million euro buyout clause.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

On 25 February 2025, Cubarsí headed in his first goal for Barcelona, in a [Copa del Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Copa_del_Rey) semi-final against [Atlético Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid) which ended in a 4–4 draw.[^cite_note-25]

On 7 January 2026, Cubarsí started in Barcelona's 5–0 win over [Athletic Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao) in the [2026 Supercopa de España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a) semi‑final, earning praise for a composed display in defense on a big stage.[^cite_note-26] In the first leg of the [2025–26 UEFA Champions League](74127997.md) quarter-finals against Atlético Madrid on 8 April, Cubarsí was sent off after a [VAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee) review overturned his initial yellow card in the 43rd minute, with [Julián Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_%C3%81lvarez) converting the resulting free kick as Barcelona lost 2–0.[^cite_note-27] At the end of the season, [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) ranked Cubarsi fourth-best centre-back, in their annual list of Best Male Footballers in the World.[^cite_note-28]

## International career



Cubarsí playing for [Spain](609825.md) against [France](250197.md) in the [2026 FIFA World Cup semi-finals](81667005.md#France_vs_Spain)

Cubarsí has represented Spain from under-15 to senior level.[^cite_note-wf-29] In March 2024, he was called up to the senior [Spain](609825.md) team for the first time by coach [Luis de la Fuente](48181421.md), debuting in a 1–0 friendly loss to [Colombia](680175.md).[^cite_note-30] He came on as a substitute in the 83rd minute replacing [Aymeric Laporte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymeric_Laporte), he became the youngest defender to ever play for Spain at the age of 17 years one month and 28 days, breaking the record previously held by [Sergio Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Ramos) in 2005.[^cite_note-31]

On 27 May 2024, Cubarsí was named in the 29-man preliminary squad for the [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024).[^cite_note-32] However, on 7 June, he was excluded from the final squad prior to the tournament.[^cite_note-33] In July 2024, Cubarsí was selected in the [Spain under-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_national_under-23_football_team) squad for the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-34] He and Spain would go on to win the Olympic [gold medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament_final) at the men's football event in the Olympics.[^cite_note-35]



Cubarsí with [Spain](609825.md) against [Argentina](454699.md) at the [2026 World Cup final](75845879.md)

On 25 May 2026, he was included in Spain's 26-man squad for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-36] Following Spain's 1–0 extra-time victory over defending champions [Argentina](454699.md) in the [final](75845879.md), Cubarsí won the [Young Player of the Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Young_Player_Award) Award.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] He is best remembered for a providential 120th-minute clearance denying Argentina's [Marcos Senesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Senesi), which secured Spain's victory in the final.[^cite_note-40]

At 19 years and 178 days old, he became the fourth-youngest player to feature in a World Cup final, behind only [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9), [Giuseppe Bergomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Bergomi) and his teammate [Lamine Yamal](71691936.md).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] He completed the second-most passes throughout the tournament, after compatriot [Rodri](48849865.md) – 668 out of 690 attempted – with a 97% passing accuracy. Moreover, with 121 passes in the final, he became one of only three players, aside from compatriot Rodri (101) in the same match, and [Brazil's](149286.md) [Dunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunga) in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_final) (130), to complete over 100 passes in a World Cup final.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] During the 2026 World Cup, Cubarsí and Spain also set new defensive records for the most clean sheets (seven) and fewest goals conceded (one) in a single edition of the tournament by a World Cup champion.[^cite_note-45]

## Player profile

### Style of play

Cubarsí is regarded as one of the most promising [centre-backs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-back) of his generation, noted for his composure, anticipation, and ability in possession. A ball-playing defender, he has been praised for reading the game "like a veteran" despite his young age, while his progressive passing and positional awareness make him well suited to teams that play a high defensive line.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

### Reception

In July 2025, *[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated)* and [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) ranked Cubarsí as the ninth-best and sixth-best centre-back in world football, respectively.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Former Barcelona midfielder and coach [Óscar García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Garc%C3%ADa_\(footballer,_born_1973\)) said in July 2026 that he expects Cubarsí to become "one of the top five centre-backs in history", praising Cubarsí's one-on-one defending and ability to eliminate opponents with his passing.[^cite_note-50]
