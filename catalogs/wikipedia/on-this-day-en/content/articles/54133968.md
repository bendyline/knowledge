**Abdelmadjid Tebboune** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عَبد الْمَجِيْد تَبُّون, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *ʿAbd al-Majīd Tabbūn*; [Berber languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages): ⵄⴱⴷⵍⵎⴰⴵⵉⴷ ⵜⴱⴱⵓⵏ; born 17 November 1945) is an Algerian politician currently serving as [President](380664.md) and [Minister of National Defense of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Defense_\(Algeria\)) since 2019.[^cite_note-1] Since the death of [Liamine Zéroual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liamine_Z%C3%A9roual) in 2026, Tebboune is the only living Algerian president.[^cite_note-2]

Tebboune was elected in the [2019 Algerian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Algerian_presidential_election). Previously, he was [Prime Minister of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Algeria) from May 2017 to August 2017. In addition, he was also [minister of housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_minister)[^cite_note-3] from 2001 to 2002 for a year and again from 2012 to 2017 for five years.[^cite_note-JAfrique_PM_Bouteflika-4]

## Early life and education



Tebboune, at a young age

Abdelmadjid Tebboune was born on 17 November 1945 in [Mécheria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9cheria),[^cite_note-5] in the current [Naâma Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na%C3%A2ma_Province), in Algeria, back then [Aïn-Sefra territory](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territoires%20du%20Sud#Territoire_d'A%C3%AFn-Sefra). He comes from a family from the commune of [Boussemghoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boussemghoun), currently in [El Bayadh Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Bayadh_Province), located in the region of the [High Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hautes_Plaines) of southwest Algeria.[^cite_note-6] His father was a sheikh member of the [Association of Algerian Muslim Ulema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Algerian_Muslim_Ulema) (and Abdelmadjid studied in a school that was controlled by the Association of Ulema in Sidi Bel Abbes before the start of [Algerian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War) between 1953 and 1954)[^cite_note-7] and also a soldier.[^cite_note-8] He graduated from the [National School of Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_School_of_Administration_\(Algeria\)) on 29 July 1969.[^cite_note-9]

## Political career

Tebboune was Minister-Delegate for Local Government from 1991 to 1992, during the last months of [Chadli Benjedid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadli_Benjedid)'s presidency. Later, under President [Abdelaziz Bouteflika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelaziz_Bouteflika), he served in the government as Minister of Communication and Culture from 1999 to 2000 and then as Minister-Delegate for Local Government from 2000 to 2001. He was the Minister of Housing and Urban Planning from 2001 to 2002. Ten years later, in 2012, he returned to the post of Minister of Housing in the government of Prime Minister [Abdelmalek Sellal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelmalek_Sellal).&#x20;

Following the [May 2017 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Algerian_parliamentary_election), President Bouteflika appointed Tebboune to succeed Sellal as prime minister on 24 May 2017. The appointment of Tebboune surprised Algerian political elites, who had expected Sellal to be reappointed.[^cite_note-JAfrique_PM_Bouteflika-4] The new government headed by Tebboune was appointed on 25 May.[^cite_note-10]

Tebboune served as prime minister for less than three months. Bouteflika dismissed him and appointed [Ahmed Ouyahia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Ouyahia) to succeed him on 15 August 2017;[^cite_note-11] Ouyahia took office the next day.[^cite_note-12]

On 12 December 2019, Tebboune was elected president following the [2019 Algerian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Algerian_presidential_election), after taking 58% from a turnout of less than 40% the voters, against candidates from both main parties (the [National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberation_Front_\(Algeria\)) and the [Democratic National Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Rally)).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-El_Watan_2019-14] On 19 December, he assumed office and received the [National Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Order_of_Merit_\(Algeria\)) from the acting President [Abdelkader Bensalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelkader_Bensalah).[^cite_note-15] He was re-elected for a second term in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Algerian_presidential_election) with an overwhelming majority, securing 84.3% of the votes cast.[^cite_note-16]

## Presidency

See also: [List of international presidential trips made by Abdelmadjid Tebboune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_presidential_trips_made_by_Abdelmadjid_Tebboune)

On the eve of the first anniversary of the [Hirak Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Algerian_protests), President Abdelmadjid Tebboune announced in a statement to the [Algerian national media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Algeria) that 22 February would be declared the Algerian "National Day of Fraternity and Cohesion Between The People and Its Army For Democracy."[^cite_note-\:5-17] In the same statement, Tebboune spoke in favor of the Hirak Movement, saying that "the blessed Hirak has preserved the country from a total collapse" and that he had "made a personal commitment to carry out all of the \[movement's] demands."[^cite_note-\:5-17] On 21 and 22 February 2020, masses of demonstrators (with turnout comparable to well-established Algerian holidays like the [Algerian Day of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Algeria\))) gathered to honor the anniversary of the Hirak Movement and the newly established national day.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

In an effort to contain the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Tebboune announced on 17 March 2020 that "marches and rallies, whatever their motives" would be prohibited.[^cite_note-20] After protestors and journalists were arrested for participating in such marches, Tebboune faced accusations of attempting to "silence Algerians."[^cite_note-21] The government's actions were condemned by Amnesty International, which said in a statement that "when all eyes \[...] are on the [management of the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Algeria), the Algerian authorities are devoting time to speeding up the prosecution and trial of activists, journalists, and supporters of the Hirak movement."[^cite_note-22] CNLD estimated that around 70 prisoners of conscience were imprisoned by 2 July 2020 and that several of the imprisoned were arrested for [Facebook](7529378.md) posts.[^cite_note-23]



Tebboune meets with U.S. Secretary of State [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) on 30 March 2022.

On 28 December 2019, the then-recently inaugurated President Tebboune met with [Ahmed Benbitour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Benbitour), the former [Algerian Head of Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Algeria), with whom he discussed the "foundations of the new Republic."[^cite_note-24]

On 8 January 2020, Tebboune established a "commission of experts" composed of 17 members (a majority of which were professors of [constitutional law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_law)) responsible for examining the previous constitution and making any necessary revisions.[^cite_note-\:0-25] Led by [Ahmed Laraba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Laraba?action=edit\&redlink=1), the commission was required to submit its proposals to Tebboune directly within the following two months.[^cite_note-\:0-25][^cite_note-26] In a letter to Laraba on the same day, Tebboune outlined seven axes around which the commission should focus its discussion.[^cite_note-\:1-27] These areas of focus included strengthening citizens' rights, combatting [corruption](66220.md), consolidating the [balance of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) in the Algerian government, increasing the oversight powers of [parliament](3748164.md), promoting the independence of the judiciary, furthering citizens' equality under the law, and constitutionalizing [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Algeria).[^cite_note-\:1-27] Tebboune's letter also included a call for an "immutable and intangible" two-term limit to anyone serving as president — a major point of contention in the initial Hirak Movement protests, which were spurred by former president Abdelaziz Bouteflika's announced intention to run for a fifth term.[^cite_note-\:1-27]



Tebboune at the Libya Summit in [Berlin](3354.md) (January 19, 2020)

In January 2020, Tebboune also instructed Prime Minister Djerad to prepare a bill against all forms of [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism), regionalism and [hate speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_speech)[^cite_note-28] based on political convictions, religion, belief or race.[^cite_note-29]

The preliminary draft revision of the constitution was publicly published on 7 May 2020, but the Laraba Commission (as the "commission of experts" came to be known) was open to additional proposals from the public until 20 June.[^cite_note-\:3-30] By 3 June, the commission had received an estimated 1,200 additional public proposals.[^cite_note-\:3-30] After the Laraba Commission considered all revisions, the draft was introduced to the [Cabinet of Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Algeria) (Council of Ministers).[^cite_note-\:3-30]

On 4 July 2020, Tebboune announced that the referendum would occur in September or October 2020.[^cite_note-31]

On 24 August 2020, the date for the referendum was set for 1 November, the anniversary of the start of [Algeria's war of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War_of_Independence).[^cite_note-32]

The revised constitution was adopted in the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Algeria) on 6 September,[^cite_note-33] in the [People's National Assembly](2806769.md) on 10 September, and [Council of the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Nation) on 12 September, but its implementation was contingent on the results of the 1 November referendum.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

In October 2020, Tebboune tested positive for [COVID-19](63030231.md) and flew to [Germany](11867.md) for treatment. Meanwhile, Prime Minister [Abdelaziz Djerad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelaziz_Djerad) assumed his tasks.[^cite_note-36] On 29 December 2020, Tebboune resumed his duties.[^cite_note-37]

The constitutional changes were approved on [the 1 November 2020 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Algerian_constitutional_referendum), with 66.68% of voters participating in favour of the changes.

On 10 January 2021, Tebboune flew back to Germany for treatment of complications in his foot resulting from the COVID-19 infection.[^cite_note-38] On 12 February 2021, he returned to Algeria.[^cite_note-39]

On 16 February 2021 [mass protests and a wave of nationwide rallies and peaceful demonstrations against the government of Abdelmadjid Tebboune began](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Algerian_protests). On 18 February 2021, Tebboune announced changes to the cabinet. He dismissed the Minister of Energy, the Minister of Industry, and the Minister of Water Resources, among others. He also dissolved the [lower house](2806769.md) of the [Algerian Parliament](3748164.md) and called early legislative elections within six months.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

In July 2021, Tebboune formed a new government with [Ayman Benabderrahmane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymen_Benabderrahmane) as Prime Minister.[^cite_note-42]

On 28 July 2021, Tebboune stated that Algeria offers Libya "total assistance," as he was hosting Mohammad Younes el-Menfi, the president of Libya's Presidency Council.[^cite_note-43] On 8 December 2021, French minister of foreign affairs [Jean-Yves Le Drian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Yves_Le_Drian) and Abdelmadjid Tebboune held a meeting. They discussed bilateral relations and peace in [Libya](17633.md) and [Mali](19127.md).[^cite_note-44]



Tebboune with Italian Prime Minister [Mario Draghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Draghi), 11 April 2022

On 24 January 2022, Tebboune visited Egypt's President [Abdel Fattah el-Sisi](36703624.md) in [Cairo](6293.md). They discussed bilateral relations,[^cite_note-45] the situation in [Libya](17633.md) after the [indefinite postponement of elections](53433900.md),[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] the [Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ethiopian_Renaissance_Dam), and the potential return of [Syria](7515849.md) to the [Arab League](52625.md).[^cite_note-48] It was the first official visit of an Algerian president to Cairo since 2008.[^cite_note-49]

On 15 February 2022, Tebboune announced that the government would introduce unemployment benefits for young adults, with the unemployment rate in the country over 15%. The president announced that payments to job hunters aged 19 to 40 will begin in March to preserve "young people's dignity."[^cite_note-50]



Tebboune with [Vladimir Putin](32817.md)'s close associate [Vyacheslav Volodin](32671558.md) in Moscow, Russia on 14 June 2023

On 26 May 2022, during a state visit to [Rome](25458.md), Tebboune agreed to increase [gas](22131.md) supply for Italy and Europe after the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] On 18 July 2022, Tebboune signed an energy contract worth €4 billion for additional gas supply to Italy with [Italian Prime Minister](273741.md) [Mario Draghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Draghi) in Algiers, making Algeria Italy's biggest gas supplier.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] In September 2022, Tebboune announced a further diversification of Algeria's economy to increase non-hydrocarbon exports.[^cite_note-55] Tebboune stated that "the goal we set is to achieve exports from outside oil and gas at \$7 billion for the current year". Algeria's non-hydrocarbon exports reached US\$5 billion in 2021.[^cite_note-56]

On 18 December 2022, Tebboune met with [French Minister of Interior](871703.md) [Gerald Darmanin](54070416.md) in [Algiers](1644.md).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] They discussed bilateral relations, migration, security, and ended the visa issue with France which had limited the number of visas issued to Algerians since September 2021.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

On 13 June 2023, Tebboune started a three-day visit to [Russia](25391.md).[^cite_note-61] During a meeting with Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), they pledged to deepen the ["strategic partnership" between Russia and Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria%E2%80%93Russia_relations).[^cite_note-62]

In 2024, he criticized [Israel](9282173.md)'s [Gaza war](74998519.md) and accused Israel of committing [genocide against Palestinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_genocide_accusation) in Gaza.[^cite_note-63]

On 10 September 2026, he decided to cut ties with the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) for what he calls provocative and hostile acts and long-standing interference in regional affairs, including the destabilization of African countries (like [Libya](17633.md), [Sudan](27421.md) and [Mali](19127.md)), the backing of [Morocco](19291.md) during the [Western Sahara conflict](4255996.md), the normalisation of ties with [Israel](9282173.md) under the [Abraham Accords](64883396.md), the diplomatic and military footprint in the [Sahel region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel_region) tension, and the blocking of [Algeria](358.md)'s entry into the [BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS) economic group. Immediate actions taken to signal the end of the [diplomatic missions between Algeria and the United Arab Emirates](28243670.md) include the expulsion of the Emirati ambassador, who was given 48 hours to leave the country, the ban on UAE-registered civilian and military aircraft from its airspace, and the exemption of commercial flights, which are allowed to continue temporarily until the end of 2026.

## Personal life

Tebboune is a [heavy smoker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_smoker).[^cite_note-76] He is married to Fatima Zohra Bella,[^cite_note-77] and has five children: Saloua, Maha, Salaheddine Ilyes, Mohamed and Khaled.[^cite_note-78]
