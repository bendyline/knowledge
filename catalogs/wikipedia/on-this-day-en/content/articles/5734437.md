**Ovine rinderpest**, also commonly known as ***peste des petits ruminants*** (**PPR**), is a contagious disease primarily affecting [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat) and [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep); however, camels and wild small [ruminants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruminant) can also be affected.[^cite_note-2] PPR is currently present in [North](21714.md), [Central](539606.md), [West](67393.md), and [East Africa](260987.md), the [Middle East](19323.md), [South Asia](21566765.md),[^cite_note-3] and [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe).[^cite_note-4] It is caused by *Morbillivirus caprinae* in the genus *[Morbillivirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbillivirus),* and is closely related to, among others, [*Morbillivirus pecoris*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinderpest) (rinderpest), [*Morbillivirus hominis*](58911.md) (measles virus), and [*Morbillivirus canis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canine_distemper) (also known as [canine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caninae) distemper virus). The disease is highly contagious, and can have an 80–100% mortality rate in [acute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_\(medicine\)) cases in an [epizootic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epizootic) setting. The virus does not infect humans.

The disease was first described in 1942 in [Ivory Coast](57744.md) and has since been detected in more than 70 countries in the world.[^cite_note-5]

In 2017, the disease was reported to be affecting [saiga antelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saiga_antelope) in [Mongolia](19271.md), causing near-catastrophic herd depletion for the [endangered species](20110668.md).[^cite_note-promedmail-4889954-6]

In 2018, the disease was reported to be present in Bulgaria close to the border with Turkey.[^cite_note-reuters-idUSKBN1K923D-7] In a flock of 540 sheep and goats, two animals tested positive and one died, with disease confirmed by only one laboratory without any further tests.[^cite_note-8] Nevertheless, over 4000 sheep and goats were killed.[^cite_note-9] The recent outbreaks in European Union (EU) countries such as Romania, Greece, and Hungary have highlighted the vulnerability of the PPR-free countries. These countries have had their free status suspended following outbreaks in 2022 and 2023, highlighting the critical need for robust surveillance, coordinated vaccination, and effective buffer-zone strategies.[^cite_note-10]

## Synonyms

PPR is also known as goat plague, *kata*, syndrome of stomatitis-pneumoenteritis, and ovine rinderpest.[^cite_note-11]

Official agencies such as the [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAO) and [OIE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OIE) use the French name "*peste des petits ruminants*", with several spelling variants.

## Signs and symptoms

Symptoms are similar to those of [rinderpest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinderpest) in [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle) and involves oral [necrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necrosis), [mucopurulent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pus) nasal and [ocular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye) discharges, cough, [pneumonia](52135.md), and diarrhea,[^cite_note-Taylor2015-12] though they vary according to the previous [immune status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunocompetence) of the sheep, the geographic location, and the time of year, or if the infection is new or chronic. They also vary according to the breed of sheep. However, fever in addition to either diarrhea or signs of oral discomfort is sufficient to suspect the diagnosis.[^cite_note-Taylor2015-12] Incubation period is 3–5 days.[^cite_note-13]

### Hyperacute cases

Hyperacute cases are found dead without previous symptoms. They die with a [serous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serous_fluid), foamy, or [haemorrhagic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeding) discharge coming out of the nose.

### Acute cases at onset

In acute cases, animals are recumbent, sometimes in self-auscultation position. [Body temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_temperature) is high (40.5 to 41.0 °C) in the beginning of the onset in acute cases. The most typical signs are seen in the [digestive tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestive_tract). When entering an affected flock, one sees many animals with hind limbs stained by sticky [faeces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faeces). Some sheep have an arched back and show pain when defecating. [Tenesmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectal_tenesmus) may be noticed when taking rectal temperature. Fluid faeces are olive green to brown. Examination of the mouth shows ulceration of the [buccal mucosae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_mucosa), especially on the inner face of the lips, and neighboring gum. There can be [periodontitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodontitis) or serous nasal [exudate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exudate) and [conjunctivitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunctivitis).

### Evolution of acute cases

Nasal discharge becomes [mucopurulent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucopurulent) and may obstruct the nose. A dry, fitful [coughing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coughing) develops. Death occurs from 5 to 10 days after the onset of the fever. Some animals may recover, but a dry, [stertorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stertorous) coughing often persists for some days.[^cite_note-14] Besides coughing, there is intensive [labial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lip) [dermatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dermatitis) with scab formation, resembling [orf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orf_\(disease\)).[^cite_note-15] Miscarriages may occur.[^cite_note-WikiVet-16]

## Cause

*Peste des petits ruminants* is caused by a *[Morbillivirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbillivirus)* – *Morbillivirus caprinae* – which is related to but distinct from the now extirpated [rinderpest virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinderpest_virus). Four [genotypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genotype) (lineages) of the virus are described.[^cite_note-Taylor2015-12] Their classification is based on the nucleoprotein (N) or previously the fusion (F) protein gene. Lineages I and II are found mainly in West Africa. Lineage III is generally found in East Africa. Lineage IV was long known as the Asian lineage, but has now spread to the African continent and become the most prevalent lineage of all.[^cite_note-17]

## Epidemiology

### Origin and spread

This virus appears to have evolved at the start of the 20th century in [Nigeria](21383.md).[^cite_note-Muniraju2014-18] The extant genotypes subsequently appeared in West Africa (lineages I and II), East Africa and Arabia (lineage III), and Pakistan–India (lineage IV).[^cite_note-Taylor2015-12]

The first description of the disease was published in 1942 and relates to an outbreak in [Ivory Coast](57744.md), West Africa, in goats and sheep in 1940.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-Taylor2015-12] It spread to East Africa and Arabia at the beginning of the 1980s and to Pakistan and India in the early 1990s (Calcutta goat markets) finally reaching [Tibet](31516.md) in 2007.[^cite_note-Taylor2015-12] The first description of this virus in India was in 1987.

The outbreak in Burkina Faso in 1999 was caused by the lineage I group. Genotype III has caused outbreaks in Ethiopia (1996) and also in Arabia, southern India, and Tamil Nadu (1992). This lineage was found in Yemen in 2001. Genotype IV has been isolated in Kuwait in 1999.

### Geographical repartition

As of 2017, the disease is present in West Africa, part of Central Africa ([Gabon](12027.md), [Central African Republic](5478.md)), East Africa (north of the Equator), the Middle East, and the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent) including [Nepal](171166.md) and [Myanmar](19457.md). The disease is [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) in the Indian subcontinent and is a major threat to fast-growing goat husbandry in India, causing an annual loss of around 1800 million Indian [rupees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupee).

In [North Africa](21714.md), only [Egypt](8087628.md) was once hit, but since summer 2008, [Morocco](19291.md) is suffering a generalized outbreak with 133 known cases in 129 [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province), mostly affecting sheep.[^cite_note-20] The outbreak has precipitated the [vaccination](32473.md) of a large number of the 17 million sheep and five million goats in the country.[^cite_note-21]

### Dissemination

The disease is transmitted by infected [aerosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosols) in situation of close contact of animals. The long-distance spread is by sick animals.[^cite_note-Taylor2015-12] As the virus soon becomes inactive outside the body, indirect contamination is generally limited.

In an affected flock, even in pest-free regions, the disease does not progress very rapidly, in spite of the close contact between animals. New clinical cases may be observed daily for a 1-month period.[^cite_note-22]

## *Post mortem* lesions

The lesions are situated in the digestive tract. Quick *post mortem* examination will lead to the discovery of many haemorrhagic patches on the [serous membranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serous_membrane), and intense pneumonia. A risk exists that it may conclude with [enzootic pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzootic_pneumonia), inability to open the mouth, and problems with the [oesophagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oesophagus) and different parts of the intestine.

Erosions and inflammation are widespread on the buccal mucosa. The same lesions are also present in the [pharynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharynx), oesophagus, and on mucus-producing [epithelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithelia) of the [gut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gut_\(zoology\)), from [abomasum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abomasum) to [rectum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectum). Zebra-striped lesions on [coecum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coecum) and [colon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colon_\(anatomy\)) are said to be typical in some cases. Rarely, petechiae are also on the [rumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumen) mucosa.[^cite_note-23]

### Microscopic lesions

Microscopic study of both natural and experimental cases revealed [congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestion_of_the_Lungs) and [edema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edema) of the lungs, while in other cases, it revealed a network of fibrin infiltrated with neutrophils, the formation of [syncytia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncytium) and [giant cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_cell), and the presence of a pink-colored bacterial colony. Infiltration of [neutrophils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrophil) and [mononuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocyte) cells was seen within the [alveoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alveolus), [bronchioles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchiole), alveolar wall, and [interstitium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstitium) of the lung. Microscopic analysis also revealed that [interstitial pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstitial_lung_disease) was followed by [sloughing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloughing) of the [bronchial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchus) epithelium in the lungs and the prominence of type II pneumocytes. Numerous inflammatory cells were found in the [submucosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submucosa) and [lamina propria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamina_propria) of intestinal samples, and [vacuolar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuole) degeneration and [syncytial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncytium) cells, which are microscopic features, were observed in [hepatocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatocyte) of liver samples from goats. [Periportal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobules_of_liver) [multifocal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifocal) [lymphocytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphocyte) infiltrations were common in the liver [parenchyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenchyma).

[Leptomeningitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptomeningitis) and nonsuppurative [encephalitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encephalitis) were visible in brain sections under a microscope, and these conditions were characterized by [vascular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_vessel) [congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestion_of_the_Lungs), [hemorrhages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleeding) in the [parenchyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenchyma), [perivascular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perivascular_space) cuffing with mild to moderate [mononuclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mononuclear) cells (mostly [lymphocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphocyte) and few [macrophages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrophage)), focal to diffuse microgliosis, [neuronal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron) degeneration, satellitosis, and neuronophagia.[^cite_note-24]

In summary, epithelial cells, alveolar macrophages, lungs, and hepatocytes all showed histopathological alterations, primarily infiltrations of inflammatory cells, syncytia, and presence of intranuclear and/or [intracytoplasmic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracytoplasmic_morphologically_selected_sperm_injection) [eosinophillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eosinophilic_granulomatosis_with_polyangiitis) inclusions.[^cite_note-25]

## Diagnosis

History and clinical signs enable a presumptive diagnosis to be made in endemic regions. The virus can be detected in acute cases from various swabs and blood samples, using [polymerase chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerase_chain_reaction) and [ELISA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELISA). [Antibodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibody) can also be detected by ELISA.[^cite_note-WikiVet-16]

## Treatment and control

Antibiotics such as [chloramphenicol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloramphenicol), [penicillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penicillin), and [streptomycin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streptomycin) can be used and supportive treatment may be helpful.[^cite_note-WikiVet-16] Additionally, a [vaccine](32653.md) has been developed that may decrease death in the flock.[^cite_note-WikiVet-16] Movement restrictions and slaughter of affected flocks may be required in an attempt to eradicate the disease.[^cite_note-WikiVet-16] A global eradication programme has been developed by the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) of the [United Nations](31769.md) and the [World Organisation for Animal Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Organisation_for_Animal_Health).[^cite_note-26] More information can be found on FAO's website on the implementation of this global plan.[^cite_note-27] It is considered feasible to eradicate ovine rinderpest in 15 years, starting in the year 2016.[^cite_note-Taylor2015-12]
