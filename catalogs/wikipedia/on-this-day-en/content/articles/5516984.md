[Bulgaria](3415.md) adopted the [euro](9472.md) on 1 January 2026, becoming the 21st [member state](10890716.md) of the [eurozone](184391.md).[^cite_note-3] When Bulgaria [joined the European Union in 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) it committed to join the eurozone and replace its currency, the [lev](268071.md), with the euro. In February 2025, the country officially requested off-cycle assessments of their convergence by the [European Commission](9974.md) and [ECB](9474.md) to determine the country's readiness.[^cite_note-bntnews-4][^cite_note-Nikolov-5] The 2025 convergence reports published on 4 June 2025 concluded that Bulgaria met the [convergence criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_criteria).[^cite_note-Meets_Criteria-6] On 8 July 2025, the [European Parliament](9581.md) endorsed Bulgaria's entry in the eurozone[^cite_note-7] and the [Council of the European Union](5866.md) adopted the final three legislative acts required for the admission.[^cite_note-Council_Final_Steps-8]

The lev was first established by the [Bulgarian National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Bank) in 1880. Under a [currency board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_board) introduced in 1997, the lev was first pegged to the [Deutsche Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mark) (1,000 BGL = 1 DEM). With the lev's 1999 [redenomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redenomination) and the arrival of the euro, the exchange rate was updated to its long-standing fixed peg of 1.95583 BGN = 1 EUR. Between 10 July 2020 and 31 December 2025, the lev remained within the [European Exchange Rate Mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Exchange_Rate_Mechanism) (ERM II), exiting the mechanism to transition into the euro on 1 January 2026. On that day, the euro became the official currency of Bulgaria; however, during the cash changeover period (January 2026), the lev and the euro circulated in parallel for cash payments. Since 1 February 2026, the euro has been the sole legal currency in Bulgaria, ending 146 years of Bulgarian lev usage.

## History

When it joined the [EU](9317.md) in 2007, Bulgaria committed to switching its currency, the [lev](268071.md), to the [euro](9472.md), as stated in the [2005 EU accession treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Accession_2005). The transition would occur once the country meets all the [euro convergence criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_convergence_criteria). As the lev was fixed to the [Deutsche Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mark) at par, the lev's peg effectively switched to the euro on 1 January 1999, at the rate of 1.95583 leva = 1 euro, which was the Deutsche Mark's fixed exchange rate to the euro.[^cite_note-9]

Before the [Bulgarian euro coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_euro_coins) had been designed, the [Madara Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madara_Rider) had already been selected as the motif on the [obverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obverse_and_reverse) ("national" side) of the coins. Two Bulgarian saints, [Ivan Rilski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Rila) and [Paisius of Hilendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paisius_of_Hilendar), are depicted on the Bulgarian euro coins. In this way, Bulgaria is the first [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) country to have a Christian character on its euro coins.[^cite_note-10] Bulgaria officially joined the [European Exchange Rate Mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Exchange_Rate_Mechanism) (ERM II) on 10 July 2020.[^cite_note-novinite-20200710-11][^cite_note-12] Bulgarian government and central bank officials adopted a draft national plan for euro adoption on 30 June 2021,[^cite_note-13] after stating that same day Bulgaria's intention to adopt the euro on 1 January 2024.[^cite_note-Euro2024-14] In May 2022, the government adopted a more definitive version of its euro introduction plan, reaffirming the country's commitment to adopt the euro on the target date.[^cite_note-15] On 21 February 2023, Bulgaria scrapped the idea of adopting the single currency on 1 January 2024 due to an internal political crisis.[^cite_note-16]

The [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty), which Bulgaria acceded to by way of its EU accession treaty, requires that all [European Union](9317.md) member states, except [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_and_the_euro), join the euro once certain [economic criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_criteria) are met.[^cite_note-ECB-2020-17] In November 2007, Finance Minister [Plamen Oresharski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plamen_Oresharski) stated that his goal was to comply with all five convergence criteria by 2009 and adopt the euro in 2012.[^cite_note-18] Bulgaria did not comply with the requirement to be an ERM II member for at least two years, nor did it satisfy the price stability criterion in 2008. Bulgaria's inflation in the 12 months from April 2007 to March 2008 reached 9.4%, well above the reference value limit of 3.2%.

Bulgaria fulfilled the state budget criterion of only having a maximum deficit of 3% of the country's gross domestic product (GDP). The country had posted surpluses since 2003, which in 2007 represented 3.4% of its GDP (at the time, the EC forecast that it would remain at 3.2% of GDP in both 2008 and 2009). Bulgaria also complied with the public debt criteria. During the prior decade, the Bulgarian debt had declined from 50% of GDP to 18% in 2007, and was expected to reach 11% in 2009.[^cite_note-19] Finally, the average for the long-term interest rate during the prior year was 4.7% in March 2008, well within the reference limit of 6.5%.[^cite_note-20] A 2008 analysis said that Bulgaria would not be able to join the eurozone earlier than 2015 due to high inflation and the repercussions of the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis).[^cite_note-21] Some members of the government, notably economy minister Petar Dimitrov, speculated about unilaterally introducing the euro, which was not well received by the [European Commission](9974.md).[^cite_note-22] Bulgaria met all five criteria in the last off-cycle convergence report published by the [European Central Bank](9474.md) in June 2025.[^cite_note-23]

### Joining ERM II

The [Bulgarian lev](268071.md) had been pegged to the euro since the latter was launched in 1999, at a fixed rate of €1 = BGN 1.95583, through a strictly managed [currency board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_board). Prior to that, the lev was pegged at par to the [German mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Mark). While the [currency board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_board) which pegs Bulgaria to the euro had been seen as beneficial to the country fulfilling criteria so quickly,[^cite_note-24] the [ECB](9474.md) pressured Bulgaria to drop it as it did not know how to let a country using a currency board join the euro. The [Bulgarian Prime Minister](335071.md) stated the desire to keep the currency board until the euro was adopted. However, factors such as high inflation, an unrealistic exchange rate with the euro and the country's low productivity were negatively affected by the system.[^cite_note-25]

[Simeon Dyankov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_Dyankov), Bulgaria's finance minister, said in September 2009 that Bulgaria planned to enter ERM II that November,[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] but this was delayed. It was then delayed further due to an increased budget deficit, outside the Maastricht criteria.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] From 2011 to 2020, Bulgaria's non-membership of the ERM II was the primary factor that prevented euro membership, as Bulgaria met the other criteria for euro adoption. In July 2011, Bulgaria's Minister of Finance [Simeon Dyankov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_Dyankov) stated that the government would not adopt the euro as long as the [European sovereign-debt crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_sovereign-debt_crisis) was ongoing.[^cite_note-Radio_Bulgaria-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In January 2015, Finance Minister [Vladislav Goranov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladislav_Goranov) (under Prime Minister [Boyko Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyko_Borisov)) changed approach and said that it was possible for Bulgaria to join ERM II before the end of 2018. Goranov said he would immediately begin talks with the [Eurogroup](21533929.md) to establish a plan for joining ERM II.[^cite_note-bulgERM-34] In July 2015, the Bulgarian government established a coordination council to prepare the country for eurozone membership.[^cite_note-BGcouncil_Law-35] The coordination council was to draft a plan for the introduction of the euro, propose a target date and organise the preparation and coordination of the expert working groups.[^cite_note-BGcouncil-36] This approach was supported by former [Bulgarian National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Bank) governor [Kolyo Paramov](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BE%20%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%B2), who had been in office when the state currency board was established. Paramov argued that adoption would "trigger a number of positive economic effects":

- Sufficient money supply (leading to increased lending, which is needed to improve economic growth)

  [^cite_note-\:1-37]

- Getting rid of the currency board that prevents the national bank from functioning as a [lender of last resort](921278.md) to rescue banks in financial trouble

  [^cite_note-\:1-37]

- Private and public lending benefiting from lower interest rates (at least half as high)

  [^cite_note-\:1-37]

Former Bulgarian National Bank deputy governor [Emil Harsev](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%95%D0%BC%D0%B8%D0%BB%20%D0%A5%D1%8A%D1%80%D1%81%D0%B5%D0%B2) agreed with Paramov, stating that it was possible to adopt the euro in 2018 and that "Bulgaria's membership in the eurozone will bring only positive effect on the economy" because "since \[the establishment of] the currency board in 1997, we have been accepting all the negative effects of accession into the eurozone without getting the positive ones (access to the European financial market)".[^cite_note-38]

### From 2017

When Borisov's government was [re-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Bulgarian_parliamentary_election) in 2017, he declared his intention to apply to join ERM II,[^cite_note-39] but Goranov elaborated that the government would only seek to join once the eurozone states were ready to approve the application, and that he expected to have clarity on the matter by the end of 2017.[^cite_note-40] On taking the [presidency of the EU Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_the_Council_of_the_European_Union) in January 2018, Borisov indicated that no clarification had been given, but announced that he was going to pursue applications for both ERM II and [Schengen](16565139.md) by July 2018 regardless.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] Bulgaria sent a letter to the Eurogroup in July 2018 expressing its desire to join ERM II and committing to enter into a "close cooperation" agreement with the [European banking union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_banking_union).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] In January 2019, Goranov said he hoped that Bulgaria could join the ERM II mechanism in July and introduce the euro on 1 January 2022.[^cite_note-47] However, the first deadline was deferred to July 2019 due to extra conditions requested by eurozone governments, namely that Bulgaria:[^cite_note-FT_BG_127-48]

- Join the [European banking union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_banking_union) at the same time as ERM II (meaning Bulgaria's banks must first pass [stress tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_test_\(financial\)))

- Reinforce supervision of the non-bank financial sector and fully implement [EU anti money-laundering rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Money_Laundering_Directive)

- Thoroughly implement the reforms of the [Mechanism for Cooperation and Verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanism_for_Cooperation_and_Verification) (CVM)

While the CVM reforms were mentioned, and progress in judicial reform and organised crime was expected, leaving the CVM was not a precondition.[^cite_note-FT_BG_127-48] As of October 2019, Goranov's target was to enter the ERM II by April 2020.[^cite_note-49] In January 2020, [IMF](15251.md) Managing Director [Kristalina Georgieva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristalina_Georgieva) said that it was possible for Bulgaria to join ERM II later in 2020 and adopt the euro in 2023.[^cite_note-50] Borisov stated in February 2020 that Bulgaria's application would be reviewed in July.[^cite_note-51] In March, the Bulgarian central bank said that this target was no longer realistic due to the ongoing [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-52] However, in April Borisov stated that he would push forward the application by the end of April.[^cite_note-53] The reason he gave for this U-turn was the 500 billion euros rescue package to deal with the economic fallout of the coronavirus pandemic, which the finance ministers of the Eurogroup had agreed upon on 10 April.[^cite_note-\:0-54] On 24 April, [Fitch Ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitch_Ratings) announced that they would probably upgrade Bulgaria's foreign currency issuer default ratings (IDR) between Bulgaria's accession to ERM II and euro adoption:

> Given that the COVID-19 pandemic response is taking up significant resources with regard to political engagement at the EU-wide level, facilitating the Bulgarian lev's ERM2 accession may decline as a relative priority for European institutions. If concerns about risks ease and the process resumes, this would be supportive of the rating, as underlined by our view that all things being equal, we would upgrade Bulgaria's Long-Term Foreign-Currency IDR by two notches between admission to the ERM II and joining the euro.[^cite_note-55]

On 30 April 2020, Bulgaria officially submitted documents to the European Central Bank to apply to join ERM II, the first step to introducing the euro.[^cite_note-novinite-20200430-56] On 12 May, European Commission Executive Vice President [Valdis Dombrovskis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdis_Dombrovskis) stated that Bulgaria could join ERM II together with [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_and_the_euro) in July,[^cite_note-57] which both countries did on 10 July.[^cite_note-novinite-20200710-11][^cite_note-58] By the ECB decision adopted on 24 June 2020, Bulgaria and its national bank became a member of the [European banking union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_banking_union) under a close cooperation agreement which entered into force on 1 October 2020.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

### Joining the eurozone



"Welcome to the euro, Bulgaria!" sign at the EU Commission on 8 July 2025, Brussels

Bulgaria adopted the euro as its official currency on 1 January 2026, following several delays. In February 2023, Finance Minister [Rositsa Velkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rositsa_Velkova-Zheleva) announced that Bulgaria's target date for entry into the eurozone would be delayed from 1 January 2024 to 1 January 2025, primarily due to the projected inflation not meeting the inflation criterion in time.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] In April 2023, enough signatures had been collected to put the entry to a referendum.[^cite_note-63] However, in June 2023 the referendum was rejected by Bulgaria's parliament with 98 votes against, 46 abstentions and 68 votes in favour.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] On 26 July 2023, the newly formed [Bulgarian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denkov-Gabriel_Government) adopted a programme which stated that the switch to the euro in January 2025 is one of the main government priorities.[^cite_note-66] In November 2023, the Bulgarian euro coin design was revealed and approved by the [Bulgarian National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Bank).[^cite_note-67]

In order to ensure full compliance with the legal criteria for euro adoption, the Bulgarian Parliament approved the necessary legislation on the [Bulgarian National Bank (BNB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Bank) on 1 February 2024.[^cite_note-BGcoinsApproved-68][^cite_note-69] However, according to the assessment of the President of BNB, [Dimitar Radev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitar_Radev), the then recently adopted constitutional revision (making it possible for the Governor or Deputy Governor of the BNB to be appointed as a temporary caretaker Prime Minister in case of failed attempts to form regular governments), and the then recently adopted revision to the law on the BNB (making it possible for such a caretaker Prime Minister to resume his work and role at BNB after having completed his tenure as temporary caretaker Prime Minister), were both unlikely to meet ECB's convergence criteria for legal compliance. The ECB was not consulted on this specific change to Bulgarian law, but was expected to reveal its assessment on this potential issue in its convergence report scheduled for publication in June 2024.[^cite_note-70] In the event, the report assessed Bulgarian legislation as compatible with the Treaties and the Statute of the ESCB, and euro adoption from 2025 was ruled out on economic grounds instead, Bulgaria having failed to meet the price stability criterion.[^cite_note-71]

On 20 January 2024, Prime Minister [Nikolai Denkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Denkov) said in an interview that Bulgaria might be forced to delay its adoption of the euro to 1 July 2025 if the inflation criterion is not met in time, although the target date of 1 January 2025 remained unchanged.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Finance Minister [Assen Vassilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assen_Vassilev) believed Bulgaria was on track for euro adoption at some point in time in 2025, as the last inflation criteria was projected to be met between June and September 2024,[^cite_note-Vasilev2024-74] and the revised Bulgarian euro adoption law was expected to be approved by the [ECB](9474.md) shortly.[^cite_note-75]

The *euro adoption law* was launched as a draft law for public consultation from 25 March to 24 April,[^cite_note-76] and subsequently will be subject to approval by the Bulgarian Parliament; the European Commission—although not yet ECB—had already approved the published version as of 26 March.[^cite_note-BG_adoption_law-77] The ECB proposed several amendments to the proposed euro adoption law on 2 May 2024.[^cite_note-ECBamendments-78] The Bulgarian Ministry of Finance later clarified that the proposed euro adoption law will never be assessed by ECB as part of their euro adoption compliance test of the national legislation in their convergence reports (due to this law only being a national matter), but stressed that it was important from a national perspective that the National Assembly vote and approve the law by the end of summer 2024.[^cite_note-79] An information website ([www.evroto.bg](https://www.evroto.bg/en)) was launched on 25 March, dedicated to communicate the progress towards introduction of the euro in Bulgaria and practicalities of the euro changeover,[^cite_note-80] and the European Commission signed a Partnership Agreement for co-financing information campaigns towards Bulgarian citizens and businesses on all euro changeover aspects on 12 April.[^cite_note-81]

After a planned [government rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_government) and negotiations for a renewed government mandate for the next nine months failed,[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] and subsequent attempts to form a new government among the elected parties were likewise unsuccessful, [Bulgarian President](335070.md) [Rumen Radev](51575780.md) announced he would appoint a new caretaker Prime Minister and caretaker government tasked with organising an [early election in June 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2024_Bulgarian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-29march-85] Analysts had warned that snap elections could risk delaying efforts for Bulgaria to adopt the euro in 2025.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] However, outgoing Finance Minister Assen Vassilev stated that preparations were so far advanced that at the current stage a caretaker government could ensure eurozone entry in 2025.[^cite_note-88] Incoming caretaker Prime Minister [Dimitar Glavchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitar_Glavchev) stated that the incoming caretaker government would continue to focus on achieving membership of the eurozone and "Schengen on land" among its priorities.[^cite_note-89]

On 26 June 2024, the ECB released their report confirming that the country had failed to comply with the inflation criteria and was therefore unable to join the eurozone on 1 January 2025 target date.[^cite_note-90] According to [BNB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Bank)'s press website evroto.bg, it was projected that the inflation criteria would be met by the end of 2024, and that if it was, the country would request an off-cycle compliance re-assessment to get approval to adopt the euro as soon as possible.[^cite_note-91] In June 2024, Bulgaria's acting finance minister said Bulgaria would need to reduce inflation in order to meet ECB requirements to adopt the euro in January 2026.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

As of July 2024, the process was in an advanced phase. Once the required level of convergence had been achieved, an off-cycle evaluation would be requested.[^cite_note-94] On 26 July 2024, the members of the Bulgarian National Assembly adopted a decision obliging the Bulgarian government to speed up the process of introducing the common European currency. On 7 August, the law on the introduction of the euro was adopted, which represented the most important legislative act necessary for the smooth running of the currency changeover process. Out of 167 present members, 106 MPs voted in support of the law, in particular MPs from the pro-European majority in the form of [PP-DB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Continue_the_Change_%E2%80%93_Democratic_Bulgaria), [GERB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GERB) and [DPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_Rights_and_Freedoms), while 48 MPs from [Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_\(Bulgarian_political_party\)) (Vazrazhdane), independents and two [Socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Socialist_Party) voted against. 77 MPs were absent and 11 MPs from [There is Such a People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_is_Such_a_People) abstained. This law determines the terms and ways in which the currency will be changed. It was the final important legal act on the way to Bulgaria's euro adoption before fulfilling the last Maastricht criteria—that of price stability and, accordingly, the subsequent decision of the [Council of the EU](5866.md) to introduce the euro in Bulgaria. After the council made another decision on the issue, some regulations and other subnormative acts were to be adopted to regulate in greater detail what was written in the package of laws that Bulgaria adopted on the path to the eurozone.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] On 20 August 2024, President Rumen Radev signed a decree[^cite_note-97] promulgating in the Bulgarian State Gazette the law on the euro, with which it officially entered into force.[^cite_note-98]

#### Final preparations and adoption



Bulgarian small [euro starter kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_starter_kits)

According to Eurostat data released in mid-January 2025, Bulgaria's inflation rate was 2.6%, only 0.1 percentage points above the threshold for meeting the inflation criterion.[^cite_note-99] On 17 January 2025, it was reported that the government was expected to request off-cycle convergence reports from the European Commission and the [ECB](9474.md) within two weeks.[^cite_note-100] On 23 January 2025, Finance Minister [Temenuzhka Petkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temenuzhka_Petkova) announced that the country would not submit such a request until the inflation criterion had been met, with Bulgaria still 0.1 percentage points above the threshold.[^cite_note-101]

On 22 February 2025, thousands of supporters of the far-right [Revival party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_\(Bulgarian_political_party\)) attempted to break into the headquarters of the European Union mission in Sofia during a protest against the country's planned adoption of the euro in 2026.[^cite_note-102] On 24 February 2025, after the government determined that Bulgaria had met the inflation criterion, the Council of Ministers adopted a decision to formally request off-cycle convergence reports from the [European Commission](9974.md) and the [European Central Bank](9474.md).[^cite_note-103] The following day, Finance Minister [Temenuzhka Petkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temenuzhka_Petkova) and Bulgarian National Bank Governor [Dimitar Radev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitar_Radev) formally submitted the request to the European Commission and the ECB.[^cite_note-bntnews-4][^cite_note-Nikolov-5]

On 9 May 2025, then Bulgarian President [Rumen Radev](51575780.md) announced that he would submit a proposal to parliament to hold a referendum on the adoption of the euro.[^cite_note-104] The proposal was subsequently rejected by the speaker of parliament. The Bulgarian Constitutional Court had previously ruled that such a referendum was inadmissible because it would conflict with Bulgaria's commitments under national and EU law.[^cite_note-105]

On 4 June 2025, the European Commission and the European Central Bank published their off-cycle convergence reports, concluding that Bulgaria fulfilled the convergence criteria for adopting the euro.[^cite_note-106] The European Commission subsequently proposed the Council measures necessary for Bulgaria to adopt the euro on 1 January 2026.[^cite_note-Meets_Criteria-6]

On 19 June 2025, the finance ministers of the [Eurogroup](21533929.md) member states unanimously recommended that Bulgaria be approved to adopt the euro.[^cite_note-107] On 20 June 2025, the [Economic and Financial Affairs Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Financial_Affairs_Council) unanimously supported Bulgaria's accession to the eurozone.[^cite_note-108] On 24 June 2025, the [Committee on Economic and Monetary Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament_Committee_on_Economic_and_Monetary_Affairs) and the Subcommittee on Tax Matters of the European Parliament approved a draft report recommending Bulgaria's adoption of the euro on 1 January 2026. The vote was 46 in favour, 3 against and 5 abstentions.[^cite_note-109]



Seat in the Sofia metro depicting the conversion rate from lev to euro, as part of an awareness campaign by the Bulgarian National Bank and Ministry of Finance.

During a meeting of the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) on 26 June 2025, EU leaders welcomed Bulgaria's fulfilment of all the convergence criteria set out in the Treaty, endorsed the Commission's proposal for the country to adopt the euro on 1 January 2026, and invited the Council to swiftly adopt the relevant proposals. On 8 July 2025, the European Parliament endorsed Bulgaria's accession to the eurozone from 1 January 2026. Members of the European Parliament adopted, by 531 votes in favour, 69 against and 79 abstentions, a report confirming that Bulgaria met the criteria for adopting the euro.[^cite_note-110] Later that day, the Council gave its final approval by unanimously adopting the three remaining legal acts, including one fixing the conversion rate at 1.95583 Bulgarian lev per euro. Their adoption completed the legal process for Bulgaria to become the 21st member of the euro area. The acts were signed by [Stephanie Lose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Lose) on behalf of the [Danish Presidency of the Council of the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Danish_Presidency_of_the_Council_of_the_European_Union), in the presence of [Temenuzhka Petkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temenuzhka_Petkova), [Valdis Dombrovskis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdis_Dombrovskis), [Paschal Donohoe](12464309.md) and [Luis de Guindos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_de_Guindos).

From 8 August 2025 to 8 August 2026, sales prices were required to be displayed in both lev and euro using the same font and colour. Books, school textbooks and school supplies were exempt until 1 January 2026, when dual pricing also became mandatory for them. Due to technical restrictions, taxi displays and fuel pumps continued to show prices only in lev before 1 January 2026 and only in euro thereafter, although gas stations were required to display prices in both currencies prominently on their premises. Purchase receipts also showed the amount paid in both lev and euro.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

On 11 December 2025, the Board of Governors of the [European Stability Mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Stability_Mechanism) approved Bulgaria's application for membership and the technical terms for the country's accession to the ESM.[^cite_note-113] Following Bulgaria's adoption of the euro on 1 January 2026, the country formally became the 21st member of the ESM on 29 June 2026, when the ESM Treaty entered into force for Bulgaria.[^cite_note-114]

## Effects of euro adoption

|  | This section needs to be **updated**. The reason given is: **this section is based on a relatively old source and covers little more than arguments in favor of the euro**. Please help update this article to reflect recent events or newly available information. *(January 2026)* |
| --- | --- |

Since the lev had been pegged to the euro at a fixed rate, it was argued before formal adoption that Bulgaria was already a *de facto* member of the [eurozone](184391.md), as it could not pursue an independent monetary policy and it was bound by the interest rate policy of the European Central Bank, without having a say in monetary strategy. Therefore, Bulgaria would not lose any financial tools by adopting the euro, at least none that it had not already voluntarily surrendered by adopting its currency board. Adopting the euro and thereby becoming a *de jure* member of the eurozone was therefore argued to enhance Bulgaria's position by giving it a seat at the European Central Bank's decision-making table.[^cite_note-\:0-54]

## Linguistic issues

See also: [Language and the euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_and_the_euro)



The [10 euro note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_euro_note) from the new Europa series is written in the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet) (EURO) and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet) (ΕΥΡΩ) alphabets, but also in the [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_alphabet) (ЕВРО) alphabet, as a result of [Bulgaria](3415.md) joining the European Union in 2007.

[Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language) [Cyrillic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_alphabet) and its non-straightforward transliteration of the word *euro* initially caused issues when the [European Central Bank](9474.md) and [European Commission](9974.md) insisted that Bulgaria adopt the name **ЕУРО** (i.e., euro) rather than the original **ЕВРО** (i.e., evro) Bulgarian pronunciation: [\[ˈɛvro\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Bulgarian) (from Bulgarian Европа [\[ɛvˈrɔpɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Bulgarian), meaning *Europe*), arguing that the currency's name should be standardised across the EU as much as possible. Bulgaria maintained that its language's alphabet and phonetic orthography warranted the exception.[^cite_note-177] At the [2007 EU Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_EU_Summit) in Lisbon, the issue was decided in Bulgaria's favour, making евро the official Cyrillic spelling from 13 December 2007.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179]

This ruling affected the design of [euro banknotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_banknotes). The second series of notes (beginning with the €5 note issued from 2013) includes the term "ЕВРО" and the abbreviation "ЕЦБ" (short for Европейска централна банка, the Bulgarian name of the European Central Bank).[^cite_note-Compare_€5-180] The first series only had the standard Latin alphabet "EURO" and Greek "ΕΥΡΩ". The Bulgarian National Bank requested that the [Institute for Bulgarian Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Bulgarian_Language) provide a linguistic guideline for the adoption of the euro. It was prescribed that the eurocent should be written евроцент [\[ˈɛvrot͡sɛnt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Bulgarian), but that the word for cent, стотинка, could also be used. In Bulgarian, the plural of euro is евро (without inflection in plural form), and this was noted as an acceptable variation. The words стотинка and стотинки will continue to be present on Bulgarian coins, or more precisely, they are minted on the national side of Bulgarian euro coins. The 1 euro cent coin reads "стотинка" and the other 2, 5, 10, 20 and 50 euro cent coins read "стотинки".[^cite_note-181]

## Design selection and circulation

The [Bulgarian euro coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_euro_coins) entered circulation on 1 January 2026, marking Bulgaria's official adoption of the currency. The designs were finalized after a public selection process and official approval by the [Bulgarian National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Bank) (BNB) and the [European Union](9317.md).

Debates regarding the design of the ["national" (obverse) side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_coins#National_sides) of the coins began in earnest in 2008. Major contenders for the national symbol included the [Madara Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madara_Rider), ancient traditional nestinars ([Bulgarian fire dancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestinarstvo)), the [Cyrillic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_script),[^cite_note-182] the [Rila Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rila_Monastery),[^cite_note-SE_BS-183] and the [Tsarevets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsarevets_\(fortress\)) medieval fortress near [Veliko Tarnovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veliko_Tarnovo).[^cite_note-SE_BS-183]

A national vote was held between 17 and 29 June 2008, via post offices, fuel stations, and schools.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] The [Madara Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madara_Rider) was announced as the winner, securing a plurality of 25.44% of the vote.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189]

On 24 July 2023, BNB Governor [Dimitar Radev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitar_Radev) announced the Governing Council's decision that the Bulgarian national side of euro coins would mirror the design of the Bulgarian lev coins previously in circulation.[^cite_note-190] This design choice was officially approved by the BNB in November 2023[^cite_note-BGcoins-191] and by the [Council of the European Union](5866.md) in February 2024.[^cite_note-BGcoinsApproved-68]

In preparation for the launch, the BNB Governing Council approved revised graphic designs on 9 April 2025, updating the issue year from "2025" to "2026".[^cite_note-evroto.bg-192] Following the Council of the EU's final approval for adoption, the [Bulgarian Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Mint) commenced minting[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194] to ensure supply for the 1 January 2026 rollout.

The coins feature the [Bulgarian alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_alphabet) (Cyrillic script), which was already present on [euro banknotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_banknotes).[^cite_note-\:2-195] They also display the name of the fractional unit of the former currency, the lev (СТОТИНКА/СТОТИНКИ). Alongside [Greek euro coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_euro_coins) and [Cyprus euro coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_euro_coins), Bulgarian euro coins are the only euro coins that do not use the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet) on their national side.

During a dual-use period through January 2026 both lev and euro were accepted for cash payments.

### Adoption controversy

The [Zhelyazkov Government](78899170.md) and a majority in the [National Assembly](2122384.md) supported Bulgaria’s adoption of the euro, describing it as part of the country’s continued integration into the European Union. Domestically, however, the issue remained contested, with contemporary polling indicating higher public opposition than support for adoption.[^cite_note-220]

Several initiatives called for a referendum on whether Bulgaria should adopt the euro or retain the lev, including popular initiatives and a proposal by President [Rumen Radev](51575780.md) on the timing of the proposed adoption. The parliamentary majority rejected these proposals and proceeded through parliamentary decision-making.[^cite_note-221] In the case of the president’s proposal, the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Bulgaria) ruled that the initial refusal to submit it to a vote was unconstitutional; the Assembly subsequently voted 135–81 against holding a referendum.[^cite_note-222] Similar proposals in 2023 and 2024 were also rejected.[^cite_note-223] In late 2025, another initiative reported collecting 200,000 in-person signatures against euro adoption.[^cite_note-224]

Protests against euro adoption were held in 2025,[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226] including demonstrations near the time of the changeover. Opponents raised concerns about inflation, reduced national discretion over fiscal and monetary policy, national identity, and the rejection of referendum requests. Supporters emphasized Bulgaria’s existing EU commitments and argued that the decision should be made through representative institutions.[^cite_note-227]

The broader political context also shaped debate. The Zhelyazkov government had resigned after the [2025 anti-corruption protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Bulgarian_protests) and was operating in an interim capacity at the time of adoption, which some observers said contributed to tensions.[^cite_note-228] In his 2026 New Year’s address, President Radev criticized the decision not to hold a referendum on the timing of the euro adoption.[^cite_note-229]
