**Santiago Peña Palacios** (Latin American Spanish pronunciation: [\[sanˈtjaɣo ˈpeɲa paˈlasjos\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish); born 16 November 1978) is a Paraguayan politician and economist who has served as the 52nd [president of Paraguay](720557.md) since 2023. Peña served on leadership boards for the [Central Bank of Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Paraguay) and [Banco Amambay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Amambay).[^cite_note-\:1-3] He taught economics at the [Catholic University of Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Cat%C3%B3lica_%22Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_de_la_Asunci%C3%B3n%22), and has published research papers on monetary policy and finance.[^cite_note-\:2-4]

Peña is a former member of the Board of Directors of the [Central Bank of Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Paraguay), and former [minister of Finance of Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_\(Paraguay\)).[^cite_note-\:0-5] Peña was a member of the [Authentic Radical Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authentic_Radical_Liberal_Party) beginning in 1996, until 2016 when he joined the conservative [Colorado Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Party_\(Paraguay\)).[^cite_note-\:0-5] He stood as a candidate in the Colorado Party's presidential primary in 2018, where he lost to [Mario Abdo Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Abdo_Ben%C3%ADtez), who went on to be elected president in the [2018 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Paraguayan_general_election). He later won the party's nomination for president in the [2023 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Paraguayan_general_election), in which he ultimately won.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## Early life

Peña was born on 16 November 1978 in [Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n), Paraguay to José María Peña Nieto (a [Paraguayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayans)) and Ana María Palacios (an [Argentine](28820502.md) from [Buenos Aires](255919.md)).[^cite_note-8] He has two brothers: Manuel and Francisco Peña.[^cite_note-9] Through his father's side he is a direct descendant of former dictator [José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Gaspar_Rodr%C3%ADguez_de_Francia), considered to be Paraguay's founding father.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Education

Peña holds graduate degrees from the [Universidad Católica Nuestra Señora de la Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Cat%C3%B3lica_Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_de_la_Asunci%C3%B3n) (2001) and the [School of International and Public Affairs, Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_International_and_Public_Affairs,_Columbia_University) (2003).[^cite_note-\:3-12][^cite_note-13]

## Academia

In 2004, Peña taught as an adjunct professor at the [Catholic University of Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Cat%C3%B3lica_%22Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_de_la_Asunci%C3%B3n%22), teaching financial theory. In 2005, he became a professor of economic policy at the same establishment.[^cite_note-\:2-4] In addition, Peña has contributed to scholarship on monetary policy and finance. In 2017, he was invited by the Professor and current Ecuadorian ambassador to Chile Roberto Izurieta to participate as a visiting professor at the [Graduate School of Political Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_School_of_Political_Management) of the [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University) in [Washington D.C.](108956.md), where Peña shared his experience on public administration in Paraguay.[^cite_note-14] He also was the thesis advisor of [Geovanny Vicente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geovanny_Vicente), [CNN](62028.md) columnist and professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## Early career, Minister of Finance, and first presidential run (1999–2018)

Peña joined the Industrial Development Fund as an analyst in 1999 and served as an economist at the [Central Bank of Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Paraguay) from 2000 to 2009.[^cite_note-\:3-12]

He was appointed as one of the four directors of the Central Bank of Paraguay in 2012, leaving his job at the [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF).[^cite_note-reuters_finmin_2015-17]

### Ministry of Finance

[Horacio Cartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Cartes) picked Peña to replace [Germán Rojas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ%C3%A1n_Rojas_\(politician\)) as finance minister in 2015, following Rojas's resignation on personal grounds. As Peña took office, Cartes described him as a "bright young man" who would reinvigorate Paraguay's economy in the face of low global soy prices and a slow regional export market.[^cite_note-reuters_finmin_2015-17]

As [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_\(Paraguay\)) in 2016 and 2017, Peña promoted the project to create the Superintendency of Retirements and Pensions.[^cite_note-18]

Peña indicated, *“There have already been situations of embezzlement with the case of the Pension Fund of the [Binational Itaipú](https://www.itaipu.gov.py/) ([Cajubi](https://www.cajubi.org.py/)).”*[^cite_note-19]

The Itaipu Retirement and Pension Fund (Cajubi) and the Itaipu Workers' Union ([STEIBI](https://www.steibi.org.py/steibi/que-es-steibi/)) opposed the project. In a joint press conference in November 2016, the two groups stated that they saw the plan as an “unconstitutional” violation, further describing the proposed legislation as *“an imminent danger from a greedy person, who is looking for ways to extract money from people and who is the Minister of Finance.”*[^cite_note-20]

On May 17, 2018, Cajubi's director and legal advisor, Adelio Dolores Aquino, presented Cajubi's position against the creation of a Superintendency on the TV Unicanal's program Sin Vueltas.[^cite_note-21]

As President, Peña continued to advocate for the creation of the Superintendency of Retirement and Pensions, arguing that it was necessary to prevent problems like the Cajubi case. *“At some point, we will probably also have to pay for the embezzlement of the Itaipu Retirement Fund (Cajubi), which is another case, and that's because there isn't a public entity that is constitutionally obligated to do so*,” he stated.[^cite_note-22]

On December 14, 2023, Peña enacted Law 7253/23, creating the Superintendency of Retirements and Pensions in Paraguay.[^cite_note-23]

#### Joining the Colorado Party

Peña joined the conservative [Colorado Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Party_\(Paraguay\)) on 29 October 2016, having previously been a member of the [Authentic Radical Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authentic_Radical_Liberal_Party) (PLRA) for 20 years since joining at the age of 17. Peña's membership in the Colorado Party caused controversy, with reports that he only joined the party after Horacio Cartes announced his intention to dismiss members of his cabinet who were not party members.[^cite_note-\:0-5] Peña released a statement stating he had joined the party due to its focus on developing Paraguay, and claimed to be the descendent of one of the party's founders, Jaime Peña.[^cite_note-24] Following this, an electoral court annulled Peña's membership to the PLRA.[^cite_note-25]

### 2018 presidential election

In 2017, Peña ran for the Colorado Party's presidential candidacy in the 2018 general election, ultimately losing out to [Mario Abdo Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Abdo_Ben%C3%ADtez),[^cite_note-26] with Abdo Benítez winning 564,811 votes (50.93%) to Peña's 480,114 (43.29%).[^cite_note-27]

Following his defeat in the Colorado Party's internal elections in 2017, Peña was elected to the board of Banco Amambay in March 2018. The bank was part of the Cartes Group, which was owned by former President of Paraguay [Horacio Cartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Cartes), for whom Peña had served as minister of finance.[^cite_note-\:1-3]

## Presidency (2023–*present*)

### Overview



Peña on his inauguration speech on 15 August 2023

In 2022, Peña ran successfully to stand as the Colorado Party's presidential candidate; he was widely seen as the candidate most closely aligned to former president [Horacio Cartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Cartes), while his opponent, [Arnoldo Wiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnoldo_Wiens?action=edit\&redlink=1), was aligned with Abdo Benítez.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Critics of Peña have said that if elected president, Horacio Cartes would be a [grey eminence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89minence_grise) and that Peña would merely act as a mouthpiece for Cartes, who is sanctioned by the U.S. as "significantly corrupt". Colorado Party politician [Blanca Ovelar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanca_Ovelar) said Peña would end up acting as a "secretary" to Cartes if he was elected.[^cite_note-30] Cartes and his supporters in Congress had previously attempted to pass a constitutional amendment to allow Cartes to run for re-election, [sparking riots in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Paraguayan_crisis).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Peña was elected [President of Paraguay](720557.md), after winning 43.9% of the vote in the [2023 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Paraguayan_general_election) in April, defeating his closest rival [Efraín Alegre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efra%C3%ADn_Alegre) by a difference of 15 percentage points. He was congratulated by outgoing president [Mario Abdo Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Abdo_Ben%C3%ADtez), and presidents [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md) of Brazil and [Alberto Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Fern%C3%A1ndez) of Argentina.[^cite_note-elliott-33] Peña called for unity to address the economic challenges the country is currently facing. Peña was inaugurated on 15 August 2023.[^cite_note-34] At 44 years old, he became Paraguay's youngest president since the restoration of democracy in 1989, surpassing Abdo Benítez, who was 46 when he assumed the role in 2018.[^cite_note-aj2-35]

On 28 September 2025, [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Paraguayan_protests) began in Paraguay against Peña's government.[^cite_note-36] Commonly described by the media as part of the [Gen Z protests](77442342.md), they are the first mass-scale protests in the country since [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Paraguayan_protests), with the protests being inspired by the [2025 Peruvian protests](81215985.md).[^cite_note-cnne-37]

### Formation of the government



Peña and Abdo Benítez (second and third from the left) at the [Mercosur](25869317.md) summit in [Puerto Iguazú](2309094.md) (Argentina), in July 2023.

The government of Santiago Peña began on 15 August 2023 following his election as [President of Paraguay](720557.md). He was sworn in as president at the age of 44, becoming the youngest president of Paraguay since the [restoration of democracy in 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Paraguayan_coup_d'etat?action=edit\&redlink=1), surpassing [Mario Abdo Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Abdo_Ben%C3%ADtez), who was 46 years old at the time of his inauguration.[^cite_note-aj2-35] In the first days of his term, administrative [reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform) aimed at the reorganization of the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) and the centralization of state functions were promoted. One of the main changes was the creation of the [Ministry of Economy and Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_and_Finance_\(Paraguay\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), enacted on 23 August 2023. This new ministry absorbed the functions of the defunct Ministry of Finance, the Technical Secretariat for Economic and Social Development Planning (STP), and the Secretariat of the Civil Service (SFP). [Carlos Fernández Valdovinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Fern%C3%A1ndez_Valdovinos) was appointed as the first minister of this institution.[^cite_note-ip-38] Likewise, the National Directorate of Tax Revenue (DNIT) was established through the merger of the Undersecretariat of State for Taxation (SET) and the National Customs Directorate (DNA).[^cite_note-ip-38]

As for the binational entities and other key institutions, Justo Zacarías Irún was appointed acting general director of [Itaipu Binacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu_Binacional), while Luis Federico Benítez Cuevas assumed office as acting director of the [Yacyretá Binational Entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yacyreta_Dam) (EBY). At the [National Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Development_Bank) (BNF), the presidency was assigned to Manuel Ochipinti. For her part, Liliana Alcaraz was appointed minister of the Secretariat for the Prevention of Money or Asset Laundering (Seprelad).[^cite_note-39]

### Domestic policy

Peña has expressed his preference for a "small state", focused on providing security, public health, and education, leaving the rest to the [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector).[^cite_note-67]

#### Education

In February 2022, before assuming the presidency, Peña emphasized the need to turn government plans into [state policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_policy), especially in [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education) and [health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care). He proposed beginning a process of educational transformation in Paraguay, focusing on improving [human capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital) as an engine of progress.[^cite_note-68]

Carlos Giménez, [Paraguay's minister of agriculture and livestock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Agriculture_and_Livestock_\(Paraguay\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), stated in late February 2024 his opposition to the admission of people with "[homosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual) tendencies" to agricultural schools. He later apologized for "the interpretation of his statements". Santiago Peña regretted the remarks but kept him in office, without imposing sanctions.[^cite_note-69]

In April 2024, Peña enacted the "Zero Hunger in Schools" law, passed by the [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Paraguay), a program that seeks to guarantee food for students in the country's public educational institutions.[^cite_note-70] According to the newspaper *[ABC Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Color)*, the program excluded hundreds of children from special schools and inclusion support centers, leaving them without food. The [Ministry of Education and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_and_Sciences_\(Paraguay\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (MEC) acknowledged the situation and proposed reviewing and correcting the scope of the program.[^cite_note-71]

During the start of the school year in February 2025, Peña acknowledged deficiencies in school infrastructure, committing himself to improving the physical conditions of educational institutions to facilitate an adequate learning environment.[^cite_note-72] He announced the construction of seventeen "large schools" in different regions of the country, intended to offer high-quality education and modern equipment, seeking to reduce school dropout and improve learning indicators. Peña expressed enthusiasm for this project and acknowledged that there were still challenges to overcome in the [national education system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Paraguay).[^cite_note-73]

In March 2025, the president highlighted the historic delivery of 430,000 school [furniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furniture) items as part of the strengthening of the education system and the improvement of learning conditions. He announced the expansion of places for government [scholarships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship), benefiting more students who passed the tests and met the established requirements.[^cite_note-74]

#### Social policies and human rights

##### Transparency and access to public information

At the beginning of May 2024, Peña signed [decree](322949.md) No. 1604, partially objecting to [bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(law\)) No. 7257, which amended Law No. 5282/2014 on access to public information. The [veto](74661.md) focused on subsection "g" of article 10, which proposed the disclosure of sensitive data, such as beneficiaries of public land and government transactions. The executive argued that such publication violated the [right to privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_privacy) and statistical secrecy, constituting a threat to citizens' fundamental rights. The bill was returned to the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Paraguay) for reconsideration.[^cite_note-75]

##### Mass media and freedom of the press

During the government of Santiago Peña, the situation of the [mass media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media) and [freedom of the press](105323.md) in Paraguay worsened, according to the Chapultepec Index of the [Inter American Press Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_American_Press_Association) (SIP). In 2023, Paraguay fell to position #10 in the [freedom of speech](21401843.md) ranking in [Latin America](18524.md), a drop from position #8 the previous year. This change led the country to be classified as "restricted" in terms of [freedom of the press](105323.md), instead of "low restriction".[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

Santiago Peña has been questioned by sectors of the press, especially by *[ABC Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Color)*, due to alleged [conflicts of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest) and use of [public assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_property). On several occasions he responded critically, accusing media outlets of [extortion](162449.md) and [manipulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_manipulation). After revelations about his stake in Ueno Holding, a company benefited during his government, he announced in April 2025 the sale of his [shares](867877.md). Although he signed the Declaration of Chapultepec, his relationship with the press has been tense.[^cite_note-78]

#### Security and justice

In August 2024, Peña announced an investment of 500 million [dollars](18717338.md) to strengthen Paraguay's security and defense, with the aim of improving the operational capacity of the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Paraguay) and the country's [police forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Police_of_Paraguay).[^cite_note-79]

On 3 December 2024, President Santiago Peña enacted Law No. 7389, establishing the "National Regime of Integrity, Transparency and Prevention of Corruption" and creating the National Anti-Corruption Council, replacing the National Anti-Corruption Secretariat.[^cite_note-80] This law was presented as progress in the fight against corruption in Paraguay.[^cite_note-81] However, its implementation has been questioned due to the exclusion of binational entities, such as [Itaipu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu), from oversight by bodies such as the National Directorate of Public Procurement (DNCP) and the Office of the Comptroller General of the Republic (CGR). The lack of oversight over [tenders](36571608.md), especially in cases of alleged overbilling in the purchase of school desks and ambulances, has generated complaints of corruption and bid rigging. In addition, the amendment of Law No. 7089 on [conflicts of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest), which removed restrictions on officials owning shares in companies that operate with the state, has also been controversial.[^cite_note-82] A report by the [United States Department of State](31975.md) criticized the "rampant corruption" and lack of legal certainty in the country, making foreign investment more difficult and highlighting that much remained to be done in terms of combating [impunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impunity).[^cite_note-83]

In April 2025, Peña signed Decree No. 3749, regulating Law No. 7027/2022, which raises to [₲](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarani_\(currency\)) 7000 million the reward for information on the whereabouts of the [kidnapped](169376.md) citizens Edelio Morínigo, Félix Urbieta, and [Óscar Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Denis). The decree authorizes the [Ministry of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Paraguay\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) to make partial or total payments, depending on the quality and truthfulness of the information, through confidential funds. The rule establishes that any citizen may provide useful information, except public officials of security and justice institutions, or their relatives up to the fifth degree of consanguinity. Confidentiality of sources is guaranteed, and false information is to be reported to the [Public Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Ministry_\(Paraguay\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-84]

#### Infrastructure and public works

On 10 May 2025, the Valenzuela 500 [kV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilovolt) Interconnection Substation, considered the largest in the Paraguayan electrical system, was inaugurated.[^cite_note-85] The project, carried out by the [National Electricity Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Electricity_Administration?action=edit\&redlink=1) (ANDE), benefits approximately 770,000 users in [Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n),[^cite_note-86] the metropolitan area, and departments such as [Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Department), [Paraguarí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguar%C3%AD_Department), and [Presidente Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidente_Hayes_Department). The project, with an investment of 79 million [dollars](18717338.md) financed by the Financial Fund for the Development of the River Plate Basin ([Fonplata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fonplata?action=edit\&redlink=1)), forms part of a strategy to strengthen the National Interconnected System and advance regional energy integration. President Peña highlighted the importance of diversifying the country's energy matrix, promoting agreements with [Argentina](18951905.md) to continue the Aña Cuá works, as well as initiatives with [Itaipu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu_Binacional) for the development of [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy). He also promoted private-sector participation in electricity generation and distribution, and announced [reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform) to the Energy Law to facilitate that process.[^cite_note-87]

#### Relations with the United States

Main article: [Paraguay-United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay-United_States_relations)



Peña (far left) together with [Donald Trump](4848272.md) (far right, center) and a group of world leaders at the [Gaza peace summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Gaza_Peace_Summit) in October 2025.

In security matters, in June 2024 the two countries agreed on a joint investment intended to improve connectivity in rural areas through satellite internet and to strengthen national [cybersecurity](7398.md). The [United States](3434750.md) committed USD 3 million for connectivity and US\$3.1 million for cybersecurity in the Paraguayan armed forces.[^cite_note-132] Likewise, in November 2024, the Ministry of Information and Communication Technologies (MITIC) and the [United States Southern Command](1590932.md) carried out a joint review of the Paraguayan government's computer systems, detecting and mitigating vulnerabilities.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

In December 2023, President Peña received General [Laura Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Richardson), commander of United States Southern Command, with whom he discussed cooperation projects in security and the fight against [organized crime](22625.md).[^cite_note-135]

In the commercial sphere, in September 2023 the T-FAST program was renewed until 2025 through a memorandum of understanding, with the aim of reducing non-[tariff](55551.md) barriers to agricultural trade and streamlining export processes. Likewise, in May 2023 a cooperation agreement between the [United States Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture) and Paraguay's National Customs Directorate was extended for two years in order to facilitate agricultural trade. The agreement estimated a reduction of 14% in costs and 30% in product release times.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

In August 2025, the [Donald Trump government](78298108.md) signed a migration cooperation agreement with Peña's government (the "Safe Third Country" agreement), which allows asylum seekers in United States territory to process their requests for protection in Paraguay.[^cite_note-138] In December of that same year, both governments signed a Status of Forces Agreement (SOFA) regulating the presence and activities of U.S. soldiers and military personnel on Paraguayan soil.[^cite_note-139] Foreign minister [Rubén Ramírez Lezcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Ram%C3%ADrez_Lezcano) confirmed that those soldiers would have immunity in Paraguay.[^cite_note-140]

#### Relations with Israel

Main article: [Israel-Paraguay relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel-Paraguay_relations)



Peña at [Mount Herzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Herzl) in December 2024.

In May 2023, Peña announced the intention to move the Paraguayan embassy in Israel from [Tel Aviv](31453.md) to [Jerusalem](16043.md).[^cite_note-141] That measure had previously been taken by [Horacio Cartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Cartes) in 2018, but reversed by [Mario Abdo Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Abdo_Ben%C3%ADtez) later that same year, causing Israel to close its embassy in [Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n).[^cite_note-142] According to Paraguayan foreign minister [Rubén Ramírez Lezcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Ram%C3%ADrez_Lezcano), the Israeli embassy in Paraguay was expected to reopen in July 2024.[^cite_note-143]

After the outbreak of the [war between Israel and Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel-Hamas_war) in October 2023, Peña's government adopted a strongly pro-Israel stance, with Paraguay abstaining or voting against several [United Nations](31769.md) resolutions calling for a cessation of hostilities between Israel and [Hamas](13913.md),[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] including a vote recognizing Palestine as a legitimate state, in which Paraguay abstained.[^cite_note-146] Likewise, Paraguay opposed the [South African case before the International Court of Justice](75736183.md) against Israel, in which the former accused the latter of committing [genocide](75668427.md) in the Gaza Strip,[^cite_note-147] and also opposed the intention of [Karim Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Khan), chief prosecutor of the [International Criminal Court](14880.md), to issue international arrest warrants against Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) and his defense minister [Yoav Gallant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoav_Gallant), calling it "incorrect".[^cite_note-148]

In public statements, Peña has argued that support for Israel goes beyond his personal view and represents, in his words, "a message from the entire Paraguayan nation", appealing to a historical and political affinity between the two countries. This policy has reinforced Paraguay's international alignment with the [United States](3434750.md) and [Israel](9282173.md), although it has generated criticism from sectors warning of possible repercussions for relations with countries of the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world) and the [Global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South).[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]

In December 2024, Peña attended the reopening of the Paraguayan embassy in Israel in [Jerusalem](16043.md).[^cite_note-152] Domestically, the government's stance was celebrated by figures in the ruling camp as a reaffirmation of shared democratic values, although it also prompted criticism in the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Paraguay) from opposition sectors that considered the diplomatic imbalance risky.[^cite_note-153]

#### Relations with Venezuela

##### Restoration of diplomatic relations

On 15 November 2023, Santiago Peña restored diplomatic relations with [Venezuela](32374.md), after the break that occurred in 2019 under the government of [Mario Abdo Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Abdo_Ben%C3%ADtez). Peña recognized [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md) as president, stating that Paraguay would reopen its embassy in [Caracas](55289.md) and that Venezuela would do the same in [Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n).[^cite_note-155] Peña defended the gradual resumption of relations with Venezuela, but expressed concern over the disqualification of opposition figure [María Corina Machado](5791389.md). He justified the rapprochement as part of a regional strategy after speaking with leaders such as [Lula da Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lula_da_Silva) and [Gabriel Boric](47329497.md), assuring that Paraguay remains committed to democracy and human rights. Although he ruled out that the decision was linked to [Petropar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petropar)'s debt to [PDVSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDVSA), he acknowledged the possibility of increasing trade with Venezuela if there were guarantees of payment.[^cite_note-156]

##### Break

> "The Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela has decided, in the full exercise of its sovereignty, to break diplomatic relations with the Republic of Paraguay and to proceed with the immediate withdrawal of its diplomatic personnel accredited in that country".[^cite_note-rvp-157]\
> \- Statement by the Venezuelan executive (c. January 2025).

Venezuela broke diplomatic relations with Paraguay on 6 January 2025 due to Santiago Peña's recognition of [Edmundo González Urrutia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmundo_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Urrutia) as the winner of the [election in Venezuela](60825396.md). Caracas considered this interference in its internal affairs and withdrew its diplomatic personnel from Asunción. In response, the Paraguayan government reaffirmed its support for González Urrutia and demanded the departure of the Venezuelan [ambassador](68651.md) and his team within 48 hours.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-rvp-157]

#### Relations with governmental organizations

##### Mercosur

Main article: [EU–Mercosur Partnership Agreement](61184322.md)



Peña together with presidents of the Mercosur countries and the [president of the European Commission](24468.md), [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md), 6 December 2024.



Peña together with [president-elect](74379670.md) [Chilean](5489.md) [José Antonio Kast](55052190.md), 2025.

At the beginning of July 2024, Santiago Peña expressed his opposition to the flexibilization of [Mercosur](25869317.md), arguing that it is more advantageous to negotiate as a bloc, and questioned [Uruguay](31843.md)'s position of moving forward with bilateral agreements.[^cite_note-amb-159] Although in the first days of his term he had shown a willingness toward extra-bloc agreements, at that time he adopted the opposite stance.[^cite_note-amb-159] Prior to the transfer of the [rotating presidency of Mercosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Mercosur?action=edit\&redlink=1#Pro_tempore_presidency) to Uruguay, Peña pointed to the need to continue efforts to strengthen regional integration, acknowledging that the bloc is going through a period of challenges.[^cite_note-160] In October 2024, Peña lowered expectations regarding an imminent [trade agreement between Mercosur and the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercosur-European_Union_free_trade_agreement?action=edit\&redlink=1), underscoring that, although the bloc has shown interest in moving toward a [free trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade) agreement, it has not found an equivalent willingness in [Europe](9239.md). He described as "unacceptable" the lack of recognition by [Brussels](3708.md) of Paraguay's sanitary certification institutions.[^cite_note-161] In December of that same year, he aligned himself with the position of the [president of Brazil](273638.md), [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md), reaffirming that Paraguay will not support unilateral negotiations that could fragment Mercosur's cohesion.[^cite_note-162]

##### OAS

Peña initially promoted the candidacy of his foreign minister, [Rubén Ramírez Lezcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Ram%C3%ADrez_Lezcano), for the [General Secretariat of the Organization of American States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_General_of_the_Organization_of_American_States) (OAS),[^cite_note-163] although it was withdrawn in March 2025 following regional support for the candidate from [Suriname](26828.md), Albert Ramdin. Nevertheless, the Paraguayan government has highlighted the importance of maintaining institutional ties with the [United States Congress](31756.md) within the framework of the bilateral agenda.[^cite_note-164]

##### OECD

During an address to Argentine businesspeople in October 2024, Peña stated that one of the principal goals of his government is for Paraguay to become a member of the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development) (OECD). He explained that, to achieve this goal, it is essential to continue promoting development in key areas such as education and security.[^cite_note-165]

By the time of the 2023 election, Peña was considered a [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative) politician who opposes the legalization of [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_Paraguay) and [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Paraguay).[^cite_note-TheGuardian-166] In May 2017, however, he had stated that he was in favor of same-sex marriage, while saying in regards of abortion that he was open for it to "be discussed in a sincere environment and without prejudice".[^cite_note-167] He reversed position shortly afterwards following criticism from conservative sectors.[^cite_note-168] During his 2023 campaign, he promised to create 500,000 jobs. He also ruled out raising taxes.[^cite_note-TheGuardian-166]

On foreign policy, Peña pledged to maintain Paraguay's diplomatic relations with the [Republic of China (Taiwan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay%E2%80%93Taiwan_relations).[^cite_note-elliott-33] In May 2023, Peña announced that he intended to move the Paraguayan embassy in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Paraguay_relations) from [Tel Aviv](31453.md) to [Jerusalem](16043.md) after being sworn in as president;[^cite_note-169] a move that was previously made by [Horacio Cartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horacio_Cartes) in 2018, but reversed by [Mario Abdo Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Abdo_Ben%C3%ADtez) later that year.[^cite_note-170] Peña eventually attended the inauguration of said embassy in December 2024.[^cite_note-171] Peña and his government expressed vocal support for Israel during the [Gaza war](74998519.md) and the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War),[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] with Paraguay siding with Israel during [South Africa's genocide case against Israel](75736183.md).[^cite_note-chileparaguay-175]

As president, Paraguay restored diplomatic relations with [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay%E2%80%93Venezuela_relations) in November 2023; relations were strained since 2019.[^cite_note-176] Relations between the two nations were broken again in February 2025, however, after Peña recognized [Edmundo González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmundo_Gonz%C3%A1lez) as the winner of the [2024 Venezuelan presidential election](60825396.md).[^cite_note-177] On the [2026 United States intervention in Venezuela](82021123.md), Peña stated that "it can only bring good news".[^cite_note-178]

In February 2023, Peña said in an interview with *[Folha de S.Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folha_de_S.Paulo)* that the [military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship_of_Alfredo_Stroessner) of [Alfredo Stroessner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Stroessner) had resulted in "more than 50 years of stability in Paraguay".[^cite_note-179] He further asserted that Stroessner, who came to power as a result of a [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Paraguayan_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), had come to a power as a result of a "political agreement".[^cite_note-180] Peña's comments were criticised by opposition politicians and activists, and he was accused of trying to attract conservative and [ultraconservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraconservative) voters within the Colorado Party.[^cite_note-181]

## Controversies

On 7 April 2025, [Arnoldo Wiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnoldo_Wiens?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([ANR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Party_\(Paraguay\))) questioned Santiago Peña's administration, accusing him of failing to fulfill his campaign promises and of allowing a group close to the government to rapidly accumulate large fortunes. According to Wiens, the citizenry, especially in the interior of the country, has not experienced substantial improvements, and he called for greater transparency and commitment to the principles defended during the campaign.[^cite_note-182]

A few days later, former deputy [Édgar Acosta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89dgar_Acosta?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([PLRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Paraguay\))) expanded the criticism, accusing the president of prioritizing his own business interests and those of his circle over the general welfare. In statements to [ABC Cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Cardinal?action=edit\&redlink=1), Acosta said that Peña had promoted changes to the Conflict of Interest Law in order to benefit his private ties, and that his name and those of his associates appear linked to various public [tenders](36571608.md). In Acosta's view, this conduct confirmed the warnings made after Peña's election, which pointed to a government oriented toward benefiting a specific economic sector, in line with a broader strategy by [cartismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartismo_\(Paraguay\)) to preserve its power through control of key resources.[^cite_note-183]

On 21 April, the criticism intensified from the religious sphere. [Bishop emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_emeritus) [Mario Melanio Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Melanio_Medina?action=edit\&redlink=1) warned about the penetration of [drug trafficking](218449.md) into state institutions and alleged that the [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) was subordinated to illicit [economic power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_power). He accused the [Colorado Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Party_\(Paraguay\)) of acting in favor of private interests, contributing to [social inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality) and the deterioration of democratic institutions. Medina expressed concern about the lack of [legal certainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_certainty), the weakness of the [political opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_opposition), and skepticism regarding the negotiations over Annex C of the [Itaipu Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu_Treaty?action=edit\&redlink=1), stating that any eventual benefits would primarily favor [Brazil](3383.md).[^cite_note-184]

Santiago Peña was criticized for his decision not to attend the [funeral of Pope Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_funeral_of_Pope_Francis).[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186] Peña's absence was regarded as a lack of reciprocity toward the affection shown by [Francis](1687680.md) for Paraguay, a deeply [Catholic](606848.md) nation.[^cite_note-Telefuturo-187] Instead, Peña opted for an eleven-day trip to the [United States](3434750.md) to receive a private award.[^cite_note-Telefuturo-187]

Peña has also attracted criticism for his international trips, which have been perceived as excessive. By mid-May 2025, Peña was estimated to have made more than 40 international trips as head of state, at a cost of [₲](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_guaran%C3%AD)4.000 billion (or US\$585,000), and to have spent a total of 126 days outside Paraguayan territory.[^cite_note-188] His supporters have defended these measures, arguing that they attract more private investment to Paraguay.[^cite_note-189]
