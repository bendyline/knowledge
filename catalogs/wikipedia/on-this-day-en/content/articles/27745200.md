**Tokyo Electron Limited** ([Japanese](15606.md): 東京エレクトロン株式会社, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Tokyo Erekutoron Kabushiki-gaisha*), or **TEL**, is a Japanese [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics) and [semiconductor company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_company) headquartered in [Akasaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akasaka,_Tokyo), [Minato-ku, Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minato-ku,_Tokyo), [Japan](15573.md).[^cite_note-fact_book_2011-4] Founded as Tokyo Electron Laboratories, Inc. in 1963,[^cite_note-history-7] TEL is best known as a supplier of equipment to [fabricate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_device_fabrication) [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuits) (IC), [flat panel displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_panel_displays) (FPD), and [photovoltaic cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_cells) (PV).[^cite_note-fact_book_2011-4] Its subsidiary, **Tokyo Electron Device** (東京エレクトロンデバイス株式会社, *Tokyo Erekutoron Debaisu Kabushiki-gaisha*; [TYO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Stock_Exchange): [2760](https://www2.jpx.co.jp/tseHpFront/StockSearch.do?callJorEFlg=1\&method=topsearch\&topSearchStr=2760)), or **TED**, specializes in [semiconductor devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_devices), [electronic components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_components), and [networking devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Networking_devices).[^cite_note-fact_book_2011-4] As of 2011, TEL was the largest manufacturer of IC and FPD production equipment.[^cite_note-fact_book_2011-4] Listed on the [Nikkei 225](40293.md),[^cite_note-Ref_Staff3_reuters.com-8] in 2024, Tokyo Electron had a market cap of US\$114.6 billion, making it the third-most valuable company in Japan in terms of market cap, and the 12th ranked semiconductor-related company worldwide.[^cite_note-Ref_Tsutsumi1_nikkei.com-9]

## Company history

### 1963-1979

On 11 November 1963 Tokyo Electron Laboratories Incorporated was founded by Tokuo Kubo and Toshio Kodaka, largely funded by [Tokyo Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Broadcasting_System) (TBS), with a capital of over five million [yen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yen). Later that year, their office opened in the TBS main building and began manufacturing thousands of quality-control and importing [diffusion furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_furnace) made by Thermco and selling Japanese-made car radios.[^cite_note-history-7]

In 1965 the company approached a rapidly growing business in the market, [Fairchild Semiconductor Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Semiconductor_Corporation), and agreed to serve as a sales agency for them, increasing their capital to twenty million yen. Tokyo Electron began exporting IC testers, [IC sockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU_socket), IC connectors, and other similar computer components.[^cite_note-history-7]

The company opened an office in [San Francisco, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco,_California) and their new branch, Pan Electron, in 1968, establishing themselves as the only stocking distributor of imported electronic components in the region.[^cite_note-history-7] One year later, they opened their [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama) office and established [Teltron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teltron), a major manufacturer and distributor of car stereos, expanding their headquarters to fill the entire TBS-2 building and raising their capital to 100 million yen.[^cite_note-history-7]

### 1980-1999

The American electronics company [Genrad Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Radio) and Tokyo Electron Ltd. in 1981 formed a joint venture manufacturing unit, Tel-Genrad Ltd., to make in-circuit board test systems in Japan.[^cite_note-Ref_StaffBreifs1_nytimes.com-10] Tokyo Electron in 1986 was described by *[The New York Times](30680.md)* as a maker of semiconductor manufacturing equipment.[^cite_note-11]

In 1990, Tokyo Electron was competing with the Japanese company [Dai Nippon Screen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_Nippon_Printing) and the American company Silicon Valley Group as the largest producer of equipment to make semiconductors.[^cite_note-Ref_PollackAndrew_nytimes.com-12] In 1991, Tokyo Electron was one of five Japanese companies criticized by the [SEMATECH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sematech) consortium for allegedly withholding new technology from the United States, specifically "precision heating equipment" made by Tokyo Electron Ltd. and [Kokusai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokusai).[^cite_note-Ref_Hayes1_nytimes.com-13] By the early 1990s, Tokyo Electron's American rival [Applied Materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Materials) was losing market share, and "sought U.S. government help in the form of an import quota" to the American market.[^cite_note-Ref_Staff2001_Forbes.com-14] In May 1995, Tokyo Electron Ltd. was Japan's largest vendor of equipment to make semiconductors.[^cite_note-15]

### 2000-2026

After import controls, Applied Materials by 2001 was double the size of Tokyo Electron, which was its next nearest competitor.[^cite_note-Ref_Staff2001_Forbes.com-14] In 2001, TEL acquired Timbre Technologies Inc. in [Fremont, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont,_California), which developed measurement software technology.[^cite_note-16] In 2004, TEL announced improvements to its plasma etch chamber products.[^cite_note-Ref_Clarke_eetimes.com-17] In 2005, Tokyo Electron was still the second-largest maker of chip manufacturing gear after Applied Materials, and above [ASML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASML_Holding).[^cite_note-18] In 2006, Tokyo Electron had a research center at the [Albany NanoTech complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Albany,_SUNY), a [nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology) center in New York.[^cite_note-Ref_StaffCenter_nytimes.com-19] The company started a venture capital unit in 2006, TEL Venture Capital, based in California.[^cite_note-20]

As of 2011, TEL was the largest manufacturer of IC and FPD production equipment.[^cite_note-fact_book_2011-4] On September 24, 2013, Tokyo Electron and [Applied Materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Materials) announced a merger,[^cite_note-OriginalRef_Merced_nytimes.com-21] forming a new company to be called Eteris.[^cite_note-OriginalRef_Staff_wsj.com-22][^cite_note-OriginalRef_Staff_reuters.com-23] Eteris would have been the world's largest supplier of semiconductor processing equipment, with a total market value of approximately \$29 billion. On 26 April 2015, the \$10 billion merger was cancelled due to antitrust concerns in the United States.\[^cite_note-OriginalRef_Staff2\_[www.reuters.com-24](http://www.reuters.com-24/)]

In June 2023, Tokyo Electron was ranked among the Forbes [Global 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Global_2000) list of the world's largest companies,[^cite_note-Ref_Frobesstaff_forbes.com-25] at which point it had around 12,742 employees.[^cite_note-forbes.com-2] In late 2023, it was the largest semiconductor equipment maker in Asia. It was generating 43 percent of its revenue from China.[^cite_note-Ref_Inagaki_ft.com-26] In October 2023,[^cite_note-Ref_Tsutsumi2_nikkei.com-27] the company announced it had developed new technology to improve advanced [3D NAND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_NAND) flash memory,[^cite_note-Ref_Tsutsumi1_nikkei.com-9] making it the only competitor to [Lam Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lam_Research).[^cite_note-Ref_Tsutsumi2_nikkei.com-27]

In early 2024 the company said it would raise its employees' starting salaries by 40% to secure talent and compete with the payrates of foreign companies.[^cite_note-Ref_Matsuura1_nikkei.com-28] In February 2024, Tokyo Electron's market cap closed at US\$114.6 billion, making it the third-most valuable company in Japan in terms of market cap, beating [Sony Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Group). It also become the 12th ranked among semiconductor-related companies worldwide in terms of market cap.[^cite_note-Ref_Tsutsumi1_nikkei.com-9] It continues to be listed on the [Nikkei 225](40293.md).[^cite_note-Ref_Staff3_reuters.com-8] In 2024, Toshiki Kawai was president and CEO, while Yoshikazu Nunokawa was chairman of the board.[^cite_note-Ref_TELstaff_.tel.com-29] Cut tie with China Head Jay Chen [^cite_note-30]

## Products and services

Supplying equipment to [fabricate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_device_fabrication) [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuits) (IC), [flat panel displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_panel_displays) (FPD), and [photovoltaic cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_cells) (PV),[^cite_note-fact_book_2011-4] according to *Barron's* in 2024, Tokyo Electron Ltd. is engaged in the "development, manufacture, and sale of semiconductor production equipment and industrial electronics products for flat panel display manufacturing equipment." It has three segments: Semiconductor Production Equipment (SPE), Flat Panel Display (FPD) Production Equipment and Others. Others includes logistics, facilities management, and insurance.[^cite_note-Ref_homestaff_barrons.com-31] In 2023, it was reported that the company's FPD business had become "minor" in its earnings, while its SPE segment was steadily growing.[^cite_note-Ref_Hsiao_digitimes-32] TEL's SPE segment develops and sells SPE such as coaters/developers, [plasma etch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_etching) systems, thermal processing systems, single wafer deposition systems, cleaning systems, and [wafer probers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wafer_prober). The FPD segment focuses on SPE for flat panel displays.[^cite_note-Ref_homestaff_barrons.com-31] In 2024, services listed by the company included field solutions, engineering services, repairs and spare parts, upgrades and modifications, and overhauling used products.[^cite_note-Ref_TELservices_el.com-33]

In 2012, TEL produced SPE for multiple purposes.[^cite_note-fact_book_2011-4] Among those purposes was thermal processing, involving the deposition of thin layers of [dielectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric) material between [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistors) onto the [silicon wafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_wafer) surface, using a heated low-pressure [chemical vapor deposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_vapor_deposition) (LPCVD) or oxidation process.[^cite_note-thermal-34] Also in 2012, products involved [photoresist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoresist) coating and developing to project a microscopic circuitry pattern on the wafer in [photolithography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photolithography).[^cite_note-photolithography-35] There was also plasma etching,[^cite_note-etch-36] and products focused on wet surface preparation, using cleaning to remove contaminants such as dust.[^cite_note-cleaning-37] Other product areas in 2012 included single wafer [chemical vapor deposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_vapor_deposition), involving the deposition of thin layers of various materials, such as [tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten), [tungsten silicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten_silicide), [titanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium), [titanium nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium_nitride), and [tantalum oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum_oxide).[^cite_note-deposition-38] TEL's wafer probers tested the functionality and performance of each [die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_\(integrated_circuit\)) on the wafer,[^cite_note-wafer_probing-39] while other products allowed corrective etching and trimming of [thin films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thin_films) such as [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon), [silicon nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride), [silicon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_dioxide), [aluminum nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum_nitride), and metals.[^cite_note-trimming-40] It also sold integrated [metrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrology) (co-developed by TEL and [KLA Tencor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLA_Tencor)) in 2012,[^cite_note-metrology-41] and products for material and surface modification and [doping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doping_\(semiconductor\)) using [gas cluster ion beam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_cluster_ion_beam) (GCIB) technology.[^cite_note-doping-42] In 2016, it was involved in selling Advanced Packaging.[^cite_note-43]

## Research and development

TEL's Leading-edge Process Development Center is located in [Nirasaki, Yamanashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirasaki,_Yamanashi). TEL also has the [Kansai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai) Technology Center in [Amagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagasaki), [Hyogo Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyogo_Prefecture) and the Sendai Design and Development Center in [Sendai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendai), [Miyagi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyagi_Prefecture). TEL Technology Center, America, LLC in [Albany, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_New_York) is the R\&D center in the [United States](3434750.md). TEL is one of the partners of [IMEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMEC), a [microelectronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microelectronics) and [nanoelectronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoelectronics) research center in [Leuven, Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuven,_Belgium).[^cite_note-annual_report_2011-3]

In July 2014 TEL announced the establishment of joint assembly lab with the Institute of Microelectronics in Singapore. The lab is focused on the research and development of Wafer Level Packaging and assembly, to address the need of [Internet of Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_of_Things) with devices of high performance and low power consumption.[^cite_note-45]

In 2023 it was selling its US headquarters in Southeast [Austin, Texas](1998.md), under CEO Toshiki Kawai.[^cite_note-Ref_STaff_therealdeal.com-46] In 2024, it leased a new American headquarters in Austin.[^cite_note-Ref_Author_bizjournals.com-47] In 2025, Tokyo Electron Technology Solutions, the company's manufacturing subsidiary, is expected to build a \$170 million chip equipment plant in [Oshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csh%C5%AB,_Iwate), Japan.[^cite_note-Ref_Matsuda_nikkei.com-48]
