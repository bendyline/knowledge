**Jiangsu**[^cite_note-6] is a coastal [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) in [East China](683693.md). It is one of the leading provinces in finance, education, technology, and tourism, with its capital in [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing). Jiangsu is the [third smallest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_area), but the [fourth most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_population), with a population of 84.75 million, and the [most densely populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_population_density) of the 22 provinces of the People's Republic of China. Jiangsu has the highest GDP per capita and second-highest GDP of Chinese provinces, after [Guangdong](65109.md).[^cite_note-7] Jiangsu borders [Shandong](105032.md) in the north, [Anhui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhui) to the west, and [Zhejiang](96602.md) and [Shanghai](27643.md) to the south. Jiangsu has a coastline of over 1,000 kilometers (620 mi) along the [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea), and the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) flows through the southern part of the province.

Since the [Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty) and [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) dynasties, Jiangsu has been a national economic and commercial center, partly due to the construction of the [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_\(China\)). Cities such as [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing), [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou), [Wuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi), [Changzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changzhou), and [Shanghai](27643.md) (separated from Jiangsu in 1927) are all major Chinese economic hubs. Since the initiation of economic reforms in 1990, Jiangsu has become a focal point for economic development. It is widely regarded as one of China's most developed provinces, when measured by its [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_administrative_divisions_of_Greater_China_by_Human_Development_Index) (HDI).[^cite_note-SHDI-3] Its 2025 [nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_GDP) per capita reached CN¥167,970 (US\$24,113), becoming the first province in China to reach the \$20,000 mark in 2021.[^cite_note-8]

Jiangsu is home to many of the world's leading exporters of electronic equipment, chemicals and textiles.[^cite_note-9] It has also been China's largest recipient of foreign direct investment since 2006. In 2025, its GDP was more than CN¥14.24 trillion (US\$2 trillion nominal), which is the [sixth-highest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_administrative_divisions_by_GRDP) of all [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division).[^cite_note-10] If it were a country, it would be the [twelfth-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) as of 2022 as well as the [19th most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population).[^cite_note-11]

Jiangsu is also one of the leading provinces in research and education in China. As of 2022, Jiangsu hosts 168 [institutions of higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Jiangsu), ranking first of all Chinese provinces.[^cite_note-12] Jiangsu has many highly ranked educational institutions,[^cite_note-13] with 16 of its universities listed in the [Double First-Class Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_First-Class_Construction), ranking second after [Beijing](18603746.md). As of 2025, six major cities in Jiangsu ranked in the world's top 200 (Nanjing 5th, Suzhou 33rd, Zhenjiang 98th, Yangzhou 118th, Wuxi 161st, and Changzhou 173rd) [cities by scientific research output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_scientific_output), as tracked by the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index).[^cite_note-\:3-14]

## Name

Jiangsu's name is a compound of the first elements of the names of the two cities of Jiangning (now [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing)) and [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou); meaning "Pacified Place". The first character is derived from [Chinese abbreviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_abbreviation) of *jiāng* (江) for Jiangning taken from its [Northern Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Song) name of Jiangning ([江寧](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%B1%9F%E5%AF%A7), *Jiāngníng*, "Pacified area of the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze)"), while the abbreviation for this province is "[苏](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%8B%8F#Simplified_Chinese)" (*Sū*), which serves the second character of its name is a contraction of the old name Gusu. It refers to "Satisfied place" in the [Old Yue language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Yue_language).[^cite_note-15]

Alternatively, the province is also spelled in various forms as *Chiangsu*, *Chiang-su*[^cite_note-16] or *Kiangsu* in postal romanization. Since 1958, the province has adopted its current pinyin name of *Jiangsu*, a spelling that would be adopted by numerous international organizations and media outlets by the 1980s.

## History

During the earliest Chinese dynasties, the area that is now Jiangsu was far away from the center of Chinese civilization, which was in the northwest [Henan](166408.md); it was home of the Huai barbarians (淮夷), an ancient ethnic group. During the [Zhou dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty) more contact was made, and eventually the [state of Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Wu) appeared in southern Jiangsu, one of the many hundreds of states that existed across northern and central China at that time. Near the end of the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period), Wu became a great power under [King Helu of Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Helu_of_Wu), and defeated in 484 BC the [state of Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Qi), a major power in the north in modern-day [Shandong](105032.md) [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province), and contest for the position of overlord over all states of China. The state of Wu was subjugated in 473 BC by the [state of Yue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Yue), another state that had emerged to the south in modern-day [Zhejiang](96602.md) province. Yue was in turn subjugated by the powerful [state of Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Chu) from the west in 333 BC. Eventually the [state of Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Qin) swept away all the other states, and unified China in 221 BC.[^cite_note-17]



One of the [tortoise stelae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bixi_\(tortoise\)) of Xiao Dan (478–522), a member of the [Liang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liang_dynasty) royal family. Ganjiaxiang, [Qixia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qixia_District), near Nanjing

Under the reign of the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) (206 BC to 220 AD), Jiangsu was removed from the centers of civilization in the [North China Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_Plain), and was administered under two *[zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_\(political_division\))* (provinces): [Xu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuzhou_\(ancient_China\)) Province in the north, and [Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzhou_\(ancient_China\)) Province in the south. During the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) period, southern Jiangsu became the base of the [Eastern Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Wu) (222 to 280), whose capital, [Jianye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiankang) (later renamed to Jiankang), is modern [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing). When nomadic invasions overran northern China in the 4th century, the imperial court of the [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(265%E2%80%93420\)) moved to Jiankang. Cities in southern and central Jiangsu swelled with the influx of migrants from the north. Jiankang remained as the capital for four successive [Southern dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_dynasties) and became the largest commercial and cultural center in China.[^cite_note-18]



The [Huqiu Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huqiu_Tower) of [Tiger Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Hill,_Suzhou), Suzhou, built in 961

After the Sui dynasty united the country in 581, the political center of the country shifted back to the north, but the Grand Canal was built through Jiangsu to link the Central Plains with the prosperous Yangtze Delta. The [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) (618–907) relied on southern Jiangsu for annual deliveries of grain. It was during the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) (960–1279), which saw the development of a wealthy mercantile class and emergent [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy) in China, that [Jiangnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangnan) (southern Jiangsu, [Shanghai](27643.md), and adjacent areas) emerged as a center of [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade). From then onwards, major cities like [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou) or [Yangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzhou), would be synonymous with opulence and luxury in China. Today the region remains one of the richest parts of China.

The Jurchen [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty,_1115%E2%80%931234) [gained control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingkang_Incident) of [North China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China) in 1127 during the [Jin-Song wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_campaigns_against_the_Song_dynasty), and [Huai River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai_River), which used to cut through north Jiangsu to reach the [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea), was the border between the north, under the Jin, and the south, under the [Southern Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Song_dynasty). The Mongols took control of China in the thirteenth century. The [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), which was established in 1368 after driving out the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) who had occupied China, initially put its capital in [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing). Regions surrounding Nanjing, corresponding to Jiangsu and Anhui today, were designated as [Nanzhili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzhili) province (literally "southern directly governed"). Following a coup by Zhu Di (later, the [Yongle Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Emperor)), however, the capital was moved to [Beijing](18603746.md), far to the north, although Nanjing kept its status as the southern capital. In late Ming, Jiangnan continued to be an important center of trade in China; some historians see in the flourishing [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textiles) industry at the time incipient [industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization) and [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism), a trend that was however aborted.



The 76 m (249 ft) tall [Beisi Pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beisi_Pagoda) of [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou), built between 1131 and 1162 during the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty)

The [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) converted Nanzhili to "Jiangnan province"; in 1666 Jiangsu and Anhui were split apart as separate provinces. Jiangsu's borders have been for the most part stable since then.

With the start of Western incursion into China in the 1840s, the rich and mercantile [Yangtze River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_Delta) was increasingly exposed to Western influence. [Shanghai](27643.md), originally an unremarkable little town of Jiangsu, quickly developed into a metropolis of trade, banking, and cosmopolitanism, and was split out later as an independent municipality. Jiangnan also figures strongly in the [Taiping Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion) (1851 – 1864), a massive and deadly rebellion that attempted to set up a [Christian](5211.md) [theocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theocracy) in China; it started far to the south, in [Guangdong](65109.md) province, swept through much of South China, and by 1853, had established Nanjing as its capital, renamed as Tianjing (天京 "Heavenly Capital").

### Modern times

The [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%9349\)) was established in 1912,[^cite_note-19] and China was soon torn apart by warlords. Jiangsu changed hands several times, but in April 1927, [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek) established a government at [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing); he was soon able to bring most of China under his control. The provincial government moved to [Zhenjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhenjiang) in 1928. This was however interrupted by the [second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War), which began full-scale in 1937; on December 13, 1937, Nanjing fell, and the combined atrocities of the occupying Japanese for the next three months would come to be known as the [Rape of Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape_of_Nanjing), after which it became the seat of the [collaborationist government of East China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Jingwei_regime) under [Wang Jingwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Jingwei), and most of Jiangsu remained under Japanese occupation until the end of the war in 1945.

After the war, Nanjing was once again the capital of the Republic of China, though now the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War) had broken out between the [Kuomintang](16903.md) government and [Communist forces](7175.md), based further north, mostly in [Northeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_China). The decisive [Huaihai Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaihai_Campaign) was fought in northern Jiangsu; it resulted in Kuomintang defeat, and the communists were soon able to cross the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) and take Nanjing. The Kuomintang fled southward and eventually ended up in [Taipei](57648.md), from which the Republic of China government continues to administer [Taiwan](25734.md), [Pescadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penghu), and its neighboring islands, though it also continues to claim (technically, at least) Nanjing as its rightful *de jure* capital. Later on, after the capture of Zhenjiang, the provincial government was then relocated to [Shengsi County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shengsi_County) in [Zhejiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chekiang_Province,_Republic_of_China).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-official沿革-21]

After the communist takeover, [Beijing](18603746.md) (formerly Peiping) was made capital of the People's Republic, while Jiangsu was dissolved into Subei and Sunan Administrative Regions. On November 15, 1952, Jiangsu Province was reconstituted and Nanjing was demoted to be the provincial capital.[^cite_note-ws1-22] The [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up) of [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) initially focused on the south coast of China, in [Guangdong](65109.md) province, which soon left Jiangsu behind; starting from the 1990s they were applied more evenly to the rest of China. [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou) and [Wuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi), two southern cities of Jiangsu in close proximity to neighboring [Shanghai](27643.md), have since become particularly prosperous, being among the top 10 cities in China in terms of [gross domestic product](12594.md) and outstripping the provincial capital of Nanjing. The income disparity between northern and southern Jiangsu however remains large.

Jiangsu is flat, with plains covering 68 percent of its total area (water covers another 18 percent). Most of the province stands not more than 50 meters (160 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level). Jiangsu also has a well-developed irrigation system, which earned it (especially the southern half) the moniker of [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 水鄕; [simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 水乡 (*shuǐxiāng* "land of water"). The southern city of [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou) has so many canals that it has been dubbed "[Venice of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_places_called_Venice_of_the_East)" or the "Venice of the Orient".[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The [Grand Canal of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_of_China) cuts through Jiangsu from north to south, crossing all the east–west river systems. Jiangsu also borders the [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea). The [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River), the longest river of China, cuts through the province in the south and reaches the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea), which divides the region into two parts: more urban, prosperous south and more poorer, rural north, and these two parts has a tense division.[^cite_note-25] [Mount Huaguo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Huaguo_\(Jiangsu\)), near the city of [Lianyungang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianyungang), is the highest point in Jiangsu, at an altitude of 625 meters (2,051 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level). Large lakes in Jiangsu include [Lake Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tai) (the largest), [Lake Hongze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Hongze), [Lake Gaoyou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Gaoyou), [Lake Luoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Luoma?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Lake Yangcheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Yangcheng).

Before 1194 A.D., the [Huai River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai_River) cut through north Jiangsu to reach the [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea). The Huai River is a major river in central China, and it was the traditional border between [North China and South China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_and_South_China). Since 1194 A.D., the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River) further to the north changed its course several times, running into the Huai River in north Jiangsu each time instead of its other usual path northwards into [Bohai Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohai_Bay). The silting caused by the Yellow River was so heavy that after its last episode of "hijacking" the Huai River ended in 1855: the Huai River was no longer able to go through its usual path into the sea. Instead it flooded, pooled up (thereby forming and enlarging [Lake Hongze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Hongze) and [Lake Gaoyou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Gaoyou)), and flowed southwards through the [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_of_China) into the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River). The old path of the [Huai River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai_River) is now marked by a series of [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) channels, the most significant of which is the [North Jiangsu Main Irrigation Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Jiangsu_Main_Irrigation_Canal) ([traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 蘇北灌溉總渠; [simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 苏北灌溉总渠), which channels a small amount of the water of the Huai River alongside south of its old path into the sea.



[Xuanwu Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanwu_Lake) in [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing)

Most of Jiangsu has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa* or *Cwa* in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)), beginning to transition into a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (Köppen *Dwa*) in the far north. Seasonal changes are clear-cut, with temperatures at an average of −1 to 4 °C (30 to 39 °F) in January and 26 to 29 °C (79 to 84 °F) in July. Rain falls frequently between spring and summer (*[meiyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiyu)*), [typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon) with [rainstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainstorm) occur in late summer and early autumn. As with the rest of the coast, tornados are possible. The annual average rainfall is 800 to 1,200 millimeters (31 to 47 in), concentrated mostly in summer during the southeast [monsoon](57630.md).

### Climate change in Jiangsu

Due to its flat terrain, low altitude, and dense population, Jiangsu is one of the most vulnerable regions in China to [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_China) and its ensuing [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise).[^cite_note-\:03-26] According to the data collected by the Center of Climate Change in Jiangsu from 1961 to 2007, on average, the province experiences an temperature increase between 0.16 and 0.45 °C per 10 years and total precipitation change between -28.7 and 37.09 mm per 10 years. [Extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather) have become stronger and more common. Jiangsu's [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_agriculture), [ecosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem), [water resource](18581463.md), transportation, and [coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion) are all negatively impacted. The speed of sea level rise exceeds the world's average by a large margin.[^cite_note-27]

Specifically, a ranking on climate change risk of global regions released in early 2023 by the [Cross Dependency Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Dependency_Initiative?action=edit\&redlink=1) (XDI) puts Jiangsu as the most vulnerable of the entire world.[^cite_note-\:03-26] Jiangsu is at more risk due to its extensive [industrial, trade, residential, and commercial development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Built_environment).[^cite_note-28]

In response to climate disturbance across the country, the fourteenth [five-year plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-year_plans_of_China), endorsed by the National People's Congress in 2021, indicates the general direction and various steps towards a low-carbon transition.[^cite_note-29] On a provincial level, the Jiangsu government aims to achieve an 18% carbon dioxide decrease per unit GDP and accelerate the development of a green, low-carbon economy, as indicated in the 14th five-year development. The province also plans to recover the damaged coastal regions such as [Lianyugang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianyungang) and [Yancheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yancheng), and improve resilience against rising sea level by implementing [seawalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawall) and river [floodgates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodgate).[^cite_note-30]

## Politics

Main articles: [Politics of Jiangsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Jiangsu) and [List of provincial leaders of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial_leaders_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China)

The politics of Jiangsu is structured in a one party (Communist) government system like all other governing institutions in mainland China.

The [Governor of Jiangsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Jiangsu) is the highest-ranking official in the People's Government of Jiangsu. However, in the province's dual party-government governing system, the Governor has less power than the Jiangsu [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) Provincial Committee Secretary, colloquially termed the "Jiangsu [CCP Party Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party_Committee_Secretary)".

### Courts

## Economy



An industrial landscape in Ganjiaxiang, [Qixia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qixia_District), Nanjing

As of 2022, Jiangsu had a gross domestic product (GDP) of [CNY¥](66958.md)12.29 trillion (US\$1.83 trillion),[^cite_note-data2022-2] the [second-highest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) in China after Guangdong. Its GDP is greater than those of Australia and South Korea, which are the world's 12th- and 13th-largest economies respectively.[^cite_note-\:5-50] In 2022, Jiangsu's GDP adjusted by [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) was \$3.04 trillion, making it the [3rd-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_administrative_divisions_by_GRDP) of any country subdivision globally, behind California and Guangdong.[^cite_note-51] Jiangsu's economy in PPP also was just behind that of Italy with a GDP PPP of \$ \$3.05 trillion, the [12th largest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)).[^cite_note-\:5-50]

Jiangsu is very wealthy among the provinces of China. Its 2022 [nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_GDP) per capita reached ¥144,390 (US\$21,467), becoming the first province in China to reach the \$20,000 mark.[^cite_note-\:1-52][^cite_note-\:5-50] Cities like Nanjing, [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou), and [Wuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi) have GDPs per capita around twice the provincial average, making south Jiangsu one of the most prosperous regions in China.

The province has an extensive irrigation system supporting its agriculture, which is based primarily on [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), followed by [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum). Main [cash crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_crop) include [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean), [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed), [sesame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame), [ambary hemp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemp), and [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea). Other products include [peppermint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peppermint), [spearmint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spearmint), [bamboo](39029.md), [medicinal herbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicinal_herbs), [apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple), [pears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pear), [peaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peach), [loquats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loquat), [ginkgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginkgo). [Silkworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silkworm) form an important part of Jiangsu's agriculture, with the [Lake Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tai) region to the south a major base of [silk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk) production in China. Jiangsu is an important producer of freshwater fish and other aquatic products.

Jiangsu has [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [petroleum](23195.md), and [natural gas](22131.md) deposits, but its most significant mineral products are non-metal minerals such as [halite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halite) (rock salt), [sulfur](27127.md), [phosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus), and [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble). The city of Xuzhou is a coal hub of China. The salt mines of [Huaiyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaiyin_District,_Huai'an) have more than 0.4 trillion [tonnes](31185.md) of deposits, one of the greatest collections of deposits in China.

Jiangsu is historically oriented toward light industries such as [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textiles) and [food industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_industry). Since 1949, Jiangsu has developed heavy industries such as [chemical industry](58721.md) and [construction materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_material). Jiangsu's important industries include machinery, electronic, chemicals, and automobile.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective1-53][^cite_note-54] The government has worked hard to promote the solar industry and hoped by 2012 the solar industry would be worth 100 billion RMB.[^cite_note-55] Jiangsu's economy growth has directly benefited from the reform Chinese's policies, and its growth trajectory reflects that of many other coastal provinces, such as Zhejiang and Shandong.[^cite_note-56] The [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up) of [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) have greatly benefited southern cities, especially [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou) and [Wuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi), which outstrip the provincial capital, [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing), in total output. In the eastern outskirts of Suzhou, [Singapore](27318.md) has built the [Suzhou Industrial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou_Industrial_Park), a flagship of Sino-Singaporean cooperation and the only industrial park in China that is in its entirety the investment of a single foreign country.

Jiangsu contains over 100 different economic and technological development zones devoted to different types of investments.[^cite_note-RightSite.Asia-57]

## Transportation

### Air

[Nanjing Lukou International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Lukou_International_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **NKG**) serves as the major airport in the province, with flights to Tokyo, Osaka, Hong Kong, Seoul-Incheon, Frankfurt, Bangkok, Milan, Vancouver and Los Angeles. Other passenger airports include [Sunan Shuofang International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunan_Shuofang_International_Airport), [Changzhou Benniu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changzhou_Benniu_Airport), [Yangzhou Taizhou International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzhou_Taizhou_International_Airport), and [Nantong Xingdong Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantong_Xingdong_Airport). Air traffic in the populated [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou) area is often diverted to [Shanghai Hongqiao International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Hongqiao_International_Airport), to which Suzhou is conveniently connected to via bus services and by expressway.

[Xuzhou Guanyin International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuzhou_Guanyin_International_Airport), [Yancheng Nanyang International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yancheng_Nanyang_International_Airport), and [Lianyungang Baitabu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianyungang_Baitabu_Airport) serve as hubs in northern Jiangsu.

### Rail

Main article: [Railways in Jiangsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_in_Jiangsu)

The southern part of the province, namely the Shanghai-Nanjing corridor, has very high-frequency rail services. Jiangsu is on the [Jinghu railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinghu_railway) from [Beijing](18603746.md) to [Shanghai](27643.md), as well as the high speed line between the two cities: [Shanghai–Nanjing intercity railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai%E2%80%93Nanjing_intercity_railway) and [Beijing-Shanghai high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing-Shanghai_high-speed_railway), completed in 2010 and 2011, respectively. Since the completion of the Beijing-Shanghai high-speed line, travel time between Beijing and Nanjing has been reduced to approximately four hours (from eleven hours previously); travel time between Nanjing and Shanghai on the fastest high-speed trains takes just over an hour.

As of 2022, all major cities in Jiangsu have been connected by high-speed lines, including: [Shanghai-Nanjing intercity railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai-Nanjing_intercity_railway) since 2010, [Beijing-Shanghai high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing-Shanghai_high-speed_railway) since 2011, [Nanjing–Hangzhou high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing%E2%80%93Hangzhou_high-speed_railway) since 2013, [Nanjing–Anqing intercity railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing%E2%80%93Anqing_intercity_railway) since 2015, [Lianyungang–Zhenjiang high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianyungang%E2%80%93Zhenjiang_high-speed_railway) since 2020, [Xuzhou–Yancheng high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuzhou%E2%80%93Yancheng_high-speed_railway) since 2019, [Yancheng–Nantong high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yancheng%E2%80%93Nantong_high-speed_railway) since 2020, [Shanghai–Suzhou–Nantong railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai%E2%80%93Suzhou%E2%80%93Nantong_railway) since 2020, and [Lianyungang–Xuzhou high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianyungang%E2%80%93Xuzhou_high-speed_railway) since 2021.

### Road

Jiangsu's road network is one of the most developed in the country.[^cite_note-72] The [Beijing–Shanghai Expressway (G2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G2_Beijing%E2%80%93Shanghai_Expressway) enters the province from the north and passes through Huai'an, Yangzhou, Taizhou, and Wuxi on the way to Shanghai; traveling from Shanghai westbound, the G2 forks at [Wuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi) and continues onto Nanjing separately as G42, the [Shanghai–Nanjing Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai%E2%80%93Nanjing_Expressway), which serves the widely traveled southern corridor of the province. The [Ningchang Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningchang_Expressway?action=edit\&redlink=1) links Nanjing with [Changzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changzhou). The [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou) area is extensively networked with expressways, going in all directions. The [Yanhai Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanhai_Expressway?action=edit\&redlink=1) links the coastal regions of the province, passing through [Nantong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantong), [Yancheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yancheng), and [Lianyungang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianyungang).

Historically, the province was divided by the Yangtze River into northern and southern regions. The first bridge across the river in Jiangsu, the [Nanjing Yangtze River Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Yangtze_River_Bridge), was completed in 1968 during the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution). The second bridge crossing, [Jiangyin Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangyin_Suspension_Bridge), opened 30 years later at [Jiangyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangyin). As of October 2014, there were 11 [cross-Yangtze bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_bridges_and_tunnels) in the province, including the five in Nanjing, which also has two cross-river tunnels. The Jiangyin Bridge (1,385 m (4,544 ft)), [Runyang Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runyang_Bridge) (opened in 2005, connecting Yangzhou and Zhenjiang, 1,490 m (4,890 ft)), and [Fourth Nanjing Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Nanjing_Yangtze_Bridge) (opened in 2012; 1,418 m (4,652 ft)) all rank among the ten [longest suspension bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_longest_suspension_bridge_spans) in the world. The [Sutong Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutong_Bridge), opened in 2008, connecting Nantong and Changshu, has one of the [longest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_longest_cable-stayed_bridge_spans) [cable-stayed bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable-stayed_bridge) spans in the world, at 1,088 m (3,570 ft).

### Metro (subway)

As of December 2022, Jiangsu has six cities that have operational subway systems, together with an extra city ([Huai'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai'an)) currently under construction. These six cities are [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing), [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou), [Wuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi), [Changzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changzhou), [Xuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuzhou) and [Nantong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantong).

The [Nanjing Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Metro) was opened in September 2005. It was the sixth city in mainland China that opened up a metro system. As of December 2019 the city currently has 11 metro lines ([Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Nanjing_Metro\)), [Line 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2_\(Nanjing_Metro\)), [Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3_\(Nanjing_Metro\)), [Line 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_4_\(Nanjing_Metro\)), [Line 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_10_\(Nanjing_Metro\)), [Line S1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_S1_\(Nanjing_Metro\)), [Line S3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_S3_\(Nanjing_Metro\)), [Line S6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_S6_\(Nanjing_Metro\)), [Line S7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_S7_\(Nanjing_Metro\)), [Line S8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_S8_\(Nanjing_Metro\)) and [Line S9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_S9_\(Nanjing_Metro\))), with several extra ones (i.e. [Line 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_5_\(Nanjing_Metro\))) under construction.

The [Suzhou Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou_Metro) was opened in April 2012. As of May 2025, it currently has nine operational lines: [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Suzhou_Metro\)), [Line 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2_\(Suzhou_Metro\)), [Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3_\(Suzhou_Metro\)), [Line 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_4_\(Suzhou_Metro\)), [Line 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_5_\(Suzhou_Metro\)), [Line 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_6_\(Suzhou_Metro\)), [Line 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_7_\(Suzhou_Metro\)), [Line 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_8_\(Suzhou_Metro\)), and [Line 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_11_\(Suzhou_Metro\)), one under construction ([Line 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_10_\(Suzhou_Metro\))), and at least 2 under planning ([Line 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_9_\(Suzhou_Metro\)) and [Line S4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_S4_\(Suzhou_Metro\))). Planned lines are expected to be operational by 2035.

The [Wuxi Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi_Metro) was opened in July 2014. The system is currently composed of four operational lines by 2022: [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Wuxi_Metro\)), [Line 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2_\(Wuxi_Metro\)), [Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3_\(Wuxi_Metro\)) and [Line 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_4_\(Wuxi_Metro\)). It also has two other lines under construction: [Line S1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_S1_\(Wuxi_Metro\)) and an extension of Line 4.

The [Changzhou Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changzhou_Metro) was opened in September 2019. The system currently only has two lines operational, [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Changzhou_Metro\)) and [Line 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2_\(Changzhou_Metro\)).

The [Xuzhou Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuzhou_Metro) was opened in September 2019, a few days after the Changzhou Metro started operations. The system currently only has three lines operational, [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Xuzhou_Metro\)), [Line 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2_\(Xuzhou_Metro\)) and [Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3_\(Xuzhou_Metro\)).

The [Nantong Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantong_Metro) was opened in November 2022. It has one operating line: [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Nantong_Metro\)) and another line under construction: [Line 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_2_\(Nantong_Metro\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-73]

The [Huai'an Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai'an_Metro?action=edit\&redlink=1), also known as the Huai'an Rail System, began construction in November 2018. There are seven lines planned: Line 1, Line 2, Line 3, Line 4, Line 5, Line S1, and Line S2. It is expected to start operations before 2025.

## Culture

The four mass migrations in the 4th, 8th, 12th and 14th centuries had been influential in shaping the regional culture of Jiangsu. According to dialects and the other factors, the province can be roughly segmented four major cultural subdivisions: [Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Chinese) (吴), Jinling (金陵), [Huaiyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinitic_languages#Huai) (淮扬) and [Xuhuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinitic_languages#Central_Plains_and_Lanyin_Mandarin) (徐淮), from southeast to northwest.[^cite_note-74] The belts of transition blurred the boundaries.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-\:0-76][^cite_note-77]

The Humble Administrator's Garden, one of the classical gardens of [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou)

Jiangsu is rich in cultural traditions. *[Kunqu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunqu)*, originating in [Kunshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunshan), is one of the most renowned and prestigious forms of [Chinese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_opera).[^cite_note-78] *[Pingtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingtan_\(artform\))*, a form of storytelling accompanied by music, is also popular: it can be subdivided into types by origin: [Suzhou Pingtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou_Pingtan) (of [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou)), [Yangzhou Pingtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzhou_Pingtan?action=edit\&redlink=1) (of [Yangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzhou)), and [Nanjing Pingtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Pingtan?action=edit\&redlink=1) (of [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing)). [Wuxi opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi_opera), a form of traditional Chinese opera, is popular in [Wuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi), while [Huaiju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaiju?action=edit\&redlink=1) is popular further north, around [Yancheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yancheng). [Jiangsu cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangsu_cuisine) is one of the eight great traditions of the [cuisine of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_China).



Kunqu

Suzhou is also well known for its silk, [Chinese embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_embroidery), [jasmine tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_tea), stone bridges, [pagodas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagodas), and classical gardens. Nearby [Yixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yixing) is noted for its [teaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaware) while Yangzhou is known for its [lacquerware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacquerware) and [jadeware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadeware). [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing)'s *[yunjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunjin)* is a noted type of woven silk.

Since ancient times, south Jiangsu has been famed for its prosperity and opulence, and simply inserting south Jiangsu place names (Suzhou, Yangzhou, etc.) into poetry gave an effect of dreaminess, as was indeed done by many famous poets. In particular, the fame of Suzhou (as well as [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou) in neighbouring [Zhejiang](96602.md)) has led to the popular saying: 上有天堂，下有蘇杭 ("above there is heaven; below there are Suzhou and Hangzhou"), a saying that continues to be a source of pride for the people of these two still prosperous cities. Similarly, the prosperity of Yangzhou has led poets to dream of: 腰纏十萬貫，騎鶴下揚州 ("with a hundred thousand [strings of coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Chinese_currency) wrapped around its waist, a [crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crane_\(bird\)) landed in Yangzhou").

## Education and research

### Higher education

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of universities and colleges in Jiangsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Jiangsu) and [Project 211](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_211).

As of 2022, Jiangsu hosts 168 institutions of higher education, ranking first of all Chinese provinces.[^cite_note-\:02-79] There are two [Project 985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_985), 11 [Project 211](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_211), and 16 [Double First-Class Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_First-Class_Construction) universities in the province. A combination of 93 members of [Chinese Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Academy_of_Sciences) and [Chinese Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Academy_of_Engineering) work in Jiangsu.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-\:2-81][^cite_note-\:3-14]

As of 2025, six major cities in Jiangsu are ranked among the world's top 200 cities for scientific research output, according to the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index). These cities include Nanjing (5th), Suzhou (33rd), Zhenjiang (98th), Yangzhou (118th), Wuxi (161st), and Changzhou (173rd).[^cite_note-\:3-14] Notably, Jiangsu is the only province in China with at least five major cities achieving this distinction.[^cite_note-\:3-14]

## Tourism

[Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) was the capital of several Chinese dynasties and contains a variety of historic sites, such as the [Purple Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Mountain_\(Nanjing\)), [Purple Mountain Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Mountain_Observatory), the [Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen_Mausoleum), [Ming dynasty city wall and gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Wall_of_Nanjing), [Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_Xiaoling_Mausoleum) (the mausoleum of the first Ming Emperor, [Hongwu Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongwu_Emperor)), [Xuanwu Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanwu_Lake), [Jiming Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiming_Temple), the [Nanjing Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Massacre) Memorial, [Nanjing Confucius Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Fuzimiao), [Nanjing Yangtze River Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Yangtze_River_Bridge), and the [Nanjing Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Zoo), along with its circus. [Suzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou) is renowned for its classical gardens (designated as a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md)), as well as the [Hanshan Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanshan_Temple), and [Huqiu Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huqiu_Tower). Nearby is the water-town of [Zhouzhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhouzhuang,_Kunshan), an international tourist destination with Venice-like waterways, bridges and dwellings, which have been preserved over centuries. [Yangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzhou) is known for [Slender West Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender_West_Lake). [Wuxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxi) is known for being the home of the world's tallest Buddha statue. In the north, [Xuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuzhou) is designated as one of China's "eminent historical cities". The official travel and tourism website for Jiangsu[^cite_note-82] was set up in 2008.
