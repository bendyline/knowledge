**Qalqilya** or **Qalqiliya** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): قلقيلية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Qalqīlyaḧ*) is a city in the [West Bank](33209.md), [Palestine](241405.md), which serves as the administrative center of the [Qalqilya Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalqilya_Governorate). The city had a population of 51,683 in 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1] Qalqilya is surrounded by the [Israeli West Bank wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier), with a narrow gap in the east controlled by the Israeli military and a tunnel to the Palestinian town of [Hableh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hableh).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Qalqilya is under the administration of the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) (as part of [Area A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves)), while remaining under [Israeli military occupation](59076253.md).



Qalqilya

According to [Edward Henry Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Henry_Palmer), the name came from "a type of [pomegranate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate)", or "gurgling of water".[^cite_note-Palmer183-4] Qalqilya was known as *Calecailes* in the Roman period, and *Calcelie* in the Frankish sources from the early Medieval times.[^cite_note-5] The word "Qalqilya" might be derived from a [Canaanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan) term which means "rounded stones or hills".[^cite_note-6]

## History

The vicinity of Qalqilya has been populated since [prehistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric) times, as attested to by the discovery of prehistoric [flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint) tools.[^cite_note-ARIJEnviro-7]

### Ottoman period

Qalqilya appeared in [Ottoman](22278.md) [tax registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter) (transliterated as *Qalqili*) in 1596, as a village in the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* (subdistrict) of Bani Sa'b in the *[Liwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_\(Arabic\))* of [Nablus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Sanjak). It paid a total of 3,910 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e) in taxes on wheat, barley, summer crops, olives, and goats or beehives.[^cite_note-hutteroth-8]

Modern qalqilya began as a daughter village of [Baqa al-Hatab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baqat_al-Hatab) in the West Bank.[^cite_note-9]

[Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) described *Kulakilieh* in 1838 as a village in *Beni Sa'ab* district, west of Nablus.[^cite_note-10] An Ottoman census listed the village in the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiyah)* (sub-district) of Bani Sa'b, in 1288 [AH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar) (1871/1872 [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era)).[^cite_note-11]

Qalqilya was described in 1882 as "a large somewhat straggling village, with [cisterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern) to the north and a pool on the south-west. The houses are badly built."[^cite_note-SWPIII-12] Residents from nearby [Baqat al-Hatab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baqat_al-Hatab) move to the city in 1883, and a municipal council to administer Qalqilya was established in 1909.[^cite_note-ARIJProfile-13]

### British Mandate period



Qalqilya 1927

An official land survey recorded 27,915 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of land owned in 1945.[^cite_note-14] Of this, 3701 dunams were for citrus and bananas, 3,232 were plantations and irrigable land, 16,197 used for cereals,[^cite_note-15] while 273 dunams were built-up (urban) land.[^cite_note-16]

According to [Sami Hadawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_Hadawi), the town had been "one of the most prosperous in Palestine, owning extensive orange groves and serving as one of the main vegetable markets of the country."[^cite_note-\:0-17]

### 1948 War

During the war, many inhabitants from [Kafr Saba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Saba), [Abu Kishk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Kishk), [Miska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miska,_Tulkarm), [Biyar 'Adas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biyar_'Adas) and [Shaykh Muwannis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaykh_Muwannis) resettled in Qalqilya.[^cite_note-18] Residents of Qalqilya who left during the fighting returned with the arrival of the [Jordanian Arab Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_Arab_Legion) and the Iraqi expeditionary force, apart from 2,000 upper-class residents who settled in [Nablus](233369.md) for economic reasons.[^cite_note-19] Hadawi argues that the [armistice lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements#With_Jordan) established in 1949 "severed all \[Qalqilya's] orange groves in favour of Israel," leaving the town "landless except for its rocky areas towards the east."[^cite_note-\:0-17]

### Jordanian occupation

After the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), the area was [occupied by Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) in 1950. On the night of 10 October 1956, the [Israeli army](39237.md) launched a raid against Qalqilya police station in response to a Jordanian attack on Israeli bus,[^cite_note-20] among other incidents.[^cite_note-21] The attack was ordered by [Moshe Dayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Dayan) and involved several thousand soldiers. During the fighting, a paratroop company was surrounded by Jordanian troops and escaped under close air-cover from four [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) aircraft. Eighteen Israelis and 70 to 90 Jordanians were killed in the operation.[^cite_note-22]

### Post 1967

In the wake of the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Qalqilya came under [Israeli rule](59076253.md). Dozens of inhabitants were expelled to [Jordan](7515964.md) as part of the Palestinian expulsions of 1967 known as the [Naksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naksa). 850 buildings were razed.[^cite_note-23] After the IDF's psychological warfare unit made a visit to the city and many of the residents had fled, the UN representative Nils-Göran Gussing noted that 850 of the town's 2,000 houses were demolished.[^cite_note-24] In his memoirs, [Moshe Dayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Dayan) wrote that these actions ultimately constituted "[collective punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_punishment)" which was contrary to government policy.[^cite_note-25] The villagers were eventually allowed to return and the reconstruction of damaged houses was financed by the military authorities.[^cite_note-26]

As part of the 1993 [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) between Israel and the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO), control of Qalqilya was transferred to the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) (PNA) on 17 December 1995.[^cite_note-27]

In 2003, the [Israeli West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier) was built, encircling the town and separating it from agricultural lands on the other side of the wall.[^cite_note-28]

In November 2015, Israel arrested what it alleged to be a network of 24 [Hamas](13913.md) militants active in the city.[^cite_note-29]

On 20 October 2017, the municipality of Qalqilya named a street after [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) and erected a memorial with his likeness. The monument was unveiled at a ceremony attended by the Qalqilya District Governor Rafi Rawajba and two other Palestinian officials. It bears the slogan "Saddam Hussein – The Master of the Martyrs in Our Age".[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-AP17-31][^cite_note-AG17-32]

On 19 June 2022, a 53-year-old Palestinian was shot and killed by Israeli forces as he sought to cross the [Israeli West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier), which encircles Qalqilya.[^cite_note-33] According to the IDF, he damaged the security fence in an attempt to cross into Israeli territory. It's unclear whether he was armed.

On 22 June 2024, a 60-year-old Israeli citizen was shot dead in Qalqilya by local residents. The victim, from Petah Tikva, regularly bought vegetables in the city. Following the shooting, his car was set on fire, and his personal documents were taken. This incident was the third in Qalqilya within 48 hours, following another attack on Thursday, where a 70-year-old Israeli was killed,[^cite_note-34] and the elimination of two [Islamic Jihad](13093253.md) operatives who planned an attack.[^cite_note-35]

## Geography



2018 [United Nations](1602284.md) map showing the [West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_barrier)

Qalqilya is located in the northwestern [West Bank](33209.md), straddling the [border with Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Israel\)). It is 16 kilometers southwest of the Palestinian city of [Tulkarm](851834.md), and the nearest localities are the Arab-Israeli city of [Tira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tira,_Israel) and the Palestinian hamlet of 'Arab al-Ramadin al-Shamali to the northeast, the Palestinian village of [Nabi Ilyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Ilyas) to the east, the Palestinian hamlets 'Arab Abu Farda and 'Arab ar-Ramadin al-Janubi and the [Israeli settlement](15123.md) of [Alfei Menashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfei_Menashe) to the southwest, and the Palestinian village [Habla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habla) and Arab-Israeli town of [Jaljuliya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaljuliya) to the south.[^cite_note-ARIJProfile-13] It contains the point in the West Bank closest to the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), with about 14 km to the coast at [Shefayim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shefayim).

The average annual rainfall 587.4 millimeters and the average annual temperature is 19 degrees Celsius.[^cite_note-ARIJProfile-13]

## Demographics



A Palestinian girl in Qalqilya in the West Bank

It had a population of 13 Muslim households, according to an Ottoman census in 1596.[^cite_note-hutteroth-8]

[Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) found a population of 200 in 1870.[^cite_note-36]

The [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine) conducted a [census in 1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine), which found that Qalqilya had a population of 2,803 (2,794 Muslims and 9 Christians),.[^cite_note-Census1922-37] The population was 3,867 (3,855 Muslims and 12 Christians), in a total of 796 houses, according to the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine).[^cite_note-Census1931-38] The population was listed at 4,503 (including 4 Jews) in the 1938 census.[^cite_note-39] The population of Qalqilya was 5,850 (5,840 Muslims and 10 Christians, according to the [1945 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945).[^cite_note-1945p21-40])

The population of Qalqilya was 11,401, according to a Jordanian census in 1961.[^cite_note-41] A census in September 1967 found 8,922 persons, of whom 1,837 were originally from Israeli territory.[^cite_note-42]

The [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS) census recorded Qalqilya's population to be 22,168 in 1997. The majority of the inhabitants (69.8%) were Palestinian refugees or their descendants.[^cite_note-PCBSRefugees-43] In the 2007 census, Qalqilya's population was 41,739 (50.9% male and (49.1% female)).[^cite_note-PCBS_Census_2007-44] The number of registered households was 7,866.[^cite_note-ARIJProfile-13] In the 2017 census, Qalqiliya's population was 51,683.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1]

## Government

[Hamas](13913.md) won the 2006 municipal elections in Qalqiliya and one of its members, Wajih Qawas, became mayor, although he was incarcerated by Israel for much of his term. On 12 September 2009, the PNA dismissed Qawas for allowing Qalqiliya's debt to grow unchecked, failing to attract international funding for city projects and ignoring orders by the Palestinian government. Qawas, however, viewed his dismissal as a result of the ongoing feud between Hamas, which dominates the PNA in the Gaza Strip and [Fatah](11104.md), which dominates the PNA in the West Bank.[^cite_note-bbc-45] Human rights groups criticized Qawas's dismissal, condemning the intervention by the central Palestinian authorities in the affairs of an elected official.[^cite_note-bbc-45] During the 2012 municipal elections, [Fatah](11104.md) member Othman Dawood was elected mayor.[^cite_note-46]

## Economy



A Palestinian farmer near Qalqilya

Between 1967 and 1995 almost 80 percent of Qalqilya's labor force worked for Israeli companies or industries in the construction and agriculture sectors. The remaining 20% engaged in trade and commerce, marketing across the Green Line. According to a field survey taken by the [Applied Research Institute-Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Research_Institute-Jerusalem) (ARIJ), 45% of Qalqilya's working population was employed by government, 25% worked in agriculture, 15% worked in trade and commerce, 10% worked in industry and 5% worked in Israeli labor. In 2012, the unemployment rate was 22%, with those most affected formerly employed in agriculture, trade and services.

Qalqilya is particularly known for its citrus crop and of its total of 10,252 dunams of land, (of which 5,930 are arable) 1802 dunams (about 17.6% of the city's land and over 30% of its arable land) are planted with citrus trees. Other major crops are olives and vegetables.[^cite_note-ARIJProfile-13] Local industries include the manufacture of foodstuffs, olive oil, dairy products, soap, glass, stone, marble and building materials, in addition to the manufacture of wood, and mineral water companies.

The [Qalqilya Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalqilya_Zoo), established in 1986, is currently the only zoo in the [West Bank](33209.md) and in [Palestine](241405.md), and, according to its owner, is the city's single-largest employer. It serves as one of Qalqilya's main attractions. The zoo houses 170 animals and works closely with zoologists from the [Jerusalem Biblical Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Biblical_Zoo) and the [Ramat Gan Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramat_Gan_Safari).[^cite_note-47] The only factory for prosthetic limbs in the northern West Bank is in Qalqilya.

### Land usage and the barrier



Couple near the [West Bank Barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier)

Of the city's total area of 10,252 dunams, 3,027 were built-up areas, 266 were used for industrial purposes, 2,894 were planted with permanent crops, 419 were used for greenhouses, 274 for livestock ranges, 2,343 others were classified as arable, and 283 dunams were occupied by the [West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_barrier). Nearly all of Qalqilya's urban area is under Palestinian civil jurisdiction and Israeli military control (Area B), while 64.7% of the city's municipal territory, mostly agricultural lands and open spaces, is under Israeli civil and military control (Area C).[^cite_note-ARIJProfile-13]

Israel's construction of the barrier began in 2002 and isolates Qalqilya from the north, west, south, and half of its eastern side, leaving a corridor in the east connecting it with smaller Palestinian villages and hamlets.[^cite_note-ARIJProfile-13] Israel states its construction of the wall is for security purposes, particularly to prevent infiltration by Palestinian militants into Israel as had occurred during the [Second Intifada](71717.md). The Palestinians state that the barrier is meant to annex Palestinian lands (since the wall often juts deep into Palestinian territory) and control the movement of Palestinians. The barrier has negatively affected Qalqilya's economy, particularly the commercial and trade sectors, because it has separated the city from nearby Palestinian localities and bordering [Arab towns in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Arab_localities_in_Israel#Central_District), which contributed about 40% of the city's income prior to the barrier's completion. The barrier has also separated 1,836 dunams of mostly agricultural lands and open spaces within Qalqilya's jurisdiction from the city proper. Social relations between Qalqilya's inhabitants and those of other Palestinian cities have also been hindered by the barrier.[^cite_note-ARIJProfile-13]

## Education

According to the 2007 PCBS census, 95.3% of the inhabitants over the age of 10 were literate. About 75% of the illiterate population were women. The town has 21 public schools, four private schools, three schools managed by [UNRWA](32270.md) and 13 kindergartens. All schools are overseen by the Palestinian Ministry of Higher Education. As of 2012, there were 12,286 residents enrolled in school, with 660 teaching staff. In 2007, 10.5% of the population had graduated from an institution of higher education, while 15.7% had completed secondary education, 27.5% preparatory education, 27.4% elementary education and 13.8% had no formal education. There are two colleges in the city: the Ad Da'wa Islamic College established in 1978 and a campus of the [Al-Quds Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quds_Open_University) established in 1998.[^cite_note-ARIJProfile-13]

## Culture



Palestinian boys practising [dabke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabke) folk dancing in Qalqilya

There are charities, recreational and cultural clubs, Qalqilya TV, and a radio station (Radio Nagham). In the Qalqilya governorate, the [NGO](46539.md) Cultural Forum Society (جمعية منتدى المثقفين الخيرية)[^cite_note-48] played a role in publicizing the residents' economic and political problems due to the occupation, the wall, and settlements.
