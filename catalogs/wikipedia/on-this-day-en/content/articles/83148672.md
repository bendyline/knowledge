The **2026 EFL Championship play-off final** was an [association football](10568.md) match that was played on 23 May 2026 at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md), London, between [Hull City](451358.md) and [Middlesbrough](298603.md). The match determined the third and final team to be [promoted](648049.md) from the [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship), the second tier of [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system), to next season's [Premier League](11250.md). The top two teams of the [2025–26 EFL Championship](79604105.md), [Coventry City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry_City_F.C.) and [Ipswich Town](352843.md), gained automatic promotion to the Premier League, while the clubs placed from third to sixth in the table took part in the [play-off semi-finals](81509303.md#Championship_semi-finals). Hull and Middlesbrough finished sixth and fifth in the regular season respectively. The match was overshadowed by [Southampton](81576.md), who defeated Middlesbrough in the semi-final, spying on Middlesbrough's training sessions in what was called the [*Spygate* scandal](83228613.md). Southampton were expelled from the final and replaced by Middlesbrough; [Millwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_F.C.) were the other losing semi-finalist. Winning the match was estimated to be worth an extra £200 million through increased broadcast, matchday and sponsorship revenue in the Premier League.&#x20;

The match, which was [refereed](501260.md) by [Jarred Gillett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarred_Gillett), was played on a hot day in front of a crowd of 84,506. After remaining goalless for the first half and most of the second half, [Yū Hirakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%AB_Hirakawa) delivered a [cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_\(association_football\)) that Middlesbrough goalkeeper [Sol Brynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sol_Brynn) spilled to Hull's [Oli McBurnie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oli_McBurnie), allowing him to score the only goal of the match in the fifth minute of second-half [injury time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injury_time). The match ended 1–0 to Hull, who won promotion back to the Premier League for the first time since 2017. There was criticism of the trophy ceremony broadcast after the camera angle was changed as Hirakawa was about to lift the trophy.

The top two teams of the [2025–26 EFL Championship](79604105.md) – [Coventry City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry_City_F.C.) and [Ipswich Town](352843.md) – were automatically [promoted](648049.md) for the [following Premier League season](82491768.md).[^cite_note-1] The next four placed teams – [Millwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwall_F.C.), [Southampton](81576.md), [Middlesbrough](298603.md) and [Hull City](451358.md) – participated in [the play-off knockout tournament](81509303.md#Championship). The semi-finals were [two-legged ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-legged_tie) with each team playing the other once at home and once away. The aggregate winners proceeded to the single-legged final at the [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md) to decide the third and final team to be promoted.[^cite_note-2]

### Hull City

Further information: [2025–26 Hull City A.F.C. season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Hull_City_A.F.C._season)

MKM Stadium

The [MKM Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKM_Stadium), Hull City's home stadium and host of the first leg

Hull City qualified for the play-off competition on the final day of the regular season, beating [Norwich City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich_City_F.C.) 2−1 after coming from behind, edging seventh-placed [Wrexham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrexham_A.F.C.), who drew with Middlesbrough 2−2,[^cite_note-Wrexham-v-Boro-3] by two points.[^cite_note-4] In contrast, Millwall were already guaranteed a play-off place prior to the final day and could have been automatically promoted had they bettered Ipswich's result against [Queens Park Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_Park_Rangers_F.C.). Ipswich won 3−0 against QPR, denying Millwall automatic promotion and forcing them into the play-offs despite their 2−0 win against [Oxford United](451165.md).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

The first leg, which took place at Hull's [MKM Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKM_Stadium) on 8 May, ended goalless. [Mohamed Belloumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Belloumi) hit the post for Hull in the first two minutes. In the final 20 minutes, Millwall's [Camiel Neghli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camiel_Neghli) also hit the post. [Ryan Leonard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Leonard_\(footballer\))'s goal for Millwall was disallowed towards the end for a foul by [Tristan Crama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_Crama) on [Charlie Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hughes_\(footballer,_born_2003\)).[^cite_note-7] The reverse leg took place at [The Den](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Den) three days later, which ended 2–0 to Hull. Belloumi cut inside to curl the opener off the far post in the 64th minute, followed by [Joe Gelhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Gelhardt) firing low through the grasp of [Anthony Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Patterson) 15 minutes later. Hull won 2–0 on aggregate, winning a place in the final.[^cite_note-8]

### Middlesbrough

Further information: [Southampton F.C. espionage incident](83228613.md) and [2025–26 Middlesbrough F.C. season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Middlesbrough_F.C._season)

Although Middlesbrough were first in the [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) as late as February, they finished the regular season in fifth place after a seven-match winless run in March and April,[^cite_note-Wrexham-v-Boro-3] four points behind Ipswich, who occupied the last automatic promotion place. Relegated from the Premier League [the previous season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Premier_League), Southampton had finished the season with a unbeaten run of 19 league games, finishing fourth ahead of Middlesbrough on [goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference), resulting in a semi-final meeting.[^cite_note-9]

Rockliffe Park

[Rockliffe Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockliffe_Park), Middlesbrough's training ground and the location of espionage

Two days before the first leg, a man wearing earphones, later identified as Southampton analyst intern William Salt,[^cite_note-10] was seen by Middlesbrough club staff using a smartphone to film a training session at Middlesbrough's training ground [Rockliffe Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockliffe_Park). Middlesbrough subsequently reported the incident to the [English Football League](14419588.md) (EFL). Southampton were charged the next day with breaking EFL Regulations 3.4, requiring clubs to act towards other clubs and the EFL "with the utmost good faith", and 127.1, which prohibited clubs from observing an opponent's training session in the 72 hours before a match.[^cite_note-Spygate-11][^cite_note-12] The incident was dubbed *Spygate* by the English media.[^cite_note-Spygate-11]

The first leg took place at Middlesbrough's [Riverside Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_Stadium) on 9 May, with the [BBC](19344654.md)'s Adam Lanigan describing the match as a "fascinating" one that "somehow" ended goalless. Although Middlesbrough dominated the first half having taken 17 shots to Southampton's none, with 76.1% possession, they failed to score with [Tommy Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Conway) hitting the inside of the post. Southampton improved in the second-half, with [Taylor Harwood-Bellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Harwood-Bellis) heading an effort against the crossbar, but the game finished goalless.[^cite_note-13] Southampton won the reverse leg 2–1 four days later at [St Mary's Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Stadium). [Ross Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Stewart_\(footballer,_born_1996\))'s header for Southampton just before half-time cancelled [Riley McGree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riley_McGree)'s goal in the fifth minute; Southampton scored in [extra time](633242.md) when [Shea Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shea_Charles)'s cross from the right wing crept in at the far post. This gave them a 2–1 aggregate lead which sent Southampton through to the final, prior to the outcome of the disciplinary proceedings in relation to the espionage incident.[^cite_note-14] Despite their defeat, Middlesbrough players returned to training at Rockliffe Park several days later, in the event they advanced to the final instead of Southampton.[^cite_note-Spygate-11]

A hearing with the EFL Independent Disciplinary Commission took place on 19 May, four days before the final. On that evening, Southampton admitted to conducting espionage against Middlesbrough, and also admitted and were charged with espionage in two regular season matches. The hearing concluded with Southampton expelled from the play-offs and Middlesbrough advancing in their place. Southampton were also deducted four points for the [following Championship season](82934740.md). The next day, Southampton appealed against the ruling; the appeal was rejected later that day.[^cite_note-Outcome-15][^cite_note-16]

## Match

### Background

Hull City had won both their previous Championship play-off finals 1−0, beating [Bristol City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_City_F.C.) in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Football_League_Championship_play-off_final) and [Sheffield Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Wednesday_F.C.) in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Football_League_Championship_play-off_final).[^cite_note-17] Their only other play-off campaign had been in 2001, losing to [Leyton Orient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyton_Orient_F.C.) 2−1 on aggregate in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-18] Middlesbrough had contested five second-tier play-off campaigns, but had only been promoted once through the play-offs, beating [Chelsea](7473.md) in 1988 over two legs. Since then, they had reached the final once, losing 2−0 to [Norwich City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich_City_F.C.) in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Football_League_Championship_play-off_final);[^cite_note-19] in their last campaign, in 2023, they lost to [Coventry City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry_City_F.C.) 1−0 in the semi-final. Middlesbrough have never won a game at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md);[^cite_note-bbcreport-20] Hull City were restricted from signing players for a fee for two [transfer windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_window) after defaulting on payment to other clubs.[^cite_note-21] Both teams last played in the top-flight in the [2016–17 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Premier_League).[^cite_note-playoff-info-22] In the regular season, the away team had won the meeting between the two sides. Middlesbrough won the meeting at Hull 4–1 on 5 December 2025, scoring four goals before half-time;[^cite_note-23] the reverse fixture 24 days later ended 1–0 to Hull City.[^cite_note-24] [Oli McBurnie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oli_McBurnie) was Hull's top scorer with 17 goals, while Middlesbrough's top marksman was [Morgan Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Whittaker) with 14.[^cite_note-playoff-info-22][^cite_note-bbcreport-20] Winning the match was estimated to be worth about £200 million from matchday and broadcast revenue and sponsorships in the [Premier League](11250.md).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

Despite the uncertainty around Southampton's participation due to their espionage incident, ticket sales commenced on 15 May with Hull and Southampton receiving an initial allocation of 35,984 tickets,[^cite_note-27] later increased by 1,620 for Southampton and 2,000 for Hull;[^cite_note-28] the EFL were working on the basis that the final would proceed with Southampton, while having contingency options in place "for all potential scenarios".[^cite_note-Spygate2-29] Southampton had already sold out their allocation by the morning of 19 May: these tickets were refunded after their expulsion.[^cite_note-30] Tickets went on sale for Middlesbrough fans on 20 May;[^cite_note-31] they sold out their allocation of 35,984 tickets two days later.[^cite_note-32] After Southampton's expulsion, the EFL announced that the final would go ahead as scheduled on 23 May at a kick-off time of 15\:30 [BST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Summer_Time), instead of 16\:30 as originally scheduled.[^cite_note-33] Hull City owner [Acun Ilicali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acun_Ilicali) threatened to take legal action against the EFL should they lose the match, describing the reinstatement of Middlesbrough as "unbelievable", and suggested that either Hull were made play-off winners or Wrexham to play a semi-final against Middlesbrough.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-guardianrep-36] In response, EFL chairman [Rick Parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Parry) was confident the result would stand.[^cite_note-guardianlive3-37]

The referee for the final was [Jarred Gillett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarred_Gillett) of Australia. Usually a Premier League referee, he had refereed one Championship game earlier in the season, Middlesbrough's 2–2 draw at [Ipswich Town](352843.md), awarding Ipswich a controversial penalty while Middlesbrough were leading 2–1.[^cite_note-38] Neil Davies and Steven Meredith were the [assistant referees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_referee), with [Michael Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Salisbury) named as the [fourth official](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_official). [Stuart Attwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Attwell) and Constantine Hatzidakis were the [video assistant referee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee) (VAR) and assistant VAR respectively.[^cite_note-bbcreport-20]

Both teams named a starting lineup with one change from their semi-final second legs: [Mohamed Belloumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Belloumi) replaced the injured [Kyle Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Joseph) for Hull, while [Alan Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Browne) replaced [Tommy Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Conway), who was also injured, for Middlesbrough. [Hayden Hackney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayden_Hackney), the Championship player of the season, was named on the bench after a calf injury in March.[^cite_note-guardianlive3-37]

### First half

Middlesbrough kicked off the match in front of a crowd of 84,506 in 30 °C (86 °F) heat.[^cite_note-guardianlive3-37] Both sides started with attacking intent; while competing for possession in the 11th minute, Hull's [Matt Crooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Crooks) stepped over [Alan Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Browne), resulting in Browne's treatment, although no [free kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_kick_\(association_football\)) was given. Middlesbrough dominated after the injury, [Aidan Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidan_Morris) firing a shot wide after winning the ball from 30 yards, followed by [David Strelec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Strelec) heading over Browne's cross after a [corner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corner_kick). By the drinks break that was taken midway through the first half, Middlesbrough controlled 76% of the possession, a pattern that continued after the break. In the 41st minute, Strelec attempted a shot towards the bottom right, but it deflected off his teammate Whittaker. Hull had two chances towards the end of the half, with Belloumi curling a shot wide of the top-left corner in the 42nd minute, while McBurnie's header deflected off [Adilson Malanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adilson_Malanda)'s back and hit the crossbar three minutes later.[^cite_note-guardianrep-36] In the three minutes of [injury time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injury_time), Middlesbrough also came close to scoring: David Strelec dribbled past a man only to shoot just wide of the left hand post from 20 yards. The first half ended goalless,[^cite_note-39] with *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*'s [Scott Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Murray_\(writer\)) attributing the lack of action to "pressure and heat".[^cite_note-guardianlive2-40]

### Second half

Oli McBurnie

[Oli McBurnie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oli_McBurnie) (*pictured in 2023 in his time with [Sheffield United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_United_F.C.)*) scored the only goal of the final.

Three minutes after the restart, Middlesbrough's [captain](633462.md), [Dael Fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dael_Fry), headed the second of two successive corners over the crossbar, followed by Giles' overhit cross going just wide and over the top-right corner.[^cite_note-guardianlive2-40] Both teams had chances in the 61st minute: Whittaker passed to McGree in the middle, whose shot was blocked, followed by Hull's counter-attack which saw Belloumi firing a shot straight at Brynn. The first substitution was made by Hull two minutes later, [Joe Gelhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Gelhardt) replacing [Liam Millar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Millar). Seven minutes later, Middlesbrough made their first change with Hackney making his first appearance in two months, replacing Strelec. [Riley McGree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riley_McGree) injured himself by overstretching his left leg in the 73rd minute, requiring treatment, and was replaced by [Sontje Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sontje_Hansen). Hull substituted Giles and Belloumi for [Yū Hirakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%AB_Hirakawa) and [Cody Drameh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Drameh) at the same time. Gelhardt attempted a [volley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volley_\(association_football\)) in the 82nd minute that went wide of the left-post.[^cite_note-guardianlive1-41][^cite_note-indierep-42]

The match was still goalless when eight minutes of injury time were signalled, with [extra time](633242.md) impending if the match finished as a draw. However, on the fifth minute of injury time, Middlesbrough goalkeeper [Sol Brynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sol_Brynn) parried a cross from the left delivered by substitute Hirakawa straight to McBurnie, allowing him to score his 18th goal of the season from six yards out.[^cite_note-indierep-42][^cite_note-bbcreport-20] Believing he had scored the goal to promote Hull, McBurnie took off his shirt and was [cautioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caution_\(football\)) for his celebration. There were two substitutions from both teams after the goal – Jeremy Sarmiento and Cruz Ibeh replacing Alan Browne and Matt Targett for Middlesbrough, and Regan Slater and Lewie Coyle being replaced by John Lundstram and Paddy McNair. Middlesbrough failed to score from a free kick in the 12th minute of injury time. The match ended with Hull winning 1−0, and they were promoted to the Premier League for the first time since 2017.[^cite_note-guardianlive1-41]

## Post-match

Hull City celebrating in an orange bus

Hull City celebrate their promotion to the [Premier League](11250.md) with a bus parade.

[^cite_note-43]

Hull City manager [Sergej Jakirović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergej_Jakirovi%C4%87) was proud of his team: "with so many problems, everything that we achieved with the Premier League is unbelievable." He added that his side was impacted by the "collateral damage" of the espionage incident. His counterpart, [Kim Hellberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Hellberg), said that "it's been two heartbreaking losses in one week".[^cite_note-44] McBurnie, the match winner, declared "it was written for me to get it", adding "I'm just delighted ... I can't speak highly enough of the lads."[^cite_note-guardianlive1-41] Hull celebrated with a bus parade on 25 May.[^cite_note-45]

### Broadcast controversy

Several South Korean and Japanese news outlets criticised the broadcast of the trophy ceremony after the match, when the live coverage changed camera angles when Japanese winger Hirakawa was about to lift the trophy.[^cite_note-SoccerDigestHirakawa-46] Described as "Asian passing" in South Korean and Japanese media, they reported that Asian players' trophy-lift moments have been repeatedly missed in English and European football broadcasts before switching back to capture the trophy lift.[^cite_note-NewsisHirakawa-47][^cite_note-48]
