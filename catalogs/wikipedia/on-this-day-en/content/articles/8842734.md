The **Indonesian Air Force** ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Tentara Nasional Indonesia Angkatan Udara, (TNI-AU)*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Indonesian National Military Air Force', sometimes shortened as **IDAF / IdAF**) is the [aerial branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) of the [Indonesian National Armed Forces](14650.md). The Indonesian Air Force is headquartered in [Jakarta](16275.md), Indonesia, and is headed by the [Chief of Staff of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_Indonesian_Air_Force) (*Kepala Staf Angkatan Udara* – KSAU or KASAU). Its [order of battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_battle) is split into three Air Operations Commands ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Komando Operasi Udara*). Most of its airbases are located on the island of [Java](69336.md).[^cite_note-SCRAMBLE-4] The Indonesian Air Force also has its ground force unit, called Air Force Quick Reaction Force Corps ([Korpasgat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korpasgat)). The corps is also known as the "Orange Berets" (*Baret Jingga*) due to the distinctive color of their service headgear.

The Indonesian Air Force has 30,100 personnel and equipped with 110 combat aircraft. The inventory includes 33 [F-16 Fighting Falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16_Fighting_Falcons) as the main fighters (from the [United States](3434750.md)) supplemented by five [Su-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-27) and eleven [Su-30MK's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKK) (from [Russia](25391.md)), [Hawk 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_Hawk_200), [KAI T-50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle) and [Embraer EMB 314](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_314_Super_Tucano).[^cite_note-TEMPO191011-5] The Indonesian Air Force also intends to purchase 50 [KF-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KF-X) fighter jets from South Korea.[^cite_note-6] As of 2023, the Indonesian Air Force purchased 42 [Rafale](33731380.md) from France,[^cite_note-7] along with [C-130J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_C-130J_Super_Hercules) transport aircraft and a [Medium-altitude long-endurance unmanned aerial vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-altitude_long-endurance_unmanned_aerial_vehicle).[^cite_note-AA_Rafale_F-15-8]

After Japan announced their surrender on 15 August 1945 at the end of World War II, Indonesian nationalist leader [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) declared [Indonesian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Declaration_of_Independence) on 17 August 1945. Several days later on the 23 August, the Indonesian [People's Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Security_Agency) (*Badan Keamanan Rakyat*) was formed to undertake security duties. The Air Component of this force, *BKR Udara*,[^cite_note-9] was also formed. The air arm would captured and use ex-Japanese planes scattered everywhere, especially in the island of Java.[^cite_note-10] The most numerous of these aircraft were the [Yokosuka K5Y1 "Willow"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokosuka_K5Y) trainers, which was given the nickname *Cureng* by the Indonesians, and they were hastily used to train newly recruited cadets.[^cite_note-11] The new roundel was created simply by painting white on the lower part of the Japanese Hinomaru, reflecting the red and white of the [Indonesian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Indonesia).[^cite_note-12]

In 1945, there was only one Indonesian holding a multi-engine pilot license from the pre-war Dutch Flying School, Flying Officer [Agustinus Adisucipto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agustinus_Adisucipto). Adisucipto flew a *Cureng* with red and white markings from [Maguwo airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguwo_airfield) in [Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogyakarta) on 27 October 1945, becoming the first Indonesian pilot to fly an aircraft after the country's unilateral independence. In December 1945, a flight school was created at Maguwo airfield, commanded by Adisucipto.[^cite_note-Indonesian_Air_Force_1977,_p._26-13] He was assisted by a few Japanese pilots who decided to stay in the country. The People's Security Agency was re-organized in October 1945, and again in January 1946 to form the nascent formal armed forces. This marked the birth of the Indonesian Air Force on 9 April 1946. Commodore [Soerjadi Soerjadarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soerjadi_Soerjadarma), a former bombardier and navigator in the Dutch colonial air force, was appointed as its first chief-of-staff.[^cite_note-14]

Throughout 1946 and early 1947, the Indonesian Air Force carried out liaison duties between cities in Java and Sumatra, and its pilot cadets performed cross-country flights. Training facilities spread across various airfields for technicians, military police, radio operators, and airbase defense were also created during this time.[^cite_note-Indonesian_Air_Force_1977,_p._26-13] On 23 April 1946, three Air Force [Tachikawa Ki-55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachikawa_Ki-55) *Cukiu* transported Indonesian delegation from Yogyakarta to [Jakarta](16275.md) for a negotiation with the Allies on resolving the issue of Allied prisoner-of-war repatriation.[^cite_note-15]

Main article: [Air raids on Semarang–Salatiga–Ambarawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_raids_on_Semarang%E2%80%93Salatiga%E2%80%93Ambarawa)

However, tensions rose as the Dutch tried to re-claim their former colony and launched [an offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Product) on 21 July 1947, destroying most of the Indonesian Air Force planes on the ground. Some planes survived and were hidden in remote bases. On 29 July 1947, three surviving aircraft, comprising two Yokosuka K5Y1 *Cureng* and a [Mitsubishi Ki-51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Ki-51) "Guntei" (the fourth aircraft, a [Nakajima Ki-43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakajima_Ki-43) *Hayabusa* flown by Air Cadet Bambang Saptoadji, should also have been involved in the raid as an escort, but as of when it was launched, the aircraft was not airworthy due to engine troubles)[^cite_note-16] conducted [air raids before dawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_raids_on_Semarang%E2%80%93Salatiga%E2%80%93Ambarawa) on the Dutch Army positions in Semarang, Salatiga and Ambarawa, dropping incendiary bombs. It was the first combat mission of the Indonesian Air Force. Tactically, these raids did not have any effect on the Dutch positions, but psychologically, it was a great success as it proved that the Indonesian Air Force still existed. The Dutch had previously claimed the destruction of Indonesian Air Force in their assault before and did not expected any attack from the sky.[^cite_note-17]

The first airborne mission took place on 17 October 1947, when a fourteen men guerillas led by Major [Tjilik Riwut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tjilik_Riwut) was airdropped by a C-47 around Kotawaringin in southern Kalimantan.[^cite_note-18] Out of the original fourteen, one of them did not jump, another went missing, three were killed in firefight, and the other were captured by Dutch forces, the last of them were captured on 7 December. Although it was an unsuccessful mission, the 17 October was celebrated as the founding day of [Korpasgat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korpasgat), the Air Force's infantry and special forces corps.[^cite_note-19]

### Sukarno era (1950–1967)



B-25 Mitchell bombers of the AURI in the 1950s

Under pressure from the [United Nations](31769.md), the Dutch finally agreed to acknowledge Indonesian independence. Following the 1949 [Round Table Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch-Indonesian_Round_Table_Conference), sovereignty was officially transferred to the [United States of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Indonesia). The Dutch armed forces left (but remained in [West Papua](1432822.md) until 1963), and the aeroplanes were handed over to the Indonesians. These comprised, among others, [North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American) [P-51 Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-51_Mustang), North American [B-25 Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-25_Mitchell), North American [T-6 Texan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-6_Texan), [Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Aircraft_Company) [A-26 Invader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-26_Invader), Douglas [C-47 Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-47_Skytrain) and [Consolidated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_Aircraft) [PBY-5A Catalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBY_Catalina), which served as the main forces of the Indonesian Air Force for the following decade. During this era, Indonesia received its first jet aircraft; De Havilland DH-115 *[Vampire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Vampire)*. It was also during this era that the national roundels were changed to the red and white pentagon (which was supposed to signify Indonesia's national ideology of "Panca Sila", or the "Five Principles", created by Sukarno in 1945).

Political instability meant that the Indonesian Air Force saw action against several regional rebellions in Indonesia such as [PRRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Government_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia), [Permesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permesta), [Darul Islam-Tentara Islam Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Islam_\(Indonesia\)) (DI/TII) and the [Republic of South Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Maluku) separatists.

Several Indonesian pilots scored their first kills, including [Captain Ignatius Dewanto](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignatius%20Dewanto) with his [North American P-51 Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_P-51_Mustang), who in 1958 shot down a Permesta [Douglas B-26 Invader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-26_Invader) over Ambon. Its pilot, [Allen Pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Lawrence_Pope), an American [CIA](5183633.md) agent, was captured and tried in Jakarta, thus revealing the significant involvement of the CIA's "Operation Haik" in the rebellion.

The most famous Indonesian fighter pilot in this era was [Rusmin Nurjadin](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roesmin%20Noerjadin), who became [Chief of Staff of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_Indonesian_Air_Force) from 1966 to 1969. Nurjadin commanded [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21) squadrons in 1962–65 and founded an acrobatic team in 1962 that flew the [MiG-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-17)F/PF *Fresco* over some cities in Indonesia. Small numbers of Indonesian Air Force pilots gained their reputation as aces in this era.



Indonesian Air Force [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21) in the [Yogyakarta Air Force Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirgantara_Mandala_Museum)

The need to prop up to what became [Operation Trikora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Trikora) in [Netherlands New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_New_Guinea) and the rise of the [Communist Party of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partai_Komunis_Indonesia) drew Indonesia closer to the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc). Several Soviet-built aircraft began to arrive in the early 1960s including the [MiG-15UTI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-15UTI) from [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), [MiG-17F/PF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan_MiG-17), [MiG-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-19)S and MiG-21F-13, in addition to [Ilyushin Il-28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-28), [Mil Mi-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-4), [Mil Mi-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-6), [Antonov An-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-12) and [Avia 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avia_14) also from Czechoslovakia. Indonesia also received [Lavochkin La-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavochkin_La-11), as well as [Tupolev Tu-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-2) from China, intended to replace the B-25, but they never reached operational status. These aircraft served along with the remaining American aircraft such as [North American B-25 Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_B-25_Mitchell), Douglas A-26 Invader, [Douglas C-47 Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-47_Skytrain) and North American P-51 Mustang. It was during this period that the Indonesian Air Force became the first Air Force in Southeast Asia which acquired the capability of strategic bombing by acquiring the new [Tupolev Tu-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-16) in 1961, before the acquisition of Ilyushin Il-28 by [Vietnam People's Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_People's_Air_Force). Around 25 Tu-16KS were delivered complete with [AS-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS-1_Kennel) air-surface missiles. One crashed at the end of 1962.[^cite_note-GORDONRIGMAT-20][^cite_note-DJOKOP97-21] To add with all these purchases was the first ever surface to air defence missile operated by Southeast Asians, the Soviet-designed [S-75 Dvina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-75_Dvina), which were acquired in 1961.

This era also marked the last confrontation with the Dutch in Papua, before the Dutch, again under pressure of the United Nations, left in 1963. Several missions of Taiwan-based [Lockheed U-2s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_U-2) from [35th Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Cat_Squadron) flew over [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands) (Moluccas) and reported to Dutch military that there was a strong possibility that the Dutch would lose their air superiority over Papua if they continued the war.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Operasi_Udara_Trikora_2011-23] During *Operation Trikora*, the air force was deployed as follows:

- 7 P-51Ds based at [Laha airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattimura_International_Airport), [Ambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambon,_Maluku). Prasetyo died after bailing out from the aircraft, landing in high-tide seas and could not be rescued by the Air Force's [PBY-5 Catalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBY-5_Catalina) due to the extreme weather after his accident.

### New Order era (1967–1998)



Indonesian [S-75 Dvina (SA-2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-75_Dvina) [Surface-to-air missile](182664.md) system

The coup attempt led by the [30 September Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_September_Movement) in 1965 changed everything and a new anti-communist regime from the Army, led by [Major General](36301471.md) [Suharto](217513.md), took power. The Chief of Staff of the Air Force, [Air Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Marshall) [Omar Dani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Dani) was removed from his position and court-martialed for his purported involvement in the coup. Ties with the Eastern bloc countries were cut, and thus support and spare parts for the planes became short. Unlike other branches of the Indonesian Armed Forces, the Air Force was broadly not implicated in the [Indonesian mass killings of 1965–1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_mass_killings_of_1965%E2%80%9366), due to the purported leftist sympathies of its members.[^cite_note-31] By August 1968 the situation was critical, and in early 1970, the Chief of Staff of the Air Force, Suwoto Sukandar, said that the spare parts situation meant that only 15–20 percent of aircraft were airworthy.[^cite_note-32] The MiG force made its farewell flight with a flypast of [Jakarta](16275.md) in 1970. Several of the relatively new MiG-19s were sold to [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] By October 1970, only one Tu-16 was still flying, but after an in-flight engine failure, it too was grounded.[^cite_note-DJOKOP926-35] But despite the problems, the Air Force still served with distinction in fighting militant remnants of the CPI in Java's provinces, particularly in Central and East Java.

With Suharto's assumption of the presidency and the office of Commander in Chief in 1967, the focus shifted to fighting the communist [PGRS/Paraku insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_insurgency_in_Sarawak). The Air Force launched Operation Lightning Strike ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Operasi Samber Kilat*) to support ground troops eradicate Sarawak communists that were present in [West Kalimantan](453636.md) and along Indonesia-Malaysia border by dropping troops to the target area, dropping logistical assistance, VIP transportation, medical evacuation and recon flights. The Air Force deployed Air Squadron 6 and 7 which were equipped with Mi-4, [Bell 204B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_204/205) and [UH-34D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_H-34) helicopters to the operation.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]



An Australian-built [CAC Sabre Mk.32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAC_Sabre), as used by the Indonesian Air Force.

The Air Force began to be re-equipped by receiving former [Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force) (RAAF) [CAC Sabres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAC_Sabre) – an Australian re-design of the [F-86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-86) Sabre – to replace the MiG-21s. The Sabre was used by the TNI-AU until 1982. In 1973, the United States supplied military assistance including [T-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-33_Shooting_Star) trainers and UH-34D helicopters in exchange for four old MiG-21F-13s. [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) took over the responsibility to train Indonesian pilots in the Sabre and in logistical aspects of the Air Force. Over the next three years, the US supplied 16 [North American Rockwell OV-10 Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Rockwell_OV-10_Bronco) counter-insurgency aircraft and [F-5E/F Tiger II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-5) fighters, in exchange for which the Indonesian Air Force handed over the majority of its remaining MiG-21F-13s, which were used to form a [US Air Force](32090.md) [Aggressor squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggressor_squadron).[^cite_note-DAVIES-38] Indonesia also purchased [BAE Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Hawk) Mk 53s from the United Kingdom in the 1970s.

The Indonesian Air Force took part in the 1975 Indonesian [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_invasion_of_East_Timor) of [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor).

### Reform era (1998–present)



An Indonesian Air Force Lockheed C-130H-30 at [Adisucipto International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adisucipto_International_Airport)

In 1999, the Indonesian military staged a [military intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_East_Timorese_crisis) in [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor) following an [independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_East_Timorese_independence_referendum). The result was that more than 1,500 civilians were killed and 70 percent of Dili's infrastructure razed. As the Indonesian military withdraws from East Timor, the Air Force sent a flight of BAE Hawk 109/209s to the [El Tari Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Tari_Airport) in [Kupang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kupang), [West Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Timor) to cover the withdrawal. On two occasions in September 1999, the Hawks were sent to intercept Australian military aircraft that were intruding Indonesian airspace.[^cite_note-39]

In response to the crisis, the United States and the European Union both imposed arms embargoes. Although the European Union chose not to renew its ban in 2000, the United States did not lift its embargo until November 2005. During this embargo, the Indonesian government turned to [Russia](25391.md) to supply them with arms including fighters, helicopters, missiles, radars and other equipment.

In 2002, the Indonesian Air Force conducted operations against separatists, such as the [Free Aceh Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Aceh_Movement) (*Gerakan Aceh Merdeka* (GAM)) and [Free Papua Movement](541409.md) (*Organisasi Papua Merdeka* (OPM)). In the conflict with GAM in Aceh, the Indonesian Air Force utilised OV-10Fs for counter-insurgency actions along with BAe Hawk 53 and 209.

The 51st Air Squadron, which at the time operated the [Aeronautics Defense Aerostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics_Defense_Systems#Unmanned_aerial_systems_\(UASs\)) unmanned aerial vehicles, provided aerial reconnaissance for the manhunt of Santoso (alias [Abu Wardah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Wardah)) and several other members of the [East Indonesia Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indonesia_Mujahideen) in the highlands of [Poso Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poso_Regency), Central Sulawesi during the [Operation Tinombala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Tinombala) in July 2016.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

On 27 September 2024, Indonesian Air Force is planning to establish a Space Unit to secure national airspace, though it will take time due to high costs and complex preparations. The Air Force is focused on regulatory, organizational, and resource preparation, recognizing the strategic importance of space in national defense.[^cite_note-42] The National Air Operation Command Space Unit (*Satuan Antariksa Koopsudnas*) was established on 29 January 2026.[^cite_note-43]

## Procurement and modernization programs

### 1980s–1990s



[A-4E Skyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-4_Skyhawk) of the Indonesian Air Force

In the early 1980s, the Air Force, needing modern strike aircraft, organised [Operation Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Alpha_\(Indonesia\)) to clandestinely acquire ex-[Israeli Air Force](177619.md) [A-4 Skyhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-4_Skyhawk). Air Force personnel were sent in secret by different routes and eventually Indonesia received 32 aircraft.[^cite_note-DJOKOP1535-44]

In 1982, Indonesia purchased 16 Northrop F-5E/F Tiger II from the United States to replace their Sabres under the Peace Komodo I and II.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] These were upgraded in [Belgium](3343.md) from 1995.

During 1986–88, there was a competition for the contract to provide a new fighter bomber, between the General Dynamics [F-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16_Fighting_Falcon) and [Dassault Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000). Indonesia eventually ordered 12 [F-16](11642.md)A/B *Fighting Falcon* Block 15 OCU as a new fighter in 1989. The Indonesian Air Force had originally planned to acquire 60 [F-16s](11642.md) to cover and defend its 12 million square kilometres of territory.[^cite_note-VIPER-47] A total of 10 F-16A and F-16B are still in service with Indonesian Air Force: 2 planes crashed in two different accidents. A follow-up order for 9 more F-16A Block-15 OCU was cancelled in favour of 24 Su-30KI,[^cite_note-FLANKER-48] but this order was also cancelled due to the [1997 Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Asian_financial_crisis). This requirement reflected the scale of Indonesia’s archipelagic territory, as its sovereignty under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea extends to the airspace above its archipelagic waters and territorial sea.[^cite_note-49]

The Indonesian Air Force ordered eight BAE Hawk Mk 109s and 32 Mk 209s in 1993. The last of these was delivered by January 1997.

### 2000s

In 2002, Indonesian F-16s underwent [Falcon UP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_F-16_Fighting_Falcon_variants#Falcon_UP) program in the Netherlands with Koolhaas Alphen bv, and Daedalus.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] In that same year, the Air Force received two [Sukhoi Su-27s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-27) and two [Sukhoi Su-30s](552808.md) from Russia. The fighters were partly paid for in Indonesian [palm oil](57561.md). The purchase, however, did not include any weaponry. Seven South Korean [KT-1B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_KT-1) basic trainers were also purchased.

By 2005 the Air Force was experiencing a logistical crisis. The A-4s were phased out of service in 2004, and to respond to the decreasing quantity of aircraft in service, in 2006, the Indonesian Air Force ordered three Sukhoi Su-27SKM and three [Su-30MK2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKK#Su-30MK2) to complete a full squadron. It was also made public that the four aircraft procured in 2003 were inactive and awaiting an upgrade of their communication systems, as they were incompatible with the Indonesian systems in use. The additional aircraft were ordered with systems complying with the Indonesian and international standards and would also include new weaponry for all variants.[^cite_note-FLANKER-48] A further 12 KT-1b trainers were also ordered in 2006.

Until 2008, the Indonesian Air Force had only purchased four types of missiles: [KS-1 Komet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KS-1_Komet), [Vympel K-13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vympel_K-13), [AIM-9 Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder) and [AGM-65 Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-65_Maverick). Starting from 2008, it started receiving more advanced Russian made [Vympel R-73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vympel_R-73) and [R-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-77) air-to-air missiles for its new Su-27 and Su-30 fighters. Also, a limited number of [Kh-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh-29), [Kh-31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh-31) and [Kh-59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh-59) air-to-ground missiles were delivered for the Su-30s.[^cite_note-52]

Local weapons are being developed such as P-100 air-to-ground bomb manufactured by PT Dahana and PT Sari Bahari Malang, East Java.[^cite_note-53] P-100 has been successfully tested in Su-27 and Su-30 for ground attack missions. Large-scale production has received certification from Ministry of Defence.[^cite_note-54]

### Minimum Essential Force (2010–2024)



Indonesian Air Force F-16D



TNI-AU [Boeing 737-2X9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) in 2011

During the visit of US President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) on 9–10 November 2010, the TNI-AU was offered 24 ex-USAF F-16 Block 25 aircraft as part of the Peace Bima-Sena II agreement.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] In October 2011, the House of Representatives approved the grant. The jets would be upgraded similar to the then latest Block 50/52 variant.[^cite_note-57] The TNI-AU is also progressing with the reactivation of all 10 units of F-16 Block 15 OCU, which resulted in the reactivation of TS-1606, TS-1609 and TS-1612 recently.[^cite_note-58] To replace the [Fokker F-27s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_F-27), the TNI-AU ordered nine Spanish [CASA C-295](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA_C-295) in joint production with PT. Dirgantara Indonesia. New [unmanned aerial vehicles](58900.md) were also purchased to strengthen TNI-AU aerial observation and were based at Supadio Air Force Base, [Pontianak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontianak,_Indonesia), [Kalimantan Barat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan_Barat).

Starting in 2010,[^cite_note-59] Minister of Defence Purnomo Yusgiantoro stated that TNI-AU will gradually purchase a total of 180 Su-27s and Su-30s to complete the needs of 10 squadrons.[^cite_note-60] India also offered TNI-AU Indian-Russian made [BrahMos](1814876.md) missiles to equip its Su-27s and Su-30s.[^cite_note-61]

Indonesia signed a memorandum of understanding to participate in the South Korean [KF-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_KF-X) programme in July 2010 and the terms of agreement was signed in 2011. Indonesia agreed to finance 20 percent of the project and would receive 50 jets in return, while South Korea would get 200 jets. A Defence ministry spokesman claimed that the jet would be more capable than the F-16 but less capable than the [F-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35). The project was started in 2009 and the first prototype is expected to roll out in the second half of 2020.[^cite_note-defenseindustrydaily1-62] But payment problems and technical difficulties had caused several delays and postponement of the KAI KF-X project.[^cite_note-defenseindustrydaily1-62] Indonesia has also signed an MOU with China to produce [C-705](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-705) anti-ship missiles which will arm the Sukhoi jets.[^cite_note-63]

In April 2011, Indonesia confirmed that it will buy 16 supersonic [KAI T-50 Golden Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle) trainer jets from South Korea for up to US\$400 million after an evaluation of the [Yakovlev Yak-130](1513240.md), [Guizhou JL-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guizhou_JL-9)/FTC-2000&#x20;*&#x20;Mountain Eagle* and [Aero L-159 Alca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-159_Alca).[^cite_note-64] The T-50 would replace the BAE Hawk MK-53 trainer jets. Deliveries were completed by January 2014 and were commissioned on 13 February 2014.[^cite_note-65]

In June 2011, Indonesia signed the final contract for 8 [Super Tucano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_314_Super_Tucano) as the replacement for the OV-10 Bronco in the counter-insurgency role[^cite_note-66] with a second contract for a further 8 aircraft in July 2012.[^cite_note-67] The first four units arrived in March 2012 with deliveries complete by 2014.[^cite_note-68]

In August 2011, Indonesia announced that it would acquire 18 [Grob G120TP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grob_G-120) for its basic trainer requirements which would likely replace the [FFA AS-202 Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FFA_AS-202_Bravo) and [Beechcraft T-34 Mentor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_T-34_Mentor) trainers.[^cite_note-grob-aircraft1-69]

On 29 December 2011 Indonesia committed to purchase 6 Su-30MK2 jet fighters in a US\$470 million procurement contract signed by the Defence Ministry and Russia's JSC Rosoboronexport.[^cite_note-JakartaGlobe311211-70] Deliveries were set to reportedly start after 2013.[^cite_note-71]

In January 2012, the Australian and Indonesian governments agreed to the transfer of four used C-130H Hercules aircraft from the Royal Australian Air Force to the Indonesian Air Force in 2012, which was approved by the US as the Hercules' producer.[^cite_note-72]

In January 2014, Defence Minister [Purnomo Yusgiantoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purnomo_Yusgiantoro) said that he hoped to start the replacement of the F-5 fighters under the upcoming 2015 to 2020 strategic plan. The Indonesian Air Force shortlisted five candidates for the replacement, comprising the [Sukhoi Su-35S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-35S), [Saab JAS 39 Gripen](87577.md), [Dassault Rafale](33731380.md), [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md), and F-16C/D Block 60.[^cite_note-Reed_Business_Information-73]

&#x20;In September 2014, Head of Indonesian National Armed Forces Public Relations and Media Office (*Kapuspen TNI*), Major General (TNI) Mochamad Fuad Basya explained the Minimum Essential Forces (MEF) Plan of TNI-AU consist of: 11 Fighter Squadrons, 6 Transport Squadrons, 2 VIP/VVIP Squadrons, 2 Patrol Squadrons, 4 Helicopter Squadrons, 2 Training Squadrons and 2 UAV Squadrons.[^cite_note-74]



Indonesian Air Force Sukhoi [Su-30MK2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-30MK2) at [Sultan Hasanuddin International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Hasanuddin_International_Airport)

In September 2015, Defence Minister [Ryamizard Ryacudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryamizard_Ryacudu) said that Indonesia had chosen the Su-35 Flanker-E to replace the F-5 Tiger II. Although in recent times, the deal to purchase Su-35 is in limbo due to Russia's refusal to give transfer of technology to Indonesia owing its small number of orders in addition to Indonesian concerns over price.[^cite_note-Indonesia_reportedly_negotiating_price_of_Russian_Su-35_fighters-75]

In January 2017, Indonesia approved for the acquisition of 5 Airbus A400M Atlas multi-role aircraft worth US\$2 billion, as part of the plans to boost the country's military capabilities. They are to be acquired in both transport and utility configurations and will be operated by the Indonesian Air Force (TNI-AU) Aviation Squadrons 31 and 32.[^cite_note-76] In November 2021, Indonesian ministry of defence officially orders two [Airbus A400M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A400M) in multirole tanker and transport configuration.[^cite_note-\:0-77]

On 12 May 2017, Defence Minister Ryamizard Ryacudu confirmed that the Indonesian Air Force (TNI-AU) will sign contract to buy 10 Su-35s. Russia will open the factory for Sukhoi Spare Parts in Indonesia as part of the contract.[^cite_note-78] On 28 November 2017, the Defence Minister confirmed that the Air Force (TNI-AU) had completed the procurement process of the aircraft.[^cite_note-79]



TS-1609, Indonesia Air Force F-16AM (Falcon STAR eMLU)

In October 2019 the Chief of Staff of the Indonesian Air Force [Yuyu Sutisna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuyu_Sutisna) said the Indonesian Air Force will purchase at least 2 Squadrons (32 aircraft) of F-16V Block 70/72 for the last phase of the 'Minimum Essential Force' program (MEF Fase 3 2020–2024) to replace the older [BAE Systems Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Hawk).[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Since 2017 the Indonesian Air Force with [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) and [Indonesian Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Aerospace) is also upgrading their existing F-16A/B with the Falcon STAR eMLU upgrade program that include new avionics, new armament capability that could carry [AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120_AMRAAM), and [JDAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Direct_Attack_Munition), new aircraft airframe that will last longer, [Sniper ATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sniper_Advanced_Targeting_Pod), [LITENING](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litening), and Bird Slicer [IFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_friend_or_foe). The upgrade is being carried out by Indonesian companies supervised by Lockheed Martin in Skadron Teknik 042.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

In January 2020, the [Indonesian Minister of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Indonesia\)), [Prabowo Subianto](2829084.md) during a bilateral meeting in [France](5843419.md) and met with his French counterpart [Florence Parly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Parly), it was reported that the Ministry is interested on French military equipment including 48 Dassault Rafales, four [Scorpène-class submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorp%C3%A8ne-class_submarine) and two [Gowind-class corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gowind-class_corvette).[^cite_note-86]

On 12 March 2020, [Bloomberg](432006.md) reported that Indonesia had cancelled the [Su-35](314292.md) deal due to diplomatic pressure from the United States. Indonesia instead opted to negotiate the purchase of [F-35](11812.md) aircraft.[^cite_note-87] This was later denied and Russia ensured that the cooperation would continue, although there were still a number of things to discuss.[^cite_note-88] On 8 July 2020 however, the Russian Ambassador to Indonesia, Lyudmila Vorobieva stated that Indonesia's plan to buy 11 Sukhoi Su-35 fighter jets from Russia is still continuing.[^cite_note-89]

On 20 July 2020, a letter written by defence minister Prabowo Subianto to his Austrian counterpart [Klaudia Tanner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaudia_Tanner), was published by Indonesian news outlets expressing interest in acquiring [Austria](26964606.md)'s *[Luftstreitkräfte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Air_Force)* entire fleet of Eurofighter Typhoon jets.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

On 18 February 2021, the Chief of Staff of the Air Force Air Chief Marshal [Fadjar Prasetyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadjar_Prasetyo) announced in the annual Air Force Leadership Meeting that the Indonesian Air Force plans to buy 36 Dassault Rafales and 8 [F-15EX Strike Eagle](495724.md#F-15EX_Eagle_II), of which 6 F-15EX are expected to arrive in 2022, along with [C-130J Super Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_C-130J_Super_Hercules) and [Medium-altitude long-endurance unmanned aerial vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium-altitude_long-endurance_unmanned_aerial_vehicle).[^cite_note-AA_Rafale_F-15-8] The Air Chief Marshal also stated that the Air Force will modernize its various fleet of combat aircraft, whose implementation will begin in 2021.[^cite_note-93]

In November 2021, Airbus confirmed that the Indonesian Ministry of Defence had signed a deal with Airbus for 2 [A400Ms](371265.md) configured for MRTT role, with an option, in the form of a letter of intent, for four additional aircraft.[^cite_note-\:0-77]

On 22 December 2021 during a Press Tour and Media Gathering, the Air Chief Marshall Fadjar Prasetyo confirmed that the Su-35 purchase will not go ahead. Regarding the planned purchase of the Sukhoi Su-35, Fadjar said it would be abandoned.[^cite_note-94]



Prototype of the [KAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Aerospace_Industries) [KF-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_KF-21_Boramae) [Air superiority fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_superiority_fighter)

Indonesia has ambitious plans for a homemade future 4.5 generation jet fighter with South Korea's [KAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Aerospace_Industries) and Indonesia's IAe, the KF-X/IF-X. On November 11, 2021, it was reported that South Korea and Indonesia had reached an agreement on the payment which Indonesia would have to make for their joint fighter jet project after concerns that Jakarta would default on the deal.[^cite_note-95] Under the renegotiated agreement, Indonesia will have to pay 1.6 trillion won (\$1.35 billion) of the 8.1 trillion-won project.[^cite_note-96]

On 10 February 2022, Dassault Aviation stated that Indonesia has officially signed an order for 42 Dassault Rafale F4s, concluding two years of negotiations with 6 aircraft for Batch I.[^cite_note-\:1-97][^cite_note-98] Hours later the [State Department of the United States](31975.md) approved Indonesia to purchase up to 36 F-15ID aircraft, the would-be Indonesian variant of the F-15EX.[^cite_note-\:2-99][^cite_note-100]

On 20 April 2022, PT Len Industri and [Thales Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group) signed a strategic partnership agreement for further collaboration on a wide array of defence-related topics including [radars](25676.md), [military satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_satellite), [electronic warfare](82272.md), UAVs and combat management systems.[^cite_note-101]

On 17 May 2022, PT Len Industri signed an agreement with Thales Group to jointly produce 13 [GCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-controlled_interception) radars.[^cite_note-102] Thales will be partnering with [state-owned](54232539.md) defence electronics firm PT Len Industri to supply [Ground Master 403](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Master_400) (GM403) air surveillance radars and SkyView command-and-control (C2) system to Indonesia.[^cite_note-103] On 18 June 2023 in a joint statement by Thales and PT Len it is revealed that Indonesia is ordering the Ground Master 400 Alpha (GM400α) variant.[^cite_note-104]

Indonesia has considered acquiring 12 used [Mirage 2000-5s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) from Qatar.[^cite_note-105] In November 2022, the Ministry of Finance of Indonesia has approved foreign loans to fund several Indonesian Air Force procurement programs, including the ex-Qatari Mirage 2000-5 proposal.[^cite_note-106] In January 2024, the spokesperson for the Minister of Defence disclosed the planned procurement of Mirage 2000s has been postponed, citing fiscal limitations.[^cite_note-107] On 9 February 2024, spokesperson for the Minister of Defence confirmed the plan to acquire Mirages have been cancelled.[^cite_note-108] The Air Force also conducts aerial reconnaissance over Indonesia's maritime territory and outer islands, including the country's three designated archipelagic sea lanes, supporting surveillance of activities such as illegal fishing, smuggling and unauthorized border crossings.[^cite_note-109]

## Rank structure

Main article: [Indonesian military ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_military_ranks)

Command and Staff rank





An example of a Second lieutenant rank (left) with red band indicating a command rank. While (right) without red band indicating a staff rank.

In the Air Force, as well as in other armed forces branches in Indonesia, the rank consists of [officer](36301328.md) known as in [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): "Perwira", [NCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer) "Bintara" and [enlisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlisted_rank) "Tamtama".

The proper title to address of rank on official document are as follows, all high-ranking officers (Marshal) use their rank followed by "(TNI)", while other officers use their rank followed by respective branch/corps abbreviation. For example, an Air Force colonel from Flying Corps use the title "Kolonel (PNB)", while an Air Force Marshal from Flying Corps use the title "Marsekal (TNI)". Enlisted airmen are not required to put their respective branch/corps specialty.[^cite_note-pangkat-123]

**Note**: Indonesia is not a member of [NATO](21133.md), so there is not an official equivalence between the Indonesian military ranks and those defined by NATO. The displayed parallel is approximate and for illustration purposes only.

**Note**: The red banding on the rank insignia denotes the personnel holding a command position which is agnostic of rank.

## Notable incidents

- A [MiG-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-17) *F-1112* strafed several strategic locations in [Jakarta](16275.md) and [Bogor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogor) on 9 March 1960. The fighter aircraft then crash landed in a paddy field in [Garut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garut). The aircraft was piloted by Lt. [Daniel Maukar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Alexander_Maukar), an Air Force pilot affiliated with [Permesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permesta). He was tasked to strafe [BPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataafse_Petroleum_Maatschappij) fuel tanks at [Tanjung Priok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Priok) harbor, before attempting to assassinate President [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) by strafing [Merdeka Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merdeka_Palace) and [Bogor Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogor_Palace). The incident is called the [Maukar Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Alexander_Maukar#Incident).
